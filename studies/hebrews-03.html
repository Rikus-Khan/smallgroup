The first readers were Jewish believers. Things had gotten hard, and some were tempted to slip back into the old religion they grew up in. So the writer of Hebrews keeps showing them one thing: whatever you'd go back to, Jesus is better.

Chapter 2 ended with Jesus as “a merciful and faithful high priest” who was tempted Himself and “is able to succour them that are tempted” (2:17–18). Chapter 3 opens with “Wherefore”: so then, look at Him.

Part one

Consider Jesus

Verses 1–6

Moses was a faithful servant. Jesus is the Son.

Fix your eyes on Him

“Consider” means look hard and keep looking, the way a man studies something until he understands it. Not a glance on Sunday.

An apostle is one who is sent: Jesus came from God to us. A high priest stood before God for the people: Jesus goes to God for us. He is both ends of the bridge.

And look what He calls these struggling men: “holy brethren.” Holy, because God set them apart for Himself, not because they had it all together. “Our profession” is what we say we believe: Jesus is Lord.

A servant, and the Son

To a Jewish believer, nobody stood higher than Moses, and God Himself called him faithful (Numbers 12:7). The writer doesn't knock Moses down.

Moses served in God's house. Christ is over it, as the Son, and He built it. The builder gets more honor than the building.

And the house is people: “whose house are we.” A forgiven drunk, an ex-con, a man with a past: all family.

Part two

To day

Verses 7–11

The second warning: they heard Him, and hardened their hearts.

“To day” keeps moving

  1. about 1440 BCIsrael in the wilderness hears God, and hardens its heart
  2. about 1000 BCDavid writes Psalm 95: “To day if ye will hear his voice”
  3. about AD 64 to 68Hebrews quotes it to struggling believers: still “To day”
  4. This morningThe same voice, the same word: To day

These lines come from Psalm 95. Notice who the writer says is speaking: “as the Holy Ghost saith.” Not “said,” long ago. Saith. The Spirit of God is still saying it, right now, to you.

David wrote it centuries after the wilderness, and Hebrews quoted it a thousand years after David, and every time it says “To day.” Today is the only day you can answer God in. Yesterday is gone, and tomorrow isn't promised.

The provocation

God brought Israel out of Egypt, through the Red sea, to Mount Sinai, then north to Kadesh-barnea, on the edge of the promised land. The map traces the route as it's commonly drawn.

At Kadesh they sent spies, heard the report of giants, and refused to go in (Numbers 13–14). They even said, “Let us make a captain, and let us return into Egypt” (Numbers 14:4). So they wandered for forty years, until that generation died.

Their children went in, led by Joshua, across the Jordan at Jericho.

“(There are eleven days’ journey from Horeb by the way of mount Seir unto Kadesh-barnea.)”

Deuteronomy 1:2

The trip God planned

11 days

From Mount Sinai (Horeb) to the edge of the land

The trip unbelief made

40 years

Round and round, in sight of what God had promised

They “saw my works forty years”: the plagues, the sea split open, manna every morning, water from a rock. They saw it all, and still didn't trust Him. “They do alway err in their heart”: the problem was never their feet. It was their hearts.

Some of us know this kind of trip. Years spent circling the same ground, the same bottle, the same mess, when God had a way through. That time is not wasted if you learn from it. You don't have to take one more lap.

Part three

Take heed, brethren

Verses 12–19

Watch your own heart, and watch out for each other.

Five warnings in Hebrews

  1. Don't drift2:1–4
  2. Don't harden your heart3:7–4:13
  3. Don't stay a baby5:11–6:12
  4. Don't throw it away10:26–39
  5. Don't refuse Him12:25–29

Hebrews has five warnings spread through it, and this is the second, the longest. They're strong words, and they should be. But read them the way the whole book reads them.

The same letter says Jesus “is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him” (7:25), and God has said, “I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee” (13:5). The warnings don't cancel those promises. So who are they for?

Who the warning is for

It shakes loose

The man who only sat near the faith

He came out of Egypt with the crowd and saw it all, but never trusted God himself. Being near it isn't the same as being in it.

It drives home

The man who truly belongs to Christ

He can't be lost. He hears the warning as a hand on his shoulder: hold on, don't harden. God uses warnings to keep His own.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

If you're afraid you've lost your salvation, that very fear shows a heart that hasn't hardened. A hard heart doesn't worry about God at all.

How a heart goes hard

Nobody wakes up hard. Sin deceives: it lies, one small lie at a time, and each one you believe lays down another layer. Then one day God speaks and you don't feel a thing.

God's answer isn't “try harder alone.” It's “exhort one another daily.” Brothers who hear your lies out loud and call them lies. That's how the crust breaks.

“Exhort” means call alongside: encourage, urge, sometimes warn. And notice “daily.” Not once a week. That's what this group is. Every day we meet, we're doing exactly what this verse says.

The man drifting rarely announces it. He just stops showing up, stops answering. “Lest any of you”: we watch for the one. Today, call or text one man here by name, and if someone calls you, pick up.

What the “if” means

  • “Hold on, or you'll lose it”Christ holds His own (John 10:28)
  • “Hold on long enough to earn it”salvation is a gift, never wages
  • Holding on shows you have itreal faith keeps going

Look at the tense: “we are made partakers”, we already have become sharers in Christ. Holding fast to the end is the proof of it, not the price of it. A man who finishes the race shows he was really in it.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Three questions, one answer

Who heard, and provoked God? (v. 16)Egypt's crowdThe very people who came out of Egypt by Moses. They'd been rescued, and they still rebelled.
Who grieved Him forty years? (v. 17)Those who sinnedTheir “carcases”, their dead bodies, fell one by one in the wilderness, short of the land.
Who did He swear would not enter? (v. 18)Those who believed notNot the weak, not the ones with the worst record. The ones who wouldn't trust Him.

They could not enter, because of unbelief.

“Not all”

Two men who believed

Caleb and Joshua

Same desert, same giants, same report. But they trusted God, and they walked into the land forty years later.

And the next generation

“Your little ones”

The fathers said their kids would be a prey. God said He'd bring the kids in, and He did.

“But your little ones, which ye said should be a prey, them will I bring in, and they shall know the land which ye have despised.”

Numbers 14:31

Some of you are afraid your past has ruined your kids' future. A father's failure didn't stop God's promise to those children. Trust Him today, and your story isn't the only one God is writing in your house.

Points to remember

  1. Consider Jesus“Consider the Apostle and High Priest of our profession” 3:1He came from God to us, and He goes to God for us. Keep your eyes on Him.
  2. Better than Moses“But Christ as a son over his own house; whose house are we” 3:6Moses was a faithful servant in the house. Jesus is the Son who built it, and we are His household.
  3. The wilderness“They could not enter in because of unbelief” 3:19Not their record, not the giants: unbelief. They saw God work and still wouldn't trust Him.
  4. The warning“Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief” 3:12It shakes loose the man who only sat near the faith, and it keeps the true believer holding on.
  5. One another“But exhort one another daily” 3:13Call alongside each other every day, and watch for the man who's drifting.
  6. To day“To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts” 3:15Today is the only day you can answer God. Answer Him now.

Question 1 of 6

Consider Jesus

Why are we told to “consider” Jesus as Apostle and High Priest?

What verses tell us about this?

“For in that he himself hath suffered being tempted, he is able to succour them that are tempted.”

Hebrews 2:18

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Answer

Because everything we need is in Him. As Apostle, He brought God's word to us. As High Priest, He stands before God for us. A man who keeps looking at Jesus, not at his past or his problems, keeps going.

Question 2 of 6

Better than Moses

How is Jesus greater than Moses?

What verses tell us about this?

“My servant Moses is not so, who is faithful in all mine house.”

Numbers 12:7

Answer

Moses was a faithful servant inside God's house. Jesus is the Son over the house, and the One who built it. And the house is us: every believer belongs to His household.

Question 3 of 6

The wilderness

Why couldn't Israel enter into God's rest?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD said unto Moses, How long will this people provoke me? and how long will it be ere they believe me, for all the signs which I have shewed among them?”

Numbers 14:11

Answer

Unbelief. They saw God's works for forty years and still wouldn't trust Him to do what He promised. An eleven-day trip became forty years in the desert. The way in has always been faith.

Question 4 of 6

The warning

If a believer is secure, who is this warning for?

What verses tell us about this?

“They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us: but they went out, that they might be made manifest that they were not all of us.”

1 John 2:19

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Answer

For the man who has been near the faith but never trusted Christ: stop standing at the edge and believe. And for the true believer: not to scare him into thinking he's lost, but to keep him holding fast. God keeps His own, and He uses warnings to do it.

Question 5 of 6

One another

What does God want us to do for each other every day?

What verses tell us about this?

“For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:10

“Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.”

Hebrews 10:25

Answer

Exhort one another: encourage, urge, and when needed, warn. Every day, because sin works every day. Notice who's missing, call the man who's drifting, and let your brothers call your lies what they are.

Question 6 of 6

To day

Why does the writer keep saying “To day”?

What verses tell us about this?

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Answer

Because today is the only day we can answer God. Hearts harden by putting Him off. But “To day” is also good news: whatever happened yesterday, God's mercy is new this morning, and the door is still open now.
