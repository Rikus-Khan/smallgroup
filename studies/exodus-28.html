Exodus 28

The man who carries the names

God tells Moses to dress Aaron as high priest, with the names of all twelve tribes on his shoulders and over his heart, a robe hung with bells, and a gold plate on his forehead: HOLINESS TO THE LORD.

Part one

“Holy garments… for glory and for beauty”

Verses 1–14

A priest is chosen, dressed, and given twelve names to carry on his shoulders.

What a priest is for

A priest stands between. He is a man taken from the people, to speak for them to God and to bring their gifts and their sins to God.

“For every high priest taken from among men is ordained for men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins: Who can have compassion on the ignorant, and on them that are out of the way; for that he himself also is compassed with infirmity. And by reason hereof he ought, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins. And no man taketh this honour unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron.”

Hebrews 5:1–4

Notice who picks him: God does. “Take thou unto thee Aaron thy brother.” No man takes the office for himself. Moses' own brother, with his four sons Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar.

Clothes do not make a priest

Notice who makes the clothes: “all that are wise hearted, whom I have filled with the spirit of wisdom.” Even the sewing is a gift of God.

Here is the hard truth about this man. Before Moses comes down from this mountain, Aaron will make a golden calf (32:4) and say, “there came out this calf” (32:24). The clothes do not make Aaron holy. They show what a priest should be, and they point to a better priest than Aaron.

Cunning work means skilled work, and curious means made with great care. The colours are gold, blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen, the same as the tabernacle's own curtains.

Twelve names on his shoulders

one onyx stoneReuben · Simeon · LeviJudah · Dan · Naphtali
the other stoneGad · Asher · IssacharZebulun · Joseph · Benjamin

“According to their birth” means by age: Jacob's sons in the order they were born (Genesis 29–35). The text does not say which six go on which shoulder. Ouches are the little gold settings that hold a stone, and wreathen means twisted, like a rope of gold wire.

A shoulder is where a man carries a load. When Aaron walked in before the LORD, he carried every tribe with him, “for a memorial.” The people stayed outside, and their names went in.

Twelve men with a past

Read the list again. These are not twelve saints. They are Jacob's sons, and their stories are in Genesis.

  • Reubendisgraced his father (Genesis 35:22)
  • Simeon and Levianswered a wrong with a massacre (Genesis 34)
  • Ten of themsold their own brother Joseph (Genesis 37)
  • Judahwent wrong with Tamar (Genesis 38)

And Levi is on a stone of gold on Aaron's own shoulder: Aaron is a Levite himself. Every name is cut in stone, set in gold, and carried before God. Not one was left out for his record.

If you have a record, God has not lost your name. The man who is carrying the names does not read them as a list of crimes. He carries them “for a memorial.” Same for you.

The breastplate is foursquare (square), folded double, one span (about nine inches) each way, and the stones sit in gold inclosings (settings). Each stone has one tribe's name cut in it. Some of these stone names, and so their colours, are hard to pin down today, and the text does not say which stone belongs to which tribe, so we won't guess. What it does say: twelve stones, twelve names, “set in gold.”

Tied on so it cannot come loose

  • gold chainsfrom the shoulder stones down to the breastplate
  • gold ringsat the corners of the breastplate and on the ephod
  • a lace of blueties the rings, so it sits above the girdle

The point of all this fixing is one line: “that the breastplate be not loosed from the ephod.” The names on his heart and the names on his shoulders are joined to each other, and joined to him. They cannot slip off.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father’s hand.”

John 10:28–29

Jesus said it of his own: no one pulls you out of his hand. Not even you.

Shoulders and heart

On his shoulders

Strength

A shoulder carries the weight. Aaron bore their names “for a memorial,” so the burden was his, not theirs.

Over his heart

Love

Right on his chest, “when he goeth in unto the holy place, for a memorial before the LORD continually.”

“Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands; thy walls are continually before me.”

Isaiah 49:16

The same picture, spoken by God himself to his people: your name is cut where I cannot lose it. Both the strong hand and the near heart carry you.

The Urim and the Thummim

Verse 30 gives the command and nothing more: “thou shalt put in the breastplate of judgment the Urim and the Thummim.” It also calls the breastplate “the judgment,” because the priest carried the means of asking God for a decision.

The Bible does not tell us what they were, or how they were used. It never says. Many have guessed, but guessing is all it is. We will not add to what God wrote.

What the Bible does say

What the Bible does say is this: they were asked of the LORD, and the answer came. They were carried upon Aaron's heart, and they were for finding out God's will.

“And he shall stand before Eleazar the priest, who shall ask counsel for him after the judgment of Urim before the LORD: at his word shall they go out, and at his word they shall come in, both he, and all the children of Israel with him, even all the congregation.”

Numbers 27:21

“And when Saul enquired of the LORD, the LORD answered him not, neither by dreams nor by Urim, nor by prophets.”

1 Samuel 28:6

King Saul, a man who had turned his back on God, asked and was given nothing. The answer depended on God, not on the stones. The question for you is simpler: God has put his word in your hands, and he answers a man who asks.

A better priest than Aaron

Aaron

A sinner, carrying sinners

Chosen from men, weak himself, offering “first for his own sins, and then for the people's.” And he died.

Jesus

Holy, and alive for ever

He carries the names of everyone who comes to God by him, and he never has to stop.

“Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession. For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:14–16

He ever liveth

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them. For such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and made higher than the heavens; Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people’s: for this he did once, when he offered up himself.”

Hebrews 7:25–27

Aaron went in with stones on his heart. Jesus goes in with you on his. Right now, today, he “ever liveth to make intercession for them.” “Come boldly,” the writer says. You do not have to wait until you've cleaned up.

Part three

“That he die not”

Verses 31–43

Bells, a gold plate, and clothes to cover the men who come near.

Bells and pomegranates

“A golden bell and a pomegranate, a golden bell and a pomegranate, upon the hem of the robe round about.” A habergeon is a coat of mail: the neck hole of the robe was bound round, like armour, so it would not tear.

Why the bells? “His sound shall be heard when he goeth in unto the holy place before the LORD, and when he cometh out, that he die not.” Most of the people could not see into the holy place. Many picture them listening for the bells: the priest was moving, and alive.

Why pomegranates? The text does not say. It pairs the sound of the bell with fruit, and that is the pairing we will keep: a priest who is heard, and who is fruitful.

“That he die not”

God has just said something sobering: going into the holy place is not casual. The holy place is where God is. A man who goes in carelessly could die. Nadab and Abihu, Aaron's own two oldest sons, would learn it (Leviticus 10:1–3).

That is why there are so many rules, so many clothes, and so much blood. Not because God is hard to reach, but because he is holy and we are not.

“But Christ being come an high priest of good things to come, by a greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands, that is to say, not of this building; Neither by the blood of goats and calves, but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us.”

Hebrews 9:11–12

Jesus went in once, with his own blood, and he lives for ever. No bells are needed to tell us he is alive: “he ever liveth to make intercession.” And he opened the way for us: “Let us therefore come boldly.”

The plate on his forehead

HOLINESS TO THE LORDpure gold, on a blue lace, on the front of the mitre, “always upon his forehead”

Notice what Aaron bears: “the iniquity of the holy things.” Even Israel's best gifts, brought to God in worship, were stained. People are not perfect, even when they are doing good. So the plate stood in between, and God said, “that they may be accepted before the LORD.”

“To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved.”

Ephesians 1:6

Iniquity is sin, twisted wrongdoing. Accepted, not because of what we bring, but because of who stands for us. Aaron wore a word he could never match himself, and Jesus wore it perfectly.

Covered before God

  • anointpour oil on, to set apart
  • consecrategive the office to; fill the hands
  • sanctifymake holy, set apart for God

The breeches are plain: “to cover their nakedness.” When a priest climbed the altar, there must be nothing of the man's shame on show before God. Whatever else the priesthood was, it was also a covering.

God has been covering people since the garden, when he made “coats of skins, and clothed them” (Genesis 3:21). He does not leave ashamed people standing bare.

“For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ.”

Galatians 3:27

Now you are a priest

The clothes could not be worn by Aaron's sons alone for ever. Peter says something stunning to every believer:

“But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:”

1 Peter 2:9

“And from Jesus Christ, who is the faithful witness, and the first begotten of the dead, and the prince of the kings of the earth. Unto him that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood, And hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father; to him be glory and dominion for ever and ever. Amen.”

Revelation 1:5–6

A “royal priesthood.” A believer does not need a man in a linen turban to go between. Jesus is our great high priest, and in him you may speak to God yourself, and pray for the man next to you.

One thing from the plate carries over: holiness to the LORD. Not a show for people, but a life set apart to him, one day at a time.

Real world: you are carried

Some of you will go to work tomorrow, have a bad day, and feel that God has given up on you. Hold the picture of the breastplate.

  • You are namedYour name is on his heart. It is not in the list of failures.
  • You are carriedHe bears the load. The bells ring: he is alive, and he is working.
  • You can come“Come boldly unto the throne of grace.” Today. In your truck, at your desk, on your break.
  • You can pray for othersYou are a priest. Today, say a brother's name to God.

Pray first, and then text a brother: “I am praying for you today.” That is priest's work, and anyone can do it.

Question 1 of 6

The names

Whose names did Aaron carry before the LORD, and where did he carry them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The names of the children of Israel, all twelve tribes: six and six on two onyx stones on his shoulders, and all twelve again on the breastplate over his heart. God carries his people by name. He has not lost yours.

Question 2 of 6

The stones

What was set in the breastplate of judgment, and how many stones were there?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Twelve stones in four rows of three, each one engraved with the name of a tribe and set in gold. Sardius, topaz and carbuncle came first, and jasper last. Every tribe has its own stone.

Question 3 of 6

The answer

What did the LORD tell Moses to put in the breastplate, and what does the Bible not tell us about it?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he shall stand before Eleazar the priest, who shall ask counsel for him after the judgment of Urim before the LORD: at his word shall they go out, and at his word they shall come in, both he, and all the children of Israel with him, even all the congregation.”

Numbers 27:21

Answer

The Urim and the Thummim, to be carried upon Aaron's heart. The Bible does not tell us what they were or how they were used. It only shows that the LORD answered through them when his people asked.

Question 4 of 6

The bells

Why did the robe have golden bells on its hem?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So his sound would be heard going in and coming out, “that he die not.” The people outside could hear that the priest was still alive and at his work. Our high priest ever lives to plead for us.

Question 5 of 6

The plate

What was engraved on the gold plate, and what did Aaron bear?

What verses tell us about this?

“To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved.”

Ephesians 1:6

Answer

HOLINESS TO THE LORD, in pure gold on his forehead. He bore “the iniquity of the holy things,” so that Israel would be accepted. We are accepted the same way: in the Beloved, not by our own record.

Question 6 of 6

The covering

Why did Aaron and his sons wear the holy garments and the linen breeches?

What verses tell us about this?

“For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ.”

Galatians 3:27

Answer

To cover their shame when they came near, “that they bear not iniquity, and die.” A holy God requires a covering, and he provides it. In Christ we have put on Christ.
