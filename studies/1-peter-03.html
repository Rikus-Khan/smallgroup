Part one

Wives and husbands

Verses 1–7

Six verses for the wives, one for the husbands. And the one for the husbands carries a warning nobody else gets.

Won without a word

A husband who will not hear the word“obey not the word”
→
Sees his wife's life“won… by the conversation of the wives”

Many women in Peter's churches became Christians while their husbands did not. Peter says her sermons will not change him: her life will. “Conversation” again means way of life, and “chaste... coupled with fear” means pure and respectful.

In subjection means a woman lines herself up under her husband by her own choice. It never means she must stay and take a beating. Hitting a wife is a sin and a crime. If a woman is in danger, safety comes first: get out, and call someone. And any man here who has raised his hand to a woman needs to stop, tell God, and get help today.

What God prices

  • the hair, the gold, the clothesPeter is not banning a good haircut. He says the outward must not be where her worth comes from. The same goes for a man's truck, his watch, his reputation.
  • making people look at the outsideLooks fade, and the world sells a lot of cures for it. None of them cure a heart.
  • the hidden man of the heartA “meek and quiet spirit”: calm, not easily rattled. “In the sight of God of great price.” This is the thing God pays the most attention to.

Meek is not weak. It is strength under control. A man at peace is worth more in any house than a man who is loud.

Not afraid with any amazement.

Our Bibles spell her Sara, as the King James does. She is Abraham's wife, an ordinary woman who “trusted in God.” The old word amazement means terror, the kind that makes a person panic and do foolish things.

Peter's point: a woman who trusts God can be steady in a hard house and does not have to live afraid. The men are not off the hook either. Making your home a place people are afraid of is the opposite of everything in this letter.

One verse, four jobs for the husband

  1. Dwell with herstay, be there. Live in the same house in more than address
  2. According to knowledgeknow her: what wears her out, what scares her, what she needs
  3. Giving honourtreat her as someone valuable. “Weaker vessel” means physically weaker, like fine china is handled with care. It does not mean lesser
  4. Heirs togetherof the same grace of life. She stands exactly where you stand with God

“There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus.”

Galatians 3:28

Prayers that hit the ceiling

A man who dishonours his wifeharsh words, ignoring her, a hand raised
→
His prayers are“hindered”

Peter does not say God stops loving him, or that his salvation is gone. He says something gets in the way of the conversation. A man cannot treat his wife like dirt on Saturday and expect to talk with the Father on Sunday as though nothing happened.

“Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;”

Ephesians 5:25

Today: ask her one real question and listen for ten minutes with the phone down. If you have hurt her, say it plainly: I was wrong. That is the first thing a good husband learns, and it is not weakness.

Part two

Called to bless

Verses 8–17

How does a man answer an insult? And what does he say when someone asks about his faith?

Five ways to treat the brotherhood

One mind

pulling the same way, not each for himself

Compassion

feeling it when a brother hurts

Love as brethren

family love, not polite love

Pitiful

tender-hearted

Courteous

humble and kind in how you speak

That is a good description of this group at its best. Notice what it asks: nothing fancy. Just show up, feel something, speak kindly.

Don't hand it back

  • evil for evilHe hit me, so I hit him. The easy answer, the one every man here knows by heart.
  • railing for railingHe cursed me, so I cursed him louder. Two men shouting, and neither of them winning.
  • contrariwise blessingContrariwise means the opposite way round. Say something good about him. Pray for him. Walk away. That is the move that is hard and strong.

“Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.”

Romans 12:21

“Knowing that ye are thereunto called, that ye should inherit a blessing.” You were given a blessing, so you have one to give away.

How to see good days

  1. Refrain your tonguehold it. Most of the damage in a life comes out of the mouth
  2. Speak no guileno tricks, no angle, no spin
  3. Eschew evil, and do goodeschew is an old word for steer clear of. Then do something good in its place
  4. Seek peace, and ensue itensue means chase it down, as hard as you would chase anything else

Peter is borrowing from Psalm 34. And the reason is in verse 12: “the eyes of the Lord are over the righteous, and his ears are open unto their prayers.” He is watching, and He is listening. The same is true for you on the day you fail.

When somebody asks

The guy at workYou're different lately. What happened to you?

YouI got in a bad place. Somebody showed me Jesus, and I trusted Him. I'm still not perfect, but I'm not who I was.
“a reason of the hope that is in you”

Verse 15 has three parts. “Sanctify the Lord God in your hearts”: put Him first inside, before you open your mouth. “Be ready always”: have your story ready in two sentences. “With meekness and fear”: gently, not like a man who has won an argument.

Your story is the answer. It does not have to be polished, only true. And keep “a good conscience,” so that those who accuse you “may be ashamed.”

Part three

The just for the unjust

Verses 18–22

Why can a man suffer unfairly and not break? Because Christ did it first, and it worked.

The just for the unjust

The crossfor sins

The way into God

Jesus“the just”: nothing wrong in Him
Us“the unjust”: guilty as charged

He took the place of the guilty. He paid for sins “once,” which means it is finished, with nothing left to add. And the purpose was a person: “that he might bring us to God.” Not to a religion, to a Father.

That is why a man who has failed can walk in. The one who stands at the door is the One who paid for the failing.

A hard text, taken humbly

One view

Christ, by His Spirit, was speaking through Noah as “a preacher of righteousness” (2 Peter 2:5) to people who are now in prison, the dead who would not listen

Another view

After the cross, Christ announced His victory to rebel spirits who are held in prison, so they know they are beaten

What is plain

Noah's neighbours had a long time to turn: “the longsuffering of God waited.” Only eight went through. And Christ has won

Faithful Bible teachers, Baptists among them, hold both of the first two views. Nobody should pretend to have it all worked out. What this passage plainly does is point at Jesus' victory and God's patience. It does not teach that a man gets a second chance after death. “It is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment” (Hebrews 9:27).

The ark: a picture of salvation

Peter says “eight souls were saved by water”, and then calls it a “like figure”: a picture of something. The flood wiped out a world. The ark carried eight people through it.

Look at what saved them: the ark. Not the water. The water was the danger, and the ark was the way through. In Peter's picture the ark is Christ. Those who were in Him came through the judgment, and those who were not did not.

Baptism: the picture and the pledge

  • a bath for the body“Not the putting away of the filth of the flesh.” Water on the outside cannot wash anything off the inside.
  • the water itselfNoah's water drowned the world. It did not save anyone. Baptism has the same limit: the water has no power in it.
  • the answer of a good conscience toward GodA man who already trusts Christ goes down into the water and says out loud: I belong to Jesus. It is a pledge to God, made with a clean conscience.

So what saves? “By the resurrection of Jesus Christ.” The Lord who rose is the one who saves. Baptism is the picture of that, the first step of obedience after it, and a good pledge to make. If you have trusted Jesus and have not yet been baptized, it is a good next step. Talk to your pastor.

Every power is under His feet.

The chapter begins with a suffering church, and it ends with Jesus “on the right hand of God.” The ones frightening these believers, from the emperor to the neighbours to the spirits behind them, are all “made subject unto him.”

“It is Christ that died, yea rather, that is risen again, who is even at the right hand of God, who also maketh intercession for us.”

Romans 8:34

The same Jesus who suffered once for sins is the one sitting there now, and He is speaking to the Father about you. Whatever you are afraid of today is smaller than He is.

Points to remember

  1. Husbands“Ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife… that your prayers be not hindered” 3:7A husband is to know his wife, honour her and treat her as an equal heir of grace. If he does not, his prayers suffer.
  2. The heart“The hidden man of the heart… a meek and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God of great price” 3:4What God prices most is not the outside: it is a calm, gentle heart.
  3. Blessing“Not rendering evil for evil, or railing for railing: but contrariwise blessing” 3:9Answer a wrong with a blessing, because you were given one.
  4. Hope“Be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you with meekness and fear” 3:15Have your story ready, and tell it gently.
  5. Christ's death“Christ also hath once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God” 3:18He paid once, in our place, to bring us to the Father.
  6. Baptism“Not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God… by the resurrection of Jesus Christ” 3:21Baptism is the picture and the pledge. What saves is Christ's resurrection, not the water.

Question 1 of 6

Husbands

What are husbands to do for their wives, and what hinders their prayers?

What verses tell us about this?

“Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;”

Ephesians 5:25

Answer

Dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour as to fellow heirs of the grace of life. Failing to honour his wife hinders a husband's prayers.

Question 2 of 6

The heart

What is the adorning that is of great price in the sight of God?

What verses tell us about this?

“for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

Answer

The hidden man of the heart: a meek and quiet spirit, which does not fade. God prices the heart, not the clothes.

Question 3 of 6

Blessing

How are we to answer evil and railing?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.”

Romans 12:21

Answer

Not evil for evil or railing for railing, but with a blessing, because we were called to inherit one.

Question 4 of 6

Hope

What are we to be ready always to give, and in what spirit?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

An answer to anyone who asks the reason for our hope, given with meekness and fear, and with a good conscience, so that those who accuse us may be ashamed.

Question 5 of 6

Christ's death

Why did Christ suffer once for sins?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The just died for the unjust to bring us to God. Once is enough, and He is now at God's right hand with every power under Him.

Question 6 of 6

Baptism

If baptism is not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, what is it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The answer of a good conscience toward God: the picture and the pledge. Like the ark, it points to Christ. What saves is the resurrection of Jesus Christ, not the water.
