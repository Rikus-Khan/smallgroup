John 16

Sorrow turned to joy

Jesus tells His men He's leaving, promises the Comforter, and leaves them with one last word: “Be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.”

Part one

The Comforter is coming

Verses 1–15

Bad news first, then the best news they could have heard.

Warned ahead of time

skandalizoskan-dah-LEED-zohto trip up, to make stumble

A skandalon was the trigger stick of a trap. Jesus tells them what's coming so it won't spring on them by surprise.

Being “put out of the synagogue” meant losing your place among your own people (9:22). Some would even kill believers and call it serving God, the way Saul of Tarsus thought before Jesus met him (Acts 26:9).

Any man in recovery knows this: you get through a trigger better when you saw it coming. Jesus doesn't promise an easy road. He tells the truth about it ahead of time.

Better that He goes?

“Expedient” means to your advantage. That sounded crazy to men whose hearts were full of sorrow (v. 6). How could losing Jesus be good?

In His body, Jesus could be in one room at a time. The Comforter He sends lives inside every believer, in every place, all at once, and “for ever” (14:16).

He moves in the moment you believe (Ephesians 1:13). You never have to wait in line to be near God.

The Spirit makes the case

“Reprove” is a courtroom word: to lay out the evidence until a man knows he's guilty. The Spirit convicts the world on three counts:

Of sin

9

The root sin isn't a list of bad deeds. It's refusing to believe on Jesus.

Of righteousness

10

The Father took Jesus back to heaven. That proves He was righteous, the One He claimed to be.

Of judgment

11

At the cross, Satan, “the prince of this world,” was judged and beaten. His sentence is already passed.

If you've ever felt that weight, the knowing deep down that you were guilty and needed Jesus, that was the Holy Spirit. Conviction isn't God pushing you away. It's Him drawing you in.

A guide who points to Jesus

The Father“All things that the Father hath are mine” (v. 15)Jesus
Jesus“he shall receive of mine”the Spirit
the Spirit“and shall shew it unto you”you

“He shall not speak of himself” means He doesn't speak on His own authority or make Himself the star. He's like a floodlight on a building at night: you don't stare at the light, you look at what it shows. The Spirit shines on Jesus.

The eleven couldn't take it all in yet (v. 12). After the Spirit came, He guided them as they wrote the New Testament. When you open it today, the same Spirit who lives in you helps you understand it.

Part two

A little while

Verses 16–24

Weeping for a weekend, then a joy nobody can take.

Seven times: “a little while”

The phrase shows up seven times in four verses. The disciples kept turning it over and couldn't crack it. Looking back, it's plain:

Thursday night“A little while…”Jesus is still at the table with them.
Friday and Saturday“Ye shall not see me”He is crucified and laid in the tomb.
Sunday morning“Ye shall see me”He is risen, and they see Him with their own eyes.

Notice Jesus knew what they wanted to ask before they asked it (v. 19). He knows the questions you're carrying too, even the ones you haven't said out loud.

Turned, not replaced

Not swapped for joy, but turned into joy. The cross that broke their hearts became the reason for their joy.

A woman in labor knows it (v. 21). The pain is real, but there's a baby at the end of it.

“For his anger endureth but a moment; in his favour is life: weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning.”

Psalm 30:5

The hard early days of getting clean can feel like that. They're not the end of the story either.

What “in my name” means

Until now, the disciples had brought everything to Jesus face to face. Soon they would go straight to the Father, on Jesus' account. So what does praying “in my name” mean?

  • A password at the end of a prayerThe words alone don't unlock anything.
  • A blank check for whatever I wantHis name isn't a way to get rich or get my way.
  • Coming on His account, asking what fits His nameWe come to the Father because of who Jesus is and what He did, and we ask for what He would ask for.

And look at why He wants us to ask (v. 24): “that your joy may be full.”

Part three

Be of good cheer

Verses 25–33

The last thing He tells them before He prays.

His whole story in one verse

Up to now He'd spoken in “proverbs”, pictures like the vine and the woman in labor. Here He says it plainly: where He came from, and where He's going.

And notice verse 27: “The Father himself loveth you.” Jesus didn't come to talk an angry Father into putting up with us. The Father sent Him because He loves us.

“Do ye now believe?”

The disciples felt sure of themselves: “Now are we sure” (v. 30). Jesus loved them enough to tell them the truth. Within hours, every one of them would run.

“And Jesus saith unto them, All ye shall be offended because of me this night: for it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered.”

Mark 14:27

He knew they'd fail Him, and He didn't give up on them. After He rose, He came looking for them. Your worst night doesn't cancel His love either.

Left alone by His friends, He still wasn't alone: “the Father is with me.” And He makes the same promise to every believer:

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5
thlipsisTHLIP-sispressure, being squeezed

Jesus never promised a life without tribulation. He promised the opposite. In the world, you'll get squeezed.

Be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.

He says it the night before the cross, as if the battle were already won. It was. Peace doesn't come from the world getting easier. It's found “in me”: in Him.

Points to remember

  1. The Holy Spirit“It is expedient for you that I go away” 16:7The Comforter lives in every believer, everywhere, for good.
  2. Conviction“He will reprove the world of sin” 16:8He shows the world its sin, Christ's righteousness, and Satan's defeat.
  3. Truth“He shall glorify me” 16:14The Spirit guides us into truth, and He always points to Jesus.
  4. Sorrow and joy“Your sorrow shall be turned into joy” 16:20He turns our sorrow into a joy no one can take away.
  5. Prayer“Ask, and ye shall receive” 16:24We come to the Father on Jesus' account, and the Father Himself loves us.
  6. Peace“I have overcome the world” 16:33Trouble in the world, peace in Him. He has already won.

Question 1 of 6

The Holy Spirit

Why was it better for the disciples that Jesus went away?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever;”

John 14:16

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

Answer

Because when He went to the Father, He sent the Comforter. In His body Jesus was in one place at a time. The Holy Spirit lives inside every believer, everywhere, from the moment we believe, and He stays for ever.

Question 2 of 6

Conviction

What does the Holy Spirit show the world?

What verses tell us about this?

“I verily thought with myself, that I ought to do many things contrary to the name of Jesus of Nazareth.”

Acts 26:9

Answer

He convicts the world of sin (most of all, not believing on Jesus), of righteousness (Jesus was right, and the Father proved it by taking Him home), and of judgment (Satan is already judged). Saul was sure he was right until the Spirit showed him the truth. That conviction is God drawing a man to Jesus.

Question 3 of 6

Truth

Where does the Spirit point us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To Jesus. The Spirit guides us into truth, but He doesn't make Himself the center. He takes what belongs to Jesus and shows it to us, so that Jesus gets the glory. If something claims to be “the Spirit” but leads you away from Jesus, it isn't Him.

Question 4 of 6

Sorrow and joy

What does Jesus do with our sorrow?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He turns it into joy. He doesn't just replace it; the very thing that caused the sorrow becomes the reason for joy, the way a mother's pain ends in holding her child. The cross broke the disciples' hearts, and the empty tomb gave them a joy no man could take.

Question 5 of 6

Prayer

What does it mean to ask in Jesus' name?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It means coming to the Father on Jesus' account, because of who He is and what He did, and asking for what fits His name. It isn't a password or a blank check. And we don't come to a Father who has to be talked into it. The Father Himself loves us.

Question 6 of 6

Peace

Where can we find peace in a world of trouble?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith.”

1 John 5:4

Answer

In Jesus, not in the world. He promised we'd have tribulation out there. But He has already overcome the world, and the believer who trusts Him shares that victory. Peace isn't the absence of trouble. It's being in Him while the trouble comes.
