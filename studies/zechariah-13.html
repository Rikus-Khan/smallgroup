Zechariah 13

A fountain opened

God opens a fountain to wash away sin and uncleanness, clears out the idols and the liars, and strikes His own Shepherd so that His people can be refined and brought home.

Part one

A fountain opened

Verse 1

Zechariah preached to the Jews back from Babylon, about 520 BC. Chapter 12 ended with tears for the One they pierced. This is what God gives the weeping.

Not a basin, a fountain

Two words. Sin is what we did. Uncleanness is what it left on us: the shame, the filthy feeling that a shower can't reach. God's fountain deals with both.

And it's a fountain, not a bowl. A bowl gets dirty after a few washings. A fountain keeps running. You don't use it up, no matter how many times you come back.

Where the fountain flows from

Zechariah 12:10 and 13:1 belong together: they look on the One they pierced, and a fountain is opened. The New Testament shows us where cleansing comes from.

The pierced side

“But one of the soldiers with a spear pierced his side, and forthwith came there out blood and water.”

John 19:34

What His blood does

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

This is for men like us. Paul wrote to a church full of former drunkards, thieves and worse, and he didn't pretend they'd always been clean:

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Part two

No more lies

Verses 2–6

When God cleans house, the idols go, and so do the liars who served them.

Cleaned out

The fountain doesn't only wash. It clears out. Three things leave the land:

  • the names of the idolsso forgotten no one says them anymore
  • the prophetsthe false ones, who spoke lies in God's name
  • the unclean spiritthe evil power behind the idols and the lies
  • the LORD alone“It is my people … The LORD is my God” (verse 9)

Verse 3 sounds harsh. Under Moses' law a false prophet was to die (Deuteronomy 18:20). In that day people will love God's truth so much that even a man's own parents won't cover for his lies. Truth will matter more than family pride.

Taking off the costume

Prophets like Elijah wore a rough, hairy cloak. False prophets put one on to look the part. In that day, they'll be ashamed and drop the act.

The former prophetI am no prophet, I am an husbandman; for man taught me to keep cattle from my youth.
just a farmer

Someone who knows himWhat are these wounds in thine hands?

The former prophetThose with which I was wounded in the house of my friends.
a cover story

Idol prophets cut themselves in their worship (“they … cut themselves after their manner with knives and lancets”, 1 Kings 18:28). Asked about his scars, this man makes up a story. Read in its place, this is still the false prophet. The verse about Jesus comes next.

Masks

Most of us have worn a costume or two. The tough guy. The guy who's got it handled. The one with a story ready for every scar.

These men only took off the costume because they were ashamed and afraid. God offers a better way to take it off: tell the truth, to Him and to a brother, before you're caught.

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

There's a fountain open for whatever's under the mask. You don't need the costume anymore.

Part three

Smite the shepherd

Verses 7–9

God calls for a sword, and points it at His own Shepherd.

Jesus read this about Himself

On the night He was arrested, on the way to the Mount of Olives, Jesus quoted this verse:

Jesus, that night

“Then saith Jesus unto them, All ye shall be offended because of me this night: for it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered abroad.”

Matthew 26:31

A few hours later

“But all this was done, that the scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled. Then all the disciples forsook him, and fled.”

Matthew 26:56

Notice who swings the sword: “saith the LORD of hosts.” Wicked men arrested Jesus, but the cross was God's plan. The Father let the blow fall on His Son so it wouldn't fall on us.

And the scattered disciples weren't thrown away. Jesus rose and gathered every one of them back, Peter included.

“The man that is my fellow”

עָמִית said amith fellow, companion, one standing next to you as an equal

God calls this Shepherd a man, and also His equal, the One who stands beside Him. That's who Jesus is: fully man and fully God. Nobody less could have carried our sin.

Through the fire

Verse 8 describes a terrible time of judgment in the land, with only a third left. God doesn't hide how hard it will be. But the third that's left isn't thrown in the fire to be destroyed. It's put there to be refined.

verse 9Through the fire

A refiner heats silver until the dross, the junk mixed into it, rises and can be skimmed off.

verse 9They call on my name

The heat drives them to pray, and God answers.

verse 9It is my people

“They shall say, The LORD is my God.”

“That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ:”

1 Peter 1:7

Said both ways

God says

“It is my people”

He claims them out loud. They belong to Him.

They say

“The LORD is my God”

They claim Him back. No more idols, no more masks.

God made the same promise through Hosea: “I will say to them which were not my people, Thou art my people; and they shall say, Thou art my God” (Hosea 2:23). These words are spoken over Israel. And they show us the kind of God He is: the fire doesn't end with Him letting go. It ends with Him saying, mine.

Points to remember

  1. Cleansing“A fountain opened … for sin and for uncleanness” 13:1God's cleansing never runs out, and it washes both the guilt and the shame.
  2. Idols“They shall no more be remembered” 13:2When God cleans house, the idols and the lies go out.
  3. Honesty“Neither shall they wear a rough garment to deceive” 13:4Take off the costume. Confessed sin finds mercy.
  4. Christ“Smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered” 13:7Jesus is the Shepherd, the man who is God's equal, struck for us.
  5. Trials“Refine them as silver is refined” 13:9God's fire refines His people. It doesn't destroy them.
  6. Belonging“It is my people” 13:9The fire ends with God saying “mine.”

Question 1 of 6

Cleansing

What did God open for sin and uncleanness?

What verses tell us about this?

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

A fountain, running water that never runs dry. It washes sin (what we did) and uncleanness (what it left on us). The New Testament shows where it flows from: the blood of Jesus, who was pierced for us. Whatever your past, “such were some of you: but ye are washed.”

Question 2 of 6

Idols

What does God do with the idols and the lies?

What verses tell us about this?

“Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen.”

1 John 5:21

Answer

He cuts them off so completely that their names are forgotten, and He drives out the lying prophets and the unclean spirit behind them. An idol is anything we run to instead of God. When He cleans a man up, He doesn't just wash him. He clears out what was ruling him.

Question 3 of 6

Honesty

What happens to the masks we wear?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

“Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another.”

Ephesians 4:25

Answer

They come off. The false prophets dropped the rough garment in shame, and still made up a story about their scars. God offers a better way: take the mask off yourself, confess, and tell the truth to your brothers. Covered sin keeps us sick. Confessed sin finds mercy.

Question 4 of 6

Christ

Who is the Shepherd that God's sword strikes?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then saith Jesus unto them, All ye shall be offended because of me this night: for it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered abroad.”

Matthew 26:31

“But all this was done, that the scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled. Then all the disciples forsook him, and fled.”

Matthew 26:56

Answer

Jesus. He said so Himself on the night He was betrayed, and that night the disciples scattered just as written. God calls Him “the man that is my fellow,” a real man and God's equal. The Father let the sword fall on His Son so that it would not fall on us.

Question 5 of 6

Trials

Why does God bring His people through the fire?

What verses tell us about this?

“That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ:”

1 Peter 1:7

Answer

To refine them, not to destroy them. Heat brings the junk to the surface so it can be taken off, and it drives us to call on His name. Hard seasons in recovery aren't proof God has left. He's a refiner, and He watches the silver the whole time.

Question 6 of 6

Belonging

What does God say over the people He refines?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I will sow her unto me in the earth; and I will have mercy upon her that had not obtained mercy; and I will say to them which were not my people, Thou art my people; and they shall say, Thou art my God.”

Hosea 2:23

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Answer

“It is my people.” God claims them, and they claim Him back: “The LORD is my God.” That promise is spoken over Israel, and it shows God's heart. Everyone who belongs to Jesus is His for good, and no one can pluck them out of His hand.
