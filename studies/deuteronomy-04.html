Deuteronomy 4

“Take heed to thyself”: no image, a consuming fire, and a way back

Moses ends his look back with a charge: keep the word whole, remember Horeb, where they heard a voice and saw no shape, and make no image. If they fall and are scattered, they can still seek the LORD and find him, “For the LORD thy God is a merciful God.” Then Moses sets apart three cities of refuge.

Part one

“That ye may live”

Verses 1–14

Hearken, keep the word whole, and remember what your eyes have seen.

Nothing added, nothing taken away

“Ye shall not add”

More than God said

Rules of our own laid on top of his, until men keep ours and miss his.

“neither shall ye diminish ought”

Less than God said

Cutting out the parts that are hard on us. Diminish means make smaller; ought here means anything at all.

“Every word of God is pure: he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him. Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar.”

Proverbs 30:5–6

The Bible ends with the same warning, in Revelation 22:18–19. A man in recovery knows both ways: adding rules he can't keep, and quietly dropping the verse that convicts him. Take the word whole.

Baal-peor: two kinds of men

“all the men that followed Baal-peor”

Destroyed

We saw it in Numbers 25: the feast, the idol, the women of Moab, and the plague.

“ye that did cleave unto the LORD”

“alive every one of you this day”

Cleave: stick fast, hold on. The men in front of Moses were the ones who held on.

Same camp, same valley, same temptation. The difference was who a man held on to.

“Who, when he came, and had seen the grace of God, was glad, and exhorted them all, that with purpose of heart they would cleave unto the Lord.”

Acts 11:23

“God so nigh”

Moses asks two questions, and they have one answer. What nation has a god so nigh, so near, that he hears whenever they call? What nation has laws so righteous? None. The nations would watch Israel live, and say, “Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people.”

“Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.”

James 4:8

Statutes are God's standing laws; judgments are his rulings for the cases men bring. The men at your work are watching too. A changed life, kept day after day, is the wisdom they see before they ever hear you say a verse.

“Lest thou forget”

Take heed to thyself: watch yourself. Keep thy soul diligently: guard your life with care, every day. A man does not drift into remembering; he drifts into forgetting.

Some of us lost the years when our sons were small. Moses gives a man the grandsons too. It is not too late to tell what God has done for you to the boys who are still listening.

“Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits:”

Psalm 103:2

A voice, and no shape

We stood at the mountain in Exodus 19 and 20. Moses now draws out one thing about that day: they heard, and they did not see. “Ye heard the voice of the words, but saw no similitude”: no likeness, no shape. “Only ye heard a voice.”

God gave Israel words, ten commandments written on two tables of stone, not a picture to bow to. He still comes to men that way:

“So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

Romans 10:17

“No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.”

John 1:18

Part two

“Take ye therefore good heed”

Verses 15–31

No image of any kind; a God who is a consuming fire; and a road home from the far country.

Every likeness, every kind

Deuteronomy 4:16–19Romans 1:23 man“male or female” (16)“corruptible man” the earth“any beast that is on the earth” (17)“fourfooted beasts” the air“any winged fowl” (17)“birds” the ground“any thing that creepeth” (18)“creeping things” the waters“any fish” (18)— the sky“the sun, and the moon, and the stars” (19)—

Moses shuts every door: man, beast, bird, creeping thing, fish, and the sky itself. And Paul, describing what men do when they turn from God, names the same kinds:

“Professing themselves to be wise, they became fools, And changed the glory of the uncorruptible God into an image made like to corruptible man, and to birds, and fourfooted beasts, and creeping things.”

Romans 1:22–23

Out of the iron furnace

Egypt was an iron furnace: the hottest fire men had, the kind that melts iron. That was the bondage. And God did not just let them out; he “hath taken you… to be unto him a people of inheritance”.

Why bow to a calf, or to the sun, when the living God reached into the furnace for you? Every image is a step back toward it.

Most of us know our own furnace. The images now have other shapes: a bottle, a pipe, a screen, money, a woman, a man's own name. Anything a man serves in God's place is a graven image to him.

“Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen.”

1 John 5:21

“I must die in this land”

For the third time Moses tells them, “the LORD was angry with me for your sakes” (1:37; 4:21), “wroth with me for your sakes” (3:26). He puts his own loss in the middle of his warning: I must not go over; ye shall go over. Don't do what cost me this.

That is how an older man in the faith helps a younger one. Not by pretending he never fell, but by telling him straight what it cost.

“the LORD thy God is a consuming fire, even a jealous God.”

We saw the jealous God in Exodus 20: jealous as a husband is for his wife, not envious of anyone. A consuming fire burns up what is not true. The fire on the mountain was not for show.

And the New Testament says it again, to Christians:

“Wherefore we receiving a kingdom which cannot be moved, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear: For our God is a consuming fire.”

Hebrews 12:28–29

Grace does not make God smaller. It brings us near the fire safely, through Christ, and it makes us serve him with reverence.

Gods that cannot hear

Moses tells them ahead of time: in the land, after years of ease, you will make images, and you will be scattered, and among the nations you will serve “wood and stone, which neither see, nor hear, nor eat, nor smell.” It came to pass: “the LORD was very angry with Israel, and removed them out of his sight” (2 Kings 17:18).

“Their idols are silver and gold, the work of men’s hands. They have mouths, but they speak not: eyes have they, but they see not: They have ears, but they hear not: noses have they, but they smell not: They have hands, but they handle not: feet have they, but they walk not: neither speak they through their throat. They that make them are like unto them; so is every one that trusteth in them.”

Psalm 115:4–8

The thing you ran to never heard you once. And a man becomes like what he serves: numb.

“From thence”

Moses saw the fall coming, and in the same breath he gave the way back. From thence, from the far country, among the idols, in tribulation, which is trouble that presses a man down, “if thou seek him with all thy heart… thou shalt find him.”

Moses warns twice, “lest thou forget” and “lest ye forget” (verses 9, 23). And once he promises the other side: the LORD will not “forget the covenant of thy fathers” (verse 31).

“And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart.”

Jeremiah 29:13

A merciful God, and a long way off

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

There is no country so far that God stops being “a merciful God”. If you are in that place this morning, among the dead gods, turn and seek him with all your heart. And don't make the walk back alone: call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor, today.

Part three

“There is none else”

Verses 32–40

Ask all of history: has anything like this ever happened?

Ask the days that are past

“Did ever people hear the voice of God speaking out of the midst of the fire… and live?”NeverOnly Israel, at Horeb.
“Or hath God assayed to go and take him a nation from the midst of another nation…?”NeverOnly Israel, out of Egypt, “before your eyes”.

Assayed means tried, set out to do. Temptations here are trials, the tests God brought on Egypt, beside the signs and wonders. Moses says: search every age since God created man, and every corner of the sky. There is nothing like it.

Out of the midst of the fire

The fire runs through this chapter. Here is every verse from 10 to 36, with the ones that speak of it:

10  11×1 12×1 13 14  15×1 16 17 18 19 20 21 22 23  24×1 25 26 27 28 29 30 31 32  33×1 34 35  36×2

Seven times, and four of them say “out of the midst of the fire” (verses 12, 15, 33, 36). The fire shows how holy he is; the voice shows he wanted to be heard. Now he has spoken plainer still:

“God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds;”

Hebrews 1:1–2

Love first, then the commandments

  1. 37he loved“thy fathers”
  2. 37he chose“their seed after them”
  3. 37–38he brought out, and in“with his mighty power”
  4. 39know it“consider it in thine heart”
  5. 40keep“his statutes, and his commandments”

Read the order. God's love comes first; the command to keep comes last. Israel did not earn the rescue by obeying; they obeyed because they had been rescued. Moses will say it plainly: “because the LORD loved you” (7:8).

Jesus was asked which commandment is first, and the scribe who heard his answer said it in this chapter's own terms: “there is one God; and there is none other but he” (Mark 12:32). There is none else. Not the bottle, not the money, not the old life.

Part four

“Then Moses severed three cities”

Verses 41–49

A refuge set apart, and the law about to be set before them.

Refuge, ready before it was needed

Bezerin the wilderness, in the plain countryReuben
Ramothin GileadGad
Golanin BashanManasseh

We learned the law of the cities of refuge in Numbers 35: three on this side, three in Canaan, for a man who killed his neighbour unawares and “hated him not in times past”. Moses could not set apart the three across the river. But these three he could, so he did, at once. Severed means set apart.

He had just finished speaking of a merciful God (verse 31). The next thing he did was set apart a place where a man who had killed unawares could run “and live”. That is the order of grace: the refuge is there before the man needs it. Christ was ready before you ever ran to him: “we… have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us” (Hebrews 6:18).

“This is the law”

  1. 1–4Looking backHoreb to the plains of Moab
  2. 5–26The law given againbeginning in chapter 5
  3. 27–30Blessing and curse“choose life”
  4. 31–34Moses' last daysJoshua, the song, the blessing

Verses 44–49 are the heading for what comes next. Testimonies are what God bears witness that he requires; statutes his standing laws; judgments his rulings. Moses names where he spoke: east of Jordan, in the valley over against Beth-peor, in the land of the two kings Israel had beaten.

Mount Sion here is Hermon, as the verse says; we met its other names in chapter 3. The springs of Pisgah are the same place 3:17 calls Ashdoth-pisgah.

Real world: keep thy soul

  • SinEvery image is a god that “neither see, nor hear”. Name yours honestly; you know what you bow to.
  • RegretMoses told them what his sin cost him. Tell a younger man what yours cost you.
  • GraceHe loved first, chose, and brought out of the furnace, before any command.
  • Hope“From thence”: from the far country, seek him with all your heart and you will find him. He is “a merciful God”.
  • Today“Take heed to thyself.” Write down one thing your eyes have seen God do, and tell it to a son, a grandson or a younger brother.

Points to remember

  1. The word“Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it” 4:2Take God's word whole: nothing added, nothing cut out.
  2. Take heed“lest thou forget the things which thine eyes have seen… but teach them thy sons, and thy sons’ sons” 4:9Guard your memory of what God has done, and pass it on.
  3. No similitude“ye heard the voice of the words, but saw no similitude; only ye heard a voice” 4:12God gave words, not a shape, so they must make no image.
  4. A consuming fire“For the LORD thy God is a consuming fire, even a jealous God” 4:24Holy and jealous for his people; Hebrews says it to us too.
  5. Seek him“if thou seek him with all thy heart and with all thy soul” 4:29Even from the far country, the man who seeks will find a merciful God.
  6. None else“the LORD he is God in heaven above, and upon the earth beneath: there is none else” 4:39There is no other God, and he loved them first.

Question 1 of 6

The word

What did Moses say they must not do to the word he commanded?

What verses tell us about this?

“Every word of God is pure: he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him. Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar.”

Proverbs 30:5–6

Answer

They must not add to it or take anything away from it, but keep the commandments of the LORD as he gave them.

Question 2 of 6

Take heed

What did Moses warn them not to forget, and whom were they to teach?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The things their eyes had seen, above all the day at Horeb, and the covenant of the LORD. They were to teach them to their sons and their sons' sons.

Question 3 of 6

No similitude

What did Israel hear at Horeb, and what did they not see?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They heard the voice of the LORD speaking out of the fire, but saw no likeness of any kind, and so they must make no graven image.

Question 4 of 6

A consuming fire

What does Moses say the LORD thy God is?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore we receiving a kingdom which cannot be moved, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear: For our God is a consuming fire.”

Hebrews 12:28–29

Answer

A consuming fire, even a jealous God. Hebrews says the same of our God, so we serve him with reverence and godly fear.

Question 5 of 6

Seek him

What did Moses promise the scattered man who seeks the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That he would find him, if he sought him with all his heart and soul, because the LORD is a merciful God who will not forsake him or forget the covenant of his fathers.

Question 6 of 6

None else

What were they to know and consider in their heart?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That the LORD he is God in heaven above and on the earth beneath, and there is none else. He loved their fathers and brought them out, so they should keep his commandments.
