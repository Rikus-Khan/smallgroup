Numbers 35

Forty and eight cities, and six for refuge

The Levites get cities among every tribe, and six of them become a place to run to. A man who killed without meaning to is safe there, until the high priest dies. “That the manslayer die not, until he stand before the congregation in judgment.”

Part one

“Cities to dwell in”

Verses 1–8

The tribe with no land of its own is given homes among all the others.

A city, and its suburbs

  • suburbsnot what we mean by the word. The open ground round a city, for the Levites' cattle and goods (verse 3)
  • a thousand cubitsoutward from the wall (verse 4), about fifteen hundred feet
  • two thousand cubitsmeasured on the east, the south, the west and the north (verse 5), the city in the midst

A cubit is about eighteen inches. The Levites had no field to plough (Numbers 18:20, 24), so what they were given was a house and room for their flocks.

Forty and eight, and six of them for refuge

6cities for refuge
+ 42“forty and two cities” besides
48cities for the Levites (verse 7)

Twelve tribes gave the forty and eight, “from them that have many ye shall give many; but from them that have few ye shall give few” (verse 8): about four from each tribe on average, each giving by its own share. The six for refuge were not extra cities: they were among the Levites' forty and eight (verse 6), so the man running for his life would run to a Levite's door.

Think what that put in every corner of the land: a city of men whose whole life was the worship of the LORD, and whose gates stood open to a man in trouble.

Part two

“Cities of refuge”

Verses 9–15

Where a man could run, and who was let in.

Old words: slayer, manslayer, avenger

  • slayer, manslayera man who has killed someone. The word does not say yet whether he meant to
  • at unawareswithout knowing, without meaning it
  • avenger, revenger of bloodthe dead man's nearest kinsman, whose duty was to pursue the one who killed him (verses 12, 19, 21)
  • stand before the congregation in judgmentbe tried by the people, so the matter is settled by judgment, not by a man's own hot anger

The refuge did not decide a man was innocent. It gave him time: a place to be safe until he could be heard (verse 12).

Three on this side, three on that

  1. West of Jordan (Joshua 20:7)
  2. Kedeshin Galilee, in mount Naphtali
  3. Shechemin mount Ephraim
  4. Kirjath-arbawhich is Hebron, in the mountain of Judah
  5. East of Jordan, “this side” (Joshua 20:8)
  6. Bezerin the wilderness, of the tribe of Reuben
  7. Ramothin Gilead, of Gad
  8. Golanin Bashan, of Manasseh

Close enough to reach

Chapter 35 names no city: Joshua 20 does, once the land was won. The text puts three on each side of the Jordan, so that wherever a man lived, a city of refuge was within reach. Moses says why that mattered:

“Lest the avenger of the blood pursue the slayer, while his heart is hot, and overtake him, because the way is long, and slay him; whereas he was not worthy of death, inasmuch as he hated him not in time past.”

Deuteronomy 19:6

And one thing more: verse 15 says it was “both for the children of Israel, and for the stranger, and for the sojourner among them”. The man who was born in the camp and the man who had only just come in were let in the same gate. A sojourner is one who lives somewhere as a stranger.

Fled for refuge

“That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation, who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us:”

Hebrews 6:18

The writer of Hebrews takes the words of this chapter and says them of every believer: fled for refuge. A man in a city of refuge was running from something that would have killed him. The picture is plain, and the New Testament gives us the words: we are men who ran.

Nobody pointed to his own good record at the gate. He ran in, and the elders took him in (Joshua 20:4). The only question was whether he had run.

Part three

“A murderer”, or a slayer

Verses 16–24

The difference is in the heart, and the congregation must judge it.

Murderer, or slayer?

A murderer (verses 16–21)

put to death

He struck with iron, with a stone or with a weapon of wood so that the man died. He “thrust him of hatred”, or hurled “by laying of wait”, or struck “in enmity”. Laying of wait is lying in ambush, and enmity is hatred.

A slayer (verses 22–25)

sent to the city of refuge

He pushed “suddenly without enmity”, or cast a stone “seeing him not”, one “that was not his enemy, neither sought his harm”. The congregation judges between him and the revenger of blood, and restores him to his refuge.

The law counts what was in the heart: hatred, the ambush, the plan. Jesus goes to the same root: “whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment” (Matthew 5:22). Murder starts long before the blow.

An axe head that slips

Moses gives the picture himself, a man at honest work:

“As when a man goeth into the wood with his neighbour to hew wood, and his hand fetcheth a stroke with the axe to cut down the tree, and the head slippeth from the helve, and lighteth upon his neighbour, that he die; he shall flee unto one of those cities, and live:”

Deuteronomy 19:5

The helve is the handle. The man is not a murderer, and a man is dead. The law does not pretend the death does not matter, and it does not treat an accident as a crime. It gives him a place to live, and it makes him stay there. Some of the men in this room carry a death or a grave harm on their hands: a crash, a fight, a thing done in a haze. Not all of it was an axe head slipping, and we will not pretend it was. But the dead stay dead, and the LORD still made a way for the man who runs to him: “Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief” (1 Timothy 1:15).

Part four

“Until the death of the high priest”

Verses 25–34

Stay inside, and one day go home; no price buys a life.

The way home

  1. Fleesto a city of refuge35:11
  2. Is judgedby the congregation35:24
  3. Abidessafe inside the city35:25
  4. The high priest dies“anointed with the holy oil”35:25
  5. Goes home“the land of his possession”35:28

The refuge had one rule: stay inside. If the slayer left the border and the revenger found him outside, the revenger “shall not be guilty of blood” (verse 27). Inside, he was safe; outside, he was on his own.

A man in recovery knows what it is to stay in the safe place, and what comes of wandering out of it again, to the old corner, the old crowd, the old road. Stay close to the refuge, Christ himself, and to what he gives to keep you there: the Word, the brothers in Christ, the pastor, the church.

Witnesses, and no ransom

  • statutea law that stands “throughout your generations in all your dwellings” (verse 29)
  • by the mouth of witnessesnever by one witness alone, so that no man dies on one man's word (verse 30)
  • satisfactiona payment to settle the matter. A murderer could not pay his way out of it (verse 31)
  • and the slayer could not pay his way homeno payment shortened his stay in the city; only “the death of the priest” ended it (verse 32)

Both ways, no money bought a life, or a release. The judgment was not for sale. This is civil law for Israel's courts: it is the refuge that tells us something about our own case.

Blood defiles the land, and the LORD dwells in it

Blood shed“it defileth the land”
→
Because“I the LORD dwell among the children of Israel”

That is why verse 34 ends as it does. The land is not just ground: the LORD lives there, and the tabernacle stands in the middle of the camp. The God who lives among them cannot have innocent blood crying from the ground, and he does not let a man quietly forget it.

Defile means to make unclean. Blood is serious to the LORD, and the answer to it is blood (Leviticus 17:11); for a man who has run to the right place, it has already been answered.

The high priest who lives

“Which hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast, and which entereth into that within the veil; Whither the forerunner is for us entered, even Jesus, made an high priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec.”

Hebrews 6:19–20

Read the verses straight after “fled for refuge” (Hebrews 6:18): the writer goes at once to a high priest. Israel's priests died, one after another (Hebrews 7:23); ours “continueth ever” (7:24), and entered “by his own blood” (9:12).

Hebrews does not lay the cities of refuge over Jesus line by line, so we will not. It does say that “he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them” (7:25). The man who has fled to Christ has this: “There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus” (Romans 8:1).

Real world: run, and stay

  • SinSome of what we did was done in hatred, and some without meaning it. Chapter 35 tells the two apart, and the LORD knows the heart, even when we do not.
  • RegretThe revenger of blood was real: the old consequences, the voice that says you will never be free of it. The law did not tell the slayer to be unafraid. It gave him a door.
  • GraceThe city was open to Israelite, stranger and sojourner alike, and nobody asked for a clean record at the gate. The man who has fled to Christ has a high priest who lives, and who has already entered in for him.
  • HopeThe slayer went home: “after the death of the high priest the slayer shall return into the land of his possession.” Today is not the end of your story.
  • TodayStay inside the city. Pick the one place you must not wander back to today, and tell a brother in Christ you will stay away from it. If the weight of it is more than you can carry, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor, today, not later.

  2. The refuge“they shall be unto you cities for refuge from the avenger” 35:12A place to run to, and to be safe in until the matter was judged.
  3. Who could flee“both for the children of Israel, and for the stranger, and for the sojourner among them” 35:15Open to everyone; the only question was whether he ran.
  4. The murderer“if he thrust him of hatred, or hurl at him by laying of wait” 35:20Hatred and the ambush made a murderer; a death without enmity sent a man to the refuge.

Question 1 of 6

The Levites' cities

How many cities did the Levites receive, and what lay round about them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Forty and eight cities, with their suburbs: open ground round each for their cattle, their goods and all their beasts. Six of the forty and eight were cities of refuge.

Question 2 of 6

The refuge

What were the cities of refuge for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For the man who killed someone without meaning to. He could flee there from the avenger of blood, and not die until the congregation had judged him.

Question 3 of 6

Who could flee

Who was allowed to flee to a city of refuge?

What verses tell us about this?

“That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation, who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us:”

Hebrews 6:18

Answer

Every one that killed any person unawares: the Israelite, the stranger and the sojourner. In Hebrews, the believers are those who “have fled for refuge”.

Question 4 of 6

The murderer

What made a man who killed a murderer, and not a manslayer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Hatred, enmity and laying of wait made him a murderer, and he was put to death. A blow struck suddenly, without enmity and without laying of wait, made him a slayer, who could flee to the refuge.

Question 5 of 6

The high priest

How long must the slayer abide in his city of refuge?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Until the death of the high priest. Then he went home to the land of his possession. Out of the city, the revenger of blood was not guilty if he found him.

Question 6 of 6

The land

Why was the land not to be defiled?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because blood defiles the land, and the LORD dwells in it, among the children of Israel.
