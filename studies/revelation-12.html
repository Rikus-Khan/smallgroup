Revelation 12

Overcome by the blood

The curtain pulls back on the war behind every other war. A woman, a dragon and a child; Christmas seen from heaven's side. Then the accuser is thrown out of heaven, and ordinary believers beat him with two weapons: the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony.

The seventh trumpet has just sounded: “The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord” (11:15). Before the last judgments pour out, John is shown why the world is at war with God and with His people.

Chapters 12 to 14 are the story behind the story. They go back to Bethlehem, and behind that to Eden. Every war in Revelation, and every war inside a believer, is part of one old war between the serpent and the woman's Seed.

Revelation tells us how to read it: these are “wonders” (12:1, 3), that is, signs. Pictures that point to real things. Scripture itself tells us what most of them mean, so we'll let it.

Part one

Two wonders

Verses 1–6

A woman about to give birth, and a dragon waiting to devour her child.

We've seen this picture before

Jacob's son Joseph once dreamed it:

“And he dreamed yet another dream, and told it his brethren, and said, Behold, I have dreamed a dream more; and, behold, the sun and the moon and the eleven stars made obeisance to me.”

Genesis 37:9

Sun, moon and stars were Jacob, his wife and their twelve sons: Israel. Teachers differ on the woman: many say Israel, the nation Christ came from; others, all God's people. Either way, she is God's people, and God protects her.

The dragon, named

We don't have to guess who he is. Verse 9 says it plainly: “that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan.”

Red: “a murderer from the beginning” (John 8:44). Crowns: he claims to rule. Horns mean power, like Daniel's ten-horned beast (Daniel 7:7). The fallen stars, many teachers say, are the angels who fell with him (v. 9).

What does he want? To “devour her child.” His oldest goal is to destroy Christ, and all that belongs to Him.

Christmas, from heaven's side

The child is Christ, plainly: the King of Psalm 2.

“Thou shalt break them with a rod of iron; thou shalt dash them in pieces like a potter’s vessel.”

Psalm 2:9

On earth, a quiet night with shepherds. In heaven, war: the dragon used Herod to kill Bethlehem's baby boys (Matthew 2:16).

Verse 5 jumps from manger to throne in one breath: life, cross, empty tomb, and He was “caught up unto God” (Acts 1:9). The dragon lost.

The oldest war in the world

  1. Eden“it shall bruise thy head”Genesis 3:15
  2. BethlehemHerod's soldiersMatthew 2:16
  3. The crossa bruised heel, a crushed headJohn 12:31
  4. Caught upto God's throne12:5; Acts 1:9
  5. Cast outthe accuser thrown down12:9–10
  6. The endthe lake of fire20:10

“And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.”

Genesis 3:15

The first promise of a Saviour, given the day man fell. The serpent would wound the woman's Seed, but the Seed would crush his head. Revelation 12 is that promise playing out.

Verse 6: the woman flees to “a place prepared of God” for 1,260 days, the same three and a half years as chapter 11. God feeds her there, as He fed Israel with manna and Elijah by the brook.

Part two

War in heaven

Verses 7–12

The accuser loses his place, and ordinary believers beat him.

Five names, one enemy

  1. The great dragonfierce, and out to devour
  2. That old serpentthe same one from Eden (Genesis 3)
  3. The DevilGreek diabolos: the slanderer
  4. SatanHebrew for the adversary, the accuser
  5. The deceiverhe “deceiveth the whole world”

Notice who beats him: Michael, an angel. Daniel calls him “the great prince which standeth for the children of thy people” (Daniel 12:1). Satan is not God's equal opposite. He is a creature, and one of God's servants throws him out. “Prevailed not”: that is his story.

Jesus said it too: “I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven” (Luke 10:18). Whatever he whispers to you, remember he is the one who lost.

The accuser of our brethren

Satan means accuser, and he works “day and night.” Some of it is even true. But every charge against a believer is paid by “the blood of the Lamb,” and heaven threw the prosecutor out.

Then the brother speaks, “the word of their testimony.” He doesn't argue he's not so bad. He tells what Christ did.

The voice at three in the morning

Some of you know this voice. It lists your failures in the middle of the night. It sounds like your own mind, and it never runs out of material. Zechariah saw it in a vision of Joshua, the high priest:

“And he shewed me Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the LORD, and Satan standing at his right hand to resist him.”

Zechariah 3:1

“And he answered and spake unto those that stood before him, saying, Take away the filthy garments from him. And unto him he said, Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment.”

Zechariah 3:4

Joshua's clothes really were filthy. God didn't deny it. He took them off him and dressed him clean. When the accuser reads your list, don't argue the facts. Point to the blood: “There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus” (Romans 8:1).

What He did

The blood of the Lamb

The answer to every charge. Not your good week, not your promise to do better. Jesus is our “advocate with the Father” (1 John 2:1), and His blood has already paid.

What you say

The word of their testimony

Saying out loud what Christ has done for you. Secrets give the accuser power; a story told in the light takes it away. This is why telling your story in this group matters.

“Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God’s elect? It is God that justifieth.”

Romans 8:33

“They loved not their lives unto the death”: they held nothing back from Christ, not even their lives. The devil can't buy a man who has already given himself away.

Your testimony has three parts

BeforeWhat I was

Honest, short, no bragging about the old days. One or two sentences.

“such were some of you”

The turnHow Christ found me

When and how you trusted Him. Paul told his own story this way (Acts 26).

SinceWhat He's doing now

Not that you're perfect. What He changed, and what He's still working on.

“all things are become new”

“Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee.”

Mark 5:19

The rage of a loser on a clock

Heaven rejoices; earth gets a warning. The devil has “great wrath,” and the reason is good news: “he hath but a short time.”

A cornered animal fights hardest. If the fight feels fierce right now, that's not a sign you're losing. It's a sign he is. “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you” (James 4:7).

And his end is already written: “the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly” (Romans 16:20). Under your feet.

Part three

On eagles' wings

Verses 13–17

The dragon can't reach the child, so he goes after the child's family. God carries them.

Carried, fed and kept

God used the same words when He brought Israel out of slavery:

“Ye have seen what I did unto the Egyptians, and how I bare you on eagles’ wings, and brought you unto myself.”

Exodus 19:4

The serpent sends a flood to sweep her away, and God makes the ground itself swallow it, as it once swallowed Pharaoh's army (Exodus 15:12). “A time, and times, and half a time” is the same 1,260 days again: a set time, and she is nourished all through it.

Why the fight feels personal

The dragon couldn't touch the Child, who is on the throne. He couldn't drown the woman. So he goes after her other children: people who keep God's commandments and “have the testimony of Jesus Christ.” That is you.

If following Jesus has made your life feel more like a war, not less, this verse explains it. You are not imagining it. You are now on the side the dragon hates.

But look at the whole chapter: he loses to the Child, loses in heaven, loses to the woman, and is beaten by believers with a story and the blood. He is “wroth”, but he is not winning.

Today, under the blood

  1. When the accuser speaksDon't argue the facts. Confess the sin to God (1 John 1:9), then point to the blood: it is paid.
  2. Tell your storyBefore, the turn, since. Tell it to one person today, even if it's only three sentences.
  3. Remember the clockHis rage is loud because his time is short. Christ is on the throne, and you are carried.

Regret is real, and the accuser will use it. But the man who has been forgiven much has the best story in the room. Today, tell somebody what the Lord has done for you.

Points to remember

  1. The woman“a woman clothed with the sun … a crown of twelve stars” 12:1Joseph's dream explains the picture: God's people, from whom Christ came. Teachers differ on the details; God protects her.
  2. The dragon“that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan” 12:9Scripture names him. He wants to devour Christ and everything that belongs to Him.
  3. The child“her child was caught up unto God, and to his throne” 12:5The child is Christ, the King of Psalm 2. Christmas was war, and the dragon lost.
  4. War in heaven“And prevailed not” 12:8Michael and his angels won. Satan is a creature, cast out, not God's equal.
  5. The accuser“they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony” 12:11The blood answers every charge. Your story, told out loud, takes away his power.
  6. A short time“he knoweth that he hath but a short time” 12:12His rage is the rage of a loser on a clock. God carries and feeds His people through it.

Answer

Question 2 of 6

The dragon

Who is the great red dragon, and what does he want?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The Bible names him: the old serpent of Eden, the Devil, Satan, the deceiver. His crowns claim to rule and his horns show his power. He wants to destroy Christ, and when he can't, he goes after Christ's people.

Question 3 of 6

The child

Who is the man child caught up to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou shalt break them with a rod of iron; thou shalt dash them in pieces like a potter’s vessel.”

Psalm 2:9

Answer

Jesus Christ, the King of Psalm 2 who rules the nations with a rod of iron. The dragon tried to kill Him at His birth through Herod, but He lived, died, rose and was caught up to God's throne. It is Christmas told from heaven's side.

Question 4 of 6

War in heaven

Who won the war in heaven?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto them, I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven.”

Luke 10:18

Answer

Michael and God's angels. The dragon “prevailed not” and was thrown out with his angels. Satan is not God's equal; he is a creature, beaten by one of God's servants. Whatever he whispers, he is the one who lost.

Question 5 of 6

The accuser

How do believers overcome the accuser?

What verses tell us about this?

“My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:”

1 John 2:1

Answer

By the blood of the Lamb, which has paid every charge, and by the word of their testimony: telling out loud what Christ has done. Don't argue the facts with the accuser. Confess, point to the blood, and tell your story.

Question 6 of 6

A short time

Why is the devil so angry, and how does God keep His people?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He has lost in heaven and knows his time is short, so he attacks God's people. But God carries them on eagles' wings, as He carried Israel out of Egypt, feeds them in a place prepared, and makes the earth swallow the flood.
