Matthew 13

Treasure hid in a field

From a boat, Jesus tells seven stories about the kingdom of heaven: seed and soil, wheat and tares, a mustard seed, leaven, a treasure, a pearl, and a net. Then His own town turns Him away.

Part one

A sower went forth to sow

Verses 1–23

A boat for a pulpit, a crowd on the shore, and a story about seed that every farmer knew.

The same day

It had been a long day already. In chapter 12 Jesus healed a man who was blind and dumb, the Pharisees said He did it by the prince of devils (12:24), and His own mother and brethren stood outside asking for Him (12:46).

Now He walks out of the house, most likely in Capernaum, and sits down by the lake. The crowd gets so big that He climbs into a boat and teaches from the water.

This is the first time Matthew uses the word parables. A parable is a story from everyday life that carries a truth about God. It's simple enough for anybody to remember, and it keeps giving to the man who keeps thinking about it.

Seven pictures of one kingdom

Every story today begins the same way: “The kingdom of heaven is like…” The kingdom of heaven is God's rule, where He is King and His word is obeyed. Here are the seven, in order:

  1. The sowerseed on four kinds of ground (vv. 3–23)
  2. Wheat and taresgood and bad grow together (vv. 24–30, 36–43)
  3. The mustard seedtiny, then a tree (vv. 31–32)
  4. The leavenhidden, then everywhere (v. 33)
  5. The treasurefound in a field (v. 44)
  6. The pearlworth everything (vv. 45–46)
  7. The netsorted at the end (vv. 47–50)

The first four He told the crowd from the boat. The last three He told only His disciples, in the house (v. 36). Matthew writes “kingdom of heaven” where Mark and Luke say “kingdom of God.” It's the same kingdom. Jews of the day often said “heaven” out of reverence for God's name.

Same seed, different ground

A farmer walked his field throwing seed by the handful. Footpaths ran through the fields, rock lay just under the soil in places, and thorn roots waited in the ground. Some seed always landed where it couldn't grow.

Why stories?

“unto you”

came and asked

The disciples came close and wanted to know. To them the meaning was given.

“to them”

heard a story and went home

The same words, but no wish to understand them.

A mystery in the Bible isn't a riddle. It's something God kept hidden for a long time and now makes known. The door was open to anyone who would come and ask. The disciples weren't smarter than the crowd. They just came.

Verse 12 is how it works: “whosoever hath, to him shall be given.” Take in what God shows you, and He shows you more. Push it away, and you lose even the little you had.

Eyes they have closed

Esaias is Isaiah. About 700 years earlier God told him that the people would hear him and not take it in (Isaiah 6:9–10). Jesus says it came true again in His own day. Waxed gross means grown fat and dull, like a heart wrapped in fat.

Look at the end of verse 15. What God wanted all along was to heal them. They shut their eyes so they wouldn't have to see, because seeing would mean turning. The parables don't shut anyone out. They leave the door open for whoever will come and ask.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

For two thousand years, God's best men looked ahead to the Christ and died without seeing Him. These fishermen and a tax collector sat in a boat and heard Him tell stories.

You have it better still: the whole story, His cross and His empty tomb, in a book you can open any day. “Blessed are your eyes.”

Fast up, fast down

The way side: the word lands on a heart packed hard and never gets in. The wicked one, the devil, “catcheth away” what was sown. He snatches it, because he knows what it can do.

Anon means right away. Dureth means lasts. By and by here means at once, and offended means tripped up. Most of us have seen this man, or been him: fired up after a meeting or a scare, and gone the first time trouble comes.

Roots grow where nobody sees them: daily in the word, in prayer, with other believers. That's what holds a man up when the sun gets hot.

Understandeth it

Thorns don't kill in a day. “The care of this world” (worry about bills, court, the job, the family) and “the deceitfulness of riches” (money that promises to fix everything) grow up beside the word until there's no room for it.

Matthew's key word in this chapter is understand. Count where it turns up:

The good-ground man isn't a better kind of man. He's the one who lets the word in, thinks it over until it makes sense, and lives by it. Then it bears fruit, and Jesus is glad of thirty as well as a hundred.

Part two

Let both grow together

Verses 24–43

An enemy in the field at night, a seed too small to see, and a pinch of leaven in a big batch of dough.

Tares in the wheat

Tares were most likely darnel, a weed that looks almost exactly like wheat while it's young. Sowing it in a man's field was a real way to ruin a neighbor.

You only tell them apart when the heads come out. Ripe wheat bows under the weight of its grain. Darnel stands up straight, with small dark seed.

By then the roots are wound together. Pull the tares, and you tear up the wheat. So the owner says to wait: “Let both grow together until the harvest.”

Not our job

The servants were eager: “Wilt thou then that we go and gather them up?” The owner said no. They couldn't be trusted to tell wheat from tares, and they'd do more harm than good.

Jesus explains it in verses 36–43: the field is the world. Good and evil grow side by side until the end, and God is patient with it for now. That doesn't mean He doesn't see. “An enemy hath done this.” He knows exactly where the evil came from.

For us it means two things. It isn't our job to rip up every man we think is a fake. Leave the sorting to the Lord of the harvest. And when you find bad men among good ones, even in a church, don't let it shake you. Jesus said it would be this way.

The least of all seeds

sown

a speck

A mustard seed is about the size of a pinhead, the smallest seed a Galilean farmer sowed.

grown

a tree

By the lake it can grow ten feet tall or more, with birds sitting in its branches.

That's the kingdom of heaven. It started with a carpenter from Nazareth and a dozen ordinary men. Today there are believers in nearly every nation on earth.

God works the same way in a man. One honest prayer. One day clean. It looks like nothing. Don't despise it. He grows big things from seeds too small to see.

Hidden, then everywhere

Leaven is yeast, a lump of old dough saved from the last baking. Three measures of meal was a huge batch of flour, enough bread for a crowd. A woman works a little lump into it and covers it up.

a little leaven, hid in the meal“till the whole was leavened”

You can't see it work. You can't hurry it. But it doesn't stop until it has gone through the whole batch. Elsewhere leaven pictures sin spreading (16:6). Here Jesus uses it for His kingdom: quiet, from the inside, and it reaches everything.

The mustard seed grows out where people can see it. The leaven works inside, where nobody sees. That's how He changes a man too: from the inside out, until it shows in everything.

Spoken by the prophet

  1. ✓I will open my mouth in a parable: I will utter dark sayings of old:Psalm 78:213:34–35

Psalm 78 was written by Asaph, one of David's chief singers, who is also called a seer, a prophet (2 Chronicles 29:30). He told Israel's history as a lesson for the next generation. Matthew sees Jesus doing the same thing, only more so.

“Kept secret from the foundation of the world”: God's plan wasn't made up as He went along. It was there from the start, waiting to be told. Jesus is the One who tells it.

The key to the story

the sower→the Son of man
the field→the world
the good seed→the children of the kingdom

The disciples didn't get it, so they asked. That's the right move. Jesus never sends away a man who comes asking.

Notice that the good seed are people, and Jesus plants them in the world on purpose. A believer isn't meant to hide from the world but to grow right where he's been planted, tares and all.

Two endings

“them which do iniquity”

a furnace of fire

“There shall be wailing and gnashing of teeth.” Grief, and anger, that never ends.

“the righteous”

shine forth as the sun

At home “in the kingdom of their Father.”

Nobody in the Bible spoke about hell more plainly than Jesus did, and He did it because He doesn't want anyone to go there. Things that offend are things that trip people into sin. God will clear them all out.

Who are “the righteous”? Not men who were never tares. Every one of us was. It's the man who has believed on Jesus, and He has made him righteous. The door is open to whosoever will come today:

“Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.”

John 5:24

Part three

Treasure, a pearl, and a net

Verses 44–52

Inside the house, just the disciples: what the kingdom is worth, and how it ends.

One stumbled on it, one searched

the treasure (v. 44)

wasn't looking

A working man digging in another man's field hits a pot of coins. With no banks, people buried their valuables, and sometimes the owner died and nobody knew.

the pearl (vv. 45–46)

looked for years

A merchant who dealt in fine pearls, searching all his life for the best. Then he finds the one.

Some men come to Christ like the digger: we weren't looking for God at all, and He found us, in a cell, a hospital, a meeting we got dragged to. Others searched for years through everything else first. Both found the same treasure.

And look at the digger's face: “for joy thereof.” He isn't grieving what he sold. He can't believe what he got.

Worth everything

Before modern pearl farms, a large, perfect pearl was one of the most valuable things in the world. Both men sold all. Neither one was cheated.

This isn't about buying salvation. Nobody can pay for it. “The gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord” (Romans 6:23). It's about what Christ is worth once you see Him. Paul saw it:

“Yea doubtless, and I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord: for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and do count them but dung, that I may win Christ,”

Philippians 3:8

The net

The fishermen in the room knew this one by heart. A dragnet was a long net with floats on top and weights on the bottom. Boats dragged it in a wide sweep and hauled it to the beach, full of everything in its path.

Then the men sat down on the shore and sorted: good fish into baskets, the bad thrown out. The Law called some fish unclean (Leviticus 11:9–12), and some were simply worthless.

The gospel net goes out to everyone and gathers “of every kind.” Nobody is left out of the invitation. But there is a day of sorting, and the angels do it, not us. The time to settle where you stand is now, while the net is still in the water.

Things new and old

Old truth, new light.

A scribe was a man who studied and taught the Old Testament. A householder is the man of the house, with a storeroom he can bring things out of.

A scribe “instructed unto the kingdom” has both. He has the old: Moses, the prophets, the Psalms. And he has the new: Jesus, who fills all of it up. He doesn't throw out the old. He finally understands it.

“Yea, Lord,” they said. They had understood, and now they were to pass it on. That's you too: what you learn here isn't only for you. Somebody you know needs it.

Part four

Is not this the carpenter's son?

Verses 53–58

Jesus goes home to Nazareth, and the people who knew Him best can't get past who they think He is.

Home to Nazareth

His own country is Nazareth, the hill village where He grew up (2:23). Capernaum, His base now, was about a day's walk away.

He teaches in the synagogue, the town's meeting house for prayer and reading the Scriptures every Sabbath. These were the same benches He sat on as a boy.

And they were astonished. Nobody denied the wisdom or the mighty works. They just couldn't figure out where a man like Him got them.

We know him

The townIs not this the carpenter's son?

The townIs not his mother called Mary? and his brethren, James, and Joses, and Simon, and Judas?

The townWhence then hath this man all these things?
they never asked the answer

A carpenter built in wood and stone. It was Joseph's trade, and Jesus learned it. His brothers were Mary and Joseph's sons, born after Him.

They didn't believe in Him yet (John 7:5). But after He rose, there they were, praying with the disciples. James came to lead the church in Jerusalem, and Judas wrote the letter of Jude.

“These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brethren.”

Acts 1:14

Too familiar

“Offended in him”: they tripped over Him. He was too ordinary, too local, too much like them. “A prophet is not without honour, save in his own country”: the people who knew you before are often the slowest to believe you've changed.

His power wasn't too small for Nazareth. They just wouldn't come to Him for it. Unbelief doesn't weaken Christ. It closes the door on Him. And if you're short on faith, bring that to Him too, honestly. He has never turned away a prayer like this one:

Question 1 of 6

Hearing

What does good ground do with the word?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It hears the word and understands it: lets it in, thinks it through, and lives by it. Then it bears fruit, some a hundredfold, some sixty, some thirty. The hard path, the shallow rock, and the thorns all hear too, but the word never takes root or gets choked out.

Question 2 of 6

Wheat and tares

Why does God let the wheat and the tares grow together?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because pulling the tares now would tear up the wheat too. God knows the evil came from the enemy, and He is patient with the world until the harvest. Then His angels will do the sorting. Our job isn't to rip up everyone we think is a fake, but to grow where He planted us.

Question 3 of 6

Small beginnings

How does the kingdom of heaven grow?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It starts small, like the least of all seeds, and grows into a tree. And it works quietly from the inside, like leaven, until the whole batch is changed. That's how it spread through the world, and that's how God changes a man.

Question 4 of 6

Treasure

What is the kingdom of heaven worth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Everything. Both men sold all they had, and gladly. We can't buy salvation, because it's God's free gift. But once a man sees who Christ is, everything else looks small next to Him. Some of us stumbled on Him without looking, and some searched for years. Same treasure.

Question 5 of 6

The end

What happens at the end of the world?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The Son of man sends His angels to gather out everything that offends and everyone who does evil, and there is a furnace of fire. The righteous, those made right with God through faith in Jesus, shine like the sun in their Father's kingdom. The invitation is open to everyone now, before the sorting.

Question 6 of 6

Unbelief

What did unbelief cost Jesus' hometown?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They had the Son of God teaching in their own synagogue, and they missed it. They couldn't get past knowing Him as the carpenter's son, so He did not many mighty works there. Unbelief doesn't make Him weak. It keeps us from coming to Him.
