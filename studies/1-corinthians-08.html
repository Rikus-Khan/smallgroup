What Paul heard
What they wrote to ask: “Now concerning…”

Chapter 7 answered their questions about marriage. Now the next one: “Now as touching things offered unto idols.” Paul takes three chapters to answer it.

Chapter 8: love limits my freedom. Chapter 9: Paul's own example, a man who gave up his rights. Chapter 10: run from idolatry, and do everything to the glory of God.

Where Corinth's meat came from

Corinth was a city of temples, and most meat started at an altar.

Part was burned. Part was eaten at a feast: archaeologists have found dining rooms at Corinth's temples, where a wedding or a birthday might be held.

The rest was sold in the meat market. A believer could buy a roast and never know where it had been.

Part one

Charity edifieth

Verses 1–3

Being right isn't the same as being loving.

Air, or bricks

“We all have knowledge” was probably the strong Corinthians' own line. We know idols aren't real. So we can eat whatever we like.

Paul doesn't say they're wrong about idols. He says knowledge alone swells a man up like a balloon: big, and full of air. “Charity”, love, “edifieth”: it builds, one brick at a time, like a house.

You can win the argument and lose the brother.

Known of him

The man who thinks he has it all figured out “knoweth nothing yet as he ought to know.” Real knowledge always comes with humility.

Then Paul turns it around. The thing that matters most isn't what you know about God. It's that God knows you.

“But now, after that ye have known God, or rather are known of God, how turn ye again to the weak and beggarly elements, whereunto ye desire again to be in bondage?”

Galatians 4:9

Some of us were never good at school. You don't have to be. If you love God, He knows you by name. That's the knowledge that counts.

Part two

But to us there is but one God

Verses 4–6

A city full of gods, and only one is real.

Gods many

  • Apollohis temple stood in the middle of Corinth
  • Aphroditegoddess of love, on the hill above the city
  • Asclepiusthe god of healing, with his dining rooms
  • Poseidongod of the sea, honoured at the games near Corinth
  • one God, the Father … and one Lord Jesus Christof whom, and by whom, are all things

Devout Jews prayed it every day: “Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD” (Deuteronomy 6:4). Paul says the same, and puts Jesus right inside it. The Lord who made all things is the Lord who died for us.

An idol is nothing

A statue can't hear, help, or hurt you. It's wood and stone. The power an idol has over a man is the power he hands it.

We had idols too. Not on a hill: in a bottle, a pipe, a bag, a screen, a bank account. Things we gave our whole lives to, that gave nothing back.

But nothing doesn't mean harmless. Two chapters on, Paul says devils stand behind the idols (10:20), and he says plainly:

“Wherefore, my dearly beloved, flee from idolatry.”

1 Corinthians 10:14

Part three

For whom Christ died

Verses 7–13

What's harmless to you may be deadly to him.

Unto this hour

The strong man sees

a plate of meat

Just dinner. The idol is nothing. He eats and gives thanks.

The weak man sees

the old altar

He worshipped there for years. The smell, the place, the people pull him straight back to the old life.

A “weak” conscience here isn't a bad man. It's a new believer whose past is still close. We know this one. A smell, a song, a street corner, a certain kind of night: “unto this hour” it can bring the old life right back.

Meat commendeth us not

“Commendeth” means brings us closer, wins us credit. Eating doesn't make you more spiritual. Not eating doesn't either.

On the scale before God, the plate weighs nothing. What we stand on with Him is Christ alone.

So the strong man is right. But being right doesn't settle it, as the next verse shows.

Emboldened

A strong believer goes to a dinner in a temple's dining room. He knows the idol is nothing. He's fine.

But a new believer sees him through the door, and follows him in. For him it's not dinner. It's worship of the old god, and his conscience is wounded.

“Emboldened” is the same Greek word as “edifieth” in verse 1: built up. Your freedom built him up, right into his fall.

This liberty of yours

Maybe you can sit in a bar with a soda and be fine. Maybe you can watch that show, or drive down that street, and it doesn't touch you.

But the man two weeks out of rehab who sees you there doesn't see a soda. He sees permission. “If he can, I can.”

“For, brethren, ye have been called unto liberty; only use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by love serve one another.”

Galatians 5:13

Freedom in Christ is real. It was given to serve with, not to show off.

Against Christ

To “perish” here is to be ruined: a brother wrecked, pulled back under. And Paul calls him “the weak brother … for whom Christ died.” Jesus counted him worth His blood. Is he worth a plate of meat to you?

Wound a brother and you wound Christ. Paul learned that on the road to Damascus, when he was hurting believers:

“And he fell to the earth, and heard a voice saying unto him, Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?”

Acts 9:4

Jesus takes it personally when His people are hurt. That's how close He is to them.

While the world standeth

Ifmeat make my brother to offend,
→
thenno meat for me, ever.

For us the same rule reaches further. Put anything in the place of the meat:

  1. a drink at dinnerwith a man in recovery
  2. the beer in your fridgewhen he comes over
  3. a bar you can handlethat he can't
  4. a joke, a show, a songthat drags him back

Paul isn't being forced. He chooses it, gladly, for a brother. That's what love does.

Both sides of the table

Some of us have been the weak brother. Somebody's example, or somebody's offer, and we went down. Hear verse 11: you are a brother “for whom Christ died.” He hasn't given up on you. Get up and come back.

Some of us have been the stumblingblock. We brought it to the party. We said, “One won't hurt.” That's sin against Christ, and it's forgiven the same way as any other:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Then, if you can, go to that brother and make it right. Today, look at your own liberty: is there one thing you should set down for someone else's sake?

Points to remember

  1. Knowledge“Knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth” 8:1Being right swells a man up. Love builds his brother up.
  2. One God“An idol is nothing in the world” 8:4There is one God, the Father, and one Lord Jesus Christ. Every idol is empty, so run from it.
  3. Conscience“With conscience of the idol unto this hour” 8:7A new believer's past is still close. What's nothing to you can pull him back.
  4. Food and God“Meat commendeth us not to God” 8:8What's on the plate doesn't make us better or worse. We stand on Christ alone.
  5. Stumbling“Take heed lest … this liberty of yours become a stumblingblock” 8:9Your freedom can build a brother up, right into a fall. Watch who's watching.
  6. Love“I will eat no flesh while the world standeth” 8:13Love gladly sets down the drink, or anything else, that trips a brother.

Question 1 of 6

Knowledge

What's the difference between knowledge that puffs up and love that builds up?

What verses tell us about this?

“Charity suffereth long, and is kind; charity envieth not; charity vaunteth not itself, is not puffed up,”

1 Corinthians 13:4

Answer

Knowledge alone swells a man with pride; he's right, and he lets everyone know it. Love builds others up, brick by brick. And what matters most isn't what I know about God, but that God knows me.

Question 2 of 6

One God

What do believers know about idols?

What verses tell us about this?

“Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen.”

1 John 5:21

Answer

An idol is nothing: there is one God, the Father, and one Lord Jesus Christ, who made all things. Empty as they are, idols still enslave the men who serve them, so keep away from them, whatever shape they take.

Question 3 of 6

Conscience

What is a weak conscience?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It's the conscience of a believer whose old life is still close. Something harmless in itself still means the old idol to him. If he goes against it, his conscience is wounded and he can be pulled back under.

Question 4 of 6

Food and God

Does what I eat make me better or worse with God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God.”

1 Corinthians 10:31

Answer

No. Food doesn't bring us closer to God, and skipping it doesn't either. We stand before Him on Christ alone. So eat with thanks, and do it all to His glory.

Question 5 of 6

Stumbling

How can my freedom hurt a brother?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He sees me do it and follows, against his own conscience, into what ruins him. Christ died for that brother. When I wound him, I sin against Christ.

Question 6 of 6

Love

What will love give up for a brother?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is good neither to eat flesh, nor to drink wine, nor any thing whereby thy brother stumbleth, or is offended, or is made weak.”

Romans 14:21

“For, brethren, ye have been called unto liberty; only use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by love serve one another.”

Galatians 5:13

Answer

Anything. Meat, drink, a place, a habit: whatever trips him. Paul would rather never eat meat again than make a brother fall. Love doesn't ask what I'm allowed. It asks what will help him.
