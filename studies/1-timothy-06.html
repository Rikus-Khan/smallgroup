Paul wrote to Timothy, about AD 62 to 64, to steady a church at Ephesus with false teachers in it. Chapter 4 was about Timothy himself. Chapter 5 was about the people around him. Chapter 6 deals with work and money, and then gives the man of God a last charge.

It's the last chapter of the letter. At the end of the study we'll look back over all six.

Part one

On the job

Verses 1–5

Your work is the first thing the world reads about Jesus.

The name on your shirt

The “servants under the yoke” were slaves, and many Christians in Ephesus were. Paul isn't approving slavery. He is telling believers how to live as Christ's people inside a world they couldn't change overnight, and the gospel was already wearing slavery down.

His reason is the point: “that the name of God and his doctrine be not blasphemed.” The men you work with can't see your faith. They see whether you're on time, honest, and doing the job.

Most of us don't have a master, but we have a boss. Today: work as if His name is stitched on your shirt. It is.

If your boss is a believer

Here's a trap, and it's a real one: “He's a brother, so he'll go easy on me.” Paul closes the door. Because he is a brother, work harder, and be glad he is “faithful and beloved.”

The same goes for a Christian friend who gives you a job, a place to stay, or a loan. Don't take advantage of grace. Honour it. That's how the next man gets a chance too.

How a teacher goes bad

  1. proud(v. 4)
  2. knowing nothing(v. 4)
  3. doting about strifes of words(v. 4)
  4. envy, strife, railings(v. 4)
  5. gain is godliness(v. 5)

“Doting” means sick with arguing: a man in love with winning arguments. And the last step is the worst: “supposing that gain is godliness.” They treated religion as a way to make money. Paul says: “from such withdraw thyself.”

Godliness is not a business plan. Be careful of anyone who tells you God will make you rich if you send him a gift. Jesus never promised that, and neither did Paul. The next verse says what is gain.

Part two

Enough, and the love of money

Verses 6–10

What is real gain, and what's the trap?

What you can take with you

There are no pockets in a shroud. Job said it long before Paul: “Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD.” (Job 1:21)

That is not a reason to be careless with money. It is a reason to hold it loosely.

Enough

Contentment doesn't mean I don't want to improve. It means my peace doesn't wait on my next paycheck. “Food and raiment” is food and clothes: the basics. If you have those, you can rest.

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Notice what contentment rests on: not on what you have, but on “I will never leave thee.” The man who has God has what he needs. And Paul, writing from prison, said he had learned it: “in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content.” (Philippians 4:11) It is learned, not born in you.

Today: name three things you have that you didn't have a year ago. Thank God for each, out loud.

The root

Read it exactly. Not money is the root of all evil. The love of money is a root of every kind of evil.

Money is a tool. The love of money is a master. Many of us know where it led: lying to get it, stealing to keep a habit, gambling to win it back, losing the home.

“Pierced themselves through” is the sad part. Nobody else did it. The sorrows came from the thing they chased.

Tool or master?

Money as a tool

You use it

Pay the rent, feed the kids, pay what you owe, help a brother. Then put it down.

Love of money

It uses you

It sets your hours, picks your friends, and keeps you awake. There is never enough.

“No man can serve two masters: for either he will hate the one, and love the other; or else he will hold to the one, and despise the other. Ye cannot serve God and mammon.”

Matthew 6:24

Paul doesn't say godly men get rich. He says godliness with contentment is the gain. If you're broke today, you haven't failed God. If you're doing well, hold it open-handed.

Part three

O man of God

Verses 11–16

Two directions, and one good fight.

Flee, and follow

It's a two-way street. Don't just run from the old things. Run toward something. A man who only flees has an empty space where the old life was, and the old life will move back in.

Joseph ran

When temptation is strong, the Bible's advice isn't to stand and argue with it. It is to run.

“And she caught him by his garment, saying, Lie with me: and he left his garment in her hand, and fled, and got him out.”

Genesis 39:12

“Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart.”

2 Timothy 2:22

Joseph lost his coat. He kept his integrity. Fleeing is not cowardice: it is the smartest move a man can make. Leave the room, the bar, the website, the old phone number, the friend with the pills. Then call someone, “with them that call on the Lord.”

A good fight

Fight

The good fight of faith

Not a fight with people. A fight to keep believing God when everything says quit. “Good” because it's worth winning.

Lay hold

Grab what is already yours

Eternal life is a gift you already have if you believe. “Lay hold” means take a firm grip on it: live today like it's true.

Paul reminds Timothy that he made “a good profession before many witnesses.” He said it out loud. Have you? Tell one person today, in plain words, that you belong to Jesus. That's a rep in the good fight.

King of kings, and Lord of lords.

Paul ends the charge by lifting Timothy's eyes. Jesus stood before Pontius Pilate, with a cross coming, and still told the truth about who He is. That was the “good confession.”

Pilate had power for a few years. The One he sentenced “only hath immortality” and will appear in His own time. Your boss, your judge, your old enemies, and your own record are all under Him.

So Timothy is told to keep the charge “without spot, unrebukeable, until the appearing.” You aren't told to be perfect. You're told to keep going until He comes, and He holds you the whole way.

Part four

Rich, and keeping the trust

Verses 17–21

A word for those with something, and a last word for Timothy.

Rich toward God

Paul's charge to anyone with something: don't be highminded (looking down on others), and don't trust uncertain riches. Money can vanish in a day. Trust “the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy.”

“Rich in good works, ready to distribute, willing to communicate”: that last word means share. Jesus said it plainly: “It is more blessed to give than to receive” (Acts 20:35).

You may feel you've nothing to give. A man with twenty dollars can share five. A man with no money can give a ride, a meal, an hour. Give something, and the foundation starts.

The deposit

“That which is committed to thy trust” was a banker's word: a deposit. Paul gave Timothy the gospel the way you'd leave something valuable with a friend. Keep it safe, and pass it on.

Beware “oppositions of science falsely so called”: claims of secret knowledge that aren't from Christ. You don't need a secret. The gospel is plain: Jesus died for sinners and rose again.

The last words of the letter are the best: “Grace be with thee.”

Part five

Looking back

The whole letter

That was the last chapter. Here is where 1 Timothy has taken us.

Six chapters, one man of God

  1. 1Stay trueJesus came to save sinners
  2. 2Pray, and worship in orderone mediator, Christ Jesus
  3. 3LeadersGod's house, the church
  4. 4Traingodliness; an example
  5. 5Familytreat people right
  6. 6Flee, follow, fightcontentment; keep the trust

Paul wrote this to a young man about AD 62 to 64. A few years later came 2 Timothy, Paul's last letter, written from a Roman prison. The one who told Timothy to keep the trust was about to hand it over for good.

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

What this letter gave us

  1. Sin“of whom I am chief” (1:15): Paul says it of himself, and so can we
  2. Regret“I obtained mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief” (1:13)
  3. Grace“the grace of our Lord was exceeding abundant” (1:14)
  4. Hope“we trust in the living God, who is the Saviour of all men” (4:10)
  5. The real world“follow after righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, meekness” (6:11)

If you remember one thing, make it this: the man who wrote this letter was a persecutor, a blasphemer, and the chief of sinners, and Jesus made him a man of God. If He did that for Paul, He can do it for you.

So wherever you fell yesterday, get up today. Flee the old thing, follow the new, fight the good fight, and “lay hold on eternal life.”

Points to remember

  1. Work“That the name of God and his doctrine be not blasphemed” 6:1Your work carries His name. Be honest and on time, and don't take advantage of a Christian boss.
  2. Contentment“Godliness with contentment is great gain” 6:6You brought nothing in and take nothing out. Peace comes from God, not from getting more.
  3. Money“The love of money is the root of all evil” 6:10Money is a tool; loving it is a trap that pierces a man with sorrow. No prosperity promise here.
  4. Flee and follow“Flee these things; and follow after righteousness” 6:11Run from the old lure and toward something. Fight the good fight, and lay hold on eternal life.
  5. The rich“Rich in good works, ready to distribute” 6:18Trust the living God, not riches. Whatever you have, share some of it.
  6. The whole letter“O Timothy, keep that which is committed to thy trust” 6:20Keep the gospel, live it as a man of God, and pass it on. Grace from first to last.

Question 1 of 6

Work

How should a believer treat his boss?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

With honour, so that God's name and teaching aren't spoken against. If the boss is a believer, work even harder: don't take advantage of a brother.

Question 2 of 6

Contentment

What is great gain?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Godliness with contentment. We brought nothing into the world and can carry nothing out, so with food and clothing we can be content, because God never leaves us.

Question 3 of 6

Money

What does Paul say is the root of all evil?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto them, Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth.”

Luke 12:15

Answer

The love of money. Not money itself, but loving it, because that leads into temptation, a snare, and many sorrows.

Question 4 of 6

Flee and follow

What must the man of God flee, and what must he follow?

What verses tell us about this?

“Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart.”

2 Timothy 2:22

Answer

Flee the love of money and the things that go with it. Follow righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, and meekness, and fight the good fight of faith.

Question 5 of 6

The rich

What should the rich be charged to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not to be proud or to trust uncertain riches, but to trust the living God, do good, be rich in good works, and share. That lays up a good foundation.

Question 6 of 6

The whole letter

What has 1 Timothy taught us about how a man of God lives?

What verses tell us about this?

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

“Let no man despise thy youth; but be thou an example of the believers, in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in purity.”

1 Timothy 4:12

Answer

He knows he is a sinner saved by grace, trains in godliness, is an example, treats people like family, and keeps the trust God has given him.
