Part one

Lord of the sabbath

Verses 1–11

Two sabbaths, two run-ins with the Pharisees: one in a grain field, one in a synagogue.

A snack, or a crime?

Corn in the King James means grain: wheat or barley. Walking along a path through a field, hungry men picked a few heads, rubbed off the husks, and ate.

What God's law said

Allowed

A hungry man could pick by hand in his neighbour's field. He just couldn't bring a sickle and harvest it.

What the Pharisees' rules said

“Not lawful”

Picking was reaping. Rubbing was threshing. Both were work, so both broke the sabbath.

“When thou comest into the standing corn of thy neighbour, then thou mayest pluck the ears with thine hand; but thou shalt not move a sickle unto thy neighbour’s standing corn.”

Deuteronomy 23:25

David and the holy bread

In the house of God stood a table with twelve loaves, one for each tribe. The shewbread was changed every sabbath, and only priests ate the old loaves.

David, running from King Saul, came hungry, and the priest gave him that bread (1 Samuel 21). God's law was given to help people, not to starve them.

Then Jesus said something only God could say: “The Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.” The One who made the day of rest was standing in the field.

Two plans on the same sabbath

Jesus

Made a man whole

He saw a man with a dead right hand, his working hand, and gave it back.

The scribes and Pharisees

Planned harm

They came to watch for a charge, and left “filled with madness,” working out what to do to Jesus.

Jesus answered their real question with His own: if you won't do good on the sabbath, you're doing evil. Doing nothing for a man in need isn't holiness. And who was really breaking the sabbath that day?

He did so

Think about what Jesus asked. “Stretch forth thy hand” was the one thing this man couldn't do. He had to stand up in front of everyone, with the whole room watching, and try.

He didn't wait to feel healed first. He obeyed, and the power came with the obeying. “And he did so: and his hand was restored.”

Many of us have something withered: a marriage, a trust, a working life, a part of us we've given up on. Jesus still says, stretch it out to me. Take the step He asks today, before you can see how it will work.

Part two

He chose twelve

Verses 12–19

A night of prayer, twelve names, and a crowd from every direction.

All night, then the choice

  1. EveningHe goes up a mountain alone
  2. All nightin prayer to God
  3. When it was dayHe calls His disciples
  4. Twelve chosennamed apostles, “sent ones”
  5. Down to the plainto the crowds (v. 17)

The Son of God faced His biggest decision so far, and He spent the whole night talking to His Father about it. If He didn't make big choices without prayer, we shouldn't either.

Luke shows Jesus praying more than any other gospel does: at His baptism (3:21), alone in the wilderness (5:16), here, and on to Gethsemane.

Not the men you'd pick

  • Simon Peterfisherman, big mouth
  • Andrewhis brother, a fisherman
  • Jamesfisherman, a temper
  • Johnhis brother, the same
  • Philipfrom Bethsaida
  • Bartholomewlikely Nathanael
  • Matthewa tax collector, worked for Rome
  • Thomasthe twin, slow to believe
  • Jamesson of Alphaeus
  • Simon Zelotesa Zealot, hated Rome
  • Judasbrother of James
  • Judas Iscariot“which also was the traitor”

No priests, no scholars. Fishermen, a tax man, and a rebel who would once have counted Matthew a traitor, all at one table. Jesus makes enemies into brothers.

Even Judas sat under the same teaching and saw the same miracles. Being close to Jesus isn't the same as trusting Him.

From every direction

Jesus came down from the mountain to a level place, probably near Capernaum and the lake.

People had walked up from Judaea and Jerusalem, around 80 miles, several days on foot.

Others came down from the coast of Tyre and Sidon, Gentile country. Hurting people will walk a long way to find help.

Virtue here means power. It flowed out of Him, and “healed them all.” Nobody went home unhelped.

Part three

Love your enemies

Verses 20–38

Jesus looks at His disciples and turns the world's thinking upside down.

Blessed, and woe

Four blessings, then four woes, each one the mirror of the other. Blessed means happy, favoured by God. Woe is a cry of grief: look out.

blessedye poorye that hunger nowye that weep nowwhen men hate you
the kingdom, or comfort nowfilled, or hungrylaugh, or mournprophets, or false prophets
woeyou that are richyou that are fullyou that laugh nowwhen all men speak well of you

Notice the word now. Today is not the end of the story. The man who has everything now may have nothing then. The man at the bottom who comes to Jesus has the kingdom.

Good news at the bottom

Being poor doesn't save anybody, and money doesn't damn anybody. The danger in riches is “ye have received your consolation”: a man who has all he wants doesn't think he needs God.

Many of us found Jesus at the bottom: broke, hungry, crying, and cut off from the people we used to run with. Jesus says that's not a curse. It's often where God meets a man.

And if people mock you now because you follow Christ, Jesus says “leap for joy.” You're in good company: they did the same to the prophets.

Four commands, four kinds of people

  • Loveyour enemies
  • Do goodto them which hate you
  • Blessthem that curse you
  • Prayfor them which despitefully use you

This isn't about feelings. It's what you do: a kind act, a good word, a prayer. You can pray for a man you can't stand yet.

The cheek and the coat are about an insult and a lawsuit, not about letting someone harm your family. The point: don't hit back, don't keep score.

“As ye would that men should do to you, do ye also to them”

Like Father, like son

“What thank have ye?” means: what credit is that? Anybody loves his friends. Crooks lend to crooks, expecting payback. Jesus asks for more than the world gives.

Why? Because that's how God treated us. He is kind to the unthankful and to the evil. That was us. We didn't earn His love. He loved us first, while we were still His enemies.

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

On the cross, Jesus did exactly what He taught here:

“Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots.”

Luke 23:34

Good measure

Picture the grain market. A seller fills your measure. An honest, generous seller doesn't stop there.

He pours it “into your bosom”: the fold of your robe above the belt, held out like an apron. Jesus says God pays back like that seller. And the measuring cup you use on other people, with judgment or with mercy, is the one used on you.

What “judge not” means

It doesn't mean you can never call wrong wrong. A few verses on, Jesus tells us to judge a tree by its fruit. It means: don't take God's seat. Don't write a man off, and don't pass a final sentence on him.

We are not forgiven because we forgive. We're forgiven by grace, through faith in Christ. But a man who has been forgiven a mountain of debt and won't forgive his brother a pebble has forgotten where he came from.

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Part four

Hear, and do

Verses 39–49

Blind guides, a plank in the eye, trees and their fruit, and two houses in a flood.

The speck and the plank

A mote is a speck of sawdust. A beam is a roof timber. Jesus was a carpenter, and you can almost hear Him laughing at the picture: a man with a plank sticking out of his eye, squinting to help his brother with a speck.

Firstcast out the beam out of thine own eye,
→
thenthou shalt see clearly to help thy brother.

Jesus doesn't say leave your brother's speck alone. He says deal with yourself first, honestly, and then help him gently.

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Fruit comes from the root

Trying harder

Tie on new fruit

Swear less, drink less, try to act right. Good things, but a thorn bush with figs tied on is still a thorn bush.

What Jesus does

A new tree

He changes the heart, and the heart changes the mouth, the hands, and the habits.

Your mouth tells on your heart. What comes out when you're tired, angry, or cornered is what's stored up inside. That's why we need more than willpower. We need a new heart.

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Dig deep

Luke gives a detail: the wise man “digged deep.” Down through the loose earth, all the way to the rock. That's slow, dirty, unseen work, and nobody admires a foundation.

Then the flood comes to both houses. From the road they looked the same. The storm showed the difference.

Hearing isn't building

Both men heard. Both sat in the same crowd. The only difference was that one went home and did what Jesus said.

Calling Jesus “Lord” means doing what He says. Not perfectly, and not to earn salvation, but as the way a saved man lives. Floods will come: a loss, a temptation, a craving, a bad day. The man who has been digging into God's word every day finds rock under his feet.

“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.”

James 1:22

“For other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ.”

1 Corinthians 3:11

Points to remember

  1. The sabbath“Lord also of the sabbath” 6:5God's day of rest was made to do men good, and Jesus is its Lord.
  2. Prayer“All night in prayer to God” 6:12Jesus prayed before He chose. So should we.
  3. Blessed“Blessed are ye that hunger now” 6:21The bottom is often where God meets a man.
  4. Enemies“Love your enemies” 6:27Do good, bless, and pray, because God was kind to us when we were His enemies.
  5. Judging“Cast out first the beam” 6:42Deal honestly with yourself first, then help your brother gently.
  6. Foundation“Digged deep, and laid the foundation on a rock” 6:48Hear Jesus and do what He says. That's what stands in the flood.

Question 1 of 6

The sabbath

What is the sabbath for, and who is Lord of it?

What verses tell us about this?

“The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:”

Mark 2:27

Answer

God gave the sabbath as a gift of rest and a day to do good, not a trap of rules. Jesus, the Son of man, is Lord of it. He fed the hungry and healed the hurting on that day, because that is what God's day is for.

Question 2 of 6

Prayer

What did Jesus do before He chose the twelve?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He went up a mountain and prayed to God all night. Only when it was day did He call His disciples and choose the twelve. If the Son of God prayed through a big decision, we need to pray through ours.

Question 3 of 6

Blessed

Who did Jesus call blessed, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The poor, the hungry, the weeping, and the hated who follow Him. Not because hardship saves, but because God meets people who know they need Him. They will have the kingdom, be filled, and laugh. Those who have everything now and need nothing are in danger.

Question 4 of 6

Enemies

How are we to treat the people who hurt us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Love them, do them good, bless them, and pray for them. Treat them the way you'd want to be treated. We can do it because God did it first: He was kind to us when we were unthankful and evil.

Question 5 of 6

Judging

What should a man look at first, before he points out another man's fault?

What verses tell us about this?

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Answer

At himself. He should deal honestly with the beam in his own eye first. Then he can see clearly enough to help his brother, gently, remembering he could fall too.

Question 6 of 6

Foundation

What made the difference between the two houses?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not the storm, and not the hearing. Both men heard Jesus, and both faced the flood. One did what Jesus said and dug down to the rock. The other heard and did nothing, and his house fell.
