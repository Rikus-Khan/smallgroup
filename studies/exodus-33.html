Exodus 33

My presence shall go with thee

After the calf, God offers the land without himself. The people mourn, Moses meets the LORD face to face outside the camp, and he asks for two things: God's presence, and a sight of his glory.

Part one

“I will not go up in the midst of thee”

Verses 1–6

Everything they wanted, except God.

The offer

  • ✓The land he swore to Abraham, Isaac and Jacobv. 1
  • ✓An angel to go before themv. 2
  • ✓Their enemies driven outv. 2
  • ✓Milk and honey, a good, rich landv. 3
  • ✗“I will not go up in the midst of thee”v. 3

Hence means from here. Lest I consume thee in the way: a holy God in the middle of a stubborn people would burn them up. Keeping his distance was a kind of mercy.

Here is the question for every man: would you take the blessings without God? A sober life, a job, the family back, but God not in it? Israel's answer is the rest of this chapter.

The ornaments come off

  • evil tidingsbad news
  • ornamentsjewellery, finery: the gold they wore
  • Horebanother name for Mount Sinai

Gold earrings are where the calf came from (32:2). Now the gold comes off, and nobody puts it back on. Not a show. They mourned. They knew what they had lost.

“That I may know what to do unto thee.” God waits to see what they do with the bad news. Sorrow is the right answer, if it moves a man toward God:

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Regret that only beats you up leads nowhere. Regret that takes the old things off and turns to God leads to life.

Part two

“As a man speaketh unto his friend”

Verses 7–11

A tent outside the camp, and the cloud at its door.

The tent outside the camp

The great tabernacle of chapters 25–31 was not built yet (chapter 40). This was a tent Moses pitched, called “the Tabernacle of the congregation”: the tent of meeting. And he put it without the camp, outside. The camp had sinned. Whoever wanted the LORD had to walk out to him.

“Every one which sought the LORD went out.” Not only Moses. Any man who wanted God could make that walk.

Face to face, as a friend

The cloudy pillar that led them out of Egypt (13:21) came down and stood at the tent door. The people watched from their own doors and worshipped. Moses went in.

Face to face does not mean Moses saw God's full glory; verse 20 says no man can. It means God spoke with him openly, directly, the way friends talk. Not through dreams or riddles.

“Henceforth I call you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have called you friends; for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you.”

John 15:15

Jesus says the same to every man who follows him. You are not just allowed near. You are his friend.

The young man who stayed

Moses went back to the camp to lead the people. “But his servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man, departed not out of the tabernacle.”

Joshua lingered. He stayed where God had been speaking when nobody required him to. Forty years later he is the man who leads Israel into the land.

“Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach.”

Hebrews 13:13

Jesus died outside the city gate (Hebrews 13:12), and the call is the same: go out to him. Go out from the old crowd, even if they watch from their doors. Make the walk to the place where God speaks, and stay a while. The men who linger are the men who lead later.

Part three

“My presence shall go with thee”

Verses 12–17

Moses will not go without God.

“That I may know thee”

God had said, “Bring up this people,” and “I will send an angel.” Moses pushes back: which angel? Who is going with me? “Thou hast not let me know whom thou wilt send.”

Then he asks the deepest thing: “shew me now thy way, that I may know thee.” Not just directions. He wants to know God himself.

And he does not forget the people: “consider that this nation is thy people.” Even after the calf, they are Yours.

I know thee by name: God knew Moses personally. He knows your name too, not your record, not your case number. Your name.

“If thy presence go not with me”

Ifthy presence go not with me,
→
thencarry us not up hence.

Moses would rather stay in the desert with God than go into the promised land without him. The land was never the point. God was.

And what makes God's people different from everybody else? “Is it not in that thou goest with us?” Not that they are better behaved. That he is with them.

God says yes: “I will do this thing also that thou hast spoken.” One man's prayer, and the presence is given back to the whole people.

“I will give thee rest”

Exodus 33:14

My presence shall go with thee, and I will give thee rest.

Matthew 11:28

Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

The rest is not in the land, or the job, or the day going well. It is in him going with you. Conversation there means your way of life. Take the promise to work with you: you will not walk into anything alone.

Part four

“Shew me thy glory”

Verses 18–23

Moses asks for more, and God hides him in a rock.

His goodness, and his name

Moses asks to see God's glory. God answers by promising his goodness and his name. His glory is not just brightness. It is what he is like.

“I will be gracious to whom I will be gracious, and will shew mercy on whom I will shew mercy.” Mercy is his to give. Nobody earns it, and nobody can demand it. That is good news for a man who has nothing to bring: it is not given for your record. In the next chapter God tells us what he is like: “merciful and gracious.”

“There shall no man see me, and live.” Not because God is cruel, but because sinful men cannot stand his full holiness. So how does anyone ever see God?

“Jesus saith unto him, Have I been so long time with you, and yet hast thou not known me, Philip? he that hath seen me hath seen the Father; and how sayest thou then, Shew us the Father?”

John 14:9

In the cleft of the rock

A clift of the rock is a cleft: a crack, a split in the rock face. God puts Moses in it, covers him with his hand, and passes by.

Then the hand is lifted, and Moses sees his back parts: where God has just been, the glory going on ahead. Not the face. Enough to know him, and live.

Hidden in the rock, covered by God's own hand. That is where a man is safe before a holy God. The old hymn says it: Rock of Ages, cleft for me, let me hide myself in Thee.

Hidden, and safe

“For in the time of trouble he shall hide me in his pavilion: in the secret of his tabernacle shall he hide me; he shall set me up upon a rock.”

Psalm 27:5

“For ye are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God.”

Colossians 3:3

“For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.”

2 Corinthians 4:6

Moses saw God's back. We see the glory of God “in the face of Jesus Christ.” A man who has trusted Christ is hidden in him, and the hand that covers him is the same hand that holds him.

Real world: walk out to the tent

This chapter starts after the worst failure of Israel's life, and it ends with a man closer to God than anyone had ever been. A fall does not have to end with distance.

  • Take it offLike the ornaments: put away the thing that led you to the calf. Today, not someday.
  • Walk outFind the place, the time, the brothers where God speaks, even if the old crowd watches from their doors.
  • LingerLike Joshua. Stay a little longer with God's word than you have to.
  • Ask for himNot just for things to go well: “If thy presence go not with me, carry us not up hence.”

Points to remember

  1. The offer“I will send an angel before thee… for I will not go up in the midst of thee.” 33:2–3Everything they wanted, without God. The blessings are not the point; he is.
  2. The ornaments“They mourned: and no man did put on him his ornaments.” 33:4Godly sorrow takes the old things off and turns toward God.
  3. The tent“Every one which sought the LORD went out unto the tabernacle of the congregation, which was without the camp.” 33:7Any man who wanted God could walk out to him.
  4. Face to face“The LORD spake unto Moses face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend.” 33:11God spoke openly with Moses as a friend. Jesus calls his followers friends.
  5. The presence“My presence shall go with thee, and I will give thee rest.” 33:14Moses would not go without God, and God promised to go, and to give rest.
  6. The glory“I will put thee in a clift of the rock, and will cover thee with my hand while I pass by.” 33:22Hidden in the rock, covered by his hand, Moses saw God's goodness and lived.

Question 1 of 6

The offer

What did the LORD promise to give, and what did he say he would not do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The land, an angel before them, their enemies driven out, milk and honey. But God would not go up among them, lest he consume them. Would you take the blessings without God? Israel would not, and neither should we.

The ornaments

What did the people do when they heard the evil tidings?

What verses tell us about this?

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Answer

They mourned, and took off their gold ornaments, the very thing the calf was made from. Sorrow that turns a man toward God leads to life. Put off whatever led you to your calf.

The presence

What verses tell us about this?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

He asked to know God's way and to have God go with them, or not go at all. God promised, “My presence shall go with thee, and I will give thee rest.” Jesus makes the same promise of rest to every tired man who comes to him.

Question 6 of 6

The glory

What did the LORD do when Moses asked to see his glory?

What verses tell us about this?

“For ye are dead, and your life is hid with Christ in God.”

Colossians 3:3

Answer

He promised to make his goodness pass by and proclaim his name. He hid Moses in a cleft of the rock, covered him with his hand, and let him see his back. And Paul says a man in Christ is hidden too: “your life is hid with Christ in God.” Safe before a holy God.
