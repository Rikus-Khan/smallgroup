Numbers 10

Silver trumpets and the road

After nearly a year at Sinai the cloud lifts. Two silver trumpets call the camp, the tribes march in order behind the ark, and Moses prays as it goes: “Rise up, LORD.”

Part one

Two trumpets of silver

Verses 1–10

Before the camp moves, the LORD gives it a way to hear his orders: two trumpets, one voice.

One call everyone could understand

  • Both trumpetsthe whole assembly comes to the door of the tabernacle (verse 3)
  • One trumpetonly the princes, the heads of the thousands, come to Moses (verse 4)
  • An alarma different sounding from the plain blast, that means: move out (verses 5–6)
  • Blow, no alarma plain blast that means: gather (verse 7)

Moses was to make them “of a whole piece”: each trumpet beaten out of one piece of silver, no seams. An alarm here is not a scare. It is a signal blast, different from the steady one. Six hundred thousand fighting men and their families could not all see Moses, but every one of them could hear a trumpet.

There were two sounds and a few simple rules, and every man knew which was which. A signal half understood would be worse than none, and Paul says so: “For if the trumpet give an uncertain sound, who shall prepare himself to the battle?” (1 Corinthians 14:8). And “the trump of God” will sound when the Lord himself comes down from heaven: “the dead in Christ shall rise first” (1 Thessalonians 4:16). The last trumpet is a sound of rescue.

Four jobs for the trumpets

  • Callto gather the assembly or the princes
  • Marchan alarm, camp by camp: east first, then south
  • Waran alarm against “the enemy that oppresseth you”: “ye shall be remembered before the LORD your God, and ye shall be saved”
  • Gladnessover the burnt offerings and peace offerings, on feast days and at each new month

Notice the third. When the enemy pressed them, the trumpet was not a call for the army to be braver. It was a cry to the LORD: “ye shall be remembered before the LORD your God.” A man in recovery has an enemy that oppresses him. Blow the alarm: pray, and call your brothers in Christ. That is what the trumpet was for.

The priests blew them, “an ordinance for ever”, and the same silver sounded on the happy days too. Gladness has its own trumpet. Don't let the only time you reach for God be the war.

Part two

The cloud is taken up

Verses 11–28

The camp has stood at Sinai for about eleven months. One morning the cloud lifts, and twelve tribes move in order.

About eleven months at the mount

  1. third monththey come to SinaiExodus 19:1
  2. 1st month, year twothe tabernacle reared upExodus 40:17
  3. 2nd month, day 1the people numberedNumbers 1:1
  4. 2nd month, day 20the cloud liftsNumbers 10:11

They came into the wilderness of Sinai in the third month of the first year and left on the twentieth day of the second month of the second year: about eleven months. The census of chapter 1 began on the first day of that second month, so the camp moved nineteen days after the counting began.

“The cloud was taken up.” They did not choose the day. The same cloud that covered the tabernacle (9:15–23) rose, and a whole nation moved at that sign. “And they first took their journey according to the commandment of the LORD by the hand of Moses.”

Out of Sinai into Paran

“The children of Israel took their journeys out of the wilderness of Sinai; and the cloud rested in the wilderness of Paran.” They went “three days’ journey” from “the mount of the LORD” (verse 33), and the cloud led the way.

Taberah, Kibroth-hattaavah and Hazeroth come next (11:3, 34, 35). The text does not say where they lay, so we won't pin them. Mount Sinai stands at Jabal al-Lawz in Arabia (Galatians 4:25: “mount Sinai in Arabia”).

Twelve tribes and twelve princes, each prince named with his father (verses 14–27). God does not lose track of one man. A list like this is the Bible's way of saying, every man counted.

Old words in the march

  • standardthe flag of a camp, a banner on a pole. Each of the four camps marched under its own
  • rerewardthe rear guard: the last camp in the line (Dan, with Asher and Naphtali)
  • their armiestheir hosts: the tribes arranged in ranks like an army
  • set forwardstarted out, moved off
  • against they camebefore they came: the tabernacle was already pitched when the Kohathites arrived with the holy things

Wagons and shoulders

Gershon and Merari: the wagons

The tent itself

They took down the tabernacle and loaded it on the wagons Moses gave them: two wagons and four oxen to the sons of Gershon, four wagons and eight oxen to the sons of Merari (7:7–8). They went ahead of the Kohathites, so the tent could be pitched.

Kohath: the shoulders

The holy things

“But unto the sons of Kohath he gave none: because the service of the sanctuary belonging unto them was that they should bear upon their shoulders” (7:9). The holy things were carried by hand, never on a cart.

Everyone had a job, and each job was named. Nobody was too small, and nobody was too big to carry his load. A man in a small group needs the same: a place where he is expected, and a load he carries for the others.

Judah goes first

“In the first place went the standard of the camp of the children of Judah.” Judah, on the east, led the march. The New Testament tells us who came out of Judah.

“For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood.”

Hebrews 7:14

“And one of the elders saith unto me, Weep not: behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailed to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof.”

Revelation 5:5

The tribe that marched first would one day give Israel its king, and the world its Saviour. That is a picture, not something this verse says: the verse only tells us the order. But the Lord who “sprang out of Juda” is the one who goes before his people.

Part three

“Leave us not”

Verses 29–36

Moses asks a man for his eyes, the ark leads the way, and there is a prayer at each stop.

“Thou mayest be to us instead of eyes”

MosesWe are journeying unto the place of which the LORD said, I will give it you: come thou with us, and we will do thee good.

HobabI will not go; but I will depart to mine own land, and to my kindred.

MosesLeave us not, I pray thee; forasmuch as thou knowest how we are to encamp in the wilderness, and thou mayest be to us instead of eyes.

Forasmuch as means since. Moses had the cloud (9:15–23; verse 34), and still asked a brother for his eyes: a man who knew the desert could see what a cloud does not show. Both were from God. (The chapter doesn't say what Hobab decided.)

Ask for a brother's eyes

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

God had given Moses a cloud, and Moses still said, Leave us not, I pray thee. There is no shame in a man with the Holy Spirit living in him asking an older brother to look at the road with him. A new believer can't see his own blind spots. He sees them better when another man is looking too.

Moses asked as a man with a promise to share: “the LORD hath spoken good concerning Israel.” Find the man who knows the desert, someone who has walked your road and kept walking, and ask him to come along. What the LORD does for you, he can do through a brother.

A prayer at every start and stop

When the ark set forward

“Rise up, LORD”

“…and let thine enemies be scattered; and let them that hate thee flee before thee.” The camp moves into dangerous ground behind the LORD, and Moses asks him to deal with the enemy first.

When it rested

“Return, O LORD”

“…unto the many thousands of Israel.” At the end of the day he asks the LORD to come back and stay among them. Rest is the LORD among his people.

“Let God arise, let his enemies be scattered: let them also that hate him flee before him.”

Psalm 68:1

David took up Moses' words in a psalm, and another psalm keeps the second half: “Arise, O LORD, into thy rest; thou, and the ark of thy strength” (Psalm 132:8). Pray when you set out, and pray when you lie down. Every morning, “Rise up, LORD”. Every night, “Return, O LORD.”

Real world: walk in order

  • Wait for the callThey moved when the cloud lifted and not before. Don't run ahead of him, and don't sit when he moves.
  • Know your loadGershon, Merari and Kohath each had a share. What is yours today: the job, the kids, the meeting?
  • Blow the alarmWhen the enemy presses, cry to the LORD and call your brothers. That is what the trumpet was for.
  • Ask for eyesName one older man who knows the road, and ask him to walk it with you.

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Moses begged Hobab, “Leave us not.” Hobab could say no, and a man can. The LORD says: I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee. If you fell yesterday, you are still under the cloud. Get up, listen for the trumpet, and fall in. Chapter 11 shows what these same people did, and what the LORD did about it.

Points to remember

  1. The trumpets“Make thee two trumpets of silver… for the calling of the assembly, and for the journeying of the camps” 10:2One clear signal for the whole camp, blown by the priests. When the enemy presses, blow the alarm to the LORD.
  2. The march“the cloud was taken up from off the tabernacle of the testimony” 10:11They moved when the LORD moved, in order, Judah first.
  3. A brother's eyes“thou mayest be to us instead of eyes” 10:31God's cloud and a brother's eyes both. Ask a man who knows the road.
  4. The promise“the LORD hath spoken good concerning Israel” 10:29He has spoken good about you, too. What he does for you, share.
  5. Prayer“Rise up, LORD… Return, O LORD” 10:35–36Pray when you set out and when you lie down.

Question 1 of 6

The trumpets

What were the two silver trumpets for, and who blew them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To call the assembly and to move the camps, and in war to cry to the LORD (“ye shall be remembered before the LORD your God”), and in gladness to sound over the offerings. The priests, the sons of Aaron, blew them. When your enemy presses, blow the alarm: cry to the LORD and call your brothers.

Question 2 of 6

The march

How did Israel know when to move, and which tribe went first?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The cloud was taken up, the trumpet sounded an alarm, and Judah went first. They moved at the LORD's word and not before. Learn to move when he moves.

Question 3 of 6

A brother's eyes

Why did Moses ask Hobab to come with them, when the cloud was already leading them?

What verses tell us about this?

“For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:10

Answer

Hobab knew the desert and could be “instead of eyes”. God guided them by the cloud and gave them a man's eyes too. Ask a brother who knows the road to walk with you, and don't try to walk alone.

Question 4 of 6

The promise

What did Moses say the LORD had spoken concerning Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That he has spoken good: he will give them the land. Moses offers Hobab a share of it. The LORD has spoken good about you in Christ too, and the good he does for you is meant to be shared with your brothers.

Question 5 of 6

Prayer

What did Moses say when the ark set forward, and when it rested?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Rise up, LORD, and let thine enemies be scattered”, when it set forward; “Return, O LORD, unto the many thousands of Israel”, when it rested. Begin the day asking him to go before you, and end it asking him to stay.

Question 6 of 6

Christ

What does the New Testament say came out of Judah, the tribe that marched first?

What verses tell us about this?

“For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda;”

Hebrews 7:14

“Weep not: behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailed to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof.”

Revelation 5:5

Answer

Judah marched first (verse 14). And our Lord sprang out of Judah: he is the Lion of the tribe of Juda. The tribe that went first gave us the one who goes before us. He has prevailed, and he does not leave his people.
