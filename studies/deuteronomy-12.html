Deuteronomy 12

One place to worship, and no way of their own

Israel is to destroy every place where the nations served their gods, to bring their offerings only to “the place which the LORD your God shall choose”, to eat flesh at home but never the blood, and to add nothing to his word and take nothing from it.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. DestroyWhat were they to do to the places where the nations served their gods?
  2. The placeWhere were they to bring their offerings and eat before the LORD?
  3. Whose eyesWhose eyes were they to follow, and not their own?
  4. At homeWhat could they eat in their own towns, and what never?
  5. Take heedWhat were they told not to enquire about the nations' gods?
  6. The wordWhat must they not do to the words the LORD commanded?

Part one

“Ye shall utterly destroy all the places”

Verses 1–7

The statutes and judgments begin with what to tear down, and where to go instead.

Old words: verses 1–7

  • utterly destroywipe out completely, leave nothing standing
  • pillarsstanding stones set up to honour a false god
  • grovestrees and wooden poles set up beside the altars of false gods
  • graven imagescarved idols
  • habitationa dwelling, a home (the LORD's)
  • heave offeringsgifts lifted up before the LORD, then given to the priests

Verse 1 opens the long run of laws that fills chapters 12 to 26. The first one is about worship, because what a man worships is what he becomes.

Why tear it all down?

A grown man should hear this plainly. The nations' worship was not harmless. Moses puts the worst of it in verse 31.

That is why the places must go: “burn their groves”, “overthrow their altars”, “destroy the names of them out of that place” (verse 3). We saw in chapters 7 and 9 that the LORD drove those nations out for their wickedness, after waiting generations (Deuteronomy 9:5; Genesis 15:16), and not for Israel's goodness.

There is a second reason, for the people left in the land: “that thou be not snared by following them” (verse 30). Anything left standing would call to them. Moses is tearing the temptation out by the root.

Tear down your own high places

No man today is told to pull down a hill-altar. But each of us had places where the old gods were served: a phone number, a corner, a website, a friend who always has it. We saw in chapter 7 how the believers at Ephesus burned theirs (Acts 19:19).

The old altar is not a thing to keep standing.

You don't do it alone, and you do not do it to earn anything: Christ has already forgiven. You do it because it is a snare. Take one brother with you. If something is too hard to tear down by yourself, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today.

What they were to bring there

  • Burnt offeringsgiven whole to the LORD on the altar
  • Sacrificesofferings the family shared as a meal before the LORD (verse 7)
  • Tithesa tenth of the harvest and the flock
  • Heave offeringslifted up before the LORD, then given to the priests
  • Vowswhat a man had promised God
  • Freewill offeringsgifts brought because he wanted to
  • Firstlingsthe first-born of the herd and the flock

Not one of these is a payment to buy the LORD's love. They were the way he himself appointed for his people to come to him. The end of the list is not a tax collector's receipt: “there ye shall eat before the LORD your God, and ye shall rejoice” (verse 7).

Part two

“Every man whatsoever is right in his own eyes”

Verses 8–14

Not what seems right to each man, but the place and the way the LORD has chosen.

Right in his own eyes

Moses says it of the way things are “this day”: each man doing what seemed right to him. It is the oldest excuse there is. The Bible uses the same words again, at the worst time in Israel's story.

“In those days there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes.”

Judges 21:25

“There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.”

Proverbs 14:12

“It feels right to me” has put more men in the ground than any outright lie. The things we did in our own eyes looked fine from the inside, and “the end thereof are the ways of death.” Moses does not leave them with a bare “no”: he gives them a place to go.

Everyone at the table, the Levite too

Verse 12 names them all: the man, his sons and daughters, his menservants and maidservants, and “the Levite that is within your gates; forasmuch as he hath no part nor inheritance with you.” The Levite had no land. He is set at the table because he has no field of his own to eat from.

Many of us came into this with no inheritance: no home place, no family table. This is the God who sets a place for the man with nothing. And then he says to those who have a place: “Take heed to thyself that thou forsake not the Levite as long as thou livest” (verse 19). Be the table for somebody else.

Not just anywhere

  • not “upon the high mountains”verse 2
  • not “upon the hills”verse 2
  • not “under every green tree”verse 2
  • not “in every place that thou seest”verse 13: the place that looks right to the eye
  • but “the place which the LORD shall choose”“to put his name there” (verse 5); “in one of thy tribes” (verse 14)

The phrase “the place which the LORD shall choose” comes five times in this chapter (verses 5, 11, 14, 18 and 26), and verse 21 says it again as “hath chosen”. Moses never names the place.

Where did he choose?

Moses doesn't say. Later the Bible does: first at Shiloh, then at Jerusalem.

“But go ye now unto my place which was in Shiloh, where I set my name at the first, and see what I did to it for the wickedness of my people Israel.”

Jeremiah 7:12

“But I have chosen Jerusalem, that my name might be there; and have chosen David to be over my people Israel.”

2 Chronicles 6:6

“In this mountain”, and at Jerusalem

“Our fathers worshipped in this mountain; and ye say, that in Jerusalem is the place where men ought to worship. Jesus saith unto her, Woman, believe me, the hour cometh, when ye shall neither in this mountain, nor yet at Jerusalem, worship the Father.”

John 4:20–21

“But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him. God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth.”

John 4:23–24

The woman at the well, at Sychar by the ground Jacob gave to Joseph (John 4:5), pointed at “this mountain”: the hills by Shechem, where the blessing of chapter 11 was to be spoken. She and the Jews argued about which was the place. Jesus said the argument was over: the place is no longer a hill. The true worshippers “worship the Father in spirit and in truth”, and they come to him through the Son (John 14:6).

Part three

“Only ye shall not eat the blood”

Verses 15–25

Eat meat at home as you wish, and leave the life to the LORD.

Old words: verses 15–25

  • killslaughter an animal for food
  • gatesthe towns: a man's gates were where his town began and ended
  • lusteth, longethdesires strongly, wants badly
  • roebuck, harttwo kinds of deer, ordinary game from the field
  • enlarge thy bordergive you more land

“These are the beasts which ye shall eat: the ox, the sheep, and the goat, The hart, and the roebuck, and the fallow deer, and the wild goat, and the pygarg, and the wild ox, and the chamois.”

Deuteronomy 14:4–5

The hart and the roebuck are on the clean list. That is why “the unclean and the clean may eat thereof”: it was ordinary food from the field, not a holy offering, so a man who was unclean by the law could sit down to it with the clean.

At the place, and at home

At the place the LORD chooses

The holy things

Tithes, firstlings, vows, freewill and heave offerings: “thou must eat them before the LORD thy God in the place which the LORD thy God shall choose” (verse 18).

At home, within thy gates

Ordinary flesh

“Thou mayest kill and eat flesh in all thy gates, whatsoever thy soul lusteth after” (verse 15). No altar, no priest needed: a meal is a meal.

The same law holds both: the holy things go to the place, and the daily bread is no sin to eat. Only one thing is shared by both tables: the blood goes on the ground (verse 16), or on the altar (verse 27).

“Rejoice before the LORD”

Look where it falls in verses 1 to 18. The word rejoice comes three times, and always before the LORD:

1 2 3 4 5 6  7×1 8 9 10 11  12×1 13 14 15 16 17  18×1

Worship at the place is a feast, with the family and the servants and the Levite, and “ye shall rejoice in all that ye put your hand unto” (verse 7). He asks them to bring their work, and to be glad in it.

A new man can have the idea that following the LORD means giving up joy. Moses says the opposite: he wants his people at a table, glad, before him.

Wanting is not the sin

The LORD says three times that a man may eat “whatsoever thy soul lusteth after” (verses 15, 20, 21). The want itself is not condemned. Compare what happened in the wilderness:

“And the mixt multitude that was among them fell a lusting: and the children of Israel also wept again, and said, Who shall give us flesh to eat?”

Numbers 11:4

Same wish, different way. In the wilderness it was a cry against the LORD, “Who shall give us?”, because they would not take what he was giving. Here it is “when the LORD thy God shall enlarge thy border, as he hath promised thee” (verse 20): the LORD gives in his time, and the want is met in his way.

“For every creature of God is good, and nothing to be refused, if it be received with thanksgiving: For it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer.”

1 Timothy 4:4–5

Our appetites were given by God, and the trouble is the way, the time, and the master. That is no licence to go back to anything that held you. A man who is free keeps clear of what had him.

“The blood is the life”

“For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.”

Leviticus 17:11

Moses gives the reason in a few words: “the blood is the life; and thou mayest not eat the life with the flesh” (verse 23). The word blood comes five times in this chapter (verses 16, 23 twice, and 27 twice). A man could eat the meat. The life was the LORD's, and it was to go back to the ground “as water”, or to the altar.

He had given the blood for something: “to make an atonement for your souls.” So it was not a thing to be eaten, and it was not common.

What the altar's blood pointed to

“And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.”

Hebrews 9:22

“And he took the cup, and gave thanks, and gave it to them, saying, Drink ye all of it; For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins.”

Matthew 26:27–28

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Every drop on those altars was given for sins, and every drop pointed ahead to the precious blood of Christ. This is the grace for the man who fell last night: his sin was paid for in blood, once, and it is finished.

The council at Jerusalem, and the blood

“Wherefore my sentence is, that we trouble not them, which from among the Gentiles are turned to God: But that we write unto them, that they abstain from pollutions of idols, and from fornication, and from things strangled, and from blood.”

Acts 15:19–20

“For it seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us, to lay upon you no greater burden than these necessary things; That ye abstain from meats offered to idols, and from blood, and from things strangled, and from fornication: from which if ye keep yourselves, ye shall do well. Fare ye well.”

Acts 15:28–29

When the question came whether Gentile believers had to keep the whole law, the answer was no: “no greater burden than these necessary things.” And the short list includes the two things of this chapter: idols (verses 2, 3, 30, 31) and blood (verses 16, 23). The new believers were to stay clear of the old altars, and to respect the life that is in the blood.

Part four

“Take heed to thyself that thou be not snared”

Verses 26–32

Do not ask how they did it. Do not change what God said.

Whose sight?

Verse 8

“right in his own eyes”

Each man his own judge. Every place, every way, whatever looked right.

Verses 25 and 28

“right in the sight of the LORD”

“That which is right in the sight of the LORD” (verse 25); “that which is good and right in the sight of the LORD thy God” (verse 28). His eyes, his word, his place.

Both verses promise “that it may go well with thee, and with thy children after thee”. That is the covenant with Israel in their land. It is no promise of an easy life for a man who obeys, but it is true that the way he marks out is the way that holds a family together.

“Just asking how they did it”

The thoughtHow did these nations serve their gods? Even so will I do likewise.

The LORDThou shalt not do so unto the LORD thy God.
not even to look

The trap starts as curiosity: “I'll just go see the old crowd.” “Enquire not after their gods” (verse 30): it begins with a question, and ends with “even so will I do likewise”.

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall. There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:12–13

Add nothing, take nothing

“Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it, that ye may keep the commandments of the LORD your God which I command you.”

Deuteronomy 4:2

“Every word of God is pure: he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him. Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar.”

Proverbs 30:5–6

“For I testify unto every man that heareth the words of the prophecy of this book, If any man shall add unto these things, God shall add unto him the plagues that are written in this book: And if any man shall take away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God shall take away his part out of the book of life, and out of the holy city, and from the things which are written in this book.”

Revelation 22:18–19

The same warning stands near the beginning of Moses' sermons (4:2), in the middle (12:32), and at the very end of the Bible. Two ways to break it: adding (my own rules, my own feelings as if they were his word) and taking away (cutting out the hard parts). The Book is to be taken whole, and he never takes away what he gave.

Real world: one place, his way

  • Sin“Right in his own eyes.” It feels right, so a man does it, and the places of the old worship stay standing in his life.
  • RegretWe served the old gods in more places than we like to count. Moses does not say “you will never forgive yourself”. He says tear the places down, and go to the place he gives you.
  • GraceThe altar blood pointed to Christ's: “without shedding of blood is no remission” (Hebrews 9:22), and he has shed it. The man in Christ comes to the Father himself, “in spirit and in truth” (John 4:24).
  • HopeThe picture the LORD paints is a table: “ye shall rejoice before the LORD your God”, with your household, and a place even for the man with no inheritance.
  • TodayName one high place (a person, a number, a website, a habit) and tell one brother. Ask him to help you take it down. And if it is heavier than that, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today.

Points to remember

  1. Destroy“Ye shall utterly destroy all the places, wherein the nations which ye shall possess served their gods” 12:2Tear down what called them away. Do not keep an old altar “just in case”.
  2. The place“unto the place which the LORD your God shall choose out of all your tribes to put his name there” 12:5Worship where he says, and be glad there. Jesus says the hour has come to worship the Father in spirit and in truth, not on a hill.
  3. Whose eyes“that which is good and right in the sight of the LORD thy God” 12:28Not what is right in his own eyes, but what is right in the LORD's.
  4. At home“Only be sure that thou eat not the blood: for the blood is the life” 12:23Eat at home freely, and leave the life to the LORD. His blood was given, in the end, for us.
  5. Take heed“that thou enquire not after their gods, saying, How did these nations serve their gods?” 12:30Do not go looking at the old life to see how it was done.
  6. The word“thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it” 12:32Take the word whole: add none, cut none.

Question 1 of 6

Destroy

What were they to do to the places where the nations served their gods?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Utterly destroy them: overthrow the altars, break the pillars, burn the groves, hew down the graven images, and destroy the names of the gods out of that place.

Question 2 of 6

The place

Where were they to bring their offerings and eat before the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To “the place which the LORD your God shall choose out of all your tribes to put his name there”, and not in every place that they saw. There they would eat before the LORD and rejoice.

Question 3 of 6

Whose eyes

Whose eyes were they to follow, and not their own?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD's: “that which is right in the sight of the LORD”, and not what is “right in his own eyes”.

Question 4 of 6

At home

What could they eat in their own towns, and what never?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They could kill and eat flesh in all their gates, whatever their soul desired. They were never to eat the blood, which they poured on the earth as water, “for the blood is the life”.

Question 5 of 6

Take heed

What were they told not to enquire about the nations' gods?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“How did these nations serve their gods?” They were not to ask, lest they be snared and do likewise. “Thou shalt not do so unto the LORD thy God.”

Question 6 of 6

The word

What must they not do to the words the LORD commanded?

What verses tell us about this?

“Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it, that ye may keep the commandments of the LORD your God which I command you.”

Deuteronomy 4:2

Answer

Add nothing to it, and take nothing from it: observe to do whatever he commands.
