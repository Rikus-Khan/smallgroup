Leviticus 7

What is holy, what is shared, and what is the LORD’s alone

The last of the offering laws. The trespass offering is most holy. The priests are fed from the altar. A man gives thanks at a meal with God. And the fat and the blood are never to be eaten.

Part one

“It is most holy”

Verses 1–10

The trespass offering, and what the priests received for their work.

Meat offering does not mean flesh. It is the offering of flour, oil and frankincense that Leviticus 2 describes. In 1611 “meat” meant food.

The trespass offering is the one for a wrong done to God or to a neighbour. Moses has already given its rules; here the LORD adds what happens to the animal afterward.

The altar paid the priests

  • the skinof a burnt offering, to the priest who offered it (verse 8)
  • baked and cookedmeat offerings, to the priest who offered them (verse 9)
  • every othermeat offering, mixed with oil or dry: to all the sons of Aaron, “one as much as another” (verse 10)

The priests had no land of their own and no field to plant. They lived on what the people brought to the LORD, and he saw to it that they were fed. Notice verse 10: “one as much as another.” No priest got a bigger share for being better known.

“Do ye not know that they which minister about holy things live of the things of the temple? and they which wait at the altar are partakers with the altar? Even so hath the Lord ordained that they which preach the gospel should live of the gospel.”

1 Corinthians 9:13–14

Paul points back to this very arrangement. The men who give their time to the word are to be looked after by the people they serve.

Part two

“The sacrifice of peace offerings”

Verses 11–21

A thank-you meal with God, and the rule about when to eat it.

A meal with God

The peace offering was different from the rest: the man who brought it ate it too. God, the priest and the worshipper all had a share.

Read it across. The LORD takes his portion first. The priests are fed. And the man who brought the offering sits down to eat in the LORD's presence.

Peace made, and a table to eat at

“Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:”

Romans 5:1

“And not only so, but we also joy in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom we have now received the atonement.”

Romans 5:11

The peace offering pictures what the blood bought: not only forgiveness but peace, a seat at the table. The sin offering deals with the guilt. This is the meal afterward.

One bread, one body

“The cup of blessing which we bless, is it not the communion of the blood of Christ? The bread which we break, is it not the communion of the body of Christ? For we being many are one bread, and one body: for we are all partakers of that one bread.”

1 Corinthians 10:16–17

Paul says the Lord's table is the same kind of thing: a shared meal, and one body. Communion means sharing in something together. A man in recovery who has lived alone with his shame needs to hear that the table is for him.

Eat it while it is fresh

  1. thanksgivingthe same day“not leave any of it until the morning” (15)
  2. a vow, or a free giftthat day and the next“on the morrow also” (16)
  3. the third dayburnt with firenot to be eaten (17)

Morrow means the next day. Imputed means counted to a man's account. A thanksgiving was eaten the day it was given. A vow or a free gift was allowed one more day. After that, if any was eaten, “it shall not be accepted, neither shall it be imputed unto him that offereth it.”

Thanks that is left on the shelf spoils. Say it the day it is due.

“Saying, Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered. Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin.”

Romans 4:7–8

Here the offering is not imputed, not counted to the man, because he handled it wrongly. In Romans the word comes round the other way: for the man who trusts Christ, the Lord will not impute sin.

“Cut off from his people”

Verses 20 and 21 are sobering. A man who ate the peace offering while “having his uncleanness upon him,” or after touching an unclean thing, “shall be cut off from his people.” The same words come in verses 20, 21, 25 and 27: four times in this one chapter.

Moses does not explain here how a man was cut off. What the text says is clear: the meal was holy, and a man could not bring his uncleanness to it as if it did not matter.

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

The answer for a man who knows he is unclean is not to stay away from the table. It is to be cleansed. The blood of Jesus Christ cleanses from all sin, and then he walks in the light, and we have fellowship one with another.

Part three

“Ye shall eat no manner of fat”

Verses 22–27

Two things that belong to the LORD and were never to be eaten.

The fat and the blood

The fat

Of ox, sheep or goat

The LORD gives the reason in verse 25: it is the part “of which men offer an offering made by fire unto the LORD.” The fat of a beast that died of itself could be used for other things (verse 24), but still not eaten.

The blood

Of fowl or of beast

“in any of your dwellings” (verse 26): not only at the altar, but at home, in every house in Israel.

Manner means kind. “No manner of blood” means no kind of blood at all. “Cut off from his people” is the penalty for both.

The reason for the blood comes in chapter 17, and it is the heart of the book:

“For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.”

Leviticus 17:11

The fat was the best part, and it was God's. The blood was the life, and it was given for atonement. Both were holy, so neither was a man's to eat.

Part four

“By a statute for ever”

Verses 28–38

The priests' portion, and where and when the LORD gave all of it.

Wave and heave

The wave breast

Waved before the LORD

Waved before the LORD, to show it was given to him first (verse 30).

The heave shoulder

Lifted up

Lifted up high and then given to the priest who offered the blood (verses 32–33).

Oblation is an offering, a thing brought and given. In verse 29 the man brings his own oblation “unto the LORD,” and in verse 30 “his own hands shall bring” it. Nobody could do it for him.

Look at verse 34: “For the wave breast and the heave shoulder have I taken of the children of Israel… and have given them unto Aaron.” The LORD takes it from the people and gives it to the priests. The priests do not take it. He gives it.

The whole law of offerings

Verse 37 gathers up the first seven chapters of the book in one sentence: “This is the law of the burnt offering, of the meat offering, and of the sin offering, and of the trespass offering, and of the consecrations, and of the sacrifice of the peace offerings.”

  1. burntall to Godchapter 1
  2. meatflour and oilchapter 2
  3. sinfor sinchapter 4
  4. trespassfor a wrong donechapters 5–6
  5. consecrationssetting priests apartchapter 8
  6. peacethe shared mealchapter 3, and here

One law, six offerings, and every one of them pointed to a sacrifice that could take sin away for good.

“In the wilderness of Sinai”

Verse 38 says it plainly: “the LORD commanded Moses in mount Sinai, in the day that he commanded the children of Israel to offer their oblations unto the LORD, in the wilderness of Sinai.”

Moses wrote this at the foot of the mountain, in the month after the tabernacle was reared up on “the first day of the first month” of the second year (Exodus 40:17). A month later, on “the first day of the second month, in the second year,” the people are numbered (Numbers 1:1).

Real world: give thanks the same day

The thanksgiving offering was eaten on the day it was given. “Sacrifice of thanksgiving” is not a one-time event for a man in recovery. It is a habit, and the verse gives the pattern.

“And let them sacrifice the sacrifices of thanksgiving, and declare his works with rejoicing.”

Psalm 107:22

“By him therefore let us offer the sacrifice of praise to God continually, that is, the fruit of our lips giving thanks to his name.”

Hebrews 13:15
  • TodayName one thing God did for you today, and say thanks out loud before you sleep.
  • With someoneThe peace offering was a meal shared. Tell a brother what you are thankful for. Better still, eat with one.
  • Don't carry it overRegret and unspoken guilt do not improve on the third day. Take them to the LORD, and to a brother, the day they come.

Points to remember

  1. Most holy“Every male among the priests shall eat thereof: it shall be eaten in the holy place: it is most holy” 7:6The trespass offering was killed where the burnt offering was, and eaten by priests in the holy place.
  2. The priest's share“have I taken of the children of Israel… and have given them unto Aaron the priest” 7:34The LORD fed his priests from the altar. He still looks after those who serve him.
  3. Thanksgiving“he shall not leave any of it until the morning” 7:15Give thanks the day it is due, and eat it with others.
  4. The third day“on the third day shall be burnt with fire” 7:17Left too long, the meal could not be accepted. Don't carry guilt over to the third day.
  5. Fat and blood“Ye shall eat no manner of fat… Moreover ye shall eat no manner of blood” 7:23, 26The fat was the LORD's best, and the blood was the life, given for atonement.
  6. At Sinai“the LORD commanded Moses in mount Sinai” 7:38Six offerings, one law, given at the foot of the mountain.

Question 1 of 6

Most holy

Where was the trespass offering killed, and who was allowed to eat it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It was killed in the same place as the burnt offering, and every male among the priests ate it in the holy place, because it is “most holy.”

Question 2 of 6

The priest's share

What part of the offerings belonged to the priests?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The skin of the burnt offering, the meat offerings (shared equally among the sons of Aaron), and the wave breast and heave shoulder of the peace offerings, “by a statute for ever.”

Question 3 of 6

Thanksgiving

What did a man bring with his peace offering to give thanks, and when did it have to be eaten?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Unleavened cakes and wafers, and also leavened bread, with the sacrifice of thanksgiving. It was eaten the same day, and none of it left until the morning.

Question 4 of 6

The third day

What became of peace-offering flesh that was left until the third day?

What verses tell us about this?

“Saying, Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered. Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin.”

Romans 4:7–8

Answer

It was burnt with fire. If any was eaten on the third day, the offering was not accepted. In Christ it is the other way round: “Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin.”

Question 5 of 6

Fat and blood

What two things were the children of Israel never to eat?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.”

Leviticus 17:11

Answer

Fat and blood. The fat was the LORD's, offered to him by fire. “The life of the flesh is in the blood,” and the LORD had given it upon the altar to make an atonement.

Question 6 of 6

At Sinai

Where did the LORD give these commands, and which offerings did they cover?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In mount Sinai, in the wilderness of Sinai. They covered the burnt offering, the meat offering, the sin offering, the trespass offering, the consecrations and the peace offerings.
