Genesis 6

But Noah found grace

The whole world goes bad, and it breaks God's heart. Judgment is coming. But in the middle of it, one man finds grace, and God tells him to build a way through.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. SinHow bad had things become on the earth before the flood?
  2. God's heartHow did God feel about what man had become?
  3. GraceWhat does it mean that Noah “found grace in the eyes of the LORD”?
  4. Walking with GodWhat kind of man was Noah, and how did he show it?
  5. WarningDoes God warn before He judges?
  6. SalvationHow does the ark point to Jesus?

Part one

Whatever they chose

Verses 1–4

The world fills up with people, and the line between God's way and man's way gets rubbed out.

Who were “the sons of God”?

Bible teachers read this two main ways. Both take the Bible seriously.

Seth's line

The men of the godly line of Seth (chapter 5) married women from the ungodly world around them, and the faith was lost in a generation. Jesus said angels “neither marry, nor are given in marriage” (Matthew 22:30).

Fallen angels

In Job, “the sons of God” are angels (Job 1:6). Peter and Jude speak of angels that “sinned” and “kept not their first estate,” now held for judgment (2 Peter 2:4, Jude 6).

Either way, the text is plain about the sin: they saw, they wanted, and they took “of all which they chose.” Appetite was in charge, and nobody asked God. Fair = good-looking.

Not always

Strive here = contend with, plead with, keep working on. God's Spirit had been pulling on men's hearts, and they kept pulling away.

“Yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years.” Many read this as a countdown: 120 more years before the flood. Others read it as a shorter life for man. Either way it is mercy with a limit. God waited, and He said how long.

“Which sometime were disobedient, when once the longsuffering of God waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing, wherein few, that is, eight souls were saved by water.”

1 Peter 3:20

If God is pulling on your heart today, don't treat it as something that will always be there. Answer while He's calling.

Giants and famous men

נְפִלִים said nephilim “giants”; perhaps from a word meaning “to fall”

The same word comes back when Israel's spies saw huge men in Canaan: “there we saw the giants, the sons of Anak” (Numbers 13:33). We aren't told much more, and it's wise not to guess.

Men of renown = famous men, the heroes everybody talked about. Not one of their names is written down. A few verses later the one name God records is Noah's. Famous and faithful are not the same thing.

Part two

It grieved him at his heart

Verses 5–8

God sees what man has become, and it hurts Him. Then one word changes everything: But.

All the way down

  1. the wickedness of manwas great in the earth
  2. every imaginationnot some of them
  3. of the thoughts of his heartnot just what he did
  4. only evilnothing good mixed in
  5. continuallyall day, every day

Imagination = what a man's heart dreams up and plans. God looked past the deeds to the place they started. So does every fall: it starts in the imagination long before the hands do anything.

“The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?”

Jeremiah 17:9

God's broken heart

It repented the LORD = the LORD was sorry, deeply grieved. It doesn't mean God made a mistake or was caught by surprise. He doesn't change the way men do:

“God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: hath he said, and shall he not do it? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good?”

Numbers 23:19
נָחַם said nacham to grieve, to be sorry; and to comfort

It's the same Hebrew word Lamech used when he named Noah: “This same shall comfort us” (5:29). The God who grieves over sin is the God who sends comfort. Our sin isn't nothing to Him. It hurts Him, because He made us and loves us.

But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.

Noah=נח⇄חן=graceIn Hebrew, Noah's name and the word for grace are the same two letters, turned around.

This is the first time the word grace appears in the Bible: favour a man receives but didn't earn. Noah didn't make grace. He found it, already waiting in the eyes of the LORD.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Verse 5 is the worst news in the chapter. Verse 8 begins with But. However bad your yesterday was, grace is bigger.

Part three

Noah walked with God

Verses 9–13

One man going God's way in a world going the other.

Grace first, then the walk

found grace (v. 8)thena just man (v. 9)whowalked with God (v. 9)anddid all God commanded (v. 22)

Just = righteous, right with God. Perfect = whole, blameless, a man of one piece. Not sinless: chapter 9 shows Noah's own fall. In his generations = among the people of his day.

He walked with God, like his great-grandfather Enoch. Notice the order: grace came first, and the walk came out of it. God doesn't wait for you to get your life right before He gives you grace. His grace is what gets you walking.

Warned ahead of time

Corrupt = rotten, ruined. Violence = cruelty, men hurting men. The whole earth was full of it. Yet God told Noah what was coming before it came, and Noah became a preacher to the people around him.

“And spared not the old world, but saved Noah the eighth person, a preacher of righteousness, bringing in the flood upon the world of the ungodly;”

2 Peter 2:5

Jesus said the end of this world will come the same way. People will be living ordinary lives with no thought of God:

“But as the days of Noe were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be. For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, And knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be.”

Matthew 24:37–39

Part four

Make thee an ark

Verses 14–22

God judges sin, and in the same breath He builds a way to be saved.

The size of it

Fifty cubits wide: about 75 feet. At about 18 inches to the cubit, it was longer than a football field and as tall as a four-story building, built by hand.

Old words: gopher wood = a wood we can't name for sure (perhaps cypress); pitch = tar, to seal it; fashion = pattern, design; stories = decks, floors.

Covered, with one door

כָּפַר said kaphar to cover; the word later used for “make an atonement”

“Pitch it … with pitch” is kaphar … kopher. Later, in Leviticus, the same Hebrew word, kaphar, is the one translated make an atonement: sin covered by a sacrifice. The ark was covered inside and out, so the waters of judgment could not get in.

And it had one door. When the flood came, “the LORD shut him in” (7:16). Noah didn't have to hold the door. God did.

“I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”

John 10:9

The first covenant

This is the first time the word covenant appears in the Bible. A covenant is a binding promise. Here God makes it, God sets the terms, and God keeps it.

Verse 17

“I, even I”

God Himself brings the flood. Judgment on sin is real, and it is His.

Verse 18

“But with thee”

In the same breath, a promise: Noah, his wife, his three sons and their wives. Eight souls brought through.

God told Noah to bring his whole family in. A man who walks with God changes things for the people under his roof.

Thus did Noah.

No speeches from Noah in this chapter. Not one word. Just a man doing what God said: the wood, the pitch, the decks, the door, the animals, the food. For years, with no rain yet in sight.

“By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear, prepared an ark to the saving of his house; by the which he condemned the world, and became heir of the righteousness which is by faith.”

Hebrews 11:7

Faith isn't only believing. It's building. You won't finish your ark today. But you can do today's part of what God has told you: the meeting, the phone call to a brother, the honest word, the next right thing.

Old words: fowls = birds; cattle = livestock; creeping thing = small animals that crawl.

The ark and Jesus

Peter reads the ark as a picture of salvation in Christ:

“Which sometime were disobedient, when once the longsuffering of God waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing, wherein few, that is, eight souls were saved by water. The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us (not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God,) by the resurrection of Jesus Christ:”

1 Peter 3:20–21
  1. One arkOnly one way through the flood. “Neither is there salvation in any other” (Acts 4:12).
  2. One door“I am the door” (John 10:9).
  3. CoveredPitched within and without; the judgment fell on the ark, not on those inside.
  4. Shut in“The LORD shut him in.” Kept by God, not by his own grip.

Points to remember

  1. Sin“Only evil continually” 6:5Sin starts in the heart's imagination, and it filled the earth with violence.
  2. God's heart“It grieved him at his heart” 6:6Sin isn't nothing to God. It hurts Him.
  3. Grace“But Noah found grace” 6:8Favour he didn't earn, found in the eyes of the LORD.
  4. Walking with God“Thus did Noah” 6:22Grace first; then a walk that does what God says.
  5. Warning“The longsuffering of God waited” 1 Peter 3:20God warned for years before the flood came.
  6. Salvation“I am the door” John 10:9One ark, one door, covered and shut in by God.

Question 1 of 6

Sin

How bad had things become on the earth before the flood?

What verses tell us about this?

“The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?”

Jeremiah 17:9

Answer

As bad as it gets: every thought of the heart only evil, all the time, and the earth full of violence. It didn't start with the violence; it started in the heart. That's why we can't fix ourselves from the outside in. We need God to deal with the heart.

Question 2 of 6

God's heart

How did God feel about what man had become?

What verses tell us about this?

“And grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption.”

Ephesians 4:30

Answer

Grieved, to the heart. God isn't a cold judge ticking boxes. Sin hurts Him because He made us for Himself. That should sober us, and it should comfort us too: a God who grieves over us is a God who cares what happens to us.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

What does it mean that Noah “found grace in the eyes of the LORD”?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

“Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound. But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound:”

Romans 5:20

Answer

God looked on Noah with favour he hadn't earned. Grace comes before the good life, not after it: verse 8 before verse 9. The same grace is held out to every man today, through faith in Jesus. It's a gift, and it's bigger than the worst thing you've done.

Question 4 of 6

Walking with God

What kind of man was Noah, and how did he show it?

What verses tell us about this?

“By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear, prepared an ark to the saving of his house; by the which he condemned the world, and became heir of the righteousness which is by faith.”

Hebrews 11:7

Answer

A just man, whole-hearted, who walked with God when nobody else did. He showed it by doing what God said, all of it, for years, before he ever saw rain. He wasn't sinless. He was faithful. You can be that man today by doing the next thing God has told you to do.

Question 5 of 6

Warning

Does God warn before He judges?

What verses tell us about this?

“And spared not the old world, but saved Noah the eighth person, a preacher of righteousness, bringing in the flood upon the world of the ungodly;”

2 Peter 2:5

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Yes. God pleaded with men, set a time, and sent Noah to preach while the ark went up. He is patient because He wants every man to come to Him. But patience has an end. The time to answer Him is today, while He is still calling.

Question 6 of 6

Salvation

How does the ark point to Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”

John 10:9

“Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.”

Acts 4:12

Answer

There was one ark and one door, and everyone inside was safe. The judgment fell on the ark, not on them. Jesus is that one way: the judgment for our sin fell on Him, and everyone who comes to God through Him is kept, shut in by God's own hand.
