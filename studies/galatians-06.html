Part one

Restore and carry

Verses 1–5

Exactly what this group is for.

Mend him

“Overtaken” means caught: caught off guard, or caught in the act.

“Restore” is the word for mending a torn net, or setting a broken bone. James and John were “mending their nets” when Jesus called them (Matthew 4:21): same word. You don't throw out a good net because it tore. You sit down and fix it.

How to go get a brother

  1. ✓ye which are spiritual, restore such an oneGo to him. “Spiritual” doesn't mean perfect. It means walking in the Spirit (5:16, 25).Go
  2. ✓in the spirit of meeknessGo gently. Strength under control, not a lecture.Gently
  3. ✓considering thyself, lest thou also be temptedGo humbly. It could have been you. Next time it might be.Humbly

This is exactly what this group is for. If you fell yesterday, the worst thing you can do is hide it. Tell us. And if a brother falls, don't gossip about him and don't write him off. Go get him.

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

Verse 2 and verse 5 use two different Greek words. The “burdens” of verse 2 are crushing weights: a relapse, a death, a court date, a craving at two in the morning. Nobody carries those alone. Pick up the phone. Show up.

The “burden” of verse 5 is a soldier's pack, the gear every man carries for himself: your own choices, your job, your own walk with God. Nobody can carry that for you, and you shouldn't ask them to.

Stop measuring by the next man

Next to him→“at least I'm not as bad as he is”“he deceiveth himself” (v. 3)
Next to him→“I'll never be like him”that's the same mistake, upside down
Before God→“prove his own work”test your own work honestly, and be glad of what God is doing in you

The man who thinks he's too strong to fall won't restore anybody. He'll look down on them. Carry each other's weight; answer for your own walk. Both at once.

Part two

Whatsoever a man soweth

Verses 6–10

The crop always comes up. So sow the right seed, and don't quit.

The crop comes up

“Mocked” means to turn your nose up at someone, as if he'd never notice. A farmer can't plant weed seed and pray for wheat.

Every day you're sowing into one field or the other: what you watch, who you run with, where your money goes. Verse 6 is one kind of sowing too: “communicate” means share, so support the men who teach you the Word.

Grace, and the crop

What grace does

The debt is gone

Confessed sin is forgiven, completely, the moment you bring it to God. No condemnation. He doesn't hold it over you, and you don't have to pay it off.

What can still come up

The crop

Grace forgives the sin, but seeds already planted can still sprout: a record, a damaged body, a wife who doesn't trust you yet, kids who are wary. God is not mocked.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Don't let regret tell you that the bad crop means God hasn't forgiven you. He has. Face the harvest honestly, with Him beside you. And here's the hope: you can start sowing to the Spirit today. That crop comes up too.

In due season

  1. Sowtoday's right choice
  2. Waterkeep at it
  3. Waitnothing showing
  4. Waitstill nothing
  5. Reap“in due season”if we faint not

Every man in recovery knows the waiting part: thirty days clean and the bills are still there, the court date is still coming, and she still won't call. That's the season Paul is talking to. “Faint” means give out, quit. The harvest is God's job; not quitting is yours.

“They that sow in tears shall reap in joy. He that goeth forth and weepeth, bearing precious seed, shall doubtless come again with rejoicing, bringing his sheaves with him.”

Psalm 126:5–6

Verse 10: do good “as we have therefore opportunity.” Today will hand you a few chances. Take one.

Part three

With mine own hand

Verses 11–18

Paul signs off: the cross, a new creature, and grace.

Paul takes the pen

A helper writes as Paul speaks

And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not. As we have therefore opportunity, let us do good unto all men, especially unto them who are of the household of faith.

Then Paul writes himself

Ye see how large a letter I have written unto you with mine own hand.

Paul usually spoke his letters to a helper who wrote them down (Romans 16:22), then added a line in his own hand at the end (2 Thessalonians 3:17). Many Bible teachers take verse 11 to mean see what large letters I write: Paul's own handwriting was big, maybe from his weak eyes (4:15), maybe to make sure they didn't miss this part.

And this part is about the false teachers. They wanted to “make a fair shew in the flesh”: to count circumcised Gentiles like trophies. They didn't even keep the law themselves.

Nothing to boast about but the cross.

The world→“crucified unto me”its applause, its pleasures and its opinion of me are dead to me now
And I→“unto the world”and I'm dead to it: it has nothing left to offer me and no hold on me

“Glory” means boast. The false teachers boasted in numbers. Paul could have boasted in his record (1:14). He boasted in one thing: a Roman cross where Jesus died for him. That's the only boast a forgiven man has, and it's enough.

What counts

  • Circumcisedwhat was done to your body
  • Uncircumcisedwhat wasn't
  • “a new creature”what God has done in you

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Verse 16 blesses everyone who walks by “this rule”: the cross, and a new creature. Bible teachers differ on “the Israel of God”: some take it as Jewish believers in Christ, others as all believers together. Either way, peace and mercy rest on every man who walks this way.

Scars they had seen

στίγματα said STIG-ma-ta brands: the marks burned into a slave to show who owned him

The false teachers wanted to put a mark on the Galatians' flesh. Paul already carried Jesus' marks: scars from whips, rods and stones. Some were made in Galatia itself. At Lystra a mob stoned him and dragged him out for dead (Acts 14:19).

Many of us carry marks too: from fights, needles, prison, the years we threw away. In Christ even those can tell whose you are now, and how far He brought you.

It starts and ends with grace

The first words · Galatians 1:3
“Grace be to you and peace from God the Father, and from our Lord Jesus Christ,”
The last words · Galatians 6:18
“Brethren, the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Amen.”

After six chapters of hard words, the very last thing Paul calls them is “Brethren.” He is still their brother. Every bit of the letter was written to get them back to the grace they started with.

If you've drifted into trying to earn God's love, or you've used grace as an excuse, the way back is the same: grace. It's where the letter starts, and it's where it ends.

Galatians in one breath: Jesus gave Himself for your sins. You're made right with God by faith in Him, not by keeping rules. That makes you a son, not a slave. So stand free, walk with the Spirit, and carry your brothers.

If you fell yesterday, chapter 6 says your brothers should come and get you. If you're tired of trying to be good enough, chapter 3 says you never could be, and Christ already was. Start it again any time you need it.

Points to remember

  1. Restoring“Restore such an one in the spirit of meekness” 6:1When a brother falls, go get him: gently, humbly, knowing it could be you.
  2. Burdens“Bear ye one another’s burdens” 6:2Help carry what crushes your brother. Carry your own pack yourself.
  3. Sowing“Whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap” 6:7Grace forgives the sin; old seed can still come up. Start sowing to the Spirit today.
  4. Weary“In due season we shall reap, if we faint not” 6:9The harvest is God's job. Not quitting is yours.
  5. The cross“God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross” 6:14Not your record, not your numbers: only the cross is worth boasting about.
  6. A new creature“But a new creature” 6:15What counts isn't what was done to your body, but what God has done in you.

Question 1 of 6

Restoring

What should we do when a brother is overtaken in a fault?

What verses tell us about this?

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.”

James 5:16

Answer

Restore him: go to him and help mend what tore. Do it gently, not with a lecture, and humbly, remembering you could fall too. And if you're the one who fell, don't hide it. Confess it to a brother and let him help.

Question 2 of 6

Burdens

Which burdens do we carry together, and which does each man carry himself?

What verses tell us about this?

“For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:10

Answer

We carry together the crushing weights no man can lift alone: a fall, a loss, a crisis. Each man carries his own pack: his own choices and his own walk with God. Both are true at once.

Question 3 of 6

Sowing

If God forgives our sin, why do the consequences still come?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Because God is not mocked: what a man sows comes up. Forgiveness takes away the guilt, completely, but seed already in the ground can still sprout. God walks with us through that harvest, and we can start sowing to the Spirit today.

Question 4 of 6

Weary

What does God promise the man who keeps doing right and sees nothing yet?

What verses tell us about this?

“They that sow in tears shall reap in joy.”

Psalm 126:5

Answer

A harvest, “in due season.” Not on our schedule, but surely. Our part is not to faint: to keep sowing and keep doing good while we wait.

Question 5 of 6

The cross

What was the one thing Paul would boast about?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me.”

Galatians 2:20

Answer

The cross of Jesus Christ. Not his past, his record or his converts. The cross is where Jesus loved him and gave Himself for him, and there the world lost its grip on him.

Question 6 of 6

A new creature

What really counts with God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

Not rituals or marks in the flesh, and not a clean record. What counts is a new creature: a man made new by God through faith in Christ. Old things passed away. That's what happened to you.
