Philippians 1

The letter of joy, written in chains

Paul is locked up in Rome, chained to a guard, and the first thing he writes is thank you. The church he loves best has sent help, and he sends back joy.

The letter to the Philippians

“Paul and Timotheus, the servants of Jesus Christ, to all the saints in Christ Jesus which are at Philippi, with the bishops and deacons”

Written from
Rome, where Paul was under house arrest, chained to a soldier (Acts 28:16)
When
about AD 60 to 62
To whom
the church at Philippi, the first church in Europe, started on Paul's second journey (Acts 16). Epaphroditus carried the letter back.
Why
they had sent Paul a gift and a friend to look after him. He writes to say thank you, to tell them he's all right, and to call them to joy and to one mind.

Where Philippians fits in Paul's life

  1. about AD 50Philippi begins: Lydia and the jailorActs 16
  2. about 50 to 51Gifts follow Paul, again and again4:15–16
  3. about 53 to 57Third journey: back through PhilippiActs 20
  4. about 57 to 60Prison at Caesarea, then the voyage to RomeActs 27–28
  5. about 60 to 62House arrest in Rome: Philippians“my bonds in Christ”
  6. about 62 to 64Paul hoped to be freed, and to visit them1:25–26

About ten years. Paul was beaten and jailed in Philippi itself, and the church that began that night never forgot him. They were the only church that kept sending him money.

Philippians is the warmest of Paul's letters. There's no big argument to win and no church out of control. It's a thank-you note from a man who has every reason to complain, and doesn't.

Part one

Thankful and sure

Verses 1–11

From a prison cell, Paul thanks God, and tells them what he is sure of.

Saints, bishops and deacons

Saints

v. 1

Not a few super-Christians in stained glass. Saint means set apart. Everyone who belongs to Jesus is one, and that includes a man six weeks out of jail.

Bishops

v. 1

The word means overseers: the pastors and elders who watch over a church. Nothing grand, only men who look after people.

Deacons

v. 1

The word means servants. These are the men who do the practical work of caring for the church's people.

Paul calls himself and Timothy “the servants of Jesus Christ”, not bosses. Notice the double address: “in Christ Jesus” and “at Philippi.” You live in two places at once: in a real town with real bills, and in Christ.

The church that began that night

Lydia

Acts 16:14

A businesswoman who sold purple cloth. “Whose heart the Lord opened.” Paul and his friends stayed in her house (Acts 16:15, 40).

A slave girl

Acts 16:16–18

Owned by men who used her for money. Paul set her free from the spirit that held her.

The jailor

Acts 16:27–34

A hard man in a hard job, ready to end his own life. He asked, “What must I do to be saved?” and was baptized that night with his whole household.

“Sirs, what must I do to be saved? And they said, Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house.”

Acts 16:30–31

That's “the first day” of their fellowship (v. 5): a rich woman, a slave and a jailor, in one church. And every time Paul remembers them, he's glad. Think of a person God has put in your life like that.

Look who the verb belongs to: “he which hath begun” and “will perform it.” It's not “you started, so you'd better finish.” God did the beginning, and God does the ending.

The road dips. A man falls on a Tuesday, and the road is still the road. Perform means carry through to the end, and the end is “the day of Jesus Christ”, when He comes.

So what do I do with yesterday?

Be honest about it. The fall was real, and it was sin. But it's not the end of the work God began, and it's not proof He gave up on you. Do what He has already said to do:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Tell Him, tell somebody you trust, and get back up. A believer can't be plucked out of His hand. He's working on you today, and He doesn't quit.

What the good work looks like

  1. love growsmore and more, with knowledge and good sense
  2. you see what matters“approve things that are excellent”
  3. you're straight“sincere and without offence”
  4. fruit“fruits of righteousness”, for God's glory

Approve means test and prove, the way a jeweler tests gold. Growing up in Christ isn't only trying harder. It's learning to tell the real thing from the fake, a skill the old life never taught. And notice that the fruit is “by Jesus Christ”, not by willpower.

Part two

The chain that carried the gospel

Verses 12–18

Paul's life fell apart on paper. He says it went the other way.

Chained to a soldier, and it spread

Paul was chained to a Roman soldier, day and night (Acts 28:16). Guards took turns, so one new man after another was chained to Paul, with nowhere to go, listening to him pray and talk about Jesus.

By the time Paul writes, “all the palace” knows why he's locked up: for Christ. Palace here means the emperor's own guard. The word was out from the barracks to “all other places.”

And the other believers saw him, and got braver: “much more bold to speak the word without fear” (v. 14).

“Fallen out rather unto the furtherance of the gospel.”

Furtherance means moving forward, like a road crew clearing the way. Paul didn't say prison was good. He said God used it. The things that “happened unto me” were things he never chose.

Here's something to do today. Where are you stuck: a hard job, a house with rules, probation, a body that's slow to heal? Some people are watching you there. They'd never listen to a preacher, but they see how you handle this. You may be the only Jesus a certain man at your work ever meets.

Two kinds of preachers

Envy and strife (v. 15–16)

“Supposing to add affliction to my bonds”

These men wanted the spotlight while Paul sat in a cell. They preached the true message, but out of jealousy and rivalry. They hoped it would sting him.

Good will and love (v. 15, 17)

“Knowing that I am set for the defence of the gospel”

These men stood with Paul. They understood he was in prison for a reason, and they carried on his work out of love.

Paul doesn't call the first bunch wonderful. Their motives were wrong, and envy is a sin. But he isn't going to let someone else's pettiness steal his joy: “Christ is preached; and I therein do rejoice.” The message is bigger than the messenger. Envy of another man's success is easy to fall into. Paul wouldn't go there.

Part three

To live is Christ

Verses 19–26

Paul might be put to death. Hear how a man talks when he isn't afraid.

“For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.”

Ask most men what they live for and you'll hear a list: money, women, a high, a name, a family, a comeback. Paul gives one word. Everything else he had was already taken from him, and he still had that.

Notice “Christ shall be magnified in my body.” To magnify something is to make it look as big as it really is. Paul wants his whole life, free or chained, alive or dead, to show people how big Jesus is.

A man who's not afraid

To live (v. 22, 24–26)

“Is Christ”

More time means more work, more fruit, and more joy for the Philippians. For Paul, living is a chance to serve.

To die (v. 21, 23)

“Is gain”

Death means “to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better.” He doesn't see it as a trap door. It's more of the One he already loves.

“I am in a strait betwixt two” means pulled both ways, like a man on a narrow road with a wall on each side. He'd love to go to Christ. He knows the Philippians need him here. So he expects to stay.

What this does and doesn't mean

  • not “I wish I were dead”Paul wasn't running away from life. He was “in a strait” and still chose to stay for others.
  • not “my life doesn't matter”He says staying is “more needful for you.” Your life matters to people who need you.
  • but “Christ is enough, either way”If a man's life rests on Jesus, then neither a bad day nor the last day can take it from him.

Some of us have been in a dark place where we wished it would all stop. If that's you today, don't carry it alone. Tell a friend, your pastor, or a crisis line, today. Paul's hope wasn't an escape hatch. It was that Christ holds a man both here and after.

Part four

As it becometh the gospel

Verses 27–30

Whether Paul comes or not, here's how to live.

πολιτεύεσθε said politeuesthe “live as citizens”

Conversation in old English means way of life, not just talk. The Greek word is about citizenship. Philippi was a Roman colony (Acts 16:12), and its people were proud Roman citizens, living by Rome's ways far from Rome.

Paul says the same of them in Christ: you're citizens of another kingdom, in a foreign town. Act like it. At work, in line, at the dinner table, in your texts: live the way your King would be proud of.

Shoulder to shoulder

Two things in verses 27–28. First, “stand fast in one spirit, with one mind striving together.” They're to be a team, like men pulling one rope. Second, “in nothing terrified by your adversaries.”

A man alone gets picked off. A group that stands together holds. That's why recovery works better in a room than in a head, and why Christians need each other. Don't try to do this by yourself. Show up, today.

Two gifts

The first gift

“To believe on him”

Believing is a gift, not a prize we earned. God offers it to everyone, and whoever believes receives it: saved by grace, through faith (Ephesians 2:8).

The second gift

“To suffer for his sake”

For a Philippian, that could mean losing a job, being mocked, or being pushed around by neighbors. Given means graciously granted. It's an honor to share what He went through.

Be careful: this is suffering “for his sake”. The trouble that comes from our own mess, the consequences of old choices, isn't this. Own those, and take them to Him. But when a man gets laughed at for going to church, or loses a drinking buddy for staying sober, he's in the same conflict Paul was in (v. 30). He's not alone, and it isn't wasted.

Points to remember

  1. The finisher“He which hath begun a good work in you will perform it” 1:6God began it, God finishes it. A bad day doesn't stop the road.
  2. Chains“My bonds in Christ are manifest in all the palace” 1:13A hard place can still be useful. The people near you are watching.
  3. Wrong reasons“Christ is preached; and I therein do rejoice” 1:18Don't let other men's petty motives, or your own envy, steal your joy.
  4. Life and death“For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain” 1:21With Christ, a man has nothing to fear from life or from death.
  5. Worthy“Only let your conversation be as it becometh the gospel of Christ” 1:27Live as a citizen of heaven, and stand with the others.
  6. Given to you“Not only to believe on him, but also to suffer for his sake” 1:29Faith is a gift, and so is the honor of standing with Him.

Question 1 of 6

The finisher

Who started the good work in you, and who will finish it?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

God did. He began the good work, and He will carry it through until the day of Jesus Christ. A man who falls gets up by confessing, and the One who started it hasn't let go.

Question 2 of 6

Chains

What happened to the gospel because Paul was in chains?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It moved forward. The palace guard and many others heard why Paul was in chains, and the other believers grew bolder. God used what looked like a loss.

Question 3 of 6

Wrong reasons

What did Paul say about men who preached Christ for the wrong reasons?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Some preached out of envy and strife, hoping to hurt him. Paul didn't let it rob him. He was glad Christ was being preached, and he rejoiced anyway.

Question 4 of 6

Life and death

What did life and death mean to Paul?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To live was Christ, and to die was gain. Life was a chance to serve and magnify Him. Death meant being with Christ, which is far better. Either way he was safe in Him.

Question 5 of 6

Worthy

How did Paul say believers should live while he was away?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As it becometh the gospel: living as citizens of Christ's kingdom, standing fast in one spirit and one mind, working together, and not terrified by those who oppose them.

Question 6 of 6

Given to you

What two things had been given to the Philippians in Christ's behalf?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To believe on Him, and to suffer for His sake. Both are gifts. Faith is received by grace, and standing with Christ in hard times is a share in what He went through.
