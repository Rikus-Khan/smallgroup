Part one

Before the council

Verses 1–11

A hot word, a room that splits in two, and the Lord standing by in the night.

A hot word, and then “I wist not”

Ananias, the high priest, has Paul struck on the mouth. Paul answers“God shall smite thee, thou whited wall: for sittest thou to judge me after the law, and commandest me to be smitten contrary to the law?”
true, but said hot

Those standing by“Revilest thou God’s high priest?”

Paul“I wist not, brethren, that he was the high priest: for it is written, Thou shalt not speak evil of the ruler of thy people.”
sorry, and Scripture says so

“I wist not” means I did not know. The moment Paul saw he was wrong, he didn't dig in. He quoted the Bible against himself (Exodus 22:28) and backed off.

Even the apostle lost his temper once. The anger isn't the whole story. The quick “I was wrong” is. If you blew up at a boss, a wife, a cop or a brother today, say so before the sun goes down (Ephesians 4:26).

One question splits the room

Sadduceesno resurrection, no angel, no spirit

Paul

Phariseesthey confess both

The council was about seventy men from two rival groups. The Sadducees were the rich priestly families. They held mostly to the five books of Moses and said there is no rising from the dead. The Pharisees said there is. One sentence from Paul, and they were shouting at each other. (The dots are only a picture, not a head count.)

Was it a trick? Not really. Resurrection was the true center of the whole matter. He says it again to Felix: “Touching the resurrection of the dead I am called in question” (24:21). Jesus rose, and that changes everything.

That's your hope, too. If Jesus came out of the grave, then no grave is final, no ruined year, no “it's over for me.” The Pharisees even say it better than they know: “let us not fight against God” (v. 9).

The night the Lord stood by him

  1. Corinth“Be not afraid, but speak”Acts 18:9
  2. Jerusalem“Be of good cheer, Paul”Acts 23:11, our verse
  3. The storm at sea“Fear not, Paul”Acts 27:24

Look at where Paul was: a Roman barracks cell, beaten and nearly torn apart by a mob, with a council that fell into a brawl instead of a verdict. For years he'd wanted to see Rome (Acts 19:21). Now it looked like he'd die in Jerusalem.

And “the Lord stood by him.” Notice what He didn't say. Not “cheer up.” He gave a reason: “so must thou bear witness also at Rome.” God wasn't finished with him. He isn't finished with you either.

“These things I have spoken unto you, that in me ye might have peace. In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.”

John 16:33

Part two

The forty men

Verses 12–22

A plot, an oath, and a young man who hears about it.

More than forty men, one big oath

more than forty men “bound themselves under a curse”, and the chief priests and elders helped

They swore not to eat or drink until Paul was dead, with a curse on themselves if they broke it. The Bible doesn't say how it ended for them. It only tells us the plan fell through.

The chief priests and elders helped. Being religious doesn't make a man safe. Hatred can wear respectable clothes.

Most of us have sworn something big at two in the morning, “I'm done, never again,” in our own strength. A loud vow isn't what holds. “There are many devices in a man’s heart; nevertheless the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand” (Proverbs 19:21). Hold on to Him, not to your promise.

One young man spoke up

Paul's nephew: the only time the Bible mentions Paul's family
a nephewheard the plotPaulsent him toa centurionwho led him toLysias, the chief captainwho sent470 soldiers

Look how ordinary God's rescue was. A young man heard something, walked into a Roman barracks, and told it. An officer was willing to listen. A Roman commander took a boy by the hand and heard him out in private.

And Paul didn't just sit and pray. He used what was in front of him, a centurion, a nephew, a captain. God had just said “Be of good cheer.” That didn't mean Paul stopped using his head.

One thing for today. If something is building against you, or something in you is, tell someone. The nephew told. Secrets are where plots and relapses grow.

Part three

The night ride

Verses 23–35

Four hundred and seventy soldiers, a letter, and a road to Caesarea.

Four hundred and seventy soldiers for one prisoner

200soldiers on foot
+ 70horsemen (“threescore and ten”)
+ 200spearmen
= 470for one man in chains

“Threescore and ten” is old English for seventy. “The third hour of the night” is about nine o'clock at night. And “beasts” just means horses and mules to ride.

Lysias had found out Paul was a Roman citizen (22:25–29), and he knew over forty men were ready to kill him. So he sent a small army. The men who swore to kill Paul were answered by Rome's army, with Paul riding in the middle.

That's how God sometimes works. He doesn't always remove the danger. Sometimes He puts 470 men around you.

A Roman officer's letter

Claudius Lysias unto the most excellent governor Felix sendeth greeting.

Written from
The castle in Jerusalem, the Roman barracks
When
about AD 57
To whom
Felix, the Roman governor, at Caesarea
Why
To hand up a prisoner with a clean report: “nothing… worthy of death or of bonds”

A Roman officer, who didn't believe in Jesus at all, wrote the first official finding on Paul: innocent. Not worthy of death, not even of chains.

Read verse 27 closely, though. Lysias says he rescued Paul “having understood that he was a Roman.” In fact he had Paul tied up to be flogged and found out he was a Roman after that (22:24–29). The letter makes Lysias look good. People polish their stories.

And God still used it. He can work through a self-serving letter and a mixed-up commander. He doesn't wait for a spotless man to carry out His plan. That's good news for the rest of us.

The night ride to Caesarea

They rode out about nine at night, from Jerusalem to Antipatris, a city Herod the Great built and named for his father Antipater. It is some 35 to 40 miles, most of it through the night.

Next day the foot soldiers turned back, and the horsemen took Paul the rest of the way to Caesarea, the Roman capital of Judaea on the coast, about 60 miles in all. The ambush country was behind them.

Felix asked what province Paul came from. “Cilicia”, Tarsus, his hometown. He was held in “Herod’s judgment hall,” the governor's headquarters, until his accusers came. He wasn't free, but he was alive, and a harbor town is a step toward Rome.

Rome, but not the way he planned

Paul's plan

“I must also see Rome”

“After I have been there, I must also see Rome” (Acts 19:21). He pictured it as a free man, on a ship, with friends.

God's way

Chains, and still Rome

Arrested, beaten, a mob, a plot, 470 soldiers, two years in a cell, a storm and a shipwreck. And Paul ends up in Rome after all.

If your life took a detour, prison, a hospital, a lost decade, God hasn't lost your map. The detour can be part of the road. “So must thou”: what He promised, He does.

Points to remember

  1. Courage“Be of good cheer, Paul” 23:11The Lord stands by you in the worst night, and He has a next chapter for you.
  2. Hope“Of the hope and resurrection of the dead I am called in question” 23:6Jesus rose, so no grave and no ruined past is final.
  3. Temper“I wist not, brethren” 23:5Anger happens. A quick “I was wrong” and Scripture's correction is what you do next. Say it today.
  4. Plots“We have bound ourselves under a great curse” 23:14Forty men's oath couldn't stop God's plan. Hold to Him, not your own promise.
  5. Protection“Paul’s sister’s son heard of their lying in wait” 23:16God often uses ordinary people. Be the one who speaks up, and tell someone today.
  6. Conscience“I have lived in all good conscience before God until this day” 23:1A clean conscience isn't a spotless record. It's honesty and forgiveness, because Christ came to save sinners.

Question 1 of 6

Courage

What did the Lord say to Paul the night after the riot?

What verses tell us about this?

“But straightway Jesus spake unto them, saying, Be of good cheer; it is I; be not afraid.”

Matthew 14:27

“These things I have spoken unto you, that in me ye might have peace. In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.”

John 16:33

Answer

“Be of good cheer, Paul.” It wasn't just “cheer up.” He gave a reason: Paul would bear witness in Rome. Jesus says the same kind of thing to every follower in a storm. He stands by you, and He isn't done with you.

Question 2 of 6

Hope

What did Paul say he was on trial for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“The hope and resurrection of the dead.” That one sentence split the council: the Sadducees said there is no resurrection, and the Pharisees said there is. It is the heart of the gospel, because Jesus rose, and anyone who trusts Him has a hope that the grave can't end.

Question 3 of 6

Temper

What did Paul do after he spoke sharply to the high priest?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath:”

Ephesians 4:26

Answer

He backed down at once. When he saw it was the high priest, he said he hadn't known, and he quoted Scripture against his own words. Anger happens, even to an apostle. What counts is how fast you say “I was wrong.”

Question 4 of 6

Plots

Can men's oaths and plots stop what God has planned?

What verses tell us about this?

“There are many devices in a man’s heart; nevertheless the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand.”

Proverbs 19:21

Answer

No. More than forty men swore to kill Paul, and the Lord had already said he would reach Rome. The plot fell apart because a young man spoke up and Rome sent an army. What God has planned will stand.

Question 5 of 6

Protection

Who did God use to keep Paul safe?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Ordinary people: a young nephew who told what he heard, a centurion who listened, and a Roman commander who took it seriously and sent soldiers. Paul used the help God put in front of him. God still uses ordinary people, and sometimes that's you.

Question 6 of 6

Conscience

How could Paul say he had lived “in all good conscience before God”?

What verses tell us about this?

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

“How much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?”

Hebrews 9:14

Answer

Not because he was perfect. Paul called himself the chief of sinners. His conscience was clean because he'd been forgiven by the blood of Christ and was living honestly before God from then on. That's available to every man here, whatever he did yesterday.
