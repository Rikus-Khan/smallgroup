Paul wrote this letter to Timothy, the younger man he trusted most, whom he had left at Ephesus to steady a church with false teachers in it. The first three chapters were about the church. From chapter 4, Paul talks to Timothy the man: how he trains, how he treats people, and what he does about money.

Read alone, chapter 4 still makes sense. It is an old man's advice to a young one, and every word of it fits a man who is new in the faith.

Timothy, left behind at Ephesus

About AD 62 to 64, Paul was free again. He went on to Macedonia and left Timothy at Ephesus with one job: “that thou mightest charge some that they teach no other doctrine” (1:3).

Ephesus was the big city of Asia. Timothy had known Paul since Lystra, as a young man (Acts 16:1–3). Now he was the one in charge, and some of the older men weren't thrilled about taking orders from him.

That is why this chapter is full of youth, example, and take heed.

Part one

A warning about drifting

Verses 1–5

Some will walk away from the faith. Here is how it starts.

Nobody leaves in one jump

“The latter times” means the stretch of history we are living in now, between Jesus' first coming and His second. Paul says some who were in the faith depart. The word means to stand away from: you step off a little at a time.

If you've already drifted, hear this: the line turns around. A man who steps off can step back on, and the road back starts with one honest word to God and one call to a brother.

A seared conscience

A live conscience

It stings, and that's good

Like skin on your hand: you feel the hot pan and pull away. Guilt after a lie is the alarm doing its job.

A seared one

Burned until it can't feel

“Seared with a hot iron” is a brand: scar tissue, numb on purpose. The man says one thing and does another (“speaking lies in hypocrisy”) until he stops noticing.

Most of us know what it is to do something over and over until it stopped bothering us. If your conscience bothers you today, don't drown it. Take it to God. That sting is a gift, and “he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” (1 John 1:9).

Rules that look holy

The false teachers in Ephesus had a bigger list than God's. No marriage. No certain foods. It looked serious, and some men were proud of how much they gave up.

A rule to look holy

“Don't touch it, or God won't love you”

It makes the man proud, or it makes him feel like a failure on the first slip. Nothing in it saves anyone.

A boundary to stay free

“I leave it alone because it hurts me”

That is wisdom, not a way to earn anything. It is allowed, and it is often smart.

Paul isn't against drawing a line for yourself. He is against telling people the line makes you holy, or that God made good gifts bad. A man whose life was wrecked by a drink or a drug can leave it alone for good and be thankful for the freedom. Just don't call it your salvation. Jesus is that.

Received with thanksgiving

“Every creature of God” means everything God made, food and marriage among them. Marriage and a good meal are His gifts. The way to use a gift is to receive it with thanksgiving, and to say thanks over it, “sanctified by the word of God and prayer.”

A man who has had a rough life can find it hard to receive anything good. Today: eat a meal and say grace. It may be the first time you've thanked God for a plain, good thing.

Part two

Train for godliness

Verses 6–10

A body trains for years. What about the rest of you?

Spiritual reps

Paul says “exercise thyself”. His word is gumnazo, where we get gym. Godliness isn't a mood. It is trained like a muscle:

  1. Open the Bookone chapter, five minutes
  2. Pray out loudplain words, to a Father who listens
  3. Text a brotherone honest line about where you are
  4. Say no to one thingone small refusal, early in the day
  5. Show upat work, at the meeting, at home

Reps, not results

MonTueWedThuFriSatSun Open the Book Pray out loud Text a brother Say no to one thing Show up

Look at the missed dot. Nobody's thrown out of the gym for missing a day. The next rep is just rep one again, and the muscle is still there.

Get this one clear: the training doesn't earn God's love. You were saved by grace, not by workouts. The training is how a saved man grows, like an athlete who already made the team.

“Profiteth little” doesn't say a healthy body is bad. A man who's quit the bottle needs to eat, sleep and move. But the body gets only a short stretch of the road. Godliness has “promise of the life that now is, and of that which is to come.”

Notice it pays now too: a clearer head, a steadier home, a man people can trust. It isn't only pie in the sky.

The Saviour of all men

Paul isn't saying everyone is automatically saved. Look at the end of the verse: “specially of those that believe.”

God is the Saviour of all men in this sense: the offer is open to every man, with no exceptions and no one who's too far gone. He is the Saviour specially of those who believe, because they are the ones who take it.

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

John 3:16

Whosoever means you

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

No list of who qualifies. No test of how bad you were. If you're in the inner circle, you're kept. If you're still in the outer one, the door is open today.

And why does Paul “labour and suffer reproach” (v. 10)? “Because we trust in the living God.” A dead idol can't save anybody. The living God can, and He is worth suffering for.

Part three

Be an example

Verses 11–16

You don't have to wait to be old before you can show somebody the way.

Too young? Too new?

Timothy was probably in his thirties, young to lead older men. Some looked down on him. Paul does not tell him to demand respect. He says earn it, by example.

For a man new in the faith, “youth” fits another way. You may be forty and two months old in Christ. Some will look at your past and your record and decide you've got nothing to teach.

Don't argue. Be an example. A man two weeks sober and saved is a living example to the man who is two days in. You are ahead of someone. Start there.

Six places to be an example

Paul names six. The old words need a translation.

1

Word

What you say: no filthy talk, no lies, no tearing people down.

2

Conversation

In the KJV it means your whole way of life, how you live, not just talk.

3

Charity

Love in action: how you treat the man who can do nothing for you.

4

Spirit

Your attitude and drive: the spirit you bring to a bad day.

5

Faith

Being someone people can count on: faithful, keeps his word.

6

Purity

Clean in what you look at, what you think, and what you do with a woman.

Pick the one you're weakest in, and do one thing about it today. Somebody is watching, even if you don't know it.

Three habits, and one gift

  1. 1Readingreading the Scriptures aloud: most people then couldn't read, so someone had to read it to them
  2. 2Exhortationencouraging a brother to keep going, like a coach on the sideline
  3. 3Doctrineteaching: the plain facts of who God is and what Jesus did

“Neglect not the gift that is in thee.” Every believer has the Spirit and a gift to use for the others. “The laying on of the hands of the presbytery” was the elders setting Timothy apart for his work, and praying over him. It didn't give him something he lacked. It was the church saying: go, we are with you.

A gift you don't use rusts. What are you good at? Cooking, driving, listening, fixing things? Use it for the men in this group.

Watch two things

Thyself

Your life

What you do when nobody is looking. Doctrine without a life behind it is just talk.

The doctrine

What you believe

The plain truth about Jesus. A good life with a wrong belief ends in the ditch too.

“Continue in them.” Keep at it. And what about “thou shalt both save thyself, and them that hear thee”? No man earns his way to heaven. Jesus did that. Paul means keeping yourself on the true road, and the men who listen to you will follow it too.

Your one thing for today: take heed. Look hard at yourself for five minutes, and tell God one honest thing you saw.

Points to remember

  1. Warning“Some shall depart from the faith” 4:1Drift is slow and quiet. If you've stepped off, step back on, today.
  2. Training“Exercise thyself rather unto godliness” 4:7Small reps, every day. A missed day is not the end: do the next rep.
  3. The Saviour“The Saviour of all men, specially of those that believe” 4:10The offer is open to every man. Believers are the ones who take it.
  4. Example“Be thou an example of the believers” 4:12You can be an example now, to someone who is a few steps behind you.
  5. Heed“Take heed unto thyself, and unto the doctrine” 4:16Watch your life and your belief, together, and keep going.

Question 1 of 5

Warning

What did the Spirit say would happen in the latter times?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Some will depart from the faith, listening to lies and men with numb consciences, and adding rules God never gave. We guard against it by receiving God's gifts with thanks, and by staying close to Him and to each other.

Question 2 of 5

Training

What is worth training for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Godliness. A strong body pays a little, for a little while. Godliness pays now and forever. Train it the way you'd train a muscle: a little every day.

Question 3 of 5

The Saviour

Whom is God the Saviour of?

What verses tell us about this?

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

John 3:16

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

All men, in that His offer is open to everyone, and specially those that believe, because they are the ones who receive it. Whosoever calls on the Lord will be saved.

Question 4 of 5

Example

What can a young man be an example in?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In word, in conversation (his whole way of life), in charity, in spirit, in faith, and in purity. A man young in years or young in the faith can start being an example today.

Question 5 of 5

Heed

What must Timothy take heed to?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Himself and the doctrine: his life and his belief, side by side. He is to keep at both, and not neglect the gift God gave him.
