Exodus 8

Frogs, lice and flies: “To morrow”

Three more plagues. Pharaoh begs for relief, promises the world, and hardens his heart the moment the trouble stops. The magicians say, “This is the finger of God.” And the LORD makes a difference between Goshen and Egypt.

Part one

“I will smite all thy borders with frogs”

Verses 1–15

The second plague, and the first time Pharaoh begs.

Frogs in the bedchamber

  • bordersthe edges of the country: all of it
  • bedchamberbedroom
  • ovensthe baking ovens of the house
  • kneadingtroughsthe bowls where dough was mixed and left to rise

The God of the whole land has a simple message: Let my people go, that they may serve me. Plague after plague opens with this same word. This one is not about punishing for its own sake: it is a warning with a way out.

A frog is harmless. A thousand frogs in your bed, your oven and your bread bowl is not. There is no room in the house where a man can rest. Egypt had a frog-headed goddess, Heqet, linked with birth and the river, and teachers often note that here the frogs she was said to bless became the curse.

They could add frogs

Again the magicians copy the sign. Look at what they copy: more frogs. More of the thing that is already everywhere.

The magicians

Could make more

They brought up frogs too. Nothing in the text says they took a single frog away.

Pharaoh

Needs them gone

And notice who he calls for next. Not his own men. Verse 8: he sends for Moses and Aaron.

When the frogs are in the oven, a man finds out which of his helpers can actually help. A trick that adds to the mess is no help at all.

“To morrow”

PharaohIntreat the LORD, that he may take away the frogs from me, and from my people; and I will let the people go, that they may do sacrifice unto the LORD.

MosesGlory over me: when shall I intreat for thee, and for thy servants, and for thy people, to destroy the frogs from thee and thy houses, that they may remain in the river only?

PharaohTo morrow.

Pharaoh could have said now. He said to morrow. One more night with the frogs.

Why “to morrow”?

Intreat means to plead, to pray. Glory over me was a courtesy: Moses lets Pharaoh name the time, so that Pharaoh can see afterward that it was the LORD's doing and not chance.

Pharaoh named the next day. Maybe he hoped the frogs would go by themselves. Maybe he was not quite ready to be rid of them, because being rid of them meant keeping his word.

Men say it about real things. I'll stop after the weekend. I'll tell her after Monday. I'll deal with it once things settle.

Not to morrow. To day.

“Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth.”

Proverbs 27:1

“…behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.”

2 Corinthians 6:2

Pharaoh did not mean it as an insult. He probably thought he was being reasonable. That is the trap: to morrow always sounds reasonable. And it is the easiest word in the world to say.

Today, one thing: the call you have been putting off, the confession, the first step. Not tomorrow. Today, before you go to work. And if you do not know how to begin, text a brother in Christ and say, “I need help with something.”

“He hardened his heart”

Respite is a breathing space, relief after pressure. The moment the pressure lifted, Pharaoh's promise was gone. And here the Bible says plainly who did the hardening:

He hardened his heart. The writer does not say “the LORD hardened.” He says Pharaoh did it, when the pain eased. God had said it would happen (as the LORD had said), but the hand on the heart here is Pharaoh's own.

In the strip from the first slide, plague two is marked Pharaoh hardened it himself. Keep watching that line.

The loop

Trouble, a cry for help, a big promise, relief, and then the promise is forgotten. Pharaoh runs this circle again and again. It is not that his words are all lies. In the moment he means them. They just do not last longer than the pain.

Any man who has said God, if You get me out of this, I will never do it again knows this loop from the inside. The relief comes, and the old heart is still there.

The way out is to deal with the heart while the relief is coming, not after. Tell a brother during the pain, and keep telling him when it stops.

“The land stank”

The LORD did what Moses asked. The frogs died, out of the houses, the villages and the fields. And what was left?

The plague was gone. The mess was not. Egypt had to gather the dead frogs up in heaps and live with the smell.

That is true of sin and its trouble too. When God forgives, the guilt is gone, but there may be heaps to clear: a debt, a record, a marriage to rebuild, a trust to earn back. Forgiven men still have clean-up to do. They do it with God beside them, one heap at a time, not hiding in the house.

Part two

“This is the finger of God”

Verses 16–19

Lice out of the dust, with no warning at all, and the magicians give up.

Out of the dust, with no warning

Look at what is missing. For the river and the frogs, God sent Moses to Pharaoh first. Not this time: no warning at all. Plagues three, six and nine come with none.

The Hebrew word can mean lice or tiny biting gnats. Either way it came out of the dust itself, on man and beast, throughout all the land. There was nowhere to be clean. The same dust that Egypt walked on turned against it.

And no frogs to scrape up, no water to dig for. Just an itching that went with a man everywhere he went. Not a great disaster, but a small one that never lets up. Many a man has been brought low by something small he could not get away from.

“They could not”

For the first time the magicians try, and fail. They are out of tricks. And they say to Pharaoh, plainly:

Those are Pharaoh's own experts. They have seen the rod, the blood and the frogs, and now they tell the king: this is not a trick. This is God. And what does Pharaoh do with that?

Knowing is not the same as obeying. He had it from his own advisers and still would not listen.

The finger of God, in Jesus' mouth

“But if I with the finger of God cast out devils, no doubt the kingdom of God is come upon you.”

Luke 11:20

Jesus used the same words. When He freed people from demons, He said it was the same power breaking in: the finger of God, and the kingdom with it.

Egypt's magicians knew God when they saw Him, and could not make themselves obey. The question for every man is not whether he can see it. It is what he does next.

Part three

“A division between my people and thy people”

Verses 20–32

Flies in Egypt and none in Goshen, and a king who starts to bargain.

Goshen, set apart

Goshen was the green pasture land in the east of the delta where Jacob's family had settled (Genesis 47). The Israelites and the Egyptians lived in the same country, and until now the story has not said that Israel was spared.

Sever means to cut apart. This time the LORD draws a line: flies for Egypt, none in Goshen. A division between my people and thy people.

The reason is not that Israel was better. They were slaves, tired and complaining (5:21). The line was drawn because they were His, by the promise He made to Abraham. The word is my people.

“The Lord knoweth them that are his”

The LORD says the reason for the division is so that Pharaoh may know that I am the LORD in the midst of the earth. He wants Egypt to see: this God knows who belongs to Him.

“Nevertheless the foundation of God standeth sure, having this seal, The Lord knoweth them that are his. And, Let every one that nameth the name of Christ depart from iniquity.”

2 Timothy 2:19

If you belong to Jesus, there is a line around you, and He drew it, not you. It does not mean no trouble. It means you are known. And the second half of that verse is the way of life that goes with it: if you name His name, turn from what is wrong.

The swarm, and “not meet”

The Hebrew says only a swarm. The KJV supplies of flies to make the sense plain. It came into Pharaoh's house, into his servants' houses and across the land. Corrupted here means ruined.

Pharaoh gives in a little: Go ye, sacrifice to your God in the land. Stay in Egypt and worship there. Moses says no: It is not meet so to do. Meet means fitting, proper.

Here is the reason. What Israel would sacrifice was the abomination of the Egyptians: something Egypt found detestable. Many teachers think it means animals Egypt counted sacred. Worship in Egypt would have started a riot: will they not stone us?

So Moses repeats what God had said at the bush: a three days' journey into the wilderness, away from Egypt, to worship as the LORD commands (3:18).

Pharaoh's offers

Pharaoh's offers

Keep Egypt close

Verse 25, “sacrifice in the land”: keep your religion, but keep it here in Egypt, where I can see you. Verse 28, “not very far”: all right, go, but stay in reach, so you can be called back.

The LORD

“Three days into the wilderness”

A real break from the old place. Out of Egypt, to serve Him as He says.

Every man here knows these offers. Be a Christian, but keep the old life close. Keep the old numbers on your phone, the old friends, the old places, just in case. Only not very far. A man who keeps Egypt in reach will be back there.

There is more of this to come (10:8–11, 24). Pharaoh does not give up. But God does not take the half-deal. Worship in the wilderness, away from the old master. If you are serious, get distance. Change the number, change the route home, change the company.

“At this time also”

Moses prays, and the flies go, there remained not one. Moses had warned him: let not Pharaoh deal deceitfully any more. Deceitfully means falsely, with a promise he does not mean to keep.

At this time also: so it has happened before, and he knew it. He asked for prayer, intreat for me, and he took the prayer, and did not take the God who answered it. He wanted the relief without the surrender.

Plague four, and again the strip says: Pharaoh hardened it himself. Twice now, in his own words and deeds.

One “not yet” at a time

  1. “To morrow”one more night (8:10)
  2. relief comesand the heart hardens (8:15)
  3. “the finger of God”and still no (8:19)
  4. “sacrifice in the land”a half-obedience (8:25)
  5. “not very far”keep Egypt close (8:28)
  6. “at this time also”hardened again (8:32)

Notice: not one of these steps is a big dramatic evil. Each is a small, reasonable delay. That is how a heart hardens: not in a day, but one not yet at a time.

And that is how it softens: one yes at a time, said to God and to a brother. Real world: name the “not yet” you are carrying, and say it out loud to someone today.

The door is still open

It would be easy to read this chapter and think, that man is lost. But look at what God keeps doing. He sends Moses back again, with a plain word, and answers the prayer Moses prays for Pharaoh, both times he is asked.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

If you have said to morrow too many times, the answer is not to give up. It is to say today. The same God who answered Moses answers you, and He is not tired of you.

Points to remember

  1. The frogs“the magicians did so with their enchantments, and brought up frogs” 8:7They could add to the trouble. They could not take any of it away.
  2. To morrow“And he said, To morrow.” 8:10“To morrow” always sounds reasonable. Today is the day.
  3. A hard heart“when Pharaoh saw that there was respite, he hardened his heart” 8:15Pharaoh hardened his own heart the moment the pain stopped. Deal with the heart while the relief comes.
  4. The finger of God“This is the finger of God” 8:19Even his own experts told him it was God. Knowing is not the same as obeying.
  5. A division“I will put a division between my people and thy people” 8:23The LORD knows who are His. The line is drawn by His promise.
  6. A compromise“only ye shall not go very far away” 8:28“Not very far” keeps Egypt in reach. Get real distance from the old life.

Question 1 of 6

The frogs

What did the frogs do, and what could the magicians do about them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The frogs went into the houses, the beds, the ovens and the kneadingtroughs, and covered the land. The magicians could only bring up more frogs. They could not remove one, so Pharaoh had to send for Moses.

Question 2 of 6

To morrow

When Moses asked Pharaoh when to pray the frogs away, what did Pharaoh answer?

What verses tell us about this?

“Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth.”

Proverbs 27:1

Answer

“To morrow.” He could have said now, and chose one more night of frogs. A man can say “to morrow” about anything God has put in front of him. The Bible's answer is today.

Question 3 of 6

A hard heart

What did Pharaoh do when he saw that there was respite?

What verses tell us about this?

“But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

Hebrews 3:13

Answer

He hardened his own heart and broke his promise. Here the text plainly says he did it himself. Relief is the dangerous moment, when the pressure lifts and the promises fade. Keep talking to your brothers when it is easy.

Question 4 of 6

The finger of God

What did the magicians admit when they could not make lice?

What verses tell us about this?

“But if I with the finger of God cast out devils, no doubt the kingdom of God is come upon you.”

Luke 11:20

Answer

“This is the finger of God.” Pharaoh's own experts told him it was God's power, and he still would not listen. Knowing the truth is not enough: it has to be obeyed.

Question 5 of 6

A division

What difference did the LORD make between Goshen and the rest of Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

“Nevertheless the foundation of God standeth sure, having this seal, The Lord knoweth them that are his. And, Let every one that nameth the name of Christ depart from iniquity.”

2 Timothy 2:19

Answer

No flies in Goshen, where His people lived, and swarms everywhere else. The division was made by God, because they were His. The Lord knows those who are His.

Question 6 of 6

A compromise

What did Pharaoh offer Moses instead of letting Israel go?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

First, “sacrifice in the land,” staying in Egypt. Then, “only not very far.” Moses held to the three days' journey God had commanded. A half-leaving keeps the old life in reach, so make a clean break.

Looking ahead: Exodus 9

Pharaoh has now refused four times. The next plagues are heavier, and they come with a new line: God starts to speak about the heart.

In chapter 9 there is a plague on the cattle, and a plague of boils, and a hail like none before. And it is the first time the LORD is named as the one who hardened it (9:12). We will look at what came before it.
