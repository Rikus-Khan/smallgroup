Two kinds of sorrow

Where we are in 2 Corinthians

About AD 56, Paul writes from Macedonia to the church he started in Corinth. It had been a rough year. He sent one letter, then a harder one, and then waited for news.

1–2Comfort in trouble
3–4The new covenant
5Reconciled to God
6Open hearts
7Titus brings news
8–9The collection
10–13Paul answers his critics

This chapter is the turning point. Paul has been sick with worry that his hard letter had hurt the church for good. Then Titus arrives with news. Read alone, it still makes sense: a man is sorry, a friend brings good news, and Paul explains what real sorrow looks like.

Part one

Clean, and comforted

Verses 1–7

The promises come first. Then, a worn-out man and a friend at the door.

The promises come first

Havingthese promises: “I will receive you”
→
thereforelet us cleanse ourselves

“Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you, And will be a Father unto you, and ye shall be my sons and daughters, saith the Lord Almighty.”

2 Corinthians 6:17–18

Look at the order. God has already said, “I will receive you.” The cleaning isn't the price of getting in. It's what a son does once he knows he's in. You aren't scrubbing yourself to earn a Father. You have one, so you clean house.

“Flesh and spirit” covers both what you do and what you hold on to inside: the habit, and the resentment. Real life today might mean getting rid of a number in your phone, a stash, a site, a grudge. And “perfecting” means finishing a job that's still being done. You are not behind.

No rest in Macedonia

Paul left Ephesus for Troas, where a door for the gospel stood open, and he still “had no rest in my spirit, because I found not Titus my brother” (2:13). So he crossed into Macedonia, where Titus was to meet him with news from Corinth.

And Titus came, up the road from Corinth. “Without were fightings, within were fears.” Paul doesn't dress it up: enemies outside, panic inside. A man of God, and he was a wreck.

Where did the comfort come from? “God, that comforteth those that are cast down”, and He did it through a friend walking in the door. If you're alone and afraid right now, call somebody. God often does His comforting with a man's voice.

Part two

Two kinds of sorrow

Verses 8–11

The same pain can lead to life or to death. What decides it?

Two roads out of the same sorrow

The sorrow of the world. It's about me: what a loser, what will people think. It ends in hiding and numbing, and the numbing is the next drink. Verse 10 calls the end of that road “death.”

Godly sorrow. It's about what I did, to God and to people, and it runs toward Him. It ends in “salvation,” and in a turning you'll never have to take back.

Same night, same failure, two men

Judas

Sorry, and alone

“I have sinned in that I have betrayed the innocent blood.” He even confessed, to the wrong people. “See thou to that,” they said. He took his sorrow to men who had nothing for him, and it ended in the dark (Matthew 27:3–5).

Peter

Sorry, and turned

“And Peter went out, and wept bitterly.” He'd denied Jesus three times. But he stayed among the disciples, and on the shore Jesus asked him three times, “lovest thou me?”, once for each denial, and handed him his work back (John 21:15–17).

Both men were truly sorry. Being sorry isn't the test, because feeling bad is cheap. The test is where the sorrow goes. Judas carried his to the people who couldn't help him and never to the One who could. Peter carried his back to Jesus.

If your thoughts have gone to Judas's end, don't carry that alone. Call or text your brothers in Christ today, and your pastor. You are one of many men fighting the same enemy, and they need you as much as you need them. That voice saying you're finished is the sorrow of the world. It isn't God's.

What godly sorrow made in Corinth

Paul lists seven things (7:11). That's how you know it was the real kind: it did something.

  1. CarefulnessTaking it seriously, not shrugging it off
  2. Clearing of yourselvesSetting the record straight, honestly
  3. IndignationAnger at the sin, not at yourself
  4. FearRespect for God, enough to stay away from it
  5. Vehement desireA longing to be right with them again
  6. ZealEnergy to do the right thing now
  7. RevengeSeeing it put right. Not getting even

Notice what's not on the list: beating yourself up for a month. Self-hatred feels like repentance, but it changes nothing. These seven are all things you do. Pick one to do today.

Part three

Why the hard letter

Verses 12–16

Hard words, written in love, and the joy that followed.

Hard words, soft heart.

Paul wasn't settling a score. He says why he wrote: “that our care for you in the sight of God might appear unto you” (v. 12). The letter that made them sorry for a while was love in a hard form. He even admits he nearly regretted sending it (v. 8).

So when a brother tells you the truth, and it stings, ask whether he's against you or for you. The man who only says what you want to hear is no friend. And if you're the one who has to say a hard thing, do it the way Paul did, so that the care shows.

And the ending is joy. “I rejoice therefore that I have confidence in you in all things.” The same Paul who wrote the hard letter now says, I trust you. That is where godly sorrow goes: back into the family, trusted again.

Something to do with your sorrow today

  1. 1. Name itSay plainly what you didno excuses, no “but”
  2. 2. Tell HimConfess it to GodHe said “I will receive you”
  3. 3. Tell a brotherSay it to one man you trustout loud, today
  4. 4. Make it rightOne thing you can do about ita call, a repayment, an apology

That's the whole road up. None of it is you paying. It's you turning, with God already facing you. And a repentance like that “not to be repented of” means you'll never look back and wish you hadn't.

Points to remember

  1. Clean“Having therefore these promises… let us cleanse ourselves” 7:1God receives you first. Cleaning up is what a son does, not what buys the door.
  2. Comfort“God, that comforteth those that are cast down, comforted us by the coming of Titus” 7:6God comforted a worn-out Paul with a friend. Let someone in.
  3. Two sorrows“Godly sorrow worketh repentance… but the sorrow of the world worketh death” 7:10Same pain, two roads. One runs to Jesus and life. One runs inward, to hiding.
  4. Turning“What carefulness it wrought in you” 7:11Real sorrow does something: sets it right, wants to be restored, goes to work.
  5. Hard words“That our care for you in the sight of God might appear unto you” 7:12A hard word from a friend is love. Hear it, and speak it that way.
  6. Joy“I rejoice therefore that I have confidence in you in all things” 7:16A turned-around man gets trusted again. Today isn't the end of your story.

Question 1 of 6

Clean

What does Paul ask us to clean up, and what comes first?

What verses tell us about this?

“I will receive you, And will be a Father unto you, and ye shall be my sons and daughters, saith the Lord Almighty.”

2 Corinthians 6:17–18

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

Answer

Everything that dirties the flesh and the spirit: what you do and what you hold inside. But the promise comes first: God has already said He will receive you and be your Father. So you clean house as a son, not to earn the door.

Question 2 of 6

Where did Paul find comfort when he was worn out?

What verses tell us about this?

“I had no rest in my spirit, because I found not Titus my brother.”

2 Corinthians 2:13

Answer

From God, and God sent it by a friend: Titus walked in with good news. When you're cast down, don't sit alone with it. Call somebody today.

Question 3 of 6

Two sorrows

What is the difference between godly sorrow and the sorrow of the world?

What verses tell us about this?

“I have sinned in that I have betrayed the innocent blood.”

Matthew 27:4

“And Peter went out, and wept bitterly.”

Luke 22:62

Answer

Both hurt. Godly sorrow turns toward God and ends in repentance and life. Worldly sorrow is all about me, turns inward to shame and hiding, and ends in death. Judas and Peter were both sorry. Peter took it to Jesus.

Question 4 of 6

Turning

What did godly sorrow produce in the Corinthians?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Seven things, and all of them were actions: carefulness, clearing themselves, indignation at the sin, fear, longing to be restored, zeal, and putting it right. Real sorrow makes a man do something.

Question 5 of 6

Hard words

Why did Paul write his hard letter?

What verses tell us about this?

“Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.”

Proverbs 27:6

Answer

So that his care for them would show. It wasn't to win an argument or punish anyone. A hard truth from a man who loves you is a gift, and it's how Paul loved them.

Question 6 of 6

Joy

What gave Paul joy and confidence at the end?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Seeing the Corinthians turn around. A church that took its sorrow to God was back, and Paul could trust them again. If you fell yesterday, this is where a turned-around man ends up: back in the family, and trusted.
