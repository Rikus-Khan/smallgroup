Acts 9

Turned around

A man hunting Christians is stopped by Jesus on a road, a frightened church has to decide whether to trust him, and Peter finds the Lord already at work in Lydda and Joppa.

Part one

Why persecutest thou me?

Verses 1–9

A man full of threats, on his way to arrest believers, is stopped cold by a light from heaven.

The road to Damascus

Damascus was roughly 130 miles from Jerusalem, about a week on foot. Saul wasn't going for a walk. The high priest's letters were his permission slip: the synagogues there would hand over any follower of Jesus.

He's after “this way”. The first believers had no name yet. They were simply the people of the Way, because of what Jesus said about Himself.

“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6

Saul's record

Luke doesn't make Saul (the man we later know as Paul) smaller than he was. Here's the bill, before the road.

Against Saul

  • Dragged people to prison (8:3)owed
  • Threats and slaughter (9:1)owed
  • Letters to arrest believers (9:2)owed
Balance duemore than he could ever pay
MERCY“I obtained mercy”

“Who was before a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and injurious: but I obtained mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief.”

1 Timothy 1:13

Later he wrote that sentence about himself. No excuses, no shrinking it down: a persecutor. And then, mercy. Some of us have a record too, and the same mercy is on offer.

“Why persecutest thou me?”

The LordSaul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?

SaulWho art thou, Lord? he doesn't know yet

The LordI am Jesus whom thou persecutest.

Jesus doesn't say, why are you hurting my people? He says me. Whatever is done to a believer is done to Him.

“And the King shall answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.”

Matthew 25:40

And look at what Jesus did with the chance. He could have ended it right there. He used Saul's name twice, the way you'd call a man you care about, and He asked a question.

“It is hard for thee to kick against the pricks”

A goad (the pricks) was a long stick with a sharp point that a ploughman used to steer his ox. Kick at it and you only drive it deeper.

Saul had been fighting something that was already pushing on him. Likely it was his own conscience: he'd stood there while Stephen died praying for the men who killed him.

“And he kneeled down, and cried with a loud voice, Lord, lay not this sin to their charge. And when he had said this, he fell asleep.”

Acts 7:60

Some of us know that feeling: running hard from something we already know is true. It's hard. Jesus doesn't say it to shame him. He says it to tell him he can stop.

Two questions, then three dark days

“Who art thou, Lord?”I am JesusThe first question any man has to ask, and he gets a straight answer. The One he'd been fighting.
“Lord, what wilt thou have me to do?”AriseNo bargaining, no excuses, no list of terms. Trembling and astonished, he stops arguing and asks what the Lord wants.
Three days, no sight, no foodWaitThe man who strode out so sure of himself was led into Damascus by the hand. He'd come to take prisoners and arrived as one.

Notice what Jesus gave him: “Arise, and go into the city, and it shall be told thee what thou must do.” Not the whole plan. Just the next step. You don't have to see the whole road today. You need the next step, and a Lord who goes with you into the city.

Part two

Brother Saul

Verses 10–22

The Lord sends a frightened man to the one he fears, and the first word between them is “Brother.”

The Lord arranged both ends

Ananias hears

“Arise, and go”

To the street called Straight (Damascus still has one), to the house of Judas, to ask for Saul of Tarsus.

Saul sees

A man named Ananias

Coming in and putting his hand on him, so that he might receive his sight.

“Behold, he prayeth.” The Lord says it the way you'd tell a friend good news. Saul had said prayers all his life. Now, blind and broken, he was talking to Jesus himself.

Saul wasn't left alone in the dark for one day more than he had to be. The Lord had somebody on the way.

Ananias was afraid, and said so

AnaniasLord, I have heard by many of this man, how much evil he hath done to thy saints at Jerusalem: And here he hath authority from the chief priests to bind all that call on thy name. a fair worry

The LordGo thy way: for he is a chosen vessel unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles, and kings, and the children of Israel.

Ananias wasn't being faithless. People had died. Saints here just means believers, God's people. He'd heard the stories, and he told the Lord the truth about his fear. The Lord didn't scold him. He said go, and Ananias went.

Some day the Lord may ask you to sit with a man you're afraid of, or one who has hurt you. Say so honestly, then go anyway. The Lord who sends you has already been there first.

A chosen vessel

A vessel is a tool, something God can use. Chosen here is about the job He gave him. Three groups were waiting:

  1. The Gentileseverybody who wasn't Jewish. That's most of us.
  2. KingsPaul really did end up before governors, a king, and Caesar's court.
  3. The children of Israelhis own people, who'd once been his friends.

The Lord is honest about the cost too: “how great things he must suffer for my name's sake.” The man who made others suffer would suffer himself. Following Jesus is never promised to be easy. It is promised to be worth it, and He never sends anyone alone.

“But by the grace of God I am what I am: and his grace which was bestowed upon me was not in vain; but I laboured more abundantly than they all: yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me.”

1 Corinthians 15:10

“Brother Saul”

Saul, the persecutor → Brother Saul The first thing the afraid man said. He laid his hands on someone who'd come to arrest people like him.

Nothing in that room said brother except the gospel. Ananias had been told, and he believed it: Saul wasn't a threat to the family any more. He was in it.

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

From dark to light

9:8–9Night

Blind, with no food or water, led by the hand. He had nothing left to run on.

9:11Dawn

“Behold, he prayeth.” He turned toward Jesus before anyone came to help him.

9:17–18Day

“As it had been scales” fell, his sight came back, he ate, and he was baptized.

Baptized straight away. Baptism is the believer's step of obedience that pictures what has already happened: a man's old life buried with Christ, and a new one raised. The water doesn't wash anything away. Saul did it because he belonged to Jesus now, and he said so out loud.

Luke was a doctor, and he describes it like one: something like scales fell from his eyes. The proud man was humbled for three days, and then he could see.

“Filled with the Holy Ghost”

Acts is a book of transition. The good news was moving out to new groups, and each time God made it plain that these people truly belonged: the Jews at Jerusalem, the Samaritans, the Gentiles in Acts 10, the men at Ephesus. For Saul, the church's worst enemy, God made sure everyone could see it too.

That doesn't make it a pattern you have to repeat. Today the Holy Spirit comes to live in every believer the moment he trusts Christ. There's no second step to wait for and no more you need to earn.

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

“But ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his.”

Romans 8:9

“Is not this he?”

What they remembered

The old Saul

He destroyed those who called on this name, and came to Damascus to bring them bound.

What they were looking at

The new Saul

Preaching Christ in the very synagogues, that he is the Son of God, and proving it.

The believers who'd been afraid of him were glad. They'd say it all over Judaea:

“But they had heard only, That he which persecuted us in times past now preacheth the faith which once he destroyed. And they glorified God in me.”

Galatians 1:23–24

Some of the people in your life will say isn't this the guy who…? They have a reason to. You don't have to win that argument today. Live it. A changed life talks louder than an explanation, and eventually some of them will give God the glory too.

Part three

But Barnabas took him

Verses 23–31

A plot, a basket, a church that won't open the door, and one man who does.

Out through the wall

The hunter had become the hunted. The very people he'd counted on now wanted him dead, and they watched the gates day and night. So the disciples, the same people he'd come to arrest, took him by night and let him down in a big basket.

“In Damascus the governor under Aretas the king kept the city of the Damascenes with a garrison, desirous to apprehend me: And through a window in a basket was I let down by the wall, and escaped his hands.”

2 Corinthians 11:32–33

Paul tells this years later, and not as a boast. It's the least heroic thing in his story. He didn't win the fight. He left through a window. Sometimes the wise thing is the back way out, away from the crowd or the place or the phone number that's going to hurt you. There's no shame in taking the basket.

Who stood with him

Saul“assayed to join himself”the disciples“all afraid of him”
Barnabas“took him, and brought him to”the apostles
Barnabas“declared”: he saw the Lord, the Lord spoke to him, he preached boldly in DamascusSaul is in

You can't blame the church. They'd buried Stephen. A story like the persecutor is a believer now sounds exactly like a trap. Trust takes a while.

“And Joses, who by the apostles was surnamed Barnabas, (which is, being interpreted, The son of consolation,) a Levite, and of the country of Cyprus,”

Acts 4:36

Barnabas means son of consolation, a man who comes alongside and encourages. He put his own name behind a man everyone feared. If you're the one coming back, you'll need a man like that. Someone here may need you to be one today.

Home to Tarsus

After the basket and Barnabas, Saul spoke boldly in Jerusalem and argued with the Greek-speaking Jews, the same crowd that had argued with Stephen. They went about to slay him.

The brothers took him down to Caesarea, the harbor. They were protecting him.

Then they sent him forth to Tarsus, by ship, to the city where he grew up.

Years of waiting

  1. about AD 34 to 35The Damascus roadActs 9:3
  2. soon afterDamascus, and ArabiaGalatians 1:17
  3. about three years onJerusalem, and BarnabasActs 9:26–27
  4. yearsBack home: Tarsus, Syria and Ciliciaout of sight
  5. about AD 46 to 48The first missionary journeyActs 13–14

From the road to the first journey is about a dozen years, most of them at home, out of the spotlight. God wasn't done with Saul, and He wasn't in a hurry.

“Then departed Barnabas to Tarsus, for to seek Saul: And when he had found him, he brought him unto Antioch.”

Acts 11:25–26

Barnabas again. When it was time, he went and found him. If you feel set aside after a fresh start, with nothing much happening, that's not wasted time. Your story isn't over. Be faithful in the small place where you are.

The churches had rest

Look who disappeared from the story: the chief persecutor. The believers had been scattered since Stephen's death. Now they had rest across Judaea and Galilee and Samaria, and they grew.

God ended the persecution by turning the persecutor around.

“But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.”

Acts 1:8

Three rings of Acts 1:8 are filled. One is left, the uttermost part of the earth, and Peter is about to be sent across that line.

What do you do with a record like that?

He didn't hide it

“I persecuted the church of God”

He said it out loud, for years, and never pretended it away.

1 Corinthians 15:9

He didn't live in it

“By the grace of God I am what I am”

The past was real. It just wasn't the last word.

1 Corinthians 15:10

“Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before, I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 3:13–14

That's the answer to regret. Own it, once and honestly, to God and to the people you hurt where you can. Then stop carrying it. Forgetting doesn't mean it never happened. It means you stop letting it steer. Today, take one step forward.

Part four

Arise

Verses 32–43

While the church rests, Peter walks the coast road, and meets a man who hasn't left his bed in eight years and a woman everyone loves.

Eight years on a mat

Peter passed throughout all quarters. At Lydda, on the road to the coast, he found Aeneas, sick of the palsy: paralyzed, in bed eight years.

Look at how Peter says it. Not I heal you. “Jesus Christ maketh thee whole.” The power was never Peter's, and Peter was the one who'd denied Him.

“Arise, and make thy bed.” Do what you couldn't do. He got up, and the whole town turned to the Lord. It was a sign that the message about Jesus was true. God still answers prayer for the sick, but a sick believer isn't a failed one.

Tabitha, who is called Dorcas

Her name means a gazelle, but that's not what people remembered her for. Almsdeeds are gifts to the poor. When she died, the widows stood weeping, shewing the coats and garments she'd made for them.

Dorcas isn't a preacher in this story, and she isn't an apostle. She sewed. And the Lord had it written down.

“For God is not unrighteous to forget your work and labour of love, which ye have shewed toward his name, in that ye have ministered to the saints, and do minister.”

Hebrews 6:10

You don't have to do something big. Do something with your hands today, a job done well, a favor for a man who has nothing. It counts.

The same word, again

Jesus, with a little girl, Mark 5:41

Talitha cumi; which is, being interpreted, Damsel, I say unto thee, arise.

Peter, with Dorcas, Acts 9:40

Tabitha, arise.

Peter had been in the room when Jesus said it. Like Jesus, he sent the weeping crowd out first. But look at what he did before he spoke: he kneeled down, and prayed. Again, the power is the Lord's.

Dorcas would die again some day. The promise isn't that we'll never die. It's what Jesus said to Martha:

“Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?”

John 11:25–26

Peter stayed with a tanner

“he tarried many days in Joppa with one Simon a tanner”

A tanner turned animal skins into leather. That meant handling dead animals all day, and many Jews looked down on the work as unclean. Peter moved in anyway, in a house by the sea side (10:6).

Something is already loosening in Peter. God is getting him ready for the next chapter, where He'll ask him to cross a much bigger line.

Points to remember

  1. Sin“Breathing out threatenings and slaughter” 9:1Saul's sin was big, and Luke names it. So did Saul. God never pretended it away.
  2. Jesus“Why persecutest thou me?” 9:4Jesus counts anything done to His people as done to Himself. And He still stopped to speak to His enemy.
  3. Grace“A chosen vessel unto me” 9:15God's plan for Saul didn't get ruined by his past. It ran right through it.
  4. Fear“Lord, I have heard by many of this man” 9:13Fear is human. Say it to the Lord, then go anyway. Ananias's first word was “Brother.”
  5. A friend“But Barnabas took him” 9:27One man put his name behind Saul. Find a Barnabas, and be one.
  6. Power“Jesus Christ maketh thee whole” 9:34The power was never Peter's. And plain hands like Dorcas's count with God.

Question 1 of 6

Sin

What was Saul doing when the Lord stopped him?

What verses tell us about this?

“As for Saul, he made havock of the church, entering into every house, and haling men and women committed them to prison.”

Acts 8:3

Answer

He was on his way to Damascus to arrest believers and drag them to Jerusalem, still breathing out threats and murder. He thought he was serving God. He was wrong, and he was hurting the people of Jesus. Even so, Jesus stopped him on the road.

Question 2 of 6

Jesus

Whom did Jesus say Saul was hurting?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the King shall answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.”

Matthew 25:40

Answer

Jesus Himself. Saul was going after believers, but the Lord said, “Why persecutest thou me?” Jesus is so joined to His people that what's done to them is done to Him.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

What did the Lord choose Saul to do?

What verses tell us about this?

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

Answer

To carry Jesus' name to the Gentiles, to kings, and to the people of Israel, and to suffer for it. The worst enemy of the church became its best-known messenger, because of mercy, not because he'd earned it.

Question 4 of 6

Fear

Why were Ananias and the church afraid of Saul?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because of what he'd done. People had died, and the story of the persecutor turning believer sounded like a trap. Their fear was fair. Ananias said it to the Lord, then went and called him “Brother.” The church needed Barnabas to vouch for him.

Question 5 of 6

A friend

What did Barnabas do for Saul?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then departed Barnabas to Tarsus, for to seek Saul: And when he had found him, he brought him unto Antioch.”

Acts 11:25

Answer

He took Saul to the apostles and told them what had happened on the road and in Damascus. He put his own name behind a man everyone feared. Years later he went to Tarsus and found him again.

Question 6 of 6

Power

What did Jesus do through Peter at Lydda and Joppa?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He healed Aeneas, who'd been paralyzed for eight years, and raised Dorcas from the dead. Peter said it plainly: “Jesus Christ maketh thee whole.” Whole towns turned to the Lord because of what they saw.
