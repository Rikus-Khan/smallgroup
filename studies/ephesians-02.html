Part one

Dead

Verses 1–3

Paul tells the truth about where every one of us started, himself included.

Not sick. Dead.

What we like to think

Sick

A sick man needs some help, some medicine, a better plan. He can still do his part.

What Paul says

Dead

A dead man can't do his part. He needs somebody to give him life. Quickened means exactly that: made alive.

Trespasses is stepping over the line; sins is missing the mark. Both, all the way down. And notice: dead men still walk (v. 2). A man can get up, go to work and party all weekend, and be dead to God the whole time.

Three pulls

  1. The world“the course of this world” · v. 2
    going along with everybody
  2. The devil“the prince of the power of the air” · v. 2
    an enemy at work behind it
  3. The flesh“the lusts of our flesh” · v. 3
    what's inside us wanting it

Conversation is old English for the way a man lives. “Children of disobedience” and “children of wrath” means men marked by disobeying, and headed for God's judgment on it.

Look at the small words: “we all”, “even as others.” Paul, the strict Pharisee, puts himself in the same line as the pagans of Ephesus. Nobody here is worse than the rest. Nobody is better.

Part two

But God

Verses 4–10

Two words that turn the whole story around.

What we were · vv. 1–3
  • “dead in trespasses and sins”
  • “walked according to the course of this world”
  • “in the lusts of our flesh”
  • “by nature the children of wrath”
But God“who is rich in mercy”
What He did · vv. 4–6
  • “quickened us together with Christ”
  • “raised us up together”
  • “made us sit together in heavenly places”
  • “by grace ye are saved”

Why did He do it? Not because we got better. “For his great love wherewith he loved us, even when we were dead in sins.” Every recovery story worth telling turns on these two words. If you're in Christ, you have a “but God” too. Tell it to somebody today.

What He did for Christ, He did for us

Christ · 1:20“raised him from the dead”“set him at his own right hand”“in the heavenly places”
raisedseatedthe same place
us · 2:6“raised us up together”“made us sit together”“in heavenly places in Christ Jesus”

You sit when the work is done. The man in Christ isn't trying to earn a seat; he's seated. And verse 7 says why: so that for ages to come God can point to you and show “the exceeding riches of his grace.” Your story isn't what disqualifies you. It's what He'll show off.

Saved: how, and how not

  • “not of yourselves”you didn't think it up, clean up for it, or deserve it
  • “Not of works”not church, not baptism, not good deeds, not a clean month
  • “by grace… through faith… the gift of God”God gives it freely; faith is the empty hand that takes it

“That not of yourselves” points to the whole thing, being saved by grace through faith: it's God's gift, not your achievement. So nobody in heaven brags. The man with the worst record and the man with the best stand in the same line, holding the same gift.

His workmanship

Workmanship is poiēma: a thing made by a craftsman. Our word poem comes from it. God isn't finished with you, but you're His work, not a scrap.

before→“ye walked”→“according to the course of this world”verse 2
now→“we should walk”→in good works God preparedverse 10

Good works don't save you; they're what saved men were made for. “Before ordained” means God laid them out ahead of time. Somewhere in your day today there's one waiting with your name on it: a call, an apology, a hand to someone.

Part three

The wall is down

Verses 11–18

The cross didn't just bring men to God. It brought them to each other.

Remember what you were without

A Gentile in Ephesus, before Christ (v. 12)

  • “without Christ”no Savior
  • “aliens from the commonwealth”no people
  • “strangers from the covenants”no promises
  • “having no hope”no future
Bottom line“without God in the world”
BUT NOWmade nigh, v. 13

Paul says remember, not to drag them back into shame, but so they'll never take grace for granted. Remember where you came from, and thank God. Don't move back in.

The middle wall

At the temple in Jerusalem a stone wall kept Gentiles out of the inner courts. Signs on it warned that a foreigner who went past would have himself to blame for his death.

That wall put Paul in chains: a mob thought he'd taken “Trophimus an Ephesian” past it (Acts 21:29). He's writing from that prison.

Jesus “hath broken down the middle wall of partition.” Far off and near, Gentile and Jew, both come in by the same blood.

One new man

a Jewa GentileThe law's rules and rituals had marked them apart. Christ ended that in His own body on the cross.
a man fresh out of prisona deacon of forty yearsSame Savior, same seat, same family.
one raceanother race“For ye are all one in Christ Jesus” (Galatians 3:28).

Twain means two. Christ didn't make Gentiles into Jews; He made something new out of both: one body. Enmity, the hostility, died at the cross. If a brother's past still bothers you, or yours bothers him, the wall is already down.

Peace, peace

Isaiah 57:19, over 700 years earlier

“I create the fruit of the lips; Peace, peace to him that is far off, and to him that is near, saith the LORD; and I will heal him.”

Ephesians 2:17

“And came and preached peace to you which were afar off, and to them that were nigh.”

Access is being brought in to see the King. Look who does what: “through him” (the Son), “by one Spirit”, “unto the Father.” You don't need a priest, a saint or a good week to get in. You can go to the Father right now.

Part four

The household of God

Verses 19–22

Outsiders become family, and living stones in God's own house.

No more strangers

strangers and foreigners → fellowcitizens, and family A stranger was a visitor passing through. A foreigner lived in town but had no rights there. Now: full citizens, and children in God's own house.

A lot of men feel like visitors at church: on the edge, waiting to be found out. God says you live here. A household is family: you have a place at the table, and a job to do. So show up, and take your place.

A house still going up

The chief corner stone was laid first; every other stone was lined up by it. The foundation is the apostles and prophets, and the Word they gave us.

The old temple had a wall to keep men out. God's new temple is built out of the men who were kept out, “lively stones” (1 Peter 2:5), and He lives in it by His Spirit. Nobody gets built in alone.

The whole chapter in three steps

Verses 1–3Dead

“dead in trespasses and sins… even as others.”

Verses 4–10Made alive

“But God, who is rich in mercy… by grace are ye saved through faith.”

Verses 11–22Brought home

“no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God.”

From the grave to the family table. If you fell yesterday, you didn't fall out of the household. Get up, go to the Father (v. 18), and walk in the good work in front of you today.

Points to remember

  1. Dead“Dead in trespasses and sins” 2:1Without Christ, every man is dead to God, not just sick, and can't make himself alive.
  2. But God“But God, who is rich in mercy” 2:4God stepped in out of His mercy and great love, while we were still dead.
  3. By grace“By grace are ye saved through faith” 2:8Salvation is God's gift, received by faith. Not of works, so no one can boast.
  4. His workmanship“Created in Christ Jesus unto good works” 2:10We aren't saved by good works, but for them. God has some ready for today.
  5. Made nigh“Made nigh by the blood of Christ” 2:13Christ's blood brings the far-off near to God, and breaks down the walls between people.
  6. The household of God“Fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God” 2:19A believer belongs: a citizen, family, a living stone in the house where God lives.

Question 1 of 6

Dead

What were we like before God stepped in?

What verses tell us about this?

“For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found. And they began to be merry.”

Luke 15:24

Answer

Dead in sin, following the world, the devil and our own desires, and headed for judgment. All of us, “even as others.” Nobody started out better than anybody else.

Question 2 of 6

But God

Why did God step in?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

Because He is rich in mercy and loves us with a great love. Not because we improved: He did it while we were still dead in sin. And He means to show His kindness to us for ages to come.

Question 3 of 6

By grace

How is a man saved, and how is he not saved?

What verses tell us about this?

“Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;”

Titus 3:5

Answer

By grace, through faith: God's free gift, received by trusting Christ. Not by works, not by anything we do or earn, so no one can brag. The worst sinner and the best citizen are saved the same way.

Question 4 of 6

His workmanship

What did God save us for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

We are His handiwork, made new in Christ to walk in good works He prepared ahead of time, and to be part of the house where God lives by His Spirit. Not saved by good works, but saved for them.

Question 5 of 6

Made nigh

How are people who were far off brought near?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By the blood of Christ. He is our peace: at the cross He broke down the wall between Jew and Gentile and brought both to God in one body. Now anyone in Christ can come to the Father by the Spirit.

Question 6 of 6

The household of God

Where does a believer belong now?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In God's household: a full citizen with the saints, family, and a stone in a holy temple built on Christ the corner stone. You're not a visitor. Show up and take your place.
