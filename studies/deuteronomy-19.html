Deuteronomy 19

Three cities and a road, the landmark, and the false witness

Three cities of refuge in the land, with a way made ready to them, and no refuge for the man who killed in hate. Don't move your neighbour's landmark. No charge stands on one witness. And the false witness gets the very sentence he wanted for his brother: eye for eye, tooth for tooth.

Part one

“Thou shalt prepare thee a way”

Verses 1–10

A refuge in every part of the land, and a road to it.

A road to the gate

We learned the law of refuge in Numbers 35, and saw Moses set apart the three cities east of Jordan in chapter 4. Here he adds three things for the land itself: three cities “in the midst of thy land”, the land divided “into three parts”, and a road: “Thou shalt prepare thee a way.” (Succeedest in verse 1 means to take their place.)

A refuge you can't reach is no refuge. So God had the land cut into three, a city in each part, and the way to it made ready, “that every slayer may flee thither”.

Chapter 4 showed the refuge ready before it was needed; here God makes the road to it. Christ is near, and the way to him is open: “him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out” (John 6:37).

“While his heart is hot”

We met the axe head flying off the helve (the handle) in Numbers 35. Ignorantly means without meaning to. What Moses adds here is the reason for the road: the avenger's heart is hot, and the way is long.

The avenger was not a wicked man. His brother was dead, and grief burns. But a hot heart does not weigh what really happened. God put a road and a gate between that anger and a man who “was not worthy of death”.

Most of us have been both men. When your heart is hot, don't act on it while it burns. And when you are the one being chased, run to the refuge.

Three, three, and three more

3east of Jordan, set apart by Moses (4:41–43)
3in the land, west of Jordan (19:2)
3 more“if the LORD thy God enlarge thy coast” (19:8–9)

“And they appointed Kedesh in Galilee in mount Naphtali, and Shechem in mount Ephraim, and Kirjath-arba, which is Hebron, in the mountain of Judah.”

Joshua 20:7

Joshua named the three in the land: north, middle and south. To enlarge thy coast is to widen your borders. The three more hung on an if: “If thou shalt keep all these commandments… to love the LORD thy God, and to walk ever in his ways”. We do not read that they were ever added. And the reason for them all is in verse 10: “That innocent blood be not shed in thy land… and so blood be upon thee.” If no refuge was there, the whole land would carry the guilt.

Part two

“If any man hate his neighbour”

Verses 11–13

The city walls were for the accident, not for hate.

No refuge for hate

The slayer (verses 4–6)

flees, and lives

He killed “ignorantly”, a man “whom he hated not in time past”. The gate opens for him.

The murderer (verses 11–12)

fetched back out

He did “hate his neighbour, and lie in wait for him”. Running inside the walls changes nothing: the elders of his own city send and fetch him out.

This was the law of Israel's courts, and it is hard: “Thine eye shall not pity him.” It shows how seriously God takes a life taken in hate. The city was not a hiding place from the truth. The elders of his own town, not a mob, sent for him.

A man no city could take in

King David did not kill by accident. He planned Uriah's death and had him set where he would fall (2 Samuel 11). Under this law there was no city for him. Then Nathan came, and David stopped hiding it:

“And David said unto Nathan, I have sinned against the LORD. And Nathan said unto David, The LORD also hath put away thy sin; thou shalt not die.”

2 Samuel 12:13

“Deliver me from bloodguiltiness, O God, thou God of my salvation: and my tongue shall sing aloud of thy righteousness.”

Psalm 51:14

David still lived with what followed (2 Samuel 12:10). But where no city could take him in, God did. If you carry blood on your hands that was no accident, the walls of the old cities could not hide you. The God of David can forgive you.

Part three

“Thy neighbour's landmark”

Verse 14

The stone that marks what is his, and what is yours.

Moving the stone

A landmark was a boundary stone between two men's fields, set long before by the men of old: “which they of old time have set”. Move it a few feet in the night, and a strip of your neighbour's living, and his children's, is now yours.

No one sees the stone move. That is the point. It is stealing that looks like nothing. The law puts a curse on it: “Cursed be he that removeth his neighbour’s landmark” (27:17).

“Their redeemer is mighty”

“Remove not the ancient landmark, which thy fathers have set.”

Proverbs 22:28

“Remove not the old landmark; and enter not into the fields of the fatherless: For their redeemer is mighty; he shall plead their cause with thee.”

Proverbs 23:10–11

The men most easily robbed this way were the ones with no one to stand up for them: the widow's boy, the fatherless. God says he stands up for them himself.

We still move landmarks: hours on a time sheet, a little from the till, a share of the family's money that was never ours. If you have moved one, put it back where you can. Zacchaeus did, and Jesus said, “This day is salvation come to this house” (Luke 19:9).

Part four

“At the mouth of two witnesses”

Verses 15–21

No charge on one man's word, and a lie that comes home.

Any iniquity, any sin

In chapter 17 the rule of two or three witnesses was for a man on trial for his life. Here it covers every charge: “for any iniquity, or for any sin”. Jesus and Paul both stood on it:

“It is also written in your law, that the testimony of two men is true.”

John 8:17

“Against an elder receive not an accusation, but before two or three witnesses.”

1 Timothy 5:19

One story, told by one man, is not enough to judge a brother, or a pastor. Before you believe the worst about someone, ask who else saw it. And don't be the one witness who starts it.

The lie comes home

Diligent inquisition means a careful search for the truth. If the witness had lied, the judges were to do to him what he “had thought to have done unto his brother”.

Then they trade places. The brother goes home. The liar takes the sentence he wanted for him.

“So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then was the king’s wrath pacified.”

Esther 7:10

False witness against Jesus

“Now the chief priests, and elders, and all the council, sought false witness against Jesus, to put him to death; But found none: yea, though many false witnesses came, yet found they none. At the last came two false witnesses,”

Matthew 26:59–60

The council went looking for the very thing this law forbids, “Neither shalt thou bear false witness against thy neighbour” (5:20). And it found two. The one man who was truly innocent was condemned by false witnesses.

“Who, when he was reviled, reviled not again; when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously:”

1 Peter 2:23

He did not pay them back. He died for liars too. If you have lied about a man, or been lied about, there is grace at the cross for both.

Eye for eye

“Eye for eye, tooth for tooth” is the same law we read in Exodus 21 and Leviticus 24. It was given to the judges (verse 17), not to the man with a grudge. It set the sentence at the size of the wrong.

Left to himself, a hurt man pays back double: a life for an eye. The law held the court to “eye for eye”, no more and no less. And here the false witness got exactly what he had planned for his brother.

“But I say unto you”

“Ye have heard that it hath been said, An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth: But I say unto you, That ye resist not evil: but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also.”

Matthew 5:38–39

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

Men had taken the judges' rule and made it their own: you hit me, I hit you. Jesus speaks to the man himself. Justice belongs to God, and to the judges he sets up. Your own hand is to stay open.

For a man who has lived by getting even, this is the hardest verse in the chapter, and the way to freedom. You don't have to carry the score any more. God will settle it.

Real world: run, and tell the truth

  • SinThe hot heart that wants to get even, the landmark moved a little at a time, the story told about a brother with only one witness to it.
  • RegretSome of us carry a death, an accident or worse, or a lie that cost another man dearly. God does not tell us it doesn't matter.
  • GraceGod made a road to the refuge before anyone needed it. And where no city could take David in, God forgave him.
  • HopeThe refuge is near and the way is open. A man who runs to Christ is not turned back at the gate.
  • TodayIf you've moved a landmark, put it back. If you've passed on a story with one witness, stop it. If your heart is hot at someone, don't act on it today: call or text a brother in Christ first.

Points to remember

  1. The cities“Thou shalt separate three cities for thee in the midst of thy land” 19:2Three in the land, and three more if the land grew, for the man who killed without meaning to.
  2. The way“Thou shalt prepare thee a way… that every slayer may flee thither” 19:3The way was made ready, so a hot heart could not overtake him on a long road.
  3. The murderer“the elders of his city shall send and fetch him thence” 19:12The walls were for the accident, not for hate.
  4. The landmark“Thou shalt not remove thy neighbour’s landmark” 19:14Don't steal a little at a time where no one sees.
  5. The witnesses“at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established” 19:15No charge stands on one man's word.
  6. The false witness“Then shall ye do unto him, as he had thought to have done unto his brother” 19:19The lie comes home; and Jesus, condemned by false witnesses, did not pay them back.

Question 1 of 6

The cities

How many cities were to be set apart in the land, and for whom?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Three in the midst of the land, and three more if the LORD enlarged their coast. They were for the slayer who killed his neighbour ignorantly, whom he hated not in time past.

Question 2 of 6

The way

Why was Israel to prepare a way to the cities?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So that every slayer could flee there, and the avenger of blood, with his heart hot, would not overtake him on a long road and kill a man who was not worthy of death.

Question 3 of 6

The murderer

What happened to the man who hated his neighbour and fled to one of the cities?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The elders of his own city sent and fetched him out, and gave him into the hand of the avenger of blood. The city of refuge did not shelter hate.

Question 4 of 6

The landmark

What were they not to remove?

What verses tell us about this?

“Remove not the old landmark; and enter not into the fields of the fatherless: For their redeemer is mighty; he shall plead their cause with thee.”

Proverbs 23:10–11

Answer

Their neighbour's landmark, the boundary stone set of old time, which marked his land from theirs.

Question 5 of 6

The witnesses

How many witnesses did it take to establish a matter?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is also written in your law, that the testimony of two men is true.”

John 8:17

Answer

Two or three. One witness was not enough for any iniquity or any sin.

Question 6 of 6

The false witness

What was done to a false witness?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

After a careful search by the judges, he was given the very sentence he had wanted for his brother: life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth.
