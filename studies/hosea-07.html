Hosea 7

A cake not turned

Kings murdered one after another, a nation half-baked, a man going gray without noticing, and a God who says, “I have redeemed them.”

Part one

Before my face

Verses 1–2

God reached out to heal, and the sin came into the light.

The cover comes off

Listen to how God puts it: “When I would have healed Israel...” He came near to heal. And the closer He came, the more the sin showed: lying, stealing, robbery in the streets.

That's what God's light does. It doesn't create the mess. It shows what was under the cover all along. Israel thought God had forgotten. “They are before my face.”

Here's what they wouldn't do, and what any of us can do today: say it out loud to God. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” (1 John 1:9). What He uncovers, He's ready to heal.

Part two

Hot as an oven

Verses 3–7

Passions banked all night, and in the morning, a fire.

The banked fire

A village baker kept his oven going overnight. He banked the coals, kneaded the dough, and slept while it rose. By morning the fire was roaring, ready to go.

Hosea says Israel's leaders were like that oven: lust, ambition, and plots kept smoldering all night, “whiles they lie in wait.” Come morning, it all burst into flame.

Desire works this way in all of us. It doesn't usually explode out of nowhere. It gets fed and kept warm, quietly, until the morning it burns.

The party and the plot

On “the day of our king”, a feast day or his crowning, the princes kept pouring until the king was sick drunk. A drunk king is easy to steer. He was shaking hands with scorners, men who mocked God, and the same men were plotting his death.

Many of us know that table. The bottle never comes alone. It brings company, and the company usually wants something from you. A man who's drunk can't see the knife.

“But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.”

James 1:14–15

Notice the saddest line of all: “there is none among them that calleth unto me” (v. 7). King after king fell, and nobody thought to pray.

“All their kings are fallen”

  1. Jeroboam II41 years, rich and strong
  2. Zachariah6 months, murdered
  3. Shallum1 month, murdered
  4. Menahempaid Assyria to stay king
  5. Pekahiah2 years, murdered
  6. Pekahmurdered
  7. Hosheaprison, and Samaria falls (about 722 BC)

Hosea lived through all of this (2 Kings 14:23–17:6). After Jeroboam II died, four of the next six kings were murdered by the men who took their thrones, in about twenty years.

A nation that won't call on God reaches for the knife. And every man who seized power by murder was seized the same way. That's where a life without God goes: down, one step at a time, until the lights go out.

Part three

Half-baked

Verses 8–10

Burned on one side, raw on the other, and gray before his time.

A cake not turned

Flat bread was baked on hot stones or on the side of an oven. You had to flip it. Forget to, and you'd get a cake that was charred black on the bottom and raw dough on top. Good for nothing.

The side down→burnedEphraim “mixed himself among the people”: blended in with the nations, their gods, their deals, until it scorched him.
The side up→rawToward God, nothing ever got done. Never turned, never finished.

Half in and half out is the most miserable place to live. Too much of the world to enjoy God, too much of God to enjoy the world. God wants all of you, turned all the way over to Him.

He knoweth it not

young
 
 
 
 
 
gray

Foreign kings were draining Israel: tribute, raids, lost land. Ephraim thought he was as strong as ever. Like a man who doesn't notice the gray hairs until one day the mirror tells him he's old.

“And she said, The Philistines be upon thee, Samson. And he awoke out of his sleep, and said, I will go out as at other times before, and shake myself. And he wist not that the LORD was departed from him.”

Judges 16:20

Addiction drains a man this way. Years go. Health goes. Family goes. And the whole time he's telling himself, I'm fine, I've got this. The first step out is honesty: seeing what has really been taken. And it's not too late. God restores what we thought was gone for good.

Part four

A silly dove

Verses 11–16

Flying everywhere except home.

Flapping back and forth

Silly in old English meant simple, easily fooled. “Without heart” means without sense. A dove that panics flies toward whatever looks safe, and right into the fowler's net.

Ephraim“Egypt, help us!”
Ephraim“No, Assyria, help us!”
Ephraim“Egypt again!” (2 Kings 17:4)
The LORD“I will spread my net upon them”

Israel's last kings swung between the two superpowers, paying one and then betraying it for the other. They never once flew home. God's net wasn't cruelty. It was the end of running.

Howling isn't praying

On their beds

Howling

Pain, panic, self-pity. Crying out to the dark, or to Baal, for “corn and wine,” for the crop and the drink. Wanting the trouble gone, not wanting God.

With the heart

Crying unto God

Turning to Him, not just from the pain. Honest about the sin, not just sorry about the hangover.

“The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.”

Psalm 51:17

And don't miss verse 13: “though I have redeemed them.” God had already bought them out of Egypt. He wasn't a stranger they were begging. He was the Redeemer they were running from. Lying awake at 3 a.m., you can cry to Him, with your heart, and He hears.

Almost turning

A deceitful bow was a warped bow. You aim it true, and the arrow veers off anyway. Israel turned, sort of. They turned to other kings, other gods, other plans. “But not to the most High.”

Changing the drug isn't turning. Changing the city isn't turning. Changing the woman isn't turning. Real turning has a target: God Himself.

And the One they kept missing is still the One who says, “Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest” (Matthew 11:28).

Points to remember

  1. God sees“They are before my face” 7:2God remembers what we hide, and He comes near to heal it, not just to expose it.
  2. Desire“They have made ready their heart like an oven” 7:6Desire fed in secret all night bursts into flame by morning.
  3. Half-hearted“Ephraim is a cake not turned” 7:8Half in and half out ruins a man. God wants all of us.
  4. Unnoticed loss“Strangers have devoured his strength, and he knoweth it not” 7:9Sin drains a man quietly. Honesty is the first step back.
  5. Real prayer“They have not cried unto me with their heart” 7:14God hears the honest cry of a broken heart.
  6. Turning“They return, but not to the most High” 7:16Real turning has a target: God Himself.

Question 1 of 6

God sees

Does God remember what we've done?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Yes. Israel told itself God had forgotten, but “they are before my face.” Yet God came near in order to heal. What we confess, He forgives and cleanses. The sin He remembers is the sin He's ready to deal with, if we'll bring it to Him.

Question 2 of 6

Desire

What does Hosea's oven teach us about desire?

What verses tell us about this?

“But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.”

James 1:14–15

Answer

Desire that's kept warm in secret doesn't stay small. Like a banked oven, it smolders all night and roars by morning. The time to deal with it is early, while it's still coals: bring it into the light and don't feed it.

Question 3 of 6

Half-hearted

What is wrong with “a cake not turned”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.”

James 4:8

Answer

It's burned on one side and raw on the other, good for nothing. Ephraim mixed himself with the nations until it scorched him, and never let God finish the work on the other side. Half-hearted faith is miserable. God wants a man turned all the way over to Him.

Question 4 of 6

Unnoticed loss

How can a man lose his strength and not know it?

What verses tell us about this?

“And she said, The Philistines be upon thee, Samson. And he awoke out of his sleep, and said, I will go out as at other times before, and shake myself. And he wist not that the LORD was departed from him.”

Judges 16:20

Answer

A little at a time, while he keeps telling himself he's fine. Pride keeps him from seeing it (v. 10). Samson didn't notice either. The way back starts with honesty about what's been lost, then returning to the LORD, who can restore it.

Question 5 of 6

Real prayer

What's the difference between howling on your bed and crying to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.”

Psalm 51:17

Answer

Howling wants the pain gone. Crying to God wants God. Israel wailed for corn and wine but never turned their hearts to the One who had redeemed them. God never despises a broken and honest heart.

Question 6 of 6

Turning

Why is turning “but not to the most High” no real turning?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

Because it only trades one master for another, like a warped bow that always misses the mark. Israel flew from Egypt to Assyria and back. Swapping one habit, place, or person for another isn't turning. Turning means coming to God Himself, and He promises rest.
