Genesis 23

A stranger buys a grave

Sarah dies at a hundred and twenty-seven. Abraham weeps, and then does the honest, public thing: he buys a burial cave at full price from the sons of Heth, and gets the first piece of the promised land.

Part one

Sarah dies

Verses 1–6

A long marriage ends, and a man of faith weeps.

Sarah's years

  1. about 2090 BCThe callSarai is about 65
  2. about 2065 BCIsaac is bornSarah is 90 (17:17)
  3. about 2028 BCSarah dies127 years; Isaac is about 37
  4. about 2025 BCIsaac marrieshe is 40 (25:20)

Genesis counts her years: “these were the years of the life of Sarah”. Hardly any woman in the Bible has her age at death written down. She is remembered as a person, a wife and a mother, and she is in Hebrews 11 among the people of faith.

Isaac was about thirty-seven. He lost his mother, and the book of Genesis will come back to how much that mattered to him (24:67).

“Kirjath-arba; the same is Hebron”

Abraham was living at Beer-sheba (22:19), and Sarah died at Hebron, about twenty-five miles north. So Abraham “came”: the journey itself is part of the grief.

Kirjath-arba means “city of Arba”, the old name. Moses wrote for readers who knew the place as Hebron, so he adds, “the same is Hebron”. Hebron is the town where Abraham had pitched his tent by the oaks of Mamre.

Abraham came to mourn, and to weep.

The father of the faithful wept. There are two words in the verse, mourn and weep, the grief and the tears. Nobody told him that faith meant not feeling.

“When Jesus therefore saw her weeping, and the Jews also weeping which came with her, he groaned in the spirit, and was troubled, And said, Where have ye laid him? They said unto him, Lord, come and see. Jesus wept.”

John 11:33-35

Jesus wept at the grave of his friend. Tears are not unbelief. A lot of men have learned to put grief in a bottle or a pill instead of letting it out. You don't have to. Take it to the LORD, and to your brothers in Christ and your pastor, who will weep with you.

Who were the sons of Heth?

  • Ham
    • Canaan
      • Sidonhis firstborn
      • Heththe sons of Heth, the Hittites of Hebron

Genesis 10:15 tells us: “Canaan begat Sidon his firstborn, and Heth”. The people Abraham is talking to here are Canaan's family, the people who held the land God had promised him. He is among strangers, and they are not God's people.

Yet look how they speak to him: “my lord”, and “a mighty prince among us”. The KJV margin gives the Hebrew: “a prince of God”. As with Abimelech in chapter 21, the neighbours can see that God is with him.

A stranger in his own promise

What he had (v. 4)

“A stranger and a sojourner”

More than sixty years in Canaan, and not a foot of ground, not even a grave to bury his wife in.

What God had said (17:8)

“All the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession”

A promise. It was true, and not yet his.

“By faith he sojourned in the land of promise, as in a strange country, dwelling in tabernacles with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise: For he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God.”

Hebrews 11:9-10

Abraham knew that Canaan was a gift to wait for, not a place to get comfortable. If you are a believer, you are a stranger here too. Your real home is where the Builder is.

Part two

At the gate

Verses 7–16

A polite offer, a plain answer, and a price paid in public.

How Abraham asked

  1. He bowedto the people of the land, in respect (v. 7)
  2. He asked, and did not take“If it be your mind” (v. 8). He did not simply help himself to the land God had promised
  3. He named the placethe cave of Machpelah, “in the end of his field” (v. 9)
  4. He offered to pay“for as much money as it is worth” (v. 9)

Abraham had a promise from God that this whole land would be his family's. He did not take it by force, or trick anyone out of it. He bowed, and asked, and offered to pay. God's promise did not make him rude.

Machpelah may mean “the double”, and many think it was a double cave. It is the name you will see again and again in Genesis.

The generous offer

EphronThe field give I thee, and the cave that is therein, I give it thee.

AbrahamBut if thou wilt give it, I pray thee, hear me: I will give thee money for the field; take it of me.

EphronThe land is worth four hundred shekels of silver; what is that betwixt me and thee? Bury therefore thy dead.

It is how bargaining went in that part of the world. The first offer is a gift, and the price is slipped in, in passing: “what is that betwixt me and thee?” Many Bible teachers read it as the usual courtesy, not a trap. Betwixt means between.

Abraham understood it perfectly. He did not haggle, and he did not accept the “gift”.

Why not take the gift?

Take the gift

A favour owed

The sons of Heth could say later, “we gave it to you.” A man who takes a gift owes something, and the title can always be argued.

Pay the price

A clean title

Public, witnessed, paid in full. Nobody could ever say they had made Abraham rich, or take the cave back.

“And Abram said to the king of Sodom, I have lift up mine hand unto the LORD, the most high God, the possessor of heaven and earth, That I will not take from a thread even to a shoelatchet, and that I will not take any thing that is thine, lest thou shouldest say, I have made Abram rich:”

Genesis 14:22-23

Abraham had been here before. In chapter 14 he refused a king's reward for the same reason. A man whose life is built on God's promise does not need to take favours that come with strings.

For a man in recovery this is close to home. Paying what you owe, in full and in the open, is what making amends looks like. Don't take the shortcut, and don't take the freebie that comes with strings.

Four hundred shekels

≈ 0.4 ozone shekel of silver
400shekels, weighed on the scales
≈ 10 lbabout four and a half kilograms of silver

There were no coins yet. Silver was weighed out on scales, “current money with the merchant”, silver the traders would take. “Abraham weighed to Ephron the silver”: every shekel was counted in front of the whole town.

Many think it was a high price for the land. Abraham did not argue about it. He had a grave to bury, and he paid.

Part three

Made sure

Verses 17–20

The first piece of the promised land is a grave.

A deed, about four thousand years old

  1. the fieldof Ephron, in Machpelah, “before Mamre”
  2. the cave“which was therein”
  3. all the trees“that were in the field”
  4. all the borders“round about”
  5. the witnesses“in the presence of the children of Heth, before all that went in at the gate”

Every detail is written out: the field, the cave, even the trees. Moses is writing what amounts to a title deed. Made sure means legally secured. It is a public, witnessed sale at the city gate, as binding as any sale could be.

It matters, because it is the first piece of the promised land that Abraham ever owned, as far as Genesis tells us. God promised him all of it, and for now he holds one cave. A man who buys a grave in a land he does not yet own is a man who believes God will give the rest.

Who lies in the cave

  • Abraham and Sarah23:19; 25:10
    • Isaac and Rebekah49:31
      • Jacob and Leah49:31; 50:13

“There they buried Abraham and Sarah his wife; there they buried Isaac and Rebekah his wife; and there I buried Leah.”

Genesis 49:31

Three generations in one cave, on the ground Abraham paid for. Jacob, an old man dying in Egypt, made his sons promise to take him back to this very spot. The family never forgot that the land was theirs by promise.

At Abraham's grave

“And his sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the cave of Machpelah, in the field of Ephron the son of Zohar the Hittite, which is before Mamre; The field which Abraham purchased of the sons of Heth: there was Abraham buried, and Sarah his wife.”

Genesis 25:9-10

When Abraham died, who came to the cave? “His sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him”. The two boys of chapter 21, split apart by an old family wound, stood together at the same grave.

Years of separation, and still a moment of coming together. If you have a family wound that has gone on for years, don't count it out. God was true to his word to both of those sons.

Grief with hope

“But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.”

1 Thessalonians 4:13-14

Abraham did not bury Sarah among strangers, in a tomb that belonged to someone else. He wanted her in the land God had promised. It was an act of faith: God will keep his word, even past the grave.

Paul does not say believers don't sorrow. He says they sorrow “not as others which have no hope”. If you have lost someone, weep, as Abraham did, and hold on to hope as he did.

When the one you lost belonged to the Lord

“Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of his saints.”

Psalm 116:15

A believer's death is not a small thing to God. It is precious to him. Jesus died and rose again, and he will bring with him those who sleep in him.

If the person you lost did not seem to know the Lord, you carry a different grief. Take that to him, and to your brothers in Christ and your pastor. Leave that one in the hands of the One who judges perfectly.

Points to remember

  1. Grief“Abraham came to mourn for Sarah, and to weep for her.” 23:2Faith weeps. Grief is not weakness, and it is not unbelief.
  2. Strangers“I am a stranger and a sojourner with you” 23:4He owned none of it yet, and he lived as a man who was waiting for God.

Question 1 of 6

Grief

What did Abraham do when Sarah died?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus wept.”

John 11:35

Answer

He mourned and wept. Then he stood up from beside her and did what had to be done. The father of the faithful grieved out loud, and Jesus wept at a grave too. A man does not have to numb his grief or swallow it. Take it to God, and to your brothers in Christ.

Question 2 of 6

Strangers

What did Abraham call himself in the land God had promised him?

What verses tell us about this?

“These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off, and were persuaded of them, and embraced them, and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.”

Hebrews 11:13

Answer

“A stranger and a sojourner”: a foreigner just passing through. After more than sixty years in Canaan he did not own a grave. Hebrews says the patriarchs “confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth”. If this world has beaten you up, remember it is not your home. Jesus has gone to prepare a place.

Question 3 of 6

Honest dealing

How did Abraham treat the people around him, who did not know God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He bowed to them twice, asked instead of taking, and paid what was asked. The Hittites were not the people of the promise, yet they said, “Thou art a mighty prince among us.” The KJV margin gives the Hebrew: “a prince of God”. Your neighbours and workmates are watching. Courtesy and honest dealing say more for the LORD than a speech.

Question 4 of 6

The price

Why did Abraham insist on paying full price?

What verses tell us about this?

“That I will not take from a thread even to a shoelatchet, and that I will not take any thing that is thine, lest thou shouldest say, I have made Abram rich:”

Genesis 14:23

Answer

Ephron offered it free, but a gift can leave a favour owed, and a claim somebody can dispute later. Abraham had already refused to let anyone say “I have made Abram rich” (14:23). So he weighed out four hundred shekels at the gate, in front of everyone. If you owe something, pay it in full and in the open.

Question 5 of 6

The cave

Why does it matter that a cave became the first land Abraham owned?

What verses tell us about this?

“There they buried Abraham and Sarah his wife; there they buried Isaac and Rebekah his wife; and there I buried Leah.”

Genesis 49:31

Answer

It was the first piece of the promised land Abraham ever owned, and it is a grave. In time it held Abraham and Sarah, Isaac and Rebekah, and Leah and Jacob. Jacob, dying in Egypt, asked to be taken back to it. A man who buys a grave in a land he does not yet own believes God will give the rest.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

What does Abraham's burying of Sarah teach about hope when we lose someone?

What verses tell us about this?

“But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.”

1 Thessalonians 4:13-14

Answer

That grief and hope go together. Abraham wept, then laid Sarah in the land God had promised, because he believed God would keep his word even past the grave. Paul says believers sorrow, but “not as others which have no hope”. Jesus died and rose again, and those who sleep in him, he will bring with him.
