Luke 17

Where are the nine?

Jesus teaches forgiveness, faith and plain duty, ten lepers are cleansed and one comes back, and He warns that His coming will be as sudden as lightning.

Part one

Increase our faith

Verses 1–10

Hard words about stumbling blocks and forgiving, and men who suddenly feel they need more faith.

Don't be the stumbling block

An offence here is a stumbling block: something that trips a man up and makes him fall into sin. Jesus says plainly that they will come. This world is full of them.

But “woe unto him, through whom they come.” A millstone was the big round grinding stone, heavy enough that a donkey turned it. Better to go into the sea with one of those around your neck than to drag someone else down.

“These little ones” are the weakest believers, the newest in the faith. Every man here has had someone pour him a drink, hand him something, or laugh him back into the old life. Jesus takes that seriously.

Turn it around: there is always a man newer than you. Be the one who helps him stand, never the one who trips him.

Seven times in a day

  1. trespass“I repent”forgive
  2. again“I repent”forgive
  3. again“I repent”forgive
  4. again“I repent”forgive
  5. again“I repent”forgive
  6. again“I repent”forgive
  7. again“I repent”forgive

Notice the order. First, “rebuke him”: forgiveness doesn't pretend nothing happened. Say it to his face, honestly. Then, if he turns, let it go. Seven in a day isn't a limit to count up to. It means: keep on forgiving.

Forgiving a man doesn't mean you hand him your car keys the same afternoon. Trust can take time to rebuild. But the grudge goes. Holding it only poisons you.

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Small faith, great God

The apostles had just heard “forgive him seven times in a day,” and they knew they didn't have it in them. “Increase our faith!”

The sycamine was a mulberry tree with deep, spreading roots. Jesus says faith no bigger than a mustard seed could pull it up and plant it in the sea.

The size of your faith isn't the point. What matters is who it's in. A little faith in a great God moves what you could never move: an old grudge, an old habit, a root that's gone down deep.

Just doing our duty

A farm servant came in from plowing or feeding cattle and still had supper to make. Nobody threw him a party for it. “I trow not” means I think not. By and by means right away.

→
Then say“We are unprofitable servants”

Unprofitable doesn't mean worthless. It means God doesn't owe us anything. Obeying Him, forgiving, serving: that's not extra credit, it's the job. We never put God in our debt.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God:”

Ephesians 2:8

Part two

Where are the nine?

Verses 11–19

Ten men cry for mercy and all ten are healed. Only one turns around.

On the border

Jesus is still on His last journey to Jerusalem, about AD 29–30. Here Luke puts Him on the edge between Galilee and Samaria, likely heading east to the Jordan and down the valley, the road most Jews took to avoid Samaria.

On a border, Jews and Samaritans lived close. That's why one band of lepers could have both in it. Sickness had thrown them together when nothing else would.

“Which stood afar off”

The Law kept a leper away from everyone. He couldn't go home, couldn't go to the synagogue, couldn't touch anybody. He had to shout his own shame wherever he went.

“All the days wherein the plague shall be in him he shall be defiled; he is unclean: he shall dwell alone; without the camp shall his habitation be.”

Leviticus 13:46

Only a priest could look a man over and declare him clean again (Leviticus 14). So when Jesus said, “Go shew yourselves unto the priests,” He was telling them to go and get the all-clear, while they still had the disease.

“As they went, they were cleansed.” They started walking before they saw anything change. That's faith: taking Him at His word and moving.

Ten cleansed, one came back

ten cried, “have mercy on us”ten were cleansed

One turned back. He didn't wait to see the priest first. He came back with a loud voice, fell on his face at Jesus' feet, and thanked Him.

And Luke saves the surprise for last: “and he was a Samaritan.” The outsider, the one the others would have looked down on once they were well, was the only one who said thank you.

Jesus noticed who didn't come back. “Where are the nine?” He still notices.

Cleansed, or made whole?

The nine

“Cleansed”

They got their skin back and went on with their lives. They got the gift and walked off without the Giver.

The one

“Made thee whole”

He got the gift and came back for the Giver. Jesus sent him off with more than clean skin: “thy faith hath made thee whole.”

It's easy to cry out to God in the pit and forget Him once the crisis passes. A clean month, a job back, the family talking again: go back and thank Him. Gratitude keeps a man close to the One who healed him.

“O give thanks unto the LORD, for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever.”

Psalm 107:1

Part three

As the lightning

Verses 20–37

The Pharisees want a date. Jesus tells His men how to be ready instead.

Not “Lo here!” or “lo there!”

The Pharisees wanted a timetable and signs to watch: when would God throw out Rome and set up the kingdom? Observation means watching for signs.

  • with observationnot a show you can track on a calendar
  • Lo here!not a place to go looking
  • lo there!not a rumour to chase
  • “within you”where the King rules a man's heart

They were looking past the King while He stood in front of them. The kingdom starts wherever Jesus is received and obeyed as King, one heart at a time.

What He told us, and what He didn't

These verses are about Jesus' coming again. Good Bible-believing men see some of the details differently, so we'll hold them humbly and stick to what is plain.

Plain in the text

He is coming

As sudden and as plain as lightning across the whole sky. Nobody will need to tell you where. But first the cross: “first must he suffer.”

Not given to us

The day

When men say, “See here; or, see there,” “go not after them.” Anyone selling you a date is selling something Jesus didn't give.

“But of that day and that hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels which are in heaven, neither the Son, but the Father.”

Mark 13:32

Just an ordinary day

Noedid eatdrankmarried wiveswere given in marriage→“the flood came”
Lotdid eatdrankboughtsoldplantedbuilded→“fire and brimstone”

Look at that list. Nothing on it is wicked by itself: meals, weddings, business, farming, building. The trouble was living as if God would never step in. Noe preached and built in plain sight for years, and they went on with their day.

God's patience is real, and the door is open to every man today:

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Remember Lot's wife.

“But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt.”

Genesis 19:26

The angels had pulled her out of Sodom by the hand. She was out. But her heart was still back there with the house and the stuff, and she turned for one more look.

Hold on to the old life→“shall lose it”
Let it go for Him→“shall preserve it”

Every man in recovery knows that look back: the old crowd, the old places, “the good times.” Don't go back down for the stuff. What He's leading you to is better than what He brought you out of.

Side by side, and divided

“in one bed”
“grinding together”
“in the field”
“the one shall be taken, and the other shall be left”

Same house, same work, same day, and then parted. Christians differ on how these verses fit with other passages about the end, and we don't need to settle that today. The plain point: that day will divide people who lived right next to each other.

Nobody gets in on his wife's faith, his mother's prayers, or the man beside him at the meeting. Each man must be ready himself.

Don't hunt for the place

“Where, Lord?” Jesus answers with a saying: where the carcass lies, the eagles (the word covers vultures too) gather. When judgment is due, it comes, as sure as birds circling a dead body. Don't hunt for the place. Be ready.

For the man who belongs to Jesus, His coming isn't a threat. It's a promise:

“And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also.”

John 14:3

Points to remember

  1. Forgive“Thou shalt forgive him” 17:4Be honest about the wrong, then keep on forgiving, as God has forgiven you.
  2. Faith“Faith as a grain of mustard seed” 17:6A little faith in a great God moves what you can't.
  3. Thanks“Where are the nine?” 17:17Don't take the gift and forget the Giver. Go back and thank Him.
  4. The kingdom“The kingdom of God is within you” 17:21Not a date to watch for, but a King to receive.
  5. Ready“Until the day that Noe entered into the ark” 17:27Ordinary days end. Be ready now, while the door is open.
  6. Looking back“Remember Lot’s wife” 17:32Don't look back with longing at what He brought you out of.

Question 1 of 6

Forgive

How often did Jesus say to forgive a brother who turns again and says, “I repent”?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Answer

Even seven times in a day: every time he turns and repents. First rebuke him honestly; then, if he repents, forgive him. We forgive because God, for Christ's sake, has forgiven us far more.

Question 2 of 6

Faith

When the apostles said, “Increase our faith,” what did Jesus tell them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That faith as small as a mustard seed could uproot a tree and plant it in the sea. It isn't the size of faith that matters but the God it rests in. Then He told them to get on with the job: obeying is simply our duty, not a favour to God.

Question 3 of 6

Thanks

Ten lepers were cleansed. How many came back to give thanks, and who was he?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Only one, and he was a Samaritan, the outsider. He turned back, glorified God with a loud voice, and fell at Jesus' feet to thank Him. Jesus told him, “thy faith hath made thee whole.”

Question 4 of 6

The kingdom

When the Pharisees asked when the kingdom of God would come, what did Jesus answer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That it doesn't come with signs to watch for, and nobody can say “Lo here!” or “lo there!” The kingdom of God is within you: the King was standing among them, and His kingdom comes wherever a heart receives Him.

Question 5 of 6

Ready

What were people busy doing in the days of Noe and Lot when the end came?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Ordinary things: eating, drinking, marrying, buying, selling, planting, building. They lived as if God would never step in, until the flood came and the fire fell. His coming will be just as sudden, so we get ready now.

Question 6 of 6

Looking back

Why did Jesus say, “Remember Lot's wife”?

What verses tell us about this?

“But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt.”

Genesis 19:26

Answer

She was brought out of Sodom, but her heart stayed behind, and she looked back. Jesus warns us not to go back for the old life. Whoever clings to it loses it; whoever lets it go for Him keeps his life.
