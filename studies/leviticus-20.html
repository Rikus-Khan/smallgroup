Leviticus 20

I am the LORD which sanctify you

Chapter 18 drew God's lines. Chapter 20 says what crossing them cost in Israel: Molech, familiar spirits, and the sins of the bed, with the sentence for each. In the middle stands one verse that changes everything: “I am the LORD which sanctify you.”

Part one

“I will set my face against that man”

Verses 1–8

Children given to Molech, and men who go after familiar spirits. Then the call to be holy, and who does the making.

Molech, again

  • Molecha false god of the nations; children were burned as offerings to him (18:21)
  • his seedhis children
  • the people of the landthe whole community, not one angry man
  • set my face againstturn against him in judgment
  • profane my holy namedrag God's name into the dirt

Chapter 18 forbade it. Chapter 20 puts a double sentence on it, stoning by the people and the LORD's own face set against the man, and starts the list with it. Of all the sins here, harming a child is named first.

“And he defiled Topheth, which is in the valley of the children of Hinnom, that no man might make his son or his daughter to pass through the fire to Molech.”

2 Kings 23:10

That was king Josiah, years later, tearing it down. One man who turned to the LORD put out that fire. A man can still be the one in his family who stops what has been passed down.

Don't hide your eyes

The LORD speaks not only to the man who does it, but to the neighbours who hide their eyes: who see, and look the other way, and let it go on. Their silence would not make it go away: “I will set my face against that man, and against his family” (verse 5). The LORD himself would do what they would not.

“But when Jesus saw it, he was much displeased, and said unto them, Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God.”

Mark 10:14

Suffer means let, allow. Jesus wanted children brought close and kept safe. If you know a child is being hurt, don't hide your eyes. Call the police, and tell your pastor. Silence protects the wrong person.

Go a whoring (verse 5) is the Bible's word for unfaithfulness to God: like a husband who walks out on his wife for another woman, Israel walking out on the LORD for an idol.

Familiar spirits

A familiar spirit was a spirit a medium claimed to call up, to speak with the dead; a wizard claimed secret knowledge. King Saul knew this law, and in his worst hour he went anyway:

“So Saul died for his transgression which he committed against the LORD, even against the word of the LORD, which he kept not, and also for asking counsel of one that had a familiar spirit, to enquire of it;”

1 Chronicles 10:13

When a man is scared and God seems silent, he reaches for anything that promises an answer. The cards, the psychic, the voice that isn't God's. Don't. Bring the fear to the LORD himself, and to a brother who will pray with you.

Who does the making?

Verse 7

“Sanctify yourselves”

Set yourself apart. Be holy. Keep his statutes, and do them. Your part is real.

Verse 8

“I… sanctify you”

But the LORD is the one who makes you holy. You obey; he does the setting apart.

“By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.”

Hebrews 10:10

“And the very God of peace sanctify you wholly; and I pray God your whole spirit and soul and body be preserved blameless unto the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ. Faithful is he that calleth you, who also will do it.”

1 Thessalonians 5:23–24

“Who also will do it.” You are not making yourself holy by willpower. He is, and he finishes.

Part two

“Their blood shall be upon them”

Verses 9–21

The laws of chapter 18 again, now with the sentence Israel's courts were to carry out.

“His blood shall be upon him”

To curse a father or mother is to call down evil on them, to damn them with your mouth. His blood shall be upon him means his death is his own doing; the blame is his, not the judge's.

The phrase comes six times in this chapter: verses 9, 11, 12, 13, 16 and 27.

Some of us said things to our mothers and fathers in the old life that we would give anything to take back. If they are living, today is a good day to call. If they are gone, God has heard your sorrow, and the blood of Jesus is enough for that too.

This law, before Jesus

Hundreds of years later, men dragged a woman to Jesus, and they used this very law:

“They say unto him, Master, this woman was taken in adultery, in the very act. Now Moses in the law commanded us, that such should be stoned: but what sayest thou?”

John 8:4–5

“So when they continued asking him, he lifted up himself, and said unto them, He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her.”

John 8:7

“She said, No man, Lord. And Jesus said unto her, Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more.”

John 8:11

Jesus did not say adultery was fine. He said “sin no more.” But he did not throw the stone, and he was the only one there who could have. He was on his way to take the death this law demanded, in her place and in ours.

Same laws, read twice. Chapter 18 said, “Thou shalt not.” Chapter 20 says what it cost. Read it calmly. It is not here so we can feel better than anyone. It is here so we see how heavy sin is to a holy God.

The words of the sentence

  • put to deathby Israel's courts, at the word of witnesses
  • cut off… in the sight of their peopleput out of God's people, openly
  • bear their iniquitycarry the guilt of it themselves, before God
  • die childlessGod himself would leave them without children
  • having her sicknessher monthly time; discovered her fountainuncovered her flow of blood
  • wrought confusiondid what overturns God's order; unclean thinga defiling thing

Some sentences were for the judges; some, like “childless,” God kept in his own hand. Either way, the man who did it could not say it was nothing.

A court in Israel, and the court of heaven

These were sentences for Israel's judges, in a nation where God was king. They were not for a man's own hand, and never on one man's word:

“At the mouth of two witnesses, or three witnesses, shall he that is worthy of death be put to death; but at the mouth of one witness he shall not be put to death.”

Deuteronomy 17:6

But the sentence tells us what God thinks of sin, and it is the same in every age:

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

Every man in this room has earned that wage somewhere. That is why the cross was not a small thing. The death in this chapter is the death Jesus died for us: “being made a curse for us” (Galatians 3:13).

Part three

“Ye shall be holy unto me”

Verses 22–27

Why all this? Because God took a people out of the nations to be his own.

Separated, to be given a land

  • manners of the nationthe ways of the people who lived there
  • abhorredfound them disgusting, could not bear them
  • spue you not outthe land would throw them up, as in 18:28
  • milk and honeya rich, good land, with plenty for all
  • separated youset you apart, as a man sets aside what is his

Look at the order in verses 23 and 24. “Ye shall not walk in the manners of the nation.” But: “Ye shall inherit their land, and I will give it unto you.” God never only takes something away. He separates a man from the old ways so he can give him something better.

“That ye should be mine”

The clean and unclean animals of chapter 11 were a lesson Israel ate every day: as God sets apart the clean from the unclean, he has severed, cut apart, Israel from the nations. When Peter saw a sheet of unclean beasts in a vision, God was teaching that same lesson about people, and opening the door to the nations:

“And he said unto them, Ye know how that it is an unlawful thing for a man that is a Jew to keep company, or come unto one of another nation; but God hath shewed me that I should not call any man common or unclean.”

Acts 10:28

“Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you, And will be a Father unto you, and ye shall be my sons and daughters, saith the Lord Almighty.”

2 Corinthians 6:17–18

Separate is not lonely. He separates you to himself: “that ye should be mine,” “I will be a Father unto you.”

Real world: his, and being made holy

The chapter ends where part one began, with familiar spirits: the sin of seeking anyone but God. Between the two stands the whole weight of the law. And then this:

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11
  • SinGod takes it seriously enough to write death beside it. So should we.
  • Regret“Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more.” Both halves are for you.
  • Grace“I am the LORD which sanctify you.” He does the making.
  • TodaySeparated to him: cut off one thing from the old manners today, and tell a brother which one.

Points to remember

  1. Molech“if the people of the land do any ways hide their eyes from the man” 20:4If the people looked away, the LORD himself would set his face against the man and his family. Don't hide your eyes when a child is hurt.
  2. Spirits“the soul that turneth after such as have familiar spirits, and after wizards” 20:6Seeking them is unfaithfulness to God. Bring your fear to the LORD himself.
  3. Sanctify“I am the LORD which sanctify you.” 20:8You obey; he makes you holy, and he will finish it.
  4. The penalty“their blood shall be upon them” 20:11The wages of sin is death. Jesus took that death for us.
  5. Separated“I the LORD am holy, and have severed you from other people, that ye should be mine” 20:26Separated from the old manners, to him, to receive what he gives.
  6. The woman“Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more.” John 8:11No condemnation, and a new way to walk.

Question 1 of 6

Molech

What did the LORD say about the people who hid their eyes when a man gave his child to Molech?

What verses tell us about this?

“But when Jesus saw it, he was much displeased, and said unto them, Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God.”

Answer

If they hid their eyes and let him live, the LORD himself would set his face against that man and his family. Looking away from a child being harmed is not neutral. Don't hide your eyes: speak up.

Question 4 of 6

The penalty

What does the penalty tell us about how God sees sin?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

Answer

That sin is deadly serious to a holy God: its wage is death. These sentences were for Israel's courts, but the wage is for every sinner, and Jesus paid it.
