Part one

The wedding at Cana

Verses 1–12

The party runs dry, and Jesus does His first sign.

A wedding feast could go on for a whole week (Samson's lasted “seven days,” Judges 14:12). The groom's family supplied the food and drink for every guest.

“They have no wine.”

Running out wasn't a small slip. It was a public disgrace for the new couple and their family, the kind of thing a small town remembers for years.

Was Jesus being rude to His mother?

No. “Woman” was a respectful way to address a lady. Jesus uses the same word from the cross, when He makes sure she'll be cared for: “Woman, behold thy son!” (John 19:26).

“What have I to do with thee?” was a common saying, something like “What does that have to do with you and me?” Jesus is gently telling her that He now moves on His Father's timetable, not the family's.

And that timetable has a name: “mine hour.” Watch it through John's Gospel:

2:4not yet come
7:30not yet come
8:20not yet come
12:23is come
17:1is come

“His hour” is the cross: His death, His resurrection, and His return to the Father (John 13:1). Everything in His life was heading there on purpose.

Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it.

These are the last words of Mary recorded anywhere in the Bible. And they point away from herself, straight to Jesus.

She didn't know what He would do. Neither did the servants. Obedience usually comes before understanding, not after.

Six stone jars

That's about 600 to 900 bottles. Way more than one party needed.

The jars held water for ceremonial washing before meals (Mark 7:3). They were made of stone because, under Jewish custom, stone couldn't become “unclean” the way clay pots could (Leviticus 11:33). And the servants filled them up to the brim. They left no room for anything but what Jesus would do.

Water made wine

No words over the jars, no show. The water simply became wine somewhere between the jar and the cup.

Notice who knew: “the servants which drew the water.” The man in charge had no idea. The ones who just did what Jesus said got the inside view.

The best, saved for last

The “governor of the feast” was the master of ceremonies. He knew the usual trick: serve the good stuff first, then the cheap stuff once nobody can tell.

The world's way

Sin works like the world's party: it starts sweet and ends bitter. Jesus works the other way around. With Him, the best is always still ahead.

A word about the wine

This sign is about Jesus, not about drinking. The same Bible says:

“Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging: and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise.”

Proverbs 20:1

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit.”

Ephesians 5:18

For a man who knows what a bottle can do, here's the point of Cana: Jesus takes what is empty and fills it to the brim. A bottle never filled anything. Being filled with the Spirit is what actually fills the empty place.

Not just a miracle: a sign

The word John uses is sēmeion, a sign. A sign doesn't point at itself. It points to something bigger. John picks seven of them:

  1. Water to wineJohn 2
  2. An official's son healedJohn 4
  3. A lame man walksJohn 5
  4. Five thousand fedJohn 6
  5. Walking on the seaJohn 6
  6. A blind man seesJohn 9
  7. Lazarus raisedJohn 11

Why? “These are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name” (John 20:31). Every vine turns water into wine slowly, over a season. Jesus, the Creator, did it in a moment.

Part two

My Father's house

Verses 13–22

Jesus walks into the temple and turns over the tables.

A market in the house of prayer

At Passover, huge crowds came to offer sacrifices. Merchants sold approved animals, and money changers swapped foreign coins for the special silver coin the temple tax required, usually at a markup.

They set up in the Court of the Gentiles, the only part of the temple where non-Jews were allowed to come and pray. The noise and haggling crowded them out.

Doves were the offering of the poor, for people who couldn't afford a lamb (Leviticus 5:7). The people with the least got squeezed the most.

Angry, without sin

Jesus calls the temple “my Father's house.” No prophet ever talked that way. He's claiming to be the Son of the One who owns the place.

His disciples remembered Psalm 69:9: “For the zeal of thine house hath eaten me up.” Zeal is burning love for something. What made Jesus angry wasn't an insult to Him. It was people being blocked from God and worship being turned into a business.

“Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath.”

Ephesians 4:26

Anger isn't always sin. The question is what you're angry about, and what you do with it. Jesus took His time (He “made a scourge”), He wasn't defending His pride, and He hurt no one.

Two temples

The building

46 years

Herod started rebuilding around 20 B.C. It still wasn't finished. The Romans destroyed it in A.D. 70.

His body

3 days

Crucified, buried, and raised on the third day, just as He said. It will never be destroyed again.

The temple was the place where God met with people. Jesus is saying He is that place now (remember John 1:14, God “pitched His tent” among us). They demanded a sign, and He gave them the greatest one of all: His own resurrection.

It made sense later

The disciples didn't understand this when Jesus said it. Years later, after the empty tomb, it all clicked.

Some of what God is doing in your life won't make sense today. Hold on to His word anyway. One day you'll remember, and you'll see He knew exactly what He was doing.

Part three

What was in man

Verses 23–25

The crowd is impressed. Jesus isn't fooled.

Believed, but not trusted

In Greek, “believed” and “commit” are the same word, pisteuō: to trust, to put your weight on.

The crowd→believed→in JesusImpressed by the miracles they saw.
Jesus→did not commit (trust) Himself→to themBecause He knew what was in their hearts.

Being impressed by what Jesus can do isn't the same as trusting Him with your life. Nobody can fake it with Him. He knows what is in man. And knowing every bit of it, He still went to the cross for us.

Points to remember

  1. Who Jesus is“Manifested forth his glory” 2:11His signs point to who He is: the Creator, the Son of God.
  2. Obedience“Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it” 2:5Do what He says, even before it makes sense. The obedient see the miracle up close.
  3. New life“Thou hast kept the good wine until now” 2:10He fills what is empty, and with Him the best is still ahead.
  4. Worship“Make not my Father's house an house of merchandise” 2:16His anger was love for God's house and for the people shut out of it.
  5. The resurrection“In three days I will raise it up” 2:19His resurrection is the sign that proves everything He said.
  6. Real faith“He knew what was in man” 2:25Don't just be impressed by Jesus. Trust Him. He knows your heart.

Question 1 of 6

Who Jesus is

What do Jesus' signs tell us about Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“But these are written, that ye might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing ye might have life through his name.”

John 20:31

Answer

A sign points past itself. Turning water into wine in a moment shows His glory: He is the Creator, doing instantly what a vine takes a whole season to do. John wrote the signs down so we would believe Jesus is the Son of God and have life through Him.

Question 2 of 6

Obedience

What happens when we do whatever He says?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The servants didn't understand the plan, but they obeyed all the way, “up to the brim.” They're the ones who saw the miracle up close. Obedience usually comes before understanding, and the people who obey are the ones who get to see what God does.

Question 3 of 6

New life

What does Jesus do with what is empty?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He fills it. He took jars meant for religious washing, filled them, and turned them into the best wine of the whole feast. Jesus doesn't just patch up an old life. He makes something new, and with Him the best is saved for last.

Question 4 of 6

Worship

Why was Jesus angry in the temple?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They had turned the house of prayer into a marketplace and crowded out the people who came to find God. His anger was zeal, a burning love for His Father's house. It wasn't a bad temper or wounded pride. Anger at sin, handled God's way, isn't sin.

Question 5 of 6

The resurrection

What sign did Jesus give to prove His authority?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

His own resurrection. “Destroy this body, and in three days I will raise it up.” He did exactly that. The empty tomb is the sign that proves He had the right to clean out His Father's house, and the right to everything else He claimed.

Question 6 of 6

Real faith

What's the difference between being impressed by Jesus and trusting Him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The crowd liked the miracles, but Jesus didn't trust Himself to them, because He saw their hearts. The disciples believed and followed Him. Real faith doesn't just admire what Jesus can do. It puts its whole weight on Him. He knows exactly what is in us, and He still came to save us.
