Genesis 8

God remembered Noah

The waters go down, a dove comes home with an olive leaf, and Noah steps out onto a washed earth. The first thing he builds is an altar, and God makes a promise that still holds every morning.

Part one

God remembered Noah

Verses 1–5

Five months of nothing but water. Then a wind.

Remembered

זָכַר said zakhar to call to mind, and then to act on it

God hadn't forgotten Noah for five months and then suddenly thought of him. In the Bible, when God “remembers” someone, He moves. He had made Noah a promise (“with thee will I establish my covenant,” 6:18), and now He keeps it: He sends a wind.

A dying thief asked Jesus for the same thing, and got more than he asked:

“And he said unto Jesus, Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom. And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.”

Luke 23:42–43

The tap turned off

The same God who opened the fountains and the windows (7:11) now shuts them. He started the flood, and He decides when it ends. Psalm 29:10 says it: “The LORD sitteth upon the flood.”

Whatever flood you're in, it has a ceiling and an end, and both are in God's hands.

The mountains of Ararat

Ararat was a land of high mountains north of Assyria, in what is now eastern Turkey, Armenia and Iran. (In 2 Kings 19:37 the KJV calls the same land “Armenia.”)

The verse says “the mountains of,” a range. It doesn't name the peak. The tallest, called Mount Ararat today, rises about 16,900 feet.

The ark came to rest exactly five months after the flood began: seventeenth day of the second month, to the seventeenth day of the seventh (7:11, 8:4). From these mountains, Noah's sons will spread south and west into the lands on this map.

Part two

The raven and the dove

Verses 6–12

Noah opens a window, and waits.

Two birds, two natures

The raven

At home in the wreckage

A raven eats what's dead. It could live on what the flood left floating. It went “to and fro” and never needed the ark again.

The dove

No rest out there

A dove needs clean ground and clean food. It found nothing to stand on, so it flew back to the only safe place it knew.

The Bible doesn't explain the birds, but men have long seen a lesson in them. Some of us lived like the raven for years, feeding on the wreckage and calling it freedom. A man whom God has made new finds out he can't rest out there anymore.

Pulled her in

Read it slowly. The dove comes back tired and empty-beaked. Noah doesn't shoo her off or scold her for coming back with nothing. He put forth his hand, took her, and pulled her in unto him.

If you went out and came back with nothing, if you fell and found no rest in it, that's how you're received. Come back to the window.

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

And come back to your brothers. A man who has slipped needs a hand to pull him in, not a lecture through the window. Call one of them today, and be that hand for the next man.

Notice what Noah did in between: he stayed. Seven days, then seven more. God's answers often come one small leaf at a time. Take the leaf, and keep waiting.

From the seventeenth day of the second month to the twenty-seventh day of the second month the next year: Noah's family lived in the ark a year and ten days.

He waited for the word

On the first day of the first month Noah lifted the covering and saw dry ground with his own eyes (v. 13). He stayed in nearly eight more weeks, until “God spake unto Noah.” He went in on God's word, and he came out on God's word.

Many of you know what it is to have a season in an ark: a program, a jail term, a sober house, a hard year shut in. When the ground looks dry, it's tempting to jump. Wait for the word. Pray, read, and ask your pastor and your brothers before you step out.

A second start

To Adam

“Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it…”

Genesis 1:28

To Noah

“…be fruitful, and multiply upon the earth.”

Genesis 8:17

Same blessing, second start. Everything that went into the ark came out, family and animals together, and God hands the world back to men.

A new start isn't a punishment to get through. It's a commission. When God brings you out of your ark, He sends you out to build, to raise a family, to work, and to bless the people around you.

Part four

While the earth remaineth

Verses 20–22

An altar, a sweet savour, and a promise.

The first thing he built

Not a house. Not a barn. An altar. It's the first altar named in the Bible. Before Noah built anything for himself on the new earth, he gave thanks to the God who had carried him through.

Now you see why God had him bring the clean animals “by sevens” (7:2). God had planned for this worship a year before. A burnt offering was burned whole: nothing kept back, all of it given to God.

We don't build stone altars now; Christ was the sacrifice once for all. But first things still come first:

“I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.”

Romans 12:1

Same heart, different answer

Before the flood

“And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually.”

Genesis 6:5

After the flood

“…for the imagination of man’s heart is evil from his youth; neither will I again smite any more every thing living, as I have done.”

Genesis 8:21

Same diagnosis both times: man's heart is evil. The flood washed the world, but not the heart. Sin climbed off the ark with Noah's family, as chapter 9 will show. What changed? An altar and a sacrifice stand between the two verses. Now God answers with mercy, not because man got better, but because an offering was made.

A new town, a new job, thirty days clean: good things, but none of them is a new heart. Only God gives that:

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Question 1 of 6

God's faithfulness

What does it mean that God remembered Noah?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I will remember my covenant, which is between me and you and every living creature of all flesh; and the waters shall no more become a flood to destroy all flesh.”

Genesis 9:15

“And he said unto Jesus, Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom. And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.”

Luke 23:42–43

Answer

It doesn't mean God had forgotten. It means He acted on His promise: He sent the wind and dried the earth. God's remembering is never just a thought. If you belong to Him, He has His eye on you in the middle of your flood, and He will move at the right time.

Question 2 of 6

Waiting

What did Noah do while he waited for dry ground?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the LORD.”

Psalm 27:14

Answer

He watched, he sent out the birds, and he stayed put. He didn't sit idle and he didn't panic. He kept looking for signs of God's work, took the olive leaf as a gift, and waited seven days more. Waiting on God is active: pray, watch, keep doing the next right thing.

Question 3 of 6

Obedience

Why didn't Noah leave the ark as soon as the ground looked dry?

What verses tell us about this?

“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.”

Proverbs 3:5–6

Answer

Because God had put him in, and he trusted God to tell him when to come out. What looked dry to Noah's eyes wasn't yet ready; nearly two months later the earth was fully dried, and then God spoke. Big moves, out of a program, back home, into a new job, go best on God's timing and with godly counsel.

Question 4 of 6

Worship

What was the first thing Noah did on the new earth?

What verses tell us about this?

“I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.”

Romans 12:1

“And walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour.”

Ephesians 5:2

Answer

He built an altar and worshipped, before a house or a field. Christ has made the sacrifice for us. Our part is to give ourselves to God first: the first minutes of the day, the first word of thanks when we come through something hard.

Question 5 of 6

Sin

Did the flood change the human heart?

What verses tell us about this?

“The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?”

Jeremiah 17:9

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

No. God says the heart is the same after the flood as before. Changing a man's surroundings doesn't change his heart. That's why a geographic cure never works by itself. What a man needs is to be made new, and that happens in Christ.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

What has God promised for as long as the earth remains?

What verses tell us about this?

“For this is as the waters of Noah unto me: for as I have sworn that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth; so have I sworn that I would not be wroth with thee, nor rebuke thee.”

Isaiah 54:9

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Answer

That planting and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night will keep coming. Through Isaiah, God points to that same oath to promise His people He would not stay angry with them. If the sun came up today, God kept His word today. He'll keep the rest of it too.
