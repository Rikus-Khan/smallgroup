Where we are in 2 Corinthians

About AD 56, Paul is writing from Macedonia to the church he started in Corinth. In chapters 1 to 9 he was warm. From chapter 10 he answers the men who have been running him down. Chapter 10 settled how he will fight: not with the flesh. Now, in the second half of his answer, he does something he hates doing.

1–7Paul's heart, a church set right
8–9The offering for the poor
10The weapons
11–12The fool's boast
13Examine yourselves; goodbye

Newcomers have been bragging about their credentials and mocking Paul. So he plays their game, and calls it foolishness each time, because he knows boasting is not what matters. Read alone, this chapter still makes sense: a pastor guards his people from smooth impostors, then shows them what a real servant of Christ looks like.

Part one

A jealous love

Verses 1–6

Paul is afraid for his people, and he explains why.

Promised to one husband

  1. BetrothedYou came to Christ, and Paul brought you“I have espoused you”
  2. WaitingNow: keep faith with Him alone“godly jealousy”
  3. The dayPresented to Christ, whole“a chaste virgin to Christ”

Paul pictures himself as the father of the bride, who promised her to one husband and now wants her faithful until the wedding. “Espoused” means promised in marriage. His jealousy is the godly kind: the protective love of a father, like God's own (Exodus 34:14).

“A chaste virgin” is not about your past. It is about undivided loyalty from now on. Some of the Corinthians had a rough history, and Paul knew it: “And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus” (1 Corinthians 6:11). Washed means washed. Today your eyes go to Him alone.

The serpent's way, in three moves

1 · Doubt“Yea, hath God said…?”

He starts with a question about what God really meant (Genesis 3:1).

2 · Deny“Ye shall not surely die”

Then he says the price isn't real (Genesis 3:4).

3 · Flatter“Ye shall be as gods”

Then he offers something better than God gave (Genesis 3:5).

Notice what he never says: “Come and be bad.” It always sounds clever, reasonable, even kind. “Beguiled” means deceived. Eve said it herself afterwards: “The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat” (Genesis 3:13).

Paul wants the Corinthians to stay in “the simplicity that is in Christ”, which just means trusting Him plainly. Every time that “good reason” for the thing you know is wrong shows up, hear the serpent. It is the same voice, three moves at a time.

Another Jesus, another spirit, another gospel

Another Jesus

a Jesus who is less

A good teacher only, or a Jesus plus a list of rules. The Jesus of the Bible is God come as a man, who died for sinners and rose again.

Another spirit

a spirit you have to chase

You received the Holy Spirit when you believed. Nobody has to bring you a second blessing, or sell you one.

Another gospel

grace plus something

Faith in Jesus, plus a ritual, a payment, or good behavior. The true gospel is a gift, received by faith.

“But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed.”

Galatians 1:8

Paul's last line in verse 4 is a bitter joke: “ye might well bear with him.” In other words, you put up with this nonsense far too easily. Don't. If a man changes who Jesus is, or what saves you, stop listening, however nice he is.

Rough in speech, sure in knowledge.

The “very chiefest apostles” is a dig. Most Bible teachers think Paul means the newcomers who gave themselves that grand title (he calls them “false apostles” in verse 13), not Peter and the Twelve. He admits he's no polished speaker. But he knows Christ, and what he teaches is sound.

If you don't talk pretty, don't let a smooth talker tell you what's true. A plain man who knows Jesus and says so is worth ten men with perfect sentences. The test is always what a man says about Christ, and not how he says it.

Part two

Free of charge, and in disguise

Verses 7–15

Paul took no pay. The impostors took everything, and looked like angels.

Who paid Paul's bills in Corinth?

At Corinth Paul worked with his hands, “for by their occupation they were tentmakers” (Acts 18:3). When that wasn't enough, gifts came down from Macedonia, from the churches at Philippi and Thessalonica.

That is what he means by “I robbed other churches.” It is a joke with a point: he took support from them so that Corinth wouldn't have to pay. Philippi had helped him from the start (Philippians 4:15–16).

So no one could say he was after their money. He worked, he took from friends, and he preached to them “freely.”

Taking nothing, so there is nothing to say

If Paul had taken their money,the impostors could say “we're just the same.”
→
But he took none,so they can't claim to be like him.

“Occasion” just means an opening. Paul closes it. The men who wanted a reason to look as good as him couldn't have one while he took nothing. “Wherefore? because I love you not? God knoweth.” He refuses their money because he loves them.

Real love is sometimes free, and sometimes gives up what it's owed. Watch for the man who has a price on everything about Jesus.

Paul's words are plain: “no marvel.” Don't be surprised. The enemy does not appear in a red cape. He comes as a friend, a bargain, or a preacher. “Whose end shall be according to their works” (v. 15): what they are headed for catches up with them in the end.

Three true things about the enemy

He is real

a prowling adversary

“Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour” (1 Peter 5:8).

He is a liar

he deceives

“That old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world” (Revelation 12:9).

He is beaten

and you're not on your own

“Ye are of God, little children, and have overcome them: because greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world” (1 John 4:4).

Don't be afraid of him, and don't be careless with him. If you are in Christ, the Holy Spirit lives in you from the day you believed, and He is greater. And a believer cannot lose Christ by being deceived for a day. If you fell for a lie yesterday, say so to God (1 John 1:9) and get up.

Part three

Boasting as a fool

Verses 16–22

Paul answers the braggers, with a smile and a sting.

Five things a false leader does to you

  • Binds you“bring you into bondage”more rules, more fear, a leash on you
  • Eats you“devour you”lives off your money, your time, your work
  • Takes“take of you”always asking, never giving
  • Lifts himself“exalt himself”it is all about him, not Christ
  • Hits you“smite you on the face”shame, threats, or worse

Verse 19 is Paul being sarcastic: “ye suffer fools gladly, seeing ye yourselves are wise.” You pride yourselves on your wisdom, and you let this happen. Many of our men have been under a boss or a preacher who did these five things. If you have, it wasn't Christ's doing, and you can walk away. Jesus does the opposite: He gives, He serves, and He sets free.

Same pedigree, and more

The newcomersWe are Hebrews! We are Israelites! We are the seed of Abraham!

PaulSo am I. So am I. So am I.

Paul has the same background they brag about. But he doesn't stop there, because a family tree isn't what makes a man a servant of Christ. The next verses show what does: what a man has been willing to suffer for the sake of others.

A man's papers, his past, his family name or his record is not what Paul counts. In Christ, a man with nothing on his résumé and a man with everything stand in the same place.

Part four

What it cost

Verses 23–33

A list no impostor could match, and a basket.

The tally, so far

Beaten by the Jews“forty stripes save one”: 39 each time5
Beaten with rodsby Roman officers (Philippi, Acts 16:22–23)3
Stoned, and left for deadat Lystra (Acts 14:19)1
Shipwreckedand the one in Acts 27 is still ahead3
“A night and a day … in the deep”in the open sea, holding on1

Five times thirty-nine lashes is 195 lashes. The Jews kept to thirty-nine so as never to go over the limit of forty set in Deuteronomy 25:3. And Acts tells only two of these stories before this letter: the rods at Philippi, and the stoning at Lystra. Paul has been through far more than anyone knows.

Where some of it happened

You can follow the list on a map, because Acts gives us places for some of it.

Philippi: beaten with rods, then jailed. “Commanded to beat them. And when they had laid many stripes upon them, they cast them into prison” (Acts 16:22–23).

Lystra: “having stoned Paul, drew him out of the city, supposing he had been dead” (Acts 14:19).

Damascus: a basket and a wall. That one is at the end of this chapter.

Danger on every side

The last one is worst: “in perils among false brethren.” The wounds that hurt most come from men who say they're on your side. Paul came through rivers and robbers; what stayed with him was being let down by people he trusted.

And v. 27 adds the body: “weariness and painfulness, watchings often, hunger and thirst, fastings often, cold and nakedness.” Tired, sleepless, hungry, cold, and short of clothes. If you have slept rough or gone hungry, you know a little of this.

And then, daily, the care of the churches

All of these were his. Every church he planted was a family he worried about: “that which cometh upon me daily.” The beatings were over in a day. The care never stopped.

He wasn't made of iron. He leaned on the One who cares for him: “Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you” (1 Peter 5:7). Whatever you're carrying today (a child, a debt, a brother in trouble), hand it over by name.

A pastor who feels it

“Weak” means struggling. “Offended” means made to stumble, caused to fall. When a believer is struggling, Paul feels it with him. When someone causes one to stumble, he burns with anger and grief.

Think about that if you have fallen. Somewhere there is somebody who is not shaking his head at you. He is hurting for you, and wants you back on your feet. That is a pastor's heart, and it is only a small picture of Christ's.

I will glory in my weaknesses.

Verse 30 is the turn. After the whole list, what Paul boasts about is not his strength but his weakness. And to prove he is telling the truth, he calls on God Himself (v. 31). One last example follows.

The great apostle in a basket

Paul had come to Damascus years before to arrest Christians. Now the governor under King Aretas had it watched, and the Jews were watching the gates too, “day and night to kill him” (Acts 9:24). So his friends did the one thing that worked: they put him in a basket and lowered him down the outside of the wall.

The man who has just listed his beatings now tells the one story that makes him look foolish. He is not proud of it. He tells it because it is true, and because his strength was never the point.

From the basket to this letter

  1. about AD 34–35Saul meets Jesus on the road to DamascusActs 9
  2. soon afterLet down in a basketActs 9:25
  3. about AD 46–48Stoned at LystraActs 14:19
  4. about AD 49–52Beaten with rods at PhilippiActs 16:22
  5. about AD 56Writes this letter“the care of all the churches”
  6. about AD 59–60The shipwreck of Acts 27 is still aheadhe is not finished

Twenty years of this, and he is still on his feet. That is how grace works over time. The man in the basket is the same man who is still at it. If you ran, if you fell, if you got out of a bad place any way you could, hear this: running away is not the same as failing. Sometimes a basket is the wise move.

Today: if there is a place or a person that is a trap for you, don't argue with it. Make a basket. Pick one door out, tell one brother, and write his number where you'll see it.

Points to remember

  1. The jealousy“I am jealous over you with godly jealousy” 11:2Paul loves his church like a father who promised his daughter to one husband. “Washed” means washed.
  2. The serpent“as the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty” 11:3Doubt, deny, flatter. Temptation always sounds reasonable, so hold to the simplicity that is in Christ.
  3. Another Jesus“another Jesus, … another spirit, … another gospel” 11:4Test any teacher: does he keep the true Jesus and grace through faith, and does he bind, devour, take, exalt himself, or hit?
  4. The disguise“Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light” 11:14It will look good. Don't be shocked, and don't be afraid: the One in you is greater.
  5. The cost“the care of all the churches” 11:28Beaten, stoned, wrecked, hungry, and carrying the churches daily. Real service costs something. Hand your cares to Him.
  6. Weakness“If I must needs glory, I will glory of the things which concern mine infirmities” 11:30Paul boasts in a basket. The strength was God's, and escaping a trap is not failure.

Question 1 of 6

The jealousy

Why is Paul jealous for the Corinthians, and what picture does he use?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

He loves them like a father who has promised his daughter to one husband, and wants her faithful to Christ. His jealousy is protective love. And he does not hold their past against them: they are washed.

Question 2 of 6

The serpent

What does the serpent's trick in Eden teach about how temptation works?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made. And he said unto the woman, Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?”

Genesis 3:1

“And the serpent said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die: For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.”

Genesis 3:4–5

Answer

It starts by making you doubt God's word, then says the price isn't real, then offers something better than what God gave. It always sounds reasonable. Stay with the plain trust that is in Christ.

Question 3 of 6

Another Jesus

How can a man tell a false teacher from a true one?

What verses tell us about this?

“But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed.”

Galatians 1:8

Answer

A true teacher keeps the real Jesus and the gospel of grace, and serves you. A false one changes Jesus or the gospel, and binds, devours, takes from, exalts himself over, or hits the people he leads.

Question 4 of 6

The disguise

Why is it no marvel that evil comes dressed as good?

What verses tell us about this?

“Ye are of God, little children, and have overcome them: because greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world.”

1 John 4:4

Answer

Because Satan himself comes as an angel of light, so his servants do too. Expect it, and test what you hear. You are not on your own: the One in you is greater.

Question 5 of 6

The cost

What did serving Christ cost Paul?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Beatings, a stoning, three shipwrecks, danger on every side, hunger and cold, and the daily care of all the churches. Real service costs something, and he never quit.

Question 6 of 6

Weakness

What does Paul boast about instead of his strength?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

His weaknesses: his infirmities, and the day he was let down a wall in a basket. The strength was always God's, and a man who has to run is not a man who has failed.
