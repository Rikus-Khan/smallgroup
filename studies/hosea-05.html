Part one

A snare and a net

Verses 1–7

The men who should have protected the people were trapping them.

Traps on the high places

A quick reminder: Hosea preached to the northern kingdom, Israel, often called Ephraim after its biggest tribe, in about 750–715 BC. Assyria was rising, and Israel had only a few years left before it fell.

Three groups are called out: the priests, the people, and the king's house. These were supposed to lead the nation to God. Instead they were “a snare” and “a net.”

Mizpah and Tabor were hilltops, probably places of false worship, one near the southern border and one far north. From one end of the land to the other, the leaders were catching people like birds.

One word, two directions

God toward Israel→“I know Ephraim”Nothing is hidden. He sees the altars, the deals, the nights. And He still calls them by name.
Israel toward God→“they have not known the LORD”They knew about Him: the feasts, the songs, the old stories. They didn't know Him.

“Know” is one of Hosea's big words. In Hebrew it's the word for the closest kind of knowing, the way a husband and wife know each other. That's what God wanted with His people. Not a nodding acquaintance. A marriage.

God knowing everything about you could sound frightening. But the One who sees it all is the same One who keeps calling you home.

Why they couldn't just turn

“Frame their doings” means arrange their lives. Their habits were built in such a way that turning back to God never happened. And “the spirit of whoredoms”, a pull toward other gods, as strong as lust, had taken up residence “in the midst of them.”

  • try harderthey had tried, in their way. A pull that strong doesn't let go because you frown at it.
  • promise to do betterpromises made inside the trap keep getting broken inside the trap
  • be set freefrom outside. A slave needs Someone to buy him out.

“Jesus answered them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin.”

John 8:34

Anyone who's fought an addiction knows this. Sin isn't only something you do. After a while it's something that has you. That's why Hosea's whole book is about a God who comes after His people and buys them back (Hosea 3).

They still went through the motions: big sacrifices, the new-moon feasts. But they treated God like one more god to keep happy. You can't bribe your way to Him.

“Strange children” (v. 7) means a generation raised without knowing the LORD. And “a month shall devour them”: the very feast days they trusted would soon be the days their ruin came.

Part two

Sound the alarm

Verses 8–12

War on the border, and a slow rot from the inside.

Town to town

The cornet was a ram's horn, blown to warn of an attack. Hosea hears the alarm passing north from hilltop to hilltop through Benjamin, the border land between Judah and Israel.

This probably points to the war of about 734 BC, when Israel and Judah fought each other and Assyria stepped in (2 Kings 16:5–9). God's own people, at each other's throats.

“That which shall surely be” (v. 9): God isn't guessing. He warned them ahead of time, so they could still turn.

A town renamed

Beth-el → Beth-aven Beth-el means “house of God.” Beth-aven means “house of nothing,” or “house of wickedness.”

Bethel was where Jacob met God and said, “this is none other but the house of God” (Genesis 28:17). Two hundred years before Hosea, King Jeroboam set up a golden calf there (1 Kings 12:28–29). Hosea won't even call it by its old name.

A place can keep the sign that says “house of God” and have nothing of God left inside. So can a man. The good news is that it works the other way too: God can take a life that's been a house of nothing and make it His house again.

A moth, not a hammer

The moth is quiet. You don't notice it until the coat is full of holes.

Before the lion of verse 14, God says He'll be like a moth and like rottenness: slow, quiet decay from the inside. Judah's princes were “like them that remove the bound”, men who secretly moved a neighbor's boundary stone to steal his land (Proverbs 23:10). Small, quiet theft. Small, quiet ruin.

Many of us lived this. It didn't fall apart in one night. Little by little, the job, the marriage, the health, the trust wore thin. The moth is a warning, and a mercy: it gives time to wake up before the lion comes.

Part three

The wrong doctor

Verses 13–15

Sick and wounded, they went to everyone but God.

EphraimWe're sick. We're bleeding. Help us.

The king of AssyriaOf course. That will be a thousand talents of silver.
could not heal

“And Pul the king of Assyria came against the land: and Menahem gave Pul a thousand talents of silver, that his hand might be with him to confirm the kingdom in his hand.”

2 Kings 15:19

“King Jareb” means something like “King Pick-a-fight,” a nickname for the Assyrian king. Israel paid him to make the pain stop. He took the money and later took the nation.

That's what the wrong cure always does. The bottle, the pipe, the woman, the next deal: they all promise to treat the wound, they all send a bill, and none of them can heal it. Only the LORD can: “I am the LORD that healeth thee” (Exodus 15:26).

The lion, and the open door

Assyria would be the lion's paws, but God says plainly, “I, even I.” This was His discipline, after years of warnings.

Then: “I will go and return to my place, till...” He steps back and waits. Not to get rid of them, but to let them feel the emptiness, until they're ready to say, “We were wrong,” and come looking for Him.

“Seek me early” means seek Me eagerly, first thing, like a man up before dawn. Bottom can be the place where a man finally looks up.

He came to himself

Jesus told a story about the same thing: a son who took his share, wasted it, and ended up feeding pigs.

“And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father's have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee,”

Luke 15:17–18

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

Hard times aren't proof that God has given up on you. Sometimes they're the very thing that turns you toward home. And when you turn, you'll find He's been watching the road the whole time.

Points to remember

  1. God sees“Israel is not hid from me” 5:3Nothing is hidden from God, and the One who sees it all still calls us by name.
  2. Bondage“They will not frame their doings to turn” 5:4Sin becomes a master. A slave needs Someone to set him free.
  3. Empty religion“They shall not find him” 5:6God isn't found by bringing everything but the heart.
  4. The wrong cure“Yet could he not heal you” 5:13Every false cure sends a bill. Only the LORD heals.
  5. Judgment“I will be unto Ephraim as a moth” 5:12God's discipline is real, and it comes after many warnings, meant to wake us up.
  6. Hope“Till they acknowledge their offence, and seek my face” 5:15God is waiting for us to own it and come home.

Question 1 of 6

God sees

Can a man hide what he's doing from God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. “I know Ephraim, and Israel is not hid from me.” God saw every altar and every deal. But He didn't see it all in order to walk away. He saw it all and still called them to come home. Being fully known by God is the start of being fully forgiven.

Question 2 of 6

Bondage

Why couldn't Israel just turn back to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus answered them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin.”

John 8:34

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

Answer

Their whole way of life was arranged around their idols, and “the spirit of whoredoms” had them. Sin had become their master. That's why trying harder isn't enough. A slave needs to be set free, and Jesus sets free everyone who comes to Him.

Question 3 of 6

Empty religion

Why didn't Israel find God, even with all their sacrifices?

What verses tell us about this?

“And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart.”

Jeremiah 29:13

Answer

Because they brought their flocks and herds but kept their hearts, and their idols. They treated God like one more power to pay off. God isn't found by bribes. He's found by those who search for Him with all their heart.

Question 4 of 6

The wrong cure

Where did Ephraim go for help, and why didn't it work?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Pul the king of Assyria came against the land: and Menahem gave Pul a thousand talents of silver, that his hand might be with him to confirm the kingdom in his hand.”

2 Kings 15:19

“He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.”

Psalm 147:3

Answer

To Assyria, the big power of the day, and they paid dearly for it. But Assyria couldn't heal them, and later destroyed them. Every false cure takes your money and leaves the wound. Only the LORD heals the broken in heart.

Question 5 of 6

Judgment

Why does God sometimes let things fall apart?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is good for me that I have been afflicted; that I might learn thy statutes.”

Psalm 119:71

Answer

Israel had ignored years of warnings. So God let the decay come, first slowly, like a moth, then hard, like a lion. His judgment was real. But even the lion had a purpose: to wake His people up before it was too late. Some of us learned more on the floor than we ever did on our feet.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

What is God waiting for?

What verses tell us about this?

“And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father's have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee,”

Luke 15:17–18

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

For His people to “acknowledge their offence, and seek my face.” Not to clean themselves up first. Just to stop blaming, own what they did, and turn toward Him. He's watching the road, ready to run to meet whoever comes home.
