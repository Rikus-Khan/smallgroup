Genesis 40

Forgotten in prison

Joseph is still in the dungeon, and still serving. Two of Pharaoh's officers dream in the same night, he tells them what God shows him, and the man he helps walks out and forgets him.

Part one

Two men in the ward

Verses 1–8

Joseph has every reason to look only at himself. Instead he looks at their faces.

From the day he was sold to the day he stood before Pharaoh was thirteen years. Genesis does not say how many of them were in the prison, but this chapter finds him there, and the next one adds two more years.

The dates are worked out from the ages in Genesis, so they are “about”. The years of waiting are not made up. Those are in the text.

Old words in verses 1–4

  1. butlerPharaoh's cupbearer, the man who served his wine. A trusted place, because he stood close to the king's cup
  2. bakerthe man over the king's bread and cakes, also close to the king's table
  3. offendedwronged, or angered (the king). Genesis does not say what they did
  4. wardcustody; here a guarded part of the prison
  5. chargedput in charge of, gave him the care of
  6. a seasona while; some time

Both men worked at the king's table, so whatever happened, it touched Pharaoh's food or drink. The text only says they “offended”, and that Pharaoh was “wroth” (very angry). Take it as it stands: two men who had failed their king, now locked up.

The LORD was with him in the prison

Verse 3 says “the house of the captain of the guard”. That is the title Potiphar carried (Genesis 37:36; 39:1), so this is the same man's house. Genesis 39 had already told us what God was doing there:

“But the LORD was with Joseph, and shewed him mercy, and gave him favour in the sight of the keeper of the prison.”

Genesis 39:21

Now the captain of the guard puts two high officers in Joseph's care: “he served them”. A prisoner, who was a slave first, waiting on men who had been in Pharaoh's palace. He could have resented it. He did the work.

That is where a man stands when life has gone wrong and nothing is fair: God is with him, and there is still something in front of him to do. Do that, in the place you are.

“Wherefore look ye so sadly?”

wherefore old word “why”. Joseph asks: Why are your faces so low today?

He is the one who has been sold by his brothers, falsely accused, and left in a prison. If any man had the right to look only at his own trouble, it was Joseph. But he looks up, sees two sad faces, and asks.

“Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others.”

Philippians 2:4

Pain can make a man look only at himself. Joseph shows another way, and it is the way back. A man who asks a brother, “What's wrong?” is already getting better himself. Look at the faces in your group today, and ask.

“Do not interpretations belong to God?”

Interpretations belong to God.

The two officers say, “there is no interpreter of it”. In the ancient world a dream could be taken as a message, and a dream with no one to read it left a man afraid. Joseph does not say, “I am gifted.” He points past himself: it is God's to give. “Tell me them, I pray you.”

“But there is a God in heaven that revealeth secrets, and maketh known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days.”

Daniel 2:28

Hundreds of years later Daniel says the same thing to another king. A man who gives God the credit has nothing to fall over when he is praised, and nothing to hide when he is not. Say it that way about your own good days: God did that.

Part two

Two dreams, two endings

Verses 9–19

The same words go to both men. The same night, the same three days, and not the same road.

“Restore thee unto thy place”: restored, “after the former manner when thou wast his butler”.

Notice who is doing the reading. Joseph says nothing of luck and nothing of his own cleverness. It is a message, and he hands over what he was given.

“Think on me”

Joseph has one request. Verse 14: “think on me when it shall be well with thee, and shew kindness, I pray thee, unto me, and make mention of me unto Pharaoh.” And verse 15: “here also have I done nothing that they should put me into the dungeon.”

בּוֹר said bor a pit, a cistern: the KJV says “dungeon” here

It is the same Hebrew word as the pit his brothers threw him into in Genesis 37. Thirteen years later he is still in a hole, and still asks only to be remembered.

There is nothing weak about asking for help. A man who asks the right person at the right time is wise. Say so to your brothers and pastor, before it gets dark. The danger is the next part: putting your whole hope in one man's memory.

The same words, an opposite end

Yet within three days shall Pharaoh lift up thine head, and restore thee unto thy place

Yet within three days shall Pharaoh lift up thy head from off thee, and shall hang thee on a tree

Verse 13 to the butler, verse 19 to the baker. Same prison, same night, same “three days”. In the first, a man's head is lifted up to the king's cup. In the second, it is lifted off.

The baker was the one who saw “that the interpretation was good” and spoke up. Joseph did not soften what came next. He did not make anything up either. He said what God showed him, even though it was the worst thing he could say to a man.

“Hang thee on a tree”

In verse 19 hanging is a public, shameful death, with the body left for the birds. The baker's end is meant to be seen.

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

Paul uses that picture for the cross. A man who hangs on a tree is under a curse in the law. The baker hung for his own offence. Jesus hung for ours, and took the curse that was ours, so that none of us has to carry it.

The baker is not a picture of Christ, and Genesis does not say it is. But when you read “hang thee on a tree”, remember who took that place for you.

Part three

Remembered, and forgotten

Verses 20–23

Everything Joseph said came true. Then the man he helped went home.

Exactly as he said

  1. ✓within three dayswhat Joseph told both men40:13, 19
  2. ✓lifted up the head of the chief butler and of the chief bakerboth men, at Pharaoh's birthday feast40:20
  3. ✓restored the chief butler unto his butlership again“as Joseph had interpreted to them”40:21–22
  4. ✓he hanged the chief bakerjust as he said40:22

Joseph's word held, twice, on the same day. That is what Pharaoh will hear about two years later. God was lining up the pieces.

One thing did not happen. The butler, whom he had asked to remember him, did not.

“But forgat him”

Yet did not the butler remember Joseph, but forgat him.

“Forgat” is the old way to say “forgot”. The verse says it twice, as if to make sure we feel it. The butler was not cruel. He was glad and busy, back at the cup, and Joseph slid out of his mind.

That is a common, quiet sin: ingratitude. Forgetting who helped us when we were low: a sponsor, a brother, a friend who answered at two in the morning, once we got better and got busy.

Do not shame yourself over it. Fix it today. Think of one man who helped you, and call him, or send him a message that says thank you.

Forgotten by a man, not by God

“Until the time that his word came: the word of the LORD tried him.”

Psalm 105:19

The psalm looks back on these years and says that God was working through them: his word tested Joseph until the time came. Two more years in the prison were not a mistake in his plan.

“Can a woman forget her sucking child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb? yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee.”

Isaiah 49:15

“For God is not unrighteous to forget your work and labour of love, which ye have shewed toward his name, in that ye have ministered to the saints, and do minister.”

Hebrews 6:10

If you feel forgotten, say so to your brothers in Christ and your pastor. Call or text them. Do not wait alone. People can forget. God does not.

Question 1 of 6

Serving

What did Joseph do when he noticed two men looking sad?

What verses tell us about this?

“Rejoice with them that do rejoice, and weep with them that weep.”

Romans 12:15

Answer

He looked at their faces and asked. He was in prison himself, with far more reason than they had to be sad, and he stopped to ask about theirs. He had been serving them already. Do the same: pick the man in your group who looks low, and ask him what is wrong. Sometimes one question is the help.

Question 2 of 6

Credit

Who does Joseph say gives the meaning of dreams?

What verses tell us about this?

“But there is a God in heaven that revealeth secrets, and maketh known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days.”

Daniel 2:28

Answer

God. Joseph never says, “I'm good at this.” He says it belongs to God, and then he asks for the dream. When you do something well, or when you stay sober another day, give it away the same way. It was a gift from him.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

Did the chief butler earn his place back?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. He had offended the king, he was in prison, and the word of restoration came as a gift, with nothing he had done to earn it. He got back the cup, his old place, “after the former manner”. That is what grace looks like. If you have a record behind you, God offers you a restored place too, because of Jesus, not because of your record.

Question 4 of 6

The truth

Did Joseph tell the baker only what he wanted to hear?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. The baker heard the good news given to the butler and hoped for the same. Joseph gave him the real word, “hang thee on a tree”, and did not dress it up. That is hard to do. A brother who tells you the truth, gently and without cruelty, is worth more than ten who tell you what you want. Be that brother, and let yourself be told.

Question 5 of 6

Forgotten

What did the chief butler do once he was free?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He forgot Joseph. Joseph had asked one thing, “think on me when it shall be well with thee”, and when it was well, the butler did not. It is the quiet sin of ingratitude. Think of the man who helped you when you were low and call him today. And do not let your hope rest on one man's memory.

Question 6 of 6

Waiting

When a man has been forgotten, has God forgotten him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Until the time that his word came: the word of the LORD tried him.”

Psalm 105:19

“yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee.”

Isaiah 49:15

“I waited patiently for the LORD; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry.”

Psalm 40:1

Answer

No. Men forgot Joseph, but the LORD did not, and the next chapter shows how. If you feel forgotten, you are not alone: call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor, and tell them. Waiting is easier with the company of men who are waiting beside you.
