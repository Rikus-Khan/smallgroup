Chapter 3 ended in the desert: a whole generation “could not enter in because of unbelief.” God had sworn, “They shall not enter into my rest.”

Chapter 4 asks the next question: is that rest closed for good? No. It's still open, and the writer spends the chapter showing what it is, how a man gets in, and Who is waiting for him.

Part one

A promise left us

Verses 1–5

The same good news they heard. This time, mix it with faith.

Heard, but not mixed with faith

Unto them

“The land is yours”

Israel heard good news: God would give them Canaan. They heard it, nodded, and didn't trust Him. The word sat in them like seed on concrete.

Unto us

“The rest is yours”

We've heard better news: Christ has done it all. Hearing isn't enough. Faith takes what God says and leans the whole weight of your life on it.

“Let us therefore fear” isn't fear of losing your salvation. It's the fear of a man standing at the border, hearing the good news, and never going in. “Come short” means fall behind, arrive too late. Don't stop short of what God has already given.

One rest, many pictures

  1. The seventh dayGod rests from finished workGenesis 2:2
  2. The sabbatha day off every week, a reminderExodus 20
  3. Canaana land at rest from warJoshua
  4. “To day”still offered, long afterPsalm 95, David
  5. Christthe rest itself: “I will give you rest”Matthew 11:28
  6. Glorythe rest that remains, for ever4:9

God's rest began on the seventh day, when His work was “finished.” He wasn't tired. He was done. Every rest after that, the weekly sabbath, the land of Canaan, was a picture pointing ahead.

Look at the tense in verse 3: “we which have believed do enter.” Not someday. A man who trusts Christ steps into God's rest now, and he'll have it in full in glory.

Part two

There remaineth a rest

Verses 6–11

Stop working for it. It's finished.

Which Jesus?

Here “Jesus” means Joshua, who led Israel into Canaan. It's the same name in Greek, and it means the LORD saves.

Here's the argument. Joshua brought them into the land. But hundreds of years later, David was still saying, “To day … harden not your hearts.” So Canaan wasn't the real rest. If Joshua had given it, God wouldn't still be offering another day. The first Joshua gave them land. The greater Jesus gives the rest itself.

Put the rock down

A lot of us came to Christ and kept pushing the same rock: if I stay clean long enough, if I make it up to everybody, maybe God will finally accept me. It slides back every time.

Entering God's rest means you “ceased from” your own works, the way God rested when creation was done. Jesus said “It is finished” on the cross. You don't finish it. You rest in it.

“Let us labour to enter” sounds backwards. It means be in earnest, make haste. Work hard at one thing: trusting the One who did the work.

Rest is not quitting

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Resting from your works for salvation doesn't mean you stop working at life. You still go to your job, still stay away from the old places, still make amends where you can. The difference is why. Not to get God to love you, but because He already does.

Today, when the voice says, “You haven't done enough to be forgiven,” answer it: “No, I haven't. Jesus has.”

Part three

Naked and opened

Verses 12–13

A word that cuts to the heart, so it can heal it.

Sharper than any sword

The Bible isn't an old book lying still on a shelf. It's “quick,” alive. You read it, and it reads you.

It cuts past the layers: past the face we show people, past the story we tell ourselves, down to why we really do what we do. “Discerner” is a judge's word. It sorts out what's true in us.

A surgeon's knife cuts too, but it cuts to heal. When a verse lands hard on you, that isn't God against you. That's God operating.

Naked and opened

Ever since Adam hid in the garden, men have hidden: “I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself” (Genesis 3:10). “Opened” pictures a neck bent back, face up, nowhere to look away.

That sounds terrifying, until you realize it means you can stop hiding. He already knows all of it, and He hasn't left.

Say it to Him plainly. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us” (1 John 1:9). Where the hidden thing was, there is mercy.

Part four

Come boldly

Verses 14–16

The One who sees everything is the One who understands.

He knows how it feels

“Touched with the feeling” means He suffers along with us. Jesus was hungry, tired, lonely, betrayed, and tempted by the devil himself (Matthew 4:1).

Being sinless didn't make it easier. The man who gives in never feels the last of the pressure. Jesus never gave in, so He felt every bit of it. Nobody knows temptation better.

So He isn't shocked by you, and He isn't disgusted. He's the one Person who knows exactly what you're up against.

Under the old law, only the high priest went behind the veil to God's mercy seat, once a year, “that he die not” (Leviticus 16:2).

When Jesus died, the veil was “rent in twain from the top to the bottom” (Matthew 27:51). God tore it. The way is open.

“Boldly” means freely, plainly, without hiding. Not cocky, not cleaned up first. Your High Priest is already there.

And you don't walk out empty: mercy for what you did, and grace to help with what you face.

If you fell yesterday

What shame says

“Stay away a while”

Clean up first. Give it a few days. Go back once you've earned the right to show your face.

What God says

“Come boldly”

Today. Now. To a throne of grace, not a judge's bench, where a High Priest who was tempted Himself is waiting for you.

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

“In time of need” means help that comes right on time. Today, the moment the pull hits, at work, in the truck, at 2 a.m., pray right then, one line: “Lord, I need help now.” That's coming to the throne.

Points to remember

  1. Rest“There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God” 4:9Not just a day or a land: rest with God in Christ, begun now by faith, and full in glory.
  2. Faith“Not being mixed with faith in them that heard it” 4:2Hearing isn't enough. The good news has to be trusted.
  3. Works“He also hath ceased from his own works” 4:10Stop pushing the rock to earn God's love. It is finished. Rest in it.
  4. The word of God“A discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart” 4:12It's alive. It cuts past every layer to the heart, and it cuts to heal.
  5. Our High Priest“In all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin” 4:15He felt the full weight of temptation. He understands you, and He's not ashamed of you.
  6. The throne of grace“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace” 4:16Don't stay away. Come today: mercy for yesterday, grace for today.

Question 1 of 6

Rest

What is the rest that still remains for God's people?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

God's own rest: the peace of a finished work. The sabbath and Canaan were pictures of it. A man who trusts Christ enters it now, and he'll have it in full in glory.

Question 2 of 6

Faith

Why did the word preached not profit Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They heard it but didn't trust it. Good news only helps the man who believes it. The same is true of us: hearing sermons doesn't save; trusting Christ does.

Question 3 of 6

Works

What does it mean to cease from your own works?

What verses tell us about this?

“But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness.”

Romans 4:5

“When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost.”

John 19:30

Answer

To stop trying to earn God's acceptance. Jesus finished the work on the cross. We still work hard at life, but out of thanks, not to pay for our salvation.

Question 4 of 6

The word of God

What does God's word do in a man's heart?

What verses tell us about this?

“Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts: And see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.”

Psalm 139:23–24

Answer

It's alive and at work. It cuts past our excuses to our real thoughts and motives, and shows us the truth about ourselves, not to destroy us but to heal us and lead us.

Question 5 of 6

Our High Priest

Why does it matter that Jesus was tempted?

What verses tell us about this?

“For in that he himself hath suffered being tempted, he is able to succour them that are tempted.”

Hebrews 2:18

“He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief: and we hid as it were our faces from him; he was despised, and we esteemed him not.”

Isaiah 53:3

Answer

Because He understands. He felt the full force of temptation and never gave in, so He knows what we face and is able to help us in it, not stand far off.

Question 6 of 6

The throne of grace

How should a man who fell yesterday come to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Answer

Boldly, and today. Not hiding, not waiting until he's cleaned himself up. He comes honestly to a throne of grace, through Jesus, and finds mercy for yesterday and help for today.
