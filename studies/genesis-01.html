Genesis 1

In the beginning God

Before there was anything, there was God. He spoke, and light, land, life and man came out of nothing, and he called it very good.

The book of Genesis

Who: Moses. Jesus calls these books “the law of Moses” (Luke 24:44), and says of Moses, “he wrote of me” (John 5:46).

When: in the forty years in the wilderness (Numbers 14:33; Acts 7:36), after God brought Israel out of Egypt.

To whom: Israel, a nation of freed slaves on their way to Canaan, who needed to know who their God was and where they came from.

Genesis means beginnings: of the world, of man, of sin, and of God's plan to bless the whole earth through one family, Abraham's.

Moses wrote it down after the LORD brought Israel out of Egypt, through the Red Sea, to Mount Sinai in Arabia (Galatians 4:25), where God gave them his law.

Genesis at a glance

  1. CreationSix days, and God restsch. 1–2
  2. The fallSin comes into the worldch. 3
  3. The floodNoah and the arkch. 6–9
  4. BabelThe nations scatteredch. 11
  5. Abram, 75God calls Abrahamch. 12
  6. Jacob, 130Jacob's family goes down to Egyptch. 46
  7. after 430 yearsOut of Egypt; Moses writes it downExodus 12:41

Chapters 1–11 cover the whole world: creation, the fall, the flood, and Babel. Then the book slows down and follows one family, four men in a row: Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and Joseph.

Genesis doesn't date things in years BC. It gives the ages of the fathers (chapters 5 and 11), and adding them up puts the flood 1,656 years after creation. We'll see that in chapter 5. After that it counts by the ages of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and Joseph.

Part one

Let there be light

Verses 1–5

Before anything else existed, God was already there.

In the beginning God

בָּרָא said bara “created.” In the Bible, only God is ever the one who does it.

Genesis doesn't argue that God exists. It starts with him. Before there was time, space, or one speck of dust, there was God, and everything else came from him.

“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.”

John 1:1

John starts his gospel the same way, and tells us who was there: the Word, Jesus. “All things were made by him” (John 1:3). The God who made everything is the God who came to save you.

Shapeless, empty, and dark

תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ said tohu va-vohu “without form, and void”: shapeless and empty

That's the starting point: a world with no shape and nothing living in it, under water and in the dark. Watch what God does with it in six days. First he gives it shape. Then he fills it.

“The Spirit of God moved”: the Hebrew word pictures a bird hovering over its nest. Moses uses it again of an eagle that “fluttereth over her young” (Deuteronomy 32:11).

Some men's lives look like verse 2 right now: no shape, empty, dark. That is exactly the kind of place where God starts working.

God said … and there was

Verse 2Darkness

“darkness was upon the face of the deep”

Verse 3God speaks

“Let there be light”

Verse 4Light

“God saw the light, that it was good”

God didn't work at it or fight for it. He said it, and it was. And this light came on the first day; the sun isn't made until the fourth. Light starts with God, not with the sun.

“For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.”

2 Corinthians 4:6

The evening and the morning

Notice the order. Each day starts with the evening, and then comes the morning. To this day, a Jewish day begins at sundown.

These are plain days: a number, an evening and a morning, six times over. Later, God sets Israel's whole working week by them (Exodus 20:11).

Here's what that says to a man who fell yesterday: the dark part comes first, but it is not the end of the day. God brings the morning.

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Part two

Shaping the world

Verses 6–13

Days two and three: God gives the shapeless world its shape.

The firmament

רָקִיעַ said raqia a spread-out expanse, like metal hammered out thin

Firmament is the old word for the expanse of the sky. God set it between the waters below and the waters above, and named it Heaven.

“The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork.”

Psalm 19:1

Every time you look up, the sky is preaching. It says: Somebody made this.

After his kind

  1. herba green plant
  2. after his kindeach true to its own kind
  3. whose seed is in itselfit carries the seed of the next one

Ten times in this chapter, God makes things “after his kind” or “after their kind.” He built order into life. An apple seed grows an apple tree, and a calf grows into a cow. Nothing is random.

On the third day the dry land comes up out of the water, and before the day is over it is green. God doesn't leave a thing bare. He shapes it, and then he makes it grow.

Part three

Filling the world

Verses 14–25

Days four, five and six: God fills what he has shaped.

He made the stars also

Moses doesn't even name the sun and the moon. The nations around Israel bowed down to them as gods; Egypt worshipped the sun. Genesis calls them lights that God made and hung in the sky, to mark the days, the seasons and the years.

And the stars, more than anyone can count, get five words: “he made the stars also.”

Whatever a man is tempted to bow down to, a bottle, money, a woman, his own strength, it's something God made, or something made out of what he made. Paul says that's where people go wrong:

“Who changed the truth of God into a lie, and worshipped and served the creature more than the Creator, who is blessed for ever. Amen.”

Romans 1:25

Part four

Let us make man

Verses 26–31

Until now, God has said “Let there be.” Now he says something new.

“Let us”

Before man, God doesn't just say “Let there be.” He stops, as it were, and says “Let us make man in our image.” Christians have long seen here a first hint of what the rest of the Bible makes plain: one God, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

  1. God spoke“And God said” (1:3)
  2. The Spirit moved“the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters” (1:2)
  3. The Word made“All things were made by him” (John 1:3)

Man was made by a God who has never been alone. That's why a man was never meant to live alone either, as chapter 2 will show.

In the image of God

So God created man in his own image,

in the image of God created he him;

male and female created he them.

Verse 27 is the Bible's first poem, and it's about man. An image is a likeness, a reflection: man was made to show what God is like, the way a son takes after his father. No animal was given that.

“Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God.”

James 3:9

That goes for every man you'll meet today, even the one you can't stand. And it goes for you. Whatever you've done, you are not trash. You're an image of God that sin has damaged, and he knows how to restore what he made.

A man's job

  1. replenishfill (not “fill again”)
  2. subduebring under control, put to use
  3. dominionrule, charge over

Before sin ever came into the world, God gave man work: fill the earth, take charge of it, look after the creatures. Work is not a curse. It was part of the very good world.

Dominion isn't a license to wreck things. A good king rules for the good of what's in his care, and he answers to the King above him. Go to work today as God's man, doing God's work.

“I have given you”

Look at the order. God hasn't yet told man one thing he must not do. First he gives: the plants, the trees, the food, the whole earth to look after.

“God that made the world and all things therein, seeing that he is Lord of heaven and earth, dwelleth not in temples made with hands; Neither is worshipped with men’s hands, as though he needed any thing, seeing he giveth to all life, and breath, and all things;”

Acts 17:24–25

That's grace: God gives before he asks. Salvation works the same way. It's not wages for a good week. It's a gift.

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

Seven times God looks at what he has made and calls it good. The last time, after he has made man, he calls it very good. There was nothing broken in it: no death, no shame, no addiction, no grave.

So what went wrong?

If the world God made was very good, the wreckage didn't come from him. Chapter 3 will show how sin came in, through one man:

“Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:”

Romans 5:12

Every man in this room has added to the mess. That's not a reason to give up; it's the reason we need a Saviour. And the God who made a very good world in six days can make a man new:

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Points to remember

  1. The Creator“In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth” 1:1God made everything there is, by his word, and it all belongs to him.
  2. God's word“Let there be light: and there was light” 1:3What God says happens. His word made the world, and it can remake a life.
  3. Man“In the image of God created he him” 1:27Every man bears God's likeness. No animal does.
  4. Sin“Behold, it was very good” 1:31The mess didn't come from God. Sin came in later, and God can make a man new.
  5. Grace“Behold, I have given you” 1:29God gives before he asks.
  6. A new day“The evening and the morning were the first day” 1:5The dark comes first, but God brings the morning.

Question 1 of 6

The Creator

Who made everything, and how did he do it?

What verses tell us about this?

“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.”

John 1:1–3

“Through faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear.”

Hebrews 11:3

Answer

God made it all, and he made it by speaking. Jesus, the Word, was there, and nothing was made without him. We know it the way Hebrews says: by faith in what God has told us. And if God made everything, then everything belongs to him, including you.

Question 2 of 6

God's word

What happens every time God speaks?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he spake, and it was done; he commanded, and it stood fast.”

Psalm 33:9

“For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.”

2 Corinthians 4:6

Answer

It happens. Over and over in this chapter: “And God said … and it was so.” His word doesn't need help. The same God still speaks through his word, and when he says “light” to a dark heart, the light comes on.

Question 3 of 6

Man

What makes a man different from every other creature?

What verses tell us about this?

“What is man, that thou art mindful of him? and the son of man, that thou visitest him? For thou hast made him a little lower than the angels, and hast crowned him with glory and honour. Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet:”

Psalm 8:4–6

Answer

He is made in God's image. The animals were made “after their kind”; man was made after God's likeness, to know him and to show what he is like. That's why every man matters, and why no man is beyond God's interest, however far he has fallen.

Question 4 of 6

Sin

If God made everything very good, why is a man's life such a mess?

What verses tell us about this?

“For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;”

Romans 3:23

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

Because sin came in after creation, and every one of us has joined in. The mess is real, and some of it is ours. But it isn't the way God made things, and it isn't the end. The God who made the world very good makes a new man out of anyone who is in Christ.

Question 5 of 6

Grace

What did God give man before he asked anything of him?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

Answer

Everything: life, his blessing, a whole world full of food, and work worth doing. God was giving long before he asked. He still works that way. Eternal life isn't something we earn by trying harder. It's his gift, received by faith in Jesus.

Question 6 of 6

A new day

What does “the evening and the morning” say to a man who fell yesterday?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

In God's days, the dark comes first and the morning follows. Yesterday's fall is real, but it isn't the end of the story. Confess it to God, plainly; he is faithful to forgive. Then tell a brother, get up, and live today in his new mercies.
