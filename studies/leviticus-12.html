Leviticus 12

A mother made clean

After a child was born, the mother was kept from the holy things for a set number of days. Then she brought an offering, the priest made an atonement for her, and she was clean. And if she could not afford a lamb, God made room for that too.

Part one

“The days of her purifying”

Verses 1–5

A birth, an eighth day, and days counted out.

A child is not the problem

  • conceived seedbecome pregnant
  • man childa boy; maid child, a girl (verse 5)
  • separation for her infirmitythe days a woman is set apart for her monthly weakness (chapter 15)
  • the eighth daythe day of circumcision, the sign of God's covenant with Abraham

Leviticus does not call the baby unclean, and it does not call the birth a sin. What made her unclean, verse 7 says, was “the issue of her blood.” A child is a gift:

“Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD: and the fruit of the womb is his reward.”

Psalm 127:3

“And he that is eight days old shall be circumcised among you, every man child in your generations, he that is born in the house, or bought with money of any stranger, which is not of thy seed.”

Genesis 17:12

Days counted out

A son
733 days
seven days, then “three and thirty days”: forty
A daughter
1466 days
“two weeks,” then “threescore and six days”: eighty

Threescore is sixty. Purifying is cleansing. Hallowed things are holy things. In the first days she was unclean “as in her separation”; in the longer days that followed she did not “come into the sanctuary,” and touched no hallowed thing.

Why twice as long for a daughter? Moses doesn't say, and we won't make up a reason. What he does say is that the days had an end: “until the days of her purifying be fulfilled.”

Part two

“She shall be clean”

Verses 6–8

An offering at the door, and a way back in.

A lamb, or two birds

If she could

a lamb and a bird

A lamb of the first year for a burnt offering, and a young pigeon or a turtledove for a sin offering.

“If she be not able”

two birds

Two turtles or two young pigeons: one for the burnt offering, one for the sin offering.

A lamb of the first year is a lamb under a year old. Turtles here are turtledoves, small wild doves. And look at the last words of each: the same priest, the same atonement, the same “she shall be clean.” The poor woman was not half clean. God priced the way back so that no mother in Israel was shut out.

The mother who brought two birds

Long afterward, a young mother came to the temple in Jerusalem to keep this very law:

“And when the days of her purification according to the law of Moses were accomplished, they brought him to Jerusalem, to present him to the Lord; (As it is written in the law of the Lord, Every male that openeth the womb shall be called holy to the Lord;) And to offer a sacrifice according to that which is said in the law of the Lord, A pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons.”

Luke 2:22–24

Mary brought the offering of “if she be not able to bring a lamb.” Jesus was born into a family that couldn't afford the lamb. They didn't need to bring one. “The Lamb of God” (John 1:29) was in Mary's arms.

“For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich.”

2 Corinthians 8:9

Real world: the mothers and the children

“But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law, To redeem them that were under the law, that we might receive the adoption of sons.”

Galatians 4:4–5
  • SinSome of us were not there when our children were born, or after. Call it what it was.
  • RegretYou can't go back to the eighth day. You can start being a father on this one.
  • GraceThe same atonement for the woman with a lamb and the woman with two birds. Nobody is too poor to be made clean.
  • HopeThe days of uncleanness had an end. Christ was born under the law to redeem those under it, and that includes you.
  • TodayThank the mother of your children, or your own mother, and pray for your kids by name.

Points to remember

  1. The child“in the eighth day the flesh of his foreskin shall be circumcised” 12:3The sign of God's covenant with Abraham; the child is a gift, not the problem.
  2. The days“until the days of her purifying be fulfilled” 12:4Forty days for a son, eighty for a daughter, and then they were over.
  3. The offering“make an atonement for her; and she shall be cleansed” 12:7A burnt offering and a sin offering at the door, and the priest made her clean.
  4. The poor“if she be not able to bring a lamb, then she shall bring two turtles, or two young pigeons” 12:8The same atonement and the same “she shall be clean” for the poor.
  5. The mother of Jesus“A pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons” Luke 2:24Mary brought the poor woman's offering, and carried the Lamb.

Question 1 of 5

The child

What was done for a baby boy on the eighth day?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he that is eight days old shall be circumcised among you, every man child in your generations, he that is born in the house, or bought with money of any stranger, which is not of thy seed.”

Genesis 17:12

Answer

He was circumcised, the sign of the covenant God made with Abraham. Jesus himself was circumcised on the eighth day (Luke 2:21).

Question 2 of 5

The days

How long was a new mother kept from the holy things?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For a son, seven days and then thirty-three: forty in all. For a daughter, two weeks and then sixty-six: eighty in all. Then the days of her purifying were fulfilled.

Question 3 of 5

The offering

What did she bring when her days were fulfilled, and what did the priest do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A lamb for a burnt offering and a young pigeon or a turtledove for a sin offering, at the door of the tabernacle. The priest offered them, made an atonement for her, and she was clean.

Question 4 of 5

The poor

What if she could not afford a lamb?

What verses tell us about this?

“For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich.”

2 Corinthians 8:9

Answer

Two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one for each offering. The same atonement and the same “she shall be clean.” Nobody was too poor to be made clean.
