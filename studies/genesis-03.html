Part one

Yea, hath God said?

Verses 1–7

The first temptation didn't start with a bite. It started with a question.

Who is the serpent?

Subtil is the old spelling of subtle: crafty, cunning, sly. This is no ordinary snake. The Bible tells us who was speaking through it:

“And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world: he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him.”

Revelation 12:9

He doesn't show up with horns and a pitchfork. He shows up as something beautiful and clever, in a perfect garden, and he asks a friendly question. Temptation still works that way. It rarely looks like what it is.

What did God really say?

What God said, Genesis 2:16–17

“Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.”

What she said back, Genesis 3:2–3

“We may eat of the fruit of the trees of the garden: But of the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the garden, God hath said, Ye shall not eat of it, neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die.”

Put them side by side. She dropped “freely”, so God sounds stingy. She added “neither shall ye touch it”, so God sounds harsh. She softened “surely die” to “lest ye die.” Small changes, and God already looks smaller.

The serpent's first move was to get her to talk about God's word instead of trusting it. The defense is to know what he actually said. That's one reason we read every verse.

Three lies in two verses

“Ye shall not surely die”→it won't cost you anythingverse 4
“For God doth know…”→God is holding out on youverse 5
“Ye shall be as gods”→you can run your own lifeverse 5

Every man in recovery has heard these three. Just once won't hurt. Everybody else gets to. You know what's best for you. Same voice, new words.

“He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?”

Romans 8:32

Temptation still knocks on these three doors. And look at the last words: her husband was “with her.” Adam stood right there and said nothing. Sin isn't only what we do. Sometimes it's what we stand by and let happen.

What sin promised, and what it paid

The promise, verse 5

“Your eyes shall be opened”

You'll be like God. You'll know. You'll be free.

The payday, verse 7

“They knew that they were naked”

Their eyes did open, on their own shame. They knew, and they couldn't un-know.

Sin always keeps the letter of its promise and breaks the spirit of it. The high was real. So was the morning after. Aprons here are coverings tied around the waist: the first cover-up. Man's first answer to his guilt was to hide it with something he made himself.

Part two

Where art thou?

Verses 8–13

Man runs and hides. God comes walking, and calling his name.

Hiding in the trees

“The cool of the day” is the evening breeze. God came walking, as if this were their usual time. Adam heard him coming and hid.

God knew where Adam was. “Where art thou?” was for Adam's sake: come out, and say where you are. That's what confessing is.

Shame makes a man hide, from God and from the brothers who could help. But God came looking, and he still does (Luke 19:10).

Passing the blame

Adam“The woman whom thou gavest…”the woman, and God
the woman“The serpent beguiled me”the serpent
the serpentGod asks him nothing, and goes straight to judgment (v. 14)

Beguiled means tricked, deceived. It was true; she had been. But it wasn't the whole truth. Adam even blamed God: the woman You gave me. Not one of them said, I did it.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Part three

It shall bruise thy head

Verses 14–19

Judgment falls, and right in the middle of it, the first promise of a Saviour.

The first gospel

Enmity is lasting hostility: war. Bruise here means strike or crush. God promises that one of the woman's descendants, “her seed,” will crush the serpent's head.

The serpent gets a strike at his heel: the cross, a real wound that would not last. He gets a crushed head: the empty tomb, a blow he can't come back from.

“…For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil.”

1 John 3:8

Follow the seed

The rest of the Bible keeps track of this promise, one family at a time:

EveSeth, “another seed” 4:25NoahAbraham 22:18
Judah 49:10DavidJesus, “made of a woman”

“But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law,”

Galatians 4:4

God made this promise to the serpent's face, on the worst day in history, before Adam had even said he was sorry. Grace got there first.

What the fall broke

The serpent

1415

Cursed. On his belly in the dust, and at war with the woman's seed.

The woman

16

Pain in bearing children, and a struggle in marriage: wanting her husband, and being ruled.

The man

171819

The ground cursed. Thorns, sweat, hard toil, and in the end, death.

Some old words: sorrow here means pain and hard toil; hearkened means listened to and obeyed; the herb of the field is the plants he'd have to grow; to till is to work the soil.

Work isn't the curse. Adam was given work in the garden before he ever sinned (2:15). What's new is the toil: the thorns and the sweat. And notice what God cursed: the serpent and the ground, not the man and the woman.

Dust, and a second Adam

“Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:”

Romans 5:12

What Adam brought in

The curse

Thorns, sweat, and a grave. Every funeral you've ever been to started here.

What Christ took on

The curse, carried

A crown of thorns on his head (Matthew 27:29), and a grave he walked out of.

“For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive.”

1 Corinthians 15:22

Part four

Coats of skins

Verses 20–24

God clothes the guilty, then shuts the gate behind them, for their good.

Clothed by God

חַוָּה said Chavvah, our “Eve” “life,” “living”

Adam had just heard “unto dust shalt thou return.” And he named his wife Life. That's faith: he believed God's promise of a seed.

Verse 7

Fig leaves

Made by their own hands. Cost them nothing. Couldn't cover the shame.

Verse 21

Coats of skins

Made by God. An animal died to make them: the first death in the Bible.

Christians have long seen here a picture of the cross: the innocent dies so the guilty can be covered. We can't cover our own sin. God covers it, and it cost him.

Our fig leaves

Every man has tried to stitch together his own cover: I'll make it up to them. I'll stay clean ninety days and then I'll be right with God. At least I'm not as bad as him.

Fig leaves wilt. God's covering doesn't. A man isn't saved by fixing himself up; he is clothed by God when he trusts what Christ did for him.

“I will greatly rejoice in the LORD, my soul shall be joyful in my God; for he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decketh himself with ornaments, and as a bride adorneth herself with her jewels.”

Isaiah 61:10

The gate shut, and opened

Cherubims are mighty angels who guard God's holiness. Shutting the gate was mercy too: to eat of the tree of life and live forever in a fallen body, sin and all, would have been a sentence, not a gift.

Genesis 3:24Shut out

Cherubims and a flaming sword keep the way to the tree of life.

Exodus 26:31A vail

Cherubims woven into the curtain that closed off God's presence in the tabernacle.

Matthew 27:51Torn open

“the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom”

When Jesus died, God tore the curtain from the top down. The way back in is open, and it's a Person: “I am the way” (John 14:6). The tree of life shows up again on the Bible's last page (Revelation 22:2).

Where art thou, today?

Maybe you fell yesterday. Maybe you've been hiding in the trees for a while. Here's what this chapter tells you to do:

  1. Come outAnswer God's question. Tell him plainly where you are. He already knows, and he came looking.
  2. Own itNo “the woman you gave me.” Say I did it, to God and to a brother you trust.
  3. Put on his coveringDrop the fig leaves. Trust what Christ did, not what you'll do to make up for it.

Points to remember

  1. The tempter“Yea, hath God said” 3:1He twisted God's word, then called God a liar. Know what God actually said.
  2. Sin“They knew that they were naked” 3:7Sin promised to make them like God. It delivered shame.
  3. Hiding“Where art thou?” 3:9They hid and blamed. God came looking and called.
  4. The promise“It shall bruise thy head” 3:15The woman's seed, Jesus, would crush the serpent, and be wounded doing it.
  5. The cost“Unto dust shalt thou return” 3:19Pain, thorns, toil and death came in through one man's sin. Christ carried the curse.
  6. Covered“Coats of skins, and clothed them” 3:21Fig leaves can't cover shame. God covers it, at the cost of a life.

Question 1 of 6

The tempter

How did the serpent get the woman to doubt God?

What verses tell us about this?

“But I fear, lest by any means, as the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ.”

2 Corinthians 11:3

Answer

First a question that made God's word sound stingy. Then a flat denial: you won't die. Then an attack on God's heart: he's keeping something good from you. He still works the same way, so know what God really said, and trust that he is good.

Question 2 of 6

Sin

What did sin promise, and what did it really deliver?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

Answer

It promised open eyes, knowledge and freedom: be your own god. It delivered shame, fear and hiding, and in the end death. Sin always pays, but never what it promised.

Question 3 of 6

Hiding

What did Adam and Eve do when God came, and what did God do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They hid, and when they were found, they blamed: Adam blamed the woman and God, and the woman blamed the serpent. God didn't wait for them to come to him. He came walking, and called, “Where art thou?” He still comes looking for the man who's hiding.

Question 4 of 6

The promise

What did God promise in verse 15, and who is it about?

What verses tell us about this?

“But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law,”

Galatians 4:4

“…For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil.”

1 John 3:8

Answer

That a son of the woman would crush the serpent's head, and be wounded in the heel doing it. It's about Jesus, born of a woman, bruised at the cross, and risen to destroy the devil's work. God promised it on the very day man fell.

Question 5 of 6

The cost

What did the fall break?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:”

Romans 5:12

Answer

Almost everything: man's walk with God, the trust between husband and wife, the ground itself, and life, because death came in. Every grave goes back to this day. But Christ came to carry the curse, so the story doesn't end in the dust.

Question 6 of 6

Covered

How did God cover their shame?

What verses tell us about this?

“I will greatly rejoice in the LORD, my soul shall be joyful in my God; for he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decketh himself with ornaments, and as a bride adorneth herself with her jewels.”

Isaiah 61:10

Answer

Their own fig leaves weren't enough. God made them coats of skins, and an animal died so they could be covered. It points to Christ, who died so we could be clothed in his righteousness. We don't make the covering; we receive it.
