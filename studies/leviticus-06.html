Leviticus 6

Make it right, and keep the fire burning

A man who lied or took what was not his must give it back and add a fifth. Then the LORD tells the priests how to keep the altar burning every night, and calls the sin offering itself “most holy.”

Things to watch for

Part one

“He shall even restore it in the principal”

Verses 1–7

Forgiveness from the LORD, and putting right what was done to a man.

Five ways to wrong a neighbour

  • a trust“delivered him to keep”: a thing left in his care
  • fellowshipa dealing between partners
  • violencea thing taken away by force
  • deceit“hath deceived his neighbour”
  • a lost thingfound, then lied about, and sworn false

Look at how the LORD begins: “If a soul sin, and commit a trespass against the LORD, and lie unto his neighbour.” A lie told to a man is a sin against the LORD. That is why the offering goes to the LORD, though the neighbour was the one hurt.

Every one of these is a sin of the pocket and the mouth: cheating, keeping, grabbing, lying. Many of us have done them. The chapter does not flinch, and neither does it shut the door.

Sweareth falsely means takes a false oath. Appertaineth (verse 5) means belongs to.

Principal means the whole amount. The fifth is one in five: if he took ten, he gives back twelve. And he gives it “in the day of his trespass offering”: the same day, not next year, when he can afford it.

The LORD asked for two things, and neither could stand in for the other. The man had to put right what he had done to his neighbour, and he had to bring his offering to the LORD. This chapter and the last one (5:16) both give the fifth.

“And Zacchaeus stood, and said unto the Lord; Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.”

Luke 19:8

Zacchaeus, chief among the publicans, gave back fourfold, more than the law asked. Jesus did not say it bought his salvation. He said it was already there: “This day is salvation come to this house” (verse 9). The restoring came out of the salvation.

“For any thing of all that he hath done”

The chapter ends this part with a wide word: “it shall be forgiven him for any thing of all that he hath done in trespassing therein.” Lying, violence, deceit, a false oath: all of it, anything.

“For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God; Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.”

Romans 3:23–24

The ram without blemish pointed to the Lamb. We are justified “freely”: we do not pay a ram, we do not earn it by paying back. We pay back because we have been forgiven, not to be forgiven.

Real world: make it right, wisely

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28
  • Write it downWhom did I take from, lie to, or leave owing? Be honest, and be specific.
  • Take it to a brotherBefore you contact anyone, talk to your pastor or a brother in Christ. Some things must be paid back carefully, in the right way.
  • StartA small payment today is better than a big plan someday. “In the day of his trespass offering.”

You may not be able to give all of it back, and some of it you cannot. The LORD knows. Christ's offering covers what you can never repay. What he wants from you is a heart that starts.

“Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.”

Matthew 5:23–24

Part two

“It shall never go out”

Verses 8–13

The burnt offering, the fire that burns all night, and the priest who clears the ashes.

A night and a morning at the altar

all nightthe burnt offering burnsverse 9
linen onthe garment and the breechesverse 10
the ashesbeside the altar, then carried outside to a clean placeverses 10 and 11
wood onevery morning; the offering laid in orderverse 12

Breeches are the linen undergarment of the priest. He puts on one set of garments to take up the ashes, and then “put off his garments, and put on other garments” to carry them out.

The text gives the priest himself the lowest job at the altar: carrying out the ashes, every morning. The ashes are what is left when the offering has been burned, and it is done. They were not thrown anywhere: “unto a clean place.”

Notice the small daily tasks of an ordinary priest: wood on the fire, ashes out, wood on the fire. Holy things are often kept by faithful, ordinary work.

The fire that was never to go out

The chapter says it three times: “the fire of the altar shall be burning in it” (verse 9), “it shall not be put out” (verse 12), “it shall never go out” (verse 13).

“And there came a fire out from before the LORD, and consumed upon the altar the burnt offering and the fat: which when all the people saw, they shouted, and fell on their faces.”

Leviticus 9:24

The fire was not struck by the priests. On the day the glory appeared, fire came out from before the LORD and consumed the offering (9:24). Their job was to keep it: wood every morning, so that what he lit did not die down.

“Quench not the Spirit.”

1 Thessalonians 5:19

In the same way, what God has lit in you, he keeps. But a fire needs wood. A little each morning: a verse, a prayer, a call to a brother. The fire may burn low, but the Spirit he gave you will not leave: “that he may abide with you for ever” (John 14:16).

Part three

“It is most holy”

Verses 14–30

What the priests ate, the offering a priest could not eat, and the sin offering that is called most holy.

The meat offering, and the priests' bread

  • meat offeringa food offering of flour and oil (meat means food)
  • handfula handful of flour, oil and frankincense burned for the LORD
  • memoriala reminder, burned before the LORD
  • bakenbaked; “not baken with leaven”: no yeast
  • statutea law that stands

The handful was burned for a sweet savour (a pleasing smell) to the LORD. The rest was not wasted. “The remainder thereof shall Aaron and his sons eat” (verse 16), without leaven, in the holy place, in the court of the tabernacle.

God feeds the men who serve him. “I have given it unto them for their portion of my offerings made by fire” (verse 17): their bread came from his table. “Most holy,” the LORD says of it.

The priest's own offering

When a priest was anointed, he brought “the tenth part of an ephah of fine flour for a meat offering perpetual, half of it in the morning, and half thereof at night.” About two quarts: the same measure the poorest man brought for his sin offering (5:11).

And “it shall be wholly burnt: it shall not be eaten” (verse 23). Every other meat offering fed the priests. This one fed no one. The priest could eat nothing from his own offering. It all went up.

“And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins: But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;”

Hebrews 10:11–12

Aaron's priests had to give for themselves, every day, before they could serve the people. Jesus did not need to. He offered himself once, and it was enough.

The sin offering is “most holy”

The offering for the worst thing a man did is not called filthy, or shameful. It is called most holy. That phrase comes three times in this chapter: for the meat offering (verse 17), for the sin offering (verses 25 and 29).

Verse 26

The priest ate it

“The priest that offereth it for sin shall eat it: in the holy place shall it be eaten.” When the blood was only on the outer altar, as for a ruler or a common man (4:25, 30), the priest ate the flesh.

Verse 30

The priest burned it

If the blood was brought into the tabernacle, as for a priest or the whole people (4:5–6, 16–17), the flesh was not eaten: “it shall be burnt in the fire.” (This is the bullock burned outside the camp.)

The sin itself was never holy. But the offering that stood in the sinner's place was, because it belonged to the LORD. The thing that carried the guilt was set apart for him.

What touches it is made holy

  • soddenboiled
  • earthen vessela clay pot: broken, once used
  • brasen pota pot of brass: scoured and rinsed
  • scouredrubbed clean and bright
  • reconcileto make peace, put right (verse 30)

Verse 27: “Whatsoever shall touch the flesh thereof shall be holy,” and a garment sprinkled with the blood was washed “in the holy place.” The clay pot it was boiled in was broken; a brasen pot was scoured and rinsed. The same word is said of the meat offering (verse 18): “every one that toucheth them shall be holy.”

Everywhere else in these laws, touching the unclean made a man unclean. Here it is the other way round: touching what is holy makes the toucher holy.

“And, behold, there came a leper and worshipped him, saying, Lord, if thou wilt, thou canst make me clean. And Jesus put forth his hand, and touched him, saying, I will; be thou clean. And immediately his leprosy was cleansed.”

Matthew 8:2–3

Nobody touched a leper. Jesus did. And the uncleanness did not pass to him; the cleanness passed to the leper. The holiness of Jesus is stronger than the dirt.

Real world: you are not too dirty to touch

Some of us feel too far gone for the LORD to put his hand on them. This chapter says otherwise: the offering for sin is “most holy,” and the Holy One touches the unclean to make them clean.

  • Bring itWhatever it was. The LORD says “for any thing of all that he hath done.”
  • Put it rightGive back, and a fifth more, the same day you can. Ask your pastor how.
  • Tend the fireWood each morning: a verse and a prayer. A brother to ask, “Did you?”

He has already given what no ram could. Today, say it plainly to him, and then do the next right thing.

Points to remember

  1. Restore“he shall restore that which he took violently away” 6:4Forgiveness from God, and putting right what you did to a man. Both.
  2. The fifth“shall add the fifth part more thereto… in the day of his trespass offering” 6:5Give it back and a fifth more, and do not wait.
  3. The fire“The fire shall ever be burning upon the altar; it shall never go out” 6:13The LORD lit it. We keep wood on it, a little every morning.
  4. The ashes“put off his garments, and put on other garments, and carry forth the ashes without the camp unto a clean place” 6:11The lowest job at the altar was the priest's own.
  5. Eating“The priest that offereth it for sin shall eat it” 6:26God feeds the men who serve him, from his own table.
  6. Most holy“Whatsoever shall touch the flesh thereof shall be holy” 6:27The offering for sin is most holy. Jesus touched the unclean and made them clean.

Question 1 of 6

Restore

What did a man have to do with what he had taken or got by deceit, before and besides his offering?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28

Answer

He had to restore it, all of it, to the man it belonged to, and then bring his trespass offering to the LORD. He puts right what he did to the man, and the LORD forgives what he did against him.

Question 2 of 6

The fifth

How much did he add, and on what day did he give it?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Zacchaeus stood, and said unto the Lord; Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.”

Luke 19:8

Answer

The fifth part more, one in five, and he gave it the same day, “in the day of his trespass offering.” Zacchaeus, once saved, gave back even more.

Question 3 of 6

The fire

What did the LORD say about the fire on the altar, and who looked after it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Quench not the Spirit.”

1 Thessalonians 5:19

Answer

“It shall never go out.” The priest looked after it, putting wood on every morning. The LORD had sent the fire (Leviticus 9:24); the priests' job was to keep it going.

Question 4 of 6

The ashes

What did the priest do with the ashes in the morning, and what did he change first?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He took up the ashes and put them beside the altar, then put off his garments, put on other garments, and carried them “without the camp unto a clean place.”

Question 5 of 6

Eating

Who ate what was left of the meat offering and of the sin offering, and where?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Aaron and his sons: the priests, only the males (verses 18 and 29), in the holy place, in the court of the tabernacle. God fed the men who served him from his own offerings.

Question 6 of 6

Most holy

What does the LORD call the sin offering, and what happens to whatever touches its flesh?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Jesus put forth his hand, and touched him, saying, I will; be thou clean. And immediately his leprosy was cleansed.”

Matthew 8:3

Answer

“It is most holy,” and whatever touches the flesh “shall be holy.” The holiness passes to what touches it, and Jesus touched the leper and made him clean.
