Acts 20

Midnight in Troas, and goodbye at Miletus

A young man falls from a window and is lifted up alive. Then Paul looks his friends in the eye and says what he has spent three years telling them: the grace of God.

Part one

Back through Greece to Troas

Verses 1–12

Paul leaves Ephesus, winters in Greece, and spends a long night in an upper room.

A winter in Greece

After the uproar in Ephesus, Paul left for Macedonia, encouraging the churches as he went, and came down to Greece. This is about AD 56 to 57, the end of his third journey.

He spent three months there, most likely in Corinth, and it was here that he wrote the letter to the Romans, about AD 57.

He planned to sail straight for Syria, but the Jews “laid wait” for him: a plot, likely to take him on the ship. So he changed course and went back north through Macedonia. A plan changed because of danger isn't cowardice. It's wisdom.

Seven men from everywhere

Paul never traveled alone. The lines show where each man was from, not the roads they took:

Sopater of Berea. Aristarchus and Secundus of Thessalonica. Gaius of Derbe, and Timotheus, who was from nearby Lystra (16:1). Tychicus and Trophimus of Asia.

They went ahead and waited at Troas. Then comes a small word: “for us.” Luke, who wrote Acts, is back with the party. After five days at sea, Paul's team is together again for seven days.

A long night in the upper room

This was the first day of the week, when the believers met to break bread and hear teaching. Paul was leaving in the morning, so he kept going until midnight. The room was packed and warm, with oil lamps burning everywhere.

Eutychus, a young man, sat on the window ledge. His name means “fortunate.” After a long day he nodded off, and fell three floors. Luke, the physician, writes that he was “taken up dead.”

Paul went down, threw his arms around him and said, “Trouble not yourselves; for his life is in him.” And they brought the young man up alive.

Down, and up again

Eutychus's fall was an accident, not a sin. But what happened next is for every man who has fallen. Paul went down to where he lay. He didn't send down a lecture.

Then they went back upstairs. Paul broke bread, ate, and talked until daybreak, and the believers “were not a little comforted.” Nobody told Eutychus he'd blown it. They were glad to have him back.

“For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.”

Proverbs 24:16

If you fell yesterday, you're not the first, and Jesus isn't done with you. Get up and get back to the room.

Part two

Island by island to Miletus

Verses 13–16

Paul is in a hurry, but he has one stop to make.

Past Ephesus on purpose

The ship sailed round the coast to Assos, but Paul chose to “go afoot.” He walked the shortcut over land, about twenty miles, and met them there. A man who's been working with people all week may need a day alone with God.

Then island by island down the coast: Mitylene, Chios, Samos, and finally Miletus. They sailed right past Ephesus, because Paul was in a hurry to reach Jerusalem by Pentecost, which is the feast fifty days after Passover.

But he didn't want to skip the Ephesians altogether. So from Miletus, about thirty miles south, he sent for their elders.

Part three

Goodbye to the elders

Verses 17–38

The only sermon of Paul's to believers that Acts records, spoken straight from the heart.

A man who wasn't ashamed to cry

“All humility… many tears.”

Paul asked them to look at his life: “from the first day… after what manner I have been with you at all seasons.” He didn't say he was perfect. He said he was the same man, in good times and bad, with real feeling in it.

Humility doesn't mean thinking little of yourself. It means being honest about who you are and who God is. And tears here weren't weakness. A man who had once hunted down believers now wept over them.

Men in recovery know this. The ones who can say “I was wrong, and I'm sorry” without flinching are the ones others trust, and the ones God uses.

Turn, and trust

μετάνοια said metanoia “repentance”: a change of mind that turns you around

Repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ. It's the one message for Jew and Greek alike. Turn from the old road, and trust Jesus. Faith is what saves you; repentance is the turn that goes with it. Neither earns a thing.

And notice “publickly, and from house to house.” Paul taught in the hall, and he sat in living rooms. “I kept back nothing” means he left out nothing that would help them, including the hard parts.

What lay ahead for Paul

  1. About AD 57Miletus: goodbye to the elders
  2. About AD 57Arrested in Jerusalem
  3. 57 to 59Two years in prison at Caesarea
  4. 59 to 60Storm and shipwreck on the way to Rome
  5. 60 to 62House arrest in Rome
  6. 66 to 672 Timothy, his last letter

Paul didn't know the details, only that “bonds and afflictions abide me” in every city. And still: “none of these things move me.” His aim wasn't comfort. It was to “finish my course with joy.”

Look at that goal. Not a perfect record. A finished race. Paul had a past he never forgot, but he said so and kept running. You don't have to run it clean. You have to keep running, and tell the truth about the gospel of the grace of God, which gave him his new start, and gives you yours.

All the counsel of God

Paul says he's “pure from the blood of all men”: if anyone is lost, it's not because Paul stayed quiet. Look what he said in this one chapter:

  1. repentance toward Godv. 21
  2. faith toward our Lord Jesus Christv. 21
  3. the gospel of the grace of Godv. 24
  4. the kingdom of Godv. 25
  5. the whole counsel of Godv. 27

He didn't pick the easy parts. Sin, turning around, forgiveness and the coming kingdom: all of it, and none of it to shame them. It's all aimed at one thing, grace, and it's the best news there is.

Elders, overseers, shepherds

Paul uses three words for one job: elders (v. 17), overseers and feed (shepherd) in verse 28. He's talking to the men who lead the church, and telling them how seriously God takes the flock, “which he hath purchased with his own blood.”

“Grievous wolves” will come from outside, and some will rise “of your own selves.” Paul isn't scaring them. He's telling them to stay awake, and to stay in the flock.

The blood of Jesus paid for every one of those sheep. A wolf can disturb a believer, but he can't undo what Christ bought.

“The word of his grace”

Built up. Not by willpower.

Paul knows he can't protect them anymore. So he does the best thing he can: “I commend you to God,” hands them over to Him, “and to the word of his grace.” He leaves them with a person and a book.

That word “is able to build you up, and to give you an inheritance among all them which are sanctified.” Sanctified means set apart for God, and that's already true of every believer. You haven't earned a place. You've been given one.

Watch means pay attention. Remember means keep going back to what you've been taught. Read the Word today, even a few verses. It builds a man who can't build himself.

A saying you won't find in the Gospels

Jesussaid itPaulremembered itLukewrote it downyou

No Gospel records these words of Jesus. Paul passes them on from memory. Jesus said and did far more than ever got written down.

Paul practiced it: “these hands have ministered unto my necessities.” He worked as a tentmaker (18:3) so he'd never be a burden. He didn't preach for money.

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28

If you used to take, this is the other way to live. Work with your hands, and then give something away today: a ride, an hour, a meal, a listening ear for a newer guy. The man who gives is no longer the man who takes.

Ephesus, through the years

  1. About 53 to 56Paul teaches here three years
  2. About 57Miletus: the farewell
  3. About 60 to 62Paul writes the letter to the Ephesians
  4. About 62 to 64Timothy at Ephesus (1 Timothy 1:3)
  5. About 85 to 95John writes from Ephesus

Strong men wept, held on to him, and walked him to the ship. Nobody there was embarrassed. That's what three years of someone telling you the truth, with tears, builds.

Paul didn't know if he'd see them again, but he left them in God's hands. Ephesus went on: Timothy led there, and years later John lived in that city. Everything good starts with someone willing to say the truth, and to say goodbye.

Points to remember

  1. Grace“the gospel of the grace of God” 20:24The good news is a gift. Not earned, only received, and it keeps building you up.
  2. Repentance and faith“repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ” 20:21Turn from the old way and trust Jesus. The same message for everyone.
  3. Falling and rising“his life is in him” 20:10A man who falls isn't left lying there. God lifts him up and gives him back to the room.
  4. Courage“none of these things move me” 20:24Paul wanted to finish with joy. You don't need a perfect record, just a finished race.
  5. Staying alert“Take heed therefore unto yourselves, and to all the flock” 20:28Stay awake and stay in the flock. The Word of His grace builds you up.
  6. Giving“It is more blessed to give than to receive” 20:35The man who used to take gets to give. Give something away today.

Question 1 of 6

Grace

What does Paul mean by “the gospel of the grace of God”?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Grace is God's free gift: forgiveness and a new life that nobody earns. It comes through faith in Jesus, not by works, and the same grace goes on building a believer up and giving him an inheritance. Paul counted his whole life worth spending to tell people this.

Question 2 of 6

Repentance and faith

What did Paul teach everyone, Jew and Greek alike?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Repentance toward God and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ. Turn from the old way, and trust Jesus. The message is the same for Jew and Greek, in public and from house to house. Paul kept back nothing that would help them.

Question 3 of 6

Falling and rising

What does God do for a man who has fallen?

What verses tell us about this?

“For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.”

Proverbs 24:16

Answer

He goes down to where the man is, lifts him up, and brings him back to the group. Eutychus fell by accident and was brought up alive, and the church was comforted. A man who falls can rise again. Get up and get back in the room.

Question 4 of 6

Courage

What kept Paul going when he knew trouble was ahead?

What verses tell us about this?

“I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith:”

2 Timothy 4:7

Answer

A purpose bigger than his comfort. Paul wanted to finish his course with joy and tell people the gospel of the grace of God. He didn't know what Jerusalem held, but he knew who he served. Years later he could say he had finished the race.

Question 5 of 6

Staying alert

Why did Paul warn the elders about wolves?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour:”

1 Peter 5:8

Answer

Because the flock is precious and trouble is coming, from outside and from within. Jesus purchased the church with His own blood, so Paul told the leaders to stay awake. Stay in the flock, stay in the Word, and stay alert.

Question 6 of 6

Giving

What did Jesus say about giving?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28

Answer

“It is more blessed to give than to receive.” Paul passes on a saying of Jesus that no Gospel records. A man who used to take can work with his hands and give. Give something away today.
