Leviticus 23

The feasts of the LORD

The LORD gives Israel a calendar: the sabbath, the passover, the firstfruits, the fifty days, the trumpets, the day of atonement and the booths. The New Testament says what they were pointing to, and that is Christ.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. The sabbathHow many days is work done, and what is the seventh day?

  3. The firstfruitsWhat did the priest wave before the LORD, and on what day?

Everything in the chapter fits on this page: three feasts at the start of the year, one fifty days later, then three in the seventh month. The months are only counted: “the first month,” “the seventh month.” The Bible does not name them here. Between the two sets lies the summer, and in the middle of the feasts verse 22 stops to say something about the corners of the field. We will come to it.

Old words for the feasts

  • feastan appointed time, a set day, whether or not there was a meal
  • convocationa calling together, a holy gathering
  • proclaimannounce out loud
  • servile workslaves' work, the ordinary labour of the week
  • at evenin the evening
  • an offering made by firean offering burned on the altar
  • sabbatha rest; a day to stop
  • in their seasonsat their own appointed times

Eleven verses in this chapter use the words “holy convocation” (2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 21, 24, 27, 35, 36, 37). Every feast is a day God calls his people together. “Servile work” is forbidden six times (7, 8, 21, 25, 35, 36). It means the work of the week, the toil. On the sabbath and on the day of atonement, the word is even wider: “ye shall do no work therein.”

The passover, and the week after it

The passover was already a year old when this was spoken. They kept the first one in Egypt, on the fourteenth day of the first month, with blood on the doorposts (Exodus 12). Now the LORD gives it a place in the calendar: “in their seasons.”

  • the fourteenththe passover lamb, “at even”
  • the fifteenththe feast of unleavened bread begins
  • seven daysno leaven; “in the seventh day is an holy convocation”

“Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.”

1 Corinthians 5:7-8

Paul says the lamb is Christ, and the week of bread without leaven is the life of a man who belongs to him. Leaven works through the whole lump, and here Paul makes it stand for the sin that spreads. The passover is what God did. The seven days are how we live in it: not with the old leaven, but “with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.”

Part two

“A sheaf of the firstfruits”

Verses 9–22

The first of the harvest, the fifty days, and the corner of the field.

The first handful, and then the harvest

  • a sheafa bundle of the first cut barley or grain
  • wavelift it up and hold it out before the LORD, then give it up
  • parched cornroasted grain (corn is grain in the KJV)
  • green earsfresh heads of grain, not yet dry
  • two tenth dealstwo tenth parts of an ephah, of fine flour

Notice the order. The first handful goes to the LORD, and “ye shall eat neither bread, nor parched corn, nor green ears, until the selfsame day that ye have brought an offering unto your God.” Nobody ate a mouthful of the new crop until the sheaf was waved. The Giver comes first. And the sheaf is also a promise: the first of the harvest means more is coming behind it.

Verse 9 says “When ye be come into the land which I give unto you.” They were still at Sinai. This feast looked ahead to a land they had not yet seen, and to a harvest they had not yet planted.

The morrow after the sabbath

“In the end of the sabbath, as it began to dawn toward the first day of the week, came Mary Magdalene and the other Mary to see the sepulchre.”

Matthew 28:1

“The morrow after the sabbath” is the first day of the week. That is the day the women came to the tomb at dawn, and found it empty.

The first, and those who follow

Paul calls the risen Christ by the name of this feast:

“But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.”

1 Corinthians 15:20

“But every man in his own order: Christ the firstfruits; afterward they that are Christ’s at his coming.”

1 Corinthians 15:23

The sheaf comes first, and the harvest follows after it. Christ rose first, and those who are his will follow “at his coming.” The first handful is already with the LORD, and the whole harvest is promised.

Fifty days, counted

1234567 891011121314 15161718192021 22232425262728 29303132333435 36373839404142 43444546474849 50: “a new meat offering,” two wave loaves

“Seven sabbaths shall be complete,” counted “from the morrow after the sabbath,” the day the sheaf was waved; and “unto the morrow after the seventh sabbath shall ye number fifty days.” The word Pentecost in the New Testament means “fiftieth.” Fifty days from the passover week brings us to about the third month, the month Israel had come to Sinai a year before.

Two loaves, with leaven

On the fiftieth day they brought “out of your habitations two wave loaves,” baked from the new harvest, “baken with leaven.” That is strange. Leaven was forbidden in the offerings burned on the altar (2:11). But the firstfruits were different:

“As for the oblation of the firstfruits, ye shall offer them unto the LORD: but they shall not be burnt on the altar for a sweet savour.”

Leviticus 2:12

Baken means baked. The loaves were waved before the LORD and eaten by the priest. They were not burned. And with the loaves came the offerings of verses 18 and 19: seven lambs, a bullock, two rams, “one kid of the goats for a sin offering,” and two lambs for peace offerings.

So there is leaven in the loaves: a people who are not sinless, brought to the LORD with a sin offering beside them. And that is how the New Testament describes the first believers:

“Of his own will begat he us with the word of truth, that we should be a kind of firstfruits of his creatures.”

James 1:18

The day the fifty days came to

“And when the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all with one accord in one place.”

Acts 2:1

Fifty days after the sheaf is fifty days after Christ rose. The disciples were together in Jerusalem “when the day of Pentecost was fully come,” and the Holy Ghost came on them. Before the day was out:

“Then they that gladly received his word were baptized: and the same day there were added unto them about three thousand souls.”

Acts 2:41

The firstfruits of the church, three thousand souls on the day of the feast of firstfruits. Not men who had never sinned, but the men of Jerusalem who had cried out against Jesus weeks before, and who were pricked in their heart and were washed. The two loaves had leaven in them, and they were accepted all the same.

Leave the corners

Right in the middle of the feasts, between the spring and the autumn, comes this. Riddance means clearing away, and the gleaning is what falls or is left behind when the harvesters have gone through. “Thou shalt not make clean riddance of the corners of thy field… thou shalt leave them unto the poor, and to the stranger: I am the LORD your God.”

He said the same in chapter 19:

“And when ye reap the harvest of your land, thou shalt not wholly reap the corners of thy field, neither shalt thou gather the gleanings of thy harvest. And thou shalt not glean thy vineyard, neither shalt thou gather every grape of thy vineyard; thou shalt leave them for the poor and stranger: I am the LORD your God.”

Leviticus 19:9–10

The feasts are not only about worship in a holy place. They are about what the harvest does to your hands. A man who has been given a harvest leaves the corner for the one who has nothing. It is not charity from a distance. The poor man walks into the field himself and picks it up with his own hands.

Part three

“A day of atonement”

Verses 23–32

The seventh month opens with a trumpet, and the tenth day is the one day in the year for atonement.

The trumpet

The first day of the seventh month was “a memorial of blowing of trumpets.” A memorial is a reminder. The trumpet called the people to stop and to remember, and the day of atonement follows on the tenth of the same month. That is all this chapter says about it: a sound, a rest, a day set apart.

The New Testament speaks of a trumpet to come, and it says it plainly:

“For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord.”

1 Thessalonians 4:16-17

“The trump of God.” The Lord himself will come, the dead in Christ will rise first, and those who are alive will be caught up to meet him, “and so shall we ever be with the Lord.” Paul says the same in 1 Corinthians 15:52, “at the last trump.” It is a hope for believers, and it is a sound the New Testament still looks ahead to. The trumpet is a reminder to stay awake and be ready.

The day that is all about sin

  • atonementa covering for sin, being made at one with God
  • afflict your soulshumble yourselves; go without; sorrow for sin (three times: 27, 29, 32)
  • no manner of worknone at all: not “servile work” only
  • cut offshut out from his people
  • from even unto evenfrom the evening of the ninth day to the evening of the tenth

This is the one day in the year when the whole nation stopped, went without, and faced what it had done. The full account of what the high priest did is in chapter 16. Here the chapter only sets the day, and gives its weight: those who would not humble themselves “shall be cut off from among his people,” and one who works that day, “the same soul will I destroy.”

There is no day like it for a man who has made a mess of his life: a day on which the whole camp admits it, and God provides the covering. Not a day to hide.

Once a year, and then once for all

“But into the second went the high priest alone once every year, not without blood, which he offered for himself, and for the errors of the people:”

Hebrews 9:7

Once every year, and “not without blood.” Year after year the high priest went in, and year after year it had to be done again. Then:

“Neither by the blood of goats and calves, but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us.”

Hebrews 9:12

“Once.” Not once a year, but once for all. Christ went in with his own blood and “obtained eternal redemption.” The day of atonement was a shadow, and its body is the cross. If you wonder whether it is finished, the answer is in that word. And where the day of atonement said, “Afflict your souls,” Christ has already taken the affliction.

Part four

“Ye shall dwell in booths seven days”

Verses 33–44

The harvest in, the palm branches, and the booths that remind Israel where they came from.

Rejoice, in a shelter of branches

  • tabernaclestents; also “booths,” shelters made of branches
  • solemn assemblya sacred gathering, closing the feast on the eighth day
  • besidein addition to
  • goodly treesfine, handsome trees; fruit trees
  • thick treestrees with thick leafy branches
  • willows of the brookthe willows that grow by the stream

This is the feast of joy. The harvest was in, the year's work was done, and for seven days a whole nation lived outdoors under branches. “Ye shall rejoice before the LORD your God seven days.” The booths were a reminder, and verse 43 gives the reason: “That your generations may know that I made the children of Israel to dwell in booths, when I brought them out of the land of Egypt.”

A man who has a roof, a table and food has it for one reason: the LORD brought him out. Once a year the nation went and lived as they had in the wilderness, and said thank you for the house.

The feast, and the one who cried out at it

“Now the Jews’ feast of tabernacles was at hand.”

John 7:2

In the Gospel of John, Jesus goes up to this feast, and on its great day:

“In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.”

John 7:37-38

For seven days the people lived in a house of leaves and rejoiced over a harvest. On the last day the Lord stands and calls to the thirsty.

The tabernacle of God with men

He is the One who “dwelt among us” (John 1:14, a word that means pitched his tent). And at the end of the Bible:

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.”

Revelation 21:3

The booths were a reminder of a tent in the wilderness. Revelation says the tabernacle of God will one day be with men. That is the last feast in the calendar, and the one the whole year has been walking toward.

A tick means it has been done. The three dots mean it is still ahead: the trumpet and the tabernacle of God with men. Hebrews 10:1 says the law had “a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things.”

Shadow and body

“Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days: Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.”

Colossians 2:16-17

Paul is not saying the feasts were worthless or that the LORD did not mean them. He is saying that Christ is what they were about. A man who has the body does not have to be ruled by the shadow. And a man who has the body will read the shadow with new eyes: every feast is another picture of the one he already knows.

Paul's concern in verse 16 is that nobody be judged over such days, and in verse 17 that nobody lose sight of Christ.

Real world: the year God gave you

  • RegretAtonement was made once, for all. Whatever you carried into this room, the blood has been taken in.
  • GraceThe loaves had leaven in them, and they were accepted all the same. You are not the first handful; Christ is.

  • TodayTake your rest, thank the LORD for the roof over your head, and leave a corner of what you have been given for someone who has nothing.

Points to remember

  1. The sabbath“Six days shall work be done: but the seventh day is the sabbath of rest, an holy convocation” 23:3The first of the LORD's appointed times: six days of work, and a day of rest.
  2. The passover“In the fourteenth day of the first month at even is the LORD’S passover.” 23:5The fourteenth of the first month, and unleavened bread on the fifteenth. Christ our passover is sacrificed for us.
  3. The firstfruits“ye shall bring a sheaf of the firstfruits of your harvest unto the priest” 23:10The first handful, waved “on the morrow after the sabbath.” Christ is the firstfruits of them that slept.
  4. The fifty days“shall ye number fifty days; and ye shall offer a new meat offering unto the LORD” 23:16Seven sabbaths and a day, and two loaves baken with leaven. Pentecost, the day the Spirit came.
  5. The atonement“Also on the tenth day of this seventh month there shall be a day of atonement” 23:27A day to afflict their souls, when atonement was made. Christ went in once, with his own blood.
  6. The booths“That your generations may know that I made the children of Israel to dwell in booths, when I brought them out of the land of Egypt” 23:43A feast of joy, living in shelters to remember the wilderness. One day, the tabernacle of God is with men.

Question 1 of 6

The sabbath

How many days is work done, and what is the seventh day?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Six days. The seventh day is “the sabbath of rest, an holy convocation,” the sabbath of the LORD “in all your dwellings.” It comes first in the list of the LORD's feasts.

Question 2 of 6

The passover

On which day of which month is the LORD'S passover, and which feast comes right after it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.”

1 Corinthians 5:7-8

Answer

The fourteenth day of the first month, at even. The feast of unleavened bread begins the next day, the fifteenth, and lasts seven days. Christ our passover is sacrificed for us.

Question 3 of 6

The firstfruits

What did the priest wave before the LORD, and on what day?

What verses tell us about this?

“But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.”

1 Corinthians 15:20

Answer

A sheaf of the firstfruits of the harvest, waved “on the morrow after the sabbath.” Christ is risen, “the firstfruits of them that slept.”

Question 4 of 6

The fifty days

What was brought on the fiftieth day?

What verses tell us about this?

“And when the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all with one accord in one place.”

Acts 2:1

Answer

A new meat offering, and two wave loaves of fine flour “baken with leaven,” the firstfruits unto the LORD. The Holy Ghost came on the day of Pentecost.

Question 5 of 6

The atonement

What was to happen on the tenth day of the seventh month, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“Neither by the blood of goats and calves, but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us.”

Hebrews 9:12

Answer

A day of atonement: they afflicted their souls and did no work, “to make an atonement for you before the LORD your God.” Christ entered once, by his own blood, and obtained eternal redemption.

Question 6 of 6

The booths

Why were Israel to dwell in booths for seven days?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“That your generations may know that I made the children of Israel to dwell in booths, when I brought them out of the land of Egypt.” It reminded them where they came from, and who brought them out.
