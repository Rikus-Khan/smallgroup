Genesis 17

God Almighty, and a new name

Thirteen years after the Hagar mistake, God comes back. He names himself God Almighty, gives Abram and Sarai new names, makes a covenant that lasts forever, and promises a son named Isaac.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

I am the Almighty God

Verses 1–8

God breaks thirteen years of silence, and he starts with who he is.

Thirteen years later

  1. about 2090 BCAbram is 75called out of Haran
  2. about 2080 BCAbram is 85the plan with Hagar
  3. about 2079 BCIshmael is bornAbram is 86
  4. about 2066 BCGod appearsAbram is 99 (“ninety years old and nine”)
  5. about 2065 BCIsaac is born“at this set time in the next year”

Genesis does not say a word about those thirteen years. Then “the LORD appeared to Abram”, and notice how he opens. He doesn't open with the Hagar mistake. He opens with who he is.

This is grace in action. God did not wait for Abram to clean up the mess. He walked into it, with a covenant in his hands.

El Shaddai

אֵל שַׁדַּי said El Shaddai “God Almighty”: the all-powerful God

Shaddai is “Almighty”: the God who has all power, and is enough. It is the name he chose to give a man who has just seen what happens when he tries to help God out.

“And I appeared unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, by the name of God Almighty, but by my name JEHOVAH was I not known to them.”

Exodus 6:3

It was the name the patriarchs knew him by. Abram's helping hand wasn't needed. God Almighty doesn't need a plan B.

“Walk before me, and be thou perfect”

תָּמִים said tamim “whole, complete, with nothing held back”

“Perfect” here does not mean never sinning. God says it to a man who failed him thirteen years ago, and the covenant comes first (“I will make my covenant”, verse 2). It means whole: not half for God and half for yourself.

“Walk before me” means live in front of him. Remember what Hagar said in chapter 16: Thou God seest me. The God who sees is asking Abram to live like a man who knows he is seen.

A new name for a man of ninety-nine

Abram → Abraham From “exalted father” to “father of a multitude”. The Bible gives the meaning itself: “a father of many nations have I made thee.”

Picture it. He is ninety-nine, with one son (and not the one God promised), and from now on every time anyone says his name, they say father of a multitude. God names him by what he will be, not by what he has been. And Abraham wears the name.

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

If you are in Christ, God has done something like that with you. You are not what you were last year. The old name, with the old shame, no longer fits.

Who is doing the work?

Watch for “I will” in verses 1 to 21:

1  2×1 3  4  5  6×2 7×1 8×2 9  10  11  12  13  14  15  16×2 17  18  19×1 20×1 21 

God says “I will” 10 times in this one conversation: I will make, I will multiply, I will establish, I will give, I will be, I will bless. Abraham's own part is small: walk before me, and later, the sign.

This is a covenant of grace. God does the heavy lifting. A man's job is to trust him and walk with him, not to earn it.

“The land wherein thou art a stranger”

Abraham had been living in Canaan for twenty-four years, and he didn't own any of it. He moved his tents between Shechem, Bethel and Hebron, a foreigner in somebody else's country.

“A stranger” means a foreigner, a man with no legal claim. “All the land of Canaan, for an everlasting possession.” God gives a deed to a man who has no home and no heir.

Notice “I will be their God” in verse 8. The land is the gift. God himself is the greater one.

Part two

The sign

Verses 9–14

A small mark, carried in the body, to remind everyone whose family this is.

Old words in verses 9–14

  1. tokena sign you can see; a reminder
  2. betwixtbetween
  3. circumciseto cut away the foreskin: a small, simple surgery on a male
  4. strangera foreigner, an outsider
  5. bought with moneya servant bought into the household
  6. cut off from his peopleshut out from the covenant family

The Bible says it plainly, and so will we: it is a private mark, a small cutting on the body of a male, done on the eighth day of a baby's life. It is not shameful. It is a sign.

Another “token”

“And God said, This is the token of the covenant which I make between me and you and every living creature that is with you, for perpetual generations: I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth.”

Genesis 9:12-13

The only other covenant in Genesis was the one with Noah, and its sign was in the sky for everyone to see: the rainbow. The same word, token, is used.

Abraham's sign is the opposite: private, carried in the body, handed down from father to son. Each man had it on him every day. A promise you can't forget, because you carry it.

Who got the sign?

  1. a babyeight days old
  2. a boy born in the housea servant's son
  3. a servant bought with moneybrought in from outside
  4. “any stranger”not of Abraham's family at all (verse 12)

The covenant household was not a bloodline only. A man bought as a slave, a foreigner, any stranger who came in, wore the same mark as Abraham's own son. If you have ever felt like an outsider with no right to be here, notice who is in the picture.

And verse 14 is not about a missing operation. The man who refuses the sign is refusing the covenant: “he hath broken my covenant”. It is a warning about turning God down.

Faith first, sign later

First, years beforeAbram believed, and was counted righteous (15:6)
→
Then, in this chapterhe is given the sign (17:10–11)
→
So the sign isa seal, not the way in

“And he received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had yet being uncircumcised: that he might be the father of all them that believe, though they be not circumcised; that righteousness might be imputed unto them also:”

Romans 4:11

Paul's point: Abraham was counted right with God before he was circumcised, so circumcision can't be how a man gets right with God. It was a seal on a faith that was already there. Baptism works the same way for a believer: it shows what faith has already done. It is never the way in.

The mark that really matters

The outward sign

In the flesh, once

Visible, and good as a reminder. But a mark on the body does not change the man.

The inward work

In the heart, by God

“Circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit” (Romans 2:29). Only God can do it. That is what a believer is given.

“And the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart, and the heart of thy seed, to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, that thou mayest live.”

Deuteronomy 30:6

A church membership, a baptism date, a good testimony: none of them is a substitute for the heart that God changes. If you have trusted Jesus, he has already done that work. The sign is only the sign.

Part three

Abraham and Sarah

Verses 15–22

A new name for a wife, a laugh that becomes a name, and a father's prayer.

Sarai becomes Sarah

Sarai → Sarah Both names are close to “princess”. The new one matches the new word from God: “a mother of nations; kings of people shall be of her.”

Do not miss this. For thirteen years, the Hagar plan had been Sarai's idea of how the promise would come true. Now God says the child will be hers. “I will bless her, and give thee a son also of her.” It is the first time he says the child will be Sarah's.

She was the one who made the plan that went wrong, and God blesses her by name anyway. He does not hold the mistake over her head.

The laugh

Abraham

99

A hundred years old when the child is born (21:5).

Sarah

90

Long past the age for children (Hebrews 11:11).

Abraham fell on his face, laughed, and said in his heart: how? Was it joy, or doubt? Teachers read it both ways. When Sarah laughs in chapter 18, the LORD questions her. Here he doesn't question Abraham. And Paul says of him:

“He staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief; but was strong in faith, giving glory to God; And being fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform.”

Romans 4:20-21

Isaac: “he laughs”

יִצְחָק said Yitzchaq “he laughs”

God chose the name. The laugh at the edge of believing becomes the name the whole nation will say. The next time someone says Isaac, they say he laughs.

“Is any thing too hard for the LORD? At the time appointed I will return unto thee, according to the time of life, and Sarah shall have a son.”

Genesis 18:14

Whatever you have laughed bitterly at in your own life (the thing you gave up on) God can turn into a reason for joy.

A father's prayer

AbrahamO that Ishmael might live before thee!

GodAs for Ishmael, I have heard thee. I have blessed him, and will make him fruitful. heard

Abraham has just been told his promised son will be a different boy. He doesn't forget the one he already has. He prays for him.

Many of these men have children whose lives have been hurt by their choices. This is a pattern for you. Pray for your children by name, as Abraham did. God used the very name Ishmael to answer him (God hears) and he did hear.

Two sons, two promises

Ishmael

“I have blessed him”

Fruitful, multiplied, twelve princes, a great nation. God hears, and God blesses.

Isaac

“my covenant will I establish”

The covenant line, and the line the Saviour comes through. Born next year, at the set time.

Verse 21 is plain: the covenant goes through Isaac. But God's kindness did not stop at the covenant line. Ishmael was not a mistake to God. He was a boy he had already named, and promised to bless.

“A set time” is an appointed time. God put a date on the calendar before Isaac was conceived.

Part four

The selfsame day

Verses 23–27

Abraham does what God said, and he does it right away.

In the selfsame day.

Nobody waited for a better moment. At ninety-nine, with the whole household watching, Abraham did exactly what God had said, on the very day God said it. Ishmael at thirteen went through it too, and so did every male in the house.

And notice that Abraham counts Ishmael in. His boy is part of the household, and part of the sign.

Delayed obedience is how many of us lose a good day. The thing you know God has told you to do (the call, the apology, the meeting) do it in the selfsame day.

The covenant reaches you

God made the promise to Abraham and “his seed”. Paul says that seed is Christ. And then:

“And if ye be Christ’s, then are ye Abraham’s seed, and heirs according to the promise.”

Galatians 3:29

“And will be a Father unto you, and ye shall be my sons and daughters, saith the Lord Almighty.”

2 Corinthians 6:18

“Saith the Lord Almighty.” The same Almighty God who appeared to Abram says he will be a Father to men who trust Christ. The covenant does not end with Abraham. It reaches every man who believes.

Points to remember

  1. God's name“I am the Almighty God; walk before me, and be thou perfect.” 17:1He is God Almighty. He does not need our help, and he asks for a whole heart.
  2. Grace“I will make my covenant between me and thee” 17:2After the mistake, God came back, and he came with a covenant.
  3. New names“Neither shall thy name any more be called Abram, but thy name shall be Abraham” 17:5God names us by what we will be, not by our worst day.

Question 1 of 6

God's name

Who is the Almighty God, and what did he promise Abram?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I appeared unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, by the name of God Almighty, but by my name JEHOVAH was I not known to them.”

Exodus 6:3

Answer

El Shaddai: God Almighty, the all-powerful God, the One who needs no help. He promised a covenant that lasts for ever: many nations from Abraham, kings, the land of Canaan, and the greatest promise of all, “I will be their God.” It was his doing from start to finish.

Question 2 of 6

Grace

Did God walk away from Abram after the Hagar mistake?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

No. He was silent for thirteen years, and then he came back, with a covenant instead of a lecture. Abram's mistake had real consequences, but it did not end God's promise. If you have failed, confess it, and know that he is faithful to forgive and to start again with you.

Question 3 of 6

New names

Why did God change Abram's and Sarai's names?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

To name them by what they would become, not what they had been. “Father of a multitude” was a strange thing to call a man with one son. But God's word makes what it says. If you are in Christ, you are a new creature. Don't answer to the name of your worst day.

Question 4 of 6

The sign

Did circumcision make Abraham right with God?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had yet being uncircumcised: that he might be the father of all them that believe, though they be not circumcised; that righteousness might be imputed unto them also:”

Romans 4:11

“And the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart, and the heart of thy seed, to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, that thou mayest live.”

Deuteronomy 30:6

Answer

No. Abraham was counted righteous in chapter 15, years before he was circumcised in chapter 17. The sign was a seal on a faith he already had. What really counts is the heart, which only God can change. In the same way, baptism shows what faith has done for a believer. It is never the way in.

Question 5 of 6

The impossible

Is anything too hard for God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Is any thing too hard for the LORD? At the time appointed I will return unto thee, according to the time of life, and Sarah shall have a son.”

Genesis 18:14

“For with God nothing shall be impossible.”

Luke 1:37

Answer

No. A man of a hundred and a woman of ninety were going to have a son, at the set time. The name Isaac, “he laughs”, is a reminder. Whatever you have given up on, you can bring it to the Almighty, and to your brothers in Christ to pray with you.

Question 6 of 6

Mercy

Does God care about the child born of a mistake?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Yes. Ishmael came out of Abram and Sarai's mistake, and God said, I have heard thee. I have blessed him. Abraham prayed for him, and he was in the covenant household that very day. If you have children who bear the marks of your failures, pray for them by name. God hears, and he is not done with them.
