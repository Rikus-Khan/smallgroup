Where we are in Romans

Paul wrote this letter from Corinth, about AD 57, to the church at Rome. He lays out the gospel like a lawyer laying out a case, one step at a time.

All guilty1:18–3:20
Justified by faith3:21–5:21
New life6–8
Israel and the nations9–11
Living it out12–16
1234 5678 9101112 13141516

Chapters 1 and 2 put the pagan world and then the religious man on trial. Chapter 3 is where the case closes: everybody is guilty. And then, at verse 21, two words change everything: “But now.”

Part one

Let God be true

Verses 1–8

If everyone is guilty, what good was it to be a Jew? And doesn't our sin make God's goodness look better? Paul answers the hecklers.

Trusted with God's words

The Jews' great advantage was the oracles of God: the Scriptures, handed to them to keep. Some didn't believe what they held. A man's unbelief can't make God unfaithful.

Paul quotes, Romans 3:4

That thou mightest be justified in thy sayings, and mightest overcome when thou art judged.

David wrote it, Psalm 51:4

Against thee, thee only, have I sinned, and done this evil in thy sight: that thou mightest be justified when thou speakest, and be clear when thou judgest.

Psalm 51 is David's prayer after his adultery with Bathsheba and the murder of her husband. He stopped making excuses and said: God, You're right about me. That's where getting free from regret starts: “let God be true.”

The excuses

The excuseIf my sin makes God look good, why should I be judged? 3:5, 3:7

PaulGod forbid: for then how shall God judge the world?

The slanderLet us do evil, that good may come. 3:8

Paul…whose damnation is just.

People twisted Paul's message about grace, and the voice still says, “One more time. God forgives anyway.” Grace is bigger than your worst day. But it isn't permission.

Part two

None righteous

Verses 9–20

Paul calls his witnesses, and every one of them is from the Jews' own Bible. The verdict is the same for everyone.

Where each line comes from

Verses 10–18 aren't Paul's own words. He strings together quotes from the Psalms and Isaiah, like a lawyer calling witnesses.

3:10–12There is none righteous, no, not onePsalm 14:1–3
3:13Their throat is an open sepulchrePsalm 5:9
3:13the poison of asps is under their lipsPsalm 140:3
3:14Whose mouth is full of cursing and bitternessPsalm 10:7
3:15–17Their feet are swift to shed bloodIsaiah 59:7–8
3:18There is no fear of God before their eyesPsalm 36:1

Psalm 14 begins, “The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God.” Then it says, “They are all gone aside… there is none that doeth good, no, not one.” Paul didn't invent this verdict. God had said it for a thousand years.

Head to foot

First the words: throat, tongue, lips, mouth. Then the feet. Last, the root of it all: “no fear of God.” An asp is a cobra; an open sepulchre is a grave with the stone rolled off.

Not every man is as bad as he could be, but no part of any man is untouched. Some of us knew the cursing and the blood. Others hid it better. Same verdict.

Every mouth stopped

The case is closed. The law, God's own witness, has testified, and nobody in the dock has anything to say. No excuses, no comparisons, no “but you don't know what I've been through.” All the world is guilty before God.

That's hard to hear. But a man has to hear the verdict before he'll listen to the pardon.

What the law can and can't do

The law can

Show you the dirt

“By the law is the knowledge of sin.” Like a mirror, it tells the truth about your face.

The law can't

Wash it off

Nobody washes his face with the mirror. “By the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified.”

Trying harder to keep the rules can't clear your record. You could keep every rule from today on, and yesterday would still be on the books. Something else has to happen. And it does, in the very next verse.

Part three

But now

Verses 21–31

The guilty verdict stands. Then God does what no court on earth can do: He sets the guilty free, and stays just.

Come short

Come short means to fall short, to lack. Some get closer than others; nobody reaches it. “There is no difference.” The man in the cell and the man in the pulpit stand on the same ground.

That's good news both ways. Nobody here is worse off than anyone else at the cross. And the way across the gap is “unto all and upon all them that believe.”

Justified: the courtroom

Justified is a courtroom word. Not made better, but declared righteous: the Judge rules your record clear. Not because you were innocent, but because Christ Jesus stood in for you.

Freely is dōrean, the word in John 15:25: “They hated me without a cause.” No cause in Him for their hate; no cause in us for His grace.

Redemption: the slave market

Redemption is a slave-market word: to buy a man back and set him free by paying the price. Rome was full of slaves, and many in that church were likely slaves or freed slaves. The price here was “the precious blood of Christ” (1 Peter 1:19).

Some of us know what it is to belong to something: a bottle, a pipe, a habit that tells you when to jump. Christ didn't just forgive the slave. He bought him out. “If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed” (John 8:36).

Propitiation: the mercy seat

A propitiation is a sacrifice that satisfies God's just anger at sin and turns it away. Paul's word, hilastērion, also names the mercy seat, the gold lid of the ark (Hebrews 9:5). Once a year the high priest sprinkled blood on it (Leviticus 16:14). God looked at His broken law and saw blood in between.

Now God has “set forth” Jesus in the open. And not for a few: “not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world” (1 John 2:2).

Just, and the justifier

Here's the problem only the cross solves. A judge who lets the guilty walk is a crooked judge. A judge who punishes everyone has no one left to save.

For centuries, in His forbearance, God held back, and “sins that are past”, like Abraham's and David's, went unpunished. At the cross He showed He never let sin slide: it was punished in His Son. So God stays just, and the man who believes in Jesus goes free.

Boasting is shut out

  • “I'm not as bad as him”no difference (3:22)
  • “I was raised in church”the Jews had the oracles, and still came short (3:2, 3:9)
  • “I've cleaned up my life”not by the deeds of the law (3:28)
  • “by the law of faith”Christ did the work. I just believed Him.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Faith isn't one more good work God grades. It's an empty hand taking a gift. Nobody brags about taking a gift. And that also means nobody here gets to look down on the man next to him.

One God, one way in

The circumcision: Jews

justified by faith

With all their history, the Jews come in the same way: believing.

The uncircumcision: Gentiles

justified through faith

With none of that history, the Gentiles come in the same way: believing.

There's only one God, so there's only one door, and it's wide open. “For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved” (Romans 10:13).

Does faith throw out the law? “God forbid.” Faith agrees with the law. The law said sin must be paid for, and at the cross it was paid in full. Faith doesn't cancel the law's verdict. It stands behind the One who met it.

Three pictures of one gift

Verses 24 and 25 use three words from three different places. Each shows a different side of what Jesus did for you.

  1. The courtroomjustified: the guilty man declared righteous. Your record is clear.
  2. The slave marketredemption: the slave bought back and set free. You don't belong to sin anymore.
  3. The templepropitiation: blood on the mercy seat. God isn't angry with you anymore.

If you fell yesterday, all three are still true this morning. The verdict wasn't based on your good days, so your bad days can't reverse it. Get up, tell God the truth, and go to work a free man.

Question 1 of 6

Advantage

What advantage did the Jews have?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God trusted them with His Word, the oracles of God. Many didn't believe it, but their unbelief couldn't make God unfaithful. Having the Bible was a great privilege. It still didn't make them righteous: they were under sin like everyone else.

Question 2 of 6

Guilty

Who is under sin?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Everyone: Jews and Gentiles, religious and not. There is none righteous, no, not one. Some come closer than others, but all have sinned and come short of the glory of God. There's no difference.

Question 3 of 6

The law

What can the law do, and what can't it do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The law shows us our sin and stops every mouth: it proves we're guilty. It can't make us right with God. Like a mirror, it shows the dirt but can't wash it off. The righteousness of God comes another way, without the law, though the law and prophets point to it.

Question 4 of 6

Justified

How is a man justified before God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By faith in Jesus Christ, not by the deeds of the law. God declares him righteous freely, as a gift of grace, because of what Christ did. It's offered unto all, and comes upon all them that believe.

Question 5 of 6

The price

What did it cost to set us free?

What verses tell us about this?

“But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:19

Answer

The blood of Jesus. It's free to us because it cost Him everything. He redeemed us, buying us out of slavery to sin, and He is the propitiation, the sacrifice that turned God's just anger away. So God is both just and the justifier of the one who believes.

Question 6 of 6

Boasting

Why is boasting shut out?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Because salvation comes by the law of faith, not works. Christ did all the work; we only believe and receive. You can't brag about a gift, and you can't look down on another man who received the same one.
