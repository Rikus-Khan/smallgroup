Hebrews 1

God Has Spoken by His Son

No greeting, no name, just God speaking. A letter to tired, pressured believers opens with the biggest picture of Jesus in the Bible: the Maker of the worlds, the shining of God's glory, who washed away our sins Himself and then sat down.

The letter to the Hebrews

No name, no greeting. It opens with God: “God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son”

Written by
the writer of Hebrews; he never gives his name. Many think Paul; others, Apollos or Barnabas.
When
about AD 64 to 68. The temple in Jerusalem is still standing, and the priests still offer “daily” (10:11).
To whom
Jewish believers in Jesus. Near the end he writes, “They of Italy salute you” (13:24), so the letter has friends in Italy.
Why
They were under pressure, and some were tempted to drift back to the old system: the temple, the priests, the sacrifices. The writer shows them that Jesus is better than all of it, and begs them to hold on.

Where Hebrews fits

  1. about AD 30 to 33The cross, the empty tomb, Pentecost“spoken by the Lord” 2:3
  2. about AD 34 onPersecution: Stephen killed, believers scatteredActs 8:1; Hebrews 10:32–34
  3. about AD 64Rome burns; Nero turns on the Christianspressure everywhere
  4. about AD 64 to 68Hebrews“hold fast”
  5. about AD 70Rome destroys Jerusalem and the templethe old system gone

“But call to remembrance the former days, in which, after ye were illuminated, ye endured a great fight of afflictions;”

Hebrews 10:32

They had already paid a price: insults, jail visits, their goods taken (10:33–34). Now the pressure was rising, and the old way looked safe and familiar. Within a few years the temple they longed for would be rubble. Only Jesus would still be standing.

Part one

God has spoken

Verses 1–3

One long sentence that may be the greatest description of Jesus ever written.

about 400 years of silence
His Son“in these last days”: the last word, and the whole word
“sundry times”: many pieces · “divers manners”: many ways“by his Son”

Sundry times means in many parts, a piece here and a piece there, over about a thousand years. Divers manners means in many different ways. Every piece was true. None of it was the whole.

Then God stopped sending messages and came Himself. If you want to know what God is like, what He thinks of you, and what He wants from you, you don't have to guess. Look at Jesus.

Seven things about the Son

Before time: the heir of everything, and the One by whom God made the worlds (1, 2).

Who He is: the brightness of God's glory and the express image of His person (3, 4). Not a copy. God Himself, shown.

Right now: upholding all things by His word (5). The sun came up today because He said so.

At the cross: He by himself purged our sins (6). Then He sat down (7), because the job was done.

The shining and the stamp

Brightness is the light that streams off the sun. Nobody has ever seen the sun without its shining. The Son has always been with the Father, and He is how God's glory reaches us.

Express image is the mark a seal leaves in wax: every line of the seal, exactly. Jesus said, “he that hath seen me hath seen the Father” (John 14:9). Whatever you see in Jesus, His patience with failures, His welcome to sinners, that is what God is like.

He sat down

The priests in the temple

Always standing

There were no chairs in the holy place. Every day, more sacrifices. The work was never finished.

The Son

Sat down

By himself, with no help from us, He purged (washed clean) our sins. Then He sat down. Finished.

“And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins: But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;”

Hebrews 10:11–12

If yesterday's failure is still sitting on your chest, look at where Jesus is: seated. He isn't still working on your sins, waiting to see if you'll earn the rest. Confess it (1 John 1:9) and rest in a finished job.

Part two

Better than the angels

Verses 4–14

Seven quotes from the Old Testament, one after another, and every one of them points to the Son.

Why bring up angels?

The Jews held angels in high honor. The law itself came at Sinai with angels present: “Who have received the law by the disposition of angels” (Acts 7:53). If the old system came through angels, was it the highest thing there is? No.

The angels

Servants

Made. Sent. They worship Him (v. 6). They are ministering spirits (v. 14), standing ready.

Jesus

The Son

Heir. Maker of the worlds. Worshipped. On a throne for ever and ever (v. 8). Seated at God's right hand (v. 13).

To which angel did God ever say “Thou art my Son”? To none. That name, Son, is the more excellent name.

Six of the seven speak to or about the Son. The one about angels (v. 7) calls them ministers: servants, quick as wind and fire. The score isn't close.

He opened the Bible they already trusted

12 Samuel6Psalms
GenesisMalachi

The writer doesn't invent anything new. His readers loved the Psalms; they sang them. He opens that same book and shows them the Son on page after page.

“For unto which of the angels said he at any time, Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee?”

Hebrews 1:5

This is still how to read the Old Testament: look for Jesus. He is the One it was all pointing to.

God the Father calls the Son “O God.”

Read it slowly: “But unto the Son he saith, Thy throne, O God.” Jesus isn't the highest angel or a good teacher. He is God, with a throne that lasts for ever and ever.

Anointed … with the oil of gladness: kings were anointed with oil. In Hebrew, the Anointed One is Messiah; in Greek, Christ. And look why: He loved righteousness, and hated iniquity. Every other king in history did some wrong. This King never once did.

There's gladness in Him, too: joy above everyone around Him. Following Jesus isn't joining a gloomy club. It's coming under the happiest King there is.

Everything wears out but Him

God calls the Son “Lord” and the Maker of earth and sky. One day He'll fold up the whole universe like an old coat and put it away.

Your health, your job, your reputation, your plans: all of it wears thin. He doesn't. “Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever” (13:8). The Jesus who forgave you last year hasn't changed His mind since.

Sent for you

The Son

Sits

At God's right hand, until every enemy is under His feet (v. 13). The war's outcome is settled.

The angels

Are sent

Out on errands. And for whom? For them who shall be heirs of salvation. For believers. For you.

“The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them.”

Psalm 34:7

You'll walk into work today feeling alone. You're not. The Son is on the throne, and heaven has servants on duty for His people. We don't pray to angels or chase them; we trust the One who sends them.

God has spoken by his Son.

Prophets, angels, the law, the temple: every one of them was a voice from God. The Son is the last and best Word. The next chapter asks the obvious question: if God has spoken like this, are we listening?

Something to do today: read one chapter of a Gospel, and as you read, ask, “What does this show me about what God is like?” Then tell one man what you saw.

Points to remember

  1. God speaks“Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son” 1:2The prophets gave pieces. Jesus is God's last and full Word.
  2. Who Jesus is“the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person” 1:3To see Jesus is to see exactly what God is like, and He holds everything up.
  3. Sins dealt with“when he had by himself purged our sins, sat down” 1:3He washed our sins away Himself, and sat down: the work is finished.
  4. Better than angels“Being made so much better than the angels” 1:4Angels serve and worship. The Son is God, on the throne for ever.
  5. He never changes“They shall perish; but thou remainest” 1:11Everything wears out. Jesus is the same, and so is His grace.
  6. Help sent“sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation” 1:14Heaven's servants are sent for believers. You're not alone today.

Question 1 of 6

God speaks

How has God spoken to us “in these last days”?

What verses tell us about this?

“No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.”

John 1:18

Answer

By His Son. In the past God spoke through the prophets, a piece at a time and in many ways. Now He has spoken through Jesus, who shows us the Father fully.

Question 2 of 6

Who Jesus is

What does verse 3 say the Son is, and what does He do?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.”

Colossians 1:17

Answer

He is the brightness of God's glory and the exact image of who God is. And He holds everything together by His word, today and every day.

Question 3 of 6

Sins dealt with

What did the Son do “by himself,” and what did He do next?

What verses tell us about this?

“And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins: But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;”

Hebrews 10:11–12

Answer

By Himself He purged, washed clean, our sins. Then He sat down at God's right hand, because nothing was left to do. We rest in a finished work.

Question 4 of 6

Better than angels

Why is the Son better than the angels?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God never called an angel His Son. The angels worship Him and serve. The Son is called God, with a throne for ever, and He sits at God's right hand.

Question 5 of 6

He never changes

What will wear out, and who remains?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever.”

Hebrews 13:8

Answer

The earth and the heavens will wear out like an old coat and be folded up. The Son remains, the same for ever. His promises to you don't wear out either.

Question 6 of 6

Help sent

Whom are the angels sent to serve?

What verses tell us about this?

“The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them.”

Psalm 34:7

Answer

Them who shall be heirs of salvation: everyone who belongs to Jesus. The Son sits on the throne, and His servants are sent out for His people.
