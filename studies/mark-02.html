Not the righteous, but sinners

Part one

Through the roof

Verses 1–12

The door is blocked. Four men won't take no for an answer.

Home in Capernaum

After His preaching trip around Galilee (1:39), Jesus comes back to Capernaum, the lakeside town that had become His home base. “The house” is most likely Simon Peter's house from 1:29.

“It was noised” means word got around. A house in a town like that was small: a room or two around a courtyard. In no time it was packed, and the doorway was jammed.

And what did Jesus do with the crowd? “He preached the word unto them.” The healings drew people. The word was what He came to give.

Digging through

Houses had flat roofs, reached by a stair outside. The roof was wooden beams covered with branches and packed clay.

So they climbed up and dug a hole right over Jesus' head. Dirt and straw falling on the crowd. Somebody's roof, wrecked.

Then they let him down on ropes. The “bed” was a sleeping mat, light enough to carry. “The palsy” means paralysis: he couldn't walk at all.

Their faith

four friendscarried hima man who couldn't walkdown through the roofJesus

Jesus saw their faith, the faith of the four. Some men can't get to Jesus on their own legs. They need somebody to carry them. A sponsor, a brother, a man who keeps showing up.

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

And Jesus' first words weren't about his legs. “Son, thy sins be forgiven thee.” He went to the deepest need first. A man can walk and still be lost. Jesus fixes the inside first.

The scribes were half right

“Blasphemy” means speaking against God, or claiming what belongs only to Him. Under the law, it was punished by death.

Ifonly God can forgive sins,
→
andJesus forgives sins…

…then either He is lying, or He is God. The scribes got the first part right. God says it Himself:

“I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins.”

Isaiah 43:25

Where they went wrong was in who was standing in front of them.

Which is easier to say?

First notice: they only thought it, “in their hearts.” Jesus answered what they hadn't said out loud. He knew their thoughts.

Can't be checked

“Thy sins be forgiven thee”

Anybody can say it. Nobody in the room can see whether it happened.

Everyone will see

“Arise, and take up thy bed”

Say that, and in a few seconds the whole room knows whether you're for real.

So Jesus does the one they can see to prove the one they can't. If the legs work, the forgiveness is real too.

“The Son of man”

This was Jesus' favorite name for Himself. It sounds humble, and it is: He is truly a man. But it comes from a vision of heaven's throne room:

“I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom…”

Daniel 7:13–14

The Son of man has heaven's authority, and He uses it “on earth,” right here, in a crowded little house, on a paralyzed man's sins.

The man who was carried in walked out carrying his own bed. And “they were all amazed, and glorified God.” They all saw a man on his feet: proof of what they couldn't see, a forgiven man.

Part two

Follow me

Verses 13–22

A tax man, a dinner party, and a doctor who goes where the sick are.

The tax booth

“The receipt of custom” was a toll booth. Capernaum sat on a main road and a busy lake, so goods were taxed coming through. The tax men worked for Herod Antipas and Rome, and they collected extra for themselves. Their own people saw them as traitors and crooks.

Levi the tax man → Matthew Matthew tells the same story about himself: “a man, named Matthew, sitting at the receipt of custom” (Matthew 9:9). He became one of the twelve and wrote the first Gospel in our Bible.

Two words, “Follow me,” and he got up and left the money on the table. A fisherman could go back to fishing. A tax man who walked away couldn't get his job back.

The doctor goes where the sick are

“Publicans” are tax men. Levi had Jesus to dinner with his friends: more tax men, and people everyone knew as “sinners.” Eating together meant you accepted someone.

The scribes and PhariseesHow is it that he eateth and drinketh with publicans and sinners?

JesusThey that are whole have no need of the physician, but they that are sick: I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.

A doctor who won't go near the sick isn't much of a doctor. If you know you're sick, you're exactly who He came for.

And “the righteous”? “There is none righteous, no, not one” (Romans 3:10). The only men He can't help are the ones who think they're fine.

Nobody fasts at a wedding

To “fast” is to go without food for a time, to pray and mourn. The law set one fast day a year. The Pharisees fasted twice a week, and John's disciples fasted too.

While He is with them

A wedding feast

“The children of the bridechamber” are the groom's friends. A village wedding went on for days, and nobody mourned at it.

When He is taken away

Then they fast

“Taken away” is the first hint in Mark of the cross. The Bridegroom would be taken from them by force.

Jesus isn't against fasting. He's saying following Him isn't a funeral. When the Bridegroom is in the room, it's a celebration.

Old bottles

“Bottles” here are goatskin bags. New wine keeps working and swelling, so it goes in a new, soft skin. An old skin is stretched out and stiff.

Pour it into an old skin and the skin splits: wine and bottle both lost. A new patch tears an old coat the same way.

This isn't about drinking. It's about the container. Jesus doesn't patch up the old life. He makes a new man.

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Part three

Lord of the sabbath

Verses 23–28

A handful of grain, and a question about who the day of rest belongs to.

What the law said, and what they added

In the King James Bible, “corn” means grain: wheat or barley, not the corn we grow. Hungry men walking along a field pulled off heads of grain, rubbed them, and ate them.

God's law

Allowed

“When thou comest into the standing corn of thy neighbour, then thou mayest pluck the ears with thine hand; but thou shalt not move a sickle unto thy neighbour’s standing corn.” (Deuteronomy 23:25)

The Pharisees' rules

“Not lawful”

They had made long lists of what counted as work. To them, picking was reaping and rubbing was threshing: two jobs on the sabbath.

God gave the sabbath. The fences men built around it had grown so high you couldn't see God's kindness anymore.

David and the holy bread

The “shewbread” was twelve loaves set out on a table in the house of God, “two rows, six on a row” (Leviticus 24:6). Each sabbath fresh loaves went on, and only the priests could eat the old ones.

When David was running for his life from King Saul, hungry, he came to the house of God, and the priest gave him those loaves (1 Samuel 21):

“So the priest gave him hallowed bread: for there was no bread there but the shewbread, that was taken from before the LORD, to put hot bread in the day when it was taken away.”

1 Samuel 21:6

The Pharisees honored David. God didn't let a hungry man starve over a rule about bread. Meeting real need was never against His heart.

A gift, not a cage

“The sabbath was made for man.”

God made the sabbath as a gift: a day to stop, rest, and remember Him (Exodus 20:8–11). The Pharisees had turned it into a burden that men served.

And then the bigger claim: the Son of man is Lord of the sabbath. God made the sabbath. Jesus says it's His. Real rest is found in Him.

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Five complaints

Mark lines up five run-ins with the religious leaders, one after another. Watch them get sharper.

  1. 2:7“Who can forgive sins?”in their hearts
  2. 2:16“He eateth with sinners”to His disciples
  3. 2:18“Thy disciples fast not”to His face
  4. 2:24“Not lawful” on the sabbathan accusation
  5. 3:2–6healing on the sabbatha plot to kill Him

Every answer Jesus gives points to who He is: the One who forgives sins, the Physician, the Bridegroom, the Lord of the sabbath.

They were watching for something to accuse. The sinners at Levi's table were just glad He came. Which one you are depends on whether you know you need Him.

Points to remember

  1. Friends“When Jesus saw their faith” 2:5Some men need carrying. Be one of the four, and let others carry you.
  2. Forgiveness“Who can forgive sins but God only?” 2:7Only God can, and Jesus did, because He is God.
  3. Salvation“Not to call the righteous, but sinners” 2:17Jesus came for the sick. Knowing you need Him is the door in.
  4. Something new“New wine must be put into new bottles” 2:22He doesn't patch the old life. He makes a new man.
  5. The sabbath“The sabbath was made for man” 2:27God's rest is a gift, not a cage. Real rest is in Jesus.
  6. Who Jesus is“The Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins” 2:10The Son of man has heaven's authority, and He is Lord of the sabbath.

Question 1 of 6

Friends

What can friends do for a man who can't get to Jesus on his own?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They can carry him. They can refuse to quit when the door is blocked. And Jesus honors their faith. Some days you're the man on the mat, some days you're one of the four.

Question 2 of 6

Forgiveness

Who can forgive sins?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Only God, and the scribes were right about that. Jesus forgave the man's sins, then proved He had the power by making him walk. He forgives because He is God, and He still forgives anyone who comes to Him.

Question 3 of 6

Salvation (soteriology)

Who did Jesus come to call?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Sinners. He called a crooked tax man and sat down to eat with his friends. He is the Physician, and He came for the sick. Nobody is too far gone for Him. The only ones who miss out are those who think they don't need Him.

Question 4 of 6

Something new

Can Jesus just be patched onto an old life?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. A new patch tears an old coat, and new wine bursts old bottles. Jesus doesn't add a little religion to the old life. He makes a new man, like Levi, who got up from his table and followed.

Question 5 of 6

The sabbath

What was the sabbath made for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For man: as God's gift of rest, not a burden to serve. God never meant His day to keep a hungry man from eating. And the Son of man is its Lord, so real rest is found in Him.

Question 6 of 6

Who Jesus is

Who is the Son of man?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Jesus. It's His own name for Himself, from Daniel's vision of the One given an everlasting kingdom. He is truly a man, and He has the power of God: to forgive sins on earth, to be the Bridegroom, and to be Lord of the sabbath.
