Tested, then sent

Part one

Tempted in the wilderness

Verses 1–11

Forty days without food, and then the devil comes with an offer.

From the river to the desert

At His baptism the Spirit came down on Him, and the Father spoke:

“And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

Matthew 3:17

“Then.” Right after that, the Spirit led Him up into the wilderness of Judaea: bare, rocky hills climbing from the Dead Sea toward Jerusalem. Mark says He was “with the wild beasts” (Mark 1:13).

The test often comes right after the high point. Being tested didn't mean God had left Him. The Spirit led Him there.

Forty in the wilderness

Israel

Forty years

Grumbled for bread, put God to the test, and bowed down to a golden calf. They failed in the wilderness.

Jesus

Forty days

Hungry and alone, tested on bread, on testing God, and on worship. He did not fail once.

“And thou shalt remember all the way which the LORD thy God led thee these forty years in the wilderness, to humble thee, and to prove thee, to know what was in thine heart, whether thou wouldest keep his commandments, or no.”

Deuteronomy 8:2

Watch where all three of Jesus' answers come from: Deuteronomy, the book where Moses looked back on Israel's forty years in the wilderness. Where Israel fell, Jesus stood, for them and for us. “An hungred” is old English for hungry.

Test one: bread

The tempterIf thou be the Son of God, command that these stones be made bread.

JesusIt is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.
Deuteronomy 8:3

“If thou be the Son of God.” The Father had just said it. The devil's first move is to make Him doubt it, or prove it. He still works that way: “If God really loved you…”

Bread isn't sinful, and Jesus was starving. The temptation was to meet a real need His own way, without the Father. Ours work the same way, and they come when we're hungry and worn down.

Test two: the devil quotes Scripture

The devil quoted Psalm 91. Here is what the psalm actually says:

“For he shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways.left out They shall bear thee up in their hands, lest thou dash thy foot against a stone.” Psalm 91:11–12

He dropped the words “to keep thee in all thy ways.” The promise is for a man walking in God's ways, not for a man throwing himself off a roof to make God catch him.

The pinnacle was a high point on the temple's walls, probably the corner that looked straight down into the valley below. Jesus answered with Deuteronomy 6:16. To “tempt” God here means to test Him, to force His hand. Walking into the bar to prove you're strong, and calling it faith, is the same trap.

Test three: a crown without a cross

The devil's offer→all the kingdoms, today, for one bowNo cross, no suffering. Just a new master.
The Father's way→all the kingdoms, after the cross“All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth” (Matthew 28:18).

Jesus was always going to have the kingdoms. The devil offered Him a shortcut that skipped the cross, and the cross is the only way anyone gets saved. “Get thee hence, Satan”: Satan means the adversary. Jesus didn't argue. He sent him away.

“Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.”

James 4:7

The devil left, but Luke says only “for a season” (Luke 4:13). And the angels came and cared for Him, the very angels the devil had dared Him to call down.

Three tests, one answer

“Command that these stones be made bread.”It is writtenDeuteronomy 8:3. Trust God's word more than your hunger.
“Cast thyself down.”It is writtenDeuteronomy 6:16. Don't put God to the test.
“Fall down and worship me.”It is writtenDeuteronomy 6:13. Worship and serve God alone.

Jesus didn't use any weapon we don't have. He used the written Word, with the Spirit's help. Every believer has both.

“Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee.”

Psalm 119:11

Pick a few verses for your weak moments and learn them by heart. When the craving talks, you need something to say back.

Tempted is not the same as sinning

Tempted. Yet without sin.

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.”

Hebrews 4:15

Some men feel dirty just for being tempted. But Jesus was tempted, and He never sinned. The pull isn't the sin. Giving in is. So He understands the fight from the inside, and He doesn't look down on you for being in it.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Part two

A great light

Verses 12–17

John is in prison. Jesus moves to a lakeside town and begins to preach.

Where verse 12 fits

  1. Baptizedat the Jordan3:13–17
  2. Forty daystempted in the wilderness4:1–11
  3. Some monthsearly work, told only by JohnJohn 1–4
  4. John in prisonHerod Antipas locks him up4:12
  5. CapernaumJesus moves there and preaches4:13–17

Matthew skips ahead. John's Gospel fills in the months between: the wedding at Cana, Nicodemus, the woman at the well. John even says so:

“For John was not yet cast into prison.”

John 3:24

Herod Antipas ruled Galilee for Rome. He jailed John for saying plainly that Herod had no right to his brother's wife (Matthew 14:3–4). This is probably around A.D. 27 or 28.

From Nazareth to Capernaum

Nazareth was the hill village where Jesus grew up. When He preached there, His own town tried to throw Him off a cliff (Luke 4:29).

Capernaum was a fishing town on the lakeshore (“the sea coast”), on a busy road. Peter's house was there (8:14). It became Jesus' home base.

Zabulon and Nephthalim are Zebulun and Naphtali, two of Israel's twelve tribes. This was their land. (The lines are rough.)

First into the dark, first to see the light

about 732 B.C.Carried away

Assyria takes Galilee, “all the land of Naphtali,” and carries its people off (2 Kings 15:29).

the centuries between“Galilee of the Gentiles”

Resettled with foreigners. Mixed, ruled by outsiders, looked down on by Jerusalem.

Matthew 4:16“Great light”

Jesus makes His home there and begins to preach.

“Nevertheless the dimness shall not be such as was in her vexation, when at the first he lightly afflicted the land of Zebulun and the land of Naphtali, and afterward did more grievously afflict her by the way of the sea, beyond Jordan, in Galilee of the nations. The people that walked in darkness have seen a great light: they that dwell in the land of the shadow of death, upon them hath the light shined.”

Isaiah 9:1–2

Matthew says it plainly: “that it might be fulfilled.” The land hit first and hardest saw the light first. If you've lived in “the region and shadow of death,” Jesus doesn't just pass through. He moves in.

What “repent” means

μετανοεῖτε said metanoeite “change your mind, turn around”

Repenting isn't just feeling bad. It's a change of mind that turns the whole man around: away from sin, and toward God. And you don't have to clean yourself up first. You turn to Him, and He does the cleaning.

“The kingdom of heaven” is Matthew's way of saying the kingdom of God. Many Jews said “heaven” out of respect for God's name. Mark gives the same sermon this way:

“And saying, The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel.”

Mark 1:15

“At hand” means close enough to touch. The King was standing right in front of them.

Part three

Fishers of men

Verses 18–25

Four working men drop their nets. Then all Galilee comes looking for Jesus.

“I will make you”

A cast net is round and weighted all around its edge. Thrown by hand, it spreads open and sinks over the fish.

Jesus used what they knew: you catch fish; I'll make you catch men. He can use your story, even the hard years, to reach men nobody else can.

They had met Him at the Jordan (John 1:40–42). Now He called them to leave it all. “I will make you”: you come, and He does the making.

What it cost them

Simon and Andrew→“straightway left their nets”Their tools, and the way they made a living (v. 20).
James and John→“left the ship and their father”The family business, and the man who ran it (v. 22).

“And straightway he called them: and they left their father Zebedee in the ship with the hired servants, and went after him.”

Mark 1:20

They didn't leave their father stranded. Zebedee had hired men to help. But they did leave. Following Jesus isn't how a man gets saved; that's a free gift, received by faith. It's how a saved man lives. Some of us have nets to drop today: old friends, old places, old ways of making money.

From every direction

Word got out fast. Syria was the Roman province to the north, and Rome governed this whole region through it.

Decapolis means the Ten Cities: Greek-style towns, mostly east of the lake, full of Gentiles. “Beyond Jordan” is the land east of the river. Jews and Gentiles, city men and country men, all came.

Matthew 5 opens with this crowd. Jesus will go up the hillside and teach them.

Teaching, preaching, healing

Notice the last four words: “and he healed them.” Whatever had hold of a man, sickness, pain, or a dark spirit, Jesus was stronger. Nothing that has hold of you is stronger than He is.

Question 1 of 6

Temptation

How did Jesus fight back when He was tempted?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Every time, with Scripture: “It is written.” He didn't argue or bargain, and He used no power we don't have. The Word hidden in your heart is a weapon for your weakest moments.

Question 2 of 6

No shame in the fight

Is it a sin to be tempted?

What verses tell us about this?

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.”

Hebrews 4:15

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Answer

No. Jesus was tempted in every way we are, and He never sinned. The pull is not the sin; giving in is. He understands the fight, He doesn't look down on you for being in it, and God always makes a way of escape.

Question 3 of 6

The devil's offer

What does the devil's shortcut always leave out?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It leaves out God's way. The devil cut the words “to keep thee in all thy ways” out of the psalm, and he offered Jesus the kingdoms without the cross. His shortcuts always skip obedience, and they always come with a price: bowing down to him.

Question 4 of 6

Light

What happens when Jesus comes to people sitting in darkness?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Answer

The light breaks in. Galilee was the land hit first and hardest, and it was the first to see the light. Jesus moved in there. Whoever follows Him will not walk in darkness.

Question 5 of 6

Repentance

What does Jesus mean when He says, “Repent”?

What verses tell us about this?

“And saying, The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel.”

Mark 1:15

“Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord;”

Acts 3:19

Answer

To change your mind and turn around: away from sin, and toward God. Repenting and believing go together. You don't clean up first. You turn to Jesus as you are, trust Him, and He blots out your sins.

Question 6 of 6

Following

What did the fishermen leave, and what did Jesus promise to make them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They left their nets, their boat, and their father's business, right away. Jesus promised to make them fishers of men. He used what they already knew, and He did the making. He can do the same with your story.
