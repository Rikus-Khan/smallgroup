Numbers 4

Who carries the tabernacle

When the camp sets forward, Aaron and his sons cover the holy things first, and then three families of Levites carry them. Every man from thirty to fifty has his own service and his own burden, and the LORD counts them: eight thousand five hundred and fourscore.

Part one

“The service of the sons of Kohath”

Verses 1–20

The most holy things: covered by the priests, then carried, and never touched.

Thirty to fifty

  • the sumthe total: “take the sum” means count them
  • the hostan army, a company set in ranks: “all that enter into the host, to do the work”
  • thirty to fiftythe working years of a Levite for this carrying
  • the workservice in the tabernacle: the heavy, daily labour of a camp that moves

“From thirty years old and upward” to fifty years old comes seven times in this chapter (verses 3, 23, 30, 35, 39, 43 and 47). The Levites were left out of the war-count of the other tribes: “Only thou shalt not number the tribe of Levi, neither take the sum of them among the children of Israel” (1:49). They are counted here for their own work, and the LORD says how old a man is to be for it.

The vail goes over the ark

The first thing laid on the ark is the covering vail itself, the cloth that hung between the holy place and the most holy place. Aaron and his sons take it down; no Levite does. Then the badgers' skins, then a cloth wholly of blue over all.

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;”

Hebrews 10:19–20

Hebrews says the way through that vail is Christ's flesh. Here the vail is wrapped round the ark and carried out into the wilderness, and the ark stays hidden all the way.

The badgers' skins come seven times in the chapter (verses 6, 8, 10, 11, 12, 14 and 25): the plain outer coat the camp saw as it moved. On everything but the ark that is the outside; the ark alone has blue on top. Moses doesn't say why. The poles, or staves, go in last, and the candlestick and the instruments ride on a bar.

They shall not touch any holy thing

  • pertainethbelongs to: the oil, the incense, the meat offering and the anointing oil were Eleazar's charge
  • the burdenthe load a man carries; the word comes nine times in this chapter
  • the sons of Kohaththey bear it, but only after it is covered, and only by the staves and the bar
  • lest they diethe LORD is holy, and a man who handles holy things as if they were common is in danger

The priests do the touching. The Levites do the carrying. Neither does the other's work, and nobody lays a hand on what is most holy.

“That they may live, and not die”

Look at what the LORD does here: he has just said the Kohathites will die if they touch. Now he tells Moses and Aaron to take care of them: “Cut ye not off the tribe… But thus do unto them, that they may live, and not die.” Aaron and his sons go in first, cover everything, and appoint each man his burden. They are not even to go in and look (verse 20). The men are kept alive by the priests who go in before them.

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

A man in recovery knows what it is to be afraid of coming near to God because of what he has done. The LORD in Numbers 4 is holy, and he makes a way. In Christ, the High Priest has gone in before us, and we are told to come boldly.

Part two

“Gershon… and Merari”

Verses 21–33

The curtains, the boards and the pins: the work nobody sings about.

Who carried what

Kohath

45–1516

The most holy things: the ark, the table, the candlestick, the golden altar, the instruments and the other altar. Covered by Aaron and his sons; the oversight is Eleazar's.

Gershon

2425–2628

The curtains of the tabernacle and its coverings, the hanging for the door, the hangings of the court and the cords. Under the hand of Ithamar.

Merari

313233

The boards, the bars, the pillars and the sockets of the tabernacle, and the pillars, sockets, pins and cords of the court. “By name ye shall reckon the instruments.” Under the hand of Ithamar.

Two sons of Aaron stand over them: Eleazar over the holy things and Ithamar over the rest. Merari carried the frame of the whole tent and court: the boards, the bars, the pillars and the sockets. “By name ye shall reckon the instruments” (verse 32): every piece is listed by name and put in a man's charge.

The tabernacle they carried

  • The tent itselfboards and bars (Merari); curtains and coverings (Gershon)
  • The courtpillars and sockets (Merari); the hangings and the door (Gershon)
  • Inside and at the altarthe holy things (Kohath), covered first by the priests

Everything that stood in the court was carried by someone whose name was on a list. When the camp stopped, the Levites set it up; when it moved, they took it down.

Part three

“Every one according to his service”

Verses 34–49

The numbering of the Levites, family by family.

Eight thousand five hundred and fourscore

Kohathverse 362,750 Gershonverse 402,630 Merariverse 443,200 All the Levitesverse 488,580

2,750 and 2,630 and 3,200 make 8,580, which is exactly the total in verse 48. Fourscore is eighty, so it reads “eight thousand and five hundred and fourscore”. These are only the working men from thirty to fifty. Chapter 3 had counted every male Levite from a month old and upward: twenty and two thousand (3:39). Merari, the family with the boards and pillars, had the most men.

Every one according to his service

“But now hath God set the members every one of them in the body, as it hath pleased him.”

1 Corinthians 12:18

No man picked his own load, and none was passed over. The one carrying the pins and cords of the court had a place in the camp as surely as the one under the ark's blue cloth. Paul says the church is the same: every member set by God, and “if they were all one member, where were the body?” (1 Corinthians 12:19).

Real world: the load you carry today

Most of Numbers 4 is curtains, boards and pins. It is God's way of saying that the work nobody sees is counted, and the men who do it have a name on the list.

  • SinThe Kohathites could not handle holy things as if they were common. We do the same when we treat God as a hand to call on in a bad hour and forget the rest of the day. We also resent the small job when we wanted the big one.
  • RegretMany of us spent our thirties, or our twenties, on the wrong work. The LORD still had a service for every man in the camp, and he wrote the number of them down.
  • Grace“That they may live, and not die”: the priests went in first and made the way. In Christ the High Priest has gone in first, and we may come boldly (Hebrews 4:16).
  • Hope“God is not unrighteous to forget your work and labour of love” (Hebrews 6:10). The carrying nobody thanks you for is not forgotten.
  • TodayDo the small job in front of you well, and carry a brother's load: “Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ” (Galatians 6:2). Call or text a brother in Christ and ask what he is carrying.

Points to remember

  1. The age“From thirty years old and upward even until fifty years old” 4:3The LORD set the age of the work, and he counted every man who did it.
  2. The covering“Aaron shall come, and his sons, and they shall take down the covering vail, and cover the ark of testimony with it” 4:5The priests covered the holy things first. Nobody carried what was not covered.
  3. The warning“they shall not touch any holy thing, lest they die” 4:15God is holy, and he made a way so that the men might live.
  4. The three families“the sons of Kohath shall come to bear it” 4:15Kohath carried the holy things, Gershon the curtains and hangings, Merari the boards and pillars.
  5. The count“eight thousand and five hundred and fourscore” 4:482,750 and 2,630 and 3,200: 8,580 men, each one counted.
  6. The burden“every one according to his service, and according to his burden” 4:49God gave every man his work, and he knows it by name.

Question 1 of 6

The age

What ages were the men who did the work of the tabernacle?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

From thirty years old to fifty. It is said seven times in the chapter: twice for each family, when they were to be numbered and when they were numbered, and once more for the whole count.

Question 2 of 6

The covering

Who covered the holy things before anyone carried them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Aaron and his sons, the priests. Only after they had finished covering the sanctuary and all its vessels did the sons of Kohath come to bear them.

Question 3 of 6

The warning

What did the LORD say would happen if the sons of Kohath touched the holy things?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

Answer

They would die. So the LORD had Aaron and his sons go in first and appoint every man his burden, “that they may live, and not die”. In Christ we are told to come boldly to the throne of grace.

Question 4 of 6

The three families

What did each of the three families of the Levites carry?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Kohath bore the most holy things, covered by the priests. Gershon bore the curtains, the coverings and the hangings of the court. Merari bore the boards, the bars, the pillars and the sockets.

Question 5 of 6

The count

How many men were numbered for the service?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Eight thousand five hundred and fourscore: 8,580. That is 2,750 of Kohath, 2,630 of Gershon and 3,200 of Merari.

Question 6 of 6

The burden

How was each man's work decided?

What verses tell us about this?

“But now hath God set the members every one of them in the body, as it hath pleased him.”

1 Corinthians 12:18

Answer

By the LORD's commandment, through Aaron and his sons: each man appointed “to his service and to his burden”, with the instruments reckoned by name. God sets every member where he has pleased.
