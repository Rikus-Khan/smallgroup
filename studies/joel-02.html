Part one

The LORD's army

Verses 1–11

The locusts come back, and this time they march like soldiers.

Sound the alarm

שׁוֹפָר said shofar a ram's-horn trumpet

A watchman on the wall blew the shofar when an enemy was coming. Zion is Jerusalem's hill, where the temple stood. The swarm is so thick it turns morning into “a day of darkness”: a locust cloud can blot out the sun.

An alarm is a kindness. Nobody blows a trumpet to trap a city. He blows it so people can get ready, while there's still time.

Before them, behind them

Before them

The garden of Eden

Green fields, full vines, fig trees heavy with fruit.

Behind them

A desolate wilderness

Bare dirt and white sticks. “Nothing shall escape them.”

A locust's head really does look like a small horse's. The Italian word for a grasshopper, cavalletta, means “little horse.” Joel hears the swarm coming like chariots over the hills, and like fire crackling through dry stubble.

That's what sin does to a life. It comes in looking like nothing much, and it leaves a man's Eden looking like a wasteland.

An army no wall can stop

Rank after rank, every one in his path. They don't push each other. Swords don't stop them. They climb the walls, run over the roofs, and come in at the windows “like a thief.”

Men in recovery know this feeling. You wall off the front door, and the old thing comes in at a window: a memory, a lonely night, an old friend's text. Walls alone don't hold. A man needs the Lord, and he needs brothers on the wall with him.

“His army”

Here is the hard part. God calls the swarm his army, and in verse 25 He says, “my great army which I sent among you.” This wasn't bad luck. God sent it, to wake His people up before something worse came.

“Who can abide it?”NobodyNo man can stand up to God's judgment on his own strength or his own record.
“Therefore also now…”TurnThe very next verse. The door is still open.

Part two

Rend your heart

Verses 12–17

“Therefore also now.” It isn't too late.

Tear the right thing

When an Israelite heard terrible news, he tore his clothes. It was the normal sign of grief. God says: I don't want your shirt torn. I want your heart torn open.

  • rend your garmentsa show on the outside
  • a promise to do betteruntil the next time
  • rend your hearthonest sorrow, turning to God with all of it

“The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.”

Psalm 51:17

Why turn? Because of who He is

Exodus 34:6, God to Moses

“And the LORD passed by before him, and proclaimed, The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth,”

Joel 2:13, hundreds of years later

“for he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repenteth him of the evil.”

Joel quotes what God said about Himself at Mount Sinai. That's the reason to come back: God hasn't changed. “Repenteth him of the evil” doesn't mean God did wrong. Here “evil” means harm, disaster. He holds back the judgment He warned about when people turn.

“Who knoweth?” Joel can't promise the crops will come back. But he knows God's heart, and that's enough to come home on.

Everybody comes

Same trumpet as verse 1, but now it calls people to prayer instead of sounding an alarm. And nobody is excused:

  1. The eldersthe old men who lead
  2. The childreneven babies at the breast
  3. The bride and groomeven newlyweds, normally excused from war
  4. The priestsweeping “between the porch and the altar,” in the temple court

Listen to how the priests pray: “Where is their God?” They don't ask on their own record. They ask God to act for the sake of His own name. That's still the best way to pray when we've blown it.

Part three

I will restore

Verses 18–27

“Then.” The people turned, and God answered.

Driven into the sea

“Jealous” here isn't petty envy. It's a husband's fierce, protective love. God calls the swarm “the northern army.” Locusts usually came from the south or east, so many think Joel is also looking ahead to the armies that later came down from the north.

Either way, God drives it off: into the Dead Sea (“the east sea”) and the Mediterranean (“the utmost sea”). Locust swarms really do drown at sea and wash up rotting on the beach. “His stink shall come up.”

The rain comes back

Israel's farmers lived by two rains. Without them, nothing grew.

  1. Fallthe former rain softens the ground
  2. Winterplowing and sowing; the grain grows
  3. Springthe latter rain fills out the grain
  4. Early summerbarley, then wheat: “the floors shall be full”
  5. Late summergrapes and olives: “the fats shall overflow”

“That I will give you the rain of your land in his due season, the first rain and the latter rain, that thou mayest gather in thy corn, and thy wine, and thine oil.”

Deuteronomy 11:14

The “floors” are threshing floors; the “fats” are the vats under the presses. Joel 1 was empty barns. Now it's full floors. The wine here is a picture of a good harvest, life coming back to the land. For a man in recovery, what God restores is his life, not the bottle.

Down, and back up

Look at verse 25: the same four insects from chapter 1, one by one. God doesn't pretend the losses didn't happen. He names every one of them, and then He says, “I will restore.”

This promise was made to Judah: harvests so big they'd make up for the lost years. God kept it. And the God who restored Judah still restores. He doesn't turn back the calendar, and some consequences stay. But no man is too wasted for God to make his life fruitful again.

“He restoreth my soul: he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name’s sake.”

Psalm 23:3

And the best part isn't the grain. It's verse 27: “I am in the midst of Israel.” God Himself among His people. Twice He says, “my people shall never be ashamed.”

Part four

My Spirit

Verses 28–32

A promise so big that Peter stood up on the day of Pentecost and said, “This is that.”

All flesh

In the Old Testament, the Spirit came on certain men for a certain job: a prophet, a judge, a king. Joel promises something new: the Spirit poured out on all kinds of God's people. Watch the walls come down:

“For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit.”

1 Corinthians 12:13

“This is that”

Joel 2:28

“And it shall come to pass afterward, that I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh…”

Acts 2:16–17, Peter at Pentecost

“But this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel; And it shall come to pass in the last days, saith God, I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh: and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, and your young men shall see visions, and your old men shall dream dreams:”

About fifty days after Jesus rose, the Holy Spirit came on His followers in Jerusalem. Peter told the crowd: this is what Joel promised.

And it isn't only for a few. Every believer receives the Spirit the moment he believes (Ephesians 1:13). He doesn't have to earn it later or wait for a second experience. God lives in him from day one.

Between now and that day

Peter quoted these verses too (Acts 2:19–20). Pentecost opened what the New Testament calls “the last days.” The final day of the LORD is still ahead, and signs in the sky will come before it.

  1. Joelthe promise
  2. Pentecostthe Spirit poured out (Acts 2)
  3. Today“whosoever shall call”
  4. Signssun dark, moon blood
  5. The day“great and terrible”

We live in the middle: the Spirit has come, the day hasn't. That's why the next verse matters so much.

“And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Acts 2:21, Peter

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13, Paul

Peter and Paul both quote this verse, and Paul shows that the Lord to call on is Jesus (Romans 10:9). No fine print. No man too far gone. The same day that will be terrible for those who refuse Him is deliverance for anyone who calls.

  3. God's character“He is gracious and merciful, slow to anger” 2:13God hasn't changed. That's why it's worth coming back.
  4. Restoration“I will restore to you the years” 2:25God kept this promise to Judah, and He still restores broken lives.
  5. The Holy Spirit“I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh” 2:28Since Pentecost, every believer receives the Spirit.

Question 1 of 6

God's warnings

Why does God warn before He judges?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Because He wants people to turn and live. The trumpet in Zion was an alarm, sounded while there was still time. Right after asking “who can abide it?” God says, “Therefore also now… turn ye even to me.” He is patient, not willing that any should perish.

Question 2 of 6

Repentance

What does it mean to rend your heart?

What verses tell us about this?

“The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.”

Psalm 51:17

Answer

Tearing your clothes was the outward sign of grief. God wants the real thing: a heart torn open, honest about sin, turning to Him with all of it. Not a show, and not just a promise to do better. God never turns away a broken and contrite heart.

Question 3 of 6

God's character

What is God like toward people who turn back?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD passed by before him, and proclaimed, The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth,”

Exodus 34:6

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Gracious, merciful, slow to anger, and full of kindness. That's how God described Himself to Moses, and Joel says He's still the same. When Judah turned, God pitied His people and answered. When we confess, He is faithful to forgive and to cleanse.

Question 4 of 6

Restoration

Can God give back the years we wasted?

What verses tell us about this?

“He restoreth my soul: he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name’s sake.”

Psalm 23:3

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

The promise of verse 25 was God's word to Judah, and He kept it with harvests that made up for the lost years. The principle stands for us: God restores. He doesn't turn back the clock, and some consequences remain. But He makes the man in Christ new, and He can make the years ahead fruitful in ways the wasted years never were.

Question 5 of 6

The Holy Spirit

Who receives the Spirit God promised?

What verses tell us about this?

“But this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel; And it shall come to pass in the last days, saith God, I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh: and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, and your young men shall see visions, and your old men shall dream dreams:”

Acts 2:16–17

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

Answer

Every believer. Joel promised the Spirit on all kinds of God's people: men and women, young and old, even servants. Peter said it began at Pentecost. Paul says that when you believe, you are sealed with the Holy Spirit. If you belong to Christ, the Spirit lives in you right now.

Question 6 of 6

Salvation (soteriology)

Who can call on the Lord and be saved?

What verses tell us about this?

“And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Acts 2:21

“That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.”

Romans 10:9

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

Whosoever. Anyone at all. Joel said it, Peter preached it, and Paul wrote it down: call on the name of the Lord and you will be saved. Paul shows that Lord is Jesus. Believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, call on Him, and you are His.
