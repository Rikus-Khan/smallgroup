Exodus 1

A new king, and a people in chains

Joseph's family came to Egypt as guests, seventy souls. Hundreds of years later a king who never knew Joseph turns them into slaves, and tries to end them. He is stopped by a few women who feared God.

The book of Exodus

Who: Moses (Exodus 17:14, 24:4, 34:27). Jesus calls it “the book of Moses” (Mark 12:26).

When: written in the forty years in the wilderness (Acts 7:36), after the LORD brought Israel out of Egypt.

To whom: Israel, freed slaves who needed to know the God who rescued them.

Exodus is Greek for the way out. The map follows that road, from Rameses over the sea at Nuweiba to Mount Sinai in Arabia (Galatians 4:25).

Exodus at a glance

  1. Jacob 130Jacob's family goes down to Egyptch. 1
  2. Joseph 110Joseph dies; the slavery beginsch. 1
  3. a new kingMoses is bornch. 2
  4. Moses 40Moses flees to Midianch. 2
  5. Moses 80The bush; the plagues; out of Egyptch. 3–15
  6. third monthMount Sinai: the lawch. 19–24
  7. second yearThe tabernacle is raisedch. 25–40

“The sojourning of the children of Israel, who dwelt in Egypt, was four hundred and thirty years” (12:40). Joseph dies at a hundred and ten (Genesis 50:26), and “there arose up a new king over Egypt, which knew not Joseph” (1:8). Exodus opens in those hard years.

The book has two halves. Chapters 1 to 18: God brings his people out. Chapters 19 to 40: God brings them to himself, gives his law, and comes to live among them.

Part one

Seventy souls

Verses 1–7

How a family became a nation, in a few short verses.

“Now these are the names”

שְׁמוֹת said Shemot “names”: the Hebrew name of the whole book

Exodus starts by picking up right where Genesis stopped: the same twelve sons, the same family. A list of names is a strange way to start a rescue story. But God knows his people by name, every one.

Verse 5 counts them: seventy. “Loins” is an old word for the body a child comes from: all who were Jacob's children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren. “Joseph was in Egypt already”: he had gone first, sold by his brothers, and God used it to feed them all.

From seventy to a nation

Going down

70 souls

Jacob's whole family, when Jacob was 130 (Genesis 47:9).

Coming out

600,000

“about six hundred thousand on foot that were men, beside children” (12:37)

“And he said, I am God, the God of thy father: fear not to go down into Egypt; for I will there make of thee a great nation:”

Genesis 46:3

God promised exactly this, and in Egypt it came true.

Part two

A king who knew not Joseph

Verses 8–14

The good years are forgotten, and fear gets to work.

A king who forgot

The Bible doesn't name this king (“Pharaoh” is a title, “great house”). What matters is what he forgot: Joseph had saved Egypt from starving (Genesis 41).

Pharaoh's fearIf they multiply and join our enemies…
→
his plan“Come on, let us deal wisely with them.”

Fear turned a harmless people into a problem to solve, and called the cruelty “wise”.

A man does this too: he calls a plan “smart” when it is only afraid. Ask what you are really afraid of.

Pithom and Raamses

These were the cities Israel built, in the land of Goshen, in the north-east of Egypt's Nile delta. “Raamses” is the KJV's spelling of Rameses. Pharaoh's “treasure cities” were not for gold: they were store cities, with warehouses for grain and supplies.

The same city, Rameses, is where the Exodus will start (12:37). The place they were made to build is the place they will leave.

Hard words in verses 11 to 14

  1. taskmastersslave drivers: men set over them to make sure the work got done
  2. afflictto crush, to make suffer
  3. rigourharsh, merciless severity, with no pity in it
  4. mortermortar: the wet clay and straw they trod and mixed for the bricks
  5. grieved(v. 12) not “sad”: filled with dread and disgust

The Egyptians did not just work them. They made their lives bitter. Mud and bricks, and “all manner of service in the field”, from dawn to dark, and the pile of work never got smaller.

A master who is never satisfied

  1. More bricksand then more, and then more again
  2. No restthe work is never done
  3. No wagesthe master takes, and gives nothing back
  4. No way outand he calls it “serving”

Some of the men in this room know exactly what that sounds like. A habit is a taskmaster too: it takes more every time, it never says “enough”, and it gives nothing back. Jesus said it plainly:

“Jesus answered them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin. And the servant abideth not in the house for ever: but the Son abideth ever. If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:34–36

Pharaoh could not break Israel. And no master, not Pharaoh, not the bottle, can break a man the Son has set free.

Part three

The midwives

Verses 15–22

When the king gives an evil order, two women say no.

Two names a king never wrote

Pharaoh's name is not in this chapter. Two midwives' names are: Shiphrah and Puah. Slaves with no power, and God put them in the book for ever.

  1. the stoolsthe low birthing seat a woman sat on to give birth
  2. livelystrong and full of life
  3. erebefore: the babies come before the midwife does
  4. made them housesgave them families of their own, and a place among God's people

Pharaoh's order was plain and evil: kill the boys. This was no longer slavery. It was an attempt to end a people.

Feared God, not Pharaoh

The king

Pharaoh's order

He had the army, the law and the whip. Every worker in Egypt knew what happened to anyone who said no.

The midwives

They “feared God”

They feared Someone greater than the king. So they “did not as the king of Egypt commanded them”, and the boys lived.

“And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell.”

Matthew 10:28

In verses 18 and 19 they talk their way past an angry king. The Bible doesn't tell us to copy what they said to him. It praises what they did: they feared God, and they would not take part in killing.

What God did for them

Two women with no power, and a king with an army. God “dealt well” with them, and gave them families of their own. He doesn't forget a small yes to him, said at a high price.

“The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.”

Proverbs 29:25

Your own “Pharaoh” may be a boss who wants a corner cut, or friends who want you back in the old life. You will not always be able to win the argument. But you can fear God more than you fear them. Pick the one thing, today, you will not do, and let your brothers in Christ know.

Pharaoh's plan, and God's

Pharaoh planned

Wear them down

Slave labour. Then killing the baby boys. Then an order to all his people: throw them into the river.

God had planned

A great nation

“The people multiplied, and waxed very mighty.” The more he pushed, the more they grew.

“There are many devices in a man’s heart; nevertheless the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand.”

Proverbs 19:21

The chapter ends on the worst order yet: the river. Turn the page, and the next chapter opens with a baby, and a basket, and that same river.

  1. God's promise“I will there make of thee a great nation” Genesis 46:3God kept his promise, even in a land of slaves.
  2. Pharaoh's fear“Come on, let us deal wisely with them; lest they multiply” 1:10Fear called cruelty wise, and it got him nothing.
  3. Slavery“They made their lives bitter with hard bondage” 1:14A master who never says enough. The Son sets men free (John 8:36).
  4. Fearing God“But the midwives feared God, and did not as the king of Egypt commanded them” 1:17Fear God more than any man. He sees the small yes.

  6. Hope“The more they afflicted them, the more they multiplied and grew” 1:12A crushed man is not a finished man.

Question 1 of 6

God's promise

Seventy people went down to Egypt. What had God promised about them?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said, I am God, the God of thy father: fear not to go down into Egypt; for I will there make of thee a great nation:”

Genesis 46:3

Answer

Seventy people went down, and God had promised Jacob that he would make them a great nation there. By the end of this chapter “the land was filled with them”. The promise did not fail, even in a land of slaves.

Question 2 of 6

Pharaoh's fear

Why did the new king turn on Israel, and what did it get him?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.”

Genesis 12:3

Answer

He feared what they might become, and did what fear does: he oppressed them. It got him the opposite: the more he afflicted them, the more they grew. God had told Abraham he would curse whoever cursed him.

Question 3 of 6

Slavery

What was life like under Pharaoh's taskmasters, and who else has a master like that?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus answered them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin.”

John 8:34

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

Answer

Hard work with no end, no wages and no mercy. Jesus says everyone who commits sin is the servant of sin: a master that never says enough. But he also says the better thing: if the Son makes you free, you are free indeed.

Question 4 of 6

Fearing God

What did the midwives do when the king gave an evil order?

What verses tell us about this?

“The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.”

Proverbs 29:25

“Then Peter and the other apostles answered and said, We ought to obey God rather than men.”

Acts 5:29

Answer

They did not do what he said. They saved the boys alive. They feared God more than the king, and God gave them families of their own. When someone over you says do wrong, obey God, and tell your brothers what you are facing.

Question 5 of 6

God's plan

Whose plan won: Pharaoh's, or God's?

What verses tell us about this?

“There are many devices in a man’s heart; nevertheless the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand.”

Proverbs 19:21

Answer

God's. Pharaoh had the power and lost to a few women and a promise. “There are many devices in a man's heart; nevertheless the counsel of the LORD, that shall stand.”

Question 6 of 6

Hope

What does a people who are crushed, and still growing, say to a man who feels at the bottom?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Psalm 34:18

“God setteth the solitary in families: he bringeth out those which are bound with chains: but the rebellious dwell in a dry land.”

Psalm 68:6

Answer

A crushed man is not a finished man. They were pressed down, and they grew. The LORD is near to the broken-hearted, and he “bringeth out those which are bound with chains”. Tell a brother today.
