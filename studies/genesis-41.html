Genesis 41

From the dungeon to the throne

Pharaoh dreams, and nobody in Egypt can read it. A forgotten prisoner is sent for, says “It is not in me”, and is made ruler of the land. Seven good years and seven hard ones are coming, and Joseph names his sons for what God has done.

Things to watch for

Part one

The king's dream

Verses 1–13

The most powerful man in the world is afraid, and nobody in his court can help him.

Old words in the dreams

  1. kinecows
  2. well favouredgood-looking, healthy
  3. fatfleshedplump, well fed
  4. ill favouredugly, sickly
  5. leanfleshedgaunt, skin and bone
  6. rankthick, full and growing well
  7. blastedscorched and shrivelled
  8. the east windthe hot, dry wind off the desert that burns up crops
  9. corngrain, wheat or barley

The river is the Nile, and in Egypt the Nile is life: it floods, it waters the fields, and the whole country eats from it. Cows feeding on its bank and grain standing thick beside it was the picture of a good year. Then in each dream the lean ones eat the fat ones, and nothing is left to show for it.

“There was none that could interpret”

Verse 8: “his spirit was troubled”. Pharaoh sends for “all the magicians of Egypt, and all the wise men thereof”. The “magicians” were the scribes and priests who dealt in sacred writings, spells and dreams, the best advisers money could buy.

  • the king’s powercould not read it
  • the wise men of Egyptcould not read it
  • all the magicianscould not read it
  • a prisoner and his Godthe only answer in Egypt

Not one of them could say what the dream meant. Power and learning both ran out. The answer was in a dungeon, with a man nobody had thought of for two years.

“I do remember my faults this day”

Two full years. The butler has not said a word about Joseph. Then the king is troubled, and suddenly he remembers.

Which “faults” does he mean? Probably the old offence that put him in prison. It may also be that he remembers how he forgot Joseph. The text does not say. What it does say is that his memory came back the day Pharaoh needed it.

We cannot know what would have happened if Joseph had been freed earlier. We can see what did happen. On the very morning a king needed an interpreter, the one man in Egypt who could give him one was ready, and God had put him in a position to be named.

If you are in a wait you cannot explain, this does not tell you the reason for yours. It does tell you that a wait is not the same as being left. God's timing is not late.

Part two

“It is not in me”

Verses 14–36

Joseph stands before the king of Egypt and gives God the credit before he gives the answer.

Shaved and changed

Pharaoh's messengers brought Joseph “hastily”, quickly, but he did not walk in as he was. “He shaved himself, and changed his raiment” (clothing).

The Egyptians kept their heads and faces shaved. The Hebrews, as a rule, wore beards. A man who has been in the dungeon and wants to be heard by a king makes himself fit to stand in front of him.

This is the plainest thing in the chapter, and it works. When the call comes (a court date, an interview, a meeting with a man you have wronged), wash, shave, put on clean clothes. It tells the other man you take him seriously. God had done the big thing already. Joseph did his small one.

“It is not in me: God shall give Pharaoh an answer of peace.”

Pharaoh has just said, “I have heard say of thee, that thou canst understand a dream to interpret it.” It is the best compliment he has ever had, from the most powerful man in the world, after thirteen years of the worst luck. And he says, in so many words: it is not in me.

“An answer of peace” means a good answer, one that is for Pharaoh's well-being. Joseph does not claim it is his to give. It is God's. That is not weakness, it is the only footing that will hold. A man with a gift and a crowd's praise is in more danger than a man in a dungeon.

“Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time:”

1 Peter 5:6

Two dreams, one meaning

Seven fat cows, seven full ears

Seven years of great plenty, throughout all the land of Egypt.

Seven thin cows, seven empty ears

Seven years of famine. They eat up the good ones, and “the plenty shall not be known”.

“The dream is one”

2532

It came twice because “the thing is established by God”, and he will “shortly bring it to pass”.

Notice what Joseph says: “What God is about to do he sheweth unto Pharaoh.” It is a warning, and God is giving it. That is mercy, since the first thing a warning gives a man is time.

This is a picture, not a measurement. Genesis gives no numbers, only the shape: seven years of more than enough, then seven years of famine, and a store laid up in between so that “the land perish not through the famine” (verse 36). Joseph's ages are worked out from 41:46 (thirty before Pharaoh) and 45:6 (two years of famine past at thirty-nine), so they are “about”.

Notice verse 30: “all the plenty shall be forgotten”. In the lean years nobody remembers the good ones. That is what makes the first seven the years that count.

A plan nobody asked for

Pharaoh asked what the dream meant. Joseph tells him, and then gives a plan: find “a man discreet and wise” (sensible and wise), set him over the land, take “the fifth part”, one part in five, of the harvest in the good years, and store it in the cities.

That is not a vision, it is a ledger. The good years are the years to put something by, and the day to start is while things are going well, before anybody feels the need.

“Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise: Which having no guide, overseer, or ruler, Provideth her meat in the summer, and gathereth her food in the harvest.”

Proverbs 6:6-8

For a man in recovery the same rule holds. A good month is not a reason to drop the meetings, the calls and the accountability. It is the time to stock them. Lay up the habits, the brothers and the Word now, because the lean years come to everyone.

Part three

Raised up

Verses 37–46

In one day, a prisoner becomes the second man in Egypt.

“A man in whom the Spirit of God is”

Pharaoh is not a believer, and he still sees it. Something about this man is plainly not Egyptian, and not ordinary. “Forasmuch as God hath shewed thee all this” (forasmuch: since, seeing that) “there is none so discreet and wise as thou art.”

In the Old Testament the Spirit of God came on chosen men for particular jobs. Since Jesus, it is different: the Holy Spirit lives in every believer, from the day he believes.

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

Could a man across the table from you see it in you? Not a sign, not a speech. A man who is steady, honest, and does not take the credit. That is what Pharaoh saw.

What Pharaoh gave him

  1. The ringthe royal signet ring; it stamped Pharaoh's word on any order
  2. Fine linen“vestures” (robes) of the best Egyptian linen, instead of prison clothes
  3. A gold chainabout his neck, the mark of a man the king delights to honour
  4. The second chariotthe next-best carriage in the kingdom, with men crying before him
  5. “Bow the knee”the cry that went ahead of him: a command to kneel
  6. All the land“without thee shall no man lift up his hand or foot”

“Only in the throne will I be greater than thou.” Only the king himself outranks him. Joseph used to wear a coat that made his brothers hate him. Now he wears the linen of the second man in Egypt, and his brothers will bow to him, as the dreams said, though not yet.

From the pit to the throne

Verse 14In the dungeon

Thirteen years from the pit at Dothan, still a prisoner, and forgotten by the one man who could have spoken.

Verse 14Sent for “hastily”

The call comes in a day. Nothing he did earned it. A dream, a memory, a king in need.

Verse 41Over all the land

“See, I have set thee over all the land of Egypt.” Thirty years old.

“He raiseth up the poor out of the dust, and lifteth the needy out of the dunghill; That he may set him with princes, even with the princes of his people.”

Psalm 113:7-8

That is grace's way: from the bottom, with nothing to show. If you are in the pit today, there is a God who raises men out of the dust, and does it at his time, and sometimes in a single day. The most any man can do is stay faithful in the place he is, as Joseph did, and wait for the call.

An Egyptian name

Joseph → Zaphnath-paaneah An Egyptian name. The Bible does not explain it; the old reading is “revealer of secrets”, and it fits the man who had just opened Pharaoh's dreams.

Pharaoh gives him a new name and a new wife, and Joseph is made, as far as Egypt is concerned, an Egyptian. He did not choose any of it. And inside, he stayed Joseph: look at the names of his sons in verses 51–52, and who he gives the credit to.

Asenath, daughter of the priest of On

On was the great temple city of the sun god, near where the Nile begins to spread into its delta. Later the Greeks called it Heliopolis, “sun city”. Poti-pherah was its priest.

Genesis says nothing about what Asenath believed, and we should not invent it. It records that she bore Joseph two sons who became two tribes of Israel. It does show his world: other gods, a priest for a father-in-law, and no church around him.

Many of you live and work like that. Joseph held on to the God of his fathers, and put his name on his sons.

Part four

Plenty, then famine

Verses 47–57

Joseph does the work of the good years, and gives his sons names that tell the story of his pain.

“Until he left numbering”

Corn as the sand of the sea, so much that he stopped counting.

Joseph was thirty when this began. He had been a shepherd boy, a slave, a prisoner, and he is now running the greatest food-storage plan the world had seen, and he does it quietly, year after year, before a single hungry day.

Faithfulness in the plenty is the hard work nobody sees. There is no crowd for it. The reward comes later, when the lean years come, and there is something in the barn.

Two names, two prayers

The firstborn

Manasseh

“Making forget”. “For God, said he, hath made me forget all my toil, and all my father's house.”

The second son

Ephraim

“Fruitful”. “For God hath caused me to be fruitful in the land of my affliction.”

Joseph does not say that his pain was good, or that it did not happen. “Toil”, “affliction”: he names it for what it was. He says God has done two things: eased the hurt, and made something grow in the very place that hurt him.

Not forgetting: healing

Did Joseph really forget his father's house? No. When his brothers walk in, he knows them at once. “Hath made me forget” means the wound no longer rules him. God has taken the sting out of the memory.

Many of you carry things you wish you could forget, things done to you, and things you did. God does not always take away the memory. He takes away its power to run your life, and then he plants something in the same ground.

“They that sow in tears shall reap in joy. He that goeth forth and weepeth, bearing precious seed, shall doubtless come again with rejoicing, bringing his sheaves with him.”

Psalm 126:5-6

What is your Ephraim? What has God made grow in the land of your affliction: a sober month, a restored marriage, a job, a brother you help? Name it today, to someone in your group. Joseph wrote his on his sons.

Old words in verses 53–57

  1. plenteousnessplenty, more than enough
  2. dearthfamine, a time when food is scarce
  3. famishedstarving
  4. waxed soregrew severe, grew heavier
  5. storehousesthe barns Joseph had filled
  6. corngrain

Everything Joseph said came to pass, to the year. The famine is “over all the face of the earth”, and Egypt, which had been warned, has bread. The warning, the plan, and the storehouses were all of God.

“Go unto Joseph; what he saith to you, do”

The people are hungry, and they cry to Pharaoh. He does not argue or bargain. He sends them: Go unto Joseph. And every country comes to that one man to buy bread.

“His mother saith unto the servants, Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it.”

John 2:5

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

The New Testament never calls Joseph a picture of Christ, so hold this lightly. But you can see the shape: a man hated by his brothers, sold, thrown in a pit and jailed though innocent, then raised up, with all authority, and the whole hungry world sent to him for bread. When we are starving, we do not need a better plan. We need to go to Jesus, and do what he says.

Question 1 of 6

Credit

When Pharaoh praises Joseph, where does Joseph send the credit?

What verses tell us about this?

“Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time:”

1 Peter 5:6

Answer

To God. “It is not in me.” Pharaoh gave him the opening to boast, and he took the lower seat. When someone praises you, for a job well done or a hard month sober, say it back the same way: that was God.

Question 2 of 6

God's timing

Were Joseph's two extra years in prison wasted?

What verses tell us about this?

“Until the time that his word came: the word of the LORD tried him. The king sent and loosed him; even the ruler of the people, and let him go free.”

Psalm 105:19-20

Answer

Not in God's plan. The butler remembered on the very day Pharaoh needed an interpreter, and Joseph was ready. We cannot always see the reason for a wait, and Genesis does not say why it was two years. But the psalm says it was “until the time that his word came”. Hold on, and keep doing what is in front of you.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

How far did God lift Joseph, and how fast?

What verses tell us about this?

“He raiseth up the poor out of the dust, and lifteth the needy out of the dunghill; That he may set him with princes, even with the princes of his people.”

Psalm 113:7-8

Answer

From the dungeon to the second place in Egypt, in a day. He did not earn it, and he did not arrange it. If you are in the dust today, God raises up the poor and the needy. For the believer, the lifting begins the day he is saved, and it is all grace.

Question 4 of 6

Wisdom

What was Joseph's plan for the good years?

What verses tell us about this?

“Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise: Which having no guide, overseer, or ruler, Provideth her meat in the summer, and gathereth her food in the harvest.”

Proverbs 6:6-8

Answer

Take a fifth of the harvest each good year, store it in the cities, and have it ready for the famine. For a man in recovery, the good months are for stocking up: the meetings, the brothers, the Word. The lean years come to everyone, and you will want to have something in the barn.

Question 5 of 6

Regret

Why did Joseph name his two sons Manasseh and Ephraim?

What verses tell us about this?

“They that sow in tears shall reap in joy.”

Psalm 126:5

Answer

To say what God had done. Manasseh: he took the sting out of the memory. Ephraim: he made something grow in the very place that hurt. Joseph does not pretend the pain did not happen. If you carry regret, bring it to God and to your brothers, and look for what he is growing in the same ground.

Question 6 of 6

The bread

Where did Pharaoh send the hungry, and who does that point to?

What verses tell us about this?

“His mother saith unto the servants, Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it.”

John 2:5

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

Answer

To Joseph, the one man with the bread. It echoes Jesus, the bread of life, to whom every hungry man can go. The New Testament does not call Joseph a picture of him, but the shape is plain. Go to Jesus, and do what he says. And go with your brothers, since no one comes to the barn alone.
