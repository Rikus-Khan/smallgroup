In chapter 21 John saw the new Jerusalem come down: no more tears, no more death, God living with His people, and the Lamb as its light.

Now he's taken inside, to the heart of the city. This is the last chapter of the last book of the Bible, and it sounds a lot like the first chapters of the first one.

Part one

The river and the tree

Verses 1–5

A river from the throne, the tree of life on its banks, and no more curse. Eden, given back.

The river of life

Ezekiel saw a river flow out of God's house, with trees on its banks: “the fruit thereof shall be for meat, and the leaf thereof for medicine” (Ezekiel 47:12). Here it is, fulfilled.

It comes from the throne. Life doesn't come from us; it flows out of God, and it never runs dry.

Fruit every month: never out of season, never short. And leaves to heal the nations: every wound sin opened between peoples, healed.

The tree lost, the tree given back

The Bible opens with the tree of life in Eden. Adam sinned, and God shut the way to it with a flaming sword.

Between the two trees stands a third. Jesus “bare our sins in his own body on the tree,” and took the curse.

The curse is paid, and the sword is gone. What Adam lost, Christ won back.

Everything sin broke in the garden, God puts back, and better. Eden was a garden for two; this is a city for “the nations.”

They shall see his face

Exodus 33:20

And he said, Thou canst not see my face: for there shall no man see me, and live.

1 John 3:2

…but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.

Shame makes a man look at the floor. Adam hid. You know what it is to not be able to look your mother, your wife or your kids in the face. In that city you'll look God in the face, and not be ashamed.

And his name shall be in their foreheads: not the beast's mark (13:16), but His. You belong to Him. And heaven isn't idle: “his servants shall serve him.”

Part two

I come quickly

Verses 6–15

“These sayings are faithful and true.” Jesus is coming, so live like it.

“Come,” seven times

Count the word come in this last chapter, verse by verse:

1 2 3 4 5 6  7×1 8 9 10 11  12×1 13 14 15 16  17×3 18 19  20×2 21 

Three times Jesus says, “I come quickly” (7, 12, 20). In verse 17 His people answer, “Come,” and call the thirsty to come. The Bible ends like a conversation: He says I'm coming, and we say come.

Quickly means soon and suddenly, on God's clock: “one day is with the Lord as a thousand years” (2 Peter 3:8). The wait isn't Him being slow. It's mercy: He is “not willing that any should perish” (2 Peter 3:9).

Worship God

John did this once before (19:10), and he does it again. An apostle, at the end of his life, overwhelmed, getting it wrong twice. The angel doesn't throw him out. He picks him up and points him the right way.

  • not the angel“I am thy fellowservant”
  • not the preachera fellowservant too, however much he helped you
  • not the man who got you soberthank him; don't worship him
  • worship Godand Jesus, the Lamb, whom heaven worships with Him (5:13)

Sealed, then open

Daniel 12:4

But thou, O Daniel, shut up the words, and seal the book, even to the time of the end: many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall be increased.

Revelation 22:10

And he saith unto me, Seal not the sayings of the prophecy of this book: for the time is at hand.

Daniel was told to seal his book: its time was far off. John is told the opposite. Read it, preach it, live by it. It's open.

Verse 11 is a warning, not a shrug. When He comes, every man stays what he is. The unjust stay unjust; the holy stay holy. The time to change sides is now, while the book is open.

Saved by grace, rewarded for work

This isn't how a man gets in. That's by grace, through faith. This is what the Lord does with the work His people did after they were saved:

“If any man’s work abide which he hath built thereupon, he shall receive a reward. If any man’s work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss: but he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire.”

1 Corinthians 3:14–15

Nothing you do for Him today is wasted: the shift you worked honestly, the brother you called, the drink you turned down. And He is the first and the last. He was there at the start of your story, and He'll be there at the end of it.

Inside and outside

22:14

Inside the gates

“They that do his commandments.” And what is His commandment? John told us: “That we should believe on the name of his Son Jesus Christ, and love one another” (1 John 3:23). Faith that's real goes on to obey.

22:15

Without

Without means outside. In those days a dog was a scavenger nobody let in the house, a picture of a shameless man. The list is like 21:8, and again it ends with lying.

This isn't the clean outside and the dirty inside. Everybody inside was once on that list. The difference is the Lamb.

Part three

Come

Verses 16–21

Jesus signs His name to the book, the Bible gives its last invitation, and the last word is grace.

I Jesus

Jesus signs the book Himself. He is David's root, the God who made David, and David's offspring, born of David's line. Jesus once asked, “If David then call him Lord, how is he his son?” (Matthew 22:45). This is the answer.

The morning star rises in the darkest part of the night, just before dawn, and when you see it, the day is certain. If it's dark in your life right now, look up. Morning is coming.

Three ways to hear 22:17

  1. If you've never comeThe invitation has your name on it: whosoever. Not after you clean up. Come thirsty, and take it. It's free because Jesus paid for it.
  2. If you came, and fell yesterdayThe water hasn't dried up and the invitation hasn't been taken back. Come back to the fountain this morning.
  3. If you're drinking from it“Let him that heareth say, Come.” You're one of the voices now. Somebody you know is thirsty.

“In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink.”

John 7:37

Don't rewrite the book

Moses said the same thing when he gave the law:

“Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it, that ye may keep the commandments of the LORD your God which I command you.”

Deuteronomy 4:2

This warning is aimed at the man who rewrites God's word: who adds his own "new revelation," or cuts out what he doesn't like. That's how false religions get started. Men who do that show they never had a real part in the book.

It is not aimed at a believer who struggles to understand Revelation. Nobody here has all of it figured out. Jesus keeps His own (John 10:28). Read it, believe it, and let it say what it says.

The last word

Malachi 4:6, the last verse of the Old Testament

And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.

Revelation 22:21, the last verse of the Bible

The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.

The Old Testament ends warning of a curse. The New Testament ends with grace, because Jesus took the curse. That's the last word God has for you: not your record, not your worst day. Grace.

And the Bible's last prayer is short enough to pray every morning: “Even so, come, Lord Jesus.” The early church had a word for it, Maranatha (1 Corinthians 16:22): our Lord, come.

Looking back over Revelation

  1. 1the risen Christ among His churches
  2. 2–3seven letters: He knows, warns and promises
  3. 4–5the throne, and the Lamb that was slain
  4. 6–16seals, trumpets and bowls: evil judged
  5. 17–18Babylon falls
  6. 19–20the King returns; the devil is cast out
  7. 21–22all things new

The Lamb wins.

Good Bible teachers differ on how the middle chapters fit together. But the book was written to small, struggling churches to keep them faithful, and what it says is plain: Christ is coming, evil is judged, God wipes away every tear.

The whole story, in one line

From a tree of life lost in Genesis to the tree of life given back in Revelation, it's one story, and it's about Jesus. You're in it, somewhere between the empty tomb and His return. Your worst chapter isn't the end of it. This is.

Take it to work today

22:4Look up

You don't have to hide your face from God. Bring Him today what you're ashamed of.

22:17Say “Come”

Name one thirsty man you know. Pray for him, then invite him: to church, to this group, to Jesus.

22:20Pray it every morning

“Even so, come, Lord Jesus.” Then go to work like a man who expects Him.

Points to remember

  1. Eden“There shall be no more curse” 22:3The tree of life, shut away after Adam's sin, is open again, because Christ took the curse.
  2. His face“They shall see his face” 22:4His servants will serve Him, see His face, carry His name, and reign for ever.
  3. Quickly“Behold, I come quickly” 22:7Jesus says it three times: He is coming soon, and His reward is with Him.
  4. Worship“See thou do it not… worship God” 22:9The angel was a fellowservant. Only God is to be worshipped.
  5. Whosoever“Whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely” 22:17The invitation is open to anyone who is thirsty, and it costs nothing.
  6. Grace“The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all” 22:21The Bible ends with a prayer for His coming and a word of grace.

Question 1 of 6

Eden

What was lost in Eden that is given back in the city?

What verses tell us about this?

“So he drove out the man; and he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life.”

Genesis 3:24

Answer

The tree of life, and life with God without the curse. Adam was driven out and the way was shut. In the city the tree of life grows by the river, there is no more curse, and God's people see His face. Christ won it back on the cross.

Question 2 of 6

His face

What will God's servants do, and what will they see?

What verses tell us about this?

“Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.”

1 John 3:2

Answer

They will serve Him, and they will see His face, with His name on their foreheads. They will reign with Him for ever, in a light that never goes out.

Question 3 of 6

Quickly

What does Jesus promise three times in this chapter?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“I come quickly” (verses 7, 12 and 20). He is coming soon and suddenly, with His reward. The waiting is His mercy, giving more men time to come.

Question 4 of 6

Worship

Why did the angel stop John from worshipping him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then saith Jesus unto him, Get thee hence, Satan: for it is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve.”

Matthew 4:10

Answer

Because the angel was only a fellowservant, like John and the prophets. Worship belongs to God alone, and heaven gives it to God and to the Lamb. No angel, preacher or friend should take His place.

Question 5 of 6

Whosoever

Who is invited to take the water of life?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

Whosoever will: anyone who is thirsty and willing to come. It's free. The Spirit and the bride invite him, and everyone who has heard is to pass the invitation on.

Question 6 of 6

Grace

What are the last words of the Bible?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Jesus' promise, “Surely I come quickly,” John's prayer, “Even so, come, Lord Jesus,” and a blessing: “The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.” The Bible ends with grace.
