For three chapters Paul told us what God has done. He didn't give one command. Now the letter turns on a single word: “I therefore…”

That's the order of the Christian life. First God saves you, by grace. Then you live like it. We don't walk right to get saved. We walk right because we are.

Part one

Walk worthy, together

Verses 1–6

A life that matches the call, and a peace worth keeping.

Worthy

Worthy is a word from the scales: to weigh as much as. God called you to be His son. Now let the way you walk weigh the same as that call.

Not to earn the call. You already have it. Vocation just means calling. Paul is asking a man to live up to what he already is.

And notice who's asking: “the prisoner of the Lord.” Paul is in chains, and he's still walking worthy. A hard place is no excuse to walk small.

What a worthy walk looks like

  1. Lowlinessnot thinking you're bigger than you are
  2. Meeknessstrength under control, like a strong horse that takes the bridle
  3. Longsufferinga long fuse
  4. Forbearing one anotherputting up with each other, in love

Notice what's missing: talent, money, a clean record. Every one of these is about how you treat the man next to you. Some of us came up where a short fuse kept you alive. In Christ, a long one is the strong thing.

“Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit.” You don't make the unity. The Spirit already made it. Your job is to keep it: don't be the man who breaks the peace at the meeting or the table.

Seven “ones”

The Spirit

one bodyone Spiritone hope

The Lord

one Lordone faithone baptism

The Father

one God and Father of all

This is what you share with every true believer on earth, whatever his color, his church, or his record. Father, Son and Spirit, one God, are at the center of it.

One baptism: many Bible teachers take it as the believer's baptism in water; others as the Spirit placing every believer into the one body (1 Corinthians 12:13). Either way, it's one. Baptism doesn't save you. It's a believer's first step of obedience, a picture of being buried and raised with Christ.

Part two

Gifts to grow up

Verses 7–16

The risen Christ hands out gifts, so His people can stop being children.

Down, then up

Paul borrows a picture from Psalm 68: a king coming home from war, leading his captives in a parade and handing out gifts.

“Thou hast ascended on high, thou hast led captivity captive: thou hast received gifts for men; yea, for the rebellious also, that the LORD God might dwell among them.”

Psalm 68:18

Before Christ went up, He came all the way down: to earth, to the cross, to the grave. (Teachers differ on the details of verse 9.) He went lower than you ever fell, and came back with gifts, “for the rebellious also.”

Who does the work?

  1. Christ givesapostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers
  2. They equip“for the perfecting of the saints”
  3. The saints serve“for the work of the ministry”
  4. The body grows“the edifying of the body of Christ”

Perfecting is the word for mending nets or setting a broken bone: getting something ready to work. The pastor isn't hired to do the ministry for you. He's there to get you ready to do it. (The apostles and prophets laid the foundation in the first days, 2:20; pastors and teachers keep building on it.)

The goal is “a perfect man”: a full-grown man, measured by Christ Himself. Nobody gets there alone, and nobody gets left out of the work. You have a part.

Tossed, or grown

A child believes whatever the last man told him. Every new teacher, every video, every slick voice blows him somewhere else.

Sleight is the word for playing with dice: loaded dice. Some of us know con men. There are con men with Bibles too. The answer isn't to trust nobody. It's to grow up, rooted in the Word with the church, until the wind doesn't move you.

Speaking the truth in love

  • truth without lovea club: it beats a man down and walks off
  • love without truthenabling: it leaves him in the ditch and calls it kindness
  • the truth in lovesays the hard thing, and stays to help

Then verse 16: the body grows “by that which every joint supplieth.” A joint is small, but without it the arm doesn't work. Your phone call to a brother who's slipping, the hard word you say gently: that's you doing your part.

Part three

Old clothes, new clothes

Verses 17–24

Where the old life leads, and how a man changes.

The way down

  1. vanityan empty, aimless mind
  2. darkenedcan't see straight
  3. alienatedcut off from the life of God
  4. blindnessa heart gone hard, like a callus
  5. past feelingnumb
  6. greedinessalways more, never enough

Ephesus was a city of idols and magic (Acts 19:19). But this is also the story of every addiction: the heart grows a callus, the conscience goes numb, and then it takes more and more to feel anything at all.

If you know these stairs, you're not shamed here. You're being shown where they lead, by a God who came down them to get you. “But ye have not so learned Christ.”

Change of clothes

Conversation is the old word for your whole way of life. The old man is who you were before Christ: rotten, run by “deceitful lusts”, wants that lie to you.

Put off the old man, like a filthy coat you're done wearing.

Be renewed in the spirit of your mind. The change starts on the inside, and it keeps going, day by day, as you fill your mind with God's Word.

Put on the new man, “created in righteousness and true holiness.” God made it. You put it on, every morning.

Already, and every day

Done at the cross“our old man is crucified with him”
→
So, every dayquit wearing the dead man's clothes

“Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin.”

Romans 6:6

The old man has no claim on you. But the old coat still hangs in the closet, and it fits easy. When you reach for it, and you will, don't hate yourself. Take it off again, and put on the new one. That's the Christian life: a daily change of clothes.

Part four

What it looks like on Monday

Verses 25–32

Your mouth, your temper, your hands, and your heart.

No more lies

The reason“we are members one of another”
→
So“speak every man truth with his neighbour”

Every addiction runs on lies: where you were, what you spent, how much you had. Paul's reason to quit isn't a rule. It's that we're one body. If your eye lied to your hand, you'd get burned.

Today, tell one person the plain truth about something you'd usually shade. That's how a man starts to be trusted again.

Angry, and not sinning

Anger itself isn't sin. Jesus was angry. But anger is the fastest road back to the old man, and some of us have done things in anger we can't take back.

So put a clock on it: “let not the sun go down upon your wrath.” Settle it, pray it through, walk away, make it right. Don't sleep on it and let it set.

“Neither give place to the devil.” Place means a foothold, a room. Anger you keep overnight is a room you rent out to the devil.

The thief turned giver

his neighbour→my pocket“Let him that stole steal no more”
honest work→my pocket“let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good”
my pocket→him that needeth“that he may have to give”

Watch the money turn around. The man who took becomes the man who gives. That's not just quitting a sin. That's the new man. Paul lived it: “these hands have ministered unto my necessities” (Acts 20:34), he told the Ephesian elders.

If you're stocking shelves, swinging a hammer, washing dishes: that's not a step down from the hustle. It's holy work. Then give a little, even a few dollars, to somebody worse off. The giving is the point.

Words, and the Spirit

Corrupt means rotten, like spoiled fruit. Jail talk, street talk, the joke that tears a man down. Instead: words that “minister grace unto the hearers”, that build up.

The Holy Spirit is a Person. Only a person can be grieved. Our rotten words and ways hurt Him, because He lives in us.

But look: grieved, not gone. You “are sealed unto the day of redemption.” A seal says this belongs to me, and nobody breaks it till it gets where it's going.

Put away, put on

  • bitternessthe old grudge you keep chewing
  • wrath and angerthe blow-up, and the slow burn
  • clamourshouting, the scene
  • evil speaking and malicerunning a man down, wanting him to hurt
  • kind, tenderhearted, forgiving“even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you”

These are the last pockets of the old coat. Tenderhearted is the opposite of the callused heart back in verse 18: a heart that can feel again. That's what the Spirit is growing in you.

You won't scrub these out with willpower. You put them away, again and again, and you put on kindness, at home, at work, today.

Forgiven much

Talents I owed my lord

10,000

In Jesus' story, more than a man could earn in thousands of lifetimes. Forgiven, all of it (Matthew 18:24–27).

Pence my brother owes me

100

A few months' wages. Real money, and a real hurt. But small next to what I was forgiven.

“Shouldest not thou also have had compassion on thy fellowservant, even as I had pity on thee?”

Matthew 18:33

Forgiving isn't saying it didn't matter, and it doesn't mean trusting a man who isn't safe. It means you tear up the bill. Name one person you're still holding a bill on, and give it to God today.

Points to remember

  1. Worthy“Walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called” 4:1Live up to the call you already have: lowly, meek, patient, putting up with each other.
  2. One“One Lord, one faith, one baptism” 4:5Every believer shares one body, one Spirit, one Lord, one Father. Keep the peace.
  3. Grow up“For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry” 4:12Leaders equip; every believer serves; the body grows up into Christ.
  4. Old and new“Put off… the old man… put on the new man” 4:22–24Take off the old life like old clothes, be renewed inside, put on the new.
  5. Anger“Let not the sun go down upon your wrath” 4:26Deal with it before sundown, and give the devil no room.
  6. Forgiven“Even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you” 4:32We forgive because we have been forgiven so much more.

Question 1 of 6

Worthy

What does it look like to walk worthy of God's call?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A life that weighs the same as the call. Not proving yourself, but living like what you already are: humble, gentle, slow to blow up, putting up with each other in love, and keeping the peace the Spirit made.

Question 2 of 6

One

What do all believers share?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit.”

1 Corinthians 12:13

Answer

One body, one Spirit, one hope, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all. Whatever our past or background, every believer belongs to the same family, under the same Lord.

Question 3 of 6

Grow up

Why did Christ give pastors and teachers to the church?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To equip the believers to do the work of the ministry, so the whole body is built up. The goal is grown men, steady in the faith, not children blown around by every new teaching.

Question 4 of 6

Old and new

What does a believer take off, and what does he put on?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

He takes off the old man, the old way of life run by lies and lusts. He is renewed in his mind, and puts on the new man God created, in righteousness and true holiness. He does it again every day.

Question 5 of 6

Anger

How should a believer handle his anger?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.”

James 1:19–20

Answer

Being angry is not always sin, but he must not let it turn into sin. He deals with it the same day, before the sun goes down, so the devil gets no foothold. Slow to wrath, quick to make it right.

Question 6 of 6

Forgiven

Why should we forgive one another?

What verses tell us about this?

“Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel against any: even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye.”

Colossians 3:13

Answer

Because God, for Christ's sake, has forgiven us far more than anyone will ever owe us. A forgiven man forgives. It doesn't erase the hurt, but it tears up the bill.
