Honest before God

Part one

Kept back

Verses 1–11

A husband and wife sell some land, give part of the money, and let everyone think it was all of it. This is one of the hard passages, so we'll take it slowly and honestly.

Where this comes from

The church in Jerusalem was brand new and full of love. Some of the believers were selling land and houses so that poor brothers and sisters would have enough. Nobody ordered it. It was a gift. Just before this, a man named Barnabas did exactly that, and people noticed.

And Joses, who by the apostles was surnamed Barnabas, (which is, being interpreted, The son of consolation,) a Levite, and of the country of Cyprus, Having land, sold it, and brought the money, and laid it at the apostles’ feet.

Acts 4:36–37

Ananias and Sapphira saw the respect a gift like that earned. They wanted the same name for themselves. And that is the sin here. It was not keeping some of their money. It was acting out a bigger gift than they gave.

The price tag

What they let everyone believe
“All of it, for the Lord's people”
What was true
brought and laid at the apostles' feet
kept back

Peter will say in a moment that the land was theirs, and so was the money (verse 4). They could have given a tenth, half, or nothing, and been honest about it. The only wrong move left was the one they made: “part of the price” laid down as if it were the whole.

It was never about the money

  • “You had to sell the land”no, “was it not thine own?”
  • “You had to give all of it”no, “in thine own power”
  • “Keeping some was the sin”no, the lie was
  • “Thou hast not lied unto men, but unto God”the real trouble

Notice how Peter lines up two names for the same person: “the Holy Ghost” in verse 3, “God” in verse 4. The Holy Spirit is God. They tried to fool the apostles, but the One they were really lying to saw all of it.

Peter says Satan filled Ananias's heart. Then in the same breath he asks, “Why hast thou conceived this thing in thine heart?” The tempting came from outside. The choice was Ananias's own. That's true of every one of our falls too: we can't blame it all on the devil, or on anyone else.

One question, one open door

About three hours later, Sapphira walked in with no idea what had happened to her husband. Peter doesn't spring a trap. He asks her a plain question and leaves the door open.

Peter, 5:8

Tell me whether ye sold the land for so much?

✕

Sapphira, 5:8

Yea, for so much.

She had one sentence to turn it around: “No. We kept back some.” It would have cost her pride and nothing else. Instead she held to the story. That's how a small lie becomes a big one: every time we're asked, we get one more chance to tell the truth, and one more chance to double down.

A hard text, told straight

Nobody likes this passage, and we shouldn't pretend to. Here's what it says and what it doesn't.

What it says

God took the lie seriously

Two people lied to the church about their gift. God dealt with it at once, in front of everyone. “Great fear”, deep awe, fell on the whole church.

What it doesn't say

That God hunts down men who slip

It doesn't say they lost their salvation. It never happens again to a believer in Acts. When Simon the sorcerer sins in chapter 8, he is told to repent and pray, and he is not struck down.

Why so severe, here, at the start? The church was just being born, and God guarded its first steps, much as He did with Achan at Jericho (Joshua 7) and Nadab and Abihu at the tabernacle (Leviticus 10). In each case the sin was done in the open, after plain warning, by people who knew better.

You and I have told lies bigger than theirs and are still breathing. That's God's patience, not a sign that lies are small. And a believer is held by a hand that does not let go:

And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.

John 10:28

Peter knew this road

Peter had lied too. On the night Jesus was arrested, scared of a servant girl, he said three times that he didn't know Him. And then:

And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. And Peter went out, and wept bitterly.

Luke 22:61–62

The difference between Peter and Ananias wasn't that Peter's lie was smaller. Peter went out and wept, and came back. Jesus was waiting on the beach with breakfast. Ananias had the same Lord, the same chance, and kept the mask on.

What honest looks like today

“I'm fine. Doing great.” → “I kept some back.” The true number. Said to one safe man: your sponsor, your pastor, someone in this group.

He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.

Proverbs 28:13

Today, tell one true thing to one safe person. Not the whole life story, just the number you've been rounding up. And if it's already a mess, the promise still stands:

If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

1 John 1:9

Part two

Open doors

Verses 12–26

The church keeps growing, the high priest keeps getting angrier, and a locked prison turns out to be no problem for God.

From the towns round about

Word got out. Sick people came in from “the cities round about” and were laid in the streets on beds and couches, the beds of the rich and the pallets of the poor, along the way where the apostles walked. All of them were healed.

About Peter's shadow: read it carefully. The verse says they hoped it might fall on some of them. It doesn't say the shadow did anything. The power was God's, never Peter's, and never in a shadow or a charm. A few chapters later, Simon the sorcerer will want to buy that power. He can't.

They gathered in Solomon's porch, the long covered walk of columns on the east side of the temple courts. Open, public, and free to anyone who wanted to come.

Why the Sadducees were angry

The high priest's party were Sadducees, the wealthy temple families. They held the real power, and they said there is no resurrection and no angels. Now the apostles were announcing that a man they had killed was alive.

The apostles say“He is risen.”
→
The Sadducees feeltheir whole teaching is on trial.

The word is indignation: hot anger. Notice what it was really made of. Jealousy and fear of losing their place. When a man is angry at the truth, it often means the truth has found something.

And here is the joke God plays next: He sends an angel to open the prison doors of men who did not believe in angels.

One night, three scenes

5:18Locked in

The apostles are in the common prison, the public jail. Behind a locked door, with guards outside.

5:19The doors open

An angel of the Lord opens them by night, leads them out, and says: “Go, stand and speak in the temple.”

5:21Back to work

At first light they walk straight into the temple, the most public place in the city, and start teaching.

Look at what the angel says: “all the words of this life.” Not a lecture on prison breaks. Not a warning to hide. He sends them to tell people about new life. God opened the door so they could go back to the work He'd given them. No man in a cell is out of reach of Him.

The empty cell

The officersThe prison truly found we shut with all safety, and the keepers standing without before the doors: but when we had opened, we found no man within.

The high priest and his menThey doubted of them whereunto this would grow.

Someone, running inBehold, the men whom ye put in prison are standing in the temple, and teaching the people.

It's almost funny. The locks held, the guards stood watch, and the prisoners were gone. Then the news that the men are back at work. And the captain brings them in “without violence”, not because he was gentle, but because “they feared the people, lest they should have been stoned.”

What God is doing is bigger than the council can control. The men who had power were afraid of the crowd; the men in custody were not afraid of anyone.

Part three

Counted worthy

Verses 27–42

Back before the council. Peter answers plainly, a wise old teacher speaks up, and the apostles are beaten and sent home glad.

“We ought to obey God rather than men”

“We ought to obey God rather than men.”

Same Peter, same council, same sort of questions that sent him running in the courtyard. Now he doesn't flinch. That's what the Holy Spirit does in a man's life. Not a new personality, but courage he didn't have before.

Be careful how you use this verse. It was said about being ordered to stop talking about Jesus. It is not a pass to ignore your probation officer, your boss, or the law. Those men are due respect too, and most days God is working through them. What the council could not do was command silence about the gospel.

The gospel, in Peter’s words

Peter doesn't argue. He tells them what happened. “Whom ye slew and hanged on a tree” is what the council did. Then the story turns on one word: God.

  1. Raised upGod raised Jesus from the dead (verse 30)
  2. Exaltedby His right hand, “a Prince and a Saviour” (verse 31)
  3. Repentanceturning to God, and He gives it (verse 31)
  4. Forgivenessof sins, to those who turn (verse 31)

Think of who he's talking to: the very men who had Jesus killed. And Peter still offers them forgiveness of sins. No sin is too big for it.

The Spirit is given “to them that obey him”: those who answer the gospel with faith. Just ahead, in chapter 6, many priests become “obedient to the faith” (6:7). When a man believes in Jesus, the Spirit comes to live in him from that day.

Gamaliel's two examples

The council's answer to Peter was rage: “cut to the heart, and took counsel to slay them.” Then Gamaliel stood up, a Pharisee and a respected teacher of the law. He asked the apostles to step out, and reminded the council of what happens to movements that aren't from God.

  1. TheudasBoasted he was somebody. About 400 men joined him. He was killed, and his followers scattered.
  2. Judas of GalileeRose up at the census, about AD 6. He perished too, and his followers were dispersed.
  3. Jesus' menTheir Leader was killed. But they haven't scattered: thousands more believe each day.

We don't know for sure which Theudas he meant. There were many such men around then. His point stands anyway: movements built on one man's ego usually die with the man.

Of men, or of God?

If it is of menthis counsel or this work…
→
thenit will come to nought.
But if it is of Godthis counsel or this work…
→
thenye cannot overthrow it.

It was good advice, and it saved their lives that day. Years later Gamaliel's pupil Saul would say where he'd learned the law:

I am verily a man which am a Jew, born in Tarsus, a city in Cilicia, yet brought up in this city at the feet of Gamaliel, and taught according to the perfect manner of the law of the fathers, and was zealous toward God, as ye all are this day.

Acts 22:3

But notice what Gamaliel never said: that Jesus is Lord. He said wait. Waiting to see how it turns out is a fine thing to do for a few days. It is no place to live. Someday, the time to decide arrives, and it is today.

Told one thing, did another

What the council ordered

“Not speak in the name of Jesus”

They had the apostles beaten, probably with the lash the synagogue courts used, then commanded them to be quiet and let them go.

What the apostles did

“Ceased not to teach and preach”

Every day, “in the temple, and in every house”: in the public square and in people's living rooms.

Daily. In the temple and in homes. No big plan, just faithful, ordinary days, one conversation at a time. That's a plan any of us can follow.

Glad to be counted

“Counted worthy to suffer shame for his name.”

They had just been beaten, and they were glad. They were not glad about the pain. They were glad that Jesus' name was worth it. Jesus had said this would come:

Blessed are ye, when men shall revile you, and persecute you, and shall say all manner of evil against you falsely, for my sake. Rejoice, and be exceeding glad: for great is your reward in heaven: for so persecuted they the prophets which were before you.

Matthew 5:11–12

An honest word: suffering for Jesus and suffering for our own mistakes are different things. If a man loses a job because of his record, that is a consequence, not persecution, and God still meets him there. But if friends mock you for staying sober or for going to church, then you are in good company, and you can hold your head up.

Points to remember

  1. Honesty“Thou hast not lied unto men, but unto God” 5:4The mask doesn't fool God. Tell the true number to Him and to one safe man.
  2. Fear“Great fear came upon all the church” 5:11Deep respect for a holy God. A believer is still held in His hand.
  3. Rescue“The angel of the Lord by night opened the prison doors” 5:19No locked door is beyond Him.
  4. Obedience“We ought to obey God rather than men” 5:29Peter, who once ran, now stands. The Spirit gives courage.
  5. Counsel“If it be of God, ye cannot overthrow it” 5:39Good advice, but waiting is not believing. Decide today.
  6. Joy“Rejoicing that they were counted worthy” 5:41Jesus' name is worth it, even when it costs us.

Question 1 of 6

Honesty

Whom did Ananias really lie to?

What verses tell us about this?

He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.

Proverbs 28:13

Answer

God. Peter says it twice: he lied to the Holy Ghost, and he lied to God. The money was his own to keep or give. The pretending was the sin. Honest confession is the way back, and God stands ready for it.

Question 2 of 6

Fear

What came on the church after Ananias and Sapphira died?

What verses tell us about this?

There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.

Romans 8:1

Answer

Great fear: deep awe and respect for a holy God. It didn't drive the believers away. It was a lesson in how seriously God takes honesty, and, for the believer, a God who never condemns those who are in Christ.

Question 3 of 6

Rescue

How did the apostles get out of prison?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

An angel of the Lord opened the prison doors at night and sent them back to the temple to teach. By morning the cell was empty and they were preaching again. No lock can keep God from His people.

Question 4 of 6

Obedience

What did Peter say when the council told them to stop?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“We ought to obey God rather than men.” When people ordered them to stop speaking about Jesus, they kept on. This is the same Peter who ran from a servant girl. The Spirit made the difference.

Question 5 of 6

Counsel

What did Gamaliel tell the council to do?

What verses tell us about this?

There is no wisdom nor understanding nor counsel against the LORD.

Proverbs 21:30

Answer

Leave them alone. If the work was of men, it would fail on its own; if it was of God, nobody could stop it, and they might be found fighting against God. Good advice, but he never said Jesus is Lord. Waiting is not believing.

Question 6 of 6

Joy

Why did the apostles go away rejoicing after they were beaten?

What verses tell us about this?

Blessed are ye, when men shall revile you, and persecute you, and shall say all manner of evil against you falsely, for my sake. Rejoice, and be exceeding glad: for great is your reward in heaven: for so persecuted they the prophets which were before you.

Matthew 5:11–12

Answer

Because they were counted worthy to suffer shame for Jesus' name. They didn't enjoy the beating. They were glad the name was worth it. And then they went right back to teaching daily, in the temple and in every house.
