Peace, and enough

Where we are in Philippians

About AD 60 to 62, Paul writes from Rome, under guard in a rented house, to the church he started in Philippi (Acts 16). This is the last chapter. It's a thank-you, a few quarrels to settle, and the best advice a worried man ever got.

1Joy in chainsPaul's in prison, and the gospel spreads anyway
2The mind of ChristHumble like Jesus, and light in a dark world
3Gain and loss, and the raceEverything counted loss for Christ; press on
4Peace, and enoughPray, think well, be content

Read alone, this chapter still makes sense: some advice for friends who are worried, a thank-you for a gift, and a man in chains who says he has enough.

Part one

Stand fast together

Verses 1–5

Two friends at odds, and joy that doesn't wait for good news.

Two women, one quarrel

We don't know what the quarrel was, and that's the point: it's in a letter read out to the whole church. Paul doesn't take a side. He asks both women, by name, “that they be of the same mind in the Lord.” They had “laboured with me in the gospel.” Good people, real work, and a wall.

Paul asks a trusted friend, a “true yokefellow,” to help them settle it. Nobody is told to fight it out alone. And notice what stands under both of them: their names are “in the book of life.” The quarrel can't undo that. If there's a brother you've gone quiet on, today is a good day to make the first call.

Joy, from a man in chains

Paul says “Rejoice” twice in a row, and he's chained to a soldier. How often does the word turn up in this short letter?

CH. 2

About eighteen times. And look at the little word: “in the Lord.” This isn't “be happy about your circumstances.” He had none to be happy about. It's joy in Who you belong to, and that doesn't change when the news does. Joy is also not a mood you're failing at. It's something to turn toward, and on a bad day it begins with one honest thank-you.

Verse 5 goes with it. “Moderation” means gentleness, a reasonable and fair spirit that doesn't have to win every argument. “The Lord is at hand”: He's near, and He is coming. That's why a man can let go of his temper.

Part two

Anxiety and peace

Verses 6–9

What to do with the worry that comes at three in the morning.

Instead of carrying it

“Careful” is old English for full of care: anxious. Paul doesn't scold. He gives four moves to make with a worry:

  1. 1. Name it“let your requests be made known”say the real thing, out loud, in words
  2. 2. Pray and ask“by prayer and supplication”talk to Him, and ask for what you need
  3. 3. Say thanks“with thanksgiving”for something He has already done
  4. 4. Let go“the peace of God… shall keep”and let Him stand guard

“Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.”

1 Peter 5:7

Today, name one worry, say it to God, and tell one brother. A worry said aloud gets lighter. And if anxiety is heavy and constant, getting help from a doctor or counselor is no failure of faith. It's often how God answers.

A sentry at the door of your heart

Paul's word for “keep” is a soldier's word: to stand guard, like a garrison. The Philippians lived in a Roman garrison town. They knew what a sentry at the gate was for.

“Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee.”

Isaiah 26:3

This peace “passeth all understanding”: it doesn't need your situation to make sense first. The worries still fly at you. They just can't get past the One on guard.

What you put on your plate

Eight kinds of food for the mind (4:8). That's the diet Paul prescribes:

  1. TrueReal, not spin or a story you tell yourself
  2. HonestHonourable, worth respecting
  3. JustRight and fair
  4. PureClean, nothing mixed in
  5. LovelyWorth loving, good to look at
  6. Good reportWell spoken of, with a good name
  7. VirtueExcellence, the kind that takes effort
  8. PraiseWorth praising God for

“Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life.”

Proverbs 4:23

“Think on” means weigh it, turn it over, keep it in view. A mind runs on what it's fed. For a man out of a hard life, what comes through the eyes (the screen, the playlist, the old crowd's group chat) either feeds the old appetite or starves it. Pick one thing to take off your plate today, and one thing to put on it.

Then verse 9: “do.” Think, then act on it. And the promise grows: not only the peace of God, but “the God of peace shall be with you.”

Part three

Learning to be content

Verses 10–13

A man in chains says he has learned the secret. He's not saying it came easy.

The line that doesn't move

Paul has been low and high, hungry and full, and he lists them all (verse 12). The wavy line is his circumstances. The straight one is what he “learned.” Notice the word: it was learned, not born in him. An old man in chains is still learning. So can you.

He's not saying he doesn't feel hunger or fear. He's saying it doesn't run the show. The man whose peace goes up and down with the bank balance is never at rest. The man whose peace is in Christ is free in both directions.

What “I can do all things” means

  • “I can win anything I set my heart on”that's not what he wrote, or where
  • “I can have anything I want”the man writing this was in chains
  • “I can do it alone, if I try harder”“through Christ which strengtheneth me”
  • I can face whatever I get, hungry or fullHis strength is enough for both

Read verse 13 with verses 11 and 12 right in front of it. “All things” means all the situations Paul just listed: being broke and being comfortable, both of which can wreck a man. It's a verse for the hard day and for the easy one.

“But godliness with contentment is great gain. For we brought nothing into this world, and it is certain we can carry nothing out. And having food and raiment let us be therewith content.”

1 Timothy 6:6–8

If you've lived chasing more, there's a rest in this. Today, enough is enough: a meal, a roof, a day clean, a brother who answered the phone. Say thank you for the enough you've got, then ask Christ for the strength to do the next right thing.

Part four

The gift, and the goodbye

Verses 14–23

A thank-you note, a promise, and a few names from the last page.

A gift carried across the sea

The Philippians were poor, and they were Paul's most faithful friends. They'd sent help to him in Thessalonica, and now they sent it all the way to Rome, carried by their man Epaphroditus (verse 18).

Paul thanks them warmly, but he adds a careful line: “Not because I desire a gift: but I desire fruit that may abound to your account.” He's not fishing. He sees their giving as something that's being added to their account with God.

He calls the gift “an odour of a sweet smell, a sacrifice acceptable, wellpleasing to God.” A parcel of food and clothes, handed over in love, counts as worship.

“All your need”: not all your wants

  • “give, and God will make you rich”Paul never says it, and he wrote it from prison
  • “all your wants”the word is need
  • “more, so you can finally feel safe”that's the chase verse 11 ended
  • “all your need”, from His riches, in Christenough, supplied by Someone who is not running out

The promise was made to people who had just given away money they barely had. Their own region of Macedonia is described this way:

“How that in a great trial of affliction the abundance of their joy and their deep poverty abounded unto the riches of their liberality.”

2 Corinthians 8:2

God does not promise a bigger pile. He promises that, in Christ, you won't be left without what you need. And the measure is “his riches in glory,” not your bank balance. If you're worried about rent or work today, put that need in plain words in your prayer, and tell a brother who can help.

Even in Caesar's house.

Paul's last greeting: “All the saints salute you, chiefly they that are of Caesar's household.” These weren't the emperor's family. They were his staff and slaves, the people who kept the emperor's house running (Nero was emperor). Somehow, through a prisoner chained in a rented house, the gospel had walked right in.

Within a few years, that same emperor would turn on the Christians (about AD 64), and these believers knew the risk. They stayed. If you have to work today in a place that feels like Caesar's house, a crew with a language you've left behind, a boss with no use for Jesus, you aren't the first believer there. Salute every saint. There are probably more of them than you think.

And the letter ends where it began, with grace: “The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all.”

Looking back over Philippians

The whole letter, in four stops, written by a man who had every reason to give up:

1Joy in chainsPaul's in prison, and the gospel spreads anyway
2The mind of ChristHumble like Jesus, and light in a dark world
3Gain and loss, and the raceEverything counted loss for Christ; press on
4Peace, and enoughPray, think well, be content

A single thread runs through all four: in Christ. His joy in chains, his humble mind, his lost résumé and his enough are all in Christ, and none of it depends on circumstances. The church in Philippi was small and poor and worried, and that's exactly who the letter was for.

If you fell yesterday, you are who the letter was written to. Paul was in chains, and he wrote about joy. Take one thing from it today: name a worry to God, and one to a brother.

Points to remember

  1. Unity“I beseech Euodias, and beseech Syntyche, that they be of the same mind in the Lord” 4:2Two good people, one wall. Make the first call to the brother you've gone quiet on.
  2. Joy“Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, Rejoice” 4:4Joy in Who you belong to, not in how the day goes. Paul wrote this in chains.
  3. Anxiety“Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God” 4:6Name it, pray it, thank Him, and tell a brother.
  4. Peace“the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus” 4:7His peace stands guard like a sentry, even when nothing makes sense yet.
  5. Mind“whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure, whatsoever things are lovely, whatsoever things are of good report” 4:8A mind runs on what it's fed. Take one thing off the plate, put one on.
  6. Enough“I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content” 4:11Content was learned, in both want and plenty, and God supplies every need (4:19), not every want.

Question 1 of 6

Unity

Who are Euodias and Syntyche, and what does Paul ask of them?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.”

Matthew 5:23–24

Answer

Two women of the church in Philippi who had worked beside Paul and were at odds. He asks both of them, by name, to be of the same mind in the Lord, and he asks a friend to help. Make the first move toward whoever you've gone quiet on.

Question 2 of 6

Joy

What does Paul tell us to do alway, and how can he say it from a prison?

What verses tell us about this?

“Rejoice evermore. Pray without ceasing. In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.”

1 Thessalonians 5:16–18

Answer

Rejoice in the Lord, always. It's joy in Who you belong to, so it doesn't depend on the day. Paul wrote it chained to a soldier.

Question 3 of 6

Anxiety

What does Paul say to do instead of worrying?

What verses tell us about this?

“Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.”

1 Peter 5:7

“Take therefore no thought for the morrow: for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof.”

Matthew 6:34

Answer

Pray about everything: ask, give thanks, and make your requests known to God. Don't carry it alone. Name the worry, say it to Him, and tell a brother.

Question 4 of 6

Peace

What does the peace of God do for our hearts and minds?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee.”

Isaiah 26:3

“Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid.”

John 14:27

Answer

It keeps them, which is a soldier's word for standing guard. It doesn't need your situation to make sense first. The worries still come, but the One on guard stays at the door.

Question 5 of 6

Mind

What should we think on?

What verses tell us about this?

“Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life.”

Proverbs 4:23

“And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.”

Romans 12:2

Answer

Whatsoever things are true, honest, just, pure, lovely, of good report, anything of virtue or praise. A mind runs on what it's fed. Take one thing off your plate today and put one thing on.

Question 6 of 6

Enough

What has Paul learned about being content, and who supplies our need?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Answer

He learned, in hunger and in plenty, to be content, because Christ gives strength for both. And God supplies all our need, not all our wants, out of His riches in glory by Christ Jesus.
