Part one

Two and two before his face

Verses 1–16

Seventy ordinary men, sent ahead into towns that might take them in or throw them out.

On the road to Jerusalem

“And it came to pass, when the time was come that he should be received up, he stedfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem,”

Luke 9:51

From 9:51 to 19:28, Luke follows Jesus on His last journey toward Jerusalem and the cross, about AD 29–30. He doesn't trace the road day by day. He shows us what Jesus taught along the way.

Jesus knew where the road ended. He sent men ahead anyway, to every town “whither he himself would come.”

Other seventy, two and two

the twelve went out in 9:1–6now seventy more, thirty-five pairs

They weren't apostles or famous names. Luke never even tells us who they were. Ordinary men, sent with the same message.

And nobody went alone. Two and two: someone to pray with, someone to keep you honest. Every man in recovery knows why that matters.

Before He sent them, He told them to pray. Not for an easy road, but for more workers: “pray ye therefore the Lord of the harvest.” The need is bigger than any one man.

Travel light

  • neither purseno money bag
  • nor scripno traveller's food bag
  • nor shoeslikely no spare pair of sandals
  • salute no man by the wayno long roadside greetings; keep moving
  • “Peace be to this house”what they did carry

Greetings on the road could take a long time, with family news and blessings back and forth. Jesus wasn't teaching rudeness. The job was urgent. They were to trust God for food and a bed, take what they were given, and not shop around for a better house.

Either way, the same news

A town that receives them

“Heal the sick”

“The kingdom of God is come nigh unto you.” (v. 9)

A town that refuses them

Wipe off the dust

“Notwithstanding be ye sure of this, that the kingdom of God is come nigh unto you.” (v. 11)

Wiping off the dust was a public sign: we told you, and the choice is yours. Whether a town took them in or threw them out, the message didn't change. The King had come close.

Sodom was the wicked city God destroyed with fire (Genesis 19). Jesus says a town that hears Him and refuses will be worse off on judgment day than Sodom. The more light you're given, the more you're responsible for.

Three towns, a few miles apart

Chorazin, Bethsaida and Capernaum, Jesus' home base, sat on the north shore of the lake. No towns saw more of His miracles.

Tyre and Sidon were heathen ports up the coast. Had they seen the same, they'd have repented in sackcloth and ashes, the rough clothes and dust of mourning.

Today all three towns are ruins. Seeing a lot of Jesus isn't the same as turning to Him. What we do with what we've heard is what counts.

Part two

Your names are written in heaven

Verses 17–24

The men come back thrilled, and Jesus gives them something better to be glad about.

I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven.

While seventy plain men went town to town in Jesus' name, Jesus saw what was happening behind it: the devil losing ground, fast and for real, like a flash out of the sky.

He gave them power over serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, for the work He sent them to do. It isn't a dare to handle snakes. It's a promise that the enemy could not stop the work.

The cross would finish what started here. The devil is a defeated enemy. He still fights, but he fights a losing war.

Something better to be glad about

Power over devils was what they did, through His name. A name written in heaven is what He did for them. One depends on how the day went. The other doesn't change.

Good days in ministry come and go. Good days in recovery come and go. Your name in heaven stays put, and no man can pluck you out of His hand (John 10:28).

Hidden from the wise, shown to babes

This is one of the few times the gospels tell us Jesus rejoiced, and it was over this:

“The wise and prudent”

Hid

Men sure they already know. They don't think they need teaching.

“Babes”

Revealed

Men who know they don't know, and come to be taught.

It isn't about schooling or brains. A proud man shuts the door on himself. A humble man finds it open. And the Son who reveals the Father invites every one of us:

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

“These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off, and were persuaded of them, and embraced them, and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.”

Hebrews 11:13

We have even more than the disciples had that day: the whole story, the cross, and the empty tomb, written down for us.

Part three

Who is my neighbour?

Verses 25–37

An expert asks a test question, and Jesus answers with a story about a robbers' road.

A test question

The lawyerMaster, what shall I do to inherit eternal life?

JesusWhat is written in the law? how readest thou?

The lawyerThou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart … and thy neighbour as thyself.
Deuteronomy 6:5 and Leviticus 19:18

JesusThou hast answered right: this do, and thou shalt live.

A lawyer here is an expert in the Law of Moses, not a courtroom man. He tempted Jesus: he was testing Him, and he wasn't asking because he felt lost.

The right answer, and the problem

He got the answer right. The trouble is doing it: all your heart, all your soul, all your strength, all your mind, every day, and loving everyone the way you love yourself. Nobody has, not once.

“Therefore by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight: for by the law is the knowledge of sin.”

Romans 3:20

The law is a mirror. It shows us the dirt; it can't wash it off. Eternal life isn't earned. It's a gift, received by faith in Jesus, the only One who ever kept that law all the way.

Down to Jericho

The lawyer wanted a line: who counts as my neighbour, and who doesn't? “Willing to justify himself,” he was looking for a way out.

Jesus answered with a road everyone knew. From Jerusalem down to Jericho is about 17 miles of steep, empty desert, and it drops about 3,300 feet. Rocks, ravines and caves made it a good place for robbers.

A priest served at the temple altar. A Levite, from the same tribe, helped at the temple. Both were coming down from Jerusalem, likely heading home after their time of service.

The one who stopped

The priest saw him and crossed to the other side. Luke doesn't say why. Maybe fear of the robbers, maybe fear that touching a dead man would make him unclean (Numbers 19:16), maybe just a busy day.

The Levite went closer, looked, and kept walking too.

Then a Samaritan. Jews and Samaritans had hated each other for centuries. Just a chapter ago, a Samaritan village refused Jesus, and James and John wanted to call down fire on it (9:52–54). Now Jesus makes a Samaritan the hero of His story.

What it cost him

A stranger's bill, run up at the inn

  • Stopping on a robbers' roadhis safety
  • Oil and wine for the woundshis supplies
  • His own beast to ride onhe walked
  • A night at the inn, caring for himhis time
  • Two pence to the hostabout two days' wages
“Whatsoever thou spendest more”“I will repay thee”
PAIDby the Samaritan

Olive oil soothed a wound and wine cleaned it: the first aid of the day. A penny was a day's pay (Matthew 20:2), so two pence covered days of care, and he promised to cover the rest.

Jesus' first point is plain: go and do the same. But don't miss it: we were the man half dead by the road. Jesus came where we were, paid what we couldn't, and promised to come again.

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Jesus turned the question around

The lawyer asked→“Who is my neighbour?”Who do I have to love, and who can I skip?
Jesus asked→“Which … was neighbour unto him?”Don't ask who qualifies. Be one, to whoever is lying in front of you.

The lawyer couldn't bring himself to say “the Samaritan.” He said, “He that shewed mercy on him.”

“Go, and do thou likewise.” A man who's been picked up off the road knows how to stop for the next one. That's part of staying well: carrying it to someone else.

Part four

One thing is needful

Verses 38–42

Jesus comes to dinner. One sister works in the kitchen; the other sits and listens.

Two sisters, one house

John tells us the village was Bethany, under two miles from Jerusalem, near the top of that same Jericho road: “Bethany, the town of Mary and her sister Martha” (John 11:1).

Martha

“Cumbered about much serving”

Pulled in every direction, and upset that nobody was helping.

Mary

“Sat at Jesus' feet”

The place of a disciple, listening to his teacher.

Martha wasn't wrong to serve. She opened her home to Jesus. The trouble came out in her words: “Lord, dost thou not care?” Worry had turned into blaming, Him and her sister both.

“Careful”: full of care

μεριμνάω said merimnaō “to be anxious, pulled apart by worry”

In the King James, careful means worried, not cautious. He says her name twice, gently, the way you talk to someone you love. He wasn't scolding the work. He was freeing her from the worry.

“Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.”

Philippians 4:6

One thing outweighs them all

Martha had a list. Mary had one thing: time at His feet, hearing His word.

A busy life can crowd that out, even a life busy with good things: work, family, meetings, serving. Being with Jesus comes first, and the rest finds its place after.

And what you get there “shall not be taken away.” Not by a bad day, not by anybody.

Points to remember

  1. The harvest“Pray ye therefore the Lord of the harvest” 10:2The need is great. Start by praying for workers, then go.
  2. Real joy“Your names are written in heaven” 10:20Rejoice in what He did for you more than in what you do for Him.
  3. Revealed“Revealed them unto babes” 10:21The proud shut themselves out. Whoever comes humbly is taught.
  4. Eternal life“Willing to justify himself” 10:29The law shows us we fall short. Excuses won't save us; Jesus does.
  5. Neighbour“He that shewed mercy on him” 10:37Don't ask who your neighbour is. Be one. Go and do likewise.
  6. One thing“One thing is needful” 10:42Time at His feet comes first, and no one can take it from you.

Question 1 of 6

The harvest

What did Jesus tell the seventy to pray for before they went?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For more labourers. The harvest was great and the workers few, so before they took a step, He told them to pray the Lord of the harvest to send out more. Then He sent them, two and two, as lambs among wolves.

Question 2 of 6

Real joy

What did Jesus say was a better reason to rejoice than power over devils?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Answer

That their names are written in heaven. Power over spirits was a gift for the work, and work has good days and bad. A name written in heaven is God's doing, and it stays written. That's the joy that lasts.

Question 3 of 6

Revealed

Who did the Father hide these things from, and who did He show them to?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

From the wise and prudent, who think they already know. He showed them to babes, the humble who come to be taught. Jesus invites all who are weary, and shows the Father to whoever comes.

Question 4 of 6

Eternal life

Why did the lawyer ask, “And who is my neighbour?”

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He was “willing to justify himself.” He wanted a narrow line so he could say he'd kept the law. Nobody keeps it perfectly. The law shows our need, and eternal life is a gift, received by faith in Jesus.

Question 5 of 6

Neighbour

Who turned out to be a neighbour to the man who fell among thieves?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The Samaritan, the man the lawyer would least expect. The priest and the Levite passed by. The Samaritan had compassion, bound up his wounds, and paid for his care. Jesus said, “Go, and do thou likewise.”

Question 6 of 6

One thing

What was the one thing Martha needed most?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To sit at Jesus' feet and hear His word, the good part Mary chose. Martha's serving wasn't wrong, but she was careful and troubled about many things. Time with Jesus comes first, and it can't be taken away.
