Leviticus 27

Vows, the fifth part, and the tithe

What a man has promised to the LORD, he is to pay, and the LORD sets the price. What is already his, the firstling and the tenth, no man can vow. Then the book ends: “These are the commandments, which the LORD commanded Moses for the children of Israel in mount Sinai.”

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. The vowWhat was a man to pay if he vowed a person to the LORD?

Part one

“A singular vow”

Verses 1–8

What a man's promise cost, by the priest's valuation.

A vow, and its price

  • a vowa promise made to the LORD: “I will give this”
  • singulara special, particular vow, here of a person
  • the persons shall be for the LORDthe one vowed belongs to him
  • estimationthe valuation, the price the priest sets (to estimate is to value)
  • the shekel of the sanctuarythe full, standard shekel: “twenty gerahs shall be the shekel” (verse 25)

The picture is a man who says, “LORD, I give you myself”, or his child. The vow is not undone, and it is not left without a price either: the person stays in the LORD's keeping, and the vower pays the valuation in silver to the sanctuary. A vow is a promise, and the book finishes with how to pay one.

Moses does not say why the figures differ: it is a price list for a vow, not a ranking of souls.

One price for every soul

When it is a soul's own ransom, the LORD sets one price for every man:

“The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less than half a shekel, when they give an offering unto the LORD, to make an atonement for your souls.”

Exodus 30:15

The rich and the poor, the same half shekel. And the price paid for every believer is the same, too: not silver and gold, but the blood of Christ (1 Peter 1:18–19).

According to his ability

A poor man did not walk away from his vow, and he was not crushed by it. “He shall present himself before the priest, and the priest shall value him; according to his ability that vowed shall the priest value him.” The LORD asks what a man can give, not what he cannot.

“And he called unto him his disciples, and saith unto them, Verily I say unto you, That this poor widow hath cast more in, than all they which have cast into the treasury: For all they did cast in of their abundance; but she of her want did cast in all that she had, even all her living.”

Mark 12:43–44

Jesus said it of a widow at the treasury. A man in recovery with nothing in his pocket gives God exactly as much as a rich man does, if he gives what he has. There is no shame in being poor at the altar.

Part two

“He shall add a fifth part”

Verses 9–25

Animals, houses and fields given to the LORD, and what it costs to take them back.

No swapping

  • a clean beast, given“shall be holy”: it belongs to the LORD
  • “not alter it, nor change it”not a good for a bad, nor a bad for a good
  • if he changes it anywayboth the beast and its exchange “shall be holy”: he loses both
  • an unclean beastnot offered as a sacrifice: the priest values it (verses 11–12)

The old trick: promise the best animal, and then quietly give the worst. The LORD closes it in one verse. If you swap, you lose both. What you said in front of him, you owe in full.

“When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it; for he hath no pleasure in fools: pay that which thou hast vowed. Better is it that thou shouldest not vow, than that thou shouldest vow and not pay.”

Ecclesiastes 5:4–5

Notice, “better is it that thou shouldest not vow”. Nobody has to make a vow. But a man who has made one is to pay it.

The price of taking it back

50shekels, the priest's valuation
+ 10a fifth part added
60shekels to buy it back

An example. If the priest valued a field at fifty shekels, a fifth part is ten, and the man who gave it and wants it back pays sixty. The fifth part comes five times in this chapter, for a beast, a house, a field and the tithe (verses 13, 15, 19, 27 and 31).

Why the extra? Taking back what you gave to God costs more than giving it did. A gift is not a loan.

Fields, and the jubile again

Valued by its seed: “an homer of barley seed” at fifty shekels. From the jubile it stands at the full price. After it, the price is abated by the years gone.

If he will not redeem it

If he sells it to another, it is not redeemed any more. At the jubile it is “holy unto the LORD, as a field devoted”, and the priest's.

A field he had bought

222324

Not his inheritance, so he cannot give it for good. In the jubile it returns to the family it came from.

A homer is a large measure of grain, enough seed for a good field. To abate is to lower. And the standard is fixed in verse 25: “twenty gerahs shall be the shekel”, a gerah being a twentieth part of it. The jubile of chapter 25 is still at work here: no man could give away, or be robbed of, a family's land for good.

Real world: the promise you made on a bad night

Most of us have prayed it: God, if you get me out of this, I will… in a hospital, a cell, a hangover. Chapter 27 does not mock it, and it does not wave it away.

  • SinPromises we made and broke, to God and to people who loved us.
  • Regret“Better is it that thou shouldest not vow, than that thou shouldest vow and not pay.” Some of us made too many.
  • GraceThe price was always set by the LORD, and the poor man's was set by his ability. A man who cannot pay does not run: he presents himself to the priest.
  • HopeWe have a great High Priest, and he has already paid for us.
  • TodayMake fewer big promises and keep the small ones. Say to a brother what you are going to do today, and then go and do it.

Part three

“It is the LORD'S”

Verses 26–34

What is already his, and the end of the book.

You cannot vow what is already his

“I give the LORD my firstling” → “it is the LORD'S” A firstling is the firstborn of a beast. The firstborn of man and of beast were his from the night of the Passover (Exodus 13:2).

“But who am I, and what is my people, that we should be able to offer so willingly after this sort? for all things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee.”

1 Chronicles 29:14

A man cannot give the LORD what is already his. Whatever we bring, we are handing back. The unclean beast's firstling could be redeemed at the valuation, with the fifth part added (verse 27).

A devoted thing

Verses 28 and 29 are different from the vows before them. A devoted thing is one given up wholly to the LORD, “most holy unto the LORD”, and it can be neither sold nor redeemed. And a person “devoted of men” shall be put to death (verse 29).

It is a hard verse, and we read it as it stands. Don't make a doctrine from it. It belongs to the law's courts and to judgment, not to a man's ordinary vow. The Bible's picture of a devoted city is Jericho, when everything in it was given to the LORD (Joshua 6:17).

“And the city shall be accursed, even it, and all that are therein, to the LORD: only Rahab the harlot shall live, she and all that are with her in the house, because she hid the messengers that we sent.”

Joshua 6:17

The difference with the vow of verse 2 is that this one cannot be bought back. A devoted thing is his, and that is the end.

A tithe is a tenth. The shepherd let the sheep pass one by one under his rod, and counted every tenth, good or bad: no picking, no swapping (verses 32–33). Same rule as the beast in verse 10: the LORD's share is not chosen by the one who owes it. Ought in verse 31 means anything at all.

First, because it is his

“Bring ye all the tithes into the storehouse, that there may be meat in mine house, and prove me now herewith, saith the LORD of hosts, if I will not open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there shall not be room enough to receive it.”

Malachi 3:10

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7

For a man just out of the ditch, with a bank account that looks like one, hear what verse 30 says: the tenth is the LORD'S. It was never ours to start with. Give to him first, as you are able, willingly, and start small and keep it. This is not a bargain for a bigger bank account. It is a man trusting the God who already feeds him.

The last verse

The book began with “And the LORD called unto Moses, and spake unto him out of the tabernacle of the congregation, saying,” (1:1). Count it: “the LORD spake unto Moses”, “said unto Moses”, “called unto Moses”: thirty-six times in Leviticus, and every time it is the same Moses, writing down what he was told, in the same mount. Jesus said “offer the gift that Moses commanded” (Matthew 8:4), and Paul wrote “Moses describeth the righteousness which is of the law” (Romans 10:5), quoting Leviticus 18:5.

The wilderness is the place, the mount the setting, and the tent beside them the reason: the LORD had come to live in their midst, and he told them how.

Looking back over Leviticus

  1. 1–7The offerings“without blemish”
  2. 8–10The priestswashed, clothed, anointed
  3. 11–15Clean and unclean“Unclean, unclean”, and cleansed
  4. 16The day of atonementonce a year, with blood
  5. 17–27Holy living“Ye shall be holy”

Read it as one story. A holy God has just moved into the camp (Exodus 40). A sinful people cannot live beside him unless something is done about the sin, and the first seven chapters say what: a life given in place of theirs, offering after offering. Then the priests who stand between, the unclean who are shut out and let back in, and one day in the year when the blood goes in behind the vail.

And after the forgiveness, the life: the sabbaths, the feasts, the jubile, the blessing and the warning, and the vows. Not to earn the LORD's nearness, which the blood had already given, but because a people near to a holy God are to look like him.

Points to remember

  1. The vow“When a man shall make a singular vow, the persons shall be for the LORD by thy estimation” 27:2A vow is a promise to the LORD, and he sets what it costs to keep it.
  2. The poor man“according to his ability that vowed shall the priest value him” 27:8He asks what a man can give, not what he cannot.
  3. The fifth part“then he shall add a fifth part thereof unto thy estimation” 27:13Taking back what you gave costs more than giving it did: a gift is not a loan.
  4. The firstling“the firstling of the beasts, which should be the LORD’S firstling, no man shall sanctify it” 27:26You cannot vow what is already his.
  5. The tithe“all the tithe of the land… is the LORD’S: it is holy unto the LORD” 27:30The tenth is his from the start. Give first, and give willingly.
  6. The last verse“These are the commandments, which the LORD commanded Moses for the children of Israel in mount Sinai” 27:34Moses wrote it, in the shadow of the mount, for a people the LORD had come to live among.

Question 1 of 6

The vow

What was a man to pay if he vowed a person to the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The priest's valuation in silver, by age and sex: for a man from twenty to sixty years old, fifty shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary. A vow is a promise, and it has a price.

Question 3 of 6

The fifth part

What did a man add when he bought back what he had given?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A fifth part more than the valuation: for a beast, a house, a field and a tithe. Taking back what you gave costs more than giving it did.

Question 4 of 6

The firstling

Why could no man vow the firstling of the beasts?

What verses tell us about this?

1 Chronicles 29:14

Answer

Because it was the LORD's already: “it is the LORD'S”. A man cannot give God what is already his. Whatever we bring, we are handing back.

Question 5 of 6

The tithe

What part of the land's produce and of the herd and flock was the LORD's?

What verses tell us about this?

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7

Answer

The tenth: all the tithe of the land, of the seed and the fruit, and the tenth of the herd and flock that passes under the rod. It is the LORD's and holy. Paul says to give willingly, not grudgingly.

Question 6 of 6

The last verse

Where were these commandments given, and through whom?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In mount Sinai, through Moses, who wrote what the LORD commanded him. It is the same Moses at the beginning and at the end of the book.
