Deuteronomy 30

Return, the word is very nigh, and choose life

Even if they are driven to the ends of heaven, the LORD will gather them when they return to him with all their heart, and circumcise their heart to love him. The commandment is not far off: it is in thy mouth and in thy heart. “I have set before you life and death… therefore choose life.”

Part one

“Shalt return unto the LORD thy God”

Verses 1–10

Moses looks past the failure to the way back, and what the LORD does when they come.

The way back, step by step

They remember“call them to mind among all the nations” (1)
→
They return“return unto the LORD thy God… with all thine heart” (2)
→
He comes to them“turn thy captivity, and have compassion… and will return and gather thee” (3)

The word return comes three times (2, 3, 8), and the middle one is God's: they return to the LORD, and he “will return and gather thee”. “Turn thy captivity” means bring them back from where they were carried, and put things right.

It begins with calling to mind. That is what regret is for: not to bury a man, but to turn him round.

He came to himself, and the father ran

“And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father’s have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger!”

Luke 15:17

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

It is the same road: a man in a far place calls it to mind, turns, and finds that the one he left has been looking for him. The father in the story “had compassion, and ran”, and the LORD says he will “have compassion upon thee”.

If you fell yesterday, this chapter is for you before it is for anyone. Return today, and tell a brother.

“From thence will he fetch thee”

The outmost parts of heaven

Outmost means outermost, farthest: the edge of the sky where it meets the ground. Fetch means go and bring back. There is no place a man can be driven so far that the LORD cannot go and bring him back.

“But if from thence thou shalt seek the LORD thy God, thou shalt find him, if thou seek him with all thy heart and with all thy soul.”

Deuteronomy 4:29

Moses said the same thing in chapter 4. The farthest place is not too far to start from: “from thence”. Wherever a man has ended up, that is where he begins.

Commanded in chapter 10, promised in chapter 30

Commanded, Deuteronomy 10:16

“Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart, and be no more stiffnecked”

We saw the command in chapter 10. Here is the promise behind it.

Promised, Deuteronomy 30:6

“The LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart”

The LORD does it, “to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, that thou mayest live”. And it reaches “the heart of thy seed”: the children too.

In chapter 29 they were told they had not been given “an heart to perceive” (29:4). Here is the answer. The word heart comes seven times in this chapter, and this is the heart of it: what the LORD wants is a heart that loves him, and what he promises is to do the work on it himself.

A heart cut, and a heart new

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

“But he is a Jew, which is one inwardly; and circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter; whose praise is not of men, but of God.”

Romans 2:29

Ezekiel promises the same heart. Paul calls it circumcision “of the heart, in the spirit”. It is the LORD's work, not a thing you earn.

“Rejoice over thee for good”

The LORD will again rejoice over thee

God's own joy, over a people who have come back: not put up with, but rejoiced over, “as he rejoiced over thy fathers”.

“Plenteous in every work of thine hand” (9) is the covenant blessing in their land: children, herds and fields. It is not a promise to us of money for obedience. Ours is “all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ” (Ephesians 1:3).

And verse 10 asks for one thing, again: “with all thine heart, and with all thy soul”. Not a perfect record: a whole heart.

Part two

“The word is very nigh unto thee”

Verses 11–14

Not in heaven, not beyond the sea: in thy mouth and in thy heart.

Not far off

  • not in heavenno one has to go up to bring it down
  • not beyond the seano one has to cross over to fetch it
  • not hidden from theeit has been spoken, in plain words, in front of you
  • but very nigh“in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it”

“Nigh” means near. Moses is answering the man who says it is too hard, too high, too far for someone like me. The LORD has not left his word on a mountain top. It is written in a book you can read today, and in a mouth that can say it.

Paul takes it up

Moses, Deuteronomy 30:12, 14

It is not in heaven, that thou shouldest say, Who shall go up for us to heaven… But the word is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart

Paul, Romans 10:6–8

But the righteousness which is of faith speaketh on this wise, Say not in thine heart, Who shall ascend into heaven? (that is, to bring Christ down from above:) Or, Who shall descend into the deep? (that is, to bring up Christ again from the dead.) But what saith it? The word is nigh thee, even in thy mouth, and in thy heart: that is, the word of faith, which we preach;

Paul reads it as the gospel: Christ has come down and has risen. Nobody has to bring him. The word is near.

The word of faith

“That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.”

Romans 10:9

Paul keeps Moses' two places, mouth and heart, and puts them in order: believe in the heart that God raised him, and confess with the mouth that he is Lord. Both are near, both are within reach of any man, in any room, today.

“Therefore by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight: for by the law is the knowledge of sin.”

Romans 3:20

Moses says “that thou mayest do it”, and the law shows a man what he has not done. Nobody is saved by keeping it. That is why the word has to be the word of faith, and why Christ came down. The nearness of the word is the nearness of a Saviour.

Part three

“Therefore choose life”

Verses 15–20

Two ways, one day, and a choice that is really theirs to make.

Two ways, set before them this day

this day

Life and good

  • love the LORD thy God
  • walk in his ways
  • keep his commandments
“that thou mayest live and multiply: and the LORD thy God shall bless thee” (16)

Death and evil

  • the heart turns away
  • will not hear, and is drawn away
  • worships other gods and serves them
“ye shall surely perish” (18)

“I denounce unto you this day” means I tell you plainly, I declare to you. Moses is not threatening them; he is telling them straight where each road ends, because he loves them. The words “this day” come seven times in the chapter (2, 8, 11, 15, 16, 18, 19): the choice is always today's.

A real choice

Therefore choose life

God does not say “I have chosen for you”, and he does not say “you have no say”. He puts two roads in front of them, calls heaven and earth to record it (that is, to stand as witnesses to what was said, “this day”), and tells them to choose. The choice is real, and it is theirs. The same is true of every man who hears the gospel: it is offered to him, and he is to answer.

And “that both thou and thy seed may live”: a man's choice reaches his children, one way or the other. A father who turns back to the LORD changes what his sons will see at the table.

“For he is thy life”

Look at what the choice is: “that thou mayest love the LORD thy God, and that thou mayest obey his voice, and that thou mayest cleave unto him: for he is thy life”. Cleave means stick fast to, hold on, the word for a man and his wife. Life is not a reward the LORD hands out at a distance. Life is him.

“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6

“He that hath the Son hath life; and he that hath not the Son of God hath not life.”

1 John 5:12

Moses says “he is thy life”, and Jesus says “I am… the life”. Choosing life is coming to him, and holding on.

Real world: choose life today

  • SinEvery day there are two roads: the heart that turns away, and the heart that holds on. The old life never needs a big decision. It needs only one small one, again.
  • RegretIf you fell yesterday, the chapter begins with you: “call them to mind… and shalt return”. Regret is the first step on the road back, not the end of it.
  • GraceThe LORD himself circumcises the heart, and the word is “very nigh”. He has done the hard part: Christ came down, and rose.
  • Hope“From thence will he fetch thee.” No place is too far to begin from, and he rejoices over a man who returns.
  • TodayChoose one small thing that points the right way: open the Word, say a true thing to a brother in Christ, go to the meeting, leave the corner. If it is heavy, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today.

Points to remember

  1. The way back“And shalt return unto the LORD thy God… and will return and gather thee” 30:2–3Calling to mind leads to return, and the LORD meets him, and gathers him from the farthest place.
  2. The heart“the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart” 30:6The LORD does the work on the heart himself, so that they may love him and live.
  3. The word“the word is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart” 30:14Not far, not hidden: Paul calls it the word of faith (Romans 10:8).
  4. Two ways“I have set before thee this day life and good, and death and evil” 30:15Love, walk and keep, or turn away and be drawn: and Moses says it plainly.
  5. The choice“therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live” 30:19A real choice, set before every man, and it reaches his children.
  6. Your life“for he is thy life, and the length of thy days” 30:20Life is the LORD himself: love him, obey his voice, and hold on to him.

Question 1 of 6

The way back

What will the LORD do when his people return to him with all their heart?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He will turn their captivity, have compassion on them, and himself return and gather them from all the nations, even from the outmost parts of heaven. “From thence will he fetch thee.”

Question 2 of 6

The heart

What will the LORD do to their heart, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He will circumcise their heart, and the heart of their seed, “to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, that thou mayest live”.

Question 3 of 6

The word

How near is the commandment?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Very near: not hidden, not far off, not in heaven or beyond the sea, but “in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it”.

Question 4 of 6

Two ways

What two sets of things does Moses set before them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Life and good, and death and evil: to love the LORD, walk in his ways and keep his commandments and live, or to turn away, be drawn to other gods and surely perish.

Question 5 of 6

The choice

What does Moses call heaven and earth to record, and what does he tell them to choose?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That he has set before them life and death, blessing and cursing. And he tells them to choose life, that both they and their seed may live.

Question 6 of 6

Your life

Who is Israel's life, and what are they to do with him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD himself: “he is thy life, and the length of thy days”. They are to love him, obey his voice, and cleave unto him.
