Hebrews 6

An anchor of the soul

The hardest warning in the book, and then the surest comfort: God has promised, God has sworn, and our hope is tied fast where Jesus has already gone.

Chapter 5 ended with a rebuke: you should be teachers by now, and you're still on milk. Chapter 6 says: then let's grow up. It holds the hardest warning in Hebrews, and right after it, one of the warmest promises in the Bible.

Every warning in this book has the same aim: to shake loose the man who only sat near the faith, and to make the real believer hold on tighter. None of them is meant to make a struggling believer think God has thrown him away.

Part one

Let us go on

Verses 1–3

You lay a foundation once. Then you build on it.

Build on the foundation

These six are the ABCs: turn from the dead works of your old life, trust God, the washings and the laying on of hands the Jewish believers already knew, and the coming resurrection and judgment. All true, all needed. But nobody lives in a foundation.

Perfection here means maturity, a full-grown faith, not a sinless one. You don't need to get saved again every time you fail. Stand on what Christ already did, and build: “And this will we do, if God permit.”

Part two

The hardest warning

Verses 4–8

Read it slowly, read it whole, and read the verse right after it.

What the verses say

The men in view had come very close. They were “once enlightened,” had “tasted of the heavenly gift,” were “partakers of the Holy Ghost,” and had “tasted the good word of God, and the powers of the world to come.”

Fall away here doesn't mean a stumble or a relapse. It means turning your back on Christ for good, siding with the people who crucified Him and treating Him as a fraud.

Two ways good teachers read it

Reading one

Near, but never in

Men who saw it all, sat in it, and had a share in the Spirit's work around them, but never truly trusted Christ. Like Israel in the wilderness: they saw the sea part and ate the manna, yet “could not enter in because of unbelief” (3:19).

Reading two

IF, and it can't

The writer reasons: IF a true believer could fall away, there would be no way back, because Christ would have to be crucified a second time. There will never be a second cross. So a true believer can't be lost.

Baptists hold both readings, and they land in the same place: a man truly saved is kept. Jesus said, “they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand” (John 10:28). The warning shakes loose the man who only sat near, and it makes the real believer hold on.

Once is enough

“For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.”

Hebrews 10:14

Look at the reason the writer gives: “they crucify to themselves the Son of God afresh.” A second rescue would need a second cross. God will never do that, because the first one was enough.

That is good news. The one sacrifice that saved you is the same one that keeps you. It doesn't run out, and it doesn't need doing over.

Afraid you've fallen away?

The man this passage warns doesn't care.

He has turned on Christ and holds Him in contempt. A man who is worried that he has fallen away is showing the very concern this passage says such men don't have. That ache to get right with God is the Spirit at work in you. So don't run from Him. Come back.

  • “I've sinned too much”His one offering perfected you “for ever”
  • “I need to get saved all over again”you don't; there is no second cross, and no need of one
  • Come back and confess“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” (1 John 1:9)

Same rain, two fields

Both fields got the same rain, over and over. The rain is God's goodness: the light, the word, the taste of verses 4 and 5. What comes up shows what was in the ground. A field that only ever grows thorns gets burned off.

The fruit doesn't save a man; it shows what's growing in him. Ask yourself honestly what is coming up. Even a few green shoots count, and the next verses say God sees every one.

Part three

But, beloved

Verses 9–12

The warning was for shaking. Now comes the arm around the shoulder.

Better things of you

The warning was real. So is “beloved.”

Right after the hardest words in the book, the writer calls his readers “beloved” and says he is sure of “better things”: “things that accompany salvation.” He isn't talking to them as if they were lost. He wants them awake.

And God keeps a record. The ride you gave a brother to a meeting, the call you took at midnight, the meal, the honest word: “God is not unrighteous to forget your work and labour of love.” Nobody else may remember it. He does.

“And whosoever shall give to drink unto one of these little ones a cup of cold water only in the name of a disciple, verily I say unto you, he shall in no wise lose his reward.”

Matthew 10:42

Full assurance, and keep going

  • slothfulthe same word as “dull” in 5:11: lazy, drifting
  • anxious and unsureGod wants you to have “the full assurance of hope”
  • diligent, with faith and patience“followers of them who through faith and patience inherit the promises”

God doesn't want you guessing whether you belong to Him. He wants you sure, and the sure man is the one who keeps going. Faith takes hold of the promise; patience waits for it. The next verses show one man who did both, and failed along the way too.

Part four

An anchor of the soul

Verses 13–20

God gave His word, and then He swore an oath on top of it.

He patiently endured

  1. 75leaves Haran with a promiseGen. 12:4
  2. 86tired of waiting: IshmaelGen. 16
  3. 100Isaac is bornGen. 21:5
  4. laterMoriah: God swearsGen. 22:16

Twenty-five years between the promise and the son. Abraham didn't wait perfectly: he took a shortcut with Hagar, and it cost him. But God didn't take the promise back. “After he had patiently endured, he obtained the promise.”

“And said, By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD, for because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thine only son: That in blessing I will bless thee, and in multiplying I will multiply thy seed as the stars of the heaven…”

Genesis 22:16–17

Two pillars, and a place to run

People swear by something greater to end an argument. God has no one greater, so He swore by Himself. His promise can't change, and His oath can't change: “two immutable things.” He did it, verse 17 says, to show the heirs, us, that He will not change His mind.

“Fled for refuge” recalls the cities of refuge, where a man who had killed someone by accident could run and be safe from the avenger (Numbers 35:11). Every man here has something behind him. Run to Christ. Inside the gate, you are safe.

A sailor drops his anchor down, into the mud he can't see. Ours goes up: it “entereth into that within the veil,” into the presence of God. The storm doesn't stop. The waves still throw the ship around. But the ship holds, because the anchor isn't fastened to anything in the storm.

The forerunner

Behind the veil was the holiest place, where God's presence was. Only the high priest went in, once a year, never without blood (Hebrews 9:7). Then he came back out.

Jesus went in and stays. A forerunner goes ahead so others can follow. He is there “for us,” a priest “for ever,” and your anchor line is tied to Him.

In today's storm, hold the line, not the waves. Chapter 7 picks up where this one ends: Melchisedec.

Points to remember

  1. Go on“Let us go on unto perfection; not laying again the foundation” 6:1Stand on the foundation Christ laid, and build. Grow up.
  2. The hard warning“Seeing they crucify to themselves the Son of God afresh” 6:6There is no second cross, and no need of one. A man truly saved is kept.
  3. Two fields“The earth which drinketh in the rain… receiveth blessing from God” 6:7The same rain falls on all. What comes up shows what's in the ground.
  4. Better things“God is not unrighteous to forget your work and labour of love” 6:10“Beloved.” God remembers every small kindness done in His name.
  5. God's oath“By two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie” 6:18His promise and His oath. He will not change His mind about you.
  6. The anchor“Which hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast” 6:19Your anchor is fastened in heaven, where Jesus went for you.

Question 1 of 6

Go on

What does the writer want us to leave behind, and where should we go?

What verses tell us about this?

“But grow in grace, and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ. To him be glory both now and for ever. Amen.”

2 Peter 3:18

Answer

Leave the first principles, not by forgetting them but by building on them. Don't keep laying the foundation again. Go on to maturity, a full-grown faith.

Question 2 of 6

The hard warning

What do verses 4 to 6 say is impossible, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Answer

To renew a man to repentance if he falls away, because it would take crucifying Christ again. He died once, and that was enough. Either these men only came near and never believed, or the writer is showing that a true believer can't be lost. A worried man isn't the man in this verse: come back.

Question 3 of 6

Two fields

What shows the difference between two fields that drink the same rain?

What verses tell us about this?

“They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us: but they went out, that they might be made manifest that they were not all of us.”

1 John 2:19

Answer

What grows. Both get the same rain, God's goodness and His word. One brings forth useful herbs and is blessed; the other only thorns. Fruit doesn't save, but it shows what is in the ground.

Question 4 of 6

Better things

What is the writer persuaded of, and what will God not forget?

What verses tell us about this?

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Answer

He is persuaded of better things: things that go with salvation. And God won't forget their work and labour of love toward His people. The warning was to wake them up, not to tell them they were lost.

Question 5 of 6

God's oath

What two unchangeable things did God give so we could be sure?

What verses tell us about this?

“And said, By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD, for because thou hast done this thing, and hast not withheld thy son, thine only son:”

Genesis 22:16

Answer

His promise and His oath. God can't lie, and He swore by Himself because there is no one greater. He did it so that men who have fled to Him for refuge could have strong comfort.

Question 6 of 6

The anchor

What is our anchor, and where is it fastened?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Answer

Our hope in Christ is the anchor. It isn't fastened in the storm but within the veil, in God's presence, where Jesus has gone ahead of us and lives for ever as our high priest. That is why it holds.
