Exodus 38

The altar, the looking glasses, and the count

The brasen altar is built at the door, the laver is cast from the women's mirrors, the court is hung round with linen, and every ounce of gold, silver and brass is counted.

Part one

“The altar of burnt offering”

Verses 1–8

The first thing at the door is an altar, and the water to wash in is made from mirrors.

The altar, piece by piece

  • shittim woodacacia, the hard, dry-land wood of the desert
  • foursquaresquare on every side: five cubits by five (about 7½ feet), and three high
  • brasswhat we would call bronze. A wooden altar would burn, so it was covered
  • hornsa point at each corner, cut from the same wood
  • fleshhookslong forks for turning and lifting the meat
  • firepanspans for carrying coals, and shovels for the ashes
  • gratea bronze mesh set inside, where the fire and the offering lay
  • stavespoles slid through rings, so it could be carried; and withal means “with it”

Verse 4 says the grate went “under the compass thereof.” There compass is the ledge that runs around the altar. And “hollow with boards” means it was a box of wood, empty inside, so they could carry it.

The first thing you meet

Come in at the gate, and the altar is right in front of you. Only after it come the laver, the tent, the lampstand, the table, the vail and the ark. Nobody gets past the altar.

Why a death comes first

The brasen altar stood where the fire and the blood were. It said one thing to everybody who came near: sin costs a life, and the life is given before you go any closer.

Hebrews says, “We have an altar,” and points to Jesus, who “suffered without the gate.” The brasen altar is a picture of the cross.

“We have an altar, whereof they have no right to eat which serve the tabernacle. For the bodies of those beasts, whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin, are burned without the camp. Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate.”

Hebrews 13:10–12

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

Notice who the altar was built for. Not for the clean. It was built for sinners to stand beside, with an animal that took the death instead of them. If you fell yesterday, you are not too dirty for the altar. The altar is where dirty men begin.

A basin made from mirrors

the women's looking glasses → the priests' laver Lookingglasses were mirrors of polished bronze: there was no glass. The laver is a washing basin. The women who “assembled” at the door, who gathered to serve there, gave up their mirrors, and the bronze was melted into the basin where the priests washed.

The chapter does not tell us why they gave them, and we should not invent it. We know what it cost them: a woman's mirror was how she saw herself.

“For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass: For he beholdeth himself, and goeth his way, and straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was.”

James 1:23–24

A mirror shows a man what he is. It cannot wash him. The laver could. Look at yourself honestly, and then go to the water.

The washing

Every priest washed his hands and his feet at the laver before he went in to serve (we will see Moses do it in chapter 40). The water was for men who were already priests: it cleaned the dust of the day off them.

“That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word,”

Ephesians 5:26

A believer is the same. You are not made a son again every time you fall. But you do get dirty on the road, and God has put water where you can come and get clean: his word, and confessing it to him and to your brothers.

What would you give up, if you had to, so another man could be clean? The women gave the thing they looked at themselves in. God used it.

Part two

“He made the court”

Verses 9–20

A fence of white linen, one hundred cubits by fifty, with one wide gate.

The court from above

Fifty cubits is about seventy-five feet, a hundred is about a hundred and fifty. The fence stood five cubits, about seven and a half feet: no one looked over it.

Old words at the fence

  • hangingscurtains, hung from the pillars
  • fine twined linenlinen thread, twisted: strong, and white
  • socketsthe bases the pillars stood in. Here, bronze
  • filletssilver bands round the top of each pillar
  • chapitersthe tops, the capitals, covered with silver
  • pinstent pegs, bronze, to hold the cords
  • needleworkembroidery: the gate was stitched, not woven plain
  • answerable tomatching: the gate was as high as the walls

Notice the metals going down. Gold inside the tent, silver on the pillar-tops, bronze in the feet and the pegs. The closer to God, the finer the metal. But look: even the bronze is counted and written down.

One gate, wide open

All the way round, a white fence. In the east, one gate, twenty cubits wide: thirty feet of blue, purple and scarlet. Everyone had to come in the same way, and the way was wide enough for everyone.

“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6

“I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”

John 10:9

One way in is not a trap. It is a mercy. Nobody is shut out for who he was, because the gate is not guarded against sinners. It is the only way, and it is open. Walk in.

Part three

“The sum of the tabernacle”

Verses 21–31

The builders are named, and every shekel is counted.

Two men, two tribes, one work

Bezaleel

of the tribe of Judah

The great tribe, the line of kings and of Christ. His grandfather was a Hur (probably the Hur who held up Moses' arm at Rephidim, 17:12, though the text doesn't say).

Aholiab

of the tribe of Dan

Dan came from a servant woman's son, Bilhah's. God set him beside Bezaleel, and put skill in him.

An engraver cuts designs into metal and stone. A cunning workman is a skilled one. An embroiderer stitches the blue and purple and scarlet. God did not choose these men for their family. He chose them and filled them:

“See, I have called by name Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah: And I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship,”

Exodus 31:2–3

And “the sum of the tabernacle” was counted “by the hand of Ithamar,” one of Aaron's sons: the books were kept by a priest, in the open.

Where the silver came from

603,550men twenty years old and up
× ½shekel each: a bekah
301,775shekels of silver

Verse 25 gives the same total another way: a hundred talents and 1,775 shekels. So a hundred talents is exactly 300,000 shekels, and one talent is 3,000 shekels. The Bible does its own sums here, and they agree. A talent was about seventy-five pounds, so a hundred talents of silver is a few tons.

Occupied (verse 24) means used. “The shekel of the sanctuary” was the standard weight, so nobody could cheat with a light scale. And the half shekel was the census money of Exodus 30:12–16, the same number counted again in Numbers 1.

A house on a foundation of ransom

A hundred silver sockets, a talent in each: the footings the tabernacle's boards and the vail's pillars stood in. And the silver was the money from the count. God had said what it was for:

“The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less than half a shekel, when they give an offering unto the LORD, to make an atonement for your souls. And thou shalt take the atonement money of the children of Israel, and shalt appoint it for the service of the tabernacle of the congregation; that it may be a memorial unto the children of Israel before the LORD, to make an atonement for your souls.”

Exodus 30:15–16

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Every man gave the same half shekel, and the house stood on it. The price of a soul was never silver: it was the blood of the Lamb, the same for every man.

Everything counted, everything used

  1. 1twenty and nine talents, and seven hundred and thirty shekelsThe gold, from the offering. All of it went into “the work of the holy place”: about a ton of gold.38:24
  2. 2a talent for a socketThe silver, from the census: a hundred sockets, and the hooks, and the silver tops of the pillars.38:27–28
  3. 3seventy talents, and two thousand and four hundred shekelsThe brass, from the offering: the sockets at the door, the altar, its grate and vessels, the court's sockets and every peg.38:29–31

Three sums, and every piece in them has an address. Nothing was left over that nobody could explain, and nothing was missing.

Real world: honest books

Many of us hid money for years: hid what we spent, hid where it went, lied about the count. Moses had the opposite habit. The sum was written down, and a priest's son kept it.

  • Know the numberSit down and add up what you owe and what you spend. It is less frightening on paper than in the dark.
  • Use it for what it was forThe silver was given for the tabernacle and went into the tabernacle. Pay the rent first.
  • Let a brother see itIthamar kept the books in the open. Show one trusted brother your numbers.

None of that earns anything with God. The altar at the door is already paid for. It just means you can stop running from the sum, because you are no longer the one who has to carry it.

Points to remember

  1. The altar“he made the altar of burnt offering of shittim wood… and he overlaid it with brass” 38:1–2The first thing at the door was an altar. Every way to God starts with a death in your place.
  2. The mirrors“the laver of brass… of the lookingglasses of the women assembling” 38:8The women gave what they looked at themselves in. A mirror shows you what you are, and the water makes you clean.
  3. The court“twenty cubits was the length” 38:18One gate, thirty feet wide, and everyone comes in by it. Jesus said, “I am the door.”
  4. The builders“Bezaleel… made all that the LORD commanded Moses” 38:22A man of Judah and a man of Dan, working side by side, doing what God said.
  5. The silver“A bekah for every man, that is, half a shekel” 38:26The same for rich and poor, and the house stood on it. Our ransom is the blood of Christ.
  6. The count“the brass of the offering was seventy talents” 38:29Every ounce counted and used for what it was given for. Keep honest books.

The altar

The altar of burnt offering, a wooden box overlaid with brass, set at the door. Every way to God starts with a death in your place, and Jesus is that death for us.

Question 4 of 6

The builders

Who made it all, and whose commandment did they follow?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Bezaleel of Judah and Aholiab of Dan, filled by God for the work, and they made “all that the LORD commanded Moses.” God's work gets done by men who do what he said, and he does not care which tribe they came from.

Question 5 of 6

The silver

What did every man give, and what was made of it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A half shekel of silver, rich and poor alike, and a hundred talents of it were cast into the hundred sockets the tabernacle stood on. The real price of a soul is the precious blood of Christ.

Question 6 of 6

The count

How much gold, silver and brass was given, and where did it go?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Twenty-nine talents and 730 shekels of gold, a hundred talents and 1,775 shekels of silver, seventy talents and 2,400 shekels of brass, all counted and put to the use it was given for. Keep honest books.
