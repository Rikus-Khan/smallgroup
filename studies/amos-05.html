Part one

A funeral song

Verses 1–9

Amos sings a dirge for a nation that thinks it's doing fine.

A funeral for the living

Quick reminder: Amos was a herdman from Tekoa in Judah, the southern kingdom. God sent him north to Israel, about 760 BC, while King Jeroboam II ruled and business was booming. About forty years later, Assyria would carry Israel away.

A lamentation is a funeral song. Imagine walking into a busy, cheerful marketplace and singing the song you sing at a graveside. That's what Amos did. Israel looked healthy. God said she was already as good as dead.

Not a place, a Person

Three famous shrines. Bethel had a golden calf (1 Kings 12:29). Gilgal was Israel's first camp in the promised land. Beer-sheba, down in Judah, was where Abraham and Isaac worshipped.

In Hebrew, Amos makes a pun: Gilgal sounds like “go into captivity,” and Bethel (“house of God”) would “come to nought.” The holy places couldn't save anybody. They went to church, but they weren't seeking God.

Seek Him

  • Bethela famous place
  • Gilgala proud history
  • Beer-shebaa long trip that felt holy
  • “Seek the LORD, and ye shall live”God Himself

Notice the first word God speaks in this chapter after the funeral song: live. The door is still open. God says it three times (vv. 4, 6, 14).

“Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?”

Ezekiel 33:11

Church, a meeting, a sponsor: those are good gifts, and we need them. But they're places where you meet God and His people. They aren't a substitute for Him. Seek Him.

The God of the night sky

He made themThe seven stars and Orion

A shepherd like Amos slept under them. The seven stars are the little cluster we call the Pleiades.

He turns themShadow of death into morning

The darkest night isn't too dark for Him. He brings the morning.

He rules them“The LORD is his name”

The sea, the sky, the strong and the weak (v. 9) all answer to Him.

In the middle of a list of sins, Amos breaks into a hymn. Why? Because the One calling you to seek Him is the One who hung the stars. Men “turn judgment to wormwood” (a bitter plant): they make justice taste sour. He turns death's shadow into morning.

Part two

In the gate

Verses 10–17

The courtroom was for sale, and God was watching.

A court for sale

The gate was the town's courthouse. The elders sat in the big gateway and settled cases. A poor man who'd been cheated came there for help. But the judges took bribes, and the man who spoke up for the truth got hated for it (v. 10).

What a man builds on other people's backs, he won't get to keep. And “I know”: nothing done in the dark is hidden from God. Hold on to this slide. In chapter 9, God turns these two lines around.

Hate the evil, and love the good.

Repentance isn't just feeling bad. It changes what you love and what you hate. Then it shows up in real life: “establish judgment in the gate.” Make it right where you did wrong.

“It may be.” Amos doesn't promise the whole nation will be spared. He hopes for a remnant, a group that's left. (Joseph here means the northern tribes, named for Joseph's sons Ephraim and Manasseh.)

For anyone who comes to God through Jesus, there's no “maybe” about it:

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Passed over, or passed through

Egypt, Exodus 12

The LORD passed over

On the night of the first Passover, God passed over the houses of His people and judged Egypt.

Israel, Amos 5:17

“I will pass through thee”

Now God would pass through Israel itself. Wailing in every street, every highway, every vineyard. They'd even call in farmers to help the hired mourners cry.

Being born into God's people didn't make them safe. Only blood on the door did, back in Egypt. That's still true. “Christ our passover is sacrificed for us” (1 Corinthians 5:7). The man under His blood is passed over.

Part three

The day of the LORD

Verses 18–27

Be careful what you wish for.

Out of the frying pan

  1. fled from a lionmade it out
  2. a bear met himmade it out again
  3. went into the housesafe at last, he thinks
  4. a serpent bit himdarkness, and not light

Israel looked forward to “the day of the LORD.” They pictured God showing up to beat their enemies. Amos says: you're the ones in trouble. When God comes to judge sin, He doesn't skip His own people's sin.

The picture is a man who keeps escaping, and the thing he can't escape is waiting at home. You can dodge consequences for a long time. But God is patient for a reason:

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

God turns down the music

  • feast days“I will not smell”
  • burnt offerings“I will not accept them”
  • songs and viols“I will not hear”

These were the very things God had commanded in the law. He didn't hate worship. He hated worship from people who cheated the poor all week and sang loud on the feast day. A viol was a stringed instrument, like a harp.

“And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.”

1 Samuel 15:22

Justice that never runs dry

Most streams in Israel only run in the rainy season. By summer they're dry gullies. God wants right living that keeps flowing in every season, not a burst of religion on the holiday that's gone by the next week.

“He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”

Micah 6:8

Carried, then carried off

God reaches all the way back to the forty years in the wilderness. Even then, alongside the sacrifices, some of Israel carried little shrines for Moloch and Chiun, false gods tied to the stars. The idols were old. Only the sacrifices were new.

They carried→their idols“The tabernacle of your Moloch … which ye made to yourselves.”
They would be carried→beyond DamascusAssyria lay past Damascus. About 722 BC, Assyria carried the northern kingdom away.

A man ends up carried by whatever he chooses to carry. Stephen quoted these verses in Acts 7:42–43, before the council that stoned him. Idols always cost more than they promise.

Points to remember

  1. Repentance“Seek ye me, and ye shall live” 5:4Not a place, a program, or a ritual. Seek the Lord Himself.
  2. Who God is“The LORD is his name” 5:8The God who made the stars turns the shadow of death into morning.
  3. Justice“Your treading is upon the poor” 5:11God sees how the weak are treated, and He takes it personally.
  4. God's mercy“It may be that the LORD … will be gracious” 5:15The door was still open. In Jesus, whosoever calls is saved.
  5. The day of the LORD“Darkness, and not light” 5:18God's judgment doesn't skip His own people's sin. His patience is our chance.
  6. Real worship“Let judgment run down as waters” 5:24God wants right living that keeps flowing, not just loud songs.

Question 1 of 6

Repentance

What does God call a sinking nation to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Seek Him and live. Not the famous shrines, not a proud history, not a long trip that felt holy, but the LORD Himself. Three times in one chapter God says “live.” Even with judgment coming, the door was open.

Question 2 of 6

Who God is

Why should we seek the God who made the stars?

What verses tell us about this?

“Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near:”

Isaiah 55:6

Answer

Because He's the only One big enough to help. He made the Pleiades and Orion, He commands the sea, and He turns the shadow of death into morning. He gives strength to the weak against the strong. No night you're in is too dark for Him.

Question 3 of 6

Justice

How does God feel about the way we treat the poor?

What verses tell us about this?

“He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”

Micah 6:8

Answer

He takes it personally. He knew every bribe and every bushel taken from a poor farmer. What they built on the backs of the poor, they wouldn't get to keep. God wants us to do justly and love mercy, especially toward people who can't fight back.

Question 4 of 6

God's mercy

Is it too late to turn back to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?”

Ezekiel 33:11

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

Not while God is still calling, and He was still calling Israel, even in a funeral song. God takes no pleasure in anyone's death. Turning means hating the evil and loving the good. And for anyone who calls on the Lord through Jesus, there's no “maybe”: he shall be saved.

Question 5 of 6

The day of the LORD

Why did Amos warn people who wanted the day of the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Because they thought it would be a great day for them and a bad day for their enemies. But when God judges sin, He doesn't skip His own people's sin. For them it would be darkness, like a man who escapes a lion and a bear and gets bitten at home. God's delay is patience, so we can repent.

Question 6 of 6

Real worship

What does God want more than songs and sacrifices?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.”

1 Samuel 15:22

Answer

A life that matches the songs. God had commanded the feasts, but He refused them from people who cheated the poor all week. He wants justice and right living that keep flowing like a stream that runs all year, not a burst of religion on the holiday.
