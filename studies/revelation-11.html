Revelation 11

He shall reign for ever

God measures what is His. Two witnesses preach in sackcloth, are killed, lie in the street while the world throws a party, and then stand up and go home to God. Then the seventh trumpet sounds, and heaven announces the end of every kingdom but one.

Six trumpets have blown. The last three were called woes (8:13). Chapter 10 paused before the seventh, and this chapter keeps that pause going a little longer, with a temple measured and two witnesses. Then, at verse 15, the seventh trumpet sounds.

When it does, it's no new disaster. It's an announcement: the world now belongs to Christ. This is the turning point of the book.

Part one

Measured

Verses 1–2

Before trouble comes, God takes a measuring rod to what is His.

A rod for what is His

Ezekiel saw a temple measured (Ezekiel 40–43). Zechariah saw “a man with a measuring line in his hand”, and God said:

“For I, saith the LORD, will be unto her a wall of fire round about, and will be the glory in the midst of her.”

Zechariah 2:5

To measure is to mark off and keep. The outer court is trampled for a set time, but God counts the altar and every worshipper. If you belong to Christ, you are inside the line.

One span of time, said three ways

42 monthsthe holy city trodden (11:2)
1,260 daysthe witnesses preach (11:3)
3½ years“a time, and times, and half a time” (12:14)

Revelation names the same length of time over and over, in months, in days and in “times”. It comes from Daniel, who saw an evil king given power over the saints “until a time and times and the dividing of time” (Daniel 7:25).

Good Bible teachers read it two ways. Many take it as three and a half literal years of trouble at the end. Others see a picture of the whole stretch of history between Christ's first and second coming. We won't draw charts or set dates.

What is plain either way: evil gets a short lease, and God wrote the end date on it before it began. Three and a half is half of seven. It never gets to finish.

Part two

The two witnesses

Verses 3–14

They preach, they fall, and the world throws a party. Then God speaks.

Lamps that run on someone else's oil

In Zechariah 4, two olive trees pour oil straight into a golden lampstand. No one refills it. The angel said why:

“Then he answered and spake unto me, saying, This is the word of the LORD unto Zerubbabel, saying, Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts.”

Zechariah 4:6

The witnesses don't shine on willpower, and neither do you. The oil is God's Spirit, and He lives in every believer.

Like Elijah

Heaven shut

Elijah told King Ahab there would be no rain, and there was none (1 Kings 17:1). James even gives the length: “by the space of three years and six months” (James 5:17). The same three and a half years.

Like Moses

Water to blood

Moses struck the Nile and it turned to blood, the first of the plagues on Egypt (Exodus 7:20). God used him to break a slave master's grip on His people.

Who are the two witnesses? Teachers differ. Some read them as two real prophets God will send at the end; others as a picture of the church's witness to the world. Good Baptist teachers hold both, and we won't guess names.

What is plain: God never leaves the world without a witness. Two was the number the law needed to settle a matter (Deuteronomy 19:15), and Jesus sent His men out “two and two” (Luke 10:1). A witness doesn't work alone.

Not one day early

Read verse 5 and verse 7 together. While they are preaching, nothing can touch them. Only “when they shall have finished” is the beast allowed near them. The enemy did not end their work. He only showed up after God said it was done.

“I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith:”

2 Timothy 4:7

The beast “that ascendeth out of the bottomless pit” comes up from the pit of 9:1–2. We'll meet him properly in chapter 13. Here, the thing to see is that he has to wait his turn.

If you belong to Christ, you are not done until God says you're done. Not the addiction, not the judge, not the man who hates you gets to decide that.

Three names for one city

  1. Sodoma city known for its sin and its contempt for God (Genesis 19)
  2. Egyptthe house of slavery that would not let God's people go
  3. where our Lord was crucifiedthe city that killed its own King

“Spiritually” tells us what the city is like, not just where it is. Many teachers say Jerusalem; others, the whole world that rejects God. Either way, it is where God's messengers get killed.

Verse 10 is the only holiday in Revelation: people “make merry, and shall send gifts one to another” because two preachers are dead. When you slip, some will cheer. Remember who throws that party, and how long it lasts.

Day three and a half

Ezekiel saw dry bones do the same: “the breath came into them, and they lived, and stood up upon their feet” (Ezekiel 37:10).

Then heaven calls, “Come up hither”, the words John heard in 4:1, and they rise in a cloud as their Lord did (Acts 1:9).

The world never gets the last word over God's servants. Laid out in the street, for real or inside? The same Spirit of life can stand you up.

At last, someone looks up

After the sixth trumpet, the survivors “repented not” (9:20). This time the rest “gave glory to the God of heaven.” Teachers differ on whether that was true repentance or only fear. Either way, something moved.

Notice what did it. Not the plagues. It was two dead men standing up. Nothing preaches like a life God has raised. Your changed life is a sermon the people who knew you before can't argue with.

“The second woe is past; and, behold, the third woe cometh quickly” (v. 14). The seventh trumpet is next.

Part three

The seventh trumpet

Verses 15–19

Everyone braced for the last blast. What came was a coronation.

Daniel saw a statue of kingdom after kingdom, smashed by a stone “cut out without hands” that grew into a mountain and filled the earth (Daniel 2:34–35).

“And in the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed: and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever.”

Daniel 2:44

“Are become”

He shall reign for ever and ever.

Heaven speaks as if it has already happened. Christ's open reign on earth comes later in the book (chapters 19–20), but in heaven the matter is settled. The elders already give thanks that “thou hast taken to thee thy great power, and hast reigned.”

Every kingdom that has ever pushed you around is on that statue: the bottle, the street, the gang, the pill, the old man you used to be. Every one of them is “of this world”, and every one of them is coming down.

So whose kingdom are you living in this morning? You can bow the knee to Christ now, gladly, ahead of everyone else (Philippians 2:10–11).

For the angry nations

Wrath and judgment

The dead are judged, and God “shouldest destroy them which destroy the earth.” Those who wreck what God made, and the people in it, answer for it.

For His servants

Reward, small and great

Prophets, saints, anyone who fears His name. “Small” is in the list on purpose: the man nobody noticed, who stayed sober one day at a time, is not forgotten.

The same trumpet means two different things depending on whose side you're on. For a believer, the end of the world is not a threat. It is payday.

At the center: the mercy seat

The ark was a gold-covered chest holding God's law. On its lid, the mercy seat, the high priest sprinkled blood once a year for the people's sins (Leviticus 16). It vanished when Babylon burned the temple.

John sees the real one, which the earthly ark copied (Hebrews 9:24). With lightning and hail all around, heaven shows first the place where blood covers sin. Judgment does not cancel mercy.

You fell, and someone cheered. Name the sin honestly; don't pretend it didn't happen.

“they that dwell upon the earth shall rejoice over them”

God doesn't leave His own lying there. Confess it, get up, and run on His oil, not yours.

“they stood upon their feet”

Every kingdom that ever owned you comes down. The last word is His, and it is “Come up hither.”

“he shall reign for ever and ever”

Today: witnesses go two by two. Call or text one brother from this group before noon, and tell him one thing God did for you yesterday. Be each other's second witness.

Points to remember

  1. Measured“measure the temple of God, and the altar, and them that worship therein” 11:1To measure is to mark off and keep. God counts His people, and the trampling only lasts as long as He allows.
  2. Witnesses“when they shall have finished their testimony” 11:7Their power is God's Spirit, like oil from the olive trees, and nothing can touch them till their work is done.
  3. The street“make merry, and shall send gifts one to another” 11:10The world hates being told the truth and cheers when a witness falls. Its party lasts three and a half days.
  4. Come up hither“the Spirit of life from God entered into them” 11:11God does. He stands His servants up and calls them home while their enemies watch.
  5. The kingdom“he shall reign for ever and ever” 11:15Every kingdom of this world becomes Christ's. Heaven calls it done, and for His servants it means reward.
  6. The ark“there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament” 11:19At the center of heaven, in the middle of the storm, is the mercy seat. God keeps His covenant.

Question 1 of 6

Measured

Why does God measure the temple and the people who worship there?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I, saith the LORD, will be unto her a wall of fire round about, and will be the glory in the midst of her.”

Zechariah 2:5

Answer

Measuring marks something off as God's and keeps it, the way Zechariah saw Jerusalem measured and walled with fire. The outer court is trampled, but only for forty-two months. If you belong to Christ, you're inside the line.

Question 2 of 6

Witnesses

What gives the two witnesses their power, and how long can no one touch them?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then he answered and spake unto me, saying, This is the word of the LORD unto Zerubbabel, saying, Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts.”

Zechariah 4:6

Answer

God's Spirit, pictured as oil flowing from olive trees into lamps that never go out. They act like Moses and Elijah, but not on their own strength, and no one can touch them until they have finished the work God gave them.

Question 3 of 6

The street

Why does the world celebrate when God's witnesses fall?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the truth torments people who don't want it. A city that is spiritually Sodom and Egypt killed its own Lord, and it is glad to see His witnesses silenced. When you fall, some will cheer. But their party only lasts three and a half days.

Question 4 of 6

Come up hither

Who gets the last word over God's servants?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God does. The beast killed them and the world partied, but God breathed life into them, stood them up and called them home in a cloud while their enemies watched. Death is not the end of the story for anyone who belongs to Him, and neither is yesterday.

Question 5 of 6

The kingdom

What happens when the seventh trumpet sounds?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.”

Luke 1:33

Answer

Heaven announces that every kingdom of this world now belongs to Christ, and He will reign for ever: Daniel's stone becomes a mountain. For those who reject Him it means wrath; for His servants, small and great, reward.

Question 6 of 6

The ark

What does John see when the temple in heaven is opened?

What verses tell us about this?

“For Christ is not entered into the holy places made with hands, which are the figures of the true; but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us:”

Hebrews 9:24

Answer

The ark of His testament, the real one the earthly ark was copied from. Its lid is the mercy seat, where blood covered sin. Even with thunder and hail all around, the center of heaven is where God meets sinners in mercy.
