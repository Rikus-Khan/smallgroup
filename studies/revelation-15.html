Revelation 15

The song on the far shore

Before the last plagues fall, John sees the end of the story for God's people: standing safe on a sea of glass, singing about Him.

John is an old prisoner on the island of Patmos, about AD 95, writing to seven churches under pressure. Chapters 12 to 14 pulled back the curtain: the dragon, the beast, and the Lamb standing on mount Sion with His people.

Now the last set of seven begins. Chapter 15 is short: eight verses. It is the calm before the bowls are poured, and the first thing God shows John is not the judgment. It is His people, safe, and singing.

Each set reaches further than the last. The seals touch a fourth part, the trumpets a third. The bowls (the vials, 15:7) hold nothing back. In them is filled up the wrath of God.

Bible teachers differ on how the three fit. Some read them one after another; others see the same days told three times, each sharper. Both agree on the plain thing: God warns in part before He judges in full.

What “last” means

God's wrath isn't a temper. It is His settled, holy opposition to evil: to every lie, every abuse, every innocent life thrown away. A judge who never judged anything would not be good.

Filled up means finished. Evil will not run forever. There is a last, and after it, God makes all things new (21:5).

Where are believers when the bowls pour?

Good Baptist teachers answer this differently, and they can sit at the same table.

  • Many teach the church is caught up first1 Thessalonians 4:17; those who stand here believed during those days
  • Others teach believers go through, kept by Godas Israel was kept in Goshen while Egypt was struck

“For God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ,”

1 Thessalonians 5:9

Whatever the order of events, that verse holds. The man who has trusted Jesus is not on the list for wrath. He was taken off it at the cross.

Part two

The song on the far shore

Verses 2–4

God's people have stood on the far shore singing before. They will again.

They have stood here before

Israel stood at the Red sea with Pharaoh's army behind them. God opened the sea, they crossed on dry ground, and the water came back over the chariots. On the far shore, Moses led them in a song (Exodus 15).

Now look at John's sea. It is the calm sea of glass before the throne (4:6), now mingled with fire: judgment about to fall. And God's people aren't drowning in it. They stand on it, with harps.

That is how the story of God's people always ends: through the water, and singing on the far side.

How they won

The beast had the power to say who could buy or sell (13:17). These people had none of it, and many paid with their lives. Yet God calls them the winners.

“And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.”

Revelation 12:11

They didn't win by being stronger, but by the blood of the Lamb and by refusing to bow. Some of us fought every fight the hard way and lost anyway. This victory is His, and He shares it.

“For whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith.”

1 John 5:4

Two songs, one God

Exodus 15:11 · the song of Moses

Who is like unto thee, O LORD, among the gods? who is like thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?

Revelation 15:3–4 · the song of the Lamb

Great and marvellous are thy works, Lord God Almighty; just and true are thy ways, thou King of saints. Who shall not fear thee, O Lord, and glorify thy name? for thou only art holy.

Moses sang about one rescue: out of Egypt, through the sea. The Lamb's song is about the bigger one. Back in Egypt the blood of a lamb on the door kept death away (Exodus 12:13). Jesus is that Lamb, and His blood keeps a man from the judgment itself.

Same God, same kind of song, a thousand years and more apart. He hasn't changed how He saves: He goes ahead, opens the way, and brings His people through.

Not one word about themselves

“Great and marvellous are thy works, Lord God Almighty; just and true are thy ways, thou King of saints. Who shall not fear thee, O Lord, and glorify thy name? for thou only art holy: for all nations shall come and worship before thee; for thy judgments are made manifest.”

Revelation 15:3–4

His works, His ways, His name.

These people had been through the worst. On the far shore they don't tell their story. They tell His. Your regrets won't be the last word on your life either. When you get there, you'll be singing about what He did.

Who shall not fear thee?

The song borrows from older ones. David sang it first:

“All nations whom thou hast made shall come and worship before thee, O Lord; and shall glorify thy name.”

Psalm 86:9

To fear God here is to take Him seriously: awe, respect, bowing low. Thou only art holy: nobody else is completely clean, not the preacher, not the saint, not us. That's why every man needs the Lamb.

All nations: the song is wide open. People from every kind of background, every kind of past, will stand on that sea. The door is open to whosoever.

Part three

The temple filled with smoke

Verses 5–8

Seven angels come out of God's own house, and the door closes behind them.

Out of the tabernacle of the testimony

The testimony was God's law, the two tablets Moses kept in the ark:

“And thou shalt put into the ark the testimony which I shall give thee.”

Exodus 25:16

So the angels come out from beside God's own law. Judgment doesn't come from a bad mood. It comes from His holiness, measured by His word.

They are dressed like priests, in pure and white linen with golden girdles (belts), the way John saw Jesus in chapter 1:13. They serve Him.

Next (verse 7), one of the four beasts, the four living creatures around the throne (4:6), hands them seven golden vials.

A vial here isn't a small bottle. It is a wide, shallow bowl, the kind used in the temple. Built to be poured.

The same golden bowls

In chapter 5 the golden vials were full of odours, which are the prayers of saints. Under the altar the martyrs cried, “How long, O Lord?”

Now golden vials come out full of judgment. The bowls of wrath are God's answer to His people's prayers. No prayer for justice is wasted, even one that waits years.

No man could enter. Today the door is open.

It happened at the tabernacle and at Solomon's temple: God's glory filled the place like a cloud, and even Moses was not able to enter (Exodus 40:35). Here the smoke shuts the temple till the seven plagues … were fulfilled. There's a day when the time of waiting is over.

That day isn't today. Today the door stands open:

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;”

Hebrews 10:19–20

Sing on this side of the sea

The far shore is coming. But you don't have to wait for it to start the song. Some of the best singing in the Bible happened in a cell:

“And at midnight Paul and Silas prayed, and sang praises unto God: and the prisoners heard them.”

Acts 16:25

And the prisoners heard them. Men who are still in the middle of it are listening to how you handle yours.

Today, before the day gets loud: name three things God has done for you, out loud, and thank Him for them. Not what you did. What He did. That's the song of Moses and the Lamb, started early.

  1. Last“In them is filled up the wrath of God” 15:1God warns in part before He judges in full, and evil won't run forever. In Christ, a man is saved from wrath.
  2. Victory“Them that had gotten the victory over the beast” 15:2They won by the blood of the Lamb and by refusing to bow, not by being stronger.
  3. Song“The song of Moses … and the song of the Lamb” 15:3The same God who brought Israel through the sea brings His people through. The song is all about Him.
  4. Holy“For thou only art holy” 15:4He alone is holy, and every nation will bow. That's why every man needs the Lamb.
  5. Temple“No man was able to enter into the temple” 15:8A day comes when the waiting is over. Today the way into God's presence is open by the blood of Jesus.

Question 1 of 5

Last

Why are these called the seven last plagues?

What verses tell us about this?

“Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him.”

Romans 5:9

Answer

Because in them the wrath of God is filled up: finished. The seals and trumpets struck part of the earth; the bowls hold nothing back. Evil won't run forever. And the man justified by Jesus' blood is saved from that wrath.

Question 2 of 5

Victory

Who stands on the sea of glass, and how did they win?

What verses tell us about this?

“And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.”

Revelation 12:11

Answer

Those who got the victory over the beast, his image, his mark and his name. They won by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony, refusing to bow even at the cost of their lives. Now they stand safe before God with harps.

Question 3 of 5

Song

Whose song do they sing, and who is it about?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then sang Moses and the children of Israel this song unto the LORD, and spake, saying, I will sing unto the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously: the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea.”

Exodus 15:1

Answer

The song of Moses and the song of the Lamb. Moses sang on the far shore of the Red sea; the Lamb's people sing on the sea of glass. And it is all about God: His works, His ways, His name, His judgments. Not one word about themselves.

Question 4 of 5

Holy

Why should every man fear God and glorify His name?

What verses tell us about this?

“All nations whom thou hast made shall come and worship before thee, O Lord; and shall glorify thy name.”

Psalm 86:9

Answer

Because He alone is holy, His works are great, His ways are just and true, and His judgments will be seen by all. Every nation will come and worship. Nobody else is clean, so every man needs the Lamb.

Question 5 of 5

Temple

Why could no man enter the temple?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Moses was not able to enter into the tent of the congregation, because the cloud abode thereon, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle.”

Exodus 40:35

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus,”

Hebrews 10:19

Answer

The temple was filled with smoke from God's glory and power, until the seven plagues were finished. A day is coming when the time of waiting is over. But today the way into God's presence is open, by the blood of Jesus.
