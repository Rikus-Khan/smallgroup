2 Corinthians 1

The God of all comfort

Paul's most personal letter begins with a man who nearly died, and who found that the comfort God gave him was meant to be handed on.

The second letter to the Corinthians

“Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, and Timothy our brother, unto the church of God which is at Corinth, with all the saints which are in all Achaia”

Written from
Macedonia, in northern Greece. Many think Philippi. (2:13; 7:5)
When
about AD 56
To whom

Why
Paul had been worried sick about this church. Titus had just brought good news, and Paul pours out his heart.

This church has been through a lot with Paul. He wrote them one letter (1 Corinthians), made a visit that many think went badly, then wrote another letter so hard that it cost him tears. Then he waited to hear how they took it.

Good news came back. This letter is a man talking with friends he nearly lost, and it is the most honest Paul ever gets about weakness, suffering and comfort.

The whole letter on one line

2 Corinthians has three parts, and they feel different. We start with the warmest.

  1. Chapters 1–7Paul's heart: comfort, forgiveness, a new covenant, treasure in jars of clay, being reconciled to God
  2. Chapters 8–9An offering for poor believers in Jerusalem, and cheerful giving
  3. Chapters 10–13Paul answers the men who attack him, and boasts about his weaknesses

Through all three runs one idea: God's power shows up in weak people. If you feel weak today, you are in good company.

Part one

The God of all comfort

Verses 1–7

Paul has been through the fire. The first thing he does is bless God.

To the church, and to the whole province

It is for one church, but also for “all the saints which are in all Achaia”, the whole province of southern Greece, including Athens and the harbor town of Cenchrea (Romans 16:1).

Saints means holy ones, believers set apart for God. This was the same noisy church with all its problems, and Paul still calls them saints. God names you by what He has made you, not by your worst week.

Comfort is meant to be passed on

Think of the man who helped you when you were low. He could, because someone had done it for him. God doesn't comfort you only for you. He builds a line.

The next link might be you, today. Paul doesn't say after you're fixed. The worst stretch of your life is what lets you reach the man who's in it now.

“Comfort” means more than “there, there”

παράκλησις said paraklesis “called alongside” to help, encourage, strengthen

The word family turns up about ten times in verses 3 to 7. It's not a pat on the shoulder. It's someone coming alongside and taking some of the weight. Jesus used it for the Holy Spirit, the Comforter (John 14:16).

“In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul.”

Psalm 94:19

As it abounds, so does the comfort

Paul says it twice. “As the sufferings of Christ abound in us, so our consolation also aboundeth by Christ.” The harder it gets, the more comfort there is. God never lets the trouble get bigger than His help.

The “sufferings of Christ” are the troubles that come from belonging to Him. But the principle reaches your trouble too.

It doesn't promise the trouble goes away. It promises you won't carry it alone, and “our hope of you is stedfast” (v. 7): you will come through.

Part two

Despaired even of life

Verses 8–14

An apostle admits he came to the end of himself, and says what he found there.

Why God lets us hit the bottom

  • “in ourselves”: my own strength“above strength” (v. 8). It was gone.
  • “in ourselves”: my own planHe had run out of moves.
  • “in ourselves”: my own willpowerthe “sentence of death in ourselves”
  • “but in God which raiseth the dead”the only One left to lean on

Paul doesn't say what the trouble in Asia was (some think the riot at Ephesus, Acts 19). He tells us what it was for: to break his trust in himself so it could rest on God.

Many men in recovery know this. “I can handle it” stops working, and it feels terrible. It is also the first honest step toward the One who raises the dead.

Delivered, delivers, will deliver

  1. yesterday“delivered us”from so great a death
  2. today“doth deliver”this morning, this hour
  3. ahead“will yet deliver us”“in whom we trust”

Three tenses in one verse. God saved Paul in the past, saves him now, and he trusts Him for what's coming. Your story can be told the same way. You may have fallen yesterday, and God is still in the delivering business today. Past failure isn't the last tense.

Paul asks for help

The great apostle, who has just said he trusts God to raise the dead, adds: “Ye also helping together by prayer for us.” He asks a church he has argued with to pray for him. God uses the prayers of ordinary people in the rescue.

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Most men who fall again were alone when it happened, and quiet about it. Today, text one man in the group: “I'm having a hard day. Pray for me.” That is trusting God and trusting His people at the same time.

A clear conscience

Paul has enemies in Corinth who say he is slippery, a man who says one thing and means another. His answer isn't a speech. It's his conscience: “in simplicity and godly sincerity.” Simplicity means nothing hidden. Godly sincerity means pure, with nothing mixed in.

Notice the last part, “by the grace of God.” He doesn't claim he's perfect. He says that whatever clean living he has is grace. A man with a record can still say, “I'm trying to live in the open now, and by God's grace I'm doing better.”

Verse 13 sets up the rest of the chapter: “we write none other things unto you, than what ye read.” What Paul says is what he means. Now he has to explain why he changed his travel plans.

Part three

Yea, and Amen

Verses 15–24

Paul's plans changed. God's promises never do.

Why Paul changed his plans

His critics said “he says yes and means no”: he promised to visit and didn't. A man can change his route without changing his word.

The plan (straight across the sea): Corinth, then Macedonia, then Corinth again, then Judaea. Two visits would be a “second benefit”.

What happened (the long way round): Troas and Macedonia first, and Corinth had to wait. His reason (v. 23): “to spare you I came not as yet unto Corinth.” He didn't want to arrive angry.

Every promise of God: YES

Forgiveness1 John 1:9YES
He won't leave youHebrews 13:5YES
A new heartEzekiel 36:26YES
No condemnationRomans 8:1YES
Strength and helpIsaiah 41:10YES
The Spirit in you2 Corinthians 1:22YES

Paul's point: God doesn't say “yes and no.” Everything He promised is answered “in him”, in Jesus. Christ is God's yes. Where He is, the promise stands.

Amen is a Hebrew word that means firm, so be it. God says yes. We say amen: I'm standing on that. These promises are about forgiveness, His presence and a new life. They don't promise an easy life, but they are rock-solid.

Sealed, with a down payment

The Greek word is arrabon: a down payment, the first money that guarantees the rest. God put the Holy Spirit in every believer from the day he trusts Jesus. He is the first part of the inheritance and the promise of the rest.

A seal was a mark of ownership, and it kept the thing safe until the owner came for it. You belong to Him, and He will finish what He began. Today's failure doesn't cancel the deposit.

Helpers, not bosses

“dominion over your faith” → “helpers of your joy” “Dominion” means to lord it over someone. Paul says that's not his job.

Paul had real authority as an apostle, but he refuses to run their faith for them. “By faith ye stand.” Each man stands on his own trust in Christ. The best a pastor, a sponsor or a friend can do is help him stand, and add to his joy.

That's the kind of help to look for, and the kind to be. Not a man who controls you or does your thinking, but one who stands next to you and helps you stand.

Points to remember

  1. The God of all comfort“The Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort” 1:3He comes alongside us in every trouble, and His comfort grows with the trouble.
  2. Passing it on“That we may be able to comfort them which are in any trouble” 1:4The comfort you receive is meant to be handed to the next man.
  3. Despair“That we should not trust in ourselves, but in God which raiseth the dead” 1:9Hitting the bottom ends self-trust and starts God-trust. He delivered, delivers, and will deliver.
  4. God's promises“All the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen” 1:20Every promise is answered yes in Jesus.
  5. The Spirit“Sealed us, and given the earnest of the Spirit in our hearts” 1:22The Holy Spirit in you is God's down payment and His seal. You are His.
  6. Not alone“Ye also helping together by prayer for us” 1:11God uses other believers' prayers and company. Don't fight alone.

Question 1 of 6

The God of all comfort

Who is “the God of all comfort”, and what does He do in our troubles?

What verses tell us about this?

“In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul.”

Psalm 94:19

Answer

He is the Father of Jesus, the Father of mercies, and the One who comforts us in all our tribulation. He comes alongside us, and as the trouble grows, His comfort grows with it.

Question 2 of 6

Passing it on

What are we meant to do with the comfort God gives us?

What verses tell us about this?

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Answer

Pass it on. God comforts us so that we can comfort others in any trouble, with the same comfort we received. What you've been through is what qualifies you to help the man who's going through it now.

Question 3 of 6

Despair

What did Paul learn when he “despaired even of life”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That he could not trust himself, only God, who raises the dead. His despair broke his self-trust and pointed him to the One who had delivered him, delivers him, and will deliver him again.

Question 4 of 6

God's promises

What does Paul say about all the promises of God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Answer

Every one of them is yes in Christ. God doesn't say yes and no. Jesus is His yes, so the promises of forgiveness, His presence and a new life stand firm, and we answer with amen.

Question 5 of 6

The Spirit

What has God given believers as “the earnest”?

What verses tell us about this?

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise, Which is the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, unto the praise of his glory.”

Ephesians 1:13–14

Answer

The Holy Spirit, in our hearts: a down payment on everything God has promised, and a seal that says we belong to Him.

Question 6 of 6

Not alone

What part do other believers play when a man is in trouble?

What verses tell us about this?

“For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:10

Answer

They pray, they come alongside, and they help him stand. God uses ordinary believers in the rescue. So don't go through it alone: ask for prayer today, and be that help for someone else.
