Part one

A teachable heart

Verses 1–12

Wise men listen. Scorners don't. Watch what each one ends up with.

How to read a proverb

Most of this chapter is two-line sayings. The second line usually turns on the word “but” and shows the opposite road.

Line one

One road

“A wise son heareth his father's instruction:”

Line two, after “but”

The other road

“but a scorner heareth not rebuke.”

A proverb tells you how life usually works under God. It's wisdom, not a guarantee with a date on it. Read them slowly, and ask: which line am I living in?

Two kinds of listeners

The wise son

“heareth instruction”

He knows he doesn't know it all yet. Correction stings, but he takes it, and he grows.

The scorner

“heareth not rebuke”

He has an answer for everything and a sneer for anyone who tries to help. Nobody can tell him anything.

Some of us never had a father worth listening to. But you have one now. Your heavenly Father speaks through His Word, and through older men in the faith who will tell you the truth. The question is whether you'll hear it.

Guard your mouth, and you guard your life.

“Keepeth” is a watchman's word: to guard, to keep watch over. The man who “openeth wide his lips” says whatever comes up, whenever it comes up.

Think how many jobs, marriages, and court dates went wrong because of one thing said in anger, or one lie that needed three more lies to hold it up.

“Set a watch, O LORD, before my mouth; keep the door of my lips.”

Psalm 141:3

Wanting isn't working

The sluggard

how much he wantswhat he has

The diligent

how much he wantswhat he has

The sluggard isn't short on desire. He wants plenty. He just never does anything about it. In Bible times, “made fat” meant well fed and provided for.

The same is true of a new life. Wanting to be sober, wanting to be a better father, is a good start. The diligent man does the small, daily, boring things that get him there.

Faking it

He→maketh himself rich→yet hath nothingThe show: the car, the cash, the image. Nothing underneath it, and maybe a lot of debt.
He→maketh himself poor→yet hath great richesNo show at all. But he has something real, and he doesn't need to prove it.

A lot of us lived the first line for years. Looking good on the outside while everything inside was falling apart. God isn't impressed by the show. He's after what's real.

Verse 8 adds a twist: a rich man may have to pay a ransom to keep his life, while nobody bothers to threaten a poor man. Riches bring their own troubles.

Where fights come from

  • the moneyit's often what we argue about, but it isn't the root
  • the other guyhe may be wrong, but that's not why it blew up
  • a bad dayit makes things worse, but it doesn't start them
  • “Only by pride”somebody has to be right, somebody has to win

“Contention” is strife, a running fight. Look under any long feud and you'll find pride somewhere. The way out is in the second line: “with the well advised is wisdom.” Humble men ask for advice and take it.

Fast money, slow money

“Vanity” means emptiness, something with nothing real inside. Money from a hustle, a scheme, a lucky night, or a deal that hurt somebody comes fast and leaks out fast.

The man who gathers by labour builds a handful at a time. It's slow, and it's not exciting. But it lasts, because he is being built along with it.

When hope is put off

Day 1hoping
Month 3still waiting
Year 1heart sick
Year 2still hoping
It comesa tree of life

“Deferred” means put off, pushed back. Waiting on a court date, on seeing your kids, on a family that will trust you again. The Bible is honest: that kind of waiting makes the heart sick. You're not weak for feeling it.

And when the thing finally comes, it's like “a tree of life,” the tree from the garden of Eden. Life comes back into you.

“And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.”

Romans 5:5

Part two

Who you walk with

Verses 13–25

The friends you keep, the family you leave behind, and a Father who corrects.

The hard road

Nobody in this room has to be talked into this one. We lived it. Sin promised freedom and fun. It handed us a road that was rough, lonely, and expensive.

Verse 14 shows the other road: God's teaching is “a fountain of life” that keeps a man out of “the snares of death,” the hidden traps set for an animal that never sees them coming.

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.”

Matthew 11:28–30

Why it's so hard to quit

“The desire accomplished”→is sweet to the soulGetting what you crave feels good. That's the hook.
“To depart from evil”→is abomination to fools“Abomination” is something you can't stand. To the fool, walking away feels worse than staying.

That's addiction from the inside. The fool doesn't just do evil. He loves it, so leaving it feels like losing a part of himself.

Here's the good news: God doesn't just tell us to quit. He changes what we love. “If any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new” (2 Corinthians 5:17).

You become who you walk with

“Walketh” is a daily thing, not one meeting. Spend your days with wise men and their ways rub off on you, without your even noticing.

It works the other way too. A “companion” of fools is a buddy, the guys you run with. Nobody plans to be destroyed. They just keep the same company.

Who are you walking with?

“Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.”

1 Corinthians 15:33

In recovery, you hear it put plainly: change your playmates and your playgrounds. The Bible said it first.

That can mean some hard goodbyes. You can love your old friends and pray for them without spending your nights with them.

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

And it means finding new friends on purpose: men who'll tell you the truth, a church, this group. And the best Friend of all walks with you every day. Christ “of God is made unto us wisdom” (1 Corinthians 1:30).

What will you leave behind?

your grandfather→ your father→ you → your children → their children

An inheritance isn't only money. It's a name, a faith, habits, a father who showed up sober. Many of us inherited chaos: drinking, anger, absence, going back generations.

You can be the man where the line turns. What you do with God from today on will reach grandchildren you haven't met yet.

Verse 23 adds: a poor man's field can grow plenty of food, but it can be lost “for want of judgment,” through foolishness or injustice. Good ground still needs wise hands.

What “the rod” means

The Hebrew word is shebet: a rod or staff. It's the shepherd's rod in Psalm 23 (“thy rod and thy staff they comfort me”) and the king's scepter. It stands for a father's authority, used to guide and correct.

  • permission to hurt a childrage, cruelty, and abuse are sin, never love
  • a father who looks away“spareth” means holds back; the verse calls that hate
  • correction, early and with love“betimes” means early, and with care

Christians disagree about the best ways to discipline children. The point of the verse is plain: love doesn't look away. It gets involved, early, before a small wrong becomes a way of life.

If your father got this wrong

A lot of us were raised by a father who was never there, or one who “corrected” in a rage. Neither is what verse 24 describes. God's word speaks to fathers too:

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

And here is the good news. You have a Father who does this right. When He corrects you, it's proof you belong to Him:

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

Points to remember

  1. Friends“He that walketh with wise men shall be wise” 13:20You become like the men you walk with. Choose them on purpose.
  2. A teachable heart“A wise son heareth his father's instruction” 13:1The wise man still listens. The scorner has stopped.
  3. Words“He that keepeth his mouth keepeth his life” 13:3Guard your mouth, and you guard your life.
  4. Hope“Hope deferred maketh the heart sick” 13:12Waiting hurts, and it's all right to say so. Hope in God doesn't put us to shame.
  5. Discipline“He that loveth him chasteneth him betimes” 13:24Love corrects early and carefully. Your heavenly Father does that for you.
  6. Work and money“He that gathereth by labour shall increase” 13:11Honest work, a handful at a time, outlasts the quick hustle.

Question 1 of 6

Friends

Why does the company we keep matter so much?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.”

1 Corinthians 15:33

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9

Answer

Because we become like the people we spend our days with. Walk with wise men and you grow wise. Run with fools and you get pulled down with them, even if you never meant to. Choosing godly friends isn't snobbery. It's survival, and it's how God grows us.

Question 2 of 6

A teachable heart

What separates a wise son from a scorner?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not how smart he is. The wise son listens. He takes instruction and correction, even when it stings. The scorner has decided nobody can tell him anything. One keeps growing, and the other stays stuck.

Question 3 of 6

Words

What does a man gain by keeping his mouth?

What verses tell us about this?

“Set a watch, O LORD, before my mouth; keep the door of my lips.”

Psalm 141:3

Answer

He keeps his life. Guarding your words protects your job, your family, your freedom, and your witness. A loose mouth brings destruction. And we don't have to guard it alone: we can ask the Lord to stand watch at the door of our lips.

Question 4 of 6

Hope

What happens to the heart when hope is put off?

What verses tell us about this?

“And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us.”

Romans 5:5

Answer

It gets sick. Long waiting wears a man down, and the Bible doesn't pretend otherwise. But when the thing hoped for comes, it's like a tree of life. And hope placed in God never ends in shame, because His love is already in our hearts by the Holy Spirit.

Question 5 of 6

Discipline

What does real love do when a son goes wrong?

What verses tell us about this?

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

Answer

It corrects him, early and carefully. Love doesn't look the other way, and it doesn't explode in anger either. That's how God treats us: when He corrects us, it's because we're His sons and He wants good fruit in our lives.

Question 6 of 6

Work and money

Why does honest gain last longer than fast money?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Fast money is “gotten by vanity”: there's nothing real under it, so it drains away. Honest work builds slowly, a handful at a time, and it builds the man too. A good man's steady work can even bless his grandchildren.
