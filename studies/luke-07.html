Luke 7

Thy faith hath saved thee

A Roman officer trusts a word, a widow gets her son back, John wonders, and a woman who was a sinner goes home in peace.

Part one

So great faith

Verses 1–10

A Roman officer in Capernaum, a dying servant, and a man who knew what a word of command can do.

An unlikely good man

A centurion was a Roman army officer over about a hundred soldiers. He was a Gentile, a soldier of the power that ruled the Jews. Most Jews kept their distance from men like him.

But this one was different. He loved the Jewish nation and paid to build Capernaum's synagogue. And he cared about a servant, a slave most masters would have counted as property. This one was “dear unto him.”

He didn't come himself. He sent the town's Jewish elders, and they went to bat for him: “he was worthy.” Hold on to that word.

Worthy, or not worthy?

The elders said

“He was worthy”

Look at his record: he loves our nation, he built our synagogue. He's earned it.

The centurion said

“I am not worthy”

Not worthy to have You under my roof, not even worthy to come to You myself.

The good man with the good record knew he had no claim on Jesus. That's where faith starts. Nobody comes to Christ on his résumé. We come as men who don't deserve it, and He comes anyway.

He may also have known that a Jew who entered a Gentile's house became ceremonially unclean. He wanted to spare Jesus the trouble.

A soldier understands orders

Romegives the orderthe centurion“Go,” “Come”his soldiersobey
the centurion“Do this”his servantdoes it
Jesus“say in a word”the sicknessmust go

He reasoned like a soldier. My orders work because Rome stands behind me. Your word works because God stands behind You. You don't need to be in the room. Just say it.

Jesus marvelled

Only twice do the gospels say Jesus marvelled: at this outsider's faith, and at His hometown's unbelief (Mark 6:6).

The centurion never saw Jesus. He never touched Him. He trusted His word, and the servant was healed from a distance. We're in the same place today. We don't see Him either. We have His word, and His word is enough.

“So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

Romans 10:17

Part two

Young man, arise

Verses 11–17

Two crowds meet at a town gate. One is following Jesus. The other is following a coffin.

At the gate of Nain

Nain was a small town in the hills of lower Galilee, a long day's walk from Capernaum, about 25 miles.

Just over the hill was Shunem, where the prophet Elisha once raised a mother's only son (2 Kings 4). The people of Nain knew that story.

A funeral was coming out of the gate. In that climate the dead were buried the same day, outside the town, carried on an open bier, a stretcher, with the family and mourners walking behind.

Life meets death at the gate

Jesus touched the bier. By the law, touching the dead made a man unclean. But when Jesus touched death, death didn't spread to Him. Life went the other way. “And he that was dead sat up, and began to speak.”

He saw her

Nobody asked Jesus for anything. No one in this funeral had faith that He would act. He saw a widow burying her only son, with no husband, no son, and no one left to provide for her, and He was moved.

“Weep not” is cruel from anyone else. From Jesus, it's a promise, because He was about to give her a reason to stop.

Some of our mothers have cried over us like that, sure they'd lost us. Jesus still gives sons back to their mothers. “He delivered him to his mother.”

“A great prophet is risen”

The crowd thought of Elijah. He too raised a widow's only son, and Luke uses the very same words.

1 Kings 17:23, Elijah

And Elijah took the child, and brought him down out of the chamber into the house, and delivered him unto his mother: and Elijah said, See, thy son liveth.

Luke 7:15, Jesus

And he that was dead sat up, and began to speak. And he delivered him to his mother.

But Elijah stretched himself on the child three times and cried to the LORD. Jesus just spoke. He is more than a prophet. In Him, “God hath visited his people”: God Himself had come to help.

Part three

Art thou he that should come?

Verses 18–35

The greatest of the prophets, sitting in a prison, sends Jesus a question.

A question from prison

Herod had locked John up (3:20), probably in his fortress at Machaerus, east of the Dead Sea. He heard what Jesus was doing, but not what he expected: judgment on the wicked, freedom for prisoners like him.

So he sent two of his disciples a long way north with one question: are You the One, or do we wait for someone else?

The man who had pointed at Jesus and said, “Behold the Lamb of God,” now had doubts. Doubt can come to the best of men, especially in a dark place.

Jesus answered with evidence

He didn't scold John. He let the messengers watch for an hour, then sent them back with a list John would recognise from Isaiah, the signs of the Messiah:

  1. ✓the eyes of the blind shall be openedIsaiah 35:57:21
  2. ✓Then shall the lame man leap as an hartIsaiah 35:65:25
  3. ✓the ears of the deaf shall be unstoppedIsaiah 35:57:22
  4. ✓to preach good tidings unto the meekIsaiah 61:14:18; 6:20

Plus a leper cleansed (5:13) and a dead man raised at Nain, only days before. “Blessed is he, whosoever shall not be offended in me”: happy is the man who doesn't stumble because Jesus works differently than he expected.

What did you go out to see?

  • A reed shaken with the wind?John never bent to please the crowd.
  • A man clothed in soft raiment?He wore camel's hair and lived rough.
  • A prophet, and much morethe messenger sent ahead of the Lord Himself

Notice when Jesus said this: right after John's messengers left. John's doubts didn't lower him in Jesus' eyes. Jesus defended him in public. He does the same for His struggling people.

Greater than John?

Jesus quotes Malachi: God would send a messenger to clear the road before the Lord came. John was that man, the last and greatest of the Old Testament prophets.

“Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me: and the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, whom ye delight in: behold, he shall come, saith the LORD of hosts.”

Malachi 3:1

So how is the least believer greater? Not in character. In privilege. John pointed to the cross from a distance and died before it. We stand on the far side of the cross and the empty tomb, with the Holy Spirit living in us. The weakest believer in this room has seen more of God's plan than John ever did.

Children in the marketplace

Picture kids in the town square playing pretend. Some want to play wedding and pipe a tune. Others want to play funeral and wail. Some kids won't play either game. That's this generation, Jesus says.

John the Baptist

“He hath a devil”

He came fasting, with no bread and no wine. Too strict, they said. Something's wrong with him.

The Son of man

“A gluttonous man”

He came eating and drinking at people's tables. Too loose, they said: a glutton and a winebibber, a heavy drinker.

Jesus ate ordinary meals of His day, wine included. The Bible never shows Him drunk or careless. The charge was a smear by men who had already decided. People who won't come will always find a reason.

The insult that's good news

They meant it as a slur. “A friend of publicans and sinners.” Tax collectors, drunks, prostitutes, men with records: Jesus ate with them and they felt welcome around Him.

If you've been one of those men, that's the best line in the chapter. He is still that friend.

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

And note the plain people, the publicans, “justified God”: they agreed with God's verdict on them, repented, and were baptized by John. The Pharisees and lawyers, experts in the law of Moses, said no, and missed what God had for them.

Part four

She loved much

Verses 36–50

A dinner at a Pharisee's house, and a guest nobody invited.

At His feet

Guests didn't sit on chairs. “Sat down to meat” means they lay on couches around a low table, on one elbow, feet stretched out behind.

Townspeople could wander in to watch a big dinner. She slipped in, stood behind Jesus, and broke down. Her tears fell on His dusty feet. She wiped them with her hair and poured out her alabaster box, a costly jar of perfume.

The whole town knew what she was. She came anyway. Don't let shame keep you away from Jesus either.

Simon's silent thought

Simon, to himselfThis man, if he were a prophet, would have known who and what manner of woman this is that toucheth him: for she is a sinner.

Jesus, out loudSimon, I have somewhat to say unto thee.

Simon thought Jesus didn't know who the woman was. Jesus knew exactly who she was, and He knew what Simon was thinking too. He was a prophet, and more.

Simon had sorted the room into sinners and good people, and put himself in the second group. Jesus is about to show him he's a debtor too.

Two debtors

A penny was about a day's pay for a working man.

Frankly means freely, as a gift. Big debt or small, neither man could pay, and both were forgiven the same way. Simon was the fifty-pence man. He still couldn't pay.

Simon had to answer: the one forgiven most loves most. He walked right into it.

Seest thou this woman?

A good host gave a guest water to wash the road dust off his feet, greeted him with a kiss, and put a little oil on his head. Simon skipped all three. She more than made up for it.

Simonno water for My feetno kissno oil for My head

Simon gave Jesus less than ordinary manners. She gave Him everything she had. The difference wasn't how bad they were. It was how forgiven they knew they were.

Which came first?

Did her love earn her forgiveness? No. The story of the debtors shows the order: the debt is forgiven first, then the love comes. Her love was the evidence. And verse 50 says what saved her: faith.

  1. She believedtrusted Jesus to receive her
  2. Forgiven“which are many”
  3. She loved muchtears, kisses, perfume
  4. He said it out loud“Thy sins are forgiven”
  5. Go in peacesent home whole

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Go in peace

The guests muttered, “Who is this that forgiveth sins also?” Good question. Only God can forgive sins (5:21). Jesus did, with a word, because He is God.

Jesus said it out loud, in front of the people who had judged her, so she would never have to wonder. She walked out of that house the same woman everyone knew, but forgiven, saved, and at peace with God. Her past was real. It didn't get the last word.

If you've trusted Christ, the same is true of you. Your sins, which are many, are forgiven. Go in peace.

“Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:”

Romans 5:1

Points to remember

  1. Faith“Say in a word” 7:7He knew he wasn't worthy, and he trusted Jesus' word alone.

  3. Doubt“Tell John what things ye have seen” 7:22Jesus meets honest doubt with evidence, not scolding.
  4. Critics“A friend of publicans and sinners” 7:34People who won't come will always find a reason. Jesus is still the sinner's friend.

Question 1 of 6

Faith

What was so great about the centurion's faith?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He knew he had no claim on Jesus: “I am not worthy.” And he believed Jesus' word alone was enough, with no visit and no touch. A Gentile soldier trusted Jesus more than Israel did.

Question 2 of 6

Compassion

What moved Jesus at the gate of Nain?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A widow burying her only son. Nobody asked Him. He saw her grief and had compassion. He touched the bier, raised the young man, and gave him back to his mother.

Question 3 of 6

Doubt

What did Jesus do with John's doubts?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He didn't scold him. He sent back evidence: the very signs Isaiah had promised. Then He praised John in public as more than a prophet. Jesus is patient with honest doubt and answers it.

Question 4 of 6

Critics

Why did people find fault with both John and Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because they had already rejected God's counsel. John was too strict for them and Jesus too free. When a man doesn't want to come, he will find a reason. But the insult was true in the best way: Jesus is the friend of sinners.

Question 5 of 6

Love

Why did the woman love Jesus so much?

What verses tell us about this?

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

Answer

Because she knew how much she had been forgiven. Her sins were many, and Jesus forgave them all. A man who knows he was forgiven a mountain of debt can't help loving the One who cancelled it.

Question 6 of 6

Saved

What saved the woman who was a sinner?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Her faith in Jesus. Not her tears, not her perfume, and not her love. Those came from being forgiven. She trusted Him, He forgave her, and He sent her home in peace.
