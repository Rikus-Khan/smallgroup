Luke 2

Unto you is born a Saviour

Jesus is born in Bethlehem, shepherds hear the news first, two old saints see God's promise in a baby, and a twelve-year-old boy is found in His Father's house.

Part one

No room in the inn

Verses 1–7

An emperor signs a decree in Rome, and a carpenter and his young wife set out for Bethlehem.

Caesar's decree, God's plan

Caesar Augustus was Rome's first emperor. He ruled from 27 BC to AD 14, and people hailed him as a bringer of peace. To be “taxed” here means to be registered: every man signed up in the town his family came from, so Rome knew whom to tax. Cyrenius was the Roman governor of Syria, the province that oversaw this corner of the empire.

Caesar in Romesigns a decreethe governor of Syriacarries it outevery manto his own cityJoseph and Mary, to Bethlehem

The most powerful man in the world thought he was counting his subjects. Without knowing it, he was moving a carpenter and his wife to the one town the prophet had named.

“But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law,”

Galatians 4:4

Down to the city of David

About 80 or 90 miles, most of a week on foot, with Mary “great with child.” “Espoused” means promised in marriage, as binding as marriage itself.

Joseph was of David's family line, so he registered in Bethlehem, David's hometown, the very town the prophet named:

“But thou, Beth-lehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.”

Micah 5:2, about 700 years earlier

A feed trough for a bed

Swaddling clothes were strips of cloth wrapped snug around a newborn, as every mother did. A manger is a feeding trough for animals, of stone or wood.

The inn may have been a guest room in a house already packed with family in town for the count. No space for a birth, so the baby was laid where the animals ate.

The Son of God's first bed was a feed box. He started at the bottom on purpose. Nobody who comes to Him will ever find Him too high up to reach.

Part two

Good tidings of great joy

Verses 8–20

A night shift in the fields, and heaven breaks in.

The night shift

Shepherds worked rough hours and slept in the field with the flock. They were working men, and many people in their day looked down on them.

Then “the glory of the Lord shone round about them.” The bright light of God's own presence lit up a sheep pasture.

God sent the first news of His Son to men at work in the dark, not to the palace or the temple. If you've ever thought you were too far down for God to notice, notice who heard first.

Three names for one baby

  • a SaviourHe came to rescue. “thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins” (Matthew 1:21).
  • ChristGreek for “the Anointed One,” the same as the Hebrew Messiah: the King God had promised for centuries.
  • the LordThe name above every name. The baby in the manger is God come in the flesh.

“Good tidings” means good news, which is what the word gospel means. And it's “to all people.” Not to the good people, or the religious people. All people. Then the angel makes it personal: “unto you.”

The sign was strange: a newborn lying in a feed trough. There would be only one of those in Bethlehem that night.

Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace.

A “host” is an army. Heaven's army showed up, not to fight, but to sing.

The peace they announced isn't first a world without wars. It's peace between God and men. Through this child, sinners who were God's enemies can be made right with Him.

“Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:”

Romans 5:1

A man can quit fighting God today. That's where real peace starts, the kind no bottle or pill ever gave.

Hear it, go see, tell it

the angeltoldthe shepherds“Let us now go… and see”the babe in the manger
the shepherds“made known abroad”everyone they metwondered

They didn't hold a meeting about it. They went “with haste,” found it just as the angel said, and then couldn't keep quiet.

You don't need a Bible degree to do what these men did. Tell what you've seen and heard. Here's what Jesus did for me is a testimony nobody can argue you out of.

Two ways to answer what God did

Mary

Kept and pondered

She held on to every word and turned it over in her heart. She'd keep doing that for thirty years.

The shepherds

Returned, praising God

Back to the same fields, the same sheep, the same night shift. But they weren't the same men.

Meeting Jesus doesn't always change your address or your job. It changes the man who goes back to them. Some of us go home to the same town and the same family, and they notice the difference before we say a word.

Part three

Mine eyes have seen

Verses 21–40

At the temple, an old man and an old woman who never stopped waiting.

Everything the law asked

  2. ✓his name was called JESUSthe name the angel gave before He was conceived (Luke 1:31)2:21
  3. ✓the days of her purificationforty days after a son's birth, the mother came to the temple (Leviticus 12:2–4)2:22
  4. ✓to present him to the Lordevery firstborn son belonged to God (Exodus 13:2)2:22–23

The offering tells us something. The law asked for a lamb, but made room for the poor:

“And if she be not able to bring a lamb, then she shall bring two turtles, or two young pigeons; the one for the burnt offering, and the other for a sin offering: and the priest shall make an atonement for her, and she shall be clean.”

Leviticus 12:8

Joseph and Mary couldn't afford a lamb. They didn't need to. They were carrying the Lamb of God.

A lifetime of waiting

The “consolation of Israel” means the comfort God had promised His people: the Christ who would come and set things right. Simeon had watched for Him for years.

God had told him he would see the Christ before he died. Day after day it was just another day. Then one day a poor couple walked in with a baby, and he knew.

Simeon was “just and devout,” and the Holy Ghost was on him. Waiting didn't make him bitter. Waiting kept him close to God and ready.

Salvation has a face

“Lettest thou thy servant depart”: Simeon talks like a watchman at the end of his shift. Lord, I've seen what You posted me here to see. I can go home now. He was ready to die.

He held a baby and said “mine eyes have seen thy salvation.” Salvation isn't a program or a set of rules. It's a Person, and Simeon had Him in his arms.

For the nations

“A light to lighten the Gentiles”

God promised this long before: “I will also give thee for a light to the Gentiles” (Isaiah 49:6). That's most of us in this room.

For the Jews

“The glory of thy people Israel”

The best thing that ever came out of Israel is the Messiah, born a Jew of David's line.

A child no one can ignore

Jesus would divide people. Some would trip over Him and fall. Many would be lifted up by Him. What a man does with Jesus shows what is in his heart.

Plenty of us know both halves. We fell hard, and He picked us up.

Then a hard word for Mary: “a sword shall pierce through thy own soul.” Some thirty years later, “there stood by the cross of Jesus his mother” (John 19:25).

Anna: loss that didn't end in bitterness

7 years marriedthen a widow the rest of her long life, serving God in the temple

A prophetess spoke God's word. Anna was of the tribe of Aser (Asher), one of the northern tribes scattered by Assyria some 700 years before. “Fourscore and four” is 84. That's either her age, or how long she'd been a widow, which would put her past a hundred.

She lost her husband young. Grief like that drives some people away from God. It drove Anna to Him. “Fastings” means going without food to give the time to prayer.

Then she went and told everyone in Jerusalem who was looking for redemption that He had come. Whatever you've lost, God isn't finished with you.

The early years

  1. about 5 BCborn in Bethlehem2:7

  4. after thatwise men, then EgyptMatthew 2
  5. about AD 7twelve, at the Passover2:42

Luke goes straight from the temple to Nazareth. Matthew fills in what came between: the wise men, Herod's rage, and a few years in Egypt before the family settled in Galilee (Matthew 2). Both are true. Each writer tells what serves his purpose.

Then Luke sums up about ten years in one sentence: the child “grew, and waxed strong in spirit.” To wax is to grow. And “the grace of God was upon him.”

Part four

My Father's business

Verses 41–52

A Passover trip, a missing boy, and the first words of Jesus we have.

Up to Jerusalem

The law called the men of Israel to Jerusalem for the great feasts. Joseph and Mary went “every year” for Passover, about 70 to 90 miles each way depending on the road, several days on foot.

Families from a village traveled together in a big group for safety. Some went straight south through Samaria. Others took the road down the Jordan valley and up the steep climb from Jericho. Luke doesn't say which way they went.

At twelve, a Jewish boy was close to the age when he would take on the law's duties as a man. This trip mattered.

Three days

  1. Day onea day's walk toward home“supposing him to have been in the company”
  2. Day twoback to Jerusalem“seeking him”
  3. Day threefound in the temple“after three days”

In a crowd of relatives, it was easy to assume He was somewhere up ahead with the cousins. Nobody checked until night fell.

It can happen to us. We keep going to the meetings and going through the motions, assuming Jesus is with us, and one day we notice we haven't spoken to Him in weeks. The answer is what Joseph and Mary did: turn back, and go find Him.

A boy among the teachers

The doctors weren't physicians. They were teachers of the law, the scholars who taught in the temple courts. Anyone could sit and listen.

  • hearing themHe listened first.
  • asking them questionsHe was a real student, not a show-off.
  • answersAnd when they questioned Him, His understanding amazed them.

Jesus is God's Son, and at twelve He still sat and listened. None of us is too old or too far along to sit under God's Word and ask questions.

Which father?

Mary→“thy father and I”Joseph, the man who raised Him.
Jesus→“my Father's business”God. The temple was His Father's house.

These are the first words of Jesus the Bible records. “Wist ye not” means didn't you know? At twelve He already knew who His Father was and why He had come.

He wasn't being rude. And they didn't understand yet (v. 50). Mary kept that saying too, and one day it would make sense.

Growing up whole

wisdomHis mind
statureHis body
favour with GodHis walk with the Father
favour with manHis dealings with people

The Son of God went home and “was subject unto them,” obeying Joseph and Mary in a carpenter's house in a small town for some eighteen more years.

Growth is slow and it's daily. Recovery is like that: a clearer mind, a body healing, time with God, and learning to deal honestly with people. Nobody grows all four overnight. Just keep growing.

Points to remember

  1. Who Jesus is“A Saviour, which is Christ the Lord” 2:11He came to save. He is the promised King. He is God Himself.
  2. Good news“Which shall be to all people” 2:10No one is shut out. The news came first to working men in the dark.
  3. Telling others“They made known abroad the saying” 2:17Go and see, then tell what you've seen. That's all a witness is.
  4. Waiting on God“Fastings and prayers night and day” 2:37They kept close to God while they waited, and He kept His word.
  5. Salvation“Mine eyes have seen thy salvation” 2:30Salvation is a Person: Jesus, a light for the Gentiles and the glory of Israel.
  6. Growing“Jesus increased in wisdom and stature” 2:52He grew in mind, body, spirit, and with people. Growth is daily.

Question 1 of 6

Who Jesus is

What three names does the angel give the baby born in Bethlehem?

What verses tell us about this?

“And she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins.”

Matthew 1:21

Answer

A Saviour, Christ, and the Lord. He came to rescue us from our sins, He is the Messiah God promised, and He is God come in the flesh. All three were true of the baby lying in a manger.

Question 2 of 6

Good news

Who is the good news of Jesus for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All people. The angel said so, and God showed it by telling shepherds first. Simeon said Jesus is a light for the Gentiles as well as the glory of Israel. No one is too low, too far gone, or from the wrong people. It's for you.

Question 3 of 6

Telling others

What did the shepherds do after they had seen Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They told everyone what they had been told about the child, and went back to their work glorifying and praising God. They didn't wait until they understood it all. They told what they had seen and heard.

Question 4 of 6

Waiting on God

How did Simeon and Anna spend their long years of waiting?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Close to God. Simeon lived a just and devout life with the Holy Ghost upon him. Anna stayed at the temple, praying and fasting night and day. Neither of them quit or grew bitter, and God kept His promise to both of them.

Question 5 of 6

Salvation

What did Simeon see when he took the baby in his arms?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God's salvation. Simeon held a baby and said he had seen it. Salvation isn't something we work up. It's a Person God prepared and sent: Jesus, the light for the nations and the glory of Israel. Whoever receives Him has it.

Question 6 of 6

Growing

How did Jesus grow up in Nazareth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Under His parents' roof and in obedience to them. He grew strong in spirit, filled with wisdom, with God's grace upon Him. He grew in wisdom, in body, in favour with God, and in favour with people. It happened over years, one day at a time.
