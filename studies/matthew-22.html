Matthew 22

What think ye of Christ?

In the temple, days before the cross, Jesus tells of a king's wedding feast. Then His enemies take turns trying to trap Him, until no man dares ask Him anything more.

Part one

The wedding is ready

Verses 1–14

A king throws a wedding for his son, and the guests he invited can't be bothered to come.

Still in the temple

We pick up right where chapter 21 left off: Jesus is teaching in the temple courts in Jerusalem, a few days before Passover, about AD 30. At night He stayed in Bethany, just over the Mount of Olives (21:17).

“Again by parables”: His third story in a row, after the two sons (21:28) and the wicked husbandmen (21:33). The leaders “perceived that he spake of them” (21:45).

“Behold, thy King cometh unto thee, meek, and sitting upon an ass,”

Matthew 21:5

The last week

  1. Sun.the King rides in21:1
  2. Mon.the temple cleansed21:12
  3. Tue.questions: today21:23–23:39
  4. Thu.the Passover supperch. 26
  5. Fri.the crossch. 27
  6. Sun.He is risench. 28

The gospels don't name every day, so the days here are the usual way of counting them, probably right. What's sure is this: these questions come in the last few days before the cross, from men who already wanted Him dead (21:46).

Who's who in the story

“a certain king”GodThe one throwing the feast, and paying for all of it.
“his son”JesusThe feast is in His honour. Jesus called Himself the bridegroom (9:15).
“them that were bidden”IsraelBidden means invited. God had been inviting His people for centuries.
“his servants”messengersThe prophets, John the Baptist, and soon the apostles.

A big feast had two calls: the invitation, then a servant at the door when all was ready. These guests had been asked long before. When the day came, “they would not come.”

Too busy for the King

The king's servantBehold, I have prepared my dinner… all things are ready: come unto the marriage.

One man…one to his farm,
made light of it

Another…another to his merchandise.
made light of it

“Fatlings” are calves fattened for a feast. “Made light of it” means they shrugged it off. Farms and business aren't wicked; they just mattered more than the king.

Most men don't miss God by arguing with Him. They miss Him by putting Him off: “later, when things settle down.”

From shrugging to killing

The rest went further. “Entreated them spitefully” means they treated the servants shamefully, then killed them. Israel had done that to the prophets (23:37). Herod had just killed John. In three days they would crucify the King's own Son.

Then the king “burned up their city.” Many hear in this a warning of what came about forty years later, in AD 70, when Roman armies burned Jerusalem and its temple.

God is patient, and He sends messenger after messenger. But refusing His Son is no small thing, and it doesn't end well.

As many as ye shall find

The “highways” were the roads leading out of town, where travellers, beggars, and nobodies were. The servants brought in “both bad and good.” Nobody checked a record at the door.

That's us. Men off the street, out of jail, out of the bottle. The invitation found us right where we were.

“And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him that heareth say, Come. And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.”

Revelation 22:17

Not in his own clothes

Guests pulled in off the road had no fine clothes of their own. The king must have provided the garment, and every other guest put it on. This man came in his own clothes, on his own terms.

my own clothes → the King's garment

“For he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness,”

Isaiah 61:10

Our own goodness won't get us in, not even our own work at getting clean. Christ's righteousness, given free to all who trust Him, is the only garment that fits. This man had no answer: “he was speechless.”

Called, and chosen

Many are called

everybody is invited

The king's call went out to “as many as ye shall find.” The invitation was real, and it was for all of them.

Few are chosen

those who come His way

The ones who came, and came on the king's terms, wearing his garment. The bidden “would not come.” Nobody was turned away who wanted in.

“Outer darkness” is hell, outside the light of the feast, and Jesus warned about it more than anyone. He doesn't say it to scare us off. He says it so nobody has to go. The garment is free.

Part two

Caesar, and the resurrection

Verses 15–33

First a political trap, then a riddle to make the resurrection look silly. Both fall apart in His hands.

Strange partners, and a trap

The Pharisees

hated the Roman tax

So did the crowds. Paying Rome felt like bowing to pagans.

The Herodians

backed Herod's family

Herod's family ruled with Rome's blessing, so this party was all for keeping Rome happy.

If He says“Pay it,”
→
thenthe crowds turn on Him.
If He says“Don't,”
→
thenHe's a rebel, and Pilate hears of it.

Flattery first

“Entangle” means to snare, the way you'd net a bird. Enemies who couldn't stand each other teamed up because they hated Jesus more.

And notice how they start: “Master, we know that thou art true… thou regardest not the person of men.” Every word of it was true, and none of it was meant. They were buttering Him up before the trap shut.

Most of us know that move. We've used it, and we've been worked by it. Jesus “perceived their wickedness” (v. 18). You can't flatter your way past Him.

Whose face is on it?

The “penny” was a Roman silver coin, a day's pay (20:2), stamped with the emperor Tiberius and a “superscription” calling him son of the “divine” Augustus.

They carried his money, even in the temple. Give it back to him.

But you bear an image too:

“So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.”

Genesis 1:27

Both, each in its place

Unto Caesar

the coin with his face on it

Render means pay back what's owed. A Christian pays his taxes, keeps the law, and gives leaders their due.

Unto God

the man with His image on him

Your heart, your mind, your body, your days. Caesar gets a coin. God gets you.

“Render therefore to all their dues: tribute to whom tribute is due; custom to whom custom; fear to whom fear; honour to whom honour.”

Romans 13:7

The Sadducees

The Sadducees were the rich priestly families who ran the temple and got along with Rome. They held to the written law of Moses, threw out the Pharisees' traditions, and didn't believe in life after death at all.

“For the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, neither angel, nor spirit: but the Pharisees confess both.”

Acts 23:8

Their question leans on a real law. If a man died with no children, his brother married the widow, so she wasn't left alone and poor, and so the dead man's family line went on. That's “raise up seed” (Deuteronomy 25:5). “No issue” means no children.

A riddle built to mock

  1. the firstmarried her, died
  2. the next fiveeach married her, each died
  3. the seventhmarried her, died
  4. the womandied also

Nobody thinks this happened. It's a made-up case, the kind of riddle they likely used to make Pharisees squirm. Their point: if there's a resurrection, heaven is a mess of seven husbands and one wife. So there can't be one.

Two mistakes

“not knowing the scriptures”wrong bookThey knew the words, but missed what God was saying in them.
“nor the power of God”too small a GodThey pictured the next life as this life, just carried on. God can raise the dead to a new kind of life.

In the resurrection, people don't marry. In that way they are “as the angels,” not turned into angels. And with a smile, Jesus mentions angels to the men who said there weren't any.

Married love is God's gift for this life. In the next, what it pointed to is here: the wedding of His Son. Nobody there will feel short-changed.

“I am,” not “I was”

They trusted Moses, so Jesus answers from Moses, at the burning bush. Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob had been dead for centuries when God said this:

“Moreover he said, I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. And Moses hid his face; for he was afraid to look upon God.”

Exodus 3:6
  • “I was the God of Abraham”as if they were gone for good
  • “I am the God of Abraham”they are alive to Him right now

God doesn't stay the God of dust. If He is still their God, they still live, and one day their bodies will rise. If you've buried someone who trusted the Lord, they are not lost.

Part three

What think ye of Christ?

Verses 34–46

A lawyer asks for the greatest commandment. Then Jesus asks one question, and nobody can answer it.

One out of 613

commandments the rabbis counted in the law

A “lawyer” here isn't a courtroom man. He's an expert in the law of Moses. The rabbis later counted 613 commands in it, and they loved to argue which ones were heaviest.

Jesus didn't pick one. He picked two, and said everything else hangs on them.

Two pegs

“And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.”

Deuteronomy 6:5

“…but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the LORD.”

Leviticus 19:18

Both are from Moses. “Hang”, like a coat on a peg. Every other command is one of these two worked out. Take away love, and the rest falls.

Who can keep that?

Be honest. None of us has loved God with all our heart for one whole day. For years, a lot of us gave all our heart to a bottle, a pipe, or ourselves.

Jesus is the only Man who ever kept these two perfectly. Then He died for the rest of us who didn't. Love doesn't start with us trying harder. It starts with Him:

“We love him, because he first loved us.”

1 John 4:19

Mark tells us Jesus said to this lawyer, “Thou art not far from the kingdom of God” (Mark 12:34). Close is good. But close isn't in.

Now He asks

All day they had asked Him questions. Now Jesus asks them one, while the Pharisees are still standing there together.

Their answer was right as far as it went. God had promised David a son who would reign forever (2 Samuel 7:12–13). Matthew opened his book with “Jesus Christ, the son of David” (1:1), and two days earlier the crowds had shouted “Hosanna to the Son of David” (21:9).

“In spirit” means David wrote it by the Holy Ghost. Mark says so plainly (Mark 12:36). So this isn't David's guess. It's God speaking through him.

Son, and Lord

“The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool.”

Psalm 110:1

In those days a father never called his son “my lord.” A son honoured his father. Yet David, the great king, calls his own descendant “my Lord.”

Only one answer works. The Christ would be David's son by birth, and David's Lord because He is the Son of God.

What think ye of Christ?

The Pharisees, the Herodians, the Sadducees, and the lawyers each took a turn. By the end of the day, nobody dared ask Him anything more. Mark adds, “the common people heard him gladly” (Mark 12:37).

Every trap they set was about getting something on Jesus. His question is about getting something settled in us. It's the question every man has to answer for himself: Who is He to you? A good teacher? Or your Lord?

Points to remember

  1. Invitation“As many as ye shall find, bid to the marriage” 22:9The King's invitation goes to the highways, to the bad and the good. Whosoever will may come.
  2. The garment“He saw there a man which had not on a wedding garment” 22:11We come in His righteousness, not our own. The garment is free, but we must put it on.
  3. Caesar and God“Render therefore unto Caesar the things which are Caesar’s; and unto God the things that are God’s” 22:21Caesar gets his coin. God gets the man who bears His image.
  4. Resurrection“God is not the God of the dead, but of the living” 22:32God said “I am” the God of Abraham. His people who have died are alive to Him.
  5. Love“On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets” 22:40Love God with everything, and love your neighbour as yourself. He loved us first.
  6. Christ“What think ye of Christ? whose son is he?” 22:42Jesus is David's son by birth and David's Lord as the Son of God.

Question 1 of 6

Invitation

Who did the king invite when the first guests would not come?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Anyone his servants could find on the highways, both bad and good. The ones first invited weren't worthy because they wouldn't come. The new guests didn't earn their seats; they were simply asked and came. That's how the gospel reached men like us.

Question 2 of 6

The garment

Why was the man without a wedding garment cast out?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness,”

Isaiah 61:10

Answer

He came in on his own terms, in his own clothes, instead of the garment the king provided. When asked, he had no excuse. We can't come to God in our own goodness. We come clothed in the righteousness of Christ, which He gives free to all who trust Him.

Question 3 of 6

Caesar and God

What do we owe Caesar, and what do we owe God?

What verses tell us about this?

“So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.”

Genesis 1:27

Answer

The coin bore Caesar's image, so we give the government what it's owed: taxes, obedience to the law, and honour. But we bear God's image, so we owe God ourselves: heart, soul, mind, and life.

Question 4 of 6

Resurrection

How did Jesus show from Moses that the dead will rise?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:”

John 11:25

Answer

Long after Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob died, God told Moses, “I am the God of Abraham,” not “I was.” God is the God of the living, so they live to Him still, and they will rise. Everyone who believes in Jesus has that same hope.

Question 5 of 6

Love

Which commandments did Jesus say hold up all the law?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, soul, and mind, and love thy neighbour as thyself. All the law and the prophets hang on these two. None of us keeps them perfectly, but Jesus did, and His love for us is where ours begins.

Question 6 of 6

Christ

How can the Christ be both David's son and David's Lord?

What verses tell us about this?

“Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the seed of David according to the flesh; And declared to be the Son of God with power, according to the spirit of holiness, by the resurrection from the dead:”

Romans 1:3–4

Answer

He is David's son by birth, born in David's line. And He is David's Lord because He is the Son of God, who sits at the Father's right hand. Jesus is both man and God.
