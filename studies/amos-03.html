The lion hath roared

Part one

You only

Verses 1–2

The closer the relationship, the higher the stakes.

“You only have I known”

A quick reminder: Amos is a herdman from Judah, preaching in Israel, the northern kingdom, about 760 BC. In chapters 1–2 he judged the nations, and then Israel. Now he explains why God is hardest on His own.

יָדַע said yada to know, up close; to choose as one's own
God says“You only have I known…”
→
so you'd expect“…therefore I will go easy on you”?

No: “therefore I will punish you.” God knew Israel the way a husband knows his wife or a father his son. Being that close didn't give them a pass. It made their sin worse, because they sinned against Someone who loved them.

For a believer in Jesus, this isn't a threat of losing salvation; that's secure in Him. But a father deals with his own children in a way he doesn't with the neighbor's kids. “For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth” (Hebrews 12:6).

Part two

Nothing by accident

Verses 3–8

A string of questions, and every one has the same answer.

Every effect has a cause

Amos fires off questions from the country life he knew. Every one gets the same answer.

“Will a lion roar … when he hath no prey?”NoA lion roars over a kill. The roar means it's already happened.
“Can a bird fall in a snare … where no gin is?”NoA gin is the trigger of a trap. No trigger, no trap.
“Shall a trumpet be blown … and the people not be afraid?”NoThe ram's-horn alarm on the wall meant an enemy was coming.
“Shall there be evil in a city, and the LORD hath not done it?”No“Evil” here means disaster, trouble, not sin. Nothing hits a city outside God's hand.

Here's where Amos is going: a prophet preaching judgment isn't an accident either. Something caused it. God has spoken.

Two on one road.

If you see two men walking the same road together, they must have agreed to meet and agreed on where they're going. Israel wanted God along for the trip, but they had picked their own direction.

You can't walk with God and keep walking toward the old life at the same time. Somebody has to change direction, and it isn't going to be God.

“He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”

Micah 6:8

The same goes for the men you walk with. Who you agree to walk beside will shape where you end up.

God tells first

God doesn't ambush people. Before He acts in judgment, He sends word.

  1. Genesis 18“Shall I hide from Abraham that thing which I do?”
  2. about 760 BCAmos warns Israelyou are here
  3. the next 40 yearsHosea, Isaiah, Micah keep warning
  4. 722Samaria falls to Assyria

Forty years of warnings. That's the patience of God. He told them plainly, and He told them for a long time.

And Amos couldn't keep quiet: “The Lord GOD hath spoken, who can but prophesy?” When the lion roars, everybody jumps. When God speaks, His messenger has to speak too.

Part three

Samaria on trial

Verses 9–15

Even the pagans are called in to look.

Call in the witnesses

God sends heralds to the palaces of Ashdod, a Philistine city, and of Egypt, Israel's old slave-master. Come sit on the hills around Samaria and watch what goes on in there.

Even the Philistines would be shocked.

Even Egypt would shake its head.

Samaria's palaces looked rich. God looked into the storerooms and saw what was really in them: violence and robbery. Every treasure was taken from someone. “They know not to do right”: they had done wrong so long they'd forgotten how to do right.

Two legs and an ear

The “adversary” was Assyria. Under the law, a shepherd who lost a sheep to a wild animal had to bring back what was left, to prove he hadn't stolen it:

“If it be torn in pieces, then let him bring it for witness, and he shall not make good that which was torn.”

Exodus 22:13

So Israel's “rescue” would be scraps: a couple of legs, a piece of an ear. The rich who lounged on fine couches in Samaria would be pulled out like that.

And yet, even here, something is pulled out. God never lets His people be wiped out completely. He keeps a remnant, and later in this book He promises to rebuild (9:11–15).

Nowhere to hide

An altar had four “horns,” stone corners sticking up. A man in danger could run and grab them for mercy (1 Kings 1:50). Cut off the horns, and there's nothing left to hold on to.

  • The altar at Bethelreligion without God
  • The winter house and the summer housetwo homes, one for each season
  • The houses of ivorywalls and furniture inlaid with carved ivory
  • “God is our refuge and strength”Psalm 46:1

Archaeologists digging at Samaria have found hundreds of pieces of carved ivory from those very houses. The luxury was real. So was the end of it.

We all have our places to hide: a habit, a bank account, a good reputation, a religious routine. When God speaks, only one refuge holds.

Points to remember

  1. Privilege“You only have I known” 3:2Being close to God is a gift, not a pass. He deals with His own.
  2. Walking with God“Can two walk together, except they be agreed?” 3:3Walking with God means agreeing with Him on the direction.
  3. God's warnings“He revealeth his secret unto his servants” 3:7God warns long and plainly before He judges.
  4. Hidden wealth“Who store up violence and robbery” 3:10God sees what's really behind the walls.
  5. The remnant“Two legs, or a piece of an ear” 3:12Judgment is real, and God still keeps a remnant.
  6. False refuge“The horns of the altar shall be cut off” 3:14Only God Himself is a safe place to run.

Question 1 of 6

Privilege

Why does being close to God raise the stakes?

What verses tell us about this?

“For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required: and to whom men have committed much, of him they will ask the more.”

Luke 12:48

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

Answer

Because closeness means we know better, and our sin is against Someone who loves us. Israel had been chosen and carried by God, so their sin was worse, not more excusable. A believer's place in Christ is secure, but God, like a good father, deals with His own children. That's love, not rejection.

Question 2 of 6

Walking with God

What does it take for two to walk together?

What verses tell us about this?

“He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”

Micah 6:8

“For thus saith the LORD unto the house of Israel, Seek ye me, and ye shall live:”

Amos 5:4

Answer

Agreement. You can't walk with God and head the other way at the same time. That means agreeing with Him about our sin, and about where we're going. It's not about being perfect. It's about walking humbly beside Him, one day at a time, and going where He goes.

Question 3 of 6

God's warnings

Does God warn before He acts?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD said, Shall I hide from Abraham that thing which I do;”

Genesis 18:17

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Yes. God told Abraham before Sodom, and He sent Amos, Hosea, Isaiah, and Micah to warn Israel for about forty years before Assyria came. He doesn't want anyone to perish. The warnings in His word today are the same kindness: He's telling us ahead of time so we can turn.

Question 4 of 6

Hidden wealth

What did God find stored in Samaria's palaces?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Answer

Violence and robbery. The ivory houses and the summer homes looked like success, but God saw what paid for them: people cheated and crushed. God looks past the front of the house. What's built on stolen goods won't stand, and what a man sows, he reaps.

Question 5 of 6

The remnant

Is anything left when God judges?

What verses tell us about this?

“Except the LORD of hosts had left unto us a very small remnant, we should have been as Sodom, and we should have been like unto Gomorrah.”

Isaiah 1:9

“In that day will I raise up the tabernacle of David that is fallen, and close up the breaches thereof; and I will raise up his ruins, and I will build it as in the days of old:”

Amos 9:11

Answer

Yes, but only by God's mercy. Israel's rescue would look like scraps from a lion's mouth. But God always keeps a remnant, and He promised Israel He would rebuild what fell. That's who God is: His judgment is real, and He keeps His word to restore. No man is so torn up that God can't pull him out.

Question 6 of 6

False refuge

Where do men run for safety when God speaks?

What verses tell us about this?

“That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation, who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us:”

Hebrews 6:18

“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”

Psalm 46:1

Answer

To all kinds of places: a religious routine, money, a nice house, a habit that numbs. Israel ran to the altar at Bethel and to their ivory houses, and God cut both off. The only refuge that holds is God Himself. Run to Him, and lay hold on the hope He sets before you in Christ.
