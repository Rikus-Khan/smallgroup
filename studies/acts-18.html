Acts 18

I am with thee

A worn-out, frightened Paul lands in the roughest city in Greece, gets a day job and two new friends, and hears the Lord say, “Be not afraid… I am with thee.”

Part one

Corinth

Verses 1–11

A hard city, a day job, and a promise in the night.

The city on the narrow neck

Corinth sat on a thin strip of land with a harbor on each side, so ships and goods passed through it from every direction. It was rich, busy, and rough: a sailors' town, known for drink and loose living.

Paul had just left Athens with few believers to show for it. He didn't go to a gentler town. He went to the hardest one.

He would later remind the Corinthians how he came: “in weakness, and in fear, and in much trembling” (1 Corinthians 2:3). Keep that in mind for verse 9.

A bad order, and a good friendship

Emperor Claudiusordered the Jews out of Rome, about AD 49Aquila & Priscillalost their home and trade, and landed inCorinth
Paulalone, and same tradeAquila & Priscillatook him ina team that lasted

Two refugees from Rome, with a life knocked flat by a ruler's order. Paul had nobody. God put these two in the same city with the same trade, at the same time.

Notice Priscilla and Aquila: Luke often names her first. The two of them will show up again in verse 18 and verse 26, and Paul later called them “my helpers in Christ Jesus” (Romans 16:3). A hard spot can bring you the friends you'll keep for life.

Paul had a day job

A tentmaker cut and stitched tough cloth, often goat-hair from Paul's home region, Cilicia, or leather. It was a trade for a man's hands, in a shop with other workmen.

Paul was an apostle, and he still showed up at the bench. He told the Thessalonians: “labouring night and day, because we would not be chargeable unto any of you, we preached unto you the gospel of God.” (1 Thessalonians 2:9)

If you are working a hard, plain job today, that is not second place to “real ministry.” It is how Paul paid his way and kept his word clean. Honest work is part of a new life.

Right next door

Paul had told them about Jesus until they pushed back and “blasphemed”, abused him. He shook out his clothes, a sign that he was finished with the argument, and said “I am clean.” He had told them the truth; what they did with it was now theirs to answer for.

He didn't leave town. He walked next door. Justus, “one that worshipped God,” opened his house, and Paul kept right on teaching with the synagogue's own wall for a neighbor.

Crispus, the synagogue's chief ruler, believed with his whole household, and many Corinthians heard, believed, and were baptized, in that order. Baptism came after believing. It showed what had happened already.

Who believed in Corinth?

The very people the city was known for. Years later Paul wrote them a list of what they used to be: fornicators, idolaters, thieves, drunkards. Then:

What they were

“Such were some of you”

Drunkards, thieves, cheats, men and women who had run through every kind of sin.

What God did

Washed, sanctified, justified

“in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.” Not cleaned up by effort. Cleaned by Him.

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

If your past reads like that list, you are exactly who Corinth was made of, and the church in Corinth was made of men like you.

What the Lord says to a frightened man

Think what was in Paul's head that night: a beating in Philippi, a mob in Thessalonica, a chase out of Berea, a flop in Athens, and now another hostile synagogue. The Lord spoke to him “in the night by a vision.”

  • “They'll hurt me again.”“no man shall set on thee to hurt thee”
  • “I'm the only one doing this.”“I have much people in this city”
  • “Maybe I should keep my head down.”“speak, and hold not thy peace”
  • “I am with thee.”the answer under all the others

“Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.”

Isaiah 41:10

“Much people in this city”

Before they believed, He already knew them.

God said “I have much people” about Corinth, a city that looked hopeless. The drunk in the tavern and the thief at the harbor were not beyond reach. Paul only had to keep speaking and let God do the finding.

So Paul stayed “a year and six months, teaching the word of God.” That is long, steady, ordinary work. In that time he wrote 1 and 2 Thessalonians from Corinth, among his earliest letters (about AD 50 to 51).

“And lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.”

Matthew 28:20

The same promise is yours. When the fear hits today, say it out loud: “I am with thee.” Then go and do the next thing.

Part two

Gallio

Verses 12–17

A Roman governor who couldn't care less.

The governor's ruling

The JewsThis fellow persuadeth men to worship God contrary to the law.

GallioIf it were a matter of wrong or wicked lewdness… reason would that I should bear with you. But a question of words and names, and of your law, look ye to it: I will be no judge of such matters.
case dismissed

Gallio was the Roman “deputy” (proconsul) of Achaia, the province of Greece. A stone inscription found at Delphi names him and dates his term to about AD 51 to 52. That is one of the firm dates in Paul's life.

His ruling mattered: Rome saw no crime in Paul's teaching. For years afterward, Christians could point to it.

The promise, kept

The Lord said“no man shall… hurt thee”
→
Gallio diddrove them off. Paul wasn't touched.

Be honest about what the promise was. It was not a promise of no trouble: Paul was stoned in Lystra, jailed in Philippi, and more trouble was coming. It was a promise for that city and that season, and a deeper one underneath: “I am with thee.”

“Gallio cared for none of those things.” He stood a few feet from the gospel and shrugged. A man can be near the truth for years and not care. And Sosthenes, the new synagogue ruler, was beaten while the governor looked on. He may be the Sosthenes who later joined Paul in the greeting of 1 Corinthians. A beaten man, then a brother.

Part three

Home, and Apollos

Verses 18–28

A voyage home, and a preacher who needed a quiet word.

The voyage home

Paul left Corinth from its eastern port, Cenchrea, with Priscilla and Aquila, and he had shorn his head, for he had a vow. The Bible doesn't say what the vow was for. Aquila and Priscilla stayed in Ephesus, where he stopped.

In Ephesus they asked him to stay longer. He refused: “I will return again unto you, if God will.” He kept his plans open-handed, and he did go back (Acts 19). Then Caesarea, up to “the church” (most likely Jerusalem), and down to his home base, Antioch.

The second journey, start to finish

About three years, over a thousand miles, and nearly every town ended in a beating, a riot or a shrug. The longest stop was the one where God said, “be not afraid.”

When one door is slammed or a season feels wasted, that is not a verdict. It is one stop. Keep going to the next, and stay where He tells you to stay.

A gifted man, one step behind

Apollos came from Alexandria, Egypt, the great city of learning. He was “eloquent” and “mighty in the scriptures”, and he was on fire.

But he knew “only the baptism of John”: he knew the Messiah was coming, and that people should repent, but he hadn't heard the full story of the cross, the resurrection and the Spirit given.

Later the Ephesian believers sent him on to Achaia, to Corinth, with a letter. There he “mightily convinced the Jews”, showing from the scriptures that Jesus is the Christ.

A quiet word

Apollosspoke boldly, but missed part of the storyPriscilla & Aquila“took him unto them”
Priscilla & Aquila“expounded… the way of God more perfectly”Apolloslistened, and went on to help many

Notice how they did it. They didn't shout him down in the synagogue. They took him aside, probably to the tentmakers' shop, and filled in what he lacked. And he, a famous speaker, let two workmen teach him.

Whichever side you are on, it is the same lesson. Be the man who can be told. And when you can help a new man, be the one who takes him aside kindly.

“Through grace”

Paul planted. Apollos watered. God gave the increase.

“I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase.”

1 Corinthians 3:6

Luke says Apollos “helped them much which had believed through grace.” Those believers were not saved by anything they did. They believed through grace. Grace is where it starts, and grace is what keeps it going.

Everyone in this chapter had a part: Paul, Priscilla, Aquila, Justus, Apollos. None of them made a single person believe. That was God's job. Yours is to be faithful where you are today.

Points to remember

  1. Work“By their occupation they were tentmakers” 18:3Honest work with your hands is part of a new life. Paul did it too.
  2. A new start“Many of the Corinthians hearing believed” 18:8The roughest city in Greece believed. Nobody's past is too much.
  3. Fear“Be not afraid… I am with thee” 18:9–10Say it out loud when the fear comes, then do the next thing.
  4. Protection“No man shall set on thee to hurt thee” 18:10He kept His word for that city, and “I am with thee” holds everywhere.
  5. Correction“Expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly” 18:26Be a man who can be told, and who takes others aside kindly.

Question 1 of 5

Work

What did Paul do for a living while he preached in Corinth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He was a tentmaker. He worked at his trade alongside Aquila and Priscilla through the week, and he reasoned in the synagogue every sabbath. Honest work was part of how he served, and he never thought it was beneath him.

Question 2 of 5

A new start

Who believed in Corinth?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

Crispus, the synagogue ruler, and many Corinthians, in a city known for rough living. Paul later told them that some had been drunkards, thieves and worse, but they were washed, set apart and made right with God by the Lord Jesus and the Spirit. It is the same offer for anyone today.

Question 3 of 5

Fear

What does the Lord say to a frightened man?

What verses tell us about this?

“Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.”

Isaiah 41:10

Answer

“Be not afraid, but speak… for I am with thee.” The Lord doesn't just say “don't be afraid.” He gives the reason: He is with you, and He has people He is already working on. A frightened man can take one more step because he is not alone.

Question 4 of 5

Protection

Did the Lord keep His promise to Paul in Corinth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Yes. The Jews dragged Paul before Gallio, and Gallio threw the case out and drove them away. Paul stayed a year and six months, teaching the word of God. The promise to be with him went on beyond Corinth, even when more trouble came.

Question 5 of 5

Correction

How did Aquila and Priscilla help Apollos?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They took him aside and explained the way of God more fully, privately and kindly. Apollos was a gifted speaker who knew only part of the story, and he was humble enough to listen. He went on to help many believers “through grace.”
