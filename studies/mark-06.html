Mark 6

It is I; be not afraid

His hometown won't believe, the twelve are sent out, a king kills a prophet to save face, five thousand eat, and Jesus walks out to His men on a stormy sea.

Part one

Is not this the carpenter?

Verses 1–6

Jesus goes home. The people who watched Him grow up can't get past who they think He is.

Back to Nazareth

“His own country” is His hometown, Nazareth, up in the hills about twenty miles from Capernaum. He grew up there and worked there until He was about thirty (Luke 3:23).

On the sabbath He taught in the same synagogue He'd sat in as a boy. They couldn't deny the wisdom or the miracles. They just couldn't believe they came from him.

We know his family

  1. “the carpenter”a builder who worked with wood, maybe stone too
  2. MaryHis mother
  3. Jameslater led the church in Jerusalem and wrote the letter of James
  4. Josesa brother
  5. JudaJude, likely the one who wrote the letter of Jude
  6. Simona brother

“Offended at him” means they stumbled over Him. He was too ordinary. They'd watched Him grow up and work in the shop. How could God be in that?

His own brothers didn't believe yet either (John 7:5). But after He rose, they were praying with the disciples (Acts 1:14), and James and Jude became leaders in the church. Don't give up on the family members who can't see it yet.

What made Jesus marvel

In Nazareth

He marvelled at their unbelief

The people who knew Him best trusted Him least.

In Capernaum

He marvelled at a soldier's faith

A Roman officer, an outsider, believed one word from Jesus was enough.

“When Jesus heard these things, he marvelled at him, and turned him about, and said unto the people that followed him, I say unto you, I have not found so great faith, no, not in Israel.”

Luke 7:9

“Could there do no mighty work” isn't about His power running low. Hardly anyone brought Him their sick. He doesn't force Himself on people who won't have Him. Even so, He healed the few who came, and then He went on to the next village. Unbelief didn't stop Him. It just stopped them.

Part two

By two and two

Verses 7–13

Jesus sends the twelve out with almost nothing, except each other and His power.

Travel light

A scrip is a traveler's bag for food and supplies. A purse here was often just a money belt.

  • a scripno bag of supplies
  • breadno packed food
  • moneynothing in the belt
  • two coatsone is enough
  • a staff, sandals, and His “power over unclean spirits”everything they needed

He was teaching them to depend on God for the next meal and the next bed. Later He asked them, “lacked ye any thing?” And they said, “Nothing” (Luke 22:35).

Never alone

Not one by one. Two and two.

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

Jesus could have sent twelve men to twelve towns and covered more ground. He sent six pairs instead. Someone to pray with, to keep you honest, and to pick you up.

The men who stay free usually aren't the strongest. They're the ones who don't walk alone. Who's your other man?

Shake off the dust

Jews coming home from Gentile lands would shake the dust off their feet. Here it meant: we gave you the message; what you do with it is on you now. Sodom and Gomorrha were the cities God destroyed for their wickedness (Genesis 19). Turning down Jesus' own messengers would be judged even more strictly.

They “preached that men should repent”: change your mind and turn around, from sin to God. Olive oil was the common medicine of the day (Luke 10:34). The healing came from God's power, not the oil.

Part three

It is John, whom I beheaded

Verses 14–29

A king hears about Jesus, and his conscience starts talking. Then Mark tells us why.

Who is this?

Some saidThat it is Elias.

Others saidA prophet, or as one of the prophets.

Herod saidIt is John, whom I beheaded: he is risen from the dead.

This is Herod Antipas, a son of Herod the Great. Rome let him rule Galilee and Perea from about 4 BC to AD 39. Mark calls him “king,” the title people used, though Rome never gave him that rank.

Nobody accused Herod. His own conscience did. He'd killed a good man, and it was still eating at him. Everyone had an opinion about Jesus, but nobody here had it right yet. Two chapters later Peter does: “Thou art the Christ” (8:29).

Not lawful

Herodias had been married to Herod's half-brother. Herod left his own wife, and she left her husband, so the two of them could marry. God's Law forbade it plainly:

“Thou shalt not uncover the nakedness of thy brother’s wife: it is thy brother’s nakedness.”

Leviticus 18:16

John told the most powerful man in the region, to his face, “It is not lawful.” Josephus, a Jewish historian of that time, says Herod held him at Machaerus, a fortress east of the Dead Sea.

He heard him gladly

What Herod did

Liked the preacher

Feared him, knew he was “a just man and an holy,” observed him (kept him safe from Herodias), changed “many things,” and enjoyed listening to him.

What Herod wouldn't do

Let go of the one thing

He kept Herodias. The one sin John named was the one sin Herod wouldn't give up.

You can enjoy the preaching, respect the preacher, even clean up “many things,” and still hold on to the one thing. The one thing is usually the one that takes you down. Herod's took him all the way to murder.

One step down at a time

  1. a birthday supperfor the great men of Galilee (v. 21)
  2. a dancethat “pleased Herod” (v. 22)
  3. “whatsoever thou wilt”showing off (v. 22)
  4. “he sware unto her”an oath in front of everyone (v. 23)
  5. “What shall I ask?”Herodias' moment (v. 24)
  6. “the head of John”in a charger (v. 25)

Herodias had been waiting for a “convenient day”. A charger is a large serving platter. “By and by” means right now. The girl was Herodias' daughter; Josephus names her Salome.

Herod didn't wake up that day planning to kill a prophet. He wanted to look big in front of his guests. A party, a show, a big mouth, a promise he couldn't take back. Most falls happen that way: one easy step at a time, in a room full of the wrong company.

Sorry, but not enough

Herod was “exceeding sorry.” He knew it was wrong. But he couldn't stand to look weak in front of the men at his table.

So a good man's life was lost to a king's pride. A promise to do evil is a promise you should break. Herod kept the wrong one.

“The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.”

Proverbs 29:25

John's road

  1. 1:4the wildernesspreaching repentance
  2. 1:9the Jordanbaptizes Jesus
  3. 1:14Herod's prison“put in prison”
  4. 6:27a birthdaybeheaded
  5. 6:29a tombburied by his disciples

John lost his head for telling one man the truth. He never got out of that prison. But he finished what God gave him to do: he pointed people to Jesus.

“And his disciples came, and took up the body, and buried it, and went and told Jesus.”

Matthew 14:12

Notice where John's men took their grief. When you lose someone, when the news is senseless, take it where they did: go and tell Jesus.

Part four

Give ye them to eat

Verses 30–44

Jesus tries to take His tired men away to rest. Five thousand men get there first.

Come apart and rest

Mark calls them apostles here, “sent ones,” because they're just back from being sent. They report everything: what they did, what they taught.

ifyou never come apart
→
thensooner or later you come apart

A desert place means a lonely, empty place, away from towns, not sand dunes. Jesus noticed His men hadn't even had time to eat. He cares about your rest, not just your work.

Running on empty is dangerous for any man, and more for a man in recovery. Hungry, worn out, no time alone with God: that's when the old ways start to look good. Taking a rest with Him isn't laziness. It's obedience.

They got there first

The boat cut across the top of the lake to a lonely spot near Bethsaida (Luke 9:10). People saw which way they were heading and ran around the shore on foot. By the time the boat landed, the crowd was waiting.

So much for the day off. And Jesus wasn't annoyed. He “was moved with compassion.” He saw the crowd the way a shepherd sees a flock with nobody watching it.

Sheep without a shepherd

Sheep with no shepherd scatter, get lost, go hungry, and get picked off. Moses prayed Israel would never be like that:

“Which may go out before them, and which may go in before them, and which may lead them out, and which may bring them in; that the congregation of the LORD be not as sheep which have no shepherd.”

Numbers 27:17

Notice the first thing the Shepherd gave them: “he began to teach them many things.” Before the bread came the Word. A lost man's first need is the truth.

“How many loaves have ye? go and see.” Jesus starts with what you've actually got, not with what you wish you had. It's never enough. He never asks it to be. He asks you to bring it to Him.

Part five

It is I; be not afraid

Verses 45–56

All night against the wind. Then, in the darkest hours, someone comes walking across the water.

The long night

The Romans split the night into four watches (Mark 13:35 names them: even, midnight, cockcrowing, morning).

  1. ~6 pmeven“in the midst of the sea”
  2. 9 pm2nd watchrowing
  3. 12 am3rd watchstill rowing
  4. 3 am4th watch“he cometh unto them”
  5. 6 ammorning

Constrained means He made them go. John says the crowd wanted to make Him king by force (John 6:15). He sent His men out of that, and went up the mountain alone to pray.

Hours of rowing and getting nowhere. And all that time, “he saw them.” He didn't come at the first hour. He came in the fourth watch, the darkest and longest. If you're in that watch right now, He sees you rowing.

Only God walks on the sea

“Which alone spreadeth out the heavens, and treadeth upon the waves of the sea.”

Job 9:8

Job says God alone treads on the waves. Here is Jesus doing it.

“And the LORD passed by before him, and proclaimed, The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth,”

Exodus 34:6

“Would have passed by them” sounds strange until you remember the LORD passed by Moses to show him His glory. Jesus wasn't ignoring them. He was showing them who He is.

“It is I”

Worn out, soaked, scared, they saw a shape on the water in the dark and screamed. They thought it was a ghost.

He spoke right away: “Be of good cheer: it is I.” In Greek that's ego eimi, “I am,” the name God gave Moses at the burning bush (Exodus 3:14).

Then He climbed into the boat with them, “and the wind ceased.” He didn't just calm the storm from a distance. He got in.

They forgot the bread

These were the same men who had handed out bread to five thousand that afternoon. By midnight it hadn't sunk in. They “considered not”: they didn't think it through. If He could do that with five loaves, what were they afraid of?

A hardened heart here doesn't mean they weren't His. It means dull and slow, the way a callus can't feel. They were His men, slow to learn. And He still got into the boat with them.

We do the same thing. God got us through yesterday, and today's storm makes us forget it. Remember the bread. He hasn't changed since the last time He came through.

“When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow thee: when thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned; neither shall the flame kindle upon thee.”

Isaiah 43:2

Just the border of His garment

Aiming for Bethsaida against the wind, they came ashore at Gennesaret, a farming plain on the northwest shore.

People knew Him at once. They carried the sick to Him on mats and begged to touch just the border of His garment, the fringe every Israelite wore (Numbers 15:38). Remember the woman in chapter 5?

“As many as touched him were made whole.” Not some of them. Not the deserving ones. As many as reached out.

Points to remember

  1. Unbelief“He marvelled because of their unbelief” 6:6Jesus doesn't force Himself on people who won't have Him. Unbelief shuts us out of what He'd do.
  2. Sent“They went out, and preached that men should repent” 6:12He sends His men in pairs, traveling light, trusting Him for what they need.
  3. Conscience“It is John, whom I beheaded” 6:16A guilty conscience won't stay buried. Pride and the fear of man led Herod into sin he couldn't undo.
  4. Compassion“As sheep not having a shepherd” 6:34Jesus sees lost, tired people with a shepherd's heart, and He feeds them with His Word first.
  5. Enough“They did all eat, and were filled” 6:42Bring Him what you have, however small. In His hands it's more than enough.
  6. Fear“Be of good cheer: it is I; be not afraid” 6:50He sees you rowing in the dark. He comes, He gets in the boat, and the wind stops.

Question 1 of 6

Unbelief

Why did Jesus do no mighty work in His own country?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because of their unbelief. They knew Him as the carpenter and couldn't accept that God was at work in Him, so they stumbled over Him. Few came to Him, and He healed the few who did. He marveled at their unbelief, and went on to other villages.

Question 2 of 6

Sent

How did Jesus send out the twelve, and what did they preach?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Two by two, with His power over unclean spirits, carrying nothing but a staff, sandals, and one coat. They had to trust God for every need. They preached that men should repent: turn from sin to God.

Question 3 of 6

Conscience

Why was Herod sure John had risen from the dead?

What verses tell us about this?

“The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.”

Proverbs 29:25

Answer

Because his conscience was haunting him. He knew John was a just and holy man, and he had him killed anyway, to keep a foolish oath and save face in front of his guests. Nobody had to accuse him. His guilt did.

Question 4 of 6

Compassion

What moved Jesus when He saw the crowd?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.”

Psalm 23:1

Answer

Compassion. His day of rest was interrupted, but He saw them as sheep with no shepherd, lost and unprotected. So He taught them many things, and then He fed them.

Question 5 of 6

Enough

What did Jesus do with five loaves and two fishes?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He looked up to heaven, blessed them, broke them, and gave them to His disciples to pass out. About five thousand men ate until they were full, and twelve baskets of pieces were left over. What we bring Him is never enough by itself. In His hands, it's more than enough.

Question 6 of 6

Fear

What did Jesus say to His men toiling in the storm?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Answer

“Be of good cheer: it is I; be not afraid.” He had seen them rowing all night. He came to them on the water in the darkest hours, got into the boat, and the wind stopped. He never leaves His own alone in the storm.
