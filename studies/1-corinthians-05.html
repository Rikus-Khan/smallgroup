1 Corinthians 5

A little leaven, and a door left open

A man in the church lives in open sin, and the church is proud of how tolerant it is. Paul says: don't wink at it, and don't give up on him.

Where we are in 1 Corinthians

Paul started this church himself, and about AD 55 he is writing to it from Ephesus. The letter is a pastor's answers, one problem after another. Chapters 1 to 4 dealt with the church splitting into camps. Now word has reached him of something worse.

1–4Divisions
5–6Sin the church let in
7Marriage
8–10Food and freedom
11Order in worship
12–14Gifts, and love
15The resurrection
16The offering, goodbye

Chapters 5 and 6 go together: two kinds of mess the church had let in, a man living in open sin (chapter 5) and brothers dragging each other to court and treating their bodies carelessly (chapter 6). Read alone, this chapter makes sense: a church has a hard decision to make, and Paul tells it how to do it.

The news crosses the sea

Corinth was a rich, rough port city with a name across the Roman world for loose living. Paul spent about a year and a half there (Acts 18:11), and some of the believers came out of exactly that life.

Now he is across the Aegean in Ephesus, and the report reaches him: “It is reported commonly.” Everybody knew.

His answer sails back: this letter. If a church can get this wrong, so can any of us.

Part one

A thing they were proud of

Verses 1–5

The sin was bad. What shocked Paul more was that nobody was grieved.

What was wrong, and what they said about it

A man was living with his father's wife, his stepmother. God's law had forbidden it long before (Leviticus 18:8), and Paul says even the pagans around them didn't do it. It was no secret, no private weakness he was fighting. It was out in the open, and the church was puffed up: proud of it, or proud of itself in spite of it.

  • “Who are we to judge? We're open-minded.”that's not love, that's looking away
  • “It's his business. It's a private matter.”it wasn't private: it was commonly reported
  • “have not rather mourned”grieve, as a family grieves when a son is in trouble

Notice what Paul asks for. Not anger, not a witch hunt. Mourning. A church that really loved this man would be broken-hearted for him, and not just embarrassed. Love that ignores a man walking toward a cliff isn't kindness.

The goal: that he be saved, and come home

“Put away from among yourselves that wicked person” (5:13). When a man will not turn from open sin, the church stops treating it as normal. He is sent out of the fellowship, back to the world to feel what he has traded for it.

And why? “That the spirit may be saved.” It is a door held open, not slammed. By the time Paul writes again, about a year later, it seems to have worked (many Bible teachers think this is the same man): “Sufficient to such a man is this punishment… ye ought rather to forgive him, and comfort him” (2 Corinthians 2:6–8).

Hard words: “deliver unto Satan”

This is a strange phrase, and good Bible teachers explain it a little differently. Here is what is plain, and what isn't.

  • Unto SatanOut of the church's protection and fellowship, back into the world, where Satan rules.
  • Destruction of the fleshSome say the hardship and wreckage his sin would bring; some say his body suffering; some say his sinful nature broken. We can't know exactly.
  • That the spirit may be savedThis is the plain part. The aim is his rescue, not his ruin.

Notice it says “the spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord Jesus.” He is still treated as a brother in Christ in trouble, not as a man who lost his salvation. Salvation is not what is at stake in verse 5. His life, his witness, and his peace are.

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Part two

A little leaven

Verses 6–8

Sin that is allowed to sit quietly doesn't stay small. And the way out starts with what Christ has already done.

A little leaven

Leaven is yeast: a pinch of old, sour dough worked into a new batch makes the whole batch rise. In the Bible it is a picture of how something small spreads, and it is usually a picture of sin.

Give it time and it works through every piece. That is why Paul won't let the church shrug. One man's open sin, shrugged at, tells everyone else this is allowed here.

The same is true of us one at a time. The “little” habit, the little lie, the one drink you let slide: you can't see it working. Deal with it while it's small, and with a brother beside you.

Already unleavened

→

Look at the order. Paul does not say clean yourselves up so that Christ will accept you. He says you are unleavened already, because of the Lamb. So become what you are.

The lamb in Egypt, and the Lamb on the cross

“And the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you to destroy you, when I smite the land of Egypt.”

Exodus 12:13

“Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

On the night of the first Passover the lamb died in the family's place, and the house was cleared of leaven for the feast that followed. Our Passover Lamb has already died. The feast is the whole Christian life: “with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.” If you fell yesterday, that Lamb has not been un-sacrificed.

Part three

Inside and outside

Verses 9–13

Who Paul means, and who he doesn't. He is not telling us to cut off the world.

The letter before this letter

Verse 9 shows that Paul had written to them already. That first letter is lost. In it he told them not to mix with the sexually immoral, and they took it too far.

Paul, in the earlier letterDon't keep company with fornicators.

Some in CorinthEveryone in this city is one! We'd have to leave the world.

Paul, nowNot the world's sinners. Then you must needs go out of the world. I meant someone who calls himself a brother.

Christians have been getting this wrong ever since, mostly in one of two ways: shutting themselves away from everyone who isn't like them, or never saying a word to a brother who is wrecking himself. Paul steers between both.

Two groups, two ways

The world, “them that are without”

Keep eating with them

Work beside them, live next door, be their friend. God is their judge, not you. This is how they meet Jesus, through men who are around and kind.

A “brother”, “them that are within”

Step back, together

If a man who calls himself a brother is living in one of these and calling it fine: no easy fellowship, no not to eat. It is meant to wake him up.

“Then drew near unto him all the publicans and sinners for to hear him. And the Pharisees and scribes murmured, saying, This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them.”

Luke 15:1–2

Jesus ate with sinners, and that is exactly why they came to Him. Paul's rule is not about them. It protects a family from a brother who is hurting it, and it protects the brother from himself.

Who this is not about

Paul's list in verse 11 is a fornicator, a covetous man, an idolater, a railer (an abusive talker), a drunkard, an extortioner. Some of us have been in that list. So read it carefully.

The man Paul means

Keeps on, and calls it fine

He is a “brother” in name, and he goes right on, in the open, and when the church says something he shrugs. There is no grief and no turning.

The man Paul doesn't mean

Falls, and hates it

He had a drink last night and woke up sick about it. He is fighting. He is telling someone. That man doesn't need a cold shoulder. He needs a brother next to him.

“For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.”

Proverbs 24:16

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Points to remember

  1. Pride“Ye are puffed up, and have not rather mourned” 5:2The church was proud when it should have been broken-hearted. Love grieves over a brother's sin. It doesn't wink at it.
  2. The goal“That the spirit may be saved” 5:5Putting a man out is aimed at bringing him home. Later he is forgiven and comforted, and welcomed back.
  3. Leaven“A little leaven leaveneth the whole lump” 5:6Sin allowed to sit doesn't stay small. Deal with it early, with a brother next to you.
  4. Our passover“Christ our passover is sacrificed for us” 5:7The Lamb has already died. You are unleavened in Him, so live like it.
  5. The world“Then must ye needs go out of the world” 5:10Paul does not tell us to cut off sinners in the world. God judges them, and we keep loving them.
  6. A brother“If any man that is called a brother” 5:11The warning is for a brother who keeps on and calls it fine, not for the man who fell and hates it.

Question 1 of 6

Pride

What was wrong with the church's attitude, as well as the man's sin?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They were puffed up. They should have mourned. A church that loves a man grieves when he is in open sin, and doesn't brag about how tolerant it is.

Question 2 of 6

The goal

What is the goal when a church puts a man out?

What verses tell us about this?

“So that contrariwise ye ought rather to forgive him, and comfort him, lest perhaps such a one should be swallowed up with overmuch sorrow.”

2 Corinthians 2:7

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Answer

That the spirit may be saved: his rescue, not his ruin. When he turns, he is forgiven, comforted, and welcomed back.

Question 3 of 6

Leaven

What does a little leaven do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It leavens the whole lump. A little sin, allowed to sit, spreads. So purge out the old leaven and live with sincerity and truth.

Question 4 of 6

Our passover

Who is our passover?

What verses tell us about this?

“Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Answer

Christ. The Lamb of God was sacrificed for us. His blood is why judgment passes over us, and why we are already unleavened in Him.

Question 5 of 6

The world

Does Paul tell us to cut off the sinners of this world?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the Pharisees and scribes murmured, saying, This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them.”

Luke 15:2

Answer

No. To avoid them we would have to go out of the world. God judges those outside; we keep living among them, and keep loving them, as Jesus did.

Question 6 of 6

A brother

Who are we told not to keep company with?

What verses tell us about this?

“For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.”

Proverbs 24:16

Answer

A man who is called a brother and keeps living in open sin and calls it fine. The aim is to wake him up. A believer who falls and grieves is not that man. He is to be restored.
