John 9

Once blind, now I see

Jesus gives sight to a man born blind. The man sees more clearly every hour, while the religious experts go blind.

Part one

Go, wash

Verses 1–12

A man who has never seen a thing walks home seeing.

Whose fault is it?

The disciples assumed somebody must be to blame. Most people did back then, and plenty still do. They only argued over who.

  • “This man sinned”(before he was even born?)
  • “His parents sinned”(so he pays for their failure?)
  • “That the works of God should be made manifest in him”

Jesus isn't saying the man or his parents never sinned. He's saying this blindness wasn't a payback for some sin. Some of what we carry comes from our own choices, and we know it. But not all of it. And either way, Jesus doesn't stand around assigning blame. He gets to work.

He said it, then He showed it

John 8:12, the claim

“I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 9:7, the proof

“He went his way therefore, and washed, and came seeing.”

In chapter 8 Jesus said He is the light of the world, and they picked up stones. In chapter 9 He shows it, on a man who had lived his whole life in the dark.

“While it is day: the night cometh”: Jesus knew His time on earth was short, and He didn't waste it. Neither should we. Today is the day to do the good in front of you.

The pool called “Sent”

Siloam sat at the far south end of the old city, downhill from the temple. John tells us the name means Sent.

Picture the walk: a blind man, clay on his eyes, feeling his way down through the streets with nothing to go on but a word from Jesus.

Its water came through a tunnel King Hezekiah cut through solid rock about seven hundred years earlier (2 Kings 20:20).

And who is the Sent One? Jesus calls the Father “him that sent me” (9:4). The man washed in Sent, at the word of the One who was sent.

He came seeing

Jesus could have healed him with a word. Instead He gave him something to do: go, and wash.

The man didn't ask why, and he didn't wait until he could see before he obeyed. He went with the clay still on his eyes. The sight came on the far side of obedience.

That's how it usually goes. You take the step Jesus gives you while you still can't see how it will turn out. Then you come seeing.

“I am he”

The neighbors had walked past this man for years. To them he was “he that sat and begged.” Now some of them can't even recognize him.

the blind beggar → I am he

He doesn't hide from his past, and he doesn't stay stuck in it. Yes, he's the same man. No, he's not the same at all. When Jesus changes a man, the people who knew him before will notice, and some won't know what to make of it.

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Part two

On trial

Verses 13–34

A healed man gets questioned, pressured, insulted, and thrown out.

The sabbath problem

God's law said to rest on the seventh day. Over time the teachers piled their own rules on top, and later wrote down 39 kinds of work that were banned on the sabbath.

Sowing, plowing, reaping, grinding, baking, writing, building, lighting a fire …

One of them was kneading. By their rules, mixing spit and dirt into clay was kneading, and healing a man whose life wasn't in danger had to wait for another day.

He'd been blind since birth. Jesus could have waited one more day. He didn't. He chose the sabbath on purpose, to show that the Lord of the sabbath cares more about a man than about a rulebook men had added to God's word.

Put out

Being “put out of the synagogue” meant being cut off from worship, from your neighbors, and often from your work and family. You became an outsider in your own town.

His parents→“we know not … ask him”They told the truth about what they knew, and went quiet about Jesus. They were afraid.
Their son→“one thing I know”He spoke up anyway, and it cost him.

Fear of what people will think keeps a lot of men quiet, and stuck. Following Jesus may cost you some friends, maybe some old crowds. Hold on: watch what Jesus does for this man at the end of the chapter.

One thing I know: whereas I was blind, now I see.

They wanted to drag him into a theology debate. He wouldn't take the bait. He couldn't answer all their questions, but he could tell them what had happened to him, and nobody could argue with that.

You don't need a Bible degree to be a witness. You need a before and an after. A man who was a slave-ship captain, John Newton, met this same Jesus and wrote it into a song: “I once was lost, but now am found, was blind, but now I see.”

“Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee.”

Mark 5:19

Asked again, and again

Count how many times somebody asks how it happened:

“How were thine eyes opened?”The neighbors, 9:10Clay. Wash. See.
“How he had received his sight”The Pharisees, 9:15Same story
“How then doth he now see?”The Pharisees to his parents, 9:19Ask him
“How opened he thine eyes?”The Pharisees again, 9:26Told you already

The facts never changed. What changed was the man. Every time he's pushed, he gets bolder, until he asks them, “Will ye also be his disciples?” They answer with insults.

Never been done

In all of the Old Testament, nobody born blind is ever given sight. The man is right: this had never happened. But the prophets said it would, when God Himself came:

“Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped.”

Isaiah 35:5

A beggar with no schooling reads the evidence better than the experts: “If this man were not of God, he could do nothing.”

Does God hear sinners?

The man is using plain Old Testament sense: God doesn't back a man who holds on to his sin and claims God's power.

“If I regard iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear me:”

Psalm 66:18

But don't hear it as “God won't listen to a man like me.” There's one prayer from a sinner God always hears: the one where he stops hiding and comes home.

“And the publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner.”

Luke 18:13

Jesus said that man went home made right with God.

Part three

Found

Verses 35–41

They threw him out. Jesus went looking for him.

Thrown out, then found

The Pharisees→cast him out→the healed manThe religious world shut the door on him.
Jesus→found→himJesus heard about it and went looking for him.

Notice: until now, the man had never actually seen Jesus. He was blind when Jesus sent him to the pool. This is the first time he looks at the face of the One who healed him.

“For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.”

Luke 19:10

If following Jesus has cost you people, you haven't been left alone. He comes looking for His own.

His eyes kept opening

The man sees more and more

  1. 9:11“A man that is called Jesus”
  2. 9:17“He is a prophet”
  3. 9:33“Of God”
  4. 9:38“Lord, I believe”

The Pharisees see less and less

  1. 9:16“Not of God”
  2. 9:24“A sinner”
  3. 9:29“We know not from whence he is”
  4. 9:41“Your sin remaineth”

His eyes opened in a moment. His understanding opened a step at a time, and it ended on his knees. “He worshipped him.” Jesus accepted it, because He is God.

Who's really blind?

The man born blind

“I know not”

He admitted what he couldn't see. Jesus gave him sight, then faith.

The Pharisees

“We see”

They were sure they saw everything. So they missed the Son of God standing in front of them.

The most dangerous blindness is the kind that says, “I'm fine. I see just fine.” Admitting you can't see is where seeing starts. The man who says, “I need help,” is the one Jesus can heal.

Points to remember

  1. Suffering“That the works of God should be made manifest in him” 9:3Not every hardship is a punishment. Jesus doesn't assign blame. He goes to work.
  2. Who Jesus is“I am the light of the world” 9:5He said it, then proved it on a man who had never seen light.
  3. Obedience“He went his way therefore, and washed, and came seeing” 9:7He obeyed before he could see. The sight came on the far side of obedience.
  4. Your story“One thing I know, that, whereas I was blind, now I see” 9:25You don't need every answer. Tell what He's done for you.
  5. The cost“They had cast him out; and when he had found him” 9:35When following Jesus costs you, He comes looking for you.
  6. Seeing clearly“Now ye say, We see; therefore your sin remaineth” 9:41The truly blind are the ones who say they see just fine.

Question 1 of 6

Suffering

Is every hardship a punishment for sin?

What verses tell us about this?

“And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.”

Romans 8:28

Answer

No. The disciples assumed somebody was to blame, but Jesus said this man's blindness wasn't a payback for anyone's sin. Some of our trouble does come from our own choices. But Jesus doesn't stand over a hurting man assigning blame. He goes to work, and He can turn even a lifetime of darkness into a showcase of what God can do.

Question 2 of 6

Who Jesus is

What does it mean that Jesus is the light of the world?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Answer

Without Him, every one of us is in the dark: about God, about ourselves, about where we're headed. Jesus doesn't just point toward the light. He is the light. He proved it by opening eyes that had never seen, and He still opens the eyes of anyone who follows Him.

Question 3 of 6

Obedience

What did the blind man have to do to be healed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Just what Jesus told him: go, and wash. The power was all Jesus', but the man had to take Him at His word and walk to the pool while he still couldn't see. He obeyed first and saw second. Faith usually works that way.

Question 4 of 6

Your story

What can you say about Jesus when you don't have all the answers?

What verses tell us about this?

“Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee.”

Mark 5:19

Answer

You can say what He's done for you. The man couldn't win a debate, but he could say, “I was blind, now I see,” and nobody could argue with it. Your before and after is your testimony. Tell it plainly.

Question 5 of 6

The cost

What happens when following Jesus costs you?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Answer

The man lost his place in the synagogue, and maybe his friends and family's support. But the moment he was thrown out, Jesus came and found him. People may walk away when you follow Jesus. He never will.

Question 6 of 6

Seeing clearly

Who are the truly blind in this chapter?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The Pharisees. They had perfect eyesight and all the Bible learning, and they still couldn't see the Son of God in front of them, because they were sure they already saw. The blind beggar admitted what he didn't know, and he ended up seeing best of all. Saying “I need help” is where sight begins.
