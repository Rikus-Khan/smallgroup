Numbers 28

The offerings of every day

The people are at the edge of the land, and the LORD sets in order what is to be offered on the plain day, the sabbath, the first of the month, the passover week and the day of the firstfruits. One table holds it all. Hebrews says why it never ended, and why it did.

Part one

“My bread for my sacrifices”

Verses 1–8

A lamb in the morning and a lamb at even, every day, without a break.

Notice who owns it: “my offering, and my bread for my sacrifices.” The LORD calls it his bread. The people brought it, and it was his. And notice where the wine went: “poured unto the LORD.” Poured out “in the holy place,” not one cup of it for any man. Some of you have a history with the cup. This is the only wine in Numbers 28, and it goes to the LORD.

“Now this is that which thou shalt offer upon the altar; two lambs of the first year day by day continually. The one lamb thou shalt offer in the morning; and the other lamb thou shalt offer at even:”

Exodus 29:38–39

This is not new. Verse 6 says so: “which was ordained in mount Sinai.” The people who stand on the edge of the land are a new generation, and the LORD tells them again what their fathers were told at the mountain. Their fathers fell in the wilderness, and the word the LORD gave at the mountain still stands for their children.

The lamb, and the morning

Every dawn began at an altar, with a lamb already burning. Nobody woke up in Israel to a day God had not yet been to. Lamentations, which Jeremiah wrote, puts it this way:

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

And the lamb that was offered at the day's start and the day's end points to the one John named:

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

Whatever you did yesterday, you wake up to a lamb already given. The day does not start with your record. It starts with his mercies.

Part two

“Beside the continual burnt offering”

Verses 9–15

The sabbath and the first of the month: the daily lambs stay, and more is added.

Beside, and not instead

  • besidein addition to, on top of
  • severalseparate: one tenth deal for each lamb, each its own
  • the beginnings of your monthsthe first day of every month
  • kida young goat

The word “beside” comes five times in this chapter (10, 15, 23, 24, 31). On the sabbath, on the first of the month, in the passover week, the daily lambs still go up, and the new offerings are laid beside them. A feast does not replace the ordinary day. The plain morning and the plain evening are the floor everything else stands on.

“Or have ye not read in the law, how that on the sabbath days the priests in the temple profane the sabbath, and are blameless? But I say unto you, That in this place is one greater than the temple.”

Matthew 12:5–6

Jesus used this very thing. On the sabbath the priests worked, and the LORD told them to add two lambs that day (verse 9), and they were blameless, because the work was the LORD's. He said one greater than the temple was standing there.

Read down the lambs: two every day, two more on the sabbath, seven more on a feast. The passover itself, the fourteenth day (16), has no count here: its lamb was told in Exodus 12. And look at the goat: one for a sin offering on every new month and feast day (15, 22, 30), never on a plain day. The feasts did not hide sin. They named it, and gave the answer.

Always the same, and never enough

The priests, day after day

“Daily… oftentimes the same”

“And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins” (Hebrews 10:11).

The Lamb, once

“One sacrifice for ever”

“But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God” (Hebrews 10:12).

A priest standeth, because his work is never finished. Christ sat down, because his is. The lambs of Numbers 28 were not a mistake: they were a shadow, and a daily one, so that no man would forget that sin must be answered. But the end of the matter is here: “For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified” (Hebrews 10:14).

Needing it again, and not needing it again

“Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people’s: for this he did once, when he offered up himself.”

Hebrews 7:27

There were men in that camp who woke up each morning to the same lamb and wondered whether it was enough. Many of you wake up that way: carrying yesterday, sure it has to be paid for again today. The lamb on the altar had to be offered again. He “needeth not daily.” You do not have to bring him again, and he does not have to be put on the altar again. “Once” means once.

That is not a licence to treat sin lightly. It is the reason a man can stop hiding. Confess it, and take what has been done for you (1 John 1:9), then go to work.

Real world: before the day, and after it

  • SinThe goat was on the feast days, not the plain days. But the plain days are where men fall: a Tuesday, a paycheck, a ride home. Name it honestly and early.
  • RegretThe lamb was already burning before the man woke up. Yesterday does not get the first word today.
  • Grace“Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.” Not daily, but once.
  • Hope“They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.” Each morning is a fresh start he provided.
  • TodayTwo small moments: one when you wake, one at the end of the day. Morning: thank him for the new mercies. Evening: say what went wrong and what went right, and text one brother in Christ.

Points to remember

  1. Every day“two lambs of the first year without spot day by day, for a continual burnt offering” 28:3One lamb in the morning, one at even, every day, with flour, oil and wine.
  2. The sabbath“on the sabbath day two lambs of the first year without spot” 28:9Two more lambs, beside the continual burnt offering.
  3. The month“in the beginnings of your months ye shall offer a burnt offering unto the LORD; two young bullocks, and one ram, seven lambs of the first year without spot” 28:11Two bullocks, a ram and seven lambs, on the first of every month.
  4. The passover week“in the fourteenth day of the first month is the passover of the LORD” 28:16The passover on the fourteenth, and seven days of unleavened bread from the fifteenth.
  5. The goat“And one goat for a sin offering, to make an atonement for you.” 28:22One goat on the new month and on every feast day, to make atonement.
  6. Once for all“after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God” Hebrews 10:12The priests offered the same sacrifices daily. Christ offered one, for ever, and sat down.

Question 1 of 6

Every day

What was offered every day, and at what two times?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Answer

Two lambs of the first year without spot, a continual burnt offering: one in the morning, the other at even.

Question 2 of 6

The sabbath

What was added on the sabbath day?

What verses tell us about this?

“Or have ye not read in the law, how that on the sabbath days the priests in the temple profane the sabbath, and are blameless?”

Matthew 12:5

Answer

Two more lambs of the first year without spot, with two tenth deals of flour, and the drink offering, beside the continual burnt offering.

Question 3 of 6

The month

What was offered in the beginnings of the months?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A burnt offering: two young bullocks, one ram, and seven lambs of the first year without spot, with flour, oil and wine. Every month of the year.

Question 4 of 6

The passover week

On which day of the first month is the passover, and how many days is unleavened bread eaten?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The passover is the fourteenth day of the first month. The feast begins on the fifteenth, and unleavened bread is eaten seven days, with the same offering each day.

Question 5 of 6

The goat

What was offered for a sin offering on the new month and the feast days?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

One kid of the goats for a sin offering, “to make an atonement for you”: on the new month (15), on each day of the feast (22), and on the day of the firstfruits (30).

Question 6 of 6

Once for all

What does Hebrews say Christ did that the daily sacrifices never could?
