Mark 5

Clothed, and in his right mind

A man no chains could hold, a woman no doctor could heal, and a girl it was already too late for. Jesus meets all three.

Part one

My name is Legion

Verses 1–20

Across the lake, among the graves, lives a man nobody could hold down and nobody could help.

The other side

At evening Jesus had said, “Let us pass over unto the other side” (4:35), and then stilled a storm on the way. They landed on the east shore.

This was the country of the Gadarenes, named for Gadara, a town southeast of the lake. It belonged to the Decapolis, “ten cities” of Greek-speaking Gentiles. That's why there are pigs here (v. 11). No Jewish farmer would keep them.

Jesus crossed a storm to reach one man. Then He went straight back (v. 21).

Nothing could hold him

Fetters are chains for the feet. They'd tried everything, more than once: “often bound.”

  • chains“plucked asunder by him”
  • fetters“broken in pieces”
  • anyone strong enough to tame him“neither could any man”
  • Jesus“when he saw Jesus afar off, he ran” (v. 6)

Night and day, alone among the graves, screaming and cutting himself. Luke adds that he wore no clothes and lived in no house (Luke 8:27). Some of us know what it is to live among dead things and hurt ourselves on purpose.

Chains can hold a man for a while. They can't make him new. Rules, jail, promises to yourself: they restrain, but they don't change the man inside. That takes Someone stronger.

An army in one man

Legion a Roman army unit, the troops that held the land down up to about 6,000 soldiers

Jesus asked the man his name, and the devils answered for him. That's what they'd done to him: taken his name, his mind, his home, his family. All that was left was “we are many.”

But look who's in charge. They “besought him much,” begged Him. A whole army of devils, and not one of them could argue with Him. They knew Him at once: “Son of the most high God.”

He ran to Jesus anyway

The man's feet (v. 6)He ran, and fell down at Jesus' feet.

The voices in him (v. 7)What have I to do with thee, Jesus? Torment me not.

Jesus (v. 8)Come out of the man, thou unclean spirit.

Two things were going on in the same body. Something in that man ran toward Jesus, while the voices inside screamed to be left alone.

Maybe that's you. Part of you wants Jesus, and part of you is yelling, “Leave me alone.” Run anyway. He isn't listening to the voices. He's speaking to the man.

About two thousand

each dot is 50 swine

Swine were unclean animals under the Law (Leviticus 11:7). The devils asked to go into them, and Jesus “gave them leave,” let them go.

Watch what they did the moment they got in: two thousand animals ran off a cliff and drowned. That is what they had been doing to the man, slowly. The enemy doesn't want to use you. He wants to destroy you.

The whole town knew this man. Now they found him calm, dressed, talking sense. It wasn't a program or a cleanup. It was a new man.

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

“Depart out of our coasts”

Coasts means borders, their part of the country. The herdsmen ran and told everybody. The town came out, saw the man sitting there, and “they were afraid.”

Then they begged Jesus to leave. A power like that might cost them more pigs. They'd rather have their herd than a God they couldn't control.

And He went. Jesus doesn't stay where He isn't wanted. Some of us have asked Him to leave because the change was going to cost us something. He's still near. Ask Him back.

Go home and tell

The town asked Jesus to go. The man asked to go with Him. Jesus “suffered him not”, didn't allow it. That wasn't a no to the man. It was a job.

Jesusset him freethe manwent homehis friendsheard firstDecapolisten cities“all men did marvel”

Elsewhere Jesus told people to keep quiet (1:44, and again in v. 43). Here, among Gentiles who had just sent Him away, He leaves a preacher behind. When Jesus came back to the Decapolis later, people there brought a deaf man to Him for healing (7:31–32).

He didn't need a Bible school. He had two things to tell: “how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee.” So do you. The hardest place to tell it is home, where they knew you before. That's where He sends you first.

“Come and hear, all ye that fear God, and I will declare what he hath done for my soul.”

Psalm 66:16

Part two

Who touched my clothes?

Verses 21–34

A ruler falls at His feet for his dying girl. On the way, a woman nobody would touch reaches out from behind.

One long stretch of days

Mark tells these stories one right after another, with almost no rest between:

  1. 4:35evening“pass over”
  2. 4:39the storm“Peace, be still”
  3. 5:1the tombsLegion
  4. 5:21back acrossJairus
  5. 5:25on the waythe woman
  6. 5:41the house“arise”

Jairus was a ruler of the synagogue, the man who ran the meetings in his town. Synagogue leaders were already turning against Jesus (3:1–6). Jairus came anyway, and fell on his face in front of everyone.

A dying child will strip the pride off any man. Desperation is not a bad place to start with Jesus. It's where most of us started.

Spent everything, and worse

year 12

An issue of blood is bleeding that wouldn't stop, likely for twelve straight years. She went from doctor to doctor, “suffered many things” at their hands, and paid for every one of them until the money was gone. And she “rather grew worse.”

Some of us have a story just like it. Every fix we could buy, every place that promised to help, and we came out worse and broke. She hadn't tried Jesus yet. “When she had heard of Jesus,” she came.

Unclean, for twelve years

“And if a woman have an issue of her blood many days out of the time of her separation, or if it run beyond the time of her separation; all the days of the issue of her uncleanness shall be as the days of her separation: she shall be unclean.”

Leviticus 15:25

Under the Law

Her touch made others unclean

No worship at the temple, no hand on her shoulder, for twelve years. That's why she came “in the press behind,” hidden in the crowd.

With Jesus

His touch made her clean

She touched Him, and her uncleanness didn't spread to Him. His wholeness spread to her: “straightway the fountain of her blood was dried up.”

Many pressed, one touched

Virtue here means power. A whole crowd was thronging Him, pushing and bumping against Him. The disciples thought the question was silly. But only one of them reached out to Him in faith, and Jesus felt the difference.

You can sit close to Jesus every day, in church, in a group like this one, and never reach for Him. Being near Him isn't the same as trusting Him. He knows the difference, and He's looking round for the one who reaches.

“Daughter”

She was already healed. So why didn't Jesus let her slip away? Because He wanted more for her than a dry body.

  1. “told him all the truth”no more hiding
  2. “Daughter”an outcast given a family name
  3. “go in peace”and everyone heard it: she was clean

She came trembling, expecting to be scolded for touching Him. She left with a family name. The faith that healed her was small and shaky, a reach from behind. It was enough, because of who she reached for.

Two daughters

Mark wraps the woman's story inside Jairus' story, and the two belong together:

Part three

Be not afraid, only believe

Verses 35–43

The worst news comes. Jesus answers it before Jairus can say a word.

When the news is worst

Men from his houseThy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?

JesusBe not afraid, only believe.

Think about Jairus. He had to stand there while Jesus stopped for a woman who'd been sick twelve years, and in that time his girl died. The delay was real, and so was the loss.

But “as soon as Jesus heard,” He spoke. He didn't let the bad news have the last word for a single minute. Only believe: keep on trusting Me. Not “feel brave.” Just keep holding on to Him.

“And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.”

Mark 9:24

Not dead, but sleeping

A death meant loud mourning: wailing women, and flute players hired to lead it. Ado means fuss, uproar.

“And when Jesus came into the ruler’s house, and saw the minstrels and the people making a noise,”

Matthew 9:23
dead → sleepeth to Jesus, death is something He wakes you from

They “laughed him to scorn”, because they knew she was dead. Jesus wasn't denying it. He was saying it wouldn't last. He put the scoffers out, and took in only the parents and three disciples.

“For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.”

1 Thessalonians 4:14

The words Peter never forgot

Talitha cumi Aramaic, the everyday language of Galilee “Little girl, get up”

Mark keeps the very words Jesus said. Peter was in that room, and Mark wrote down what Peter preached. He never forgot the sound of it: the words a father would use to wake his daughter in the morning.

He took her by the hand. Touching a dead body made a man unclean under the Law (Numbers 19:11). Jesus touched death, and death let go.

Give her something to eat

The room is in shock, “astonished with a great astonishment.” And Jesus says: get the girl some food.

The Lord of life cares about breakfast. After the big day comes the ordinary one, and He's in that too. After He sets a man free, there's still eating right, sleeping, showing up, doing the next small thing. That's not a step down from the miracle. That's how a new life gets lived.

“He charged them straitly”, strictly, to keep it quiet. Crowds were already crushing Him. He came to be known as Savior, not as a wonder-worker people chased for a show.

Too strong, too long, too late

Three people in one chapter that everyone else had given up on:

  1. Too strong“no man could bind him” (v. 3)
  2. Too longtwelve years, “and had spent all that she had” (v. 26)
  3. Too late“Thy daughter is dead” (v. 35)

Whatever has you, however long it's had you, however dead it looks: none of it is past Him. The man was set free, the woman made whole, the girl raised up.

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

Points to remember

  1. Bondage“Neither could any man tame him” 5:4Chains can hold a man for a while, but they can't change him. Only Jesus sets a man free.
  2. Changed“Sitting, and clothed, and in his right mind” 5:15Jesus doesn't patch a man up. He makes him new.
  3. Witness“Go home to thy friends, and tell them” 5:19Your story of what He did for you is your testimony, and it starts at home.
  4. Faith“If I may touch but his clothes, I shall be whole” 5:28Small, shaky faith is enough when it reaches for Jesus.
  5. Fear“Be not afraid, only believe” 5:36When the worst news comes, keep trusting Him. He has the last word.
  6. Life“The damsel is not dead, but sleepeth” 5:39To Jesus, death is a sleep He wakes us from. He has power over it.

Question 1 of 6

Bondage

Why couldn't chains help the man among the tombs?

What verses tell us about this?

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

Answer

Chains only held him from the outside, and he broke every one. His trouble was inside: an army of unclean spirits. No man could tame him. It took Jesus, the Son of the most high God, to drive them out and set him free.

Question 2 of 6

Changed

How did the people find the man after Jesus set him free?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

Sitting, clothed, and in his right mind. The man who ran screaming night and day was calm at Jesus' feet. That's what Jesus does: He makes a new man.

Question 3 of 6

Witness

Where did Jesus send the man, and what was he to tell?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come and hear, all ye that fear God, and I will declare what he hath done for my soul.”

Psalm 66:16

Answer

Home, to his friends. He was to tell how great things the Lord had done for him and how He had had compassion on him. He did, all through the Decapolis, and people marveled. Every saved man has that same story to tell.

Question 4 of 6

Faith

What did the woman do when she heard of Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

She came through the crowd and touched His clothes, believing it was enough. She had spent everything else. Then she told Him all the truth, and He called her “Daughter” and sent her away whole and in peace.

Question 5 of 6

Fear

What did Jesus tell Jairus when the news was worst?

What verses tell us about this?

“And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.”

Mark 9:24

Answer

“Be not afraid, only believe.” As soon as the bad news came, Jesus spoke over it. Jairus didn't have to feel brave. He only had to keep trusting Jesus, even when it looked too late.

Question 6 of 6

Life

What did Jesus call the girl's death, and what did He do about it?

What verses tell us about this?

“For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.”

1 Thessalonians 4:14

Answer

He called it sleep, because He was about to wake her. He took her by the hand and said, “Talitha cumi,” and she got up and walked. For everyone who trusts Him, death is a sleep, and He will wake us too.
