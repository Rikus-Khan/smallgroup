Genesis 18

Three visitors, and a man who pleads

The LORD comes to Abraham's tent for a meal, repeats the promise of a son, and asks Sarah a question that has only one answer. Then he tells Abraham about Sodom, and Abraham does something bold: he pleads for the city.

Part one

Three men at the tent door

Verses 1–8

An old man in the heat of the day sees three strangers, and he runs.

Mamre, and the road to Sodom

Abraham's tent stood in the oak grove at Mamre, by Hebron, up in the hills. Sodom lay down by the Dead Sea, about thirty miles away.

It is midday, “the heat of the day”, when anyone with sense stays in the shade. He sat in the “tent door”, the opening where the air moves. Verse 16 will say the men “looked toward Sodom”. That is where this visit is heading.

Abraham is ninety-nine, sitting quietly in the heat. Then three strangers walk up out of the shimmer.

Three men. Who are they?

One of the three

The LORD

Verse 1 says it first: “the LORD appeared unto him.” Verses 13, 17 and 20 keep calling the speaker the LORD. This is the Son of God, long before Bethlehem. The text itself says plainly: God, in the form of a man.

The other two

Angels

They leave for Sodom in verse 22, and the next chapter opens: “there came two angels to Sodom at even” (19:1). Same two.

“Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares.”

Hebrews 13:2

Abraham didn't know at first. He treated three dusty strangers like kings. That is what the New Testament points back to: welcome the stranger, because you never know.

Notice “a morsel of bread” from a man who then orders a whole calf and a big batch of bread. Abraham says small and gives big. That is real welcome.

An old man in a hurry

Count the words of hurry in verses 1 to 8: ran, hastened, ran, hasted.

1  2ran 3  4  5  6hastened 7ran, hasted 8 

He is ninety-nine, in the heat of the day, and he is running. He serves them himself, and then “he stood by them under the tree” while they ate, like a waiter, not like a host.

Real life: a new man walks into your group, or your church, or your work crew. Be the one who gets up and goes to meet him. A glass of water and ten minutes of your time can change a man's week.

Part two

Is any thing too hard for the LORD?

Verses 9–15

A woman laughs behind a tent door, and the LORD hears what she didn't say.

They knew her name

The visitorsWhere is Sarah thy wife?

AbrahamBehold, in the tent.

The LORDI will certainly return unto thee according to the time of life; and, lo, Sarah thy wife shall have a son. a promise

Strangers don't know a wife's name. This is the first sign that the man in the middle is more than a traveller.

“According to the time of life” means when the season of life comes round again, about this time next year (verse 14 says “at the time appointed”). It is the third time God has promised Abraham a son by Sarah (15:4, 17:16, and now). Sarah was behind him, in the tent door, listening.

The laugh nobody heard

Sarah, to herselfAfter I am waxed old shall I have pleasure, my lord being old also?

The LORD, to AbrahamWherefore did Sarah laugh, saying, Shall I of a surety bear a child, which am old? He heard

Sarah laughed “within herself”. She said nothing out loud, and the LORD answered her anyway. Nothing we think is hidden from him.

Notice one more thing. Sarah's thought was “after I am waxed old … my lord being old also”. The LORD repeats her half about herself and leaves out the half about her husband. He answers her fear and doesn't pile on.

She had waited twenty-four years, and the Hagar shortcut had cost her. That isn't a scoffer's laugh. It is the laugh of a woman who has stopped letting herself hope. Many of you know that laugh.

“Too hard”

פֶּלֶא said pele “a wonder”: beyond what a man can do or explain

The Hebrew behind “too hard” comes from the word for a wonder. So the question carries this too: Is anything too wonderful for the LORD? Not too hard. Not too far gone. The very thing that looks hopeless to us is where he likes to show up.

“Ah Lord GOD! behold, thou hast made the heaven and the earth by thy great power and stretched out arm, and there is nothing too hard for thee:”

Jeremiah 32:17

“For with God nothing shall be impossible.”

Luke 1:37

“I laughed not”

SarahI laughed not. afraid

The LORDNay; but thou didst laugh.

Here is sin, named plainly: a lie, told out of fear. “She was afraid”, so she lied. Most of our lies start exactly there. We don't lie because we are wicked all through; we lie because we're scared of what's coming.

And see how gentle the LORD is. No lightning, no lecture. He says the true thing once, and leaves it. The promise doesn't change.

A laugh, a lie, and still faith

A year later Sarah has a son, and the New Testament counts her among the people of faith:

“Through faith also Sara herself received strength to conceive seed, and was delivered of a child when she was past age, because she judged him faithful who had promised.”

Hebrews 11:11

A laugh of doubt and a lie of fear, and God still counted her faith. If you have lied lately, take it to him and to a brother in Christ, because “he is faithful and just to forgive” (1 John 1:9).

Part three

Shall I hide from Abraham?

Verses 16–22

God treats a man as a friend, and lets him in on what he is about to do.

Why Abraham is told

God says“I know him”
→
Abraham willcommand his children and household
→
so they willkeep the way of the LORD

God treats Abraham as a friend, and a friend is told. “Shall I hide from Abraham that thing which I do?” The Almighty could simply have acted. Instead he talks it over with a man.

“I know him” is also a plain statement about fathers. God knew Abraham would command (teach, give charge to) his children, and that his house would “do justice and judgment”: do right, and be fair in how they treated people.

Whatever you did to your kids' trust before you got sober, God can still make you a man he can point to and say “I know him”. It starts with the next conversation at the table, today.

“I will go down now, and see”

God didn't need to go and look. He says it to show that he is fair: he sees for himself before he acts.

“Behold, this was the iniquity of thy sister Sodom, pride, fulness of bread, and abundance of idleness was in her and in her daughters, neither did she strengthen the hand of the poor and needy. And they were haughty, and committed abomination before me: therefore I took them away as I saw good.”

Ezekiel 16:49-50

Notice what the prophet lists: pride, plenty, idleness, and no help for the poor. The next chapter shows the worst of what the city had become. “The cry … is great” is the cry of the people being wronged, rising like the blood of Abel (4:10).

The two angels go on toward Sodom. The LORD stays, and Abraham “stood yet before the LORD”. He is not done.

Part four

The Judge of all the earth

Verses 23–33

The first long prayer in the Bible is a man pleading for a wicked city.

Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?

Abraham doesn't ask for mercy first. He asks about justice: Would You sweep away the good with the bad? And he answers his own question by who God is. He knows the character of the One he is talking to. (“That be far from thee to do after this manner” means far be it from You to do such a thing.)

That is the foundation of every prayer: not “I hope God is nice”, but “I know who He is”. The Judge of all the earth does right, always. That is hard news for the proud, and good news for the man who has been wronged, and for the man who is trusting Christ instead of himself.

“Dust and ashes”

עָפָר וָאֵפֶר said afar va-efer “dust and ashes”: the stuff a man is made of, and what is left when it burns

Abraham is bold and small at the same time. He argues with God and calls himself dust. Boldness without humility is pride, and humility without boldness is fear. He has both, because he knows the One he is asking.

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

You are allowed to come to him as you are, “dust and ashes”, and you are told to come boldly. Ask.

Fifty, down to ten

  1. 50spared for their sake (v. 26)
  2. 45not destroyed (v. 28)
  3. 40“I will not do it” (v. 29)
  4. 30“I will not do it” (v. 30)
  5. 20not destroyed (v. 31)
  6. 10“for ten's sake” (v. 32)

Six times Abraham asked, and six times the answer was yes. Then Abraham stopped asking. The LORD didn't stop saying yes. Nobody knows what he would have said to five, because Abraham stopped at ten. (“Communing” in verse 33 just means talking together.)

And look at what he never says. Abraham never once says “Lot”. He prays for the city on the principle, and God hears the name he didn't say. The next chapter tells what happened.

Who prays for the guilty?

Abraham stands between God and a city that does not deserve it, and asks for mercy. Pray like that for the people in your life who are in a mess of their own making.

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.”

James 5:16

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Abraham asked God to spare the wicked for the sake of ten righteous. Jesus is the One Righteous Man who stands before the Father for guilty men, and he never stops. Someone is already praying for you, and it is him.

Points to remember

  1. Welcome“he ran to meet them from the tent door” 18:2Welcome strangers like Abraham did. You may be entertaining more than you know.
  2. The impossible“Is any thing too hard for the LORD?” 18:14Nothing is too wonderful for him, not even the thing you gave up on.
  3. Fear and lies“I laughed not; for she was afraid.” 18:15Fear makes liars of us. God sees it, says it gently, and keeps his promise anyway.
  4. A friend of God“Shall I hide from Abraham that thing which I do” 18:17God lets those he knows in on what he is doing, and he wants fathers who teach their children.

Question 1 of 6

Welcome

Who came to Abraham's tent, and how did Abraham treat them?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares.”

Hebrews 13:2

Answer

The LORD and two angels, in the form of men. Abraham ran to meet them, bowed, offered them water for their feet, and gave them his best: a calf, fresh bread and his own hands. He welcomed strangers without knowing who they were.

Question 2 of 6

The impossible

Is any thing too hard for the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

“Ah Lord GOD! behold, thou hast made the heaven and the earth by thy great power and stretched out arm, and there is nothing too hard for thee:”

Jeremiah 32:17

“For with God nothing shall be impossible.”

Luke 1:37

Answer

No. The Hebrew word comes from wonder, something beyond man's power, and the LORD does wonders. An old woman would have a son at the set time. Whatever you have given up on, you can bring it to him, and to your brothers in Christ who will pray with you.

Question 3 of 6

Fear and lies

What did Sarah do when the LORD asked about her laugh?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

She lied: “I laughed not.” She was afraid. The LORD said once, “Nay; but thou didst laugh,” and the promise stood. When you've lied out of fear, don't hide it. Confess it, and he is faithful to forgive.

Question 4 of 6

A friend of God

Why did the LORD tell Abraham what he was about to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because he knew Abraham: a man who would teach his children and his household the way of the LORD, to do what is right and fair. God shares his plans with those he knows. A father who teaches his house the way of the LORD is the kind of man God lets in.

Question 5 of 6

The Judge

Does the Judge of all the earth do right?

What verses tell us about this?

“Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?”

Ezekiel 33:11

Answer

Yes. He always does right, and he takes no pleasure in judging. Six times he agreed to spare a city for the sake of a few good men. You can trust him with your case, and with the people you can't fix.

Question 6 of 6

Prayer

What can we learn from the way Abraham prayed?

What verses tell us about this?

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.”

James 5:16

Answer

Pray humbly (“dust and ashes”) and boldly, on the strength of who God is. Ask again. And pray for others, even for people who have made a wreck of their lives. Ask your brothers in Christ to pray with you, and for you.
