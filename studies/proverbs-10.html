Part one

A new kind of book

Verses 1–12

The long speeches are over. Now come the sayings, two lines at a time.

How to read a proverb

Chapters 1–9 were long talks from a father to his son. From here on, each verse mostly stands alone. Hebrew poetry doesn't rhyme sounds. It rhymes ideas: line two answers line one, piece by piece.

line 1A wise sonmaketh a gladfather:
oppositesoppositesthe other parent
buta foolish sonis the heaviness ofhis mother.

So slow down and read each line twice. Ask: what matches what? The meaning lives in the pairing. Here: your choices land on the people who love you, for joy or for grief.

Three ways two lines pair up

≠ Contrast: the flip side A wise son maketh a glad father: but a foolish son is the heaviness of his mother. (10:1) Most of chapters 10–15 work this way. Watch for the word “but.”
= Echo: the same thing again The liberal soul shall be made fat: and he that watereth shall be watered also himself. (11:25) Line two says it again in new words, to drive it home.
≈ Picture: “as … so” As vinegar to the teeth, and as smoke to the eyes, so is the sluggard to them that send him. (10:26) Line one paints a picture; line two tells you what it means.

In Israel the harvest came in a window of a few months. Grain left in the field spoils or gets eaten. Sleep through it, and your family is hungry all winter.

Verse 4 puts it plainly: a “slack hand” leads to being poor, and a diligent one (steady, careful, sharp) builds something. Showing up to work every day isn't small. For a man rebuilding his life, it's part of the rebuilding.

A proverb isn't a promise

Does every hard worker get rich? Does no godly man ever go hungry? You know better. So what is a proverb?

  • a promise with a deadlineGod doesn't owe you a raise by Friday
  • a guarantee in every casegood men still get sick, laid off, and robbed
  • a magic formulado X, and God must do Y
  • wisdom about how life usually goesin God's world, under God's hand

Bet on these patterns and you'll be right far more often than wrong. And where life doesn't add up this side of heaven, God still has the last word.

What will people say when you're gone?

The just

“blessed”

People bring his name up and smile. His kids tell his stories. The good he did keeps going.

The wicked

“shall rot”

His name goes bad, like meat left out. People change the subject, or curse him.

You can't erase what you've done. But your story isn't finished. The name people remember is the one you're building from here on.

Verse 8 adds the “prating fool”: literally a fool of lips, a man who babbles and won't listen. The wise man takes in commands. The talker never stops talking long enough to learn, and he falls.

Nothing to hide

The upright man “walketh surely”: safely, without looking over his shoulder. He doesn't have to remember which lie he told to whom.

The man who twists his ways “shall be known.” Sooner or later, it comes out. It always does.

Verse 10's “winketh with the eye” is the con man's signal, the sly wink that says “play along” (see 6:13). That kind of slick dealing leaves sorrow behind it.

Love throws a blanket over it

Hatred goes looking for a fight. It digs up old wrongs and keeps the score. Love does the opposite: it covers. It doesn't pretend sin is fine. It just refuses to broadcast it, rub it in, or keep bringing it up.

Proverbs 10:12

Hatred stirreth up strifes: but love covereth all sins.

1 Peter 4:8

And above all things have fervent charity among yourselves: for charity shall cover the multitude of sins.

Peter quotes this verse to the church. In a group like ours, we'll see each other's worst. Love covers. That's what God did for us in Christ, and it's what we do for each other.

Part two

Words and wealth

Verses 13–21

What comes out of your mouth, and what goes into your pocket.

The easy way or the hard way

A man with understanding learns from words. A man who won't listen learns from the rod: pain, consequences, losing things. Most of us know that second school well.

The good news is you can switch schools. Every lesson you take from words is one you don't have to learn from the rod.

Sometimes a proverb just tells it like it is

This verse isn't telling you to chase money. It's an honest look at the world: money can protect you (a lawyer, a car that starts, rent paid on time), and being broke can crush you.

Proverbs 10:15

“The rich man’s wealth is his strong city”

Proverbs 18:11

“The rich man’s wealth is his strong city, and as an high wall in his own conceit.”

Later Solomon finishes the thought: the wall is real, but it's not as high as the rich man thinks. Money helps in this life. It can't save you.

Two kinds of fake

Verse 17: the man who refuses correction “erreth”, wanders off the road. And he usually takes others with him.

Verse 18 names two ways to handle hatred, and calls both of them foolish. One man smiles to your face and hides the hate (“lying lips”). The other lets it out behind your back (“slander”). Either way, the hatred is still in charge.

The wise way is neither. Bring it into the light, deal with it honestly, and let God deal with your heart.

More words, more room for sin

The more you talk, the more chances to exaggerate, gossip, boast, lie, or cut someone down. It's simple math.

So the wise man “refraineth his lips.” He doesn't have to win every argument or fill every silence.

What your words are worth

The good man's tongue is “choice silver”: refined, pure, worth something. His words “feed many”, like a meal for people who are starving for a straight answer or an honest encouragement.

Notice the pairing in verse 20: tongue is matched with heart. What comes out of the mouth shows what's in the heart. Clean up the words and you're really asking God to clean up the heart.

This isn't “follow God and get paid.” God's riches may be money, and they may be a clear conscience, a family that trusts you again, and peace at night. The point is what doesn't come with it: no hangover, no looking over your shoulder.

What's under you?

A whirlwind hits both houses. The wicked man's life gets swept off: “no more.” The righteous man's life sits on something deeper than the weather.

Jesus told the same story, and named the foundation:

“Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell not: for it was founded upon a rock.”

Matthew 7:24–25

Vinegar on the teeth. Smoke in the eyes.

That sour, stinging feeling is what a lazy worker is to “them that send him”: the boss who sends him on a job and counts on him, and he doesn't come through.

Being dependable is part of a changed life. Show up when you said you would. Finish what you were sent to do. People notice, and slowly they start to trust you again.

So who are “the righteous”?

This chapter uses “righteous” or “just” again and again. If it means “a man who never sinned,” nobody qualifies. So how does a man become righteous before God?

“Even the righteousness of God which is by faith of Jesus Christ unto all and upon all them that believe: for there is no difference:”

Romans 3:22

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Jesus took our sin and gives us His righteousness, to anyone who believes. Then He spends the rest of our lives teaching us to live righteous, which is what these proverbs describe.

Question 1 of 6

Reading Proverbs

What is a proverb, and what is it not?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A proverb is a short, two-line saying where the lines answer each other, often with a “but” in the middle. It tells you how life usually goes in God's world: diligent hands tend to prosper, the fear of the LORD tends to lengthen life. It's wise to bet on these patterns, but they aren't guarantees for every case or promises with a deadline.

Question 2 of 6

The tongue

How can my words give life or do damage?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A good man's mouth is “a well of life”: his words feed and refresh people. A wicked man's words cover up violence, slander, and lies. And the more we talk, the more room sin has, so the wise man knows when to keep quiet. Our words show what's in our hearts.

Question 3 of 6

Money and work

What does God say about how I earn what I have?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

How you get it matters. Money from wickedness profits nothing in the end. Steady, diligent work, especially when the work can't wait, tends to build something. And what God gives comes without sorrow attached. Work hard, work honest, and trust Him with the results.

Question 4 of 6

The righteous

Who are the righteous, and how does a man become one?

What verses tell us about this?

“Even the righteousness of God which is by faith of Jesus Christ unto all and upon all them that believe: for there is no difference:”

Romans 3:22

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Answer

The righteous are people who are right with God and live like it. Nobody gets there by being good enough. God gives His righteousness to anyone who believes in Jesus, who took our sin on Himself. From then on, He teaches us to walk uprightly, the way these proverbs describe.

Question 5 of 6

Love

What does love do with another man's sins?

What verses tell us about this?

“And above all things have fervent charity among yourselves: for charity shall cover the multitude of sins.”

1 Peter 4:8

Answer

Love covers it. It doesn't call sin good, but it doesn't gossip about it, rub it in, or keep bringing it up either. Hatred stirs up fights and keeps score. Love forgives and protects, the way God covered our sins in Christ.

Question 6 of 6

A sure foundation

What will still be standing when the storm passes?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell not: for it was founded upon a rock.”

Matthew 7:24–25

Answer

The righteous man, because of what he's built on. Storms hit everyone, but the wicked man's life gets swept away, while the righteous “shall never be removed.” Jesus says the rock is hearing His words and doing them. Build your life on Him.
