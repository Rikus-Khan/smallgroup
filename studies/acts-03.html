Acts 3

Silver and gold have I none

A man who has been lame for over forty years gets up on his own feet, and a crowd hears how far God's mercy reaches.

Part one

Rise up and walk

Verses 1–11

Two men on their way to prayer meet a man everybody walks past.

The hour of prayer

The Jews counted the day from sunrise, so the ninth hour is about three in the afternoon, when the evening sacrifice was offered and the temple was busy. Prayer came three times a day.

  1. About 9 a.m.The third hourmorning sacrifice and prayer
  2. About noonThe sixth hour
  3. About 3 p.m.The ninth hourPeter and John go up (3:1)

The church was brand new, and its Jewish believers still went to the temple at prayer time. Acts is a book of change, with God moving His people forward step by step.

Peter and John weren't looking for a miracle. They were keeping a habit of prayer, and the miracle was waiting at the gate. Keep showing up where God is.

What Peter didn't have, and did

  • silverwhat the man asked for, and what Peter didn't have
  • goldthe same: no money to give
  • our own power or holinessPeter says so himself in verse 12
  • the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth“rise up and walk”

Peter looked the man in the eye (“Look on us”), told him the truth about his pockets, and gave the best thing he owned: Jesus.

You may feel you've got nothing to offer: no money, no clean record. You've got more than you think. What Jesus has done in you is worth more to the next man than silver and gold.

He asked for coins. He got legs.

What he asked for

A little money

Enough for today, from whoever passed. Years of asking, and nothing had changed.

What he got

A whole new life

Peter took his hand and lifted him up, and strength came into his feet and ankle bones. Luke was a doctor, and he writes it like one.

He was “lame from his mother's womb”, and God didn't just help him cope. He changed it. Then he leapt up, stood, walked, and went in “praising God.” When God gives you new strength, stand up on it and thank Him out loud.

From the gate to the porch

The Beautiful gate led from the big outer court into the inner courts. Scholars aren't sure which gate it was, but it's often thought to be the great bronze gate on the east side. The poorest man in Jerusalem sat beside it, where every worshipper passed.

Then he walked through it on his own feet, and the crowd ran to Solomon's porch, the covered walk of columns along the east wall, where Jesus Himself had once walked and taught.

Part two

Ye denied the Holy One

Verses 12–18

The crowd wants to see the healers. Peter points them to Jesus, and tells them the truth.

“Why look ye so earnestly on us?”

“…as though by our own power or holiness we had made this man to walk?”

A crowd stares at two fishermen and wonders what makes them so special. Peter doesn't take a bow. He could have been famous in a day. Instead he turned every eye away from himself.

A man in recovery is going to get credit sometimes. People notice a changed life. When they do, you can say what Peter said: I didn't do this. That's the honest answer, because you didn't.

A murderer for the Prince of life

Peter doesn't soften it. When Pilate wanted to let Jesus go, they asked for Barabbas, a man in prison for sedition and murder (Luke 23:19).

The crosscondemned

The door outreleased

Barabbasguilty of murder
The Holy One and the Justthe archēgos of life

Prince of life is the Greek archēgos: the leader, the pioneer, the one who starts life and goes first. They swapped the Author of life for a killer. And God hath raised him from the dead: the verdict was reversed.

Barabbas walked out free because Someone else took his place. That's the gospel in one trade, and every one of us is Barabbas.

Peter knew what denying was

“And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. And Peter went out, and wept bitterly.”

Luke 22:61–62

Same Peter. He says “ye denied” twice in two verses, and he knew the shame. He also knew what came after: Jesus asked him three times, “lovest thou me?”, once for each denial, and gave him his work back (John 21:17).

If you fell yesterday, you aren't finished. Peter's worst night became the root of his best sermon: I know what it is to deny Him, and I know He takes you back.

Not a magic word

The nameJesus Christ of Nazareth: who He is and what He has the right to do
→
made strong“this perfect soundness in the presence of you all”

In the Bible a name stands for the person: his character and authority. “In the name of Jesus” isn't a spell. It means by His authority, trusting Him.

The power was never in Peter's hands, or in a sound. It was in Jesus, and the man's body was the proof: “whom ye see and know.”

How Peter spoke to men who had done the worst

  1. He named it“Ye denied… ye killed.” No dodging and no excuses.
  2. He called them “brethren”The same crowd that cried “Away with him,” and he says brethren.
  3. “Through ignorance”Most of them hadn't seen who Jesus really was. That explains it. It doesn't excuse it, as the next verse shows.
  4. “God before had shewed”Even the cross was no accident. God foretold that the Christ would suffer, and He brought good out of the worst thing men ever did.

Notice the order: the truth first, then the welcome. Honesty without mercy just crushes a man. Mercy without honesty doesn't change him. Peter gives both, and that's what he'd had given to him.

Part three

Repent, and be converted

Verses 19–26

The door is open for the very people who shut Jesus out.

Blotted out

3:19Repent, and be converted

Turn: change your mind about Jesus and turn to God. It's not a payment. It's a turn.

3:19Your sins blotted out

The Greek word, “exaleipho,” means wiped clean. Ink then was soot and gum, and could be washed right off a page with a wet sponge.

3:19Times of refreshing

Rest after the heat of the day, like fresh air after a hard climb, coming “from the presence of the Lord.”

What do you do with last night? You don't scrub it yourself. You turn to Jesus, and He wipes the page.

“Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross;”

Colossians 2:14

Where we are in the story

  1. About AD 30–33Crucified, risen“whom God hath raised” (3:15)
  2. The ascensionHeaven receives Him“until the times of restitution”
  3. TodaySins blotted outfor whoever turns to Him
  4. Still to comeRestitution of all thingsHe sends Jesus back (3:20)

Restitution means putting back what was broken: everything made right at last. Jesus went up, and He'll come back to do it. Until then, we live in the middle, in the days of the open door.

That's real hope for a man who's wrecked things. The story doesn't end with the mess. God has promised to set everything right.

A prophet like Moses

Moses said, Deuteronomy 18:15

The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken;

Peter says, Acts 3:22

A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear in all things whatsoever he shall say unto you.

Moses told Israel that God would send another prophet like him, and to listen to him. Peter says: that Prophet is Jesus. They'd been proud to be Moses' people. Following Moses meant hearing the One he pointed to.

Verse 23 is sober. “Destroyed from among the people” means cut off from God's people. The only man left outside is the one who will not hear Him. That's why Peter is pleading, not scaring them: the door is open today.

The blessing, handed down

  1. Abraham“All the kindreds of the earth be blessed”
  2. Moses“A prophet like unto me”
  3. Samuel and the prophets“Foretold of these days”
  4. Jesus“Sent him to bless you”“Unto you first”

“And in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed; because thou hast obeyed my voice.”

Genesis 22:18

The promise God made to Abraham was always bigger than one family: “all the kindreds of the earth.” Peter says it starts with Israel (“unto you first”), and it reaches everyone.

The blessing is a turning

“…to bless you, in turning away every one of you from his iniquities.”

God's blessing isn't first of all money or an easy life. It's being turned away from the very things that were wrecking you: from his iniquities. And it's for “every one of you,” even the men who shouted for Barabbas.

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

John 3:16

Today, thank God for one thing He's already turned you from. Then look at one man everybody walks past, and give him what you've got.

Points to remember

  1. Giving“Such as I have give I thee” 3:6You may not have money. You do have Jesus, and your story.
  2. The name“His name through faith in his name hath made this man strong” 3:16The power was never Peter's. It was Jesus, trusted.
  3. Sin“Ye denied the Holy One and the Just” 3:14Peter named the sin plainly, and he knew what denying felt like.
  4. Mercy“I wot that through ignorance ye did it” 3:17Truth first, then welcome. They were brethren, not enemies.
  5. Turning“Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out” 3:19Turn to Jesus, and He wipes the page clean.
  6. Blessing“In turning away every one of you from his iniquities” 3:26God's blessing is being turned from what was wrecking you.

Question 1 of 6

Giving

What did Peter give the lame man instead of money?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The name and power of Jesus. Peter had no silver or gold, but he told the man to rise up and walk in Jesus' name, took his hand, and lifted him. The man got what he'd never asked for: a whole new life.

Question 2 of 6

The name

By what power was the man made to walk?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not Peter's. By the name of Jesus, through faith in Him. Peter says plainly it wasn't their own power or holiness. Jesus did it, and the man's whole body was the proof.

Question 3 of 6

Sin

What did Peter tell the crowd they had done to Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They handed Him over, denied Him before Pilate, and chose a murderer in His place. They killed the Prince of life, and God raised Him from the dead. Peter said it plainly and didn't water it down.

Question 4 of 6

Mercy

How did Peter treat the men who had Jesus killed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As brothers who could still come home. He told them the truth, said they'd acted in ignorance, showed them God's plan, and invited them in. Truth first, then welcome.

Question 5 of 6

Turning

What must a man do for his sins to be blotted out?

What verses tell us about this?

“Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross;”

Colossians 2:14

Answer

Repent and be converted: turn from his old way and turn to God through Jesus. Then the sins are wiped off like ink from a page, and times of refreshing follow.

Question 6 of 6

Blessing

What blessing did God send Jesus to give?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To turn every one of them away from his iniquities. The blessing promised to Abraham reaches all the families of the earth, and it starts by turning a man from the sin that was wrecking him.
