James 5

Wait, pray, and go after your brother

James ends the way a good friend ends a hard talk: with how to wait, how to pray, how to confess to each other, and how to go and get a brother who has wandered. The last chapter is the warmest.

Part one

The cry of the cheated

Verses 1–6

Hard words for rich men who got rich by cheating, and a comfort for every working man who has been cheated himself.

Riches that rot

  1. Corruptedgrain and goods spoiled while the owner sat on them
  2. Motheatenfine clothes, kept and never worn
  3. Cankeredgold and silver gone dull with rust

James isn't saying money is evil, or that a man who works hard and prospers is a sinner. He is talking to men who “heaped treasure together” and kept it, while the people who earned it for them went without. Their wealth is already rotting, and the rust will “be a witness against” them.

A word for any of us: if you owe somebody (back pay, child support, a debt, a repair), the honest thing to do today is to pay what you can, or to call and tell the truth about when you will.

The cry that is heard

The workerreaps the fieldsThe rich ownerkeeps back the pay by fraud
The unpaid wagescry out, and the cries areentered into the ears of the Lord of sabaoth

Sabaoth is a Hebrew word for armies: the Lord of the armies of heaven. The unpaid man may have no lawyer and no power, and he is not unheard. God Himself hears the cry of the cheated.

If that is you, if you've been shorted, used, or never paid, hear this: God saw it. And if it is you who took what wasn't yours, the same God offers a way back, because Jesus has already paid the price for it. Pay the man back, and then rest in the forgiveness.

Part two

Patient like a farmer

Verses 7–12

Court dates, mending families, a body that heals slowly: the waiting is the hardest part, and James has a picture for it.

What a farmer knows

  1. Sowwork the ground
  2. Early rainthe seed takes root
  3. Waitnothing much to see
  4. Latter rainthe grain fills
  5. Harvestthe precious fruit

A farmer can't hurry the rain. He does his part, then he waits, with “long patience.” Most of us hate that middle box: a court date, a family not ready to talk to us, a body that heals slowly, trust that has to be rebuilt. The harvest comes, and no amount of worry made it come sooner.

Waiting without taking it out on people

“Stablish your hearts” means make them firm, like setting a post deep in the ground. And “grudge not” means don't groan against each other. When a man is waiting and worn out, the nearest brother takes the heat: the short word, the eye-roll, the cold shoulder.

James reminds us who is watching: “the judge standeth before the door.” That is a comfort as well as a warning. Jesus is near, He sees our situation, and He will make everything right. The first believers lived expecting Him any day, and so should we, without guessing dates.

So today: when you feel the wait getting to you, don't snap at the guy next to you. Say “I'm having a hard day,” and ask him to pray.

Job, and the end of the Lord

Patience here means staying power, not a quiet, smiling face. Job complained out loud, honestly, and never walked away from God. The prophets endured too: Jeremiah was dropped in a muddy pit for telling the truth. The Lord is “very pitiful, and of tender mercy.” If your waiting hasn't ended yet, the end of the Lord's story with you hasn't been written.

A plain yes is enough

The old habit“I swear on my mother's grave, on my life, I'll pay you Friday. I swear to God.”piling on oaths

James 5:12“Yes.” And then I do it.
“No.” And I mean it.

Most of us have lied a lot, and learned that nobody believes a bare promise, so we piled oaths on top. James isn't banning an honest oath in court. He is saying: be a man whose plain yes can be trusted.

That is built one small promise at a time. Today, make one and keep it exactly: show up on time, make the call, return the tool.

Part three

Prayer, confession, and the wanderer

Verses 13–20

What a group of brothers can do for each other, which is almost exactly what you do here every day.

Whatever you're in, take it to God

  1. Hard times?“let him pray”
  2. Good times?“let him sing psalms”
  3. Sick?“call for the elders of the church”

Afflicted means going through something hard: a loss, a pressure, a hard stretch. James doesn't say tough it out. He says pray. And when things are good, sing, because gratitude is part of prayer too. And when you are sick, don't go through it alone and quiet. Ask for help. Send for the men who lead the church.

The elders and the oil

What James plainly says

Call the elders, and pray

The sick man sends for the elders, the church's spiritual leaders. They pray over him “in the name of the Lord.” A man too weak to pray for himself is carried by others. It is the Lord who raises him up, and the prayer is a prayer of faith in Him.

Where good teachers differ

What the oil means

Some see a sign: oil set kings and priests apart for God, so the man is set apart in prayer. Others see medicine: olive oil was the first aid of the day, like the oil the Samaritan poured on a wounded man. Either way, no oil heals by itself, and nothing here tells a man to skip the doctor. The point is that a sick man is not left alone.

Will every sick man be healed?

Pray for healing boldly: God does heal. But the Bible doesn't promise that every sick man will be healed this side of heaven, and it would be cruel to tell a man his sickness shows his faith is weak. Paul asked three times for his thorn to be removed (2 Corinthians 12:8). The answer was no:

“And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

“Erastus abode at Corinth: but Trophimus have I left at Miletum sick.”

2 Timothy 4:20

And notice the “if” in verse 15: not every sickness is a punishment for sin, but if sin is in the picture, forgiveness is there. Pray, ask, and leave the answer with Him. Healed now, or made whole when He comes, you are held either way.

From hidden to healed

Proverbs 28:13Hidden

Kept secret, alone, in the dark.

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper”

James 5:16Said

Told to a brother who will not run.

“Confess your faults one to another”

James 5:16Healed

Prayed for, carried, set free.

“and pray one for another, that ye may be healed”

This is what this group is. You already have the forgiveness of God when you confess to Him (1 John 1:9), and you don't need a priest in the middle. But James says something more: one to another. A fault said out loud to a brother loses its power, and a man who is prayed for gets carried. Faults means slips and falls, not only the big ones. Tell somebody the real thing today.

The prayer of a righteous man availeth much.

Righteous doesn't mean perfect. It means a man made right with God through Jesus, which is every believer. Effectual fervent means a prayer that is working, and meant: not pretty, just sincere. Your prayer, as a man still learning, has real power. You don't need to be the strong one in the room to pray for the man next to you.

Elijah: just a man like us

  1. Prayedno rain1 Kings 17
  2. Three and a half yearsthe drought
  3. Prayed againand the rain came1 Kings 18
  4. Afraidran, and asked to die1 Kings 19:3-4
  5. Fed and sent backto his work1 Kings 19:5-8

“Subject to like passions as we are” means he had the same feelings we do. Elijah, the man whose prayer shut the sky, was so afraid of a queen's threat that he ran into the desert and asked God to let him die. And God fed him, and gave him work again. If a man like that can pray, so can you, on your worst day.

Go after him

To err is to wander, the word for a sheep that strays. James doesn't say write him off, and he doesn't say he's lost for good. He says one convert him: one of us goes out, and turns him back.

A soul saved, and sins covered

Good teachers differ on the “death” here: some say the wanderer is saved from the ruin where sin leads, and some say a man who only seemed to believe is truly saved. Either way, it is worth going to get him. And “hide a multitude of sins” means God covers them: forgiven. We don't earn that. We just get to carry the news.

Go the way Paul says to:

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Gently, and remembering that you could be next. That is what a brother is for.

One more thing today

Who has gone quiet on you?

Think of one brother who has stopped coming, or stopped answering, or you suspect has gone back to the old life. You don't need a speech. Send him a message today: “I was thinking about you. No lecture. Just checking in.” That is how a man gets brought back, and some day it will be you on the other end of that message.

If you are the one who has wandered, this verse is for you too: somebody in this group would come after you. Come back, and say so.

The whole letter, in five steps

  1. James 1Trials and the Wordcount it joy; do what it says
  2. James 2Faith that worksno favourites; faith shows itself
  3. James 3The tonguea small fire; wisdom from above
  4. James 4Pride and submissionsubmit, resist, draw near
  5. James 5Patience and prayerwait, confess, pray, go and get him

Looking back, one thread runs the whole way: real faith shows up on an ordinary Tuesday. It handles trouble, treats the poor man the same as the rich, watches its mouth, bows its pride, waits patiently, and prays with its brothers. None of it earns God's love, because that is a gift. All of it is what a man who has that gift starts to look like.

And James wrote it as a brother to brothers. He calls them “my brethren” over and over. That is how he speaks to you.

Points to remember

  1. Pay“The hire of the labourers… kept back by fraud, crieth” 5:4God hears the cry of the cheated. Pay what you owe, and if you were cheated, He saw.
  2. Patience“Be patient therefore, brethren, unto the coming of the Lord. Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth” 5:7Do your part like the farmer, then wait. And don't take the waiting out on your brothers.
  3. Words“Let your yea be yea; and your nay, nay” 5:12Be a man whose plain yes can be trusted. Keep one small promise today.
  4. Prayer“Is any among you afflicted? let him pray… Is any sick among you? let him call for the elders” 5:13-14Take hard times to God, and don't be sick alone. Healing is His to give, in His way.
  5. Confession“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed.” 5:16What is said out loud to a brother loses its power. Elijah was a man like us, and his prayer worked.
  6. The wanderer“He which converteth the sinner from the error of his way shall save a soul from death” 5:20Go after the brother who wandered. And if it is you, come back.

Question 1 of 6

Pay

What does God think of cheating a working man?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He hates it, and He hears. The wages kept back cry out, and the Lord of sabaoth hears. If you were cheated, God saw it. If you did the cheating, pay it back, and rest in the forgiveness Jesus already paid for.

Question 2 of 6

Patience

How are we to wait, and who shows us how?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Like a farmer, who does his part and then waits for the rain, and like Job and the prophets, who endured and saw the end of the Lord. Stablish your hearts, don't take the wait out on your brothers, and remember the Lord is very pitiful and of tender mercy.

Question 3 of 6

Words

What should a man's word be like?

What verses tell us about this?

“But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil.”

Matthew 5:37

Answer

Plain and true. A yes that means yes and a no that means no, with no pile of oaths on top. Trust is built one kept promise at a time.

Question 4 of 6

Prayer

What should we do when we are suffering, and when we are sick?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

Answer

Pray, and ask for help: send for the elders and let them pray over you. God hears and can heal, and the answer is His to give. He doesn't promise every sick man will be healed here, but He promises His grace is enough.

Question 5 of 6

Confession

What happens when we confess our faults to each other and pray?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

We are healed. A fault said out loud to a brother loses its grip, and a man who is prayed for gets carried. God forgives when we confess to Him, and brothers who pray for each other have real power, because Elijah was a man like us and his prayer worked.

Question 6 of 6

The wanderer

What should we do for a brother who wanders from the truth?

What verses tell us about this?

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Answer

Go after him, gently, and bring him back. Turning a man from the error of his way saves him from where sin leads, and God covers a multitude of sins. Today, message one brother who has gone quiet. And if it is you who wandered, come home.
