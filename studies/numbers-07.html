Numbers 7

Twelve princes, twelve days, one gift

The tabernacle is up, and the princes of Israel bring their gifts: wagons for the Levites, and then, one prince a day, twelve offerings that are word for word the same. Then Moses goes in, and the LORD speaks.

Part one

“Six covered wagons”

Verses 1–9

What the princes gave first, and who it went to.

The day, and the men

  • “the day that Moses had fully set up the tabernacle”the first day of the first month of the second year (Exodus 40:17). Chapter 1 of this book is a month later, so the chapter looks back
  • anointed, sanctifiedset apart for God, the tabernacle and everything in it
  • “the princes of Israel”the heads of the tribes, the same twelve men named in 1:5–15, who stood over the tribes when they were numbered
  • a wagona cart on wheels; covered means it had a roof. One wagon for two princes, and an ox for each prince

Moses does not say anyone asked them. They brought it “before the LORD”, before the tabernacle (verse 3), and only then does the LORD tell Moses what to do with it (verse 5). A gift brought willingly, before it is asked for, is the kind God loves: “God loveth a cheerful giver” (2 Corinthians 9:7).

Wagons for two, shoulders for one

Gershon2 wagons, 4 oxen
Merari4 wagons, 8 oxen
Kohathnone: they carry it on their shoulders

Two and four make the six wagons; four and eight make the twelve oxen. Nothing was kept back and nothing was left over. The LORD said “to every man according to his service” (verse 5), and Moses gave it that way: more to Merari, and none to Kohath, because “the service of the sanctuary belonging unto them was that they should bear upon their shoulders” (verse 9).

Three families of Levites, three different jobs, and each was given what his own job needed. That is not favouritism. A man who wants a wagon he has no load for has missed what the gift is for.

They came in the order the tribes camped round the tabernacle in chapter 2: Judah, Issachar and Zebulun on the east; Reuben, Simeon and Gad on the south; Ephraim, Manasseh and Benjamin on the west; Dan, Asher and Naphtali on the north. Read the next verses with this in front of you: only the name changes.

What was in every gift

  • a charger and a bowla charger is a large dish, a platter; here in silver, with a silver bowl. The weights are “after the shekel of the sanctuary”, the true standard
  • fine flour mingled with oilthe meat offering. Meat here means food: a grain offering, not flesh
  • a golden spoon, full of incenseten shekels of gold, with the sweet smoke that went up before the LORD
  • a bullock, a ram, a lamb of the first yearthe burnt offering, given whole to God
  • a kid of the goatsthe sin offering, for sin
  • two oxen, five rams, five he goats, five lambsthe peace offerings, shared as a meal with God

Four kinds of offering from Leviticus 1–7 are on the altar in these verses: the meat offering, the burnt offering, the sin offering and the peace offering. What the LORD had taught, twelve men now put into practice.

Part three

“The dedication of the altar”

Verses 84–89

The sum of it, and the voice that answered.

Twelve of everything

1Judah 2Issachar 3Zebulun 4Reuben 5Simeon 6Gad 7Ephraim 8Manasseh 9Benjamin 10Dan 11Asher 12Naphtali
12 × 130 = 1,560shekels of silver in the chargers
12 × 70 = 840in the bowls: “two thousand and four hundred” in all (verse 85)
12 × 10 = 120shekels of gold in the spoons (verse 86)

The animals, added up: twelve bullocks, twelve rams and twelve lambs for the burnt offering, twelve kids for the sin offering, and for the peace offerings twenty-four bullocks, sixty rams, sixty he goats and sixty lambs. That is 252 animals on one altar in twelve days, and not one prince out-gave another by so much as a lamb.

No one out-gave another

Judah went first, and the tribes that followed were not smaller men. Naphtali went last, and was no less. “An hundred and thirty shekels… seventy… ten”: the words come round twelve times, and every prince is honoured in the same sentence.

“Then Peter opened his mouth, and said, Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons:”

Acts 10:34

That is how the LORD counts. There is no first place at his altar, and no man whose gift is waved away for being small. It is a hard lesson for men who have spent years measuring themselves against the next man: who has more, who is further along, who fell less often.

“For if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath, and not according to that he hath not.”

2 Corinthians 8:12

Even a prince brought a kid for sin

12 princesheads of the tribes, men of name
12 kids“One kid of the goats for a sin offering”
every daysin dealt with at the altar

No prince skipped it. The man at the head of his tribe stood at the altar the same as the man at the back, and his sin offering had to be made, as theirs did. Rank does not cover sin. A prince needs the blood as much as a slave does.

That is good news for the man who is sure he is the worst one in the room. You come to the same altar as the best one, with the same offering, and the same acceptance.

The voice from off the mercy seat

“And there I will meet with thee, and I will commune with thee from above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubims which are upon the ark of the testimony, of all things which I will give thee in commandment unto the children of Israel.”

Exodus 25:22

The gifts were given, the altar was dedicated, and God answered. Moses goes in to speak with him, and hears “the voice of one speaking unto him”. This is what the tabernacle was for: he had promised to meet there, and he did.

Come boldly

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

Moses went in to speak with him. We are told to come boldly to the throne of grace, because the sacrifice has been made for us once for all. Mercy is not getting what we deserve; grace is getting what we do not deserve. Both are found at the mercy seat.

A man does not wait to be clean enough. He comes, as the princes came, with the offering for sin, and finds the voice still speaking.

Real world: the gift that is the same for every man

  • SinWanting to be first, to be seen, to have more to show than the man next to you. Even among the princes there was a sin offering, every day.
  • RegretYears spent taking: taking money, taking trust, taking time. A man can start giving today, and it counts from today.
  • GraceGod did not weigh the gifts against each other. Twelve men, twelve times the same, and he spoke to Moses from the mercy seat.
  • HopeThe way into his presence is open: “come boldly unto the throne of grace”.
  • TodayGive one thing, small and ordinary: an hour for a brother who is struggling, a first tenth, a phone call. And do not measure it against what the man beside you gave.

Points to remember

  1. The wagons“six covered wagons, and twelve oxen” 7:3The princes gave first, and the gift went to the Levites to carry the tabernacle, each by his service.
  2. The order“On the second day Nethaneel the son of Zuar, prince of Issachar, did offer” 7:18Twelve days, twelve princes, in the order of the camp: Judah first, Naphtali last.
  3. The gift“one silver charger, the weight thereof was an hundred and thirty shekels, one silver bowl of seventy shekels” 7:13Silver, gold, flour, incense, and four kinds of offering from Leviticus.
  4. The same“this was the offering of Ahira the son of Enan” 7:83Word for word the same twelve times. God does not rank a man's gift by his name.
  5. The sin offering“One kid of the goats for a sin offering” 7:16Even a prince needed it. Rank does not cover sin.
  6. The voice“he heard the voice of one speaking unto him from off the mercy seat” 7:89God keeps his word: he met Moses there, and in Christ he meets us.

Question 1 of 6

The wagons

How many wagons and oxen did the princes bring, and why did the sons of Kohath get none?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Six covered wagons and twelve oxen, which Moses gave to the sons of Gershon and Merari by their service. The sons of Kohath got none, because their service was to bear the holy things upon their shoulders.

Question 2 of 6

The order

Which prince offered on the first day, and which on the twelfth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Nahshon the son of Amminadab, of the tribe of Judah, offered first. Ahira the son of Enan, prince of Naphtali, offered on the twelfth day.

Question 3 of 6

The gift

What was in each prince’s offering?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A silver charger of 130 shekels and a silver bowl of 70, both full of fine flour and oil; a gold spoon of ten shekels full of incense; a bullock, a ram and a lamb for a burnt offering; a kid for a sin offering; and two oxen, five rams, five he goats and five lambs for peace offerings.

Question 4 of 6

The same

Did any prince bring more than the others?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. The first prince's offering and the twelfth are the same, down to the shekel, and the sum in verse 85 is twelve of each.

Question 5 of 6

The sin offering

What did every prince bring for a sin offering?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

One kid of the goats, twelve of them in all. Even the heads of the tribes came to the altar as sinners.

Question 6 of 6

The voice

When Moses went into the tabernacle, whose voice did he hear, and from where?

What verses tell us about this?

“And there I will meet with thee, and I will commune with thee from above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubims which are upon the ark of the testimony, of all things which I will give thee in commandment unto the children of Israel.”

Exodus 25:22

Answer

The voice of the LORD, speaking to him from off the mercy seat, from between the two cherubims, as he had promised at Sinai.
