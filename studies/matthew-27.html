Matthew 27

Truly this was the Son of God

Judas comes to a dark end. Pilate frees Barabbas and hands Jesus over to be crucified. The sky goes dark, the veil tears, and a Roman soldier confesses what the priests would not.

Part one

The price of blood

Verses 1–10

The council hands Jesus to Rome, and the man who sold Him finds out what his partners really think of him.

One long Friday

Jesus has been up all night: the garden, the arrest, the trial at Caiaphas' house (chapter 26). Now the whole of this chapter fits into one day.

  1. DawnThe council sends Him to Pilatevv. 1–2
  2. MorningBarabbas is freed; Jesus is scourgedvv. 11–31
  3. About 9 a.m.Crucified at GolgothaMark 15:25
  4. Noon to 3Darkness over all the landv. 45
  5. About 3 p.m.He cries out and diesvv. 46–50
  6. EveningLaid in Joseph's tombvv. 57–61

Why take Him to Pilate at all? Under Roman rule the Jewish council could condemn a man, but only the Roman governor could put him to death on a cross. So the men who hated Rome went to Rome for help.

“See thou to that”

Judas sees Jesus condemned and is sick over what he has done. He even says the right words: “I have sinned.” And he says them to the wrong people.

The priests had been glad to pay him a few days before. Now that he's a problem, they shrug: “What is that to us?” That's your problem. Sin's partners always do that. They're your friends until the bill comes, and then you're on your own.

Matthew's word for “repented himself” here means he regretted it. He felt the weight of it. But regret alone only looks backward at the damage. Real repentance turns around and goes to God. Judas felt terrible, and he never went to Jesus.

Where you take it

That same night Peter denied Him three times. The difference wasn't the size of the sin. It was where they took it. Judas took his to men, carried it alone, and ended his own life. Peter wept, stayed with the brothers, and the risen Lord came looking for him by name.

If you've ever thought about ending your life, or you're there now: no sin is too big for Jesus to forgive, and you were never meant to carry it alone. Tell someone today: a brother here, a pastor, a crisis line. The priests said “What is that to us?” Jesus never says that.

Thirty pieces, written down ahead of time

Zechariah 11:13, about 500 B.C.

“And the LORD said unto me, Cast it unto the potter: a goodly price that I was prised at of them. And I took the thirty pieces of silver, and cast them to the potter in the house of the LORD.”

Matthew 27:5, 7

“And he cast down the pieces of silver in the temple … and bought with them the potter’s field, to bury strangers in.”

Thirty pieces was the price the Law set for a slave (Exodus 21:32): that's what they thought the Son of God was worth. Matthew names Jeremy (Jeremiah), who went to the potter's house and bought a field (Jeremiah 18, 19, 32); Zechariah gives the price. Both prophets stand behind this field.

And notice the priests: too careful to put blood money in the treasury, not careful at all about the innocent blood.

Part two

Barabbas, or Jesus?

Verses 11–31

The governor knows He's innocent, his wife warns him, and he gives the crowd what it wants anyway.

The governor

Pontius Pilate governed Judaea for Rome from about A.D. 26 to 36. He lived at Caesarea on the coast and came up to Jerusalem at the feasts, with soldiers, to keep the crowds in order.

He's used to prisoners who shout, beg, and argue. This one stands in silence while the charges pile up, and Pilate “marvelled greatly.”

“He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth.”

Isaiah 53:7

The guilty man goes home

Barabbas was a “notable” prisoner: Mark says he had committed murder in a rebellion (Mark 15:7). Pilate's own wife called Jesus “that just man.” Pilate knew the priests were driven by envy. The crowd chose anyway.

Barabbas didn't earn it, ask for it, or deserve it. He walked out because Someone else took his place.

Water can't do it

Pilate

He washed in front of the crowd and said, “not my fault.” It didn't make him innocent. He still signed the order.

The cross

the blood of Jesus

“The blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin” (1 John 1:7). What water couldn't do, His blood does.

Pilate's question is every man's question: “What shall I do then with Jesus?” Nobody gets to stay neutral. Pilate tried.

“His blood be on us” was said in a rage. But God's answer was mercy. Weeks later, in this same city, Peter preached to these same people, and about three thousand believed and were baptized (Acts 2:41). Jesus prayed, “Father, forgive them” (Luke 23:34). This verse never gave anyone the right to hate the Jewish people.

A mock coronation that told the truth

After a Roman scourging, a whipping that some men didn't survive, the whole company of soldiers dressed Him up as a king for a joke. Every piece of the joke was true.

Part three

The place of a skull

Verses 32–50

They dare Him to come down. He stays, and at noon the sun goes out.

A stranger, and a drink refused

Cyrene was a city in North Africa where many Jews lived. Simon had likely come up for the Passover. A soldier could force any man on the road to carry a load, and Jesus, beaten and weak, couldn't carry the cross beam any farther.

Mark adds that Simon was “the father of Alexander and Rufus” (Mark 15:21), names his readers seem to have known. It looks as if the family came to faith. A man forced to carry the cross may have ended up following the One who died on it.

At Golgotha they offered Him a bitter drink, mixed to dull the pain. “He would not drink.” He chose to go through all of it with a clear head, bearing every bit of what He came to bear.

They were reading His psalm out loud

The men at the cross had no idea they were acting out Scripture. Check them off:

  1. ✓They gave me also gall for my meat; and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink.Psalm 69:2127:34, 48
  2. ✓They part my garments among them, and cast lots upon my vesture.Psalm 22:1827:35
  3. ✓he was numbered with the transgressorsIsaiah 53:1227:38
  4. ✓they shake the head, saying, He trusted on the LORD that he would deliver him: let him deliver himPsalm 22:7–827:39, 43

The chief priests knew the Psalms by heart, and they quoted Psalm 22 at the cross without noticing they were describing the Man on it. The cross was no accident. God wrote it down a thousand years ahead.

He saved others; himself he cannot save.

They meant it as an insult. It's the gospel. He could have come down: one prayer and twelve legions of angels would have come (26:53). But if He saved Himself, He couldn't save anyone else. So He stayed.

“Come down … and we will believe” (v. 42). No, they wouldn't have. Three days later He did something greater than coming down from a cross, and they paid money to cover it up (28:12).

Even the two thieves joined in (v. 44). But Luke tells us one of them changed his mind before the end: “Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom.” And Jesus said, “To day shalt thou be with me in paradise” (Luke 23:42–43). It's never too late to turn.

Dark at noon

The Jews counted the hours of the day from sunrise, about 6 a.m. So the sixth hour is about noon, and the ninth about three. For three hours in the middle of the day, the sun went dark “over all the land.”

Passover came at full moon, so this was no eclipse. The light of the world was hanging on a cross, and God turned out the lights.

Eli, Eli AY-lee, AY-lee “My God, my God”

Jesus cries out the first line of Psalm 22, in His own tongue. He isn't confused, and He hasn't lost faith: He still says “My God.” He is telling us what is happening.

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

On the cross He carried our sin, and the Father turned His face from it. Jesus was forsaken so that we never will be. Every believer can hold on to this:

“… for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Part four

The veil, the tomb, and the guard

Verses 51–66

The curtain tears, a soldier confesses, and the enemies set a guard on a grave.

The way in is open

A thick curtain hung in the temple in front of the holiest place, where God's presence dwelt. One man, the high priest, went behind it, one day a year, with blood. It said: keep out.

The moment Jesus died, it tore in two, from the top. No man could tear it from up there. God opened it from His side.

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;”

Hebrews 10:19–20

Who saw it clearly

The priests mocked, “he said, I am the Son of God” (v. 43). The one who says it's true is a Roman officer, a Gentile who had just carried out the execution.

Matthew alone tells of the graves opened in the earthquake and saints who came out “after his resurrection” and were seen in the city. He gives few details, and we shouldn't add any. It was a foretaste: “now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept” (1 Corinthians 15:20).

And look who stayed. The men had fled (26:56). The women from Galilee who had served Him all along were still there, watching from a distance. Faithfulness often looks like simply not leaving.

With the rich in His death

Crucified men were often thrown into a common grave. Joseph, a rich man and a disciple, asked Pilate for the body and laid Jesus in his own new tomb.

“And he made his grave with the wicked, and with the rich in his death; because he had done no violence, neither was any deceit in his mouth.”

Isaiah 53:9

Two women sat across from the tomb and watched where He was laid. They would be the first back on Sunday morning.

As sure as ye can

On the sabbath, of all days, the chief priests go to Pilate about a dead man. They remembered what the disciples had forgotten: “After three days I will rise again.”

  1. A great stoneacross the door, v. 60
  2. A sealon the stone: break it, and you answer to Rome, v. 66
  3. A watcharmed soldiers on guard, v. 66
  4. The third dayMatthew 28:2: none of it holds

They did everything men can do to keep Jesus in the grave. All they managed was to set up the best witnesses anyone could ask for. Whatever has been sealed over in your life, it isn't stronger than He is.

Question 1 of 6

Two sorrows

Judas and Peter both failed Jesus. Why did their stories end so differently?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Peter remembered the word of Jesus, which said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. And he went out, and wept bitterly.”

Matthew 26:75

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Answer

Not because one sin was too big to forgive. Judas regretted it, took it to the priests, and carried it alone. Peter wept, stayed with the brothers, and Jesus came back for him by name. Bring your failures to Jesus, and don't carry them alone.

Question 2 of 6

In our place

Why did Barabbas walk free?

What verses tell us about this?

“For Christ also hath once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God, being put to death in the flesh, but quickened by the Spirit:”

1 Peter 3:18

Answer

Not because he was innocent or sorry. A guilty man went free because Jesus took his place on the cross. That's our story too: the just for the unjust, so that guilty men like us can be brought to God.

Question 3 of 6

Clean hands

Can a man wash his hands of Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Answer

No. Pilate tried, and he's remembered for it. Every man has to answer the question, “What shall I do then with Jesus?” Water couldn't clean Pilate's hands. Only the blood of Jesus cleans a man from his sin, and it's offered to all, even the crowd that shouted against Him.

Question 4 of 6

He stayed

Why didn't Jesus come down from the cross?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thinkest thou that I cannot now pray to my Father, and he shall presently give me more than twelve legions of angels?”

Matthew 26:53

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

Not because He couldn't. Legions of angels were one prayer away. He stayed because saving Himself would have meant leaving us in our sins. The mockers said it without knowing: He saved others, so He would not save Himself. That's love.

Question 5 of 6

Forsaken

Why did Jesus cry, “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?”

What verses tell us about this?

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

“… for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Answer

Because on the cross He was carrying our sin, and the Father turned from that sin. He prayed the first line of Psalm 22, still calling Him “My God.” He was forsaken in our place, so that God can promise every believer, “I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Question 6 of 6

The way in

What did the torn veil mean?

What verses tell us about this?

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;”

Hebrews 10:19–20

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

The way to God is open. The curtain that said “keep out” was torn from the top, by God Himself, the moment Jesus died. We don't need a priest, a ritual, or a clean record to come near. Whosoever comes through Jesus' blood can come boldly.
