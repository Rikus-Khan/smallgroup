Graffed in

Chapters 9 to 11 answer a hard question. The gospel came through Israel, yet most of Paul's own people had turned it down. Had God's promises to them failed? Had He given up on them?

Chapter 10 ended with God holding out His hands “all day long” to a people who wouldn't come. Chapter 11 is Paul's answer: God is not finished with them. Then it ends in worship.

Part one

Seven thousand men

Verses 1–10

Has God thrown His people away? Paul points to himself, and to a prophet who thought he was the last one left.

Exhibit A: Paul

God forbid is Paul's strongest “no”: may it never be! His first proof is himself, a Jew of the tribe of Benjamin who once hunted Christians down (Acts 9). If God had given up on Israel, He'd have started with Saul of Tarsus. Instead He turned him around.

Which he foreknew: God knew this people long before, and chose them and made them promises. Nothing they did surprised Him, and He isn't going to drop them now.

Wot ye not means don't you know. Elias is Elijah, on the lowest day of his life.

“I am left alone”

Elijah had just seen God send fire from heaven on Mount Carmel. Then Queen Jezebel swore to kill him, and he ran into the wilderness:

“…and he requested for himself that he might die; and said, It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life; for I am not better than my fathers.”

1 Kings 19:4

God's answer: each dot is 100 men, seven thousand who never bowed to Baal

Isolation tells a man the lie it told Elijah: I'm the only one. That's where relapse lives. God had seven thousand men Elijah had never met. You're not alone either.

A remnant, by grace

A remnant is what's left over. In Elijah's day and in Paul's, most of Israel had turned away, but some believed: Paul himself, and thousands of Jews at Pentecost.

The election of grace: God chose to save men by grace, and those who receive it by faith (11:20) are His. It was never a reward for the best behaved.

If it's earned

Works

Then it's wages. God owes you, and you can lose it on your next bad day.

If it's given

Grace

Then it's a gift. You can't earn it on a good day, or wreck it on a bad one.

Mix the two and you lose grace altogether. “Otherwise grace is no more grace.”

The rest were blinded

A hard verse; read it slowly. Israel sought to be right with God by keeping the law, and wouldn't take it as a gift (9:31–32). Those who took it by faith got it. The rest were blinded.

They→would not see→the Christ in front of them“All day long I have stretched forth my hands” (10:21)
God→gave them what they chose→“eyes that they should not see”As He did in Moses' day and Isaiah's (Deuteronomy 29:4, Isaiah 29:10)

God doesn't blind men who want to see; He confirms a refusal already made. David's table turned snare? Their religion, God's good gifts, became the trap because they trusted it instead of Him. Anything you lean on in place of Christ can. But watch for “until” in verse 25.

Part two

The olive tree

Verses 11–24

Israel stumbled, and the gospel ran out to the nations. Then a picture every farmer knew: branches broken off, and a wild one grafted in.

Out to the nations

Paul saw it in city after city. He went to the synagogue first; most of the Jews turned him away, and the Gentiles, everyone who isn't a Jew, believed. “Lo, we turn to the Gentiles” (Acts 13:46).

Israel's no sent the gospel out to Galatia, Greece, and Rome. That's how most of us came to hear it.

But they stumbled; they didn't fall for good. Paul hopes they'll see Gentiles enjoying their Messiah and want Him back: provoke them to jealousy.

A stumble, not the end

The apostle of the Gentiles magnifies his office, makes the most of it, hoping for “emulation”: his own people wanting what the Gentiles have.

If God could bring riches for the world out of a stumble, a fall is not the end of your story either. That's no reason to fall. It's a reason not to stay down.

The root and the branches

Two pictures. The firstfruit was the first bit of dough given to God; that made the whole lump holy. The root is the same idea: the fathers, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and God's promises to them. What grows from a holy root belongs to God.

The olive tree is Israel's own picture; God once called them “A green olive tree” (Jeremiah 11:16). To graff is to graft: cut a slit in a living tree, set in a branch from somewhere else, and bind it until it grows in and lives on the tree's sap, its fatness.

Gentile believers are that wild branch. Everything we have, the promises, the Scriptures, the Messiah Himself, came to us through Israel's root. “Thou bearest not the root, but the root thee.” The branch doesn't hold up the tree.

Don't look down on the branch on the ground

The Gentile reader is tempted to brag: they were broken off so I could be put in. Paul says “Well;”, true enough, but think about why. They were broken off because of unbelief. You stand by faith. Not because you're better. Faith is just receiving what God gives. Nobody gets to brag about taking a gift.

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

Highminded is proud, looking down on others. In recovery it's the man with two years clean who sneers at the guy who used last weekend. That's exactly the pride that sets a man up to fall. Stay humble. You're standing on a gift too.

Broken off, graffed in

Natural branches, Jews by birth, broken off. Not because they were Jews, but “because of unbelief”.

A wild branch, a Gentile with no claim on this tree, set in among them, living on the root. “Contrary to nature”: farmers graft good branches onto wild trees, never the other way. Grace is like that.

And the broken ones? “If they abide not still in unbelief,” they go back in. Putting a branch back into its own tree is easier than what God already did for us.

Goodness and severity

On them which fell

Severity

God is not soft on unbelief. Refusing His Son is serious, and branches really were broken off.

Toward thee

Goodness

Kindness nobody earned: a wild branch living on a root it never planted.

“Otherwise thou also shalt be cut off” is about whole branches: the Gentiles as a people, as he has been speaking of Israel as a people. He is not saying a man who has truly trusted Christ can lose his salvation:

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

It is a warning against pride, and against a faith that is only for show.

Part three

Mercy upon all

Verses 25–36

A mystery about Israel's future, a promise God won't take back, and a song when the argument runs out of words.

Blindness in part, until…

  1. the fathersGod's promise to Abraham: the root11:16, 28
  2. about 860 BCElijah and the 7,00011:4
  3. about AD 30 to 33Pentecost: thousands of Jews believeActs 2:41
  4. about AD 57Romans: “blindness in part”11:25
  5. todayGentiles still coming inus
  6. God knows when“the fulness of the Gentiles”11:25
  7. God knows when“all Israel shall be saved”11:26

A mystery in the Bible is a secret God has now made known. Israel's blindness is only in part, Jews have been coming to Christ ever since, and it lasts only until. Paul says plainly that a day is coming when Israel as a people turns to the Deliverer, Jesus, who takes away sins.

Good Bible-believing Christians differ on exactly how and when that happens, and Paul gave no dates. So we won't either. What's certain: God hasn't finished with Israel, and nobody, Jew or Gentile, is saved any other way than through the Deliverer. And “wise in your own conceits”? Don't be smug about any of it.

He doesn't take it back

“The gifts and calling of God are without repentance.”

Without repentance: God never changes His mind about it. Israel set itself against the gospel, and God still calls them beloved, for the promises He made to their fathers.

If He kept faith with Israel through centuries of unbelief, He'll keep faith with you. Whatever you did yesterday, God didn't wake up this morning and cancel your salvation.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

That's not permission to keep falling. It's solid ground to get up on.

One cell, one way out

Concluded means shut up together, locked in. Gentiles who never knew God and Jews who had every advantage: the same cell. Nobody gets to look down on anybody.

Why? “That he might have mercy upon all”: the same “all” who were shut in. Some of us know the sound of a cell door. Every man is in this one, and the only way out is mercy, offered to every one.

“O the depth”

Eleven chapters of hard thinking, and Paul ends not with one more argument but in worship. Past finding out is like tracks you can't follow: you see where God has been, but you can't trace every step.

“For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts.”

Isaiah 55:9

Three questions, one answer

“Who hath known the mind of the Lord?”No oneGod never had to ask anybody what He should do with Israel, or with you.
“Who hath been his counsellor?”No oneHe doesn't need advice. He had this plan before any of us were born.
“Who hath first given to him?”No oneNobody can put God in his debt. Every good thing we have came from Him first.

“Of him, and through him, and to him, are all things”: everything starts with God, runs on God, and ends up giving Him glory.

That's freeing. You can't earn God's favour, so you don't have to. And you can't pay Him back, so stop trying. Receive it, and give Him the glory.

Points to remember

  1. Not alone“I have reserved to myself seven thousand men” 11:4You're never the only one. God has His people, and He puts you among them.
  2. Grace“Otherwise grace is no more grace” 11:6Earned is wages; grace is a gift. Mix them and grace is gone.
  3. The olive tree“Because of unbelief they were broken off, and thou standest by faith” 11:20Unbelief cuts off; faith joins a man to the tree.
  4. Pride“Thou bearest not the root, but the root thee” 11:18You're standing on a gift. Never look down on a man who fell.
  5. God's promises“The gifts and calling of God are without repentance” 11:29God doesn't take back what He has given.
  6. Mercy“That he might have mercy upon all” 11:32Every man is in the same cell, and mercy is offered to all.

Question 1 of 6

Not alone

What did God tell Elijah when he thought he was alone?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That He had kept seven thousand men who had not bowed to Baal. Elijah felt like the last faithful man, but he wasn't. In Paul's day there was a remnant too. A man who isolates believes a lie; God always has His people.

Question 2 of 6

Grace

Why can't grace and works be mixed?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Because if salvation is earned, it isn't a gift any more; it's wages. The moment you add your works, grace stops being grace. God saves by grace, and we receive it by faith, so nobody can boast.

Question 3 of 6

The olive tree

Why were some branches broken off, and how do the grafted ones stand?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The natural branches were broken off because of unbelief, not because they were Jews. The wild branches stand by faith, not because they're better. And God is able to graft the broken ones back in when they believe.

Question 4 of 6

Pride

Why must a grafted branch not boast?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

Answer

Because the branch doesn't hold up the root; the root holds up the branch. Everything a Gentile believer has came through Israel's root, and he stands only by faith in a gift. Pride is how a man sets himself up to fall.

Question 5 of 6

God's promises

Does God take back His gifts and calling?

What verses tell us about this?

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Answer

No. God never changes His mind about what He has given. He still loves Israel for the fathers' sakes, and He will finish the work He began in every believer. A bad day doesn't cancel God's promise.

Question 6 of 6

Mercy

Why did God conclude them all in unbelief?

What verses tell us about this?

“Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth.”

1 Timothy 2:4

Answer

So that He might have mercy upon all. Jew and Gentile are shut up in the same unbelief, so nobody can boast and nobody is beyond reach. The way out is the same for everyone: mercy, received by faith in Jesus.
