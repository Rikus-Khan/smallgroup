Exodus 9

Cattle, boils and hail: “I have sinned this time”

The cattle of Egypt die and Israel's do not. Boils break out on everyone, even the magicians. Then God tells Pharaoh why he has been kept standing, and a hail falls that Pharaoh answers with the right words and the same old heart.

Part one

“Of the cattle of the children of Israel died not one”

Verses 1–12

A plague on the herds, a plague on the skin, and for the first time the words “the LORD hardened.”

A very grievous murrain

  • murraina deadly plague that kills farm animals
  • hold them stillkeep them, refuse to release them
  • assesdonkeys
  • severset apart, cut a line between

The warning comes first, as usual: if thou refuse to let them go. The plague is aimed at the animals in the field, horses, asses, camels, oxen, sheep: the wealth of Egypt, and what its farms ran on. It says nothing of any man dying in this one.

Many teachers point out that Egypt held some cattle sacred. The text does not say that, but it fits: the LORD strikes at what Egypt leaned on. And once again He draws the line He drew in Goshen: nothing shall die of all that is the children's of Israel.

A set time, and “not one”

Pharaoh once said to morrow and meant not yet. Now the LORD says to morrow, and He means it: the LORD appointed a set time, and did it on the morrow. God's tomorrow always comes.

All the cattle of Egypt died means all those in the field, the ones the warning named (verse 3). Egypt still has animals in a few verses (verse 19), so the word all covers the herds out in the open.

And then look at what Pharaoh does. He sent. He checked the Israelite herds himself and found not one dead. He had the proof in his own hand. And the next line:

Plague five. His heart was hard, and he did not let them go. Evidence was never Pharaoh's problem.

Ashes of the furnace

  • furnacea kiln or oven, such as the one used to fire bricks
  • boila painful, swollen sore
  • blainsblisters: sores that break open
  • sprinklethrow up in handfuls, so it falls like dust

Again no warning (like the lice, plague three). Moses takes handfuls of ashes from the furnace and throws them toward heaven in front of Pharaoh, and it becomes fine dust over the whole land, and sores on man and beast.

Many readers think the furnace was the kind of kiln where Israel had been forced to make bricks (5:7–19). It may be so. If it is, the ashes of the slaves' hard labour became the plague on their masters. The text only says ashes of the furnace.

The magicians could not stand

They had copied the rod, the blood and the frogs. They had failed at the lice and said this is the finger of God. Now they cannot even stand up. They are sick with the very boils they could not stop. This is the last time they are mentioned in the whole story.

The men who told Pharaoh that it was fine, and that they could do the same, are laid low by the same plague as everyone else. A man's helpers and excuses cannot always stand up for him on the day it matters.

“And the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh”

Here it is, in verse 12. Before we decide what it means, look at what came first. Five plagues, five refusals:

7:22blood 8:15frogs 8:19lice 8:32flies 9:7cattle

Every time: his heart hard, he did not let them go. Twice the text says outright that Pharaoh hardened his own heart (8:15, 8:32). Only now, after all that, is the LORD named as the one who hardened it (9:12).

God had told Moses He would (4:21, 7:3). So this is not a trap, and it is not a surprise. It is what happens when a man keeps shutting the door: God lets him have the shut door he has chosen. That is a judgment on a heart already hard. It is not a door slammed on a man who wants to come in. Nothing in this story shows Pharaoh trying to turn back and being unable.

Part two

“For this cause have I raised thee up”

Verses 13–21

The LORD tells Pharaoh why he is still standing, and gives every man in Egypt a way to be safe.

“Raised thee up”

In verse 15 the LORD warns that He will stretch out His hand and strike Pharaoh and his people with pestilence, a deadly sickness, and Pharaoh will be cut off from the earth. Verse 16 says why Pharaoh has been given his place and kept in it:

Raised thee up can also be read made you stand or kept you standing, and some translators read verses 15 and 16 as I could have struck you down, but I have kept you standing. Either way, Pharaoh is still on his throne because God has been patient with him, and the purpose is that God's name be known in all the earth.

Paul on Pharaoh

“For the scripture saith unto Pharaoh, Even for this same purpose have I raised thee up, that I might shew my power in thee, and that my name might be declared throughout all the earth.”

Romans 9:17

Paul quotes it in Romans 9 to show that God is free to do as He wills with a proud heart. In the very next chapter he says the door is open to everyone:

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

What Pharaoh still had

It is easy to read Pharaoh and think he never had a chance. Look at what the LORD says to him here:

I will send all my plagues upon thine heart: they are aimed at the heart, not just at the land. That thou mayest know that there is none like me in all the earth. Everything God did to Pharaoh was meant to make him know the LORD.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

God is not willing that any should perish. So what the story shows is not a man who could not repent, but a man who would not, and a God who kept giving him another day, plague after plague.

A warning that is a mercy

Exaltest thou thyself means you are still lifting yourself up. Then the LORD does something that a cruel God would not do. He names the hour of the storm, and tells them how to be safe:

Verse 20

Feared the word

An Egyptian, one of Pharaoh's own servants, who feared the word of the LORD, and brought his servants and his cattle into the houses.

Verse 21

Regarded not

Another, who regarded not the word of the LORD, and left his servants and his cattle in the field.

Regarded here means paid attention to. The same warning, the same hour. One man heard it, and got under a roof. The other heard it and left everything out in the open.

Real world: be a doer

Some of Pharaoh's own men believed God's word, and did something about it. They did not need to be Israelites. They needed only to hear it and act.

“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.”

James 1:22

Everyone in this room has heard warnings. A friend, a sponsor, a pastor, a verse. The question is not who has heard. It is who has gone indoors. What warning have you heard and left lying in the field?

Today: pick one warning you have not acted on, and do one thing about it before the day is out. And tell a brother what you did.

Part three

“I have sinned this time”

Verses 22–35

A hail like nothing Egypt had ever seen, and a king who says the right words.

Hail, and fire, and Goshen untouched

Moses stretches the rod toward heaven, and thunder and hail come with fire mingled with the hail. The hail smote all that was in the field, both man and beast, every herb and tree. It was the worst thing Egypt had ever seen: none like it since it became a nation. (Mingled means mixed together; thereof, in verse 18, means of it.)

Notice that the warning in verses 18 to 21 was real. Those who had listened were safe, in the houses. Those who had not were lost in the field. A warning is only a mercy if it is taken.

And again, only in the land of Goshen, where the children of Israel were, was there no hail. The same line as before, and it holds.

The right words

Read what Pharaoh says: I have sinned. The LORD is righteous, and I and my people are wicked. You could not ask for a better confession. And Moses answers: I know that ye will not yet fear the LORD God. Why?

Pharaoh

“I have sinned this time”

He says it while the hail is falling. This time, as though it were once. And it is enough: he wants the hail to stop. He is sorry about the hail.

David, 2 Samuel 12:13

“I have sinned against the LORD”

He says it when the prophet has exposed him, and he turns from it. And the answer: The LORD also hath put away thy sin.

The difference is not the words. It is whether the man turns. That is what repentance means: a change of direction, not only a feeling of being sorry, and not only wanting the consequences to stop.

If you are sorry, come

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Some men read about Pharaoh and ask, am I like that? Here is a test. Pharaoh was sorry about the hail and nothing else. If you grieve over the sin itself, and over what it does to God and to the people you love, and you want to turn, that is not a hard heart. That is the Holy Spirit at work in you.

Confess it to God, and to a brother who will pray with you. The promise is faithful and just to forgive. David's answer from Nathan is the same as yours: the LORD also hath put away thy sin.

Flax, barley, wheat and rie

  • flaxthe plant that linen is made of
  • bolledin bud or in the seed pod: nearly ripe
  • in the earwith the grain formed on the stalk
  • rierye, a hardy grain

The crops that were furthest along, the flax and the barley, were flattened. The wheat and rye, not grown up, were still low and soft, and they came through.

So some food is left. God does not destroy everything at once. And what the hail left, the next plague will come for.

“He sinned yet more”

Moses spreads out his hands, and the storm stops. And now:

He sinned yet more. Not less. After the words I have sinned, the sin got bigger. The promise he made while the hail fell, he broke when it stopped, and his servants went with him. A leader's hard heart does not stay his own.

If you are a father, a husband, an older brother in the faith: the men and boys around you are watching what you do when the hail stops. What you do with a soft moment can soften them. What you do with a hard one can harden them.

To day

Three plagues left, and a king who still has not turned. We know how this ends for him. But it does not have to end that way for you.

“While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation.”

Hebrews 3:15

“…and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.”

John 6:37

The same God who held out His hand to Pharaoh for seven plagues holds it out to you. In no wise means in no way, ever. Whatever you have done, a man who comes to Jesus will not be turned away. Come today, while it is called today.

Question 1 of 6

The cattle

What happened to the cattle of Egypt, and what to the cattle of Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The cattle of Egypt in the field died, and of Israel's not one. God drew the line He had said He would. Pharaoh sent and saw it for himself, and still would not let them go.

Question 2 of 6

The boils

Who could not stand before Moses, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The magicians, because the boils were on them too. The men who had copied the signs could not even stand. It is the last time they appear in the story.

Question 3 of 6

A hard heart

Who hardened Pharaoh's heart in verse 12, and what had Pharaoh already done?

What verses tell us about this?

“But when Pharaoh saw that there was respite, he hardened his heart, and hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had said.”

Exodus 8:15

“And Pharaoh hardened his heart at this time also, neither would he let the people go.”

Exodus 8:32

Answer

The LORD did, in verse 12. But before it, Pharaoh had refused five times, and twice the text says he hardened his own heart. God foretold it, and then confirmed a man in what he kept choosing.

Question 5 of 6

Two kinds of men

What was the difference between the servants who feared the word of the LORD and those who did not?

What verses tell us about this?

“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.”

James 1:22

Answer

One took the warning and brought his people and cattle indoors. The other heard it and left them in the field. The same word, and what they did with it made all the difference.

Question 6 of 6

I have sinned

What did Pharaoh say when the hail fell, and what did he do when it stopped?

What verses tell us about this?

“And David said unto Nathan, I have sinned against the LORD. And Nathan said unto David, The LORD also hath put away thy sin; thou shalt not die.”

2 Samuel 12:13

Answer

He said, “I have sinned this time,” and when the hail stopped he sinned yet more and hardened his heart. The right words, but no turning. David said the same and turned, and was forgiven. Come to God today.

Looking ahead: Exodus 10

The hail left the wheat and the rye. Now comes what will eat the rest.

“And they shall cover the face of the earth, that one cannot be able to see the earth: and they shall eat the residue of that which is escaped, which remaineth unto you from the hail, and shall eat every tree which groweth for you out of the field:”

Exodus 10:5

Locusts, then darkness, then the last plague, and the Passover. Three left. Each is another door held open, and the question is still the same one: what will Pharaoh do today?
