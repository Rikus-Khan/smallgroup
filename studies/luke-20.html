Luke 20

By what authority?

One trap after another in the temple courts: priests, spies and Sadducees try to catch Jesus in His words, and every one of them walks away silent.

Part one

Who gave thee this authority?

Verses 1–8

The men who run the temple want to know who Jesus thinks He is.

Questions in the temple

It's the last week before the cross. The day before, Jesus rode in as King and drove the sellers out of the temple (19:45). Now He's teaching there every day, and the people who run the place come at Him with question after question, each one a trap:

  1. “By what authority?”chief priests, scribes, elders (1–8)
  2. “Is it lawful to give tribute?”spies (20–26)
  3. “Whose wife shall she be?”Sadducees (27–38)
  4. “How is Christ David's son?”Jesus asks (41–44)

The chief priests, scribes and elders together made up the council that ruled the Jews under Rome. They had their own authority from the temple. Who had given Him any?

Stuck either way

They huddled up and worked out both answers:

If we say“Of men,”

Notice what they never asked: which one is true? Only which one is safe for us?

Not a dodge

Jesus wasn't ducking. His question was the answer. John the Baptist had pointed straight at Him and said who He was:

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

If John was from heaven, so was Jesus' authority. They knew it. They just wouldn't say it. A man who won't be honest about the light he already has won't be given more.

Many of us have been there: we knew the truth, but we were more worried about what it would cost us. Honesty with God starts with “I know, Lord.”

Part two

My beloved son

Verses 9–19

A story about tenant farmers, told to the faces of the men it was about.

An owner far away

Husbandmen were tenant farmers. They worked another man's land and paid him a share of the crop at harvest. Absentee owners and angry tenants were a common story in Galilee.

Every Jew listening knew whose vineyard this was. Isaiah had told the same story seven hundred years earlier:

“For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant: and he looked for judgment, but behold oppression; for righteousness, but behold a cry.”

Isaiah 5:7

God planted His people and looked for fruit. The leaders put in charge of them treated it like their own.

Sent, and sent, and sent again

  1. a servantbeaten, sent away empty
  2. anotherbeaten, shamed, sent away
  3. a thirdwounded, cast out
  4. my beloved son“they will reverence him”the last one

The servants are the prophets God sent to Israel, one after another, over hundreds of years. Most were ignored, many were mistreated, and some were killed.

Any landlord would have sent soldiers by now. This one sends his son. What shall I do? That's how patient God is with people who keep turning Him away.

“Let us kill him”

They knew exactly who he was: This is the heir. They didn't kill him by mistake. They killed him because he was the son, so they could keep the vineyard for themselves.

Jesus is telling the chief priests what they would do to Him within days: take Him out of the city and kill Him. And the crowd gasped, God forbid! May it never be!

The deepest reason men reject Jesus isn't usually that they can't believe. It's that they want to keep running their own lives. The son still came. The Father still sent Him.

The stone they threw out

Jesus quotes Psalm 118, the same psalm the crowd sang two verses earlier (19:38). The builders looked at a stone, decided it didn't fit, and tossed it on the pile.

God picked it up and made it the cornerstone, the one every other stone in the building lines up with. The leaders would reject Jesus, and God would raise Him up.

“This is the stone which was set at nought of you builders, which is become the head of the corner.”

Acts 4:11

Broken, or ground to powder

Fall on the stone

Broken

A man throws himself on Jesus, and his pride gets broken. It hurts. But a broken man can be put back together.

The stone falls on him

Ground to powder

A man who never comes will one day meet that stone as his Judge. There's nothing left to mend.

Every man meets this stone one way or the other. Better to be broken now, at the feet of the One who heals the broken, than crushed later.

“Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.”

Acts 4:12

Part three

Whose image?

Verses 20–26

Flattering spies, a question about taxes, and a coin with a face on it.

The setup

The spies, smilingMaster, we know that thou sayest and teachest rightly, neither acceptest thou the person of any. flattery first

The spiesIs it lawful for us to give tribute unto Caesar, or no?

Tribute was the tax every Jew paid straight to Rome, to Caesar, the emperor Tiberius. People hated it. If Jesus said “pay it,” the crowd would turn on Him. If He said “don't,” the spies would run to the governor, Pilate, and report a rebel.

These men feigned themselves just: they pretended to be honest seekers. Flattery is often the front porch of a trap. Jesus saw straight through it.

Caesar's penny

The penny was a Roman silver denarius, a day's pay. On it: Tiberius' head, and the words Tiberius Caesar, son of the divine Augustus. Jesus didn't have one. They did.

It has his picture on it, so give it back to him. Pay your taxes. A believer can be an honest citizen.

But you're stamped with an image too. Caesar gets the coin. God gets you.

Unto God the things which be God's.

“So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.”

Genesis 1:27

Every man carries God's image, no matter how scratched up and worn down it got. Drink, drugs, prison, years thrown away: the image is still there, and it still says who you belong to.

Caesar wanted a share of what men earned. God wants the man: the heart, the body, the days. Give yourself back to the One whose likeness you bear.

Part four

Not a God of the dead

Verses 27–40

Men who don't believe in the resurrection bring Jesus a riddle about it.

The Sadducees

The Sadducees were the wealthy priestly party; the high priest's family were Sadducees. They held mainly to the five books of Moses, and they said this life is all there is:

“For the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, neither angel, nor spirit: but the Pharisees confess both.”

Acts 23:8

They start with a real law of Moses. If a man died with no children, his brother married the widow, so the dead man's name and land would carry on:

“If brethren dwell together, and one of them die, and have no child, the wife of the dead shall not marry without unto a stranger: her husband’s brother shall go in unto her, and take her to him to wife, and perform the duty of an husband’s brother unto her.”

Deuteronomy 25:5

Seven brothers, one riddle

1died2died3died4died5died6died7died the womanlast of all ?

It's a made-up story meant to make the resurrection look silly. Seven husbands in heaven? Who gets her? They'd probably used it to win arguments with the Pharisees for years.

The riddle only works if the next life is just this life, carried on. That's the mistake Jesus is about to show them.

This world, and that world

The children of this world

Marry, and die

Marriage is God's good gift for this life, and children carry a family on, because men die.

The children of the resurrection

Can't die any more

No more dying, so no need for heirs. Equal unto the angels: deathless like them. Not turned into angels, but raised whole as the children of God.

The Sadducees pictured heaven as more of the same. Jesus says it's a different order of life altogether. Our questions about it are often too small, not too big.

“I am,” not “I was”

They trusted Moses, so Jesus answered from Moses. At the burning bush, hundreds of years after those men were buried, God said:

“Moreover he said, I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. And Moses hid his face; for he was afraid to look upon God.”

Exodus 3:6

Not “I was their God.” I am. God doesn't call Himself the God of a pile of bones. All live unto him. Even the scribes said, Master, thou hast well said, and nobody dared ask Him anything else.

He is not a God of the dead, but of the living.

Many of us have buried friends: overdoses, wrecks, fights, bodies that gave out too young. Some of us came close ourselves. Death is real, and it hurts.

But it doesn't get the last word. For everyone who belongs to Jesus, the grave is not the end of the story.

“Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:”

John 11:25

Part five

David's son, David's Lord

Verses 41–47

Now Jesus asks the question, and then warns about men who love to be seen.

How can both be true?

Everyone agreed the Christ would be David's son, a king from David's family. But in Psalm 110 David calls Him my Lord. No father calls his own son “my Lord.” Unless the son is greater than the father.

By birth

David's son

Born of Mary, of David's line: “the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David” (Luke 1:32).

Before David ever lived

David's Lord

The eternal Son of God, now seated at the Father's right hand until every enemy bows.

The answer to the riddle was standing right in front of them in the temple.

Religion for show

What they loved in public

To be seen

Long robes that marked them as important. Greetings in the markets. The highest seats in the synagogue, the chief rooms at feasts.

What they did in private

Devour widows' houses

They took advantage of the poorest people in town, likely by handling their estates and taking their homes. Then they covered it with long, loud prayers.

Greater damnation: the more you know, and the more you use God's name as a cover, the heavier the judgment. Jesus isn't impressed by a show. He wants an honest heart.

The very next thing Luke shows us is a poor widow giving everything she had (21:1–4). That's the real thing.

Points to remember

  1. Authority“The baptism of John, was it from heaven, or of men?” 20:4They cared what was safe, not what was true. Be honest with the light you have.
  2. The beloved son“I will send my beloved son” 20:13God kept sending, last of all His Son, and men killed Him.
  3. The stone“The same is become the head of the corner” 20:17The One men threw away, God made the cornerstone. Be broken on Him now.
  4. Caesar and God“Unto God the things which be God’s” 20:25Caesar gets the coin. God gets the man who bears His image.
  5. The living“Not a God of the dead, but of the living” 20:38God is still the God of those who died. The resurrection is real.
  6. Beware“Beware of the scribes” 20:46Religion for show hides cruelty. God wants honest hearts.

Question 1 of 6

Authority

When the leaders asked by what authority Jesus did these things, what did He ask them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“The baptism of John, was it from heaven, or of men?” If they admitted John was from God, they would have to admit that Jesus, whom John pointed to, was too. They wouldn't, so they said they couldn't tell.

Question 2 of 6

The beloved son

Whom did the lord of the vineyard send last, and what did the husbandmen do to him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

His beloved son. They recognized him as the heir, cast him out of the vineyard, and killed him, so they could keep it. Jesus was telling the leaders what they were about to do to Him.

Question 3 of 6

The stone

What did Jesus say about the stone which the builders rejected?

What verses tell us about this?

“Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.”

Acts 4:12

Answer

It is become the head of the corner. The One the leaders rejected, God raised up as the cornerstone. Whoever falls on that stone is broken; whoever it falls on is ground to powder.

Question 4 of 6

Caesar and God

What did Jesus say to render to Caesar, and what to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.”

Genesis 1:27

Answer

Render unto Caesar the things which be Caesar's: the coin that carries his image. And unto God the things which be God's: ourselves, because we carry His image.

Question 5 of 6

The living

How did Jesus show from Moses that the dead are raised?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:”

John 11:25

Answer

At the burning bush, long after Abraham, Isaac and Jacob had died, God called Himself their God. He is not a God of the dead, but of the living: all live unto Him.

Question 6 of 6

Beware

What did Jesus warn His disciples about the scribes?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Beware of them. They loved long robes, greetings, and the best seats, while they devoured widows' houses and made long prayers for show. They will receive greater damnation.
