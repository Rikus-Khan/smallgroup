Genesis 38

More righteous than I

Joseph is in Egypt, and Judah has walked out on his brothers. This is the chapter where a man falls, is caught by his own signet ring, and says the hardest words a man can say. God puts his grandson in the line of David and of Jesus.

Part one

Down from his brothers

Verses 1–11

A man leaves his family, and his own family begins to come apart.

Which way was he going?

  1. went down from his brethren“at that time” (v. 1)
  2. turned in to a certain Adullamitea friend outside the family (v. 1)
  3. took a Canaanite wifethe very thing his father was told never to do (28:1)
  4. sons who grew up wicked“Er... was wicked in the sight of the LORD” (v. 7)

“At that time” puts this right after Joseph is sold. We are not told why Judah left. It may be that he could not bear the house, with his father in sackcloth and his own part in it (37:26-35). The Bible does not say. It only says he went down, and every step after that is farther from home.

“And Isaac called Jacob, and blessed him, and charged him, and said unto him, Thou shalt not take a wife of the daughters of Canaan.”

Genesis 28:1

The Bible does not say the first step caused the last. But the direction is plain: down.

Where Judah went

Judah left the family camp near Hebron and went down into the hills toward Adullam, a Canaanite town, where a man named Hirah took him in. He settled there, took a wife, and had three sons: Er, Onan and Shelah.

Timnath, where the story goes later, is in the same hill country. What matters is the distance from home: the people a man used to answer to are the first to be missing.

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9-10

“Wicked in the sight of the LORD”

This is a hard verse, and it is short. The Bible gives us the verdict and not the file. We are not told what Er did. We are told who saw it: “in the sight of the LORD”. Nothing a man does in private is private.

We are also told what the LORD did: He “slew him”. Be careful not to make that bigger than it is. The same God who judges here is the God who waited four hundred years before He judged the Amorites (15:16), and who sent a flood only after warning for generations. When He acts, it is not on a whim.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Judah had the evidence. He had lost a son to the judgment of God, and it should have made him look at his own house. Hold that in mind. We will see what he did with it.

What Onan was there to do

The custom

Raise up seed to thy brother

When a married man died with no son, his brother married the widow, and their first child counted as the dead man's. It kept a name alive, and it kept a widow from being left with nothing. In a world with no pension and no welfare, a childless widow had no future.

What Onan did

He took the marriage, and refused the duty

“Onan knew that the seed should not be his.” He would not give his dead brother a child. He used Tamar, and cheated her and Er. It was selfish and it was a lie, and “the thing which he did displeased the LORD”.

“If brethren dwell together, and one of them die, and have no child, the wife of the dead shall not marry without unto a stranger: her husband’s brother shall go in unto her, and take her to him to wife, and perform the duty of an husband’s brother unto her. And it shall be, that the firstborn which she beareth shall succeed in the name of his brother which is dead, that his name be not put out of Israel.”

Deuteronomy 25:5-6

Moses wrote that law down centuries later, but here it is already a known duty. People have read other meanings into verse 9. Stay with what the text says: the sin was selfishness toward a widow and a dead brother.

A promise he never meant to keep

What Judah said to TamarGo home to your father's house and wait. When Shelah is grown, he will marry you.

What he was thinking“Lest peradventure he die also, as his brethren did.” afraid

Judah was afraid of losing a third son, so he blamed the widow, as if Tamar had brought the deaths. Two sons had died for their own sin (v. 7, 10). Tamar was the one wronged: sent away, shut up, and left with no husband, no child, and nothing to look forward to.

Fear can make a man unjust. Judah wronged Tamar before she did a single thing, and he did it quietly, with a polite promise.

Part two

The road to Timnath

Verses 12–23

A widow in a veil, a man far from home, and a pledge he should never have given.

Two people, both in the wrong

What was done to Tamar

She was cheated

Shelah was grown. Judah never sent for her. She was owed a husband and a child, and she was going to get neither.

What Tamar chose

She set a trap

She took off her widow's clothes, put on a veil, and sat by the road to Timnath where she knew he would pass.

The Bible tells what she did and does not praise it. A wrong done to you does not make every answer to it right. And “Judah was comforted”: his wife had just died, and he was going up to the sheepshearing, a time of feasting and company. He was lonely, and far from his brothers.

Notice that he had a friend with him, Hirah. But Hirah was an Adullamite, not a brother in the faith, and a friend who does not hold you to anything is no help at the moment you need it.

What Judah handed over

Let it be said plainly: Judah took the woman for a prostitute, and slept with her. That is sexual sin, and the Bible calls it that. It does not dress it up, and neither will we. He paid with a promise: a kid from the flock, and a pledge until he sent it.

  1. signethis seal, worn on a cord around the neck. A man pressed it in clay as his signature.
  2. braceletsin the Hebrew, the cord the seal hung on
  3. staffthe carved walking stick that marked a man and his family

He gave her everything that said who I am: his signature, his cord, his staff. In effect, he handed over his ID, for one night with a stranger. Whatever you give in the dark is something somebody can bring out in the daylight.

“But if ye will not do so, behold, ye have sinned against the LORD: and be sure your sin will find you out.”

Numbers 32:23

“Lest we be shamed”

Hirah, in TimnathWhere is the woman who was by the road? The men there said there was no such woman here.

JudahLet her keep it, lest we be shamed. I sent the kid, and you did not find her. drop it

Listen to what Judah is worried about: his name. He is not worried that he slept with a stranger, or that he lost his seal. He is worried that people will laugh. He keeps his goat honest and his conscience quiet.

There is a difference between being ashamed of getting caught and being sorry for what you did. Judah is the first. The next scene is where he learns to be the second.

Part three

More righteous than I

Verses 24–30

Three months later, the evidence is put in his hand.

The judge was the guilty man

What Judah said

“Bring her forth, and let her be burnt”

Tamar was pregnant, and unmarried, as far as anyone knew. Judah, the head of the family, passed sentence at once.

What was true

He was the father

The man who ordered her death was the man who had gone to her. He had cheated her, and then he condemned her.

It is easy to see in others: the sin we are quickest to condemn is often the one we are hiding. Another man in the Bible did the same, and a prophet had to tell him.

“And David’s anger was greatly kindled against the man; and he said to Nathan, As the LORD liveth, the man that hath done this thing shall surely die: And he shall restore the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing, and because he had no pity. And Nathan said to David, Thou art the man. Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, I anointed thee king over Israel, and I delivered thee out of the hand of Saul;”

2 Samuel 12:5-7

The same words, back in Judah's hands

What Judah's brothers said to Jacob

“And they sent the coat of many colours, and they brought it to their father; and said, This have we found: know now whether it be thy son’s coat or no.”

Genesis 37:32

What Tamar said to Judah

“When she was brought forth, she sent to her father in law, saying, By the man, whose these are, am I with child: and she said, Discern, I pray thee, whose are these, the signet, and bracelets, and staff.”

Genesis 38:25

הַכֶּר־נָא said haker-na “recognise, please”: the same Hebrew words, in both verses

Back in his own hands

Years before, Judah helped send a coat and a lie to his father, saying know now whether it be thy son's. Now the same words come back to him: discern, I pray thee, whose are these. A man who has deceived someone with a piece of evidence will one day be handed a piece of evidence of his own.

And look how kindly Tamar does it. She does not shout his name in the street. She sends the signet, the cord and the staff, and asks him to look. She gives him the chance to speak first.

What Judah did with it

  1. 1Judah acknowledged themHe did not blame the loneliness, the grief, or the veil. He said, those are mine.38:26
  2. 2She hath been more righteous than IHe named his real fault, not only the night by the road: “because that I gave her not to Shelah my son.”38:26
  3. 3he knew her again no moreHe stopped. No excuses, and no second time.38:26

“More righteous than I” does not mean Tamar did right in everything. It means: in this matter, she was in the right and I was in the wrong. Judah could have kept quiet and let her be burnt. He chose to take the shame instead of leaving her to take the fire.

Say it first

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

Notice who says it first. Judah did not wait for the evidence to be read out in front of the crowd. He looked and said it. If you have something you've done that someone holds a piece of, you will be better off if you say it first: to God, then to a brother in Christ and your pastor.

“How hast thou broken forth?”

פֶּרֶץ said perets “a breach”: a breaking through (in KJV, Pharez)

Twins. The one with the scarlet thread on his wrist was marked as first, but his brother broke through ahead of him. The family called the child Pharez, “breaking through”, and the other Zarah.

It is a pattern in Genesis: the younger ahead of the elder. Jacob before Esau, Pharez before Zarah. God does not follow the order men expect. Being first, or being clean, is not what He is looking for.

The child of this chapter, born out of a sin and a deception, is named in the family tree of King David and of Jesus. The Bible says so on purpose, and does not hide the mother's name: “Thamar”.

  2. RachabRahab: a Canaanite woman of Jericho, with a past (Joshua 2)
  3. Rutha Moabite, a foreigner (the book of Ruth)

In a Jewish family tree, women were not usually named. Matthew names these four, each with a stained or outsider story. He could have left them out. He chose to put them in, and then he told us why Jesus came:

“And she shall bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins.”

Matthew 1:21

Where sin abounded

“Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound. But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound:”

Romans 5:20

Genesis 38 is that verse in a story. A man sins, hides it, and condemns another for it, and the evidence is in his own hand. And the result is a child in the family of Christ.

That does not mean it was fine. It cost Er and Onan their lives, it hurt Tamar, and it hurt Judah. It means that if you have fallen, as Judah did, you do not need to hide in the dark or give up. Come out, say it, and let God start again.

Question 1 of 6

Drifting

What happens to a man who goes down from his brothers?

What verses tell us about this?

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9-10

Answer

He ends up far from home, with new friends, no one to answer to, and a family that comes apart. The Bible does not blame one step, but the direction is plain: down. “Woe to him that is alone when he falleth.” Stay close to your brothers in Christ and your pastor, and tell them what you are fighting.

Question 2 of 6

God sees

What did the LORD see in Er and Onan?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Er was “wicked in the sight of the LORD”, and the Bible does not say how. Onan took a marriage and refused its one duty, and cheated a widow and his dead brother. Both were seen. Nothing a man does in private is private to the LORD.

Question 3 of 6

Double standards

What did Judah want done to Tamar, and why was that wrong?

What verses tell us about this?

“So when they continued asking him, he lifted up himself, and said unto them, He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her.”

John 8:7

Answer

He wanted her burnt, and he was the father. The man who had gone to her, and cheated her of Shelah, passed the sentence. We are quick to condemn in others what we are hiding. When you are quick to judge a man's sin, ask what you have not said about your own.

Question 4 of 6

Confession

What did Judah say when the evidence was put in his hand?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

“She hath been more righteous than I.” He acknowledged what was his, named his real fault, and stopped. He could have let her burn in silence, and he took the shame instead. God forgives a man who confesses. Say it first, to Him, and to a brother or your pastor.

Question 5 of 6

Grace

Who was born out of this broken family?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Judas begat Phares and Zara of Thamar; and Phares begat Esrom; and Esrom begat Aram;”

Matthew 1:3

“And let thy house be like the house of Pharez, whom Tamar bare unto Judah, of the seed which the LORD shall give thee of this young woman.”

Ruth 4:12

Answer

Pharez, the child of Judah and Tamar. He became the father of a line that ran to Boaz, to David, and to Jesus. Matthew names “Thamar” on purpose. God does not need a clean family tree to bring His Son into the world, and He does not need a clean record in yours.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

Can a man who fell this far still be used by God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now therefore, I pray thee, let thy servant abide instead of the lad a bondman to my lord; and let the lad go up with his brethren.”

Genesis 44:33

“The sceptre shall not depart from Judah, nor a lawgiver from between his feet, until Shiloh come; and unto him shall the gathering of the people be.”

Genesis 49:10

“Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound. But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound:”

Romans 5:20

Answer

Yes. Judah, who sold his brother and slept with a stranger, later offered himself as a slave for his brother, and his tribe became the tribe of the kings, and of the King. Where sin abounded, grace did much more abound. If you have fallen, do not hide. Confess, stop, and let Him begin again.
