Leviticus 1

The LORD called unto Moses

The glory has filled the tent, and no man can go in. So the LORD speaks from it, and tells his people how a sinful man may come near: with a gift he brings, and a life given in his place.

The book of Leviticus

Where: at the foot of Mount Sinai, in Arabia, where the tabernacle stands among Israel's tents.

Who wrote it: Moses. “The LORD called unto Moses” (1:1). The last verse says the commandments were given “in mount Sinai” (27:34). Jesus says, “offer the gift that Moses commanded” (Matthew 8:4).

To whom: Israel, a people brought out of slavery not quite a year before.

Leviticus is named for the tribe of Levi, the priests' tribe.

When: a single month at Sinai

  1. 15th, 1st monthOut of EgyptNumbers 33:3
  2. 3rd monthIsrael comes to SinaiExodus 19:1
  3. at SinaiThe law, the calf, the tabernacle madeExodus 20–39
  4. 1st day, 1st month, 2nd yearThe tabernacle reared upExodus 40:17
  5. LeviticusThe LORD speaks out of the tabernacleLeviticus 1–27
  6. 1st day, 2nd monthThe people numberedNumbers 1:1
  7. 20th day, 2nd monthThe cloud lifts; they marchNumbers 10:11

Exodus ends with the tabernacle reared up on “the first day of the first month” of the second year. Numbers begins “on the first day of the second month, in the second year”. Leviticus fits in between: about one month, with Israel standing at the same mountain.

Exodus told how God brought his people out and came to live among them. Leviticus answers the next question: how can sinful people live beside a holy God?

The shape of the book

  1. 1 to 7: the offeringshow a man comes near: burnt, meat, peace, sin and trespass
  2. 8 to 10: the prieststhe men who stand between: washed, clothed, anointed
  3. 11 to 15: clean and uncleanwhat shuts a man out, and the way back in
  4. 16: the day of atonementonce a year, blood in the most holy place
  5. 17 to 27: holy living“Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy” (19:2)

“Speak unto all the congregation of the children of Israel, and say unto them, Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy.”

Leviticus 19:2

Peter lifts that line into the New Testament (1 Peter 1:16). And the New Testament says Christ is the end of the offerings (Hebrews 10:14). If you have blown it, if you are a man with a past, this book is good news: God made the way back, and he made it himself.

Part one

The LORD calls

Verses 1–2

The tent that no man could enter now has a voice.

Out of the tent

The last thing we read in Exodus was a closed door:

“And Moses was not able to enter into the tent of the congregation, because the cloud abode thereon, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle.”

Exodus 40:35

So Leviticus begins with God speaking from inside. Sinful men did not find their way to him. He called. “The tabernacle of the congregation” is the tent where the LORD meets his people: “where I will meet you, to speak there unto thee” (Exodus 29:42).

The first word of the book is a call. If you are reading this today carrying something heavy, that is where it starts: he called first.

Part two

The bullock

Verses 3–9

The first and greatest offering: all of it given to God.

Hard words in verses 2 to 4

  1. offeringa gift brought near to God
  2. burnt sacrificealso called the burnt offering: the whole animal burned on the altar, nothing kept back
  3. blemisha flaw or defect: the animal had to have none
  4. voluntary willfreely, because he chose to
  5. atonementa covering over sin, so a guilty man can stand before God
  6. herd and flockthe herd is cattle; the flock is sheep and goats (verse 10)

Without blemish

The offering was the best, not the leftover: a male, with nothing wrong with it. Nothing damaged went on God's altar. The chapter says “without blemish” twice (verses 3 and 10).

A man in recovery knows the temptation to give God what is left of the week. But this is a picture of someone else. Peter says what it points to:

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

The animal had to be without blemish because the man was not. The only one who could really stand in our place had no sin of his own.

Nobody forced him

“If any man of you bring an offering” (verse 2): it began with the man, and “of his own voluntary will”. God does not drag a man to the altar. The offering of a man who came because he was made to would be worth nothing.

“For if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath, and not according to that he hath not.”

2 Corinthians 8:12

Come because you want to be right with God, not because somebody is watching. A man who comes freely, even with a heavy heart, is welcomed.

The hand on the head

The man pressed his hand on the bullock's head: “accepted for him”.

Deathdeserved

Welcomeaccepted

The sinnerbrings it, lays his hand on it
The offeringwithout blemish, killed in his place

They trade places: the sinner is accepted, and the offering is given up.

“All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.”

Isaiah 53:6

Every man who stood there knew it was his sin that cost a life. And Jesus has already stood in our place (2 Corinthians 5:21).

By the door

The altar is the first thing a man meets when he comes in, before the laver and the holy place. Nobody got past it without an offering. “The altar that is by the door.”

Blood, and what “flay” means

Flay (verse 6) means to strip off the skin. The man himself kills the animal. The priests, Aaron's sons, bring the blood and sprinkle it on the altar. Why the blood? The LORD tells Moses later:

“For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.”

Leviticus 17:11

“And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.”

Hebrews 9:22

It is not pretty, and it was not meant to be. Sin costs a life. That is why we need to see it plainly: the price is real, and it was paid.

All of it, and a sweet savour

The burnt offering is the one that is burned all. The priest does not keep a piece, and neither does the man. The chapter calls it a burnt sacrifice or a burnt offering eight times, and says “a sweet savour unto the LORD” three times (verses 9, 13 and 17). A savour is a smell. The LORD is pleased with it.

The inwards and the legs are washed in water first (verse 9): nothing unclean goes on the altar.

“And walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour.”

Ephesians 5:2

That is why Paul calls Christ “an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour”. And he says that because of him we are “accepted in the beloved” (Ephesians 1:6).

Part three

The flock and the fowls

Verses 10–17

The same offering, for a man who has less.

Hard words in verses 14 to 17

  1. fowlsbirds
  2. turtledoves, young pigeonssmall wild doves, and young pigeons
  3. wring offtwist off
  4. cropthe bird's throat pouch, where it keeps what it has eaten
  5. cleave(verse 17) split open
  6. asunderapart, in two pieces

The bird is split open with its wings, but is not parted in two. Everything is the same as the bullock: the blood, the fire, the altar, and the same sweet savour.

A way in for every man

  1. The herda bullock, a male without blemish (verse 3)
  2. The flocka sheep or a goat, a male without blemish (verse 10)
  3. The fowlsturtledoves or young pigeons (verse 14)

A man with a herd could bring a bullock. A man with only a few doves could bring them. Chapter 5 says it plainly: “if he be not able to bring a lamb, then he shall bring… two turtledoves, or two young pigeons” (5:7). The LORD did not close the door to a man because he was poor. Notice who brought doves:

“And to offer a sacrifice according to that which is said in the law of the Lord, A pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons.”

Luke 2:24

Mary and Joseph, with the baby Jesus. The Saviour was born into a family that brought the offering of the man with little.

Again and again, or once for all

Here, in Leviticus

Offered again and again

Every day, two lambs at the door, one in the morning and one at even (Exodus 29:38–42). Every sin, another offering. The priest stood every day, and the work was never done.

In Christ

One offering, for ever

He offered himself once, and sat down. Nothing is left to add.

“For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins.”

Hebrews 10:4

“For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.”

Hebrews 10:14

The offerings were real, and God commanded them. But Hebrews says they were “a shadow of good things to come” (10:1). A man who falls again tomorrow does not need a new offering. He needs to go back to the one that was given, and to tell his brothers in Christ the truth.

Come near, today

You do not need a bullock. What you need is what the man at the altar had: you come on purpose, you own what you did, and you trust the offering God has provided.

  1. Sinname it. He has already seen it.
  2. Regretbring it to the throne of grace, not to the bottle
  3. Grace“accepted for him”: someone else was given in his place
  4. Hopetoday is not the end: he calls you to come near
  5. Todaytalk to the LORD before the day starts, and tell one brother what you are carrying

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

Points to remember

  1. The call“And the LORD called unto Moses, and spake unto him out of the tabernacle of the congregation” 1:1He called first. He still does.
  2. A free gift“he shall offer it of his own voluntary will” 1:3Come freely, not because you must.
  3. Without blemish“let him offer a male without blemish” 1:3The best, for the man who was not. It points to Christ (1 Peter 1:19).
  4. The hand“it shall be accepted for him to make atonement for him” 1:4The offering stood in his place.
  5. A sweet savour“an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD” 1:9The LORD was pleased. Christ is that offering (Ephesians 5:2).
  6. A way for all“he shall bring his offering of turtledoves, or of young pigeons” 1:14No man is shut out for being poor.

Question 1 of 6

The call

Who spoke to Moses in this chapter, and from where?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Moses was not able to enter into the tent of the congregation, because the cloud abode thereon, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle.”

Exodus 40:35

“This shall be a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before the LORD: where I will meet you, to speak there unto thee.”

Exodus 29:42

Answer

The LORD himself, out of the tabernacle. Moses could not go in, so God called to him from inside. He meets his people at the door and speaks to them. He always makes the first move.

Question 2 of 6

A free gift

Was the man forced to bring his offering, or did he choose?

What verses tell us about this?

“For if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath, and not according to that he hath not.”

2 Corinthians 8:12

Answer

He chose. “Of his own voluntary will.” God does not drag a man to the altar, but he welcomes the man who comes, and takes the willing mind as it is.

Question 3 of 6

Without blemish

What kind of animal did the LORD accept?

What verses tell us about this?

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Answer

A male without blemish, the best, with nothing wrong with it. It points to Christ, “a lamb without blemish and without spot”, who had no sin of his own to answer for.

Question 4 of 6

The hand

What did the man do with his hand before the animal was killed, and what was it for?

What verses tell us about this?

“All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.”

Isaiah 53:6

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Answer

He laid his hand on the head of the offering, and it was accepted for him, to make atonement for him. The animal stood in his place. That is the picture of what Christ did: “the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.”

Question 5 of 6

A sweet savour

What did the LORD think of the burnt offering when it was all on the altar?

What verses tell us about this?

“And walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour.”

Ephesians 5:2

“To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved.”

Ephesians 1:6

Answer

It was “a sweet savour unto the LORD”: he was pleased. Three times in this chapter. And Paul says Christ gave himself the same way, so that we are “accepted in the beloved”.

Question 6 of 6

A way for all

Could a man with very little still come to the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

“And to offer a sacrifice according to that which is said in the law of the Lord, A pair of turtledoves, or two young pigeons.”

Luke 2:24

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

Yes. A bullock, a sheep, a goat, or a pair of birds: there was a way in for every man. Jesus says the same to the poor and the weary: “Come unto me… and I will give you rest.”
