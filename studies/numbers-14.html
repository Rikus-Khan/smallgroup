Numbers 14

The night they wept, and the forty years

The people cry all night and talk of a captain to take them back to Egypt. Joshua and Caleb plead with them, and the crowd reaches for stones. Moses prays, and the LORD pardons; but that generation will not see the land. Their little ones will, and so will one man with another spirit.

Part one

“Let us return into Egypt”

Verses 1–10

A night of tears, a plan to go back, and stones in their hands.

What they said that night

The whole congregationWould God that we had died in the land of Egypt! or would God we had died in this wilderness!

About the LORDWherefore hath the LORD brought us unto this land, to fall by the sword, that our wives and our children should be a prey?
blaming God

To one anotherLet us make a captain, and let us return into Egypt.
going back

Egypt was the place of the whip, the brick quota and the drowned sons. One bad report, and it sounded better than the land God had promised.

The pull of the old life

Every man who has tried to get free knows this night. Things get hard, and the old life starts to look good. Memory edits out the chains and keeps the fish: “We remember the fish, which we did eat in Egypt freely” (11:5).

Notice how quickly it moves. First tears, then complaint, then blame (“wherefore hath the LORD brought us”), then a plan: make a captain. A man does not go back all at once. He goes back by a conversation like this one, usually with other men who want to go back too.

If you hear yourself saying, “It wasn't that bad back then,” stop and tell a brother in Christ today. Say it out loud before it turns into a plan. Egypt never got better. It only looks better from the wilderness.

Two men against a nation

Moses and Aaron fell on their faces. Joshua and Caleb rent their clothes: they tore their own robes, the way a man in Israel showed grief at a death or a great sin. Then they spoke:

  • “an exceeding good land”the land is still good; nothing about it changed overnight
  • “If the LORD delight in us”it rests on God, not on our strength
  • “rebel not… fear not”the real danger is not the giants; it is turning against the LORD
  • “they are bread for us”we will eat them up. The ten said the land “eateth up the inhabitants” (13:32); Joshua and Caleb turn it around
  • “their defence is departed”their shelter, their protection, is gone from them, “and the LORD is with us”

Stones for the men who told the truth

The answer to “the LORD is with us” was a call to kill them. A crowd that has made up its mind to be afraid does not want a man in the room who still believes.

Then, before a stone was thrown, the glory of the LORD appeared in the tabernacle, in front of everyone. God stepped in for the two men who stood up for him.

Two questions to take with you. When a brother tells you a hard truth, is your first move to pick up a stone? And when you are the one telling it, remember: God saw Joshua and Caleb standing alone. He sees you too.

Part two

“Pardon, I beseech thee”

Verses 11–25

Moses stands in the gap, with God's own words in his mouth.

“Ere they believe me”

The LORD names the root. It was not the giants, or the walls, or even the tears. It was this: how long will it be ere they believe me? (Ere means before.) After ten plagues, a sea, the manna and the rock, they still would not trust him.

“So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief.”

Hebrews 3:19

And God made Moses an offer: smite them with the pestilence (a deadly spreading sickness), and I will make of thee a greater nation. Start over with Moses, and let the rest go. Any man who had put up with this people for a year might have said yes. Watch what Moses does with it.

Moses pleads for God's name

Moses turns the offer down without even mentioning it. He does not argue that the people deserve better. They don't. He argues from the LORD's own name.

Egypt heard what God did. The people of Canaan had heard that “thou LORD art among this people”, seen “face to face”, with the cloud by day and the fire by night. If Israel dies in the desert, the nations will say: the LORD was not able.

That is a good way to pray for a brother who has failed again, or for yourself. Not “he deserves it”, but “LORD, your name is on him. Finish what you started.”

Praying God's words back to him

Exodus 34:6–7, the LORD to Moses

The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth, Keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty…

Numbers 14:18, Moses to the LORD

The LORD is longsuffering, and of great mercy, forgiving iniquity and transgression, and by no means clearing the guilty…

Moses quotes what the LORD said of himself on the mount (longsuffering: patient on and on), and adds, “as thou hast forgiven this people, from Egypt even until now.” Past mercy was his reason to ask for more.

You can pray this way. You don't need fine words. Find what God has said about himself, and hand it back to him: “You said you forgive. I'm asking.”

Pardoned, and still a cost

“I have pardoned”

What God forgave

He did not wipe them out. He kept them as his people, fed them manna for forty years, and led them with the cloud.

“But…”

What it still cost

That generation would not see the land. They had “tempted me now these ten times”: God's own count. It was not one bad day.

This is one of the most important things in the Bible for a man in recovery. God's forgiveness is real and complete: “I have pardoned.” And some things a man broke stay broken for a while: a record, a marriage, a body, a trust. Forgiveness does not always erase the wilderness. It means God walks through it with you.

“Another spirit”

Caleb saw the same giants. The difference was inside him: another spirit, a different heart, and he followed me fully, all the way, not half. God gives him a name here: “my servant Caleb.”

Given Hebron

“lo, I am this day fourscore and five years old” (Joshua 14:10)

Forty-five years later Caleb asked for the very mountain where the giants lived: “give me this mountain.” And he drove out the three sons of Anak, the same three the spies had seen at Hebron (Joshua 15:14). The men the ten feared were the men Caleb beat, at eighty-five.

“Give me this mountain”

“As yet I am as strong this day as I was in the day that Moses sent me: as my strength was then, even so is my strength now, for war, both to go out, and to come in. Now therefore give me this mountain, whereof the LORD spake in that day; for thou heardest in that day how the Anakims were there, and that the cities were great and fenced: if so be the LORD will be with me, then I shall be able to drive them out, as the LORD said.”

Joshua 14:11–12

Caleb walked the wilderness for forty years with the men who had said no. He watched that generation fall in the wilderness. He did not get to go in early because he was right. But he held on to what God said for forty-five years, and he got every inch of it.

And verse 25: the rest are told to turn back, “by the way of the Red sea”, away from the land and back toward the Red sea. Caleb and Joshua went with them.

Part three

“Each day for a year”

Verses 26–45

The sentence, the little ones, and a battle fought without God.

“As ye have spoken”

Verse 2, the people

…or would God we had died in this wilderness!

Verse 29, the LORD

Your carcases shall fall in this wilderness…

“As ye have spoken in mine ears, so will I do to you.” They said they would rather die in the wilderness than trust him, and God gave them what they asked for. Carcases are dead bodies; doubtless means without any doubt.

The ones counted “from twenty years old and upward” are the men of war numbered in chapter 1. The men who were old enough to choose were the ones who chose. Only two of them would stand in the land: Caleb, and Joshua.

The little ones go in

In verse 3 the fathers used their children as the reason to go back: “our wives and our children should be a prey.” God takes their own words and turns them into a promise: your little ones, which ye said should be a prey, them will I bring in.

The children would “bear your whoredoms”: carry the cost of their fathers' unfaithfulness to God. The KJV often calls turning from God to something else whoredom, the unfaithfulness of a wife who leaves her husband. The children wandered because their fathers would not trust him. But they did not stay out. They went in.

Some of us have kids who have paid for what we did. That is a real grief, and it should be. But hear this: a father's failure does not finish his children's story. God can bring them in, and he can use a father who has turned back to help.

Forty days, forty years

one day in the land, one year in the wilderness

They searched the land forty days (13:25). Now: “each day for a year.” My breach of promise: they would know what it is to have the promise taken from them. Not because God failed, but because they refused it. The promise itself stood, for their children.

“And the space in which we came from Kadesh-barnea, until we were come over the brook Zered, was thirty and eight years; until all the generation of the men of war were wasted out from among the host, as the LORD sware unto them.”

Deuteronomy 2:14

The forty years

  1. out of Egyptthe first year33:3
  2. Sinai leftthe second year10:11
  3. Kadeshthe spies, and this night13–14
  4. the brook Zeredthirty and eight years laterDeuteronomy 2:14
  5. the plains of Moabthe fortieth yearDeuteronomy 1:3

From the night at Kadesh to the brook Zered, thirty-eight years. With the time already spent, forty in all, just as God said. A trip of “eleven days” (Deuteronomy 1:2) took a generation.

Yet even those years were not God walking away. Moses told the children afterward how the LORD “bare thee, as a man doth bear his son, in all the way that ye went” (Deuteronomy 1:31). Manna every morning. The cloud every day. A man in his wilderness is still carried.

“lived still” (verse 38)“died by the plague before the LORD” (verse 37)

The ten did not only doubt for themselves. They “made all the congregation to murmur”. A slander is a false report that does harm. A leader's fear spreads, and so does his faith. These ten answered for what their words did to a nation.

Every man in this room has people who listen to him, more than he thinks: his kids, his brothers here, the new man who walks in tomorrow. Your report about God travels.

Sorry, and still not listening

They “mourned greatly”. They said the right words: “we have sinned.” Then they got up early and did the one thing God had just told them not to do. Before, he had said, go up. Now he had said, “To morrow turn you, and get you into the wilderness” (verse 25). They went up anyway.

Sorrow of the world

Sorry it cost me

Grieves the loss. Then tries to fix it its own way, on its own terms, to undo the consequences.

Godly sorrow

Sorry I turned from him

Grieves the sin. Then does what God says now, even when it is the long road through the wilderness.

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Up the hill without God

They presumed: they went up on their own say-so, as if God had to back them. The ark stayed in the camp, and so did Moses. The same Amalekites and Canaanites they would not fight with God beat them without him, and chased them “even unto Hormah”.

The day before, they would not go up with God. The next day, they tried to go up without him. Both were the same sin: doing it their own way.

A lot of us know this one. A man who has blown it tries to make up for it all at once, on willpower: a big promise, a big gesture, no help, no brothers, no waiting on God. It doesn't hold. The way back is not the hill top alone. It is turning around and walking with God and his people, one day at a time.

“To day, if ye will hear his voice”

The book of Hebrews takes this very night and preaches it to us. Paul quotes the psalm, and then asks who it was that fell:

“But with whom was he grieved forty years? was it not with them that had sinned, whose carcases fell in the wilderness? And to whom sware he that they should not enter into his rest, but to them that believed not? So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief.”

Hebrews 3:17–19

Carcases, the wilderness, the oath, forty years: every word comes from Numbers 14. And the lesson is the same as God's question in verse 11: how long will it be ere they believe me? They did not stay out because the giants were too big. They stayed out because they would not believe.

Exhort one another daily

“Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God. But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

Hebrews 3:12–13

God's answer to an evil report is not one man alone with his willpower. It is brothers who speak to each other daily. That is what this group is. To exhort is to urge a man on, to call him forward.

Sin is deceitful: it tells a man Egypt was not so bad. It hardens him a little at a time. The cure is a Caleb in your phone and a Joshua at the table, every day, while it is still called To day.

“There remaineth therefore a rest”

  • God's rest“God did rest the seventh day from all his works” (Hebrews 4:4)
  • The landJoshua brought them in, “And the land had rest from war” (Joshua 14:15); but that was not the last word (Hebrews 4:8)
  • Our rest“There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God” (Hebrews 4:9)

“For we which have believed do enter into rest… For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his.”

Hebrews 4:3, 10

The rest is entered by believing, not by working your way in. A man who trusts Christ stops trying to save himself. That is the land Caleb's faith points to, and the door is open to every man who will believe him to day.

Real world: which way today?

  • When the old life callsSay it out loud to a brother before it becomes a plan to “return into Egypt”.
  • When you have failedPray God's own words back to him: longsuffering, of great mercy, forgiving iniquity.
  • When there is a costAccept the wilderness without leaving God. He still carries you in it.
  • When you want to fix it fastDon't run up the hill alone. Turn, and walk with God and his people.

And if you fell yesterday, hear this before anything else:

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

Question 1 of 6

Back to Egypt

What did the people say they wanted when they heard the report?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They wished they had died in Egypt or in the wilderness, and planned to make a captain and go back. Egypt was slavery. When the old life starts to look good, tell a brother today.

Question 2 of 6

Two men

What did Joshua and Caleb tell the congregation about the land and the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That the land was exceeding good, that the LORD would bring them in if he delighted in them, and that they must not rebel or fear, for “the LORD is with us.” The crowd answered with stones, and God's glory appeared.

Question 3 of 6

The prayer

What words of the LORD did Moses pray back to him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The words God spoke of himself on the mount (Exodus 34:6–7): longsuffering, of great mercy, forgiving iniquity. And the LORD said, “I have pardoned.” Pray his own promises back to him.

Question 4 of 6

Another spirit

Why did the LORD say he would bring Caleb into the land?

What verses tell us about this?

“Hebron therefore became the inheritance of Caleb the son of Jephunneh the Kenezite unto this day, because that he wholly followed the LORD God of Israel.”

Joshua 14:14

Answer

Because he had another spirit and followed the LORD fully. He waited forty-five years, and at eighty-five he took Hebron from the sons of Anak. What God promises, he keeps.

Question 5 of 6

The little ones

Who did the LORD say would go in, and how long would the rest wander?

What verses tell us about this?

“So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief.”

Hebrews 3:19

Answer

Their little ones, with Caleb and Joshua. The rest would wander forty years, a year for each day of the search, until they died. They stayed out because of unbelief; the rest of God is entered by believing, to day.

Question 6 of 6

Too late

What happened when the people went up the next morning?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They presumed to go up without the LORD, the ark or Moses, and were beaten back as far as Hormah. Sorrow that goes its own way is not repentance. Godly sorrow turns and does what God says now.
