Jonah 4

Angry at mercy

Nineveh is spared, and Jonah is furious. God answers His angry prophet with a plant, a worm, a hot wind, and a question.

Things to watch for

Nineveh, Israel's cruel enemy, has turned from its evil, and God has spared it. Jonah preached the warning. Now watch how he takes the mercy.

Part one

Angry at mercy

Verses 1–4

At last we hear why Jonah ran.

“I knew it”

Here's why he ran. Not fear of failing, but fear that God would forgive his enemies. Jonah is quoting what God said about Himself to Moses:

Exodus 34:6mercifulgraciouslongsufferingabundant in goodness
samesamesame ideasame idea
Jonah 4:2mercifulgraciousslow to angerof great kindness

Israel sang this about God for centuries. Jonah loved it when it was about Israel. He hated it when it reached Nineveh. His complaint is the best news in the Bible: God really is like this, for anyone who turns.

A question, not a lecture

JonahTherefore now, O LORD, take, I beseech thee, my life from me; for it is better for me to die than to live.

The LORDDoest thou well to be angry?
a question

Jonah isn't the only man of God who has said this. Elijah, worn out and afraid, said it too:

“But he himself went a day’s journey into the wilderness, and came and sat down under a juniper tree: and he requested for himself that he might die; and said, It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life; for I am not better than my fathers.”

1 Kings 19:4

God didn't strike either man down or throw him away. He kept talking to him. If you've had the thought “better to die than to live,” say it out loud today: to God, and to one of the men here. You are not alone. In the U.S. you can also call or text 988, any hour.

Part two

The gourd

Verses 5–11

God teaches His prophet with a plant he didn't plant.

Glad about the wrong thing

Jonah goes out, builds a little shelter (a “booth”), and sits down to watch, still hoping the city burns. God makes a fast-growing plant shoot up over him for shade. We don't know exactly what plant it was. Many think a castor-oil plant, with big leaves.

Jonah 4:1

“Displeased exceedingly”

A whole city spared.

Jonah 4:6

“Exceeding glad”

Shade for his own head.

Big feelings about small things, small feelings about big things. That's what self-pity does to a man. It shrinks the world down to the size of his own comfort.

God prepared

Four times in this book God “prepared” something: a great fish (1:17), a gourd (4:6), a worm (4:7), and a scorching east wind (4:8). The comforts and the hard things both came from His hand.

At dawn a worm chews the plant, and it withers.

Then a hot wind off the desert, and the sun on his head, until he's faint and wants to die again. God isn't being cruel. He's setting up the question that ends the book.

A plant, or a city?

A “score” is twenty, so “more than sixscore thousand” is more than 120,000 people who “cannot discern between their right hand and their left”: people who didn't know right from wrong before God. (Some take it to mean the little children alone.)

Jonah grieved over a plant he didn't plant, didn't water, and had for one night. God says: and should not I care about a city full of people I made? Even the cattle mattered to Him.

The book ends with a question

We never hear Jonah's answer. The book just stops. Jesus told a story that ends the same way, with an older brother who stays home and stays angry while his father throws a party for the son who came back:

“And he was angry, and would not go in: therefore came his father out, and intreated him.”

Luke 15:28

Both stories leave the question open, because the question is for us. Do we want God to be merciful to the people we can't stand: the ones who hurt us, the ones we think don't deserve it? Resentment is poison for a man in recovery. God's heart is bigger than our grudges.

“Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth.”

1 Timothy 2:4

Points to remember

  1. Anger“Doest thou well to be angry?” 4:4Bring the anger to God honestly, and let Him question it.
  2. God's character“A gracious God, and merciful, slow to anger” 4:2Jonah's complaint is our best news: God is merciful to anyone who turns.
  3. Despair“It is better for me to die than to live” 4:3God didn't throw Jonah or Elijah away. He kept talking. Say it out loud and get help.
  4. Grace“Therefore I fled before unto Tarshish” 4:2Nobody deserves mercy. Jonah had just received it, and begrudged it to others.
  5. What we care about“Jonah was exceeding glad of the gourd” 4:6Self-pity shrinks the world to our own comfort. People matter more than shade.
  6. God's heart“Should not I spare Nineveh, that great city” 4:11God cares about every lost person, even our enemies, and wants all to be saved.

Question 1 of 6

Anger

What do we do with our anger at God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath:”

Ephesians 4:26

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.”

James 1:19–20

Answer

Bring it to Him, like Jonah did, and then let Him ask the hard question: “Doest thou well to be angry?” God can handle our honesty. What He won't do is agree that our grudges are right. Anger kept and fed doesn't produce anything good. Anger brought to God can be dealt with.

Question 2 of 6

God's character

What did Jonah know about God?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD passed by before him, and proclaimed, The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth,”

Exodus 34:6

Answer

He knew God is gracious, merciful, slow to anger, and full of kindness, and that He holds back judgment when people turn. Jonah was right about God. He was wrong about what to do with it. What he complained about is the best news there is: God is like that toward you too.

Question 3 of 6

Despair

What does God do with a man who wants to die?

What verses tell us about this?

“But he himself went a day’s journey into the wilderness, and came and sat down under a juniper tree: and he requested for himself that he might die; and said, It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life; for I am not better than my fathers.”

1 Kings 19:4

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Psalm 34:18

Answer

He stays with him. With Jonah, God kept asking questions and kept teaching. With Elijah, He let him sleep, fed him, and spoke to him. God is near to the brokenhearted. If you're in that place, don't keep it to yourself. Tell God, tell a brother here today, and get help.

Question 4 of 6

Grace

Who deserves God's mercy?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he was angry, and would not go in: therefore came his father out, and intreated him.”

Luke 15:28

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Nobody. That's why it's called grace. Jonah had just been pulled out of the sea by mercy he didn't deserve, and he was angry that Nineveh got the same. The older brother did the same thing. Once a man remembers how much he's been forgiven, he stops keeping score on who deserves it.

Question 5 of 6

What we care about

What do we care about more than people?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For Jonah, it was his own comfort: shade for his head. He was angrier about a dead plant than glad about a saved city. All of us have our gourds: our stuff, our comfort, our being right. God lets the gourd wither sometimes so we can see what we've been caring about, and start caring about people the way He does.

Question 6 of 6

God's heart

How much does God care about lost people?

What verses tell us about this?

“But when he saw the multitudes, he was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd.”

Matthew 9:36

“Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth.”

1 Timothy 2:4

Answer

More than we can measure. God pitied more than 120,000 people who didn't know right from wrong, even the cattle, in the capital of Israel's enemy. Jesus looked at crowds and was moved with compassion. God wants all men to be saved, and that includes the man you would least expect. It includes you.
