Mark 11

Have faith in God

Jesus rides into Jerusalem on a colt, finds a fig tree with nothing but leaves, clears the temple, teaches His men to pray believing and forgiving, and turns the leaders' question back on them.

Part one

Blessed is he that cometh

Verses 1–11

Jesus has walked up the long road from Jericho. Now He rides the last mile into Jerusalem, and the crowd cheers Him in.

The last mile

He has climbed fifteen miles up from Jericho (10:46). Bethany sits on the far slope of the Mount of Olives, about two miles from Jerusalem. Bethphage was a village nearer the top.

From the ridge you look straight down across the Kidron valley at the temple. That's where He's riding.

He knows where the colt is and what the owners will say. Nothing ahead takes Him by surprise.

A King on a colt

How conquerors came

On a warhorse

A general came home at the head of his army, with prisoners behind him. Rome knew how to throw a parade.

How this King came

On a young donkey

Lowly, in peace, on a colt no one had ever ridden. Nothing about it threatened Rome. Everything about it fulfilled Scripture.

“Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion; shout, O daughter of Jerusalem: behold, thy King cometh unto thee: he is just, and having salvation; lowly, and riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass.”

Zechariah 9:9

Mark doesn't quote it, but Matthew (21:5) and John (12:15) both point to this promise, made about 500 years before. An animal “whereon never man sat” was fit for a holy use. Jesus had walked everywhere for three years. The one time He rides, He's telling Jerusalem plainly who He is.

What does “Hosanna” mean?

הוֹשִׁיעָה נָּא said hoshia na save now, we pray

“Save now, I beseech thee, O LORD: O LORD, I beseech thee, send now prosperity. Blessed be he that cometh in the name of the LORD: we have blessed you out of the house of the LORD.”

Psalm 118:25–26

Pilgrims sang this psalm on the way up to the feasts. Garments in the road were a welcome for a king (2 Kings 9:13). They hoped David's Son had come to throw out Rome.

He came to save them from something worse: their sins. Within days a crowd here would shout “Crucify him” (15:13). He knew it, and rode in anyway.

The last week

  1. Day onerides in, looks round11:1–11
  2. Day twofig tree, temple cleared11:12–19
  3. Day threequestions in the temple11:20–13:37
  4. Passoverthe supper, the arrest14
  5. The crosscrucified and buried15
  6. Third dayHe is risen16

Mark slows way down here. More than a third of his book covers about one week, the Passover of about AD 30. Christians have long called this first day Palm Sunday (the palms come from John 12:13).

The crowd went home. Jesus stood in the temple and “looked round about upon all things”: the tables, the trading, the noise. He saw all of it, and said nothing yet. Luke tells us that coming over the hill, “he beheld the city, and wept over it” (Luke 19:41). Then He went back to Bethany for the night.

Part two

Nothing but leaves

Verses 12–19

A tree that looks alive and has nothing on it. Then a temple that looks holy and has no room for prayer.

A tree with nothing on it

In spring a fig tree puts out small early figs with its leaves. The real crop, “the time of figs,” came in summer.

This tree had the leaves and nothing else. All show, no fruit.

Jesus wasn't losing His temper. Like the prophets, He was acting out a picture, and they often pictured Israel as a fig tree (Hosea 9:10). Watch where He goes next.

A market in the house of prayer

The temple tax had to be paid in one kind of silver coin, so moneychangers swapped coins for a fee. Doves were the poor man's offering (Leviticus 12:8).

It filled the one court where Gentiles could pray; a sign on the inner wall warned them not to pass, on pain of death. Jesus turned it over, as He had three years before (John 2:15).

Two prophets, one house

Isaiah 56:7

Even them will I bring to my holy mountain, and make them joyful in my house of prayer … for mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people.

Jeremiah 7:11

Is this house, which is called by my name, become a den of robbers in your eyes? Behold, even I have seen it, saith the LORD.

God promised to make outsiders joyful in His house. The market crowded them out.

A den is where robbers hide after robbing out on the road. Church can be a place to hide instead of a place to meet God. He wants a house of prayer, where we come honest.

Same words, two reactions

The scribes and chief priests

Sought how to destroy Him

The temple was their house, their business, and their power. He had just walked in and taken charge of it.

The people

Astonished at His doctrine

They'd never heard anyone teach like this. Doctrine just means teaching.

“And they were astonished at his doctrine: for he taught them as one that had authority, and not as the scribes.”

Mark 1:22

The truth that set the people free was the same truth that threatened the leaders. When Jesus puts His finger on something that pays us, or that we're proud of, we can either be astonished or get defensive. They “feared him”, and fear made them plot. At evening He left the city again (v. 19).

Part three

Have faith in God

Verses 20–26

The next morning the tree is dead to its roots. Jesus turns it into a lesson about prayer.

Dead from the roots up

One day, and the tree was dead “from the roots.” Mark wraps the temple inside the fig tree story, so each explains the other.

  1. Morninga tree: leaves, no fruit
  2. Middaya temple: business, no prayer
  3. Next daydried up from the roots

Busy religion with no fruit doesn't last. About forty years later, in AD 70, Rome burned that temple down (13:2).

Leaves are easy

Leaves are easy. Fruit takes roots.

Leaves are the parts people can see: the church words, the right look, showing up. None of that is bad. But Jesus came looking for fruit: a changed heart, honesty, love, a life that feeds somebody else.

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.”

John 15:5

A man can't squeeze fruit out of himself by trying harder. Fruit grows from roots that drink. Stay close to Him, in His Word, in prayer, with His people, and the fruit comes in its season.

Faith in God, not faith in faith

They were on the Mount of Olives road. On a clear day you can see the Dead Sea from up there. “This mountain … cast into the sea” was a picture of the impossible.

  • a magic formulathe words don't move God; God moves the mountain
  • a blank checkthis is not a promise to fill a wish list
  • faith in my own faithI'm not the power; He is
  • “Have faith in God”trust Him to do what only He can do

“And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us:”

1 John 5:14

Everybody has a mountain: a habit, a debt, a wreck of a marriage, a court date. You can't move it. Ask the God who can, and trust Him with the answer.

FORGIVENhanded to God

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Ought means anything. Forgiving isn't how a man gets saved; that's by grace, through faith. It's how a saved man stays close to his Father. A grudge stands between us and God.

What forgiving is, and isn't

  • saying it was all rightit wasn't, and God knows it wasn't
  • trusting them again tomorrowtrust is rebuilt slowly; forgiveness is given
  • pretending you've forgottenyou may remember it for years
  • handing the debt to Godyou stop collecting, and let Him be the judge

A man in recovery can't afford to carry resentment. It's one of the surest roads back to the bottle. And a man who has been forgiven by God of everything has the power, by His Spirit in him, to let go of what others owe.

Part four

By what authority?

Verses 27–33

The leaders come at Him with a question. He answers with one of His own.

Who's asking

  1. The chief prieststhe high priest's family and the leading priests. They ran the temple, and its money.
  2. The scribesthe experts in the Law of Moses, the Bible teachers of the day
  3. The eldersthe senior men of the leading families of Israel
  4. Togetherthe council (14:55), about seventy men: the highest Jewish court Rome allowed

“These things” means clearing out their temple. Who said you could? They had the titles. He had the authority of God.

Answering a question with a question was normal for a teacher in Israel, and this one wasn't a dodge. John had pointed to Jesus: “There cometh one mightier than I after me” (1:7). If John was from God, then so was the One John pointed to. The answer to their question was in His.

What's safe, not what's true

if we say“From heaven”
→
then he'll say“Why then did ye not believe him?”
if we say“Of men”
→
thenthe people turn on us: they all held John was a prophet

Not one of them asks, “Was he from God?” They only ask what each answer will cost. So: “We cannot tell.” They could. They wouldn't.

Jesus doesn't owe more light to a man who won't walk in the light he has. But no one who honestly seeks Him is turned away: God is “a rewarder of them that diligently seek him” (Hebrews 11:6).

Points to remember

  1. The King“Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord” 11:9He came lowly, on a colt, just as Zechariah said: a King who saves, riding toward His cross on purpose.
  2. Fruit“He found nothing but leaves” 11:13Leaves are easy. He's looking for fruit, and fruit grows from staying close to Him.
  3. Prayer“My house shall be called of all nations the house of prayer” 11:17God's house is a place to meet Him honestly, open to everyone, not a den to hide in.
  4. Faith“Have faith in God” 11:22Not faith in faith. Trust in the God who moves mountains we can't.
  5. Forgive“When ye stand praying, forgive” 11:25Forgiven men forgive. Hand the debt to God and stop collecting.
  6. Authority“We cannot tell” 11:33They asked what was safe, not what was true. Honest seekers find Him.

Question 1 of 6

The King

How did Jesus ride into Jerusalem, and what did the people cry?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On a young colt no one had ever ridden, over garments and branches spread in the road. The people cried “Hosanna” (save now), “Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord.” He came lowly, as Zechariah said the King would.

Question 2 of 6

Fruit

What did Jesus find on the fig tree?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Nothing but leaves. It looked fruitful from a distance, like the busy temple with no room for prayer, and the next morning it was dried up from the roots. He looks for real fruit, and fruit grows when we abide in Him.

Question 3 of 6

Prayer

What did Jesus say God's house should be called?

What verses tell us about this?

“Is this house, which is called by my name, become a den of robbers in your eyes? Behold, even I have seen it, saith the LORD.”

Jeremiah 7:11

Answer

“The house of prayer” for all nations. They had made it a den of thieves, a market in the one court where outsiders could pray, and a hideout for people who wanted religion without change. Jesus cleared it out.

Question 4 of 6

Faith

What did Jesus tell His men when they saw the fig tree withered?

What verses tell us about this?

“And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us:”

1 John 5:14

Answer

“Have faith in God.” Pray believing, because God can move what no man can move. The power isn't in our words or in our faith. It's in God, and we ask according to His will.

Question 5 of 6

Forgive

What must we do when we stand praying?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Answer

Forgive anyone we hold anything against. We've been forgiven far more than anyone owes us, and a grudge gets between us and the Father. Forgiving doesn't excuse the wrong. It hands the debt to God.

Question 6 of 6

Authority

Why wouldn't the leaders say where John's baptism came from?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Either answer would cost them. “From heaven” meant admitting they hadn't believed John, who pointed to Jesus. “Of men” would turn the people against them. They wanted what was safe, not what was true.
