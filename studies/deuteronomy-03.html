Deuteronomy 3

Og falls, the land east of Jordan is given, and “Let it suffice thee”

The king their fathers would have run from falls to their sons. Moses gives out the land east of Jordan, charges Joshua not to fear, and asks the LORD one last time to let him go over. The answer is no, and Moses is told to strengthen the man who will go.

Part one

“Fear him not”

Verses 1–11

Moses tells the sons how the last giant fell.

“Went up the way to Bashan”

Sihon of Heshbon had fallen (2:33). Israel turned and went up the way to Bashan, the country up to mount Hermon. Og came out to meet them with all his people.

We saw this battle in Numbers 21. What is new here is who is listening. Moses is telling it to the sons of the men who said at Kadesh, “we were in our own sight as grasshoppers” (Numbers 13:33).

The line shows the direction the text gives, “up the way to Bashan”. Edrei, where they fought, the text does not place, so the map does not either.

Said to the fathers, told to the sons

Their fathers at Kadesh

“walled up to heaven”

“The people is greater and taller than we; the cities are great and walled up to heaven; and moreover we have seen the sons of the Anakims there” (1:28). So they would not go up.

The sons, before Bashan

“Fear him not”

The same kind of enemy, a giant king with walled cities, and the LORD said three words before the fight. “So the LORD our God delivered into our hands Og also” (verse 3).

The giants had not got smaller in thirty-eight years. What changed was a generation that went up when God said go. The thing that beat your father, or beat you last year, is not bigger than the LORD's word.

Threescore walled cities

one for every fenced city

Threescore is sixty. Fenced means fortified: “high walls, gates, and bars”, “beside unwalled towns a great many”. This is exactly what scared the fathers at Kadesh, the cities “walled up to heaven”, sixty of them in one region, Argob, and “there was not a city which we took not from them”.

The walls were real. They were just not the last word.

“Utterly destroyed”

Verse 6 is hard to read, and it should be. Men, women and children of every city. It was the LORD's judgment on the Amorites, whose iniquity he had waited on until it was full (Genesis 15:16, which we saw in chapter 2). Moses will say it plainly later:

“Not for thy righteousness, or for the uprightness of thine heart, dost thou go to possess their land: but for the wickedness of these nations the LORD thy God doth drive them out from before thee, and that he may perform the word which the LORD sware unto thy fathers, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.”

Deuteronomy 9:5

This was God's command for that land, at that time; it is not a pattern for any man or nation now. What it shows us is that God's patience is long and his judgment is real, and that Israel was not better than the men they replaced.

One mountain, many names

HermonIsrael's name (3:8)
Sirionwhat the Sidonians call it (3:9)
Shenirwhat the Amorites call it (3:9)
Sion“mount Sion, which is Hermon” (4:48)

Moses stops to say what the neighbours call it, the way a man explains a place to people who will live next to it. The Sidonians are the men of Sidon on the coast. The Sion of 4:48 is this same Hermon, as the verse itself says, not mount Zion in Jerusalem.

“From the river of Arnon unto mount Hermon”: the whole east side, from the river in the south to the high mountain in the north. Two kings had held it. Now it was Israel's.

“Is it not in Rabbath?”

“is it not in Rabbath of the children of Ammon?”

We measured Og's iron bed in Numbers 21: nine cubits by four, “after the cubit of a man”, the ordinary cubit, about a man's forearm. A bedstead is the frame of a bed. What Moses adds here is a question: go and look. The bed was in Rabbath, the city of the children of Ammon, and anyone could see it.

The Bible tells history that could be checked by the men who first heard it. And Og, the one who “remained of the remnant of giants”, fell to a people who believed God.

Part two

“This land… gave I”

Verses 12–22

The land is shared out, and the men who got theirs first must still go and fight.

North to south: who got what

  1. mount Hermonthe north end (verse 8)
  2. All Bashan“the kingdom of Og… all the region of Argob”, to the half tribe of Manasseh (13). Jair took Argob and named its towns after himself (14).
  3. The rest of Gileadto the half tribe of Manasseh; “I gave Gilead unto Machir” (13, 15)
  4. the river Jabbok“the border of the children of Ammon” (16)
  5. Half mount Gilead, down to the Arnonto the Reubenites and the Gadites, with the Jordan valley “from Chinnereth even unto… the salt sea” (12, 16–17)
  6. Aroer, by the river Arnonthe south end (12)

We saw the request for this land in Numbers 32. Moses now tells the new generation it is done: “gave I” comes again and again. Machir was Manasseh's son, and his family, with Jair of the same tribe, took the north.

Then go“pass over armed before your brethren… Until the LORD have given rest unto your brethren, as well as unto you”

The men who were settled first went over first, in front. They did it: years later Joshua told them, “Ye have not left your brethren these many days unto this day” (Joshua 22:3).

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

If God has given you some rest, a few clean months, a job, a home again, you are not finished. There is a brother still fighting for his. Go over with him.

What your eyes have seen

Moses does not give Joshua a speech about courage. He points him back: you saw Sihon fall, you saw Og fall. So shall the LORD do to the kingdoms across the river. Fear goes when a man remembers what God has already done.

“What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us?”

Romans 8:31

Moses says “at that time” six times in this chapter (verses 4, 8, 12, 18, 21, 23). He is a man pinning down the days God came through, so the next man can lean on them.

Write yours down. The day you did not pick it up. The night God got you home. Those are your two kings. Read them on the bad day.

Part three

“Let it suffice thee”

Verses 23–29

Moses asks once more to go over, and the LORD says no.

How Moses prayed

  1. 24“thou hast begun”what God has shewn him
  2. 24“what God is there…?”none like him
  3. 25“let me go over”then the request

He besought the LORD: he begged him. But he starts with God, not with himself. And listen to a man of “an hundred and twenty years old” (31:2): “thou hast begun to shew thy servant thy greatness.” After the plagues, the sea, the mountain and forty years of manna, Moses thinks he has only seen the beginning.

Then he asks for what he wants, plainly: the good land, “that goodly mountain, and Lebanon”. A man may ask God for what his heart wants.

A no that stood

We saw why in Numbers 20: at the rock, “ye believed me not, to sanctify me” (Numbers 20:12). Moses says it three times in these chapters: “the LORD was angry with me for your sakes” (1:37; 4:21), “wroth with me for your sakes” (3:26). The people had provoked him, and he had sinned. Both are true.

“Let it suffice thee”

Suffice: let it be enough. Moses asked, and God answered, and the answer was no, and stop asking. Paul's thorn was not a sentence for sin, as Moses' was; but he too asked, heard no, and was given grace enough:

“For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me. And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.”

2 Corinthians 12:8–9

“Lift up thine eyes”

The no came with a gift. Go up to the top of Pisgah and look every way. Moses would see it all (34:1–4). It is how the land was first shown, to Abram:

“And the LORD said unto Abram, after that Lot was separated from him, Lift up now thine eyes, and look from the place where thou art northward, and southward, and eastward, and westward: For all the land which thou seest, to thee will I give it, and to thy seed for ever.”

Genesis 13:14–15

And the story did not end on Pisgah. Centuries later Moses stood in the land after all, on the mount with Christ: “there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him” (Matthew 17:3). We saw that in Numbers 20.

Charge him, encourage him, strengthen him

The LORD gave Moses a job in place of his wish: build up the man who would go where he could not. And Moses did it, in front of everyone:

“And Moses called unto Joshua, and said unto him in the sight of all Israel, Be strong and of a good courage: for thou must go with this people unto the land which the LORD hath sworn unto their fathers to give them; and thou shalt cause them to inherit it.”

Deuteronomy 31:7

“Wherefore comfort yourselves together, and edify one another, even as also ye do.”

1 Thessalonians 5:11

No bitterness, no sulking. There are places your past may keep you from: a job, a home, a marriage you lost. You can still strengthen the younger man who is going.

They abode, stayed, in the valley over against Beth-peor, near where the men who followed Baal-peor had fallen (4:3; Numbers 25). In a valley there, over against Beth-peor, the LORD would bury Moses, “but no man knoweth of his sepulchre unto this day” (34:6).

Real world: what your eyes have seen

  • SinMoses' sin at the rock still cost him the land. Sin has real costs, even for the best man, even forgiven.
  • RegretMoses did not hide it. He told the young men plainly what it cost him, so they would not pay the same.
  • GraceGod answered the man he said no to: Pisgah, the whole land seen, and one day the mount with Christ.
  • HopeThe giants your fathers ran from are not bigger than God's word. “Fear him not.”
  • TodayWrite down two things the LORD has already done for you, and tell one younger man in the faith. Charge him, encourage him, strengthen him.

Question 1 of 6

“Fear him not”

What did the LORD tell Moses when Og came out against Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Fear him not”: the LORD would deliver Og, his people and his land into Israel's hand, as he had done with Sihon. And he did (verse 3).

Question 2 of 6

Walls up to heaven

What kind of cities did Israel take in Bashan?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Sixty fortified cities in the region of Argob, with high walls, gates and bars, besides many unwalled towns: the very kind of city that had frightened their fathers.

Question 3 of 6

The brothers

What did Moses command Reuben, Gad and half Manasseh to do before they settled?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To leave their wives, little ones and cattle in their cities, and go over armed before their brethren until the LORD gave them rest too; then they could return to their own land.

Question 4 of 6

What your eyes have seen

What did Moses tell Joshua, and why should he not fear?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That he had seen what the LORD did to Sihon and Og, and the LORD would do the same to every kingdom ahead. He need not fear, because the LORD would fight for them.

Question 5 of 6

A prayer, and a no

What did Moses ask the LORD for, and what was the answer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To go over Jordan and see the good land. The LORD would not hear him: “Let it suffice thee.” He might see it from the top of Pisgah, but he would not go over.

Question 6 of 6

Strengthen him

What did the LORD tell Moses to do for Joshua?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Moses called unto Joshua, and said unto him in the sight of all Israel, Be strong and of a good courage: for thou must go with this people unto the land which the LORD hath sworn unto their fathers to give them; and thou shalt cause them to inherit it.”

Deuteronomy 31:7

Answer

To charge him, encourage him and strengthen him, because Joshua would go over before the people and cause them to inherit the land. Moses did it in the sight of all Israel.
