What's a “surety”?

A surety is a guarantor: the man who promises to pay if the borrower doesn't. Today we call it co-signing. “Stricken thy hand” was the handshake that sealed the deal.

Your friend's debt just became yours, and your own words put you there (v. 2). Proverbs warns about this again: “He that is surety for a stranger shall smart for it” (Proverbs 11:15).

Don't sleep on it

The father's advice is urgent: don't go to bed until you've dealt with it. Swallow your pride, go to your friend, and press him hard to get you off the hook. Run like a deer from a hunter.

This isn't only about money. It's about any promise that has you trapped: a bad deal, a loan to a buddy who's still using, a commitment you never should have made. Deal with it now. It only gets worse.

Proverbs isn't against being generous. Give what you can afford to give. Just don't sign your name to a debt you can't cover. “If thou hast nothing to pay, why should he take away thy bed from under thee?” (Proverbs 22:27).

“By so much was Jesus made a surety of a better testament.”

Hebrews 7:22

The one Surety who never got trapped

A man as surety

Snared

He promises more than he can pay. When the debt comes due, he's caught.

Jesus as surety

Paid in full

He put His name on a debt that wasn't His: ours. Then He paid every cent of it on the cross.

We owed God a debt of sin we could never pay. Jesus stood surety for us, and He had the means to cover it. Nobody who trusts Him is left holding the bill.

Part two

Get up

Verses 6–11

The smallest worker on earth has a sermon for the man on the couch.

Lessons from an ant

A “sluggard” is a lazy man, the guy who always means to get to it. The ant has no boss standing over her. She just works, and works ahead: storing up in summer for a winter she can't see yet.

In recovery, a lot of us are learning to show up without anyone making us. Nobody has to tell the ant twice.

Just a little more sleep

a little sleep a little slumber a little folding of the hands

Nobody plans to be broke. It comes one “little” at a time, like a traveler walking steadily toward you, then all at once, like an armed man at the door.

Being sick or hurt isn't being lazy. But when the only thing stopping us is that we don't feel like it, the Bible is blunt: “if any would not work, neither should he eat” (2 Thessalonians 3:10).

Part three

What God hates

Verses 12–19

The troublemaker, from his eyes down to his feet.

Spotting a troublemaker

“Naughty” here isn't a kid word. The Hebrew says a man of belial: worthless, good for nothing. You've met him. He's always got an angle.

  • His eyeswinks to signal the con
  • His feettaps out a message under the table
  • His fingerspoints and signals on the sly
  • His heartthe real source: twisted, always scheming

He says one thing out loud and another with his body. And he loves stirring people up against each other. Verse 15: his end comes suddenly, “without remedy.”

Six, yea, seven

“Six… yea, seven” is a Hebrew way of saying: pay attention to the last one. Five body parts, then 6 a false witness, and 7 the man who stirs up trouble between brothers.

The troublemaker of verses 12–14 is all seven. And number one isn't murder. It's pride.

But God “giveth grace unto the humble” (James 4:6).

Part four

Stay out of the fire

Verses 20–35

A lamp for the road, and a warning about the fire.

With you all day long

When thou goestit shall lead thee: wisdom for the decisions of the day
When thou sleepestit shall keep thee: guarding you in the hours you're weakest
When thou awakestit shall talk with thee: the first voice of the morning

“Bind them… tie them about thy neck” (v. 21) means keep it on you, like something you wear every day. Get the Word into your head so it goes where you go. “Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path” (Psalm 119:105).

It starts in the heart and the eyes

The father goes upstream, to where it starts: the heart, and the eyes. Jesus said the same thing: “whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery with her already in his heart” (Matthew 5:28).

That's why what we let our eyes rest on matters so much, on the street and on a screen. Being tempted isn't sin. Feeding the look is where it turns.

Verse 26 adds up the cost. A prostitute can bring a man “to a piece of bread,” down to his last loaf. Another man's wife hunts for something worth even more: “the precious life.” Your life itself.

Fire in your lap

Your “bosom” is your chest, the fold of your robe where a man carried things close. Nobody tucks a burning coal in there and walks away fine.

Every man who's played with this thinks he'll be the exception. He won't. Verse 29: whoever goes in to his neighbor's wife “shall not be innocent.” Fire does what fire does.

A thief can pay it back

The hungry thief (vv. 30–31)

7 times over

People understand why he did it. He still pays, even if it costs everything in his house. But it can be paid.

The adulterer (vv. 32–35)

no price

A wound, dishonor, and a husband's rage that won't take any amount of money. You can't buy back what you broke.

That's the honest truth about consequences. Some things can't be undone by men, even after God forgives. That's exactly why the time to stop is before the fire.

What men can't wipe away, God can

Proverbs 6:33

A wound and dishonour shall he get; and his reproach shall not be wiped away.

1 John 1:9

If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

People may remember it for a long time. Trust can take years to rebuild, and some of it may never come back. But before God, a man who confesses his sin is forgiven and cleansed, completely. That's where a fallen man starts over: not with a better reputation, but with the cross.

Question 1 of 6

Promises and debts

What should a man do when his own words have trapped him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Deal with it right away. Swallow your pride, go to the person, and work hard to get free. Don't sleep on it. Whether it's a debt you co-signed or a promise you never should have made, it only gets worse the longer you wait.

Question 2 of 6

Jesus our surety

Who paid the debt we couldn't pay?

What verses tell us about this?

“By so much was Jesus made a surety of a better testament.”

Hebrews 7:22

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Answer

Jesus. A man who stands surety for a friend often ends up trapped. Jesus stood surety for us, put His name on a debt of sin that was ours, and had what it took to pay it in full. For everyone who trusts Him, there's no bill left.

Question 3 of 6

Work

What can a lazy man learn from an ant?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The ant works without anybody pushing her, and she works ahead, storing up in summer for the winter. The sluggard keeps saying “a little more,” and poverty walks up on him one little bit at a time. Get up, show up, and do the next right thing.

Question 4 of 6

What God hates

What are the seven things the LORD hates?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A proud look, a lying tongue, hands that shed innocent blood, a heart that schemes evil, feet quick to run into trouble, a false witness, and a man who stirs up trouble between brothers. Pride tops the list. God hates these because they wreck people, but He gives grace to the humble.

Question 5 of 6

God's Word

How does God's Word help us all day long?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It leads us when we're on the move, keeps us when we're asleep and weakest, and talks with us when we wake up. It's a lamp that shows the next step. Keep it on you, in your head and heart, so it goes wherever you go.

Question 6 of 6

Lust

Can a man play with fire and not get burned?

What verses tell us about this?

“Flee fornication. Every sin that a man doeth is without the body; but he that committeth fornication sinneth against his own body.”

1 Corinthians 6:18

Answer

No. Fire does what fire does. Every man thinks he'll be the exception, and none of them are. So fight it upstream, at the heart and the eyes, and run from it like Joseph did. If you've already been burned, confess it. God forgives and cleanses completely.
