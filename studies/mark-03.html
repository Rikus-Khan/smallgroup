Part one

Stretch forth thine hand

Verses 1–6

A man with a dead hand, a room full of watching eyes, and the question nobody would answer.

A trap set in church

“Again” takes us back to the synagogue at Capernaum (1:21), Jesus' home base by the lake. In the last chapter the Pharisees had already caught His disciples picking grain on the sabbath (2:23–24). Now they come to watch Him.

ifHe heals him on the sabbath
→
then“they might accuse him”

God's law said to rest on the seventh day (Exodus 20:10). The teachers had piled their own rules on top, and by those rules healing was work, allowed only if a life was in danger. A withered hand could wait until tomorrow.

Notice what they came for. Not to worship, not to learn, not to see a man helped. They came to catch Jesus doing something they could use.

Two different questions

They asked

Is it allowed?

Which rule might He break? They had a list, and the man in front of them was only a test case.

Jesus asked

Is it good?

“To do good… or to do evil? to save life, or to kill?” Doing nothing for this man was not neutral.

“They held their peace.” They went quiet because any honest answer would have sunk them.

Mark is the one who tells us Jesus looked at them “with anger, being grieved.” His anger came mixed with grief. It wasn't wounded pride. It was sorrow over hard hearts that cared more about a rule than a man. Most of our anger is about us. His was about them.

He did the thing he couldn't do

“Stand forth”in front of everyone“Stretch forth thine hand”the one thing he couldn't do“whole as the other”

First, “Stand forth.” Step out in front of the whole room, with the hand he'd probably kept hidden for years. Admitting where we're broken, out loud, in front of other men, is where a lot of healing starts.

Then, “Stretch forth thine hand.” That was the very thing his hand couldn't do. But Jesus never gives a command without giving the power to obey it. The man didn't wait until he felt healed. He obeyed, and the healing came while he did.

Strange allies

The Pharisees

Strict keepers of the law and their traditions. They hated Roman rule and anyone who cozied up to it.

vs

The Herodians

Men who backed Herod Antipas, the ruler Rome had set over Galilee. Politics, not Scripture, was their concern.

“against him”

Normally these two groups couldn't stand each other. Jesus gave them something in common.

And see what they did with their sabbath. Healing a man was a sin to them, but a plot to kill the healer was fine. When a man's religion is about control, it can make room for almost anything.

Where the plot was headed

Mark 3:6 is the first time Mark tells us anyone wanted Jesus dead. The same plan runs all the way through the book:

  1. Mark 3:6Galilee“how they might destroy him”
  2. Mark 11:18Jerusalem“sought how they might destroy him”
  3. Mark 14:1two days before the passover“take him by craft”
  4. Mark 15:1the morning of the cross“delivered him to Pilate”

Jesus knew it from this day on. He kept healing, kept teaching, and kept walking toward Jerusalem anyway. Nobody took His life from Him by surprise. He laid it down for us.

Part two

That they should be with him

Verses 7–19

People pour in from every direction. Jesus goes up a mountain and calls twelve men.

From every direction

Judaea and Jerusalem, days to the south. Idumaea, old Edom, past Judaea, over a hundred miles away. The land beyond Jordan. Tyre and Sidon, Gentile ports up the coast.

They came on foot, many of them sick, because they had “heard what great things he did.” Word about Jesus still travels that way: one man telling another what He did.

The devils knew Him

Jesus kept a small ship ready so the crowd wouldn't throng, crush, Him. “Plagues” are painful diseases; the Greek word means a whip.

The unclean spirits

knew exactly who He was

They fell down and cried, “Thou art the Son of God.”

The religious leaders

wanted Him dead

They had the Scriptures, and missed Him completely (v. 6).

“Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.”

James 2:19

Knowing facts about Jesus isn't saving faith. The devils know them and tremble. Faith is trusting Him. And Jesus “straitly charged” them, strictly ordered them, to keep quiet. He would not take His introduction from devils.

What the twelve were for

“Ordained” here means appointed, made. No ceremony, just Jesus naming twelve men for a job. Twelve, like the twelve tribes of Israel. He was building something new.

  1. “be with him”first
  2. “send them forth to preach”then go
  3. “power to heal… and to cast out devils”in His strength, not theirs

Look at the order. Before they preached a word or healed anybody, they were just to be with Him. Walk with Him, eat with Him, listen to Him. You can't give away what you haven't got. Time with Jesus comes first, and everything else grows out of it.

The twelve

  2. Jamesson of Zebedee, a fisherman
  3. Johnhis brother, also a fisherman
  4. AndrewPeter's brother, a fisherman
  5. Philipfrom Bethsaida (John 1:44)
  6. Bartholomewprobably the Nathanael of John 1
  7. Matthewa tax collector, called Levi in 2:14
  8. Thomasthe one who doubted, then worshipped
  9. Jamesson of Alphaeus
  10. Thaddaeusalso called Judas, not Iscariot
  11. Simon“the Canaanite,” the Zealot (Luke 6:15)
  12. Judas Iscariot“which also betrayed him”

Fishermen, a tax collector who worked for Rome, and a Zealot from the movement that wanted Rome thrown out by force. Matthew and Simon would once have been enemies. Jesus put them at the same table.

These weren't polished men. Most of them would run the night He was arrested. He chose them anyway, and He shaped them by keeping them close.

Sons of thunder

James and John → Boanerges an Aramaic nickname: “The sons of thunder”

“And when his disciples James and John saw this, they said, Lord, wilt thou that we command fire to come down from heaven, and consume them, even as Elias did?”

Luke 9:54

That's the kind of men they were: loud, quick-tempered, ready to burn a village down. Jesus named them honestly and kept them anyway. Years later, John wrote more about love than any other writer in the Bible. Jesus doesn't throw a hot-tempered man away. He changes him.

Part three

A house divided

Verses 20–30

His family says He's lost His mind. The scribes say He's working for the devil.

“He's lost His mind”

“His friends” means His own people, His family. We'll meet them at the door in verse 31. “Beside himself” means out of his mind. They came to take Him home by force.

His familyHe is beside himself.

The scribes (v. 22)He hath Beelzebub.

Even His brothers didn't believe in Him yet (John 7:5). When a man gets saved and gets serious, the people who knew him before don't always cheer either. Some think he's gone off the deep end. Some liked him better the old way. Jesus knows exactly how that feels. His own family thought He was crazy, and He loved them anyway.

Beelzebub

Beelzebub from Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron (2 Kings 1:2) “lord of the flies”

By Jesus' day it had become a name for the prince of the devils, Satan himself. These scribes, the experts in the law, had come all the way “down from Jerusalem” to look Him over. They couldn't deny the devils were being cast out. Everyone had seen it. So they gave it the worst explanation they could think of.

It doesn't add up

A parable is a picture story with a point. Jesus answers the slander with plain common sense:

ifa kingdom fights itself
→
then“that kingdom cannot stand”
ifSatan casts out his own
→
thenhe “hath an end”

Satan isn't dumb enough to tear down his own work. If the devils are being driven out, Someone stronger than the devil is doing it.

Stronger than the strong man

The strong man is Satan. His goods are the people he holds. To spoil is to plunder.

Every devil Jesus cast out was proof that “a stronger than he” had come and tied him up (Luke 11:22).

Then He carries off the goods. Whatever has had you by the throat, a bottle, a pipe, a rage, is not stronger than Jesus.

All sins

Before the warning comes the promise. “Verily” means truly, and what Jesus says is truly true: all sins can be forgiven.

Every sin of the sons of men

  • What I did drunk or highowed
  • What I did to my familyowed
  • Every time I cursed Godowed
  • The thing I've never told anyoneowed
Can it be forgiven?“All sins”
FORGIVENin Christ

Even “blasphemies wherewith soever they shall blaspheme.” Even the words you said against God at your worst. There is nothing you've done that the blood of Jesus can't cover.

The one sin that has no forgiveness

  • a sin of the flesh, however bad“all sins shall be forgiven”
  • words said against God in angerblasphemies are forgiven too (v. 28)
  • doubting, struggling, falling backthat's a man still in the fight
  • calling the Spirit's work the devil's, and turning Him away for good“because they said, He hath an unclean spirit”

The Holy Ghost is the One who brings men to Jesus. These scribes saw His work in plain sight and called it Satan's. Shut Him out for good, and you've shut the only door there is. It isn't that God won't forgive. It's that the man won't come.

If you're afraid you've done it, you haven't. A hard heart doesn't worry about God. That worry is the Spirit still drawing you. Come: “him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out” (John 6:37).

Part four

Behold my mother and my brethren

Verses 31–35

His family stands outside. Jesus looks around the room and names a new one.

A new family

His mother and brothers stood outside. Inside, Jesus looked round about at the faces sitting at His feet.

He wasn't rejecting His mother. On the cross He saw she was cared for (John 19:26–27). But there's a family deeper than blood: “whosoever shall do the will of God.”

And the door stayed open. After He rose, His mother and brothers were praying with the disciples (Acts 1:14).

Whosoever

Not blood. Whosoever.

What is the will of God, to start with? Jesus said it Himself: “that every one which seeth the Son, and believeth on him, may have everlasting life” (John 6:40). It starts with trusting Him. Then the man who has trusted Him begins to walk in His ways.

Some of us burned the bridges to our own families. Some families burned them for us. Jesus doesn't leave a believer without one. The men in this group are your brothers, by His word.

“For both he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one: for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brethren,”

Hebrews 2:11

Points to remember

  1. Mercy“Is it lawful to do good on the sabbath days” 3:4Doing good is never against God's law. A rule that won't help a hurting man has missed God's heart.
  2. Obeying“Stretch forth thine hand” 3:5He asked him to do what he couldn't do. The power came as he obeyed.
  3. Called“That they should be with him” 3:14Being with Jesus comes before working for Him.
  4. Power“Except he will first bind the strong man” 3:27Jesus is stronger than whatever has held you, and He sets the captives free.
  5. Forgiveness“All sins shall be forgiven unto the sons of men” 3:28Any sin can be forgiven. Only turning away the Spirit who brings us to Christ shuts the door.
  6. Family“Whosoever shall do the will of God, the same is my brother” 3:35Whosoever trusts Him and follows Him is family. No believer is left without one.

Question 1 of 6

Mercy

What does Jesus say is lawful on the sabbath?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To do good and to save life. The sabbath was God's gift of rest, not a reason to leave a man suffering. The Pharisees cared more about their rules than the man in front of them. Jesus was grieved by their hard hearts, and healed him.

Question 2 of 6

Obeying

What did the man with the withered hand have to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Stand up in front of everyone, and stretch out the hand he couldn't move. He didn't heal himself. Jesus did that. But he trusted Jesus enough to obey, and the healing came as he did.

Question 3 of 6

Called

Why did Jesus choose the twelve?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

First, to be with Him. Then to be sent out to preach, with His power to heal and cast out devils. They were ordinary, rough men, fishermen, a tax collector, a Zealot. Jesus made them His by keeping them close.

Question 4 of 6

Power

Who is the strong man, and who binds him?

What verses tell us about this?

“But when a stronger than he shall come upon him, and overcome him, he taketh from him all his armour wherein he trusted, and divideth his spoils.”

Luke 11:22

Answer

The strong man is Satan, and his goods are the people he holds. Jesus is the stronger One. He binds the devil and carries off the captives. Nothing that has a grip on you is stronger than Him.

Question 5 of 6

Forgiveness

Is there any sin God won't forgive?

What verses tell us about this?

“All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.”

John 6:37

Answer

All sins can be forgiven, even blasphemies. The one sin without forgiveness is setting yourself against the Holy Ghost, calling His work in Jesus the devil's, and refusing the only One who can bring you to Christ. Any man who comes to Jesus will be received.

Question 6 of 6

Family

Who does Jesus call His family?

What verses tell us about this?

“These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brethren.”

Acts 1:14

Answer

Whosoever does the will of God, and that starts with believing on His Son. Jesus didn't reject His mother. He opened His family to everyone. Later His mother and brothers were in that circle too.
