Genesis 26

Like father, like son

A famine, the promise renewed, and the same lie his father told. Then a man who stops fighting over wells finds that the LORD has made room for him, and the men who sent him away come asking for peace.

Part one

Not Egypt

Verses 1–6

Hungry times, and a road that was closed.

The road to Egypt

When food ran short, Egypt was where you went. It had the Nile, and it nearly always had grain. Isaac had stopped at Gerar, on the road south.

His father went down to Egypt in a famine (12:10), and lied there to save his own skin. The LORD stops Isaac before he goes. “Go not down into Egypt.”

God does not take the famine away. He tells Isaac where to stand in it: "stay, and I will be with thee."

The promise is handed on

  • Abrahamthe promise given
    • Ishmael
    • Isaacthe promise renewed, here
      • Esau
      • Jacobnext, in chapter 27

God says to Isaac almost word for word what He said to Abraham: the land, the stars, and “in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed.” Isaac does not get a new plan. He gets the old promise, handed on.

The one Seed

“Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.”

Galatians 3:16

The New Testament says that the one Seed through whom every nation is blessed is Christ. Every family story from here on is the road that leads to Him.

Abraham obeyed: and what it meant

Verse 5 gives the reason: “Because that Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws.” Is God saying Abraham earned the promise?

“And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness.”

Genesis 15:6

No. Long before Abraham obeyed anything, he believed, and God counted it to him as righteousness. The obeying grew out of the believing. Verse 5 is God pointing at a faith that showed itself, not a wage that was paid.

That is good news for a man who has made a mess of things. God does not wait for you to be good before He promises you something. He asks you to believe Him, and then to walk with Him.

Part two

The same lie

Verses 7–11

A man who has just been told "I will be with thee" is afraid for his life.

Three times the same lie, in the same town

  1. Genesis 12Abraham, in Egypt“Say, I pray thee, thou art my sister”
  2. Genesis 20Abraham, in Gerar“She is my sister”
  3. Genesis 26Isaac, in Gerar“She is my sister”

Isaac must have heard the story at home. And look: the very same town, and a king with the same name, Abimelech. Isaac walks into his father's mistake, a generation later, and says the same words.

Name it plainly: sin runs in families. A father's weak spot often becomes a son's. If you grew up watching someone lie, drink, rage or run, you may find his habit waiting in you. It is not an excuse, but it is worth knowing. You can be the one in the family who stops it.

Fear forgets, quickly

What the LORD had said (v. 3)

“I will be with thee”

Only a little earlier, God had promised to be with him and to bless him.

What Isaac said (v. 7)

“Lest I die for her”

He was afraid they would kill him for his wife. So he gave away the truth, and put Rebekah in danger.

Fear of men can make a man trade away his wife, his word, and his honor. The LORD had already answered the fear, and Isaac did not use the answer.

What do you say when you are scared: "I'm fine," "I wasn't there," "it was only one"? It rarely protects you. It puts someone else at risk.

Caught by a man who does not know God

AbimelechWhat is this thou hast done unto us? One of my people could easily have slept with your wife, and you would have brought guilt on us all.

IsaacBecause I said, Lest I die for her.

Isaac was told off by a Philistine king who does not worship the LORD. It is humbling to be corrected by someone you thought stood further from God than you.

Then Abimelech orders that no one touches Isaac or his wife. God protected Isaac even through the mess Isaac made. That is grace, and it is the pattern of this chapter.

Part three

Blessed, envied, and the wells

Verses 12–22

He lied, and the LORD blessed him anyway. Then the trouble started.

An hundredfold, in a famine

1 yearof famine in the land
1 seedsown in that land
100back, the same year

Right after the lie, the Bible does not say "and God took it all away." It says “and the LORD blessed him.” In a hungry year, on dry ground, Isaac sowed and reaped a hundred times over.

That is not because Isaac was good. It is because the LORD had said "I will bless thee" (v. 3) and kept His word. Isaac stayed where God told him, and he planted. He did the next ordinary thing. God did the rest.

Careful, though: this is not a promise that every faithful man will get rich. It is the LORD keeping a particular promise to Isaac. What every believer can count on is the line that came before it: “I will be with thee.”

Four steps from blessed to sent away

  1. Blessed“the LORD blessed him”
  2. Envied“the Philistines envied him”
  3. Cut off“they had stopped them, and filled them with earth”
  4. Sent away“Go from us; for thou art much mightier than we”

The wells were the family's lifeline, dug by Abraham's servants. The Philistines filled them with earth. In dry country, that is a way of saying "we would rather you died."

Envy is an old and ugly thing. It is what the Philistines felt toward Isaac, and sometimes it is what we feel toward someone who seems to be doing better. It will fill wells with dirt, and it never gives us a drink.

Digging his father's wells again

Isaac did not dig new wells first. He went back and cleared out his father's wells, and gave them the same names. In a way, he reopened what God had already done in the family.

It is a picture for any man who has a father, a church, or a faith that was buried under dirt: you do not have to invent something new. You can go back to the old well, clear out what was thrown in, and drink.

And then, in the valley, “a well of springing water”: a spring that flowed on its own. Jesus later says, to a woman at a well, that He gives living water, a spring that does not run dry (John 4:14).

Esek, Sitnah, Rehoboth

Two wells named for fights. Sitnah comes from the same root as the word Satan, "the adversary". The third well was named for what the LORD gave: room.

Isaac named each well for what happened there. The first two held quarrels. The third held room, and he gave the LORD the credit: “For now the LORD hath made room for us.”

He did not fight. He moved.

What he could have done

Fight for it

He was "much mightier" than the Philistines, and had the men and the herds. He could have won the argument, and lost the peace.

What he did

Give way, dig again

He let two wells go, kept digging, and found a third that nobody fought over.

“If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.”

Romans 12:18

This is not weakness, and it is not a rule that you must give in to everything. It is a man who knows which fights are worth it. Some arguments over a well are just a way to lose a day, a friend, or your temper. A man who is learning to walk away from a fight can see himself in Isaac.

Part four

Beer-sheba

Verses 23–33

The night God said "fear not," and the morning the enemies came to make peace.

Up to Beer-sheba

He moved about fifteen miles inland, from the valley of Gerar to Beer-sheba. It was where Abraham had dug a well and called on the LORD (21:31–33).

And that same night, “the LORD appeared unto him.” Not at the harvest, not at the first well. At the end of a long run of quarrels, and in the dark.

The LORD speaks to Isaac's fear: “fear not, for I am with thee.” He knew what was in Isaac, and He gave him the very words he needed.

“For my servant Abraham's sake”

Why will the LORD bless and multiply Isaac? Not for Isaac's sake: he had just lied about his wife. “For my servant Abraham's sake.” Isaac stands on another man's faith.

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

If a promise could be kept for Abraham's sake, how much more for Christ's sake? A believer is not forgiven because of how his week went. He is forgiven for the sake of the One who paid for it all. That is why a fall does not undo your salvation.

Notice what Isaac does with the word: he builds an altar, calls on the LORD, pitches his tent, and his servants dig a well. He worships first, and then he gets to work. When God has spoken to your fear, the next thing to do is to give Him thanks and take up the ordinary job in front of you.

The men who sent him away

IsaacWhy have you come to me, seeing you hate me and sent me away from you?

Abimelech, Ahuzzath, PhicholWe saw clearly that the LORD was with you. Let there be an oath between us.

Isaac asks the honest question. They do not say "we were wrong." They say they saw that the LORD was with him. Even men who did not love Isaac could see it.

“When a man’s ways please the LORD, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace with him.”

Proverbs 16:7

Three men, and an old feud

There were three of them: Abimelech the king, Ahuzzath, one of his friends (a trusted adviser), and Phichol, the chief captain of his army. Phichol is also the army chief in Abraham's day (21:22), so this may be a title, or a family of officers; the text does not say.

Isaac could have thrown it back in their faces. Think who is on the other side of your old fights: a boss, an ex, a neighbour, a brother who did you wrong. When the time comes, you may be asked to sit down and eat with someone who treated you badly.

Shebah: “seven,” and “oath”

שִׁבְעָה said Shiv‘ah “seven” (and it sounds like “oath”)

Beer-sheba means “well of the oath” or “well of seven”: the Hebrew sounds alike, and both fit. In Abraham's day it was named for an oath of peace with a Philistine king (21:31). Now Isaac swears peace with the same people, and the same day, his servants find water.

The order is worth noticing. First a feast, a night's sleep, and a promise of peace. Then, “the same day,” water. Isaac did not have to buy peace with a well. Peace and water came together.

“Unto this day” means the name was still in use when Moses wrote.

Part five

A grief of mind

Verses 34–35

The chapter ends with a quiet sorrow at home.

“A grief of mind”: a bitterness of spirit, a sorrow they carried.

The Hittites were one of the peoples of Canaan, who did not worship the LORD. Abraham had taken great care that Isaac should not marry among them (24:3). Esau, at forty, did what his father had done at forty. But Isaac had waited for a wife to be found; Esau simply took two.

The point is not the nationality. It is that Esau, the firstborn, was deciding the biggest things without his parents and without the LORD. It grieved them. Many of us have broken a parent's heart, or are a parent with a broken one. In the next chapter things get worse, and the LORD is still at work.

Question 1 of 6

God's word

What did the LORD tell Isaac to do in the famine, and what did He promise?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Stay in the land, and trust Him. He did not take the famine away, but He promised His presence and His blessing, and He passed on the promise made to Abraham. When you are in a hard season, ask Him where to stand, not only how to get out.

Question 2 of 6

Sin

What lie did Isaac tell, and where had he seen it before?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Abraham said of Sarah his wife, She is my sister: and Abimelech king of Gerar sent, and took Sarah.”

Genesis 20:2

Answer

He said his wife Rebekah was his sister, out of fear. His father Abraham had done the same thing in the same town. Sin can run in a family. But you do not have to repeat it: bring it to the light and to your brothers in Christ.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

Did the LORD still bless Isaac after he lied?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Answer

Yes. He blessed him in that same chapter, and said it was "for my servant Abraham's sake" (v. 24). For us it is for Christ's sake. Your fall does not cancel what God has promised. Confess it, and keep going.

Question 4 of 6

Peace

What did Isaac do when men fought him over the wells?

What verses tell us about this?

“If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.”

Romans 12:18

Answer

He did not fight. He gave up two wells, moved, and dug again, until he came to a well that no one disputed. He named it Rehoboth, "room," and gave the LORD the credit. Some fights are not worth winning.

Question 5 of 6

Fear

What did the LORD say to Isaac the night he reached Beer-sheba?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

"Fear not, for I am with thee." The LORD answered the very thing Isaac was afraid of, and Isaac answered by worshipping and getting back to work. When you are afraid, you can say that sentence too.

Question 6 of 6

Enemies

Why did the men who sent Isaac away come to make peace with him?

What verses tell us about this?

“When a man’s ways please the LORD, he maketh even his enemies to be at peace with him.”

Proverbs 16:7

Answer

Because they could see that the LORD was with him. People who have no use for you may still notice that God is at work in your life. Be the man who answers hatred with a feast and not a fight.
