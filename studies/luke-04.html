Part one

Tempted in the wilderness

Verses 1–13

Forty days alone, hungry, and the devil comes to call.

Where this chapter goes

From the river where He was baptized, the Spirit led Jesus into the wilderness of Judaea: rocky, empty, hot by day and cold by night.

The devil took Him to Jerusalem, to a high point of the temple (v. 9).

Then home to Galilee, and to Nazareth, where He grew up (vv. 14–16).

Then down to Capernaum on the lake (v. 31). It becomes His home base.

Led by the Spirit, into a fight

Right after the Father said, “Thou art my beloved Son” (3:22), the Spirit led Jesus into the desert. Being full of the Spirit didn't keep Him out of the hard place. It carried Him through it.

Luke 3 ended with Adam. Adam fell in a garden with everything to eat. Jesus stood firm in a wilderness with nothing to eat. Where the first man lost, the Son of God won, for us.

Forty days is a long time. Moses was on the mountain forty days, Elijah walked forty days to Horeb, and Israel spent forty years in the wilderness and failed its tests. Jesus passed His.

The first test: bread

The devilIf thou be the Son of God, command this stone that it be made bread.

JesusIt is written, That man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word of God.
Deuteronomy 8:3

Notice when the devil showed up: “he afterward hungered.” He waits for a weak moment. Men in recovery know the warning: when you're hungry, angry, lonely, or tired, watch out. Jesus was hungry and alone.

Bread wasn't wrong. Doing it the devil's way, at the devil's word, to prove Himself, was. And “if thou be” was a lie wrapped in a question. The Father had just said He was.

The shortcut

Ifthou wilt bow to me, just once,
→
thenthe crown, with no cross.

The kingdoms will belong to Jesus one day. The Father has promised them to Him. The devil offered them now, the easy way, for one bow. Every temptation is a shortcut to something God already means to give us His way.

Jesus answered from Deuteronomy again: worship God, and serve Him only. Whatever you bow to, you end up serving. Many of us know that from hard experience.

The devil can quote scripture too

The pinnacle was a high corner of the temple, probably the one that dropped far down into the Kidron Valley. Jesus had quoted the Bible twice, so the devil tried it himself, from Psalm 91. Watch what he left out.

Psalm 91:11

For he shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways.

Luke 4:10, the devil

For it is written, He shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee:

God promised to keep His people in the ways He sends them, not to catch a man who jumps off a roof to force His hand. That's not faith. That's testing God.

Hearing a Bible verse isn't enough. You have to know it well enough to catch it when somebody twists it, even when that somebody is your own head.

Three tests, one answer

  • “Command this stone”Satisfy yourself. “It is written”: Deuteronomy 8:3
  • “All shall be thine”Grab it now. “It is written”: Deuteronomy 6:13
  • “Cast thyself down”Make God prove it. “It is said”: Deuteronomy 6:16

Jesus didn't argue, and He didn't use any power we don't have. He answered every time with scripture He already knew, all three from the same book.

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.”

Hebrews 4:15

“For a season”

The devil lost, and he left. But only for a season. He came back at other times, all the way to the cross. Temptation doesn't retire because you won once.

That isn't bad news. Every time, there's a way out, and the same word of God that held for Jesus holds for you.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

“Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.”

James 4:7

Part two

This day is this scripture fulfilled

Verses 14–30

Jesus comes home to Nazareth. It starts with wonder and ends at the edge of a cliff.

In the synagogue at Nazareth

A synagogue was a town's meeting house for prayer and the Scriptures. On the sabbath, men read from the Law and the Prophets. A reader stood up to read, then sat down to teach.

The book was a scroll, rolled on wooden rods. Jesus unrolled Isaiah until He found the place He wanted, what we call Isaiah 61.

“As his custom was.” Jesus made a habit of showing up where God's word was read, every week. If the Son of God needed that habit, so do we.

Where He stopped reading

Here is the place in Isaiah, written about 700 years before. Jesus read it from the scroll, and then He stopped in the middle of a sentence.

Isaiah 61:1–2 The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me; because the LORD hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek; he hath sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to them that are bound; To proclaim the acceptable year of the LORD,he closed the bookand the day of vengeance of our God; to comfort all that mourn;

He closed the book before “the day of vengeance.” When He came the first time, He came to offer mercy. Judgment is real, and a day of it is still coming. But today is the day the door stands open.

“For God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world; but that the world through him might be saved.”

John 3:17

Who He came for

  • The poorwho have nothing to bring
  • The brokenheartedwho have been crushed inside
  • The captivesheld by something stronger than they are
  • The blindwho can't see the way out
  • The bruisedbeaten down by life

That's not a list of the impressive. It's a list of men like us. If you've been chained to a bottle, a pill, a habit, or a past, Jesus read that line about you. He didn't come for the people who had it together. He came to set captives free.

“The acceptable year of the Lord”

Every fiftieth year in Israel was the jubile. A trumpet sounded, and debts were cancelled, servants went home free, and lost family land came back.

Jesus says: that year has come, and I am the One who brings it. In Him, the debt is cancelled and the prisoner walks out.

“And ye shall hallow the fiftieth year, and proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof: it shall be a jubile unto you; and ye shall return every man unto his possession, and ye shall return every man unto his family.”

Leviticus 25:10

“This day”

Jesus sat down to teach, and every eye in the room was on Him. His first words were plain: this promise is coming true right now, in front of you. I am the One Isaiah wrote about.

What they heard

“Gracious words”

They couldn't deny it. No one had ever spoken like this.

What they saw

“Is not this Joseph’s son?”

The carpenter's boy. They had watched Him grow up.

They thought they knew all about Him. That's often what keeps people from Jesus: not that they've never heard, but that they think He's old news.

Two outsiders

Nazareth wanted a show, like the miracles they'd heard about in Capernaum. Jesus reminded them of two stories they knew well.

In a long famine, God sent Elijah past every widow in Israel to a foreign widow at Sarepta (Zarephath), near Sidon, and fed her house (1 Kings 17).

Of all the lepers in Israel, Elisha's God cleansed one: Naaman, a Syrian army captain, an enemy, once he humbled himself and washed in the Jordan (2 Kings 5).

From wonder to wrath

Why so angry? Jesus was saying God's grace reaches outsiders: a Gentile widow, an enemy soldier. And that hometown people who think God owes them get nothing by right. Grace offends people who think they've earned it.

Nazareth sits on a hill. They dragged Him to the edge to throw Him off. And He simply walked through the middle of them. No one could take His life before His time.

If your own family or old crowd has turned on you since you came to Christ, He knows exactly how that feels.

Part three

His word was with power

Verses 31–44

One sabbath in Capernaum: a devil cast out, a fever rebuked, and a whole town at the door.

“Came down”

Luke means it. Nazareth sits up in the hills. Capernaum sits on the shore of the Sea of Galilee, far below sea level. The road drops nearly 2,000 feet.

Capernaum was a busy fishing town on a trade road. It became Jesus' home base, and Peter lived there.

“His word was with power.” Other teachers quoted other teachers. Jesus spoke as One who had the right to say it, because He did.

A devil in church

The unclean spiritLet us alone; what have we to do with thee, thou Jesus of Nazareth? art thou come to destroy us? I know thee who thou art; the Holy One of God.

JesusHold thy peace, and come out of him.

The peopleWhat a word is this! for with authority and power he commandeth the unclean spirits, and they come out.

The devil knew exactly who Jesus was, and was terrified. Knowing the right facts about Jesus isn't the same as trusting Him. Jesus didn't need a testimony from devils. He shut it up with a word, and the man walked away unhurt.

“Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.”

James 2:19

Healed, then serving

So Simon Peter was a married man. His mother-in-law lived with the family, and she was down with a great fever. Luke, the doctor, notes how bad it was.

Jesus rebuked the fever, the same word He used on the devil, and it obeyed. No slow recovery. She got straight up and started serving them dinner.

That's the pattern for every man Jesus lifts up: set free, then put to work serving others. Nobody gets healed just to sit on the couch.

At sundown, a crowd at the door

one at a timeevery one of them

Why wait for the sun to set? It was the sabbath, and the sabbath ended at sundown. The moment it did, the whole town brought their sick.

He didn't wave a hand over the crowd. He laid his hands on every one. No one was too far gone and no one was skipped. He still deals with men one at a time.

“Other cities also”

After a night like that, Jesus slipped out before dawn to a lonely place. Mark tells us why:

“And in the morning, rising up a great while before day, he went out, and departed into a solitary place, and there prayed.”

Mark 1:35

The crowd wanted to keep Him. He was popular, and He could have settled in. But He knew why the Father sent Him: to preach the kingdom of God everywhere. Prayer kept Him on mission when success could have pulled Him off it.

Question 1 of 6

Temptation

How did Jesus fight back when the devil tempted Him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

With the word of God. Three times the devil pushed, and three times Jesus answered, “It is written,” with verses He already knew. He didn't debate or bargain. He stood on what God had said, and the devil left.

Question 2 of 6

God's word

What does a man live by, besides bread?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he humbled thee, and suffered thee to hunger, and fed thee with manna, which thou knewest not, neither did thy fathers know; that he might make thee know that man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD doth man live.”

Deuteronomy 8:3

Answer

By every word of God. Bread keeps the body alive, but a man is more than a body. Jesus was starving and still trusted His Father's word over His own hunger. God's word is daily food for the soul.

Question 3 of 6

Why He came

What did Jesus say He was sent to do, reading from Isaiah?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To preach good news to the poor, heal the brokenhearted, free the captives, give sight to the blind, set the bruised at liberty, and announce the Lord's year of release. And He said, “This day is this scripture fulfilled.” He is the One who does it.

Question 4 of 6

Grace for outsiders

Why did the people of Nazareth get so angry?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They expected miracles because He was one of theirs. Instead He reminded them that God once passed by Israel to bless a Gentile widow and an enemy soldier. Grace isn't owed to anyone. It's for the humble, and that offended proud people who thought they had a claim on Him.

Question 5 of 6

Who Jesus is

What did the unclean spirits know about Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.”

James 2:19

Answer

They knew He was the Holy One of God, the Christ, the Son of God, and they knew He had come to destroy their work. They had the facts right and were still lost. Saving faith isn't just knowing who Jesus is. It's trusting Him.

Question 6 of 6

His mission

Why wouldn't Jesus stay where the crowds wanted Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“And in the morning, rising up a great while before day, he went out, and departed into a solitary place, and there prayed.”

Mark 1:35

Answer

Because the Father had sent Him to preach the kingdom of God to other cities too. He got alone with God before the crowds found Him, and He followed His Father's plan, not the crowd's applause.
