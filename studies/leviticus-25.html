Leviticus 25

The sabbath year and the jubile

Every seventh year the land rests. After seven sevens, on the day of atonement, the trumpet sounds and liberty is proclaimed: every man goes back to his own possession and his own family. The land is the LORD's, and a poor brother is never to be ground down.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. The restWhat was the land to do in the seventh year?
  2. The trumpetWhen did the trumpet of the jubile sound, and what was proclaimed?
  3. The ownerWhose is the land, and what are the people who live on it?
  4. The redeemerWho could redeem a poor man's land, or the man himself?
  5. The brotherHow was a brother who had grown poor to be treated?
  6. The servantsWhy could an Israelite not be sold as a bondman?

Part one

“Then shall the land keep a sabbath unto the LORD”

Verses 1–7

Six years of work, and the seventh year the land rests.

Six years of work, one of rest

  1. years 1 to 6sow and pruneverse 3
  2. year 7a sabbath of restverse 4
  3. 7 × 7forty and nine yearsverse 8
  4. year 50the jubileverse 10

Some old words. To prune a vineyard is to trim the vines so they bear. A vine undressed is one left untrimmed, growing wild. What grows of its own accord grows by itself, unsown. And the land is to have a sabbath “unto the LORD”: the same seventh-day rest God gave Israel each week, now given to the ground.

This starts “when ye come into the land which I give you” (verse 2). They were still at Sinai, in tents, with no field to sow. The LORD is already giving rules for a land he has not yet handed over.

Everyone eats

  • theethe owner of the field
  • thy servant, thy maidthose who work for him
  • thy hired servantthe man paid by the day
  • thy stranger that sojournetha foreigner living among them
  • thy cattlethe herd
  • the beastthe wild animals of the land

Meat here means food, any food. In the seventh year the field is not the owner's harvest. What grows by itself is for all of them alike, the owner no more than his servant, the hired man, the foreigner, and the animals. A year when the rich man's field is no richer than the stranger's.

Part two

“Proclaim liberty throughout all the land”

Verses 8–22

Seven sevens of years, then a trumpet, and every man goes home.

The trumpet sounds on the day of atonement

  1. tenth day, seventh monththe day of atonement16:29; 25:9
  2. thenthe trumpet sounds25:9
  3. thenliberty is proclaimed25:10
  4. thenevery man goes home25:10

Jubile is the KJV's word for the fiftieth year, the year the trumpet opens. Count how it goes: atonement first, then liberty. The day the high priest went in with the blood (chapter 16) is the day the trumpet sounds. The land and the slaves were not released by a good mood. They were released on the day sin was covered.

Moses calls it the fiftieth year, hallowed, set apart for the LORD. After seven sabbaths of years, forty and nine, comes the fiftieth.

Liberty proclaimed, then and now

Leviticus 25:10

And ye shall hallow the fiftieth year, and proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof: it shall be a jubile unto you; and ye shall return every man unto his possession, and ye shall return every man unto his family.

Luke 4:18–19

The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor; he hath sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to preach deliverance to the captives, and recovering of sight to the blind, to set at liberty them that are bruised, To preach the acceptable year of the Lord.

Jesus read these words in the synagogue at Nazareth, from Isaiah 61:1–2, and then “he began to say unto them, This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears” (Luke 4:21). The jubile was a year on the calendar, once in fifty years. Jesus is the one it points to: liberty for the captive, and a man sent home.

What a field is really worth

An example, with made-up numbers. A man who sells a field sells the harvests, “the number of the years of the fruits” until the jubile (verses 15–16). Many harvests left, a high price; few left, a low one. At the trumpet the buyer gives it back, without a refund. “Ye shall not oppress one another; but thou shalt fear thy God.”

“What shall we eat the seventh year?”

  1. year 6the blessing: fruit for three yearsverse 21
  2. year 7no sowing, no gatheringverse 20
  3. year 8they sow; they eat the old storeverse 22
  4. year 9her fruits come inverse 22

A farmer's question, and the LORD answers it before it is asked in the field. He does not say, “Don't worry.” He says what he will do: the sixth year will bring three years' worth. The forty-ninth year is the seventh sabbath of years and the fiftieth follows it (verses 8 and 11), so the land lay unsown two years running. The answer is the same: his blessing.

Resting meant trusting

“Therefore take no thought, saying, What shall we eat? or, What shall we drink? or, Wherewithal shall we be clothed? (For after all these things do the Gentiles seek:) for your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things. But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.”

Matthew 6:31–33

The ones who obeyed the sabbath year were the ones who believed the LORD would feed them without their own harvest. Every man who has gone broke staying clean knows the question: what shall we eat? The LORD does not scold the question. He answers it.

Part three

“The land is mine”

Verses 23–34

What is sold can be redeemed, and what cannot be redeemed goes out at the jubile.

Owners who are only tenants

“My land” → “for ye are strangers and sojourners with me” A sojourner is a man who lives somewhere without being from there. The LORD is the landlord, and Israel are tenants.

“For we are strangers before thee, and sojourners, as were all our fathers: our days on the earth are as a shadow, and there is none abiding.”

1 Chronicles 29:15

David said the same thing at the end of his life. “The earth is the LORD'S, and the fulness thereof” (Psalm 24:1). Every acre you have is on loan. So is your life. A man who holds a thing loosely, as a tenant, finds it easier to share it, and easier to lose it without being destroyed.

Three ways home

  1. his kincomes and redeems itverse 25
  2. himselfcounts the years, pays back the overplusverses 26–27
  3. the jubileit goes out; he returnsverse 28

Waxen poor means grown poor. To redeem is to buy back. The overplus is the extra, what is left over. A man could lose his land, but never for ever: someone near to him could buy it back, or he could, or the trumpet would.

“Redeem” and “redeemed” come 15 times in this chapter. It is the chapter's heartbeat: a poor man is never left sold.

The redeemer has to be a brother

“For both he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one: for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brethren,”

Hebrews 2:11

“Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil; And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage.”

Hebrews 2:14–15

In the law, the redeemer was “his kin”, someone near to him (verse 25). The law put that right in the hands of his near kin. That is why the Son of God took “flesh and blood”: he had to be our brother to buy us back.

Made under the law, to redeem

“But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law, To redeem them that were under the law, that we might receive the adoption of sons.”

Galatians 4:4–5

Job knew the word: “For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth:” (Job 19:25)

A man who has sold himself to his habits cannot pay his own way out. But there is a Brother who can, and he has.

Three kinds of house

A house in a walled city

2930

Redeem it within a whole year. After that it is the buyer's for ever. It does not go out in the jubile.

A house in a village without a wall

31

Counted as the fields of the country: it can be redeemed, and it goes out in the jubile.

A house in a city of the Levites

323334

The Levites may redeem at any time, and it goes out in the jubile. The field of the suburbs, the open ground round their cities, may never be sold.

The land was the family's inheritance, and so a field could not be sold for ever. A house in a walled city was a different thing: a town house, not an inheritance. The Levites had no land of their own among the tribes, and the LORD saw to it that their houses and their open ground could never be taken from them for good.

Part four

“Thy brother be waxen poor”

Verses 35–55

No usury, no ruling with rigour, and a man set free at the jubile.

When a brother goes under

  • “fallen in decay”fallen into ruin; he cannot hold up his end
  • “relieve him”take hold of him, help him up
  • “though he be a stranger”even if he is a foreigner living among them
  • “no usury”no interest on a loan to a poor man
  • “or increase”no profit on the food you lend him, either
  • “that he may live with thee”the reason, said twice

The reason is given twice (verses 35 and 36): that thy brother may live with thee. A loan was to keep a man alive, not to make money off his trouble. And the LORD sets it in his own character: “I am the LORD your God, which brought you forth out of the land of Egypt.” They had been the poor man once.

“But love ye your enemies, and do good, and lend, hoping for nothing again; and your reward shall be great, and ye shall be the children of the Highest: for he is kind unto the unthankful and to the evil.”

Luke 6:35

Which of us has not needed a man to carry us for a stretch, and not charge for it? Be that man for someone, once you are on your feet.

A brother is not a slave

A brother, grown poor

a hired servant

Not a bondservant. He serves “unto the year of jubile”, then goes out, “both he and his children with him”.

The reason

“they are my servants”

“Which I brought forth out of the land of Egypt: they shall not be sold as bondmen.” The LORD had bought them out already.

Rigour means harshness. Bondservant and bondmaid are slaves. A brother who sold himself was never to be ruled “with rigour” (verses 43, 46, 53), because he already had a Master, and the Master had said so.

Verses 44–46 say plainly that Israel might buy bondmen and bondmaids from “the heathen that are round about”, and keep them as a possession. That is what the law says, in the days it was given, and we read it as it stands. What the law never allows is the brother, one of Israel, to be treated that way.

Above a servant, a brother

The New Testament carries the thought further. Paul sent Onesimus, a runaway servant, back to his master Philemon, and asked him to receive him this way:

“For perhaps he therefore departed for a season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever; Not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved, specially to me, but how much more unto thee, both in the flesh, and in the Lord?”

Philemon 15–16

Not a servant, but a brother beloved. The jubile's principle: whoever is in Christ is a brother, and a brother is not owned.

The price of a man

many years lefthe pays back much
few years lefthe pays back little
the jubilehe goes out free, with his children

A man's redemption price was counted by the years he had left to serve, at a hired servant's rate (verses 50–52). “His uncle, or his uncle's son… or any that is nigh of kin unto him”: nigh means near. “Or if he be able, he may redeem himself.” And if no one does, the year of jubile sets him free.

“For he that is called in the Lord, being a servant, is the Lord’s freeman: likewise also he that is called, being free, is Christ’s servant. Ye are bought with a price; be not ye the servants of men.”

1 Corinthians 7:22–23

“For unto me the children of Israel are servants” (verse 55). They belonged to the LORD first, so they could not belong to any man for good. The New Testament says the same of every believer: you were bought with a price. The man who bought you will not sell you.

Real world: the trumpet is for you

Most of the men in the room have, in one way or another, sold themselves: to a drug, to a bottle, to a debt, to a bad crowd. The jubile is for men like that.

  • SinIt owned you. Name it honestly: you sold yourself, and you couldn't buy yourself back.
  • RegretThe years are gone, and the land is run down. But a jubile does not ask what you did with the forty-nine.
  • GraceThe trumpet sounds on the day of atonement: the blood first, then the liberty. “If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”
  • HopeEvery man goes home, to his possession and to his family. Today is not the end of the story.
  • TodayWhoever you owe, or have wronged, or lost touch with, take one step toward home. And be the brother who does not charge a man for staying alive.

“Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage.”

Galatians 5:1

Points to remember

  1. The rest“in the seventh year shall be a sabbath of rest unto the land” 25:4Six years of work and one of rest, and resting meant trusting the LORD to feed them.
  2. The trumpet“proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof” 25:10On the day of atonement the trumpet sounds, and Jesus read the same liberty about himself (Luke 4:18–19).
  3. The owner“the land is mine; for ye are strangers and sojourners with me” 25:23Everything we hold is the LORD's, held on loan.
  4. The redeemer“if any of his kin come to redeem it, then shall he redeem that which his brother sold” 25:25A near kinsman buys back, and Christ became our brother to do it (Hebrews 2:14–15).
  5. The brother“thou shalt relieve him: yea, though he be a stranger, or a sojourner; that he may live with thee” 25:35Help a man up, and don't make money off his trouble.
  6. The servants“they are my servants, which I brought forth out of the land of Egypt: they shall not be sold as bondmen” 25:42He bought them out first, so no man may own them for good.

Question 1 of 6

The rest

What was the land to do in the seventh year?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It rested. No sowing, no pruning, and what grew by itself was food for everyone. The LORD promised a sixth-year blessing to cover it. Resting meant trusting.

Question 2 of 6

The trumpet

When did the trumpet of the jubile sound, and what was proclaimed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On the tenth day of the seventh month, the day of atonement, in the fiftieth year. Liberty was proclaimed, and every man returned to his possession and his family. Jesus read the same kind of liberty about himself.

Question 3 of 6

The owner

Whose is the land, and what are the people who live on it?

What verses tell us about this?

“For we are strangers before thee, and sojourners, as were all our fathers: our days on the earth are as a shadow, and there is none abiding.”

1 Chronicles 29:15

Answer

The land is the LORD's. The people are strangers and sojourners with him: tenants, not owners. Everything we hold is on loan.

Question 4 of 6

The redeemer

Who could redeem a poor man's land, or the man himself?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

His kin: a brother, an uncle, an uncle's son, or any near of kin, or the man himself if he could. In Christ, our Brother took flesh and blood to redeem us, because we could not redeem ourselves.

Question 5 of 6

The brother

How was a brother who had grown poor to be treated?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He was to be relieved, helped up, and kept alive among them. No interest and no profit on his food. And all of it done “that thy brother may live with thee.”

Question 6 of 6

The servants

Why could an Israelite not be sold as a bondman?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he that is called in the Lord, being a servant, is the Lord’s freeman: likewise also he that is called, being free, is Christ’s servant. Ye are bought with a price; be not ye the servants of men.”

1 Corinthians 7:22–23

Answer

Because they were the LORD's servants: he brought them out of Egypt, and what he has bought no man may own for good. So a brother was a hired servant until the jubile, then free.
