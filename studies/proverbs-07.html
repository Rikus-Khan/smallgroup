One street, one night, one bad turn

A father watches a young man walk straight into a trap, and shows his son how to stay out of it.

Part one

Keep my words

Verses 1–5

Before the story, the father hands his son his armor.

The apple of your eye

אִישׁוֹן said ishon “the little man” of the eye: the pupil

Look into someone's eye up close and you'll see a tiny reflection of yourself in the pupil. That's the “little man.” It's the part of your body you protect without even thinking. Something flies at your face and you flinch.

Guard God's Word like that. Keep it on your fingers, where you see it all day, and write it on the table (the tablet) of your heart. “Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee” (Psalm 119:11).

Make wisdom family

Wisdom

Your sister

Close, safe, always around. She tells you the truth because she's on your side.

The strange woman

A flatterer

She “flattereth with her words” (v. 5). She tells you what you want to hear because she wants something.

A man who's lonely and on his own is easy pickings. Fill your life with good company: God's wisdom, and real brothers in Christ who'll tell you the truth.

Part two

The walk to her door

Verses 6–12

He didn't fall into it. He walked there.

in the twilight in the evening in the black and dark night

“Void of understanding” is literally lacking heart: no sense, no plan. He didn't set out to sin. He just kept walking near her corner as the light went down. In recovery we call it people, places, and things. Stay off her street.

Know what she looks like

  • Dressed for itthe attire of a harlot
  • Subtil of hearther real plans are hidden
  • Loud and stubbornrestless, won't be told no
  • Never homeher feet abide not in her house
  • Everywherein wait at every corner

She isn't waiting for him to come looking. She's out in the streets, at every corner. Today the corner is in your pocket. Temptation finds you, so you'd better know its face before it shows up.

Part three

What she says

Verses 13–23

Every line is a lie, and every line works.

Sin in Sunday clothes

A peace offering was a sacrifice where the worshiper got to take home much of the meat. It had to be eaten that day or the next. So she's saying: “I've been to the temple, I've kept my promises to God, and there's a feast at my place.”

She uses religion as cover. Temptation loves to sound spiritual: “God wants me happy.” “I already prayed about it.” “I've been doing so good, I deserve this.”

“Impudent face” means a hard, shameless face. And then the flattery: “I came out looking for you, and I found you” (v. 15). Every man wants to hear that he's wanted.

Her lines, and what they really mean

“I have decked my bed… perfumed my bed”plannedFancy linen from Egypt, imported spices. She set this up long before he walked by.
“Let us take our fill of love until the morning”a lieIt isn't love. It's a night, and then the morning comes.
“The goodman is not at home”married“The goodman” is her husband. He's on a long trip with a bag of money, and he'll be back on a set day. She's saying: no one will know, and we've got time.

Every one of her lines takes away a reason to stop. That's what temptation does. Notice who isn't mentioned at all: God, who sees every road a man takes.

He never sees it coming

Three pictures of the same man: an ox walking to the butcher, a fool marched off to the stocks, a bird hopping toward the bait. None of them knows what's about to happen.

“Straightway”: he went right after her. And the trap shuts. “Till a dart strike through his liver” means a deadly wound. The bird thought it was getting a meal. It “knoweth not that it is for his life.”

Part four

Hearken

Verses 24–27

The father turns from the window and looks his son in the eye.

Strong men fall here

“Many strong men have been slain by her.” Samson was the strongest man alive. David was a man after God's own heart. Both fell this way. Nobody is too strong for this, and the man who thinks he is should worry most.

But God never leaves you without a door: He “will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it” (1 Corinthians 10:13). The way out is usually the first door, not the last one. Take it early.

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Already been in that house?

such were some of you → washed Paul wrote this to a church full of men who had lived exactly this way. He didn't say such are. He said such were.

If you've already walked through her door, this chapter isn't the end of your story. Jesus died for exactly this sin. Confess it, turn from it, and get honest with a brother. You are washed, set apart, and made right with God in Jesus' name.

Question 1 of 6

God's Word

Where should God's words be kept?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherewithal shall a young man cleanse his way? by taking heed thereto according to thy word.”

Psalm 119:9

Answer

Close. Guard them like the pupil of your eye, keep them on your fingers where you see them all day, and write them on your heart. The Word you've stored up before temptation shows up is what keeps you when it does.

Question 2 of 6

Walking into temptation

How did the young man end up at her door?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He walked there. He had no sense and no plan, and he kept going near her corner as it got darker. He didn't decide to sin in one big moment. He made a lot of small choices about where to go. Stay off her street.

Question 3 of 6

Flattery

What lies does temptation tell?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It dresses up in religion: “I've done my duty to God.” It flatters: “I was looking for you.” It promises love and delivers a night. And it says no one will know. Every line takes away a reason to stop, and none of them mention God.

Question 4 of 6

The end of the road

Where does her road lead?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.”

James 1:15

Answer

To death. Like an ox to the slaughter or a bird to the snare, the man doesn't know it's for his life. Her house looks like a party on the main floor, but the stairs go down to the chambers of death.

Question 5 of 6

Pride

Are strong men safe from this?

What verses tell us about this?

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Answer

No. “Many strong men” have been slain this way, Samson and David among them. Nobody is strong enough on his own. But God is faithful, and He always provides a way out. The strong man is the one who takes it early.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

What if a man has already been in her house?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Answer

Then this chapter is a warning, not a verdict. Jesus died for exactly this sin. Confess it, turn from it, and bring it into the light with a brother you trust. In Christ you are washed and made right with God. Such were some of us.
