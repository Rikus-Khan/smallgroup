Don't trip your brother

Believers who disagree about food and holy days learn to stop judging each other, and to give up anything that would make a brother fall.

Chapter 13 was about living right with the government and our neighbours. Chapter 14 comes back inside the church: how believers who see things differently can live together.

The church at Rome was a mix. Jewish believers, raised on the law of Moses, with its clean and unclean foods and its holy days. And Gentile believers, raised in a city where most meat in the market had been offered to an idol first. They didn't eat the same, and they didn't keep the same days.

Any group like ours is a mix too: different churches, different pasts, different weak spots. This chapter is for us.

Part one

God hath received him

Verses 1–6

One man eats anything. Another eats only vegetables. God took them both in.

The weak and the strong

Weak in the faith

eats only herbs

A tender conscience. Maybe still careful about the old food laws, or afraid the meat had been offered to an idol. Vegetables were safe.

Strong

eats all things

Knows that food can't make a man clean or unclean before God (v. 14). He is free to eat.

“Doubtful disputations” means arguments over opinions. Welcome the weaker brother, but not so you can set him straight.

This chapter is about things God hasn't commanded or forbidden. Drunkenness and sexual sin aren't opinions. Paul called them sin just a few verses back (13:13).

Not your servant

The strong were tempted to despise, to look down on the weak as narrow. The weak were tempted to judge, to call the strong worldly.

Paul tells both: he isn't your servant. He answers to his own Master. And “God hath received him.” If God took him in, who are you to shut him out?

“Holden up” means held up. If you fell yesterday, hear this: God is able to make you stand. Your footing doesn't rest on how strong you are. It rests on Him.

One day above another

Some believers still kept the Sabbath and the feasts of the law. Others treated every day alike. Paul doesn't settle it. Each man should be “fully persuaded”, settled before God.

One man→keeps the day, “unto the Lord”and gives God thanks
Another→doesn't, “to the Lord”and gives God thanks

Today it might be the brother who won't watch certain movies, or won't eat at a restaurant with a bar. Don't mock him. He may know something about his own weak spots that you don't.

Part two

We are the Lord's

Verses 7–12

Every man belongs to one Master, and every man will give account to Him.

Living or dying, we are the Lord's.

The old life said, “It's my life. I'm only hurting myself.” It was never true. Our choices always landed on somebody: a mother, a child, a friend. “None of us liveth to himself.”

“Revived” means lived again. Christ died and rose to be Lord of everyone, the living and the dead. You belong to Him now. That isn't a chain. It's the safest place a man can be.

The judgment seat

The Greek word is bēma, a raised stone platform where a judge sat. Paul wrote this letter in Corinth, and Corinth had one in its marketplace. He had stood before it himself, dragged before Gallio (Acts 18:12). It still stands in the ruins.

Every believer will stand before Christ's judgment seat. Not to find out if he's saved: that was settled at the cross. It's where a servant gives account of how he lived, and receives his reward.

Account of himself

Not of his brother. You won't answer for what the other man ate, or how he kept his Sundays. You'll answer for you. Verse 11 quotes Isaiah:

“I have sworn by myself, the word is gone out of my mouth in righteousness, and shall not return, That unto me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear.”

Isaiah 45:23

“Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.”

John 5:24

That's why we can stop keeping score on other men. A believer's sin was judged at the cross. Our part is to keep short accounts with God: own today's sin, confess it, and walk on in grace.

Part three

No stumblingblock

Verses 13–23

Freedom that trips up a brother isn't love.

Judge this rather

Paul turns the word around. Stop judging your brother. Judge yourself: am I putting something in his way?

A skandalon was the trigger of a trap, or a stone in the road. Something that makes a man go down.

Verse 14: no food is unclean in itself. But if a brother's conscience says it's wrong, then for him it is. Love clears the road for him.

For whom Christ died

“Destroy” is a strong word: a man pushed by my example back into what nearly wrecked him.

Put them on the scale. On one side, my plate, my freedom, my right. On the other, a man Jesus thought was worth dying for. It isn't close.

“Let not then your good be evil spoken of”: your freedom is good. Don't let it give the faith a bad name.

What the kingdom is made of

  • meatwhat's on the plate
  • drinkwhat's in the glass
  • righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghostwhat the Spirit grows in a man

A church can fight for years over food rules and miss the whole thing. The kingdom shows up in a man who does right, lives at peace with God and his brothers, and is glad. That man is “acceptable to God, and approved of men.”

Good news for a man whose whole life used to be about the next drink: the kingdom isn't about what's in the glass.

Nor to drink wine

This verse speaks straight to us. Many men in this room know what one drink can start.

If you're the strong brother, a man who never had a problem: don't order a beer at dinner with a man fighting for his sobriety. Don't keep it in the fridge when he comes over. Love sets the glass down.

Paul says it's good to give up anything that makes a brother stumble, even something you could have.

Wine is a mocker

“Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging: and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise.”

Proverbs 20:1

If you're the man in recovery, there's no “strong” version of you that can handle a little. Southern Baptists have long urged believers to leave alcohol alone altogether. For a man it has ruled, it's the only safe one.

That isn't weakness, and it isn't legalism. It's a man who knows the road and chooses not to walk it.

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

The bottle promised what only God can give: peace, courage, joy. Now the Holy Spirit lives in you. Be filled with Him instead.

Whatsoever is not of faith

If youthink it's wrong, and do it anyway,
→
thenfor you, it's sin.

“Damned” here means condemned: his own conscience convicts him, and he stands guilty. Paul isn't saying a believer loses his salvation over a meal. He's saying don't go against your conscience.

If you have to talk yourself into it, don't. And verse 22 holds the other side: “Happy is he that condemneth not himself.” A man who walks with God by faith doesn't have to live looking over his shoulder.

Points to remember

  1. Disagreements“Him that is weak in the faith receive ye” 14:1Welcome the brother who sees it differently. Don't despise him, and don't judge him.
  2. Judging“God is able to make him stand” 14:4He answers to his own Master, and God holds him up. He holds you up too.
  3. Belonging“We are the Lord's” 14:8No man lives to himself. Living or dying, we belong to Christ.
  4. Giving account“Every one of us shall give account of himself” 14:12Saved by grace, we'll still answer for how we lived. Keep short accounts with God.
  5. Stumbling“Nor to drink wine, nor any thing whereby thy brother stumbleth” 14:21A brother for whom Christ died is worth more than any freedom of mine.
  6. Conscience“Whatsoever is not of faith is sin” 14:23Don't act against your conscience. If you have to talk yourself into it, don't.

Question 1 of 6

Disagreements

How should believers treat each other over things God hasn't commanded?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Receive each other. The strong shouldn't look down on the weak, and the weak shouldn't judge the strong, because God has received them both. Each man settles it in his own mind before the Lord, and gives thanks.

Question 2 of 6

Judging

Why shouldn't I judge another believer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He isn't my servant. He answers to the Lord, who is able to make him stand, and I'll stand before Christ's judgment seat myself. The judging I'm told to do is of me: am I tripping my brother?

Question 3 of 6

Belonging

Who do we live for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For the Lord. No man lives only to himself; our choices always touch other people. Christ died and rose to be our Lord, so whether we live or die, we are His. That's where a man is safest.

Question 4 of 6

Giving account

What will happen at the judgment seat of Christ?

What verses tell us about this?

“For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ; that every one may receive the things done in his body, according to that he hath done, whether it be good or bad.”

2 Corinthians 5:10

Answer

Every believer will give account of himself to God. It isn't to decide whether we're saved; whoever believes “shall not come into condemnation” (John 5:24). It's where we answer for how we lived and receive our reward.

Question 5 of 6

Stumbling

Why would I give something up for my brother's sake?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore, if meat make my brother to offend, I will eat no flesh while the world standeth, lest I make my brother to offend.”

1 Corinthians 8:13

Answer

Because Christ died for him. My freedom is worth less than a brother's soul. Love sets down the glass, or anything else, that could make him fall.

Question 6 of 6

Conscience

What does “whatsoever is not of faith is sin” mean?

What verses tell us about this?

“But without faith it is impossible to please him: for he that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him.”

Hebrews 11:6

Answer

If I believe something is wrong and do it anyway, it's sin for me, even if the thing itself isn't. Don't go against your conscience. Live by faith, with a clear conscience before God.
