Ephesians 3

Love that passeth knowledge

A prisoner in Rome, the man who once hunted Christians, prays that men like us would know a love too big to measure.

Chapter 2 ended with Gentiles, people who were once far off, built into one house with the Jews, “an habitation of God through the Spirit.” Chapter 3 opens “For this cause”, and Paul starts to pray.

But he stops after one verse to explain who he is and why he's in chains (verses 2–13). In verse 14 he says “For this cause” again and finishes the prayer. Then he closes the first half of the letter with praise.

Part one

A prisoner with a secret

Verses 1–7

A man in chains with the best news the Gentiles ever heard.

Whose prisoner?

Paul writes from Rome, about AD 60 to 62, chained to a Roman soldier in a rented house (Acts 28:16, 30). The letter goes to Ephesus, where he had spent three years.

Rome thought he was Caesar's prisoner. Paul calls himself “the prisoner of Jesus Christ.” The chain was real, but Christ held the other end.

And “for you Gentiles”: he was arrested in Jerusalem the day he told a crowd that God had sent him to the Gentiles (Acts 22:21–22).

Locked up, but not shut out

How Rome saw it

Caesar's prisoner

A Jew under guard, waiting on a trial, “with a soldier that kept him” (Acts 28:16).

How Paul saw it

Christ's prisoner

Right where his Lord put him, with a letter to write and a guard to talk to.

Dispensation (verse 2) is an old word for a job given to a steward: something put in your hands to manage for the owner. God handed Paul a trust, “to youward”, toward you Gentiles.

Some of us have done time, or are on paper now. A cell, a halfway house, a court date: none of it is outside God's reach. A man can be locked up and still be the Lord's man, with work to do right where he is.

Part two

The least, and the riches

Verses 8–13

God sent His riches by way of the man with the worst record.

Less than the least

Paul never forgot the believers he dragged off to prison (Acts 8:3). Watch him over the years:

  1. “the least of the apostles”1 Corinthians 15:9 · about AD 55
  2. “less than the least of all saints”Ephesians 3:8 · about AD 60–62
  3. “of whom I am chief”of sinners · 1 Timothy 1:15 · about AD 62–64

The longer he walked with Christ, the smaller he got in his own eyes, and the bigger grace looked.

“Who was before a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and injurious: but I obtained mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief.”

1 Timothy 1:13

That's what to do with regret. Paul didn't pretend his past away, and he didn't drown in it. He let it make him humble and grateful, and then he got up and went to work: “is this grace given.”

Unsearchable riches

Unsearchable means you can't track it to the end. Think of a gold mine where every shaft finds more, and nobody ever hits bottom.

Paul has already been naming them: forgiven, adopted, sealed with the Spirit, made alive, given a way in to the Father. And there's more under that.

“In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace;”

Ephesians 1:7

Maybe you came to Christ broke, with a record and nothing in the bank. In Him you are not poor. You can't spend it, you can't lose it, and you'll never see the end of it.

Many-colored wisdom

Manifold means many-sided, many-colored. The church at Ephesus was Jews and Gentiles, masters and slaves (6:5–9), and men who had burned their books of magic (Acts 19:19), all at one table.

Verse 10 says the whole thing is on display to “principalities and powers in heavenly places”: the angels, and the dark powers too. When a man with your past sits down at the Lord's table with a man who never did a day of trouble, heaven watches and sees how wise God is.

Access is the right to walk into a king's presence. Most people never got past the gate. In Christ, a man who was locked out of everything can walk in and talk to God the Father, today, without an appointment.

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

Verse 13: Paul tells them not to lose heart over his chains. His troubles were for their good. Your hard season isn't proof God has left you either.

Part three

On his knees

Verses 14–21

What Paul prayed for men he loved. You can pray it for yourself.

Strength on the inside

Paul doesn't pray for an easier life. He prays for strength in “the inner man”, and the strength is “by his Spirit”, who lives in every believer.

Dwell means to settle in and live there, not to visit. Christ isn't a guest in a believer's heart. He moved in.

Rooted like a tree, grounded like a wall on its foundation. A storm doesn't knock down what is rooted. Staying clean today won't come from white knuckles. It comes from a root that goes down into His love.

Measure it if you can

Breadth: wide enough for the whole world, Jew and Gentile, whosoever (John 3:16). Length: it started before the world and never runs out.

Depth: it reaches lower than the lowest place you ever went. Height: it lifts you all the way up to God.

Then every line runs off the page. Paul prays that we would know a love “which passeth knowledge.” You can know it for real, and never get to the end of it.

One prayer, climbing

Paul's prayer is one long sentence, and each part leads to the next, like steps going up:

  1. strengthenedby his Spirit in the inner man
  2. Christ dwellin your hearts by faith
  3. rooted and groundedin love
  4. comprehendbreadth, length, depth, height
  5. know the lovewhich passeth knowledge
  6. filledwith all the fulness of God

Notice where it ends: not with a man who is strong in himself, but a man filled. That's the opposite of the empty a lot of us tried to fill with a bottle or a pipe.

Today, pray this for yourself, out loud, one step at a time. And pray it for a brother by name.

Above all you ask or think

Paul runs out of words, so he stacks them up. Whatever you have asked for, and whatever you have only dared to think, God is able to do more than that.

Not a promise of a new truck. It's “according to the power that worketh in us”, the same power that raised Jesus from the dead (1:19–20). A changed man, a mended home, years sober: He can do that, and above that.

And it all ends where it should: “Unto him be glory.” That's the end of the first half of the letter. Next, Paul shows how to walk it out.

Points to remember

  1. The prisoner“I Paul, the prisoner of Jesus Christ for you Gentiles” 3:1Rome had the chain. Christ held the other end.
  2. The mystery“That the Gentiles should be fellowheirs, and of the same body” 3:6In Christ, outsiders come in as equals, with the same inheritance.
  3. Less than the least“Unto me, who am less than the least of all saints, is this grace given” 3:8The worst record didn't stop grace. It made Paul humble, and it put him to work.
  4. The inner man“Strengthened with might by his Spirit in the inner man” 3:16Strength comes from His Spirit within, and from Christ living in your heart.
  5. Four directions“The love of Christ, which passeth knowledge” 3:19Wide, long, deep, high: you can know it, and never reach the end of it.
  6. Able“Able to do exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think” 3:20God can do more than you ask, by His power working in you.

Question 1 of 6

The prisoner

Whose prisoner does Paul say he is?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Paul dwelt two whole years in his own hired house, and received all that came in unto him,”

Acts 28:30

Answer

The prisoner of Jesus Christ. Rome had him under guard, but Paul knew the Lord had put him there, for the sake of the Gentiles. Wherever you are locked in, you can still be the Lord's man.

Question 2 of 6

The mystery

What secret did God make known through Paul?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That the Gentiles would be fellowheirs with the Jews, members of the same body, sharing the same promise, all in Christ by the gospel. Nobody is let in as a second-class believer.

Question 3 of 6

Less than the least

What did Paul think of himself, and what did God give him anyway?

What verses tell us about this?

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

Answer

He saw himself as less than the least, because he had persecuted the church. God gave him grace anyway, and a job: to preach the riches of Christ. Your past doesn't disqualify you from grace, or from being useful.

Question 4 of 6

The inner man

Where does a believer's strength come from?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

From God's Spirit, who lives inside every believer, and from Christ living in his heart by faith. It isn't willpower. It is a root going down deep into the love of God.

Question 5 of 6

Four directions

How big is the love of Christ?

What verses tell us about this?

“Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

Romans 8:39

Answer

Bigger than anyone can measure: wide enough for whosoever, long enough to be everlasting, deep enough to reach the lowest man, high enough to bring him to God. It passes knowledge.

Question 6 of 6

Able

What is God able to do?

What verses tell us about this?

“And what is the exceeding greatness of his power to us-ward who believe, according to the working of his mighty power,”

Ephesians 1:19

Answer

Exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think. Not by our strength, but by the power that raised Jesus from the dead, now at work in every believer. And the glory goes to Him.
