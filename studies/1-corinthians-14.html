Where we are in 1 Corinthians

About AD 55, Paul writes from Ephesus to the church he started in Corinth. The letter answers their problems one by one. We are at the end of the three chapters on spiritual gifts.

Their meetings had become a contest. The gift that looked most spiritual, speaking in a tongue, was the one everybody wanted, and several men would speak at once. Paul's answer runs through three chapters.

Part one

Build the church up

Verses 1–12

The best gift in the room is the one that helps the man next to you.

Who gets built up?

A tongue nobody knows “speaketh not unto men, but unto God.” It may do the speaker some good, but the room is left out. Prophecy “speaketh unto men”: every man in the room is built up, urged on, and comforted.

That is the whole chapter in one picture. Paul isn't ranking gifts by how spiritual they look. He ranks them by who gets helped.

What Paul means by “prophesy”

Prophecy here is not mainly telling the future. It is speaking God's word to people so they can understand it, and be built up, urged on, and comforted (v. 3).

Then, in Corinth

Before the New Testament

The New Testament wasn't written yet. God gave some believers a message to speak, and the church weighed it (v. 29).

Now, in our group

An open Bible, made plain

We have God's whole written word. Many Bible teachers say the nearest thing for us is opening it and speaking it plainly, so the man beside you gets it.

“So they read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading.”

Nehemiah 8:8

What were tongues?

The first time, at Pentecost, they were real languages the apostles had never learned. Men from all these lands heard God praised in their own.

“And how hear we every man in our own tongue, wherein we were born?”

Acts 2:8

Christians differ on whether this gift goes on today. Many Baptists believe the sign gifts belonged to the apostles' day; other believers think they continue. Either way, Paul's rule here is plain: in church, if nobody understands it, keep quiet.

Every time we meet, something is being built, one brick at a time: a man's faith, his hope, his nerve to face tomorrow. Paul's question for any word, song, or prayer is simple: does it build?

An uncertain sound

Armies sent orders by trumpet. Every soldier knew the calls by heart, and his life could depend on hearing them right.

Uncertain sound

A trumpet blown beautifully but unclearly gets men killed. Words nobody understands are the same: you “speak into the air.”

Your life gives a sound too. Men at work are listening for whether your faith is real. Say it plainly, and live it plainly.

Part two

With the understanding also

Verses 13–25

Paul could out-tongue all of them, and he would rather say five words a man can understand.

How can he say Amen?

Amen means “so be it.” When one man prays aloud, the rest join in by saying it at the end. You can't say “so be it” to words you didn't understand.

Pray with your heart, “with the spirit,” and with your head, “with the understanding also.” When you pray out loud in our group, pray so the newest man can say Amen. Plain words are not second-class prayers.

Be children in one thing, and men in another

The Corinthians had it backwards. They were childish about gifts, wanting the shiny one, and all too grown-up in their grudges.

“In malice be ye children”

Like a little child

No scheming, no keeping score, no plotting to get even. A small child forgets yesterday's fight by lunch.

“In understanding be men”

Like a grown man

Think. Read. Ask questions. Don't chase whatever feels most spiritual. Grow up in what you know about God.

“When I was a child, I spake as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child: but when I became a man, I put away childish things.”

1 Corinthians 13:11

Some of us carry malice like a pro: years of grudges and plans. Lay it down. And some of us have never read the Bible for ourselves. Start. A chapter a day makes a man.

A sign to them that believe not

In verse 21 Paul quotes Isaiah. Israel would not listen to God's plain words, so God warned that He would speak to them through the strange languages of foreign invaders.

“For with stammering lips and another tongue will he speak to this people. To whom he said, This is the rest wherewith ye may cause the weary to rest; and this is the refreshing: yet they would not hear.”

Isaiah 28:11–12
  1. about 700 BCIsaiah: foreign tongues, a warning to men who would not hear
  2. about AD 30–33Pentecost: a sign to Jews from every land; about 3,000 believe
  3. about AD 55Corinth: tongues nobody understandsthis chapter

Tongues were a sign pointed at unbelievers. But in Corinth they were being used to impress believers, with no one to explain them. A sign nobody can read points nowhere.

A stranger walks in

Picture a man off the street, an “unlearned” outsider, walking into the meeting. What happens to him depends on what he hears.

Everyone speaking in tongues

“Ye are mad”

He hears noise, decides these people are crazy, and walks out the way he came in.

God's word, spoken plainly

“God is in you of a truth”

He understands. His own heart is laid open, the secrets he never told anyone. He falls on his face and worships God.

Being found out by the word of God is not the end of a man. It is how God brings him home. Many of us were that man once, walking in with secrets, and the word met us there.

“So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

Romans 10:17

Part three

Decently and in order

Verses 26–40

Everyone brings something. Here is how to make room for it all, without the chaos.

One at a time

  2. 2let the other judgeWhat is said gets weighed against God's word. And if someone else has something, let him speak.14:29–30
  3. 3subject to the prophetsNobody gets to say “the Spirit made me do it.” The Spirit doesn't take over a man. He gives him self-control.14:32

Not the author of confusion

God is not the author of confusion, but of peace.

This is about a church meeting, but it tells you what God is like. He doesn't work in chaos. Where there's shouting over each other, nobody listening, and everybody wanting the spotlight, that's not the Spirit at work.

“But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law.”

Galatians 5:22–23

If your life has felt like confusion, the drinking, the lies, the mess you can't keep straight, hear this: God is not the author of that. He is the author of peace, and He is not finished writing your story.

A hard text, read humbly

Good Baptist teachers read these two verses a little differently. Here is the setting, and what is plain.

  • The settingPaul is still putting order in the meeting. He has just told a tongue-speaker to keep silence (v. 28) and a prophet to hold his peace (v. 30).
  • How far it goesIn 11:5 Paul speaks of a woman praying and prophesying. So many teachers think he means something narrower here: calling out questions in the meeting, or the weighing of what was said (v. 29).
  • What is plainThe goal is the same as the whole chapter: a meeting where people learn in peace, and homes where the things of God get talked about.

Notice who this puts a job on: “let them ask their husbands at home.” That assumes a husband who knows his Bible and will talk about it at the kitchen table. Men, be the man she can ask.

Where the word comes from

The Corinthians acted as if they could do church any way they liked. Paul asks: did God's word start with you?

The Lordgave His commandmentsHis apostleswrote them downCorinthand on tous

The word came to us. It didn't come from us. That's humbling, and it's a relief: we don't have to invent the Christian life. We get to receive it and do it.

And “if any man be ignorant, let him be ignorant”: Paul won't chase an argument forever. Say it plainly, then leave the man with God.

Points to remember

  1. Edify“Let all things be done unto edifying” 14:26Everything we do together has one test: does it build the others up?
  2. Prophecy“He that prophesieth edifieth the church” 14:4God's word, spoken so others understand, helps the whole room. A tongue nobody knows helps only the speaker.
  3. Understood“Ye shall speak into the air” 14:9Words nobody understands are an uncertain trumpet. Five plain words beat ten thousand.
  4. Be men“In malice be ye children, but in understanding be men” 14:20Hold no grudges, like a child. Grow up in what you know about God, like a man.
  5. The outsider“God is in you of a truth” 14:25When he understands, his heart is laid open and he worships God. Being found out by the word is how God brings a man home.
  6. Order“God is not the author of confusion, but of peace” 14:33God works in peace, not chaos. So let all things be done decently and in order.

Question 1 of 6

Edify

What test does Paul give for everything we do when we come together?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore comfort yourselves together, and edify one another, even as also ye do.”

1 Thessalonians 5:11

Answer

Let all things be done unto edifying. Every song, word, and prayer should build the others up, not show off the one speaking.

Question 2 of 6

Prophecy

Why does Paul say prophecy is greater than tongues?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the church understands it and is built up, urged on, and comforted. A tongue nobody understands edifies only the speaker, unless someone interprets.

Question 3 of 6

Understood

What good is a word that nobody understands?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

None to the hearer. It is an uncertain trumpet, spoken into the air, and no one can say Amen to it. Five plain words do more than ten thousand.

Question 5 of 6

The outsider

What happens when an unbeliever walks in and understands what is said?

What verses tell us about this?

“So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

Romans 10:17

Answer

He is convinced. The secrets of his heart are laid open, he falls on his face and worships God, and he says God is really among them.

Question 6 of 6

Order

What is God the author of, and what is He not?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He is the author of peace, not of confusion. So His people take turns, keep self-control, and do all things decently and in order.
