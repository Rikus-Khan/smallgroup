Exodus 27

The altar, the court, and the lamp

The first thing a man meets at God's house is an altar of bronze, where blood is shed. Around it a fence of white linen with one gate. And in the tent, a lamp that is never to go out.

Part one

The altar of bronze

Verses 1–8

Before the tent, before the light and the bread, there is a place for sacrifice.

The altar, in feet

5 × 5 × 3cubits: long, wide, high
≈ 7½ × 7½ × 4½feet
foursquaresquare on every side: the same length as width
a manabout 5½ ft
the altar4½ ft high, 7½ ft across

Wood (shittim, the hard desert acacia), covered all over with brass: in the Bible, bronze or copper.

The horns are four pointed corners that stick up, cut from the same piece. We will meet them again: in Israel's story, a man in trouble could run and catch hold of the horns of the altar (1 Kings 1:50).

The tools, and how the thing was built

  • pansto catch and carry away the ashes
  • shovelsfor clearing the ashes
  • basonsbowls, to catch the blood
  • fleshhookslong forks for lifting and turning the meat
  • firepanspans for carrying the burning coals
  • a grate of networka bronze grid, a hanging shelf of mesh set in “under the compass”: the ledge that runs round the altar, half way down

The altar is “hollow with boards”: a box with the middle empty, light enough to carry. Four rings at the corners, two poles of bronze-covered wood (staves) through them, and it moves with the camp. Like the ark, it travels wherever the people go.

And “as it was shewed thee in the mount”: once again, the pattern is God's, not Moses'.

The way in, in the order a priest met it

Come in at the gate, and the first thing in front of you is the altar. Then the laver, a bronze basin for washing (chapter 30). Then the tent. No one gets to the light, the bread or the mercy seat without first meeting a sacrifice.

Why the altar comes first

Every day, at the altar

Another animal

Blood, fire and ashes. It had to be done again, and the ashes carried away, and done again.

At the cross

Once for all

One death, outside the city, for every sinner who will come.

“By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.”

Hebrews 10:10

“We have an altar, whereof they have no right to eat which serve the tabernacle. For the bodies of those beasts, whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin, are burned without the camp. Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate.”

Hebrews 13:10–12

The altar was in the open, in front of everyone, at the entrance. Sin is dealt with in the open and first. Jesus died in the open, outside the gate, where everyone could see.

Part two

The court

Verses 9–19

A wall of white linen around the tent, with one gate.

The court, in feet

100 × 50 cubitsthe whole court
150 × 75 feethalf as long as a football field
5 cubits ≈ 7½ ftthe height of the fence
  • hangingsthe linen curtains that make the fence
  • pillars and socketsposts, and the bronze stands they stood in
  • hooks and filletssilver hooks to hang the cloth, and silver bands or rods joining the tops of the posts
  • pinstent pegs, of bronze, to hold the fence against the wind

Twenty pillars along the south side, twenty along the north, ten across the west end, and ten on the east side with the gate. At 7½ feet the fence is taller than a man, so someone outside could not look in over the top.

The court from above, with one gate

Fifty cubits across, 150 feet long. The gate is 20 cubits wide, about 30 feet, with fifteen cubits of fence on either side. It is wide, but it is the only one.

One gate, wide open

The fence was plain white linen, but the gate was “of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen, wrought with needlework”: the same colours as the door and the vail, embroidered, on four pillars. The gate was the best-looking thing on the fence, and it was the way in.

“I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”

John 10:9

“For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;”

1 Timothy 2:5

One gate: not a different one for each man's religion or each man's past. But thirty feet wide, with no one posted to turn a man away. “If any man enter in,” says Jesus. Any man.

Part three

The lamp that burns always

Verses 20–21

Every evening, a man tends the light until the morning.

Oil, a watch through the night

  • pure oil olive beatenoil from olives crushed by pounding: clean oil, free of dregs
  • alwaysnot “all the time,” but every night: it is lit “from evening to morning”
  • order itset it in order: trim the wicks, fill the lamps
  • tabernacle of the congregationthe tent where the people's God meets them (“tent of meeting”)

Aaron and his sons keep it. “A statute for ever unto their generations.” Every night, through the dark, there is a man on duty so that the light does not go out. When the whole camp is asleep, somebody is awake before the LORD.

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Jesus “ever liveth.” He is the one who never leaves the watch.

Real world: the altar, the gate and the night shift

  • Start at the altarDon't try to get to the light by another road. Begin where God began: with the cross, not with your own cleaning up.
  • Come by the gateThere is one way in, and it is open to you today. You do not have to climb the fence.
  • The night is the hard partLate, alone, tired: that is when a man slips. The lamp was tended through exactly those hours. Keep a brother's number where you can reach it, and call before, not after.

Oil was needed every day, and a man had to bring it. A little time with God every day will do more than a big effort now and then. Have your oil ready before the dark comes.

Points to remember

  1. The altar“an altar of shittim wood, five cubits long, and five cubits broad… and overlay it with brass” 27:1–2Wood covered with bronze, 7½ feet square: the first thing a man meets at God's house.
  2. The carrying“Hollow with boards shalt thou make it: as it was shewed thee in the mount” 27:8Hollow, light, with poles in rings. It travels where the people go, made to God's pattern.
  3. The court“The length of the court shall be an hundred cubits, and the breadth fifty every where” 27:18150 by 75 feet, a fence around the tent.
  4. The walls“hangings for the court of fine twined linen… the hooks of the pillars and their fillets of silver” 27:9–10White linen on pillars with silver hooks, in bronze sockets: plain and clean around the holy place.
  5. The gate“for the gate of the court shall be an hanging of twenty cubits, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen” 27:16One gate, wide, in royal colours. Jesus: “I am the door.”
  6. The lamp“pure oil olive beaten for the light, to cause the lamp to burn always” 27:20The light was tended every night. Jesus ever lives to intercede for you.

Question 1 of 6

The altar

How big was the altar, and what was it covered with?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Five cubits long and broad, foursquare, three high (about 7½ by 7½ by 4½ feet), of shittim wood, with horns on the four corners, all overlaid with brass. The first thing in God's house is a place of sacrifice.

Question 2 of 6

The carrying

How was the altar to be carried, and what was it made like?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Staves of wood covered with brass, put through rings at the corners, carried on both sides. The altar was hollow, made of boards, “as it was shewed thee in the mount.” God's pattern, made to travel with his people.

Question 3 of 6

The court

How long and wide was the court of the tabernacle?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A hundred cubits long and fifty wide, five cubits high: about 150 by 75 feet, with a fence 7½ feet tall.

Question 4 of 6

The walls

What were the court's walls, pillars and sockets made of?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Hangings of fine twined linen, on pillars in sockets of brass, with hooks and fillets of silver. White cloth, bronze below, silver above.

Question 5 of 6

The gate

What hung at the gate of the court?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”

John 10:9

Answer

A twenty-cubit hanging of blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen, embroidered. It is the one gate, wide and bright. Jesus says: “I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved.”

Question 6 of 6

The lamp

What oil kept the lamp burning, and who tended it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Pure olive oil, beaten, brought by the people. Aaron and his sons kept the lamp from evening to morning. Jesus never leaves the watch: “he ever liveth to make intercession for them” (Hebrews 7:25).
