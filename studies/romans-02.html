The man who points the finger

Paul turns from the man who ran wild to the decent, religious man who judges him, and shows that he needs the gospel too.

Chapter 1 was the man who ran from God and fell all the way down. Reading it, a decent man might nod along: that's those people.

Chapter 2 turns and looks him in the eye. By chapter 3 every man is standing in the same place, and that's where the good news starts.

Part one

Thou art inexcusable

Verses 1–11

The man who judges, the kindness of God, and a Judge who plays no favorites.

One finger out, three back

Now Paul turns to the man in the front row who's been shaking his head at chapter 1: “O man, whosoever thou art that judgest.”

His problem isn't that he sees sin. It's that “thou that judgest doest the same things”: maybe not the same act, but the same heart, kept indoors. Judging others is often how we hide from our own stuff.

“Thou art the man”

Romans 1:20, the wild man

“so that they are without excuse”

Romans 2:1, the decent man

“Therefore thou art inexcusable, O man”

In Greek it's the same word, anapologētos: no defense. The man in the gutter and the man in the pew get the same verdict.

King David once heard of a rich man who stole a poor man's only lamb, and he exploded: that man deserves to die! Then the prophet looked at the king, who had taken another man's wife:

“And Nathan said to David, Thou art the man.”

2 Samuel 12:7

Why God waits

Goodness is His kindness. Forbearance is holding back the punishment we've earned. Longsuffering is patience that goes on and on.

Despisest means taking it for granted: I got away with it, so God must not care. But His patience isn't Him looking away. It's a door held open.

Repentance is a change of mind that turns a man around. And what does the turning? Not a scare, but “the goodness of God.”

Two ways to use God's patience

His patience, received→“leadeth thee to repentance”Every extra day is a chance to turn around (v. 4).
His patience, despised→“treasurest up… wrath”Every extra day is another deposit in the wrong account (v. 5).

Hardness is a heart like callused skin: it stops feeling. Impenitent means un-sorry, unwilling to repent. A man like that thinks he's saving up time. He's saving up judgment.

If you still feel the sting when you do wrong, thank God. That's His kindness keeping your heart soft. Turn today.

Judged by what we do?

verse 7

“patient continuance in well doing”

seeks God's glory, and gets “eternal life”

verse 8

“contentious… obey unrighteousness”

fights the truth, and gets “indignation and wrath”

Is Paul saying we're saved by good works? No. He's showing that God's judgment is fair: He goes by what is really there, not by a man's talk or his family or his church. Could anyone pass that test on his own? Chapter 3 answers: “There is none righteous, no, not one” (3:10).

The man who trusts Christ isn't condemned for his sins (8:1). And a changed life, slow and patient, is what real faith looks like from the outside.

No favorites

Respect of persons means favoritism. The Greek pictures receiving a face: judging a man by who he is, not what he's done.

A good lawyer or a famous name can get a man off here. Not with God. The Jew had more light, so he's first in line for blessing and for judgment (vv. 9–10), but nobody gets a pass.

It cuts both ways: your record doesn't put you beneath God's notice, and nobody's résumé puts him above it. The ground is level at the cross.

Part two

Written in their hearts

Verses 12–16

What about the man who never had a Bible? He had a conscience.

Justified

Justify is a courtroom word. It doesn't mean to make excuses. It means the judge looks at the case and declares: this man is in the right.

  • to excuse what you didGod never calls sin okay
  • to pretend it never happenedthe Judge knows every secret (v. 16)
  • to make you a better man firstthat change follows, it doesn't come first
  • the Judge declares you in the righta verdict, not a makeover

By the law, that verdict goes only to the doer who keeps it all. Hearing sermons doesn't count. Nobody qualifies (3:20). So how can a guilty man ever hear it? Paul will answer: “being justified freely by his grace” (3:24).

The voice inside

The Gentiles never got the Ten Commandments, but they still knew stealing and murder were wrong. God wrote His law on every heart, and gave every man a conscience: an inner witness that knows what he's done.

accusingYou shouldn't have said that to her. You know it was wrong.

excusingShe had it coming. Anybody would have done the same.

Every man has heard that argument at 2 a.m. Don't drown out the accusing voice; bring it to God. “The secrets of men” will be judged “by Jesus Christ,” who died for those secrets and forgives every one brought to Him.

Part three

A Jew inwardly

Verses 17–29

The religious man with the perfect résumé, and the one question that undoes it.

A religious résumé

  1. “called a Jew”God's own people
  2. “restest in the law”leans on the Bible he owns
  3. “makest thy boast of God”proud to know God
  4. “knowest his will”knows right from wrong
  5. “approvest the things… excellent”can tell the best
  6. “a guide of the blind”leads others
  7. “a light of them… in darkness”the example
  8. “a teacher of babes”teaches beginners

Every line is true, and every line is a gift from God. Israel really had the Scriptures, the promises and the knowledge of God. The trouble is what the man does with it.

Put your own name in. Grew up in church, can quote verses, knows the steps, tells the new guys what to do. Good things. None of them saves you.

Teachest thou not thyself?

“a man should not steal”dost thou steal?The preacher who skims a little off the top.
“a man should not commit adultery”dost thou?The man who condemns cheaters and cheats in his heart (Matthew 5:28).
“thou that abhorrest idols”sacrilege?Sacrilege here is robbing temples: hating idols, but happy to profit off them.

Verse 24 is the cost: “the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you.” People judge God by the men who claim Him. Your crew at work is reading your life. Let it give them a reason to respect His name.

A sign without the real thing

Circumcision was the mark God gave Abraham's family (Genesis 17): the foreskin cut away, done to every Jewish boy at eight days old. It marked him as one of God's covenant people, and many Jews trusted that mark to get them through the judgment.

the sign, with no obedience“a breaker of the law”
→
counts for nothing“made uncircumcision”

A wedding ring on a man who's cheating is just metal. The sign is only worth something when the real thing is there.

It's the same with baptism today. Baptism is a believer's step of obedience that pictures his salvation. It doesn't save him. A man can go under the water and come up unchanged.

What God is after

outwardly · v. 28

“in the flesh”

a mark on the body, the letter of the rules, and the “praise of men”

inwardly · v. 29

“of the heart”

a heart cut free from sin, the Spirit at work, and “praise… of God”

God doesn't want a man who just looks right. He wants a new man. And He doesn't ask you to carve your own heart into shape. He promised to do it:

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

Points to remember

  1. Judging“Thou condemnest thyself” 2:1The finger we point at others has three pointing back. Deal with your own heart first.
  2. Kindness“The goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance” 2:4God's patience is a door held open. Let His kindness turn you around.
  3. A hard heart“Treasurest up unto thyself wrath” 2:5Refusing to repent doesn't save up time. It saves up judgment.
  4. A fair Judge“There is no respect of persons with God” 2:11No one buys his way past God, and no one is beneath His notice.
  5. Conscience“The work of the law written in their hearts” 2:15Every man knows right from wrong. Bring the accusing voice to God.
  6. The inside“Circumcision is that of the heart” 2:29God wants a changed heart, not a religious show. And He gives the new heart.

Question 1 of 6

Judging

What happens when we judge others for the same things we do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

We condemn ourselves. Pointing at someone else's sin doesn't hide ours from God; it proves we know right from wrong and do wrong anyway. The decent man is just as inexcusable as the wild one, and needs the same Savior.

Question 2 of 6

Kindness

Why is God so patient with us?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

To lead us to repentance. His patience isn't Him ignoring sin; it's time He gives us to turn around. He doesn't want anyone to perish. His kindness, not fear, is what turns a man's heart back to Him.

Question 3 of 6

A hard heart

What is a man storing up when he will not repent?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Wrath. Every day a hard heart refuses God's kindness, it adds to what it will face on the day of judgment. The same patience that could have led him home becomes evidence against him. That's why today is the day to turn.

Question 4 of 6

A fair Judge

Does God play favorites?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. He judges every man by the truth, Jew and Gentile alike. No name, money or religion buys a pass, and no past puts a man beneath His notice. That's bad news for the proud and good news for the outsider.

Question 5 of 6

Conscience

What has God written on every man's heart?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The work of His law. Even without a Bible, men know right from wrong, and the conscience argues inside them, accusing or excusing. Don't silence it. Let it bring you to Jesus, who judges the secrets of men and forgives every one brought to Him.

Question 6 of 6

The inside

What kind of change does God care about most?

What verses tell us about this?

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

Answer

A changed heart. Outward marks, rituals and a good reputation can't make a man right with God. He wants the inside, and He gives a new heart by His Spirit to everyone who trusts Christ.
