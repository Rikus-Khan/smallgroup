Leviticus 19

Love thy neighbour as thyself

“Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy.” Then the LORD shows what holy looks like on a Tuesday: in the field, at the pay window, at the scales, with the deaf man, the blind man, the old man and the stranger. And in the middle of it, the words Jesus called the second great commandment.

Part one

“Ye shall be holy”

Verses 1–8

The whole chapter hangs on one sentence. Then it starts at home: father, mother, sabbath, idols, and a meal with God.

Holy, because he is

Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy.

Holy means set apart: different, clean, belonging to God. Not stiff, not churchy. Notice who it is said to: “all the congregation.” Not just the priests. Every man in the camp.

“But as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation; Because it is written, Be ye holy; for I am holy.”

1 Peter 1:15–16

Peter quotes this chapter to Christians. Conversation means your whole way of life. And the rest of Leviticus 19 shows what that looks like: not a feeling in church, but how you work, pay, talk and treat people.

The ten, walked out

Read chapter 19 next to the ten commandments (Exodus 20), and you keep running into them:

  • No other gods, no idolsverse 4
  • Not the name in vainverse 12
  • The sabbathverses 3, 30
  • Father and motherverse 3
  • Not killverse 16, “the blood of thy neighbour”
  • Not adulteryverses 20, 29
  • Not stealverses 11, 13
  • Not lieverses 11, 16
  • Not covetverses 17–18, the heart

Here, fear your mother and father means honour them, take them seriously. Mother comes first in verse 3. And molten gods are idols cast from melted metal, like the golden calf. Chapter 19 takes the ten off the stone tables and walks them into the field, the shop and the street.

A meal with God, on his terms

  • peace offeringsthe offering a man shared as a meal before the LORD (chapter 3, 7:15–18)
  • at your own willfreely, because you want to
  • oughtanything
  • abominablefoul; here, spoiled meat God will not accept
  • profaned the hallowed thingtreated what was holy as common, as leftovers

Two days, then burn what is left. God wanted the meal fresh and eaten while the offering was still close in mind. Fellowship with God is not something you keep in the fridge for later in the week. Come to him freely, and come today.

Part two

“Thou shalt love thy neighbour”

Verses 9–18

The poor, the hired man, the deaf, the blind, the man on trial, and the brother you are angry with.

Leave the corners

To glean is to pick up what the reapers missed. Gleanings are those leftover ears of grain and grapes. The farmer was not to go back over his field for every last bit.

“And she went, and came, and gleaned in the field after the reapers: and her hap was to light on a part of the field belonging unto Boaz, who was of the kindred of Elimelech.”

Ruth 2:3

That is how Ruth, a poor stranger, ate, and how she met Boaz. Notice: the poor still came and worked for it. God gave the needy a way to eat with dignity, and gave the farmer a way to trust him with less than everything.

The man who can't fight back

  • deal falselycheat, double-cross
  • defraudcheat a man out of what is his
  • the wages… all nightpay the day labourer the same day; he needs it to eat
  • curse the deafhe can't hear what you say about him
  • a stumblingblock before the blindhe can't see what you put in his way

Each of these is a sin you could get away with. The deaf man will never know. The blind man won't see who did it. So the LORD adds, “but shalt fear thy God”: he hears and sees what they can't.

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28

A man who used to take now works so he can give. That is what grace does to a thief.

The courtroom and the grapevine

  • respect the persontake sides because of who a man is, rich or poor, instead of what is right
  • talebearera man who carries stories about people from house to house: a gossip
  • stand against the blood of thy neighbourstand up to have him killed, or stand by while his life is in danger

“A talebearer revealeth secrets: but he that is of a faithful spirit concealeth the matter.”

Proverbs 11:13

In a group like this one, men tell the truth about themselves. What is said in the room stays in the room. A man who carries it out the door is a talebearer, and he breaks the trust that lets broken men get well.

Say it to his face, not behind his back

Thou shalt not

hate him in thine heart

Smile at him and burn inside. Avenge. Bear a grudge. Let him go on in sin and say nothing.

Thou shalt

rebuke him, and love him

In any wise: by all means. Go to him. Tell him. And do not suffer sin upon him: don't let him carry it.

“Moreover if thy brother shall trespass against thee, go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone: if he shall hear thee, thou hast gained thy brother.”

Matthew 18:15

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

If you see your brother slipping back, silence is not love. Go to him, gently, alone, knowing it could be you next.

“As thyself”

Five times in six verses the chapter says thy neighbour (verses 13, 15, 16, 17, 18). Then verse 18 gathers it up. Jesus called it the second great commandment (Matthew 22:39), and Paul said it holds the whole law:

“For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this; Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.”

Galatians 5:14

A lawyer once asked Jesus, “And who is my neighbour?” (Luke 10:29). Jesus told of a Samaritan who stopped for a stranger beaten on the road, and then asked:

“Which now of these three, thinkest thou, was neighbour unto him that fell among the thieves? And he said, He that shewed mercy on him. Then said Jesus unto him, Go, and do thou likewise.”

Luke 10:36–37

You know how to look after yourself: you feed yourself, you make excuses for yourself, you give yourself another chance. Do that for the man next to you.

Part three

“Ye shall keep my statutes”

Verses 19–29

Mixed seed, a slave girl, young fruit trees, and the customs of the nations around them.

Keep the kinds apart

  • genderbreed (here a verb)
  • a diverse kinda different kind of animal
  • mingled seedtwo kinds of seed sown in one field
  • linen and woollencloth woven of the two together

The verse gives no reason except its first words: “Ye shall keep my statutes.” We won't invent one. Israel kept these because the LORD said so.

Some obedience a man understands. Some he only does, because he trusts the One who said it. Both are faith.

“Shall be forgiven him”

A bondmaid is a slave girl; betrothed means promised in marriage. She was not free, so they were not put to death; she was scourged, whipped. But the man had to bring a ram to the door of the tabernacle for a trespass offering.

And then the best words in the chapter: “the sin which he hath done shall be forgiven him.” Not by time, not by feeling sorry long enough, but by blood at the altar.

“In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace;”

Ephesians 1:7

Wait for the fruit

  1. year 1uncircumcisednot eaten
  2. year 2uncircumcisednot eaten
  3. year 3uncircumcisednot eaten
  4. year 4holyto praise the LORD
  5. year 5eat“that it may yield unto you”

Uncircumcised fruit here means fruit not yet set apart, not yet to be touched. Three years of waiting, one year for God, and then the harvest is theirs. God gets the first, and the man who waits gets the increase.

Recovery is a young tree. The fruit doesn't come the first season. Keep planting. Give him the first of it. The increase comes.

The ways of the nations

  • with the bloodmeat with the blood still in it; the blood was for the altar (17:11)
  • enchantmentcharms and spells
  • observe timeslucky and unlucky days, telling the future by signs and seasons
  • round the corners of your headscut the hair round at the sides, as the nations did
  • mar the corners of thy beardcut it off at the edges

“For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.”

Leviticus 17:11

Moses gives no long explanation of the hair and the beard. The next verse and Deuteronomy 14 show what went with such things: the mourning customs of the nations for their dead.

Cuttings and marks

Read the verse as it stands: no cuttings, gashes in the skin, “for the dead,” and no marks printed on the body. The nations cut themselves in grief, and to call on their gods. Moses says it again, and gives the reason:

“Ye are the children of the LORD your God: ye shall not cut yourselves, nor make any baldness between your eyes for the dead. For thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God, and the LORD hath chosen thee to be a peculiar people unto himself, above all the nations that are upon the earth.”

Deuteronomy 14:1–2

“And they cried aloud, and cut themselves after their manner with knives and lancets, till the blood gushed out upon them.”

1 Kings 18:28

That is the prophets of Baal on mount Carmel. A child of the LORD did not grieve like that, and did not mark his body the way the nations did. He belonged to the living God.

No man is shut out by his skin

Many of us already carry ink: a name, a date, a gang, a loss, a night we don't remember. Some of it was grief. Some of it was the old life. None of it is a wall between you and God.

“But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

If you are thinking of a new one, bring this verse and an honest “why?” to the Lord and to a brother before you go. And when you grieve, grieve as a man with hope: “that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope” (1 Thessalonians 4:13).

Part four

“I am the LORD your God”

Verses 30–37

Daughters, the sabbath, the old man, the stranger, and an honest scale.

Protect, rest, and don't go seeking

  • prostitute thy daughtersell her body for money (verse 29). A father protects her
  • reverence my sanctuarytreat God's house with honour (verse 30)
  • familiar spiritsspirits that mediums claimed to call up, to speak with the dead
  • wizardsmen who claimed secret knowledge of what is hidden

“And when they shall say unto you, Seek unto them that have familiar spirits, and unto wizards that peep, and that mutter: should not a people seek unto their God? for the living to the dead?”

Isaiah 8:19

The psychic line, the cards, the horoscope: it is the same old seeking. When you need to know what's next, ask the living God, and ask him first.

Stand up for the grey head

The hoary head is grey or white hair. To rise up before him is to stand when an old man comes in, out of respect.

“The hoary head is a crown of glory, if it be found in the way of righteousness.”

Proverbs 16:31

A lot of us spent years thinking old men had nothing to tell us. The older brother who has walked with Christ thirty years has a map of the road you're on. Honour him: ask him, listen, and stand up when he walks in.

The stranger

A stranger is a foreigner living among Israel. To vex him is to trouble him, push him around. Verse 18 said love thy neighbour; verse 34 says the same of the outsider: “thou shalt love him as thyself.” And the reason is their own story.

“Love ye therefore the stranger: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt.”

Deuteronomy 10:19

You remember your first day in a room like this: not knowing anybody, not sure you belonged. The new man who walks in today is the stranger. You were him. Go and sit by him.

Honest measures

A meteyard is a measuring rod, for length. An ephah measured dry grain, about half a bushel; a hin, oil and wine, about a gallon. Balances and weights weighed everything else.

“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.”

Proverbs 11:1

Today it is the time card, the tape measure, the receipt, the deal you shake on. The God “which brought you out” is the God of the honest scale.

Fifteen times: “I am the LORD”

Verse 2 says why: “I the LORD your God am holy.” Then fifteen times the chapter signs off a command with “I am the LORD” (seven of them “I am the LORD your God”):

3×1 4×1 10×1 12×1 14×1 16×1 18×1 25×1 28×1 30×1 31×1 32×1 34×1 36×1 37×1

Paying a man on time, leaving the corners, standing for the old man: each one ends with God's own name on it. There is no part of an ordinary day that he isn't Lord of.

Real world: holy on a Tuesday

  • SinMost of this chapter is ordinary sin: shading the truth, holding a grudge, gossip, cheating a little. God calls it what it is.
  • RegretIf you stole, “steal no more”: work, and give. If you hurt a brother, go to him today.
  • Grace“The sin which he hath done shall be forgiven him” (verse 22). The blood of Jesus is the trespass offering that never runs out.
  • Hope“Ye shall be holy” is not only a command. Said by the LORD who sanctifies, it is a promise of what he is making you.

Today: pick one neighbour from this chapter, the new man, an old man, someone you owe, a brother you're sore at, and love him as yourself in one concrete way before you sleep.

Points to remember

  1. Holy“Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy.” 19:2We are set apart because he is. Holy shows in how we work, pay, talk and treat people.
  2. The corners“thou shalt leave them for the poor and stranger” 19:10Leave the corners and the gleanings. God gave the poor a way to eat with dignity.
  3. A brother“thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him” 19:17Not a grudge in the heart: go to him, gently, alone.
  4. Thy neighbour“thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the LORD” 19:18The second great commandment. All the law is fulfilled in it.
  5. The marks“Ye shall not make any cuttings in your flesh for the dead, nor print any marks upon you” 19:28The nations cut themselves for the dead. God's people grieve with hope, and no man is shut out by his skin.
  6. Just weights“Just balances, just weights, a just ephah, and a just hin, shall ye have” 19:36The God who brought them out is the God of the honest scale.

Question 1 of 6

Holy

Why must God's people be holy?

What verses tell us about this?

“But as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation; Because it is written, Be ye holy; for I am holy.”

1 Peter 1:15–16

Answer

Because the LORD our God is holy. His people are set apart to him, and it shows in every part of life, “all manner of conversation.”

Question 2 of 6

The corners

What were the farmers to leave in the field, and for whom?

What verses tell us about this?

“And she went, and came, and gleaned in the field after the reapers: and her hap was to light on a part of the field belonging unto Boaz, who was of the kindred of Elimelech.”

Ruth 2:3

Answer

The corners of the field, the gleanings, and the fallen grapes, left for the poor and the stranger, like Ruth in the field of Boaz.

Question 3 of 6

A brother

What does the LORD say to do instead of hating your brother in your heart?

What verses tell us about this?

“Moreover if thy brother shall trespass against thee, go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone: if he shall hear thee, thou hast gained thy brother.”

Matthew 18:15

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Answer

Rebuke him: go and tell him, and don't let him carry his sin alone. Not a grudge, not gossip, not revenge, but a gentle word face to face.

Question 4 of 6

Thy neighbour

How does the LORD sum up the way to treat your neighbour?

What verses tell us about this?

“For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this; Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.”

Galatians 5:14

Answer

“Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself,” and the stranger too. Jesus called it the second great commandment, and Paul says all the law is fulfilled in it.

Question 5 of 6

The marks

What did the LORD say about cuttings and marks in the flesh, and what were they for?

What verses tell us about this?

“Ye are the children of the LORD your God: ye shall not cut yourselves, nor make any baldness between your eyes for the dead.”

Deuteronomy 14:1

“But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

Answer

Not to make them. They were the nations' customs of mourning “for the dead”; God's children belonged to him and grieved with hope. And the LORD looks on the heart: no man is shut out by his skin.

Question 6 of 6

Just weights

What does the LORD say about weights and measures?

What verses tell us about this?

“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.”

Proverbs 11:1

Answer

Keep them just: honest length, weight and measure. The God who brought Israel out of Egypt delights in an honest scale, and so does honest work today.
