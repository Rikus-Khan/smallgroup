Malachi 3

I change not

God promises a messenger, then the Lord Himself, coming like a refiner's fire. He calls His people to come back to Him, and He keeps a book of those who do.

Things to watch for

Malachi spoke about 430 BC, to the Jews back home after the exile. Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

My messenger

Verses 1–6

“Where is the God of judgment?” they asked. God answers: He is coming, and you may not be ready.

Two who are coming

Verse 1 promises two arrivals. First a messenger who gets the road ready. Then “the Lord” Himself, coming to His own temple. About four hundred years later, Jesus told the crowds exactly who the first one was:

Malachi 3:1

Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me

Matthew 11:10, about John the Baptist

For this is he, of whom it is written, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face, which shall prepare thy way before thee.

The messenger is John the Baptist. And the Lord he prepared the way for is Jesus. In Malachi, God says “before me.” Jesus applies it to Himself: “before thy face.” The Lord who came to His temple was God the Son.

Careful what you ask for

My messenger“prepare the way”John the BaptistMatthew 11:10
The Lord“the messenger of the covenant”Jesus“suddenly come to his temple”

The people said they wanted the God of judgment to show up (2:17). They said they longed for Him: “whom ye seek … whom ye delight in.” God says: He is coming. But who may abide the day of his coming?

Malachi saw the coming of the Lord as one day. Looking back, we can see it in two parts: Jesus came first to save, and He will come again to judge. Verses 2–5 reach toward both.

The refiner's fire

A refiner heats silver until it melts. The impurities, called dross, rise to the top, and he skims them off. He sits and watches it closely, keeping the heat steady until the job is done.

What's left is pure. The fire was never meant to destroy the silver. It was meant to get the junk out of it.

“Fullers' sope” was a strong lye soap. A fuller scrubbed and beat new cloth to clean and whiten it. Fire and soap: both hurt, and both make clean.

In the fire

Look at the goal: “that they may offer … an offering in righteousness.” God purifies His people so they can serve Him with clean hands again. The priests who brought Him sick lambs in chapter 1 would one day bring Him what was right.

Recovery can feel like that crucible. The heat brings up things you'd rather keep hidden: resentments, lies, old habits. God isn't trying to burn you up. He's skimming off the dross, and He sits right beside the fire the whole time.

“That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ:”

1 Peter 1:7

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

A swift witness

Remember, they asked “Where is the God of judgment?” Here He is. And the list He brings to court is mostly about how people treat people:

  1. sorcerersthose who deal in spells and spirits
  2. adulterersthose who break the marriage covenant
  3. false swearersthose who lie under oath
  4. wage thieves“oppress the hireling in his wages”
  5. the helpless crushedthe widow, the fatherless, the stranger

And the root of all of it: “and fear not me.” When a man stops fearing God, it shows up in how he treats the people who can't fight back.

I am the LORD, I change not.

The sons of Jacob

Always changing

“Even from the days of your fathers ye are gone away” (verse 7). Faithful one year, gone the next.

The LORD

Never changes

He made promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and He keeps them. That's the only reason Israel was still standing.

Read the “therefore” carefully. They weren't wiped out because they were good. They were spared because God is faithful. That's good news for a believer, too: your security rests on His faithfulness, not yours.

“Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever.”

Hebrews 13:8

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Part two

Return unto me

Verses 7–12

God holds the door open. When they ask how to come back, He points to their wallets.

The door is open

After everything in chapters 1 and 2, God still says, “Return unto me, and I will return unto you.” That's the heart of the whole book.

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

The peopleWherein shall we return?
“we never left”

The LORDWill a man rob God? Yet ye have robbed me.

They didn't think they had gone anywhere. So God gets specific. Coming back to God is never vague. It starts with one honest, concrete thing.

What were the tithes?

A “tithe” means a tenth. Under the law of Moses, Israel gave a tenth of its crops and herds to support the Levites, who served at the temple and had no farmland of their own.

“And, behold, I have given the children of Levi all the tenth in Israel for an inheritance, for their service which they serve, even the service of the tabernacle of the congregation.”

Numbers 18:21

In Malachi's day the people stopped bringing it. Nehemiah found out what happened next:

“And I perceived that the portions of the Levites had not been given them: for the Levites and the singers, that did the work, were fled every one to his field. Then contended I with the rulers, and said, Why is the house of God forsaken? And I gathered them together, and set them in their place. Then brought all Judah the tithe of the corn and the new wine and the oil unto the treasuries.”

Nehemiah 13:10–12

Keeping it back wasn't just stingy. It shut down the worship of God and starved the men who served Him.

What this promise is, and isn't

This was a promise to the nation of Israel, living on its farms under the law. “The windows of heaven” meant rain. “The devourer” meant locusts and pests. Bring what you owe, God said, and watch Me send the rain and save the harvest.

  • “Give ten, get back a hundred.”God isn't a slot machine, and giving isn't an investment scheme
  • “If you're broke, you didn't give enough.”plenty of faithful believers are poor (James 2:5)
  • God keeps His word, and He can be trusted with what we have.“Prove me now herewith”

The promise belonged to Israel. The principle is for us: God is no man's debtor, and He takes care of those who put Him first.

“But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.”

Matthew 6:33

Giving today

Under the law

A tenth, required

Brought to the storehouse to support the Levites and the temple, and to help the poor.

In Christ

From the heart

Regular, willing, and generous, to support the church, its work, and people in need.

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7

For a lot of us, money used to go one place: the bottle, the pipe, the habit. Giving to God, even a little, retrains the heart. Jesus said it plainly:

“For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”

Matthew 6:21

Part three

A book of remembrance

Verses 13–18

Some said serving God was pointless. Others kept going, and God wrote their names down.

“What's the point?”

The LORDYour words have been stout against me.

The peopleWhat have we spoken so much against thee?

The LORDYe have said, It is vain to serve God.

“Vain” means empty, for nothing. They looked at the proud and the wicked doing well, and decided obeying God doesn't pay. A man in recovery hears that voice too: Why stay clean? The guys still partying look fine.

“For I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked.”

Psalm 73:3

“Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end.”

Psalm 73:17

God was listening

While some complained, a few men who feared the LORD kept talking to each other: encouraging, reminding, holding on. They probably thought nobody noticed. God “hearkened, and heard it,” and had it written down.

Kings in those days kept a written record of men who had served them well, so they could be rewarded (Esther 6:1–3). God keeps one too. Not one word spoken in faith is lost.

“But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

Hebrews 3:13

My jewels

“Jewels” here means a king's own treasure, what he keeps for himself. God says of the men who feared Him: “they shall be mine.” He would spare them like a father spares his own son.

Was serving God pointless?No“Then shall ye return, and discern between the righteous and the wicked” (verse 18). One day the difference will be plain to everyone.
Does God forget the faithful?NeverTheir names are written before Him. Jesus told His disciples to rejoice in that above everything else.

“Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.”

Luke 10:20

Points to remember

  1. The messenger“Behold, I will send my messenger” 3:1The messenger was John the Baptist, and the Lord who came after him was Jesus.
  2. The refiner“He shall sit as a refiner and purifier of silver” 3:3God's fire isn't meant to destroy His people. It takes the dross out.
  3. God never changes“I am the LORD, I change not” 3:6We're still standing because He is faithful, not because we are.
  4. Coming back“Return unto me, and I will return unto you” 3:7The door is open. Coming back starts with one honest, concrete step.
  5. Giving“Will a man rob God?” 3:8Holding back from God starves His work. Give from the heart, and trust Him. It's not a formula for getting rich.
  6. Remembered“A book of remembrance was written before him” 3:16God hears every word spoken in faith. Those who fear Him are His treasure.

Question 1 of 6

The messenger

Who is the messenger God promised to send?

What verses tell us about this?

“For this is he, of whom it is written, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face, which shall prepare thy way before thee.”

Matthew 11:10

“As it is written in the prophets, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face, which shall prepare thy way before thee.”

Mark 1:2

Answer

John the Baptist. Jesus said so Himself. John prepared the way, and then the Lord came to His temple: Jesus, God the Son. The God who says “before me” in Malachi is the One who came in the flesh.

Question 2 of 6

The refiner

Why does God put His people through the fire?

What verses tell us about this?

“That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ:”

1 Peter 1:7

Answer

To purify, not to destroy. A refiner heats silver so the dross comes up and can be skimmed off, and he watches it the whole time. God uses hard seasons the same way, so His people can serve Him with clean hands. The fire is never the end of the story.

Question 3 of 6

God never changes

Why is it good news that God never changes?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever.”

Hebrews 13:8

“Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.”

James 1:17

Answer

Because we change all the time, and He doesn't. Israel wasn't wiped out because God keeps His promises. A believer's salvation rests on that same faithfulness. What He promised you in Christ, He will not take back.

Question 4 of 6

Coming back

How does a man return to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.”

James 4:8

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

Answer

He turns around and comes, and finds God already coming to meet him. When Judah asked “Wherein shall we return?”, God named one concrete thing. Coming back isn't vague. Ask Him what the next honest step is, and take it.

Question 5 of 6

Giving

What does it mean to rob God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7

Answer

It meant keeping back what God's law said belonged to Him, so the temple went empty and the Levites went hungry. We aren't under that law, but the heart issue is the same. Give to God's work willingly and generously, and trust Him. It isn't a deal to get rich. It's putting Him first.

Question 6 of 6

Remembered

Does God notice the men who keep fearing Him when it seems pointless?

What verses tell us about this?

“Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.”

Luke 10:20

Answer

Yes. While others said “it is vain to serve God,” a few kept encouraging each other, and God heard every word and wrote it down. They are His treasure. Showing up, praying, and helping a brother hold on is never wasted.
