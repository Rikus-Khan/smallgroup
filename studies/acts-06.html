Part one

Seven to serve

Verses 1–7

The church is growing fast, and some people are falling through the cracks. How it handles that says a lot.

Two kinds of believers, one blind spot

The Hebrews

Spoke Aramaic and Hebrew

Jews who grew up in Judaea and Galilee. The twelve apostles were among them.

The Grecians

Spoke Greek

Jews who had lived in other lands, or whose families had, and now lived in Jerusalem. Some may have come for Pentecost and stayed.

The church was feeding its poor widows every day, and the widows of the Greek-speaking group were being missed. Nobody says it was on purpose. A fast-growing group has blind spots, and the ones who get missed are the ones nobody on the inside looks like.

Widows had no paycheck and no safety net. This wasn't a small thing. And the church's answer was not to ignore it, argue about whose fault it was, or split. They brought it out into the open.

What the twelve were saying, and what they weren't

They were not saying that feeding widows is beneath them. Look at the words: the widows were neglected in the daily ministration (verse 1), and the apostles will give themselves to the ministry of the word (verse 4). It's the same word, diakonia, service. Both are ministry.

They didn't just hand the job off. They said how to choose: “look ye out among you”, the people themselves pick. And they set three marks for the seven:

  1. Honest reporta good name, known to the people. A name is built a day at a time.
  2. Full of the Holy Ghosta man led by the Spirit, not by his moods.
  3. Wisdomgood sense. Handling food and money fairly takes it.

Notice what's missing: no famous names, no big speakers. For a man starting over, there's good news here. “Honest report” is something you earn back by how you live, day after day, in small things.

Seven names, all of them Greek

  • Stephen“full of faith and of the Holy Ghost.” He is about to be the first martyr.
  • PhilipWill carry the gospel to Samaria in chapter 8.
  • ProchorusNever mentioned again.
  • NicanorNever mentioned again.
  • TimonNever mentioned again.
  • ParmenasNever mentioned again.
  • NicolasA proselyte, a Gentile who had converted to the Jewish faith, from Antioch.

Every name is Greek. The ones who had complained were handed the job of fixing it. The church put the people who had been overlooked in charge of making sure nobody was overlooked.

And four of the seven are names and nothing else. Nobody wrote a sermon about Prochorus. He served tables, faithfully, and God had it written down. Not every man's job is on the stage, and the quiet jobs count.

They laid their hands on them after prayer: a way of saying, we send you, and God's blessing is on you. It was a public setting-apart, not magic.

The church did not stall over its problem. It named it, shared the load, and kept going. “The word of God increased.” Trouble that is brought into the light can be fixed. Trouble that is hidden, as in Acts 5, rots.

One last surprise: “a great company of the priests were obedient to the faith.” These were the temple families, the same circle that had been jailing the apostles. Obedient to the faith: they believed, and followed. Nobody is too far on the wrong side to be reached.

Part two

Stephen

Verses 8–15

A table-server turns out to be a powerful witness. His enemies lose the argument, so they reach for lies.

Whose synagogue was it?

Jewish people from all over the Roman world had settled in Jerusalem and it seems each group had its own synagogue. The Libertines were freedmen, Jews whose families had been freed from slavery, in the Roman style, and many probably came from Rome. The Cyrenians and Alexandrians came from North Africa. Cilicia and Asia are in what is now Turkey.

Cilicia is where Tarsus is, the hometown of a young man named Saul. The text doesn't say he was in that room, but we are about to meet him, and he will not be far away.

They came to argue, and “they were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit by which he spake.” Stephen wasn't a trained debater. He was a man full of the Spirit who knew what he believed.

How to win without the truth

  1. lost the argument(6:10)
  2. suborned men(6:11)
  3. stirred up the people(6:12)
  4. caught him(6:12)
  5. false witnesses(6:13)

Suborned means secretly talked into it: bribed or coaxed to lie. When people lose an argument and won't give in, a lot of them go straight to lies and a mob. You may have been on either side of that.

The charge twisted something real. Jesus really had spoken about the temple, and that is what the lie was built on. Half-truths make the best lies: they carry a little truth to make them stick.

Last chapter, Ananias lied to look better than he was. Here men lie to make someone else look worse. It's the same sin from two directions, and Jesus called the devil a liar and “the father of it” (John 8:44). Both begin in the heart.

The same court, the same lies

Jesus before the council, Matthew 26:59–60

Now the chief priests, and elders, and all the council, sought false witness against Jesus, to put him to death; But found none: yea, though many false witnesses came, yet found they none. At the last came two false witnesses,

What Jesus had said, John 2:19

Jesus answered and said unto them, Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.

This is the same court that heard false witnesses against Jesus. And the charge about “this place” comes from His own words, which His enemies twisted: He had been talking about the temple of His body, and His rising again. Stephen is treated just as his Lord was.

If someone has lied about you, or you've been blamed for something you didn't do, you're not the first. Jesus knows exactly what that is. You don't have to answer lies with lies.

Calm in the middle of it

All around Stephen: a mob, lies, a courtroom full of men who wanted him dead. And his face looked like an angel's. Not afraid, not angry, and not hiding. Peace in a man who had nothing to cover up.

That's the other side of Acts 5. Ananias had a secret to guard, and it cost him everything. A man with a clear conscience can face a hard room. If yours isn't clear, today is a good day to clear it with God and with one honest man. Then, whatever is waiting at work, you can look people in the eye.

What comes next

  1. About AD 30 to 33Jesus is crucified, risen, and ascended; Pentecost (Acts 1–2).
  2. Acts 3 to 5The lame man walks, Ananias and Sapphira, the apostles beaten.
  3. Acts 6The seven are chosen. Stephen is seized and brought before the council.
  4. Acts 7Stephen's answer, and his death. A young man named Saul stands watching.
  5. About AD 34 to 35That same Saul is turned around on the Damascus road (Acts 9).

Hold on to that name for next time. The man who would later guard the coats of Stephen's killers would one day write much of the New Testament. The church only saw Stephen's death. God was already at work on Saul.

If you think of yourself by the worst thing you've done, remember the story isn't over. It wasn't for Saul, and it isn't for you.

Points to remember

  1. Complaint“Their widows were neglected” 6:1The overlooked got noticed, and the church dealt with it.
  2. Priorities“We will give ourselves continually to prayer” 6:4Share the work. Everybody serves, and everyone has their part.
  3. Character“Men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom” 6:3A good name is earned back day by day, and the Spirit leads.
  4. Growth“The word of God increased” 6:7Trouble brought into the light gets fixed.
  5. Lies“They set up false witnesses” 6:13When truth wins, liars get desperate. Don't answer lies with lies.
  6. His face“As it had been the face of an angel” 6:15A clear conscience gives a calm face.

Complaint

Who was being overlooked, and who complained?

What verses tell us about this?

James 1:27

Answer

The widows of the Greek-speaking believers were being missed in the daily food, so the Grecians complained against the Hebrews. Widows had no income, so it mattered. The church faced it and fixed it.

Question 2 of 6

Priorities

Why did the twelve not serve tables themselves?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not because serving was beneath them. Both jobs are ministry. They needed to give themselves to prayer and the word, so the work was shared and seven men took over the tables.

Question 3 of 6

Character

What kind of men were to be chosen?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom. The church picked on character, and not on fame. A good name is built a day at a time, and a man starting over can start today.

Question 5 of 6

Lies

How did Stephen's enemies come after him?

What verses tell us about this?
