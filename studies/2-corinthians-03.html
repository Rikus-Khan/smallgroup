2 Corinthians 3

Ye are our epistle

You are a letter written by the Spirit, and everyone you meet is reading you. And the One who is writing isn't finished with you yet.

Where we are

  1. End of chapter 2“And who is sufficient for these things?” (2:16)
  2. Chapter 3“Our sufficiency is of God” (3:5). The new covenant, and the Spirit who changes us.
  3. Chapter 4“We have this treasure in earthen vessels” (4:7)

Paul has just said that he carries the fragrance of Christ everywhere. Now critics are asking who gave him the right. His answer: you are the proof. Then he explains what makes a man able to serve at all.

Some of this chapter is deep, with Moses, a veil and two covenants. It is also some of the most hopeful writing in the Bible for a man who is tired of how slowly he changes.

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

Ye are our epistle

Verses 1–6

Paul's best reference letter is a changed life.

The letter everyone reads

an epistle of Christ

Written: not with ink, but by the Spirit of the living God

On: not stone, but a heart, and a changed life

Delivered by: us (“ministered by us”)

Who reads it

  • your kids
  • your boss
  • the guys at work
  • your old friends
  • your family

A traveling teacher in Paul's day often carried a letter of recommendation. Paul's enemies said he had none. His answer: the Corinthians are his letter. A church full of changed lives is proof enough.

Most people will never open a Bible, but they will read you: how you talk to your kids, whether you show up at work, what you do when someone cuts you off. It's a letter still being written, smudges and all.

Stone, or heart

“Tables of stone” are the Ten Commandments, “written with the finger of God” (Exodus 31:18).

The law was holy and good. But it stayed outside a man, telling him what to do and showing him he couldn't. God had promised something better, and the next slide has the promise.

God promised a new heart

“But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel; After those days, saith the LORD, I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people.”

Jeremiah 31:33

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

Both prophets spoke hundreds of years before Christ. Paul says the promise has now come true for everyone who trusts Jesus: forgiveness, and God's own Spirit inside.

A new heart isn't a self-improvement project. It's a gift. When the old heart pulls at you today, remember that you were given a new one, and the Spirit is writing on it.

Able, but not by ourselves

“the letter killeth”

A rule from outside

“Do this, don't do that.” The rules are right, but they can't give a man the power to keep them. They show him he's failed, and nothing more. Every man who has tried to quit on willpower alone knows it.

“the spirit giveth life”

A life from inside

God's own Spirit comes to live in the man, and He gives the want to and the power to. That's the new covenant: not a better list, but a new heart.

“Testament” here means covenant, an agreement God makes. The new one is forgiveness and the Spirit, received by faith in Christ.

And Paul admits he isn't sufficient: “Not that we are sufficient of ourselves.” God later told him why that's fine: “My grace is sufficient for thee” (12:9).

Part two

A glory that fades, and one that stays

Verses 7–11

The old way was glorious. The new way is much more.

Fading, and “remaineth”

“Ministration of death” is the law on stone: glorious, but all it could do to a sinner was condemn him. “Ministration of righteousness” is the new covenant, where God declares a man right and then lives in him.

Paul says “much more” three times. The old was never meant to last. The new remains. What God gave you in Christ doesn't wear off by Friday.

Part three

The veil taken away

Verses 12–18

Open faces, a free heart, and a man being changed.

Why Moses wore a veil

When Moses came down from Sinai after meeting with God, his face shone, and he didn't know it (Exodus 34:29).

“And the children of Israel saw the face of Moses, that the skin of Moses’ face shone: and Moses put the vail upon his face again, until he went in to speak with him.”

Exodus 34:35

The people were afraid to come near him (34:30). Paul adds what he sees in the veil: the glory on Moses' face was fading, and the veil kept Israel from watching it go out. That glory was a loan meant to run out.

The new covenant has nothing to hide: “we use great plainness of speech.”

The veil is taken away

Paul grew up reading Moses. Men can read every word, “when Moses is read”, and miss the One it points to: “the vail is upon their heart.” Paul was one of them until the Damascus road (Acts 9), so he writes with sorrow, not scorn.

The cure: “when it shall turn to the Lord, the vail shall be taken away.” Nobody has to figure it out first. He turns, and Christ lifts the veil.

Any man who can't see God clearly, through shame or confusion or a hard heart, gets the same invitation: turn to the Lord.

Liberty

The Lord Jesus works in us by His Spirit, and where He is, there is liberty: freedom from guilt, from the law's demands, and from being owned by what used to own us.

It isn't a promise of no struggle. Chains don't always fall off at once, but the Owner has changed: “If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed” (John 8:36).

“For, brethren, ye have been called unto liberty; only use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by love serve one another.”

Galatians 5:13

Looking, and becoming

κατοπτριζόμενοι said katoptrizomenoi “looking into a mirror”

Moses went up the mountain, and the glory lit his face for a while. Now every believer, “we all”, looks at Christ with “open face”, no veil. A mirror in Paul's day was polished metal, and it showed a dim reflection.

“For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known.”

1 Corinthians 13:12

From glory to glory

“From glory to glory” is a staircase, not a light switch. Each step is a little clearer than the last, and the One doing the changing is “the Spirit of the Lord.” So don't measure yourself against yesterday, especially if yesterday was a fall. Measure against last year.

Today: spend five minutes with Jesus. Read a few verses of a Gospel and look at Him. That's the looking Paul means.

Points to remember

  1. The living letter“Ye are our epistle… known and read of all men” 3:2Your life is the letter people read, and the Spirit is the One writing it.
  2. Stone and heart“Not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart” 3:3The old covenant told men what to do. The new gives a new heart and the Spirit.
  3. Sufficiency“Our sufficiency is of God” 3:5No man is able by himself. God makes us able.
  4. The veil“When it shall turn to the Lord, the vail shall be taken away” 3:16The veil is the blindness over a heart. Turning to the Lord takes it off.
  5. Liberty“Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty” 3:17Christ's Spirit brings freedom from guilt, from the law's demands, and from old masters.
  6. Changed“Changed into the same image from glory to glory” 3:18Change is gradual, one step at a time, and the Spirit keeps working.

Question 1 of 6

The living letter

Who is “the epistle of Christ”, known and read of all men?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.”

Matthew 5:16

Answer

Believers are. A changed life is a letter written by the Spirit, and everyone who knows you is reading it. Paul didn't need a paper reference, because the Corinthians themselves were the proof.

Question 2 of 6

Stone and heart

What is the difference between the old covenant and the new?

What verses tell us about this?

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

Answer

The old was a law on stone, outside a man: it showed his sin but couldn't change him. The new is written on the heart by the Spirit, and gives life, by faith in Christ.

Question 3 of 6

Sufficiency

Where does Paul say his ability comes from?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

Answer

From God alone. Paul says he is not sufficient to think anything as of himself. God makes him able, and God's grace is enough, even in weakness.

Question 4 of 6

The veil

What is the veil, and what takes it away?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The veil is the blindness over a heart that reads God's Word and can't see Christ in it. It's taken away in Christ, and the way is simple: turn to the Lord.

Question 5 of 6

Liberty

What does the Spirit of the Lord bring?

What verses tell us about this?

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

“Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage.”

Galatians 5:1

Answer

Liberty: freedom from guilt, from the law's demands, and from the old masters. He frees us so we can serve by love, not so we can go back to the chains.

Question 6 of 6

Changed

How are believers changed into the same image as Christ?

What verses tell us about this?

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

“But the path of the just is as the shining light, that shineth more and more unto the perfect day.”

Proverbs 4:18

Answer

By looking at Jesus with an open face, and by the Spirit of the Lord, a little at a time, from glory to glory. It is gradual, and God keeps going until He finishes what He started.
