Chapters 1–11 told us what God has done: all have sinned, Christ died for us, and believers are made new.

From chapter 12 on, Paul shows what that looks like on an ordinary weekday. Chapter 12 was life in the church. Chapter 13 takes us out onto the street: the courthouse, the tax man, the neighbour next door.

And this letter was going to Rome, the emperor's own city.

Part one

The higher powers

Verses 1–7

Believers in Nero's capital are told to honour the government, the courts, and the tax man.

Written under Nero

  1. AD 54Nero becomes emperor, at 16
  2. about AD 57Paul writes Romans“be subject”
  3. about AD 59–60Paul reaches Rome, a prisonerActs 28
  4. AD 64Rome burns; Nero turns on the Christians
  5. about AD 67–68Paul put to death in Rome, by tradition

“The higher powers” are the people who govern. When Paul wrote this, the man at the top was Nero, not a good man. Paul isn't saying every ruler is godly. God set up government itself, to keep order in a fallen world, and no ruler is outside His reach.

It isn't a blank check. When a ruler orders you to sin, “We ought to obey God rather than men” (Acts 5:29). But most days, obeying God means obeying the law.

A lot of us have a record, a court date, or a probation officer. It's easy to see them as the enemy. Paul calls the man in authority God's “minister,” His servant, set there for your good. Showing up, telling the truth, and keeping your appointments isn't giving in. It's part of your new life.

“The sword” was Rome's power to punish. Paul doesn't pretend consequences aren't real. Some of what you're facing, you earned. Face it honestly. You won't face it alone.

Two reasons to keep the law

Only for wrath

So I don't get caught

Careful while somebody's watching, anything goes when they're not. That's how the old life worked.

For conscience sake

Because it's right

Does right when nobody's watching, because God is. A clean conscience lets a man sleep at night.

Your probation will end someday. Doing right shouldn't end with it. A changed man pays the fine, finishes the classes, and keeps doing right after the paperwork is closed, because his heart has changed, not just his situation.

Pay what you owe

Rome's taxes paid for Nero's government. Paul says pay them anyway, with respect.

“Render therefore unto Caesar the things which are Caesar’s; and unto God the things that are God’s.”

Matthew 22:21

Taxes, tickets, child support: paying honestly is part of following Jesus, not just staying out of trouble.

Owe no man any thing

Old debts don't disappear because we stop thinking about them. Make the list, and start paying, one call and one payment at a time.

“Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.”

Luke 19:8

Zacchaeus made amends after Jesus saved him, not to earn it. What you owed God, Christ already paid on the cross. That frees you to face what you owe people.

All of it in one line

  • Thou shalt not commit adultery
  • Thou shalt not kill
  • Thou shalt not steal
  • Thou shalt not bear false witness
  • Thou shalt not covet
“briefly comprehended in this saying”Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself

Love doesn't take a man's wife, his life, or his things, lie about him, or want what's his. “Love worketh no ill to his neighbour.”

That's the one debt you never finish paying. Your other debts have an end. This one you pay on today, and owe again tomorrow.

They come in pairs, and they travel together. The drink leads to the bed, and both of them lead to the fight. Paul names them plainly because the men in Rome knew them well. So do we. Naming it is the first step out of it.

“Walk honestly, as in the day”: live a life you wouldn't mind being seen. No second phone. No stories to keep straight.

Make no plans for the flesh

“Provision” is pronoia: planning ahead. A fall rarely comes out of nowhere. Cut off the flesh's plans before the craving comes.

Put on the Lord Jesus Christ

You don't only take things off. You put Someone on. Clothes go everywhere you go: to work, to court, into the hard hour at 2 a.m. So does He.

About AD 386, a young man named Augustine sat weeping in a garden in Milan. For years he had lived for lust and pleasure, and he couldn't break free. He picked up Paul's letter, and his eyes fell on verses 13 and 14. He later wrote that all the darkness of doubt fled away. He is still one of the best-known Christians in history.

“Enter not into the path of the wicked, and go not in the way of evil men. Avoid it, pass not by it, turn from it, and pass away.”

Proverbs 4:14–15

The fight is won by not going that way at all, not by walking past the door every day and holding on. And when temptation comes anyway, God promises “a way to escape” (1 Corinthians 10:13).

Points to remember

  1. Authority“The powers that be are ordained of God” 13:1God set up government. Doing right under it, with a clean conscience, is part of a new life.
  2. Debts“Owe no man any thing, but to love one another” 13:8Pay down every debt you can, starting today. Love is the debt you keep paying.
  3. The law“Love is the fulfilling of the law” 13:10Love does no harm to a neighbour, so it keeps every commandment about him.
  4. Waking up“The night is far spent, the day is at hand” 13:12Christ's return is nearer every day. Get up, take off the dark, and put on the light.
  5. Recovery“Make not provision for the flesh” 13:14Put on Christ, and cut off the flesh's plans: the stash, the number, the route.

Question 1 of 5

Authority

Why should a believer obey the law and the people over him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Honour all men. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honour the king.”

1 Peter 2:17

Answer

God set up government, and the man in authority is His servant for our good. We obey for conscience' sake, not just to stay out of trouble, unless a ruler commands sin.

Question 2 of 5

Debts

What is the one debt we never finish paying?

What verses tell us about this?

“Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.”

Luke 19:8

Answer

Love. Every other debt (taxes, fines, money borrowed, an apology) we work to pay off, starting today. Love for one another we pay every day and still owe tomorrow.

Question 3 of 5

The law

How does love fulfil the law?

What verses tell us about this?

“For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this; Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself.”

Galatians 5:14

Answer

The commandments about other people are all summed up in “love thy neighbour as thyself.” A man who truly loves his neighbour won't do him the harm the law forbids.

Question 4 of 5

Waking up

Why is it “high time to awake out of sleep”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the night is almost over, and Christ's return is nearer every day. It's no time to live like it's 2 a.m. Cast off the works of darkness and put on the armour of light.

Question 5 of 5

Recovery

What does it mean to “make not provision for the flesh”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Enter not into the path of the wicked, and go not in the way of evil men. Avoid it, pass not by it, turn from it, and pass away.”

Proverbs 4:14–15

Answer

Don't plan ahead for sin. Get rid of the stash, delete the number, change the route, and tell a brother. Don't leave the door open “just in case.” Put on the Lord Jesus Christ instead.
