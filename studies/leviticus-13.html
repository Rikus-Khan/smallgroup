Leviticus 13

The priest shall look

A sore on the skin, and a priest who looks, waits, looks again, and says “clean” or “unclean.” A man cut off from the camp, crying out. And the question under all of it: who will touch him?

Part one

“Brought unto the priest”

Verses 1–17

A sore on the skin is shown to the priest, who looks, waits, and pronounces.

Words for the skin

  • a risinga swelling
  • a scaba crust over a sore
  • a bright spota shiny, pale patch
  • the plaguethe KJV's word for the sore itself; it means a stroke, a blow
  • leprosythe KJV's name for the disease of this chapter
  • pronouncesay out loud, as a judge does

Look at who the sore goes to. Not to a neighbour, not to the man himself: “he shall be brought unto Aaron the priest, or unto one of his sons the priests.” This law was given to Moses and Aaron together (verse 1), at the foot of Sinai, in the one month between the tabernacle going up (Exodus 40:17) and the numbering of the people (Numbers 1:1).

Shut up, and wait

  1. day oneThe priest looksand cannot yet tell: shut up
  2. day sevenHe looks againnot spread: seven days more
  3. day fourteenHe looks againverse 6
  4. the verdictclean, or unclean“it is but a scab”

Shut up means kept apart, shut in, while he waited to see. “Seven days” comes eight times in this chapter (verses 4, 5, 21, 26, 31, 33, 50, 54), and “the seventh day” six more. Nobody was condemned in a hurry. The priest looked, waited, and looked again, and if it was “but a scab,” the man washed his clothes and went home clean.

What the priest could do, and could not

The priest could

look, and say

He looked at the sore, and “pronounced” the man clean or unclean. He kept the camp from spreading it.

The priest could not

heal

Nowhere in this chapter does he cure a man. The cure was God's. The priest only told the truth about what he saw.

Count the verdicts. The word “pronounce” comes 19 times in this chapter: unclean 11 times, clean 8 (the last of each in verse 59, for a garment). So the law was not only bad news. Seven times in this chapter a man hears the word he is longing for.

All white, and clean

This is the strangest verdict in the chapter. A man covered from head to foot is clean. A man with one raw spot left is unclean (verses 14–15). Quick raw flesh means living flesh, raw and open. Moses does not say why, and we should not make up a reason. But many have read it as a picture, and the picture has a New Testament verse beside it.

Ifthere is nothing left hidden, and it has all come out,
→
thenhe is clean.

“If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:8–9

The man who says, “I have nothing to hide, it is all out, and it is all true,” is the man the priest can pronounce clean. The man who keeps one raw spot covered is not. Hiding is what keeps us sick. Bringing everything out, to God and to a brother, is where healing begins.

Part two

“The priest shall look upon it”

Verses 18–37

Boils, burnings, and sores on the head and beard: the same careful looking every time.

The same test, every time

Unclean

when it is…

deeper than the skin; the hair in it turned white; spreading abroad. (Verses 3, 20, 25, 8, 22, 27.)

Clean

when it is…

no deeper than the skin; no white hair; staying in its place, and somewhat dark. (Verses 4–6, 21–23, 26–28.)

Inflammation and burning here mean a sore left by a burn or a boil. A boil that has healed, or a burn that has left a mark, could look like the sickness. So the priest did not go by the first look. He went by the signs, and by waiting. God gave him signs to go by, so that no man would be shut out on a guess.

More old words

  • scalla scaly sore on the head or the beard
  • shavenshaved; but the sore itself was left alone, so the priest could watch it
  • quickliving; “quick raw flesh” is raw living flesh
  • warp and woofthe threads that run the length of a cloth, and the threads woven across
  • frettingeating away, spreading
  • renttorn (the old word for tore); rend means tear
  • abroadwidely: “spread much abroad” means spread far across the skin

The washing of clothes comes again and again (verses 6, 34, 58). A man who had been shut up and found clean does not go back to the camp in the same clothes. He washes, and starts over.

Part three

“Unclean, unclean”

Verses 38–46

Some sores are nothing. And one man ends up alone, outside the camp.

Not everything is leprosy

  • a freckled spot“he is clean” (verse 39)
  • a bald head“yet is he clean” (verse 40)
  • a bald forehead“yet is he clean” (verse 41)
  • a white reddish sore in ita leprosy sprung up (verse 42)

A man is not unclean for how he looks. Freckles are clean. A bald head is clean, said twice. Uncleanness was a verdict about a plague, not about a face. If anyone ever made you feel unclean for how you looked or where you came from, the law of Moses is on your side.

Outside the camp

Torn clothes, a bare head, a covering over the upper lip, and he must call out his own shame, “Unclean, unclean,” so that no one touches him by mistake. “He shall dwell alone.” (Habitation means where he lives.)

The camp had the LORD in the middle of it, and a leper was put out for the camp's sake:

“Both male and female shall ye put out, without the camp shall ye put them; that they defile not their camps, in the midst whereof I dwell.”

Numbers 5:3

Many men in this room know this picture from the inside: living alone, ashamed, with the word on you.

Why the Bible uses it as a picture of sin

The chapter never says that a leper had sinned. It describes a sickness, and we should not say that every sick man is a sinner. But look at what leprosy does, and you see why the Bible keeps coming back to it as a picture:

  • it spreadsa spot becomes a patch
  • it hidesunder the hair, under the clothes
  • it cuts a man offoutside the camp, alone
  • he cannot fix itonly the priest can say it is over

When Miriam spoke against Moses and became leprous, white as snow, even Aaron said, “lay not the sin upon us” (Numbers 12:11). And look what the whole camp did for her:

“And the LORD said unto Moses, If her father had but spit in her face, should she not be ashamed seven days? let her be shut out from the camp seven days, and after that let her be received in again. And Miriam was shut out from the camp seven days: and the people journeyed not till Miriam was brought in again.”

Numbers 12:14–15

Seven days, and “the people journeyed not till Miriam was brought in again.” The camp waited for her. The outcast is not forgotten.

And he touched him

A leper was kept outside the camp, away from every hand. And a leper came to Jesus, kneeling:

“And there came a leper to him, beseeching him, and kneeling down to him, and saying unto him, If thou wilt, thou canst make me clean. And Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, and touched him, and saith unto him, I will; be thou clean. And as soon as he had spoken, immediately the leprosy departed from him, and he was cleansed.”

Mark 1:40–42

“Moved with compassion, put forth his hand, and touched him.” He could have spoken from a distance. He gave him what a leper was kept from: a hand. And the man was made clean.

Part four

“The garment also”

Verses 47–59

The same plague in cloth and leather, and the same careful looking.

The garment, step by step

  1. shownto the priestverse 49
  2. shut upseven daysverse 50
  3. spread?burn itverses 51–52
  4. not spread?wash, wait seven days moreverses 53–54
  5. gone?wash a second time: cleanverse 58

Warp and woof were the lengthwise and crosswise threads of woven cloth (verse 48). A mark that has gone in deep, “fret inward,” could not be washed out, and the garment was burned. But if the mark was gone, one more washing and it was clean. The same grace we saw for the man: look, wait, wash, clean.

“That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word, That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish.”

Ephesians 5:26–27

The picture goes on in the New Testament: not “spot, or wrinkle,” but “holy and without blemish,” by “the washing of water by the word.”

Real world: don't hide the spot

The leper was found by being looked at. He did not diagnose himself, and he was not left to carry it alone for ever.

  • SinIt spreads and it hides, and it cuts a man off. The hiding is half the damage.
  • RegretIf you have been the man outside the camp, hear this: the camp waited seven days for Miriam, and Jesus touched a leper.
  • GraceThe verdicts are 11 unclean and 8 clean, and the gospel is that Jesus took the unclean one and touched him.
  • TodayShow your spot to someone. Call or text your brothers in Christ, or your pastor, and tell them the one thing you have been keeping covered.

Hebrews says Jesus himself “suffered without the gate” and calls us to “go forth therefore unto him without the camp” (13:12–13). The Saviour is on the outside with the men who are on the outside.

He bore it

Isaiah looks ahead to the one who would carry what the leper carried:

“Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted. But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.”

Isaiah 53:4–5

“He hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows.” He took the place of the man outside the camp, so that the man could come in.

Points to remember

  1. The priest“he shall be brought unto Aaron the priest, or unto one of his sons the priests” 13:2A sore is brought to the one God appointed. The priest looks and tells the truth; he cannot heal.
  2. The waiting“the priest shall shut up him that hath the plague seven days” 13:4Nobody was condemned in a hurry: look, wait seven days, look again.
  3. The surprise“it is all turned white: he is clean” 13:13The man with nothing left hidden is the one pronounced clean. Bring it all out.
  4. The cry“he shall dwell alone; without the camp shall his habitation be” 13:46The outcast's cry is the cry of every man shut out. Jesus lets him come near, and touches him.
  5. The washing“he shall wash his clothes, and be clean” 13:6Cleared, washed, and back in the camp.
  6. The garment“it shall be washed the second time, and shall be clean” 13:58Where the mark was gone, one more washing and it was clean. God makes a way back.

Question 1 of 6

The priest

Who looked at the sore, and what did he do about it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Aaron the priest, or one of his sons. He looked at the sore and pronounced him clean or unclean; “pronounce” comes nineteen times in this chapter. He could not heal; he told the truth about what he saw.

Question 2 of 6

The waiting

What did the priest do when he could not yet tell?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He shut the man up seven days, looked again, and shut him up seven days more. Nobody was condemned in a hurry. If it was only a scab, the man washed his clothes and was clean.

Question 3 of 6

The surprise

When was a man pronounced clean, though leprosy covered him?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

When it had covered him all over and turned all white. One raw spot left made him unclean again. Bring it all out, nothing hidden, and he is clean: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us.”

Question 4 of 6

The cry

What did the leper wear and say, and where did he live?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, and touched him, and saith unto him, I will; be thou clean.”

Mark 1:41

Answer

Torn clothes, a bare head and a covering over his lip, crying “Unclean, unclean,” living alone outside the camp. Then Jesus came, “moved with compassion,” and touched him.

Question 5 of 6

The washing

What did a man do with his clothes when he was pronounced clean?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He washed his clothes and was clean. Shut up, looked at, pronounced, washed, and home again.

Question 6 of 6

The garment

What was done with a garment that had a fretting leprosy?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

If the mark spread, the garment was burned. If it did not spread, it was washed and shut up seven days more, and if the mark was gone, it was washed a second time and was clean.
