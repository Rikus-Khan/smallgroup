Numbers 12

Miriam, Aaron and the meekest man

Moses' own brother and sister speak against him. The LORD answers for him himself, Miriam is struck with leprosy, Moses prays for her, and a whole camp waits seven days until she is brought in again.

Part one

“Hath the LORD indeed spoken only by Moses?”

Verses 1–3

The people who spoke were the two closest to Moses, and the thing they said was not the thing they meant.

What they said, and what they meant

What they said (verse 1)

“Because of the Ethiopian woman”

The text doesn't say who she was, and when the LORD speaks he never takes up the complaint about her. She was the cover, not the point.

What they meant (verse 2)

“Hath he not spoken also by us?”

That was the real matter. Whatever they thought of the marriage, the LORD never answers it. What they were against was his place. “Hath the LORD indeed spoken only by Moses?” Why him, and not us?

Leaders who stumbled where leaders stumble

Notice who they were. Miriam is named first. She “took a timbrel in her hand” and led the women in singing at the sea (Exodus 15:20–21), and the LORD says through Micah, “I sent before thee Moses, Aaron, and Miriam” (6:4). Aaron was the high priest. They were not outsiders or enemies. They were leaders, and they stumbled by comparing.

“For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.”

James 3:16

Envy often dresses up as a concern about something else. When you find fault with a man, ask whether the fault is the real thing, or whether you wish you had his place. And look at the last words of verse 2: “And the LORD heard it.” The things said in the tents are not said in secret.

“Very meek”

The KJV sets verse 3 in brackets, an aside: “Now the man Moses was very meek, above all the men.” Meek does not mean weak. It means gentle and humble in strength: power held under God. When his own brother and sister attacked him, Moses said nothing.

Miriam and Aaron

Spake

“And they said, Hath the LORD indeed spoken only by Moses?”

Moses

Said nothing

No answer is recorded. He did not defend himself, and he did not hit back. The LORD answered for him.

“Who, when he was reviled, reviled not again; when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously:”

1 Peter 2:23

That is Peter on Jesus, who said of himself, “I am meek and lowly in heart” (Matthew 11:29). When someone speaks against you, you do not have to win. “Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord” (Romans 12:19).

Part two

“Come out ye three”

Verses 4–9

The LORD comes down, stands in the door of the tabernacle, and speaks for his servant.

Prophets, and Moses

A prophet among you

A vision, a dream

“I the LORD will make myself known unto him in a vision, and will speak unto him in a dream.” God speaks, and the prophet gets the message in pictures that need working out. “Dark speeches” are riddles: things hard to understand.

My servant Moses

Mouth to mouth

“With him will I speak mouth to mouth, even apparently, and not in dark speeches; and the similitude of the LORD shall he behold.” Apparently here means plainly, openly, with nothing hidden. Similitude means likeness: Moses sees the LORD's form.

“And there arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face,”

Deuteronomy 34:10

The LORD does not say, Moses is more gifted than you. He says, “My servant Moses… is faithful in all mine house.” Faithfulness is the ground of his place. And then the question that stops everything: “wherefore then were ye not afraid to speak against my servant Moses?” They should have feared, not for Moses' sake, but for the one who put him there.

“Come out ye three”

  1. “the LORD heard it”verse 2in the tent
  2. “suddenly”he calls all threeverse 4
  3. the cloud comes downhe stands in the doorverse 5
  4. “he departed”his anger kindledverse 9

There is no long wait. “The LORD spake suddenly.” Moses was called with them, and did not speak. Then the LORD stepped forward in the cloud, which they had followed from Egypt, and called out two names: “Aaron and Miriam.” They came forth, and he told them what he thought. When the LORD departed, “the cloud departed from off the tabernacle” (verse 10): the presence they had taken for granted was gone.

Part three

“Heal her now, O God”

Verses 10–16

Sin has a cost, a brother prays, and the whole camp waits for one woman to come home.

Leprous, and Aaron's confession

The cloud went up, and “behold, Miriam became leprous, white as snow”. Leprosy was the disease that put a person outside the camp: “he shall dwell alone; without the camp shall his habitation be” (Leviticus 13:46). Aaron, who had been at her side, turned and saw it. He did not argue.

  • “my lord”Aaron calls Moses lord. He, the older brother, bows to him
  • “we have done foolishly”the plain word for what they did: foolish
  • “we have sinned”and the plain word again: sin
  • “lay not the sin upon us”do not let it stay on us

“Let her not be as one dead, of whom the flesh is half consumed when he cometh out of his mother's womb.” That is how he saw her: a living body slipping into death. Aaron owned it, said “we”, and asked for mercy. He did not blame Miriam, and he did not blame Moses. Confession is exactly this: foolishly, sinned, mercy.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

The prayer of the man she spoke against

Moses, to the LORDHeal her now, O God, I beseech thee.

the LORD, to MosesLet her be shut out from the camp seven days, and after that let her be received in again.

Moses' prayer is eight words, and it is for the woman who had just attacked him. He had said nothing in his own defence, and his first word was a prayer for her. Jesus said, “Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you” (Matthew 5:44), and on the cross, “Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do” (Luke 23:34).

A cost, and an end

The LORD answers Moses, and his answer has a cost and an end. “If her father had but spit in her face, should she not be ashamed seven days?” A father's rebuke would shame her for seven days, and this was more than a father's rebuke. So: seven days outside the camp.

It is a real consequence, and it has a number on it. And then: “after that let her be received in again.” The outside was not for ever.

Miriam's road down and back

The same woman who had led Israel's song at the sea fell by her own tongue, and was brought back. The Bible does not hide it, and neither does it let it be the last word. “Remember what the LORD thy God did unto Miriam by the way, after that ye were come forth out of Egypt” (Deuteronomy 24:9): it was remembered as a warning, and the same story shows the way back.

The camp waited for her

“And Miriam was shut out from the camp seven days: and the people journeyed not till Miriam was brought in again.” More than six hundred thousand fighting men and all their families stayed put for one woman. Nobody went ahead without her.

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

This is what the church is meant to be for a man who has fallen: not a crowd that moves on without him, and not a court that bars the door for ever, but a camp that waits seven days for him and then takes him in. If you have fallen, there is a way back, and there are men who are waiting. And if you are one of the men who are waiting, “considering thyself”: it could be you next.

Faithful as a servant, greater as a Son

“And Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant, for a testimony of those things which were to be spoken after; But Christ as a son over his own house; whose house are we, if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end.”

Hebrews 3:5–6

“God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds;”

Hebrews 1:1–2

The Holy Spirit quotes the LORD's words from this chapter: “faithful in all mine house”. Moses was the most faithful servant, speaking with God mouth to mouth. But he was a servant in the house, and Christ is the Son over it. The LORD spoke to the fathers “in divers manners” by prophets, in visions and dreams, and Moses plainly. In these last days he has spoken to us by his Son. We are his house.

Real world: guard the tongue, keep the door open

  • The LORD heard itWhat you say about your pastor or a brother in the tent, he heard. Take the complaint to the man, or to him. Not around the camp.
  • Check your envyIs the real thing a fault, or do you wish you had his place? Say it to the LORD first.
  • Be meekWhen someone speaks against you, you don't have to win. Give it to the one who judges righteously.
  • Confess plainlyLike Aaron: “we have done foolishly… we have sinned.” No excuse, and no pointing at the other person.
  • Pray for the one who hurt youMoses' first word was “Heal her.” Whom will you pray for today?
  • Wait for the fallenIf a brother has fallen, don't move on without him. Text him today.

And if you are the one outside the camp right now, hear this: “after that let her be received in again.” The outside has a number on it, and the door opens from the inside. Confess it, call the brothers who are waiting, and come in.

Points to remember

  1. Envy“Hath the LORD indeed spoken only by Moses? hath he not spoken also by us? And the LORD heard it.” 12:2The complaint was a cover. The real thing was envy of his place, and the LORD heard it.
  2. Meekness“Now the man Moses was very meek, above all the men which were upon the face of the earth.” 12:3Meek is not weak. He said nothing, and the LORD answered for him.
  3. Moses' place“With him will I speak mouth to mouth, even apparently, and not in dark speeches” 12:8The LORD spoke to a prophet in a vision or a dream, and to Moses plainly. Fear the one who put a man in his place.
  4. Sin“we have done foolishly, and wherein we have sinned” 12:11Sin cost her seven days outside the camp. Aaron called it what it was.
  5. Mercy“Heal her now, O God, I beseech thee” 12:13Moses prayed for the one who attacked him, and the camp waited until she was brought in again.
  6. Christ“Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant… But Christ as a son over his own house” Hebrews 3:5–6Moses was the faithful servant. Christ is the Son over the house, and we are his house.
