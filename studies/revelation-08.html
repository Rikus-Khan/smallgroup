Revelation 8

Your prayers are not lost

The seventh seal opens, and heaven goes silent. The prayers of God's people rise like incense before His throne, and they are answered. Then the trumpets begin: warnings that strike a third and spare the rest, so that men will turn while there is time.

Out of the seventh seal come seven trumpets, and later seven bowls (chapters 15–16). Teachers differ on how they fit together. Some read them one after another, each worse than the last. Others think the trumpets and bowls tell the same days again from a closer angle. Either way, the Lamb opened every seal, and nothing in this chapter is out of His hands.

Part one

Silence and incense

Verses 1–6

Heaven, which has been loud with praise for four chapters, goes quiet. And the first thing that happens next is prayer.

Heaven holds its breath

Since chapter 4, heaven has never stopped singing. Now: nothing. The prophets knew this kind of silence. It is the hush before God acts.

“But the LORD is in his holy temple: let all the earth keep silence before him.”

Habakkuk 2:20

Some teachers think heaven goes quiet so the prayers in the next verses can be heard. Whether or not, there are times to stop talking and be still before God. Today, try five quiet minutes before you say a word to Him.

Your prayers are kept

The angel mixes “much incense” with the prayers of all God's people, and they go up before God. Not lost, not forgotten, not too small. Two chapters back, the elders held “golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints” (5:8). Heaven keeps them.

“Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense; and the lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice.”

Psalm 141:2

Then watch verse 5. The same censer is filled with fire from that altar and thrown to the earth. The answer to the prayers comes from the place where the prayers went.

The prayers are answered

Chapter 6:10, the saints under the altar“How long, O Lord, holy and true …?”
→
Chapter 8:5, from the same altarfire cast to the earth: God is acting

“And shall not God avenge his own elect, which cry day and night unto him, though he bear long with them?”

Luke 18:7

Prayer is not a wish shouted into the dark. It goes before the throne, and it moves the hand of God, in His time. Many of you have prayed for years for a son, a wife, a mother, your own freedom. Don't stop. Heaven has every one of them in the bowl.

And if you hardly know how to pray, that's covered too: the Spirit “maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered” (Romans 8:26). Today: pray for one person by name, and write it down.

What is a trumpet for?

In Israel a trumpet called the people together, sounded for battle, and above all, warned. A watchman who saw an enemy coming blew it so the city could get ready:

“If when he seeth the sword come upon the land, he blow the trumpet, and warn the people;”

Ezekiel 33:3

At Jericho, seven priests blew seven trumpets before the walls fell (Joshua 6:4). Here seven angels hold seven trumpets. A trumpet is loud on purpose: God wants to be heard before the end, not after.

Part two

Four trumpets

Verses 7–12

Land, sea, rivers and sky are struck in turn. Every time, a third. Every time, two thirds left standing.

The God of the Exodus

Anyone who knew his Bible would hear Egypt in these trumpets. The same God who struck Pharaoh's land to set His people free is at work again.

1st trumpet: hail and fire↔Egypt's hail, “and fire mingled with the hail” (Exodus 9:24)
2nd trumpet: the sea becomes blood↔the Nile turned to blood (Exodus 7:20)
3rd trumpet: bitter waters↔the waters made undrinkable (Exodus 7:21)
4th trumpet: darkness↔three days of thick darkness (Exodus 10:22)

How literal is it? Good teachers differ. Some expect a real burning mountain and real blood; others read a picture of judgment on the world's food, trade and water. Both agree on what is plain: God judges evil, and He is not done saving His people.

Wormwood: bitter water

Wormwood is a real plant, so bitter that its name meant sorrow. In the prophets it is what sin tastes like in the end:

“Therefore thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; Behold, I will feed them, even this people, with wormwood, and give them water of gall to drink.”

Jeremiah 9:15

You may know that taste. What looked like water turned out to be poison: the drink, the pipe, a woman who wasn't your wife. “Many men died of the waters, because they were made bitter.” Sin always gets bitter, and so does a heart that holds on to it.

Marah (Exodus 15:23)“they were bitter”
→
Moses cried to the LORD“the LORD shewed him a tree”
→
Exodus 15:25“the waters were made sweet”

Many preachers have seen the cross in that tree. Whatever has gone bitter in you, cry to the Lord as Moses did. He still sweetens water.

A third, not all

Four times, the same fraction. These are not the final judgment; they are warnings with the brakes on. God strikes part so that the rest can see, and turn.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Maybe your life has taken a hit like that: a third gone, the job, the license, the marriage, your health. That loss can be a trumpet, not an ending. Two thirds is still standing. Turn to Him with it.

Part three

Woe, woe, woe

Verse 13

Before the last three trumpets, an angel flies through the sky and cries out a warning.

The worst is still a warning

Three woes for three trumpets still to come. “The inhabiters of the earth” is Revelation's name for people who live as if this world is all there is. The angel is not gloating. He is shouting while there is still time to hear.

Chapter 9 ends sadly: after all of it, men “repented not of the works of their hands” (9:20). Don't be that man. You don't need a burning mountain to turn around. Turn today, while the trumpet is still only a warning.

So, today

  1. Be stillHeaven went quiet before God acted. Give Him five quiet minutes before the day gets loud.
  2. Pray by nameYour prayers rise like incense and are kept before the throne. Pray for one person by name.
  3. Hear the warningIf something in your life is sounding like a trumpet, don't wait for the next one. Turn now.
  4. Let Him sweeten itBring the bitter thing to the Lord, as Moses did at Marah.

Revelation 8 sounds frightening, and it is meant to be serious. But look at who is in charge. The Lamb opened the seal. God set the limits: a third, no more. And before a single trumpet sounded, He stopped to hear His people pray. You are not forgotten in the noise.

Points to remember

  1. Silence“there was silence in heaven about the space of half an hour” 8:1Heaven holds its breath before God acts. There is a time to be still before Him.
  2. Prayers“the smoke of the incense, which came with the prayers of the saints, ascended up before God” 8:4Your prayers are kept before the throne, and the answer comes from the same altar.
  3. Trumpets“to them were given seven trumpets” 8:2A trumpet warns. God sounds it before the end so men can get ready.
  4. A third“the third part of the sun was smitten” 8:12God limits His judgment. Two thirds is spared, so that people will turn while there is time.
  5. Bitter“many men died of the waters, because they were made bitter” 8:11Sin always turns bitter. The Lord who made Marah sweet can sweeten what has gone bitter in you.
  6. Woe“Woe, woe, woe, to the inhabiters of the earth” 8:13The angel cries out while there is still time. Turn today, before the next trumpet.

Question 1 of 6

Silence

Why does heaven go silent for half an hour?

What verses tell us about this?

“But the LORD is in his holy temple: let all the earth keep silence before him.”

Habakkuk 2:20

“Hold thy peace at the presence of the Lord GOD: for the day of the LORD is at hand: for the LORD hath prepared a sacrifice, he hath bid his guests.”

Zephaniah 1:7

Answer

It is the hush before God acts, the silence the prophets spoke of. Some teachers think heaven goes quiet so the prayers of the saints can be heard. Either way, it is awe: even heaven stops talking when God is about to move.

Question 2 of 6

Prayers

What happens to the prayers of God's people?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense; and the lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice.”

Psalm 141:2

Answer

They rise before God like incense and are kept there. Then fire from the same altar is cast to the earth: the prayers are answered, in God's time and His way. No prayer of yours is wasted.

Question 3 of 6

Trumpets

What is a trumpet for?

What verses tell us about this?

“If when he seeth the sword come upon the land, he blow the trumpet, and warn the people;”

Ezekiel 33:3

“Blow ye the trumpet in Zion, and sound an alarm in my holy mountain: let all the inhabitants of the land tremble: for the day of the LORD cometh, for it is nigh at hand;”

Joel 2:1

Answer

To warn. A watchman blew the trumpet when he saw danger coming, so people could get ready. God sounds these trumpets before the end, not after it, because He wants to be heard in time.

Question 4 of 6

A third

Why is only a third struck?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Because God limits His judgment. These are warnings, not the end: He strikes part and spares the rest so that people will see and turn. He is patient, not willing that any should perish.

Question 5 of 6

Bitter

What does the star called Wormwood teach us about bitter water?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore thus saith the LORD of hosts, the God of Israel; Behold, I will feed them, even this people, with wormwood, and give them water of gall to drink.”

Jeremiah 9:15

“And he cried unto the LORD; and the LORD shewed him a tree, which when he had cast into the waters, the waters were made sweet: there he made for them a statute and an ordinance, and there he proved them,”

Exodus 15:25

Answer

Sin always turns bitter in the end, and bitter water kills. But at Marah, Moses cried to the Lord and a tree made the water sweet. Bring the bitter thing to Him; He still sweetens water.

Question 6 of 6

Woe

What is the angel's “Woe, woe, woe” calling men to do?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the rest of the men which were not killed by these plagues yet repented not of the works of their hands, that they should not worship devils, and idols of gold, and silver, and brass, and stone, and of wood: which neither can see, nor hear, nor walk:”

Revelation 9:20

Answer

To turn while there is still time. The worst is yet to come, and the angel cries out before it does. Sadly, many would not repent. Don't wait for the next trumpet: turn to Christ today.
