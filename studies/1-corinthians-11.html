Order, and the table

Where we are in 1 Corinthians

About AD 55, Paul is writing from Ephesus to the church he started in Corinth. The letter is a pastor's answers, one real problem after another. Chapters 8 to 10 were about food and freedom. Now he turns to how the church behaves when it meets.

1–4Divisions
5–6Sin the church let in
7Marriage
8–10Food and freedom
11Order in worship
12–14Gifts, and love
15The resurrection
16The offering, goodbye

Two problems in one chapter. First, some in the meeting were acting in ways that shamed one another. Then, at the church's shared meal, the rich ate well while the poor went hungry. Read alone, this chapter still makes sense: a church is meeting, and Paul tells it how to meet in a way that honours Christ and each other.

Part one

Honour in worship

Verses 1–16

Follow me as I follow Christ. Then a hard text, handled carefully.

Follow me, as I follow Christ

ChristPaul followed HimPaul“Be ye followers of me”the Corinthiansand sosomeone watching you

Paul isn't boasting. The whole sentence matters: “even as I also am of Christ.” Follow him only as far as he follows his Lord. If he ever steps away from Christ, stop following him there.

And the line runs on past Paul. Somebody is watching you: a younger brother, a son, the new guy at work who knows your past. You may be the nearest picture of Christ he ever sees. You don't have to be perfect. You do have to be pointed the right way, and honest when you fall.

An order, not a ranking

Godis the head ofChristand yet the Son is fully God: “I and my Father are one” (John 10:30)
Christis the head ofevery man
the manis the head ofthe woman

“Head” was a word Corinth knew, and good Bible teachers differ on whether it means authority over or source of. Either way, look at the first line: Christ answers to the Father and is not one bit less than the Father. So an order of headship is about how we honour each other, never about whose life is worth more.

And a man's own head is Christ. Before he is anyone's husband or boss, he answers to Him.

What a head meant in Corinth

A man's head

bare in worship

In some pagan temples, Roman men covered their heads to pray. Paul says a Christian man comes to God plainly, and does not cover his head.

A woman's head

covered in public

In much of that world a respectable woman went out with her head covered. To pray or speak in the meeting with her head bare would look like a shameful break with decency.

A shaved head

a mark of shame

A woman's head shaved was a mark of disgrace. That is why Paul says a bare head is “even all one as if she were shaven.”

Notice that both men and women are “praying or prophesying” in this meeting. Prophesying here means speaking God's word to the church. Paul's concern in this passage is how they carry themselves while they do it.

The hardest line in the chapter

Verse 10 has two puzzles. “Power” most likely means a sign of her own authority, the right to pray and speak in the meeting. And “the angels”? Honest Bible teachers are not sure what Paul means, and we won't pretend to be.

What the verse seems to say is that worship happens in front of heaven's eyes, so it is no place for show or disorder. That is the plain part, and it matches the rest of the chapter.

Verses 7 to 9 go back to the creation story. They explain why Paul wants the difference between man and woman honoured. They do not say a woman matters less to God. Verse 12, a little further on, answers that directly.

Not without each other

Verses 8 and 9 look back to creation: the first woman was taken from the first man. Verse 12 answers it: every man since has come into the world through a woman. Neither of them stands alone.

Paul puts the little words in on purpose: “not… without… in the Lord.” Whatever order there is between them, in Christ neither one is a spare part. A man who looks down on women, or on any other man, has not read this verse.

A hard text, honestly

Good, Bible-believing Baptists read these verses differently, and we're not going to settle it here.

Some churches

a covering today

They take it as a command for every age, and women cover their heads when the church worships. Some teachers think the long hair of verse 15 is itself the covering.

Many churches

a custom of that day

They read it as a custom of Corinth, and point to verse 16, where Paul will not make it a fight. The principle stays; the cloth was for their street.

What is plain to all

honour, and order

Come to God with respect. Honour one another. Man and woman need each other in the Lord. And do not let your own way turn worship into a show or a fight.

Verse 16 is worth a second look: “if any man seem to be contentious…” There is always a man who would rather win the argument than worship. If you and a brother differ here, hold your view gently and pray together anyway.

Part two

A supper that was no supper

Verses 17–22

One went hungry, and another was drunk, at the same church meal.

Who ate where

Churches then met in homes, often the house of a rich member. Many scholars picture it like this: a small dining room that held about nine, and a courtyard for everyone else. The church meal was a real supper, with the Lord's table at its end.

The wealthy came early, because they had no shift to work, and they ate and drank in the dining room. The poor and the slaves came after work, and found nothing left. “One is hungry, and another is drunken.”

Paul's cure (verse 33) is simple: wait for each other, and share. Then it is a supper of the Lord, and not of the host's friends.

Shaming the ones with nothing

Paul is angry, and it is not about etiquette. A table that shames the poor in front of the rich has stopped being the Lord's table. “Divisions” and “heresies” in verses 18 and 19 mean factions: cliques inside the church.

Verse 19 has a hard edge: factions show who is genuine. “They which are approved” are the ones who have been tested and proved true.

Some of us know the other side of this table. You've been the one without: the empty plate, the seat nobody saved, the look that said you didn't belong. The Lord's table was never meant to do that to anyone. It's meant to feed everyone who comes, and every man there to be one sinner among the rest.

Part three

The Lord's table

Verses 23–34

What Jesus gave us to remember, and who it is for.

The table travels

The first Lord's Supper was in an upper room in Jerusalem, “the same night in which he was betrayed.” Paul wasn't there. He says he “received of the Lord” what he handed on.

He carried it north to Antioch, then west across Asia Minor and Greece, and gave it to the new church in Corinth (about AD 50 to 52). Now he writes to remind them what it is.

One meal, passed hand to hand

  1. about AD 30–33Jesus and the Twelve, in the upper room“the same night he was betrayed”
  2. about AD 50–52Paul gives it to the church at Corinth“that which also I delivered unto you”
  3. about AD 55Paul writes this letter, from Ephesus“I have received of the Lord”
  4. till He comethe church keeps the tableverse 26

This is one of the earliest written accounts of the Supper that we have, set down about twenty-five years after that night. Nobody invented it later. Paul got it from the Lord, and gave it on.

“And he took bread, and gave thanks, and brake it, and gave unto them, saying, This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me.”

Luke 22:19

Three looks at one table

  1. Look back“in remembrance of me”

    The cross. We remember what it cost Him: His body broken, His blood poured out for us.

  2. Look up“the new testament in my blood”

    A new covenant, bought for us: sins forgiven, and God as our Father.

  3. Look ahead“till he come”

    This is not the end of the story. He is coming back, and the table points to that day.

“Shew” means to announce. Each time we eat and drink, we are telling the whole room what He did. It's a sermon without a preacher.

For a man who feels he has wrecked everything, the second look is the one to hold. The covenant is in His blood, and that blood has already been spent. It doesn’t run out on a bad day.

A memorial, not a new sacrifice

It is not

a second sacrifice

Christ gave Himself once. We do not offer Him again, and the bread does not become anything other than bread. It is not what saves you, and you cannot earn your way to it.

It is

a memorial meal

“In remembrance of me,” twice over. A picture of what He did, that believers keep until He comes: simple, and for the forgiven.

“But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;”

Hebrews 10:12

That is why it is safe to come. The work is finished. At the table you aren't paying for anything. You're remembering that somebody already did.

The cup, and a word to a brother

“But I say unto you, I will not drink henceforth of this fruit of the vine, until that day when I drink it new with you in my Father’s kingdom.”

Matthew 26:29

Jesus called what was in the cup “the fruit of the vine.” Most Baptist churches use grape juice, and some use wine. The cup stands for His blood. What is in it is not the point.

A man in recoveryPastor, could I have a word before Sunday? I don't know what to do about the cup.

His pastorGlad you asked. Let's sort it out together. Nobody has to be tested at that table.

If the cup is a problem for you, because of the taste or smell or what it stirs up, talk to your pastor before the service. Don't skip the table out of shame, and don't take it in fear. A good pastor will work out something that lets you come.

“Unworthily” is about how, not who

  • whoever is an unworthy manno man is worthy, so the table would be empty
  • whoever failed yesterdaythe table is for forgiven sinners, and that is every one of us
  • whoever has a recordJesus ate with men like you, and He still does
  • eat and drink “unworthily”a word that tells how a man comes: in an unworthy manner, like Corinth, shaming the poor and caring nothing for Christ

If somebody has told you that you can't come to the table because of what you've done, hear this: verse 27 doesn't say that. The Corinthians weren't cut off for having a past. They were warned about how they were treating Christ and each other at that very meal.

Let a man examine himself

“Examine” means to test and prove, the way a man checks gold. It isn't a test of how good you've been. It's four honest questions:

  1. ✓Am I trusting Christ's death for my sin?not my record, and not my effort
  2. ✓Is there sin I'm hiding instead of confessing?bring it out, and He is “faithful and just to forgive” (1 John 1:9)
  3. ✓Is there a brother I'm at odds with?go and make it right, then come
  4. ✓Am I here to remember Him?not to be seen, and not to feel good about myself

“Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts: And see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.”

Psalm 139:23–24

Fell yesterday? Then the table is the right place to bring it, not the place to stay away from. Confess it, and eat and drink as a forgiven man.

Hard words in verses 29 to 32

Damnation

judgment

Not hell. The word means a verdict, a judgment from God on how they were behaving. Believers are never damned to hell for this (Romans 8:1).

Not discerning

not seeing

Not seeing what the meal means, and not seeing the brother or sister sitting beside you.

Weak and sickly

ill, and worn down

Some in Corinth were sick, and Paul links it to how they'd been treating the meal. He does not say that every sick man has sinned.

Sleep

have died

The Bible often calls a believer's death “sleep.” They were not lost. They were gone home, early.

Chastened

trained, as a son

Discipline from a Father who loves you, not punishment from a judge (Hebrews 12:6).

Condemned with the world

what God spares us

The world's final judgment. The Lord disciplines His own so that they are not swept into it.

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

Here is what to do with regret. Don't carry it in secret, and don't beat yourself up. Say it plainly to God now, at the point when it is small, and be done with it. “There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus” (Romans 8:1). Being chastened proves you belong to Him.

Tarry one for another.

“Tarry” just means wait. Don't rush ahead and eat your fill while somebody is still on the way in. Make room, and wait until everyone is served. The table is for all of Christ's people, and there is a seat at it for you.

If a man is really hungry, Paul says, let him eat at home first (verse 34). The Lord's table is not for filling the stomach. It is for remembering Him.

And that last line: “the rest will I set in order when I come.” Paul has more to say about the church's meetings, and he does so in the next three chapters.

Today: if somebody new walks in the door of your church or your group, save him a seat. You know what it's like to stand in the courtyard.

Points to remember

  1. The example“Be ye followers of me, even as I also am of Christ” 11:1Follow men only as far as they follow Christ, and somebody is following you.
  2. A hard text“Judge in yourselves: is it comely…?” 11:13Christians apply the head covering differently. What is plain: honour, order, and no fighting over it.
  3. Together“Neither is the man without the woman, neither the woman without the man, in the Lord” 11:11In Christ neither one is a spare part. Honour each other.
  4. The meal“One is hungry, and another is drunken” 11:21The rich ate first and the poor went without. A table that shames anyone is not the Lord's table.
  5. Remembering“This do in remembrance of me” 11:24A memorial of His death for us, kept until He comes. It does not save, and it does not run out.
  6. Examining“Let a man examine himself” 11:28“Unworthily” is about how you come, not what you've done. The table is for forgiven sinners.

Question 1 of 6

The example

Whom does Paul ask us to follow, and how far?

What verses tell us about this?

“Even as I please all men in all things, not seeking mine own profit, but the profit of many, that they may be saved.”

1 Corinthians 10:33

Answer

Paul asks us to follow him only as he follows Christ. Christ is the one to follow, and somebody is watching you follow Him. Today, that is where your example counts.

Question 2 of 6

A hard text

What is plain in Paul's teaching about head coverings, even where Christians differ?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let all things be done decently and in order.”

1 Corinthians 14:40

Answer

Christians read the covering differently: some as a command for today, many as a custom of Corinth. What is plain is honour, order in worship, and no fighting about it.

Question 3 of 6

Together

How do a man and a woman need each other?

What verses tell us about this?

“There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus.”

Galatians 3:28

Answer

Neither is without the other, in the Lord. The first woman came from the man, and every man since has been born of a woman, and both are from God. In Christ, no one is a spare part.

Question 4 of 6

The meal

What went wrong at the Corinthian church supper?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The rich ate and drank first, and the poor who came late went hungry. They shamed the people who had nothing, and Paul says that is not the Lord's supper. The cure is to wait for each other and share.

Question 5 of 6

Remembering

What are we doing when we eat the bread and drink the cup?

What verses tell us about this?

“But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;”

Hebrews 10:12

Answer

We remember Christ's death, and announce it, until He comes back. It is a memorial, not a new sacrifice: the work is finished, and nothing we do at the table adds to it.

Question 6 of 6

Examining

What does it mean to eat “unworthily,” and who may come to the table?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Answer

“Unworthily” describes how a man comes (carelessly, shaming others), not what kind of man he is. No one is worthy. The table is for forgiven sinners who examine themselves, confess, and come to remember Him.
