Proverbs 28

Bold as a lion

Why guilty men run, why forgiven men stand, and the one verse every man here needs to know by heart.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

Bold as a lion

Verses 1–12

Guilt makes a man run. Righteousness lets him stand.

Running from nobody

Guilt makes you jumpy. Every knock on the door, every police car could be the one. Nobody's chasing you, and you still run.

A righteous man stands like a lion. Not because he's tough, but because he has nothing to hide.

That boldness comes from being forgiven. Ordinary men grew bold when they “had been with Jesus” (Acts 4:13).

Who understands?

Evil men

understand not judgment

Sin blinds a man to right and wrong. He really can't see what's wrong with what he's doing. Verse 4: he even starts praising wicked men.

They that seek the LORD

understand all things

Not every fact in the world. But everything they need to know to live right. Seek God, and life starts making sense.

Verse 3 is a gut punch: a poor man who gets a little power and turns on other poor men is like a flash flood that washes the crops away. Remember where you came from, and treat men in that place the way you wanted to be treated.

Your company says something

a man who keeps the lawa wise son
a companion of riotous menshameth his father

“Riotous men” are partiers and gluttons: men who live for the next binge. The same word shows up in Proverbs 23:20–21, with drunkards. You become like the men you run with.

Verse 6: better to be poor and honest than rich and crooked. Verse 8: usury here is charging interest to squeeze the poor, which God's law forbade. Money made that way won't stay with the man who made it.

A one-way conversation

He→turneth away his ear→from God's wordHe won't listen to God.
His prayer→shall be abomination→to GodBut he still expects God to listen to him.

This isn't about a sinner crying out for help. God always hears that prayer: “God be merciful to me a sinner” (Luke 18:13). It's about a man who wants God's help while he plugs his ears to what God says.

Verse 10: and the man who leads others astray falls into his own pit. Be careful who you pull along with you.

Part two

Covered or confessed

Verses 13–18

The verse at the center of the chapter.

Cover it, or bring it out

Covering sin doesn't make it go away. It sits there under the blanket and rots, and a cloud follows you around. “Shall not prosper.”

Confess: pull the cover off. Say the same thing about it that God says. No excuses, no softer name.

Forsake: walk away from it. And where the sin was, there is mercy.

David tried covering it

“When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long. For day and night thy hand was heavy upon me: my moisture is turned into the drought of summer. Selah.”

Psalm 32:3–4

“I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. Selah.”

Psalm 32:5

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Hidden sin wore David out, body and soul. The moment he told the truth, he was forgiven. If you belong to Christ, your sin can't undo your salvation. But hiding it will steal your peace, your strength, and your walk with God.

Two words, then mercy

Confesstell God the truth, and tell a brother
Forsaketurn from it, with a plan and with help
Mercygiven, not earned

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed.”

James 5:16

Confessing to God brings forgiveness. Confessing to a brother you trust brings healing. Verse 14: stay tender toward God (“feareth alway”). A man who hardens his heart falls into mischief.

Four lions

  1. Bold as a lionThe righteous man, standing firm with nothing to hide. (28:1)
  2. A roaring lionA wicked ruler, crushing poor people under him. (28:15)
  3. Your adversary“the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour” (1 Peter 5:8).
  4. The Lion of JudaJesus, who “hath prevailed” (Revelation 5:5).

The devil roars, but he's not the strongest lion in the story. Jesus has already won. Verse 18: the man who walks uprightly is kept safe, while the crooked man falls “at once.”

Part three

Riches and trust

Verses 19–28

Quick money, cheap sellouts, and where to put your trust.

Fast money

The man who works his land has bread. The man who chases “vain persons” and quick schemes ends up with “poverty enough.”

Gambling, hustles, dealing, get-rich-quick deals: they shoot up fast and crash hard. And somewhere along the way, verse 20 says, a man stops being innocent.

“For the love of money is the root of all evil: which while some coveted after, they have erred from the faith, and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.”

1 Timothy 6:10

For a piece of bread that man will transgress.

Playing favorites for a payoff starts small. And the price a man will sell himself for keeps dropping. Addiction teaches that lesson the hard way: what you swore you'd never do, you end up doing for very little.

But you aren't cheap to God. He paid the highest price there is for you:

“For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God’s.”

1 Corinthians 6:20

“It is no transgression”

  • “It's not really stealing”it's family, after all
  • “They'd want me to have it”so you never asked
  • “I'll pay it back”someday
  • It was wrongown it, and make it right where you can

A lot of us took from our own folks to feed a habit, and told ourselves it didn't count. God calls it what it is. Zacchaeus met Jesus and paid back what he'd taken (Luke 19:8). Honesty and making amends are part of walking free.

Don't trust your gut. Trust God.

Trusts his own heart

a fool

“The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?” (Jeremiah 17:9). Your heart will tell you one more time won't hurt.

Trusts in the LORD

made fat

In their world, “fat” meant well fed and flourishing. It's a picture of a full life, not a promise of a fat bank account.

“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.”

Proverbs 3:5

Open eyes, open hands

The man who gives to the poor won't come up short. The man who “hideth his eyes”, who looks away so he doesn't have to help, collects curses instead.

Some of us have been the man on the corner. Now we get to be the one who notices. Remember: proverbs show how life usually works, not a guarantee. But God sees every gift.

Verses 12 and 28 say it twice: when wicked men rule, people hide; when the righteous rise, people come out of hiding and the righteous increase.

Points to remember

  1. Courage“The righteous are bold as a lion” 28:1Guilt makes a man run. Forgiveness lets him stand.
  2. Seeking God“They that seek the LORD understand all things” 28:5Seek God, and you'll know what you need to live right.
  3. Company“A companion of riotous men shameth his father” 28:7You become like the men you run with. Choose them well.
  4. Confession“Whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy” 28:13Hidden sin rots. Confess it, forsake it, and find mercy.
  5. Money“He that maketh haste to be rich shall not be innocent” 28:20Fast money crashes. Steady, honest work is blessed.
  6. Trust“He that trusteth in his own heart is a fool” 28:26Your heart can lie to you. Trust the LORD instead.

Question 1 of 6

Courage

Why do the wicked run when no one is chasing them?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

“Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.”

Acts 4:13

Answer

Because a guilty conscience chases them. They're always waiting to get caught. The righteous man has nothing to hide, so he can stand. That boldness doesn't come from being tough. It comes from being forgiven and from spending time with Jesus.

Question 2 of 6

Seeking God

What do those who seek the LORD understand?

What verses tell us about this?

“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.”

Proverbs 3:5

Answer

“All things” they need to live right: what is just, what is wrong, and how to walk. Evil blinds a man, but seeking God opens his eyes. It starts with listening to His word, not just asking Him to listen to us.

Question 3 of 6

Company

What does the company we keep say about us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A lot. Run with partiers and you'll shame your family. Follow empty men and you'll end up empty. Excuse your own wrongs and you're no better than a destroyer. Choose friends who keep God's ways, and be that kind of friend.

Question 4 of 6

Confession

What happens when a man covers his sin, and what happens when he confesses it?

What verses tell us about this?

“When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long.”

Psalm 32:3

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Covered sin doesn't prosper. It eats a man up from the inside. But when he confesses it and turns from it, he finds mercy. God is faithful to forgive and cleanse. Tell God the truth, tell a brother you trust, and walk away from it with help.

Question 5 of 6

Money

Why is hurrying to get rich so dangerous?

What verses tell us about this?

“But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition.”

1 Timothy 6:9

Answer

Because it costs a man his innocence, and it usually ends in poverty anyway. Fast money is a snare. Steady, honest work and faithfulness bring blessing over time.

Question 6 of 6

Trust

Why is trusting your own heart foolish?

What verses tell us about this?

“The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?”

Jeremiah 17:9

Answer

Because the heart lies. It will tell you what you want to hear, especially when you're craving something. The man who trusts the LORD instead, and walks wisely, is delivered and flourishes.
