Part one

Trust and honor

Verses 1–12

Some of the most-loved verses in the whole Bible.

Written where it counts

A table is a tablet, like the stone tablets of the law. God wants His ways carved into your heart.

Mercy is loyal love. Truth is being a man people can count on. Wear them where everyone can see.

Verse 4 sounds like Jesus as a young man: He “increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man” (Luke 2:52).

Put your whole weight on it

To trust here means to lean on, like trusting a chair when you sit down.

Your own understanding is a reed. Isaiah 36:6 warns that a broken reed, “if a man lean, it will go into his hand, and pierce it.” Most of us have the scars to prove it.

All thine heart. Not most of it, with one corner kept back for the old life.

He straightens the road

To acknowledge Him means to know Him and bring Him into it: your job, your money, your relationships, your recovery. All thy ways.

“Direct” means make straight or make smooth. It doesn't promise an easy life. It promises that God will get you where He's taking you.

“I got this”

Being wise in your own eyes sounds like this:

  • “I can handle one”the first lie of every relapse
  • “I don't need a meeting today”cutting off help when you need it most
  • “Nobody can tell me nothing”the fool's motto from chapter 1
  • fear the LORD, and depart from evilhumble, and moving away from it

Verse 8 says it's “health to thy navel, and marrow to thy bones.” The navel is your middle, your core; marrow is deep strength. Walking humbly with God is good for the whole man.

First, not leftovers

At harvest, Israel brought the first part to God before they knew how big the rest would be. That's trust: God first, before I see if there's enough.

Honoring God with your “substance” means your money, time, and strength. Verse 10's full barns show God caring for those who put Him first. Not a deal to get rich: a son trusting his Father to provide.

The New Testament quotes this

Proverbs 3:12

For whom the LORD loveth he correcteth; even as a father the son in whom he delighteth.

Hebrews 12:6

For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.

Chastening means training: correction that teaches. Hebrews says it's proof you're a son. A man doesn't discipline a stranger's kid. He disciplines his own, because he's invested in how that boy turns out.

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

A judge, or a father?

What it isn't

Punishment

A judge punishes to make a man pay for his crime. For a believer, Jesus already paid. “There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus” (Romans 8:1).

What it is

Correction

A father corrects to make his son better. It can hurt. It's never to pay God back. It's to grow you up.

Not every hard thing is God correcting a sin. Sometimes life is just hard. But when He does correct you, don't despise it and don't quit. It means you're His, and He “delighteth” in you.

Part two

Better than gold

Verses 13–26

The same wisdom God used to build the world, offered to you.

Worth more than anything you can want

silver
fine gold
rubies
wisdom

“Merchandise” is what you'd make trading. Wisdom pays better than any hustle. Money can be lost, stolen, or spent in a weekend. Wisdom stays with you, and it shapes who you become.

A tree of life

After the fall, the way to the tree of life was closed. Wisdom gives a taste of that life now. And Jesus promises the real thing: “To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life” (Revelation 2:7).

The wisdom that built the world

God built the world by wisdom, like a master builder with a plan, from the sky down to the deep waters.

The New Testament tells us who did the building: “All things were made by him” (John 1:3). That's Jesus, the wisdom of God.

That same wisdom is offered to a man trying to hold his life together.

Sweet sleep

A lot of us know the other kind of night: lying awake, sick, looking over your shoulder.

God offers real rest, because “the LORD shall be thy confidence” (v. 26). He keeps your foot from being taken in a trap.

“I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety” (Psalm 4:8).

Part three

Love your neighbor

Verses 27–35

Wisdom shows up in how you treat the man next door.

Don't put off doing good

“Go, and come again”TodayWhen a man needs help and you have it right there, don't send him away to come back later.
“To morrow I will give”TodayOwe someone money? Promised to show up? If it's in your hand now, do it now.

A lot of us burned people with “tomorrow.” Paying back what you owe, keeping your word, helping when you can: that's how trust gets rebuilt, one day at a time.

Five “nots” for living next to people

Withhold notDon't hold back good you owe.v. 27
Say not “to morrow”Don't stall when you can help now.v. 28
Devise not evilDon't scheme against the man who trusts you.v. 29
Strive notDon't pick fights with a man who did you no harm.v. 30
Envy thou notDon't wish you had the oppressor's life.v. 31

The oppressor is the man who gets ahead by force: the one with the car, the cash, and people scared of him. It's easy to envy him. Proverbs says choose none of his ways. Look where his road ends.

Grace for the lowly

Proverbs 3:34

Surely he scorneth the scorners: but he giveth grace unto the lowly.

James 4:6

But he giveth more grace. Wherefore he saith, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.

“His secret” (v. 32) means His close friendship, the kind where you're let in on things. God doesn't keep the humble man at arm's length. He draws him close.

The proud man finds God standing against him. The lowly man, the one who knows he needs help, gets grace. That's good news for every man who came in here broken.

Points to remember

  1. Trusting God“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart” 3:5Lean your whole weight on Him, not on your own reed. He'll straighten the road.
  2. Honoring God“The firstfruits of all thine increase” 3:9Give God the first, not the leftovers, and trust Him to provide.
  3. God as Father“Whom the LORD loveth he correcteth” 3:12His correction proves you're His son. It trains you; it doesn't condemn you.
  4. What lasts“She is more precious than rubies” 3:15Nothing you can want compares with wisdom. It's a tree of life.
  5. Peace“Thy sleep shall be sweet” 3:24The LORD is your confidence, so you can lie down in peace.
  6. Other people“Withhold not good from them to whom it is due” 3:27Do good today, keep your word, and don't envy the man who gets ahead by force.

Question 1 of 6

Trusting God

What does it mean to trust the LORD with all your heart?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It means putting your whole weight on Him instead of on your own thinking, and bringing Him into every part of life. Not most of your heart: all of it. And He promises to make your path straight.

Question 2 of 6

Honoring God

How does a man honor the LORD with what he has?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By giving God the first part, not what's left over, with money, time, and strength. It's an act of trust, not a way to buy a blessing. And part of honoring Him is doing good with what's in your hand.

Question 3 of 6

God as Father

Why does the LORD correct His children?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

Answer

Because He loves them and delights in them, like a good father with his son. Correction isn't punishment; Jesus already paid for our sin. It's training, and afterward it grows peace and righteousness in us.

Question 4 of 6

What lasts

What is wisdom worth compared to money?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

More than silver, gold, or rubies, more than anything you could want. Money comes and goes. Wisdom is a tree of life to the man who holds on to it.

Question 5 of 6

Peace

How can a man lie down without fear?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By making the LORD his confidence. When you walk in His wisdom, you don't have to look over your shoulder. He keeps your foot from the trap, and He gives sweet sleep.

Question 6 of 6

Other people

How should we treat the people around us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Do good when it's in your power, and do it today. Don't scheme, don't pick fights, and don't envy the man who gets ahead by force. Treat your neighbor the way God has treated you: with grace.
