Leviticus 3

The peace offering

Here the offering is a meal. The richest part is burned for the LORD, and all the fat is his. A man who has been made right with God sits down at the table with him.

Part one

From the herd

Verses 1–5

The offering that ends in a meal.

Three offerings, side by side

  1. Burnt offering (chapter 1)all of it burned for the LORD
  2. Meat offering (chapter 2)a handful burned; the rest is the priests'
  3. Peace offering (chapter 3)the fat burned for the LORD, and the man who brought it shares in the meal

This chapter does not say who eats. Leviticus 7:15 does: “the flesh of the sacrifice of his peace offerings for thanksgiving shall be eaten the same day that it is offered.” So the one who brought the offering ate a meal with the LORD's share burned on the altar.

One difference from chapter 1: here the animal may be a male or female (verses 1 and 6), where the burnt offering had to be a male (1:3, 10).

Hard words in verses 3 to 5

  1. peace offeringan offering of fellowship, ending in a shared meal
  2. oblation(verse 1) a gift offered to God
  3. inwardsthe inner parts of the body
  4. flanksthe loins: the sides, below the ribs
  5. caulthe fatty covering over the liver
  6. burnt sacrifice(verse 5) the burnt offering of chapter 1, already on the altar. The peace offering is laid on top of it

The hand, the blood, the door

The same three things as chapter 1: the hand laid on the head, the animal killed “at the door of the tabernacle”, the blood sprinkled on the altar. The words “lay his hand” come three times in this chapter (verses 2, 8 and 13).

Even the offering of peace begins with the blood. A man cannot sit down at the LORD's table until the price has been paid.

“For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.”

Leviticus 17:11

Peace with God never came by being good enough. It comes through a life given in the man's place.

Part two

From the flock

Verses 6–11

A lamb, a man, and a table.

A lamb, and what “hard by” means

  1. a lamba young sheep. Verse 7 sets it apart, with its own words: “If he offer a lamb for his offering”
  2. the whole rumpthe tail end of the sheep, taken off whole, with its fat
  3. hard by the backboneclose beside it: “hard by” is an old way of saying “close to”

A lamb gets a verse of its own (verse 7), and the Bible keeps coming back to the lamb. John the Baptist points at Jesus and says:

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

“The food of the offering”

Verse 11 calls what is burned on the altar “the food” of the LORD's offering. The chapter uses a picture a man can understand: God is host, and the altar is his table.

This is the offering that ends at a table, with a man sitting down in the LORD's presence. In the New Testament it is peace that the blood of Christ makes:

“And, having made peace through the blood of his cross, by him to reconcile all things unto himself; by him, I say, whether they be things in earth, or things in heaven.”

Colossians 1:20

“Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:”

Romans 5:1

Part three

The goat, and all the fat

Verses 12–17

The richest part belongs to the LORD, for ever.

All the fat is the LORD'S

The word “fat” is in this chapter twelve times. Every time, it is set apart: the fat over the inwards, on the kidneys, the rump of the sheep. “All the fat is the LORD'S.” The richest part, the best part, went to him first, before anyone sat down to eat.

Think of how often the best of a man's life goes to everything but God: the best hours, the best pay, the best of his attention. The LORD asked for the best first. Not to take from the man, but to be put first at his own table.

If your best has gone to something else for years, nothing in this chapter says the table is closed. Start with the first hour of the day, today.

Neither fat nor blood

The chapter ends with a statute, a law that stands, “perpetual”, for all their generations and in all their dwellings: no fat and no blood. The fat was the LORD'S, and the blood was the life, as Leviticus 17:11 tells us. The apostles later wrote to the Gentile believers to abstain “from blood” (Acts 15:20).

Everything the people eat now reminds them of two things: the best belongs to God, and the life is his.

“And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.”

Hebrews 9:22

A seat at the table

In the peace offering a man ate in the presence of the LORD, and “all that be clean shall eat thereof” (7:19). Those who were far off were brought near:

“But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ. For he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us;”

Ephesians 2:13–14
  1. Sinit keeps a man from the table
  2. Regreta man who is ashamed stays away from the people who would welcome him
  3. Gracethe blood has been shed, so the price is paid
  4. Hope“our fellowship is with the Father, and with his Son Jesus Christ” (1 John 1:3)
  5. Todaysit down with a brother in Christ, eat, and tell him the truth about yesterday

“By him therefore let us offer the sacrifice of praise to God continually, that is, the fruit of our lips giving thanks to his name. But to do good and to communicate forget not: for with such sacrifices God is well pleased.”

Hebrews 13:15–16

Points to remember

  1. Peace“a sacrifice of peace offering” 3:1An offering that ends in a meal with God.
  2. The hand and the blood“And he shall lay his hand upon the head of his offering, and kill it at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation” 3:2Peace begins with a life given in his place.
  3. The fat“all the fat is the LORD’S” 3:16The best part goes to God first.
  4. Every man“If he offer a lamb for his offering” 3:7Herd, flock or goat: a way in for everyone.
  5. The LORD's table“it is the food of the offering made by fire unto the LORD” 3:11God is the host. Come and eat.
  6. Fat and blood“that ye eat neither fat nor blood” 3:17The life is in the blood (17:11).

Question 4 of 6

Every man

Which animals could a man bring?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

One of the herd, a lamb, or a goat, male or female, without blemish. Whatever he had, there was a way to the table.
