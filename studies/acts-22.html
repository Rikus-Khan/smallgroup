Acts 22

Paul tells his own story

From the castle stairs, with a mob still hungry for blood, Paul tells them what he was, what happened, and what Jesus made of him.

Part one

What I was

Verses 1–5

He starts in their own language, and with the worst of himself.

A testimony in three parts

Verses 3–5What I was

A zealous, religious man, sincerely wrong, who hunted believers.

Verses 6–16What happened

Jesus met him on a road at noon. A light, a voice, a new start.

Verses 17–21What I am now

Sent out as a witness, with a job to do.

“Defence” here is the old word for explaining yourself. Paul doesn't argue theology. He just tells what happened to him. Nobody can argue with that.

It's a pattern any of us can use: what I was, what happened, what I am now. It takes two minutes. By the end of today's study you'll have yours.

Zealous, and wrong

He thought→“zealous toward God”Trained under Gamaliel, the most respected teacher of his day (Acts 5:34). Sincere to the bone.
He was→“binding and delivering into prisons both men and women”“This way” was the first name for followers of Jesus.

Notice how plainly Paul says it. No excuses, and no long gory details either: I persecuted this way unto the death. That's what an honest testimony sounds like.

And notice: you can be very sincere and very wrong. Paul never doubted himself. If you've spent years sure you were fine, or sure you had it under control, you're in good company.

Part two

What happened

Verses 6–16

Noon, a road, a light, and a question.

Saul's road

Born in Tarsus, a busy city in Cilicia, far to the north. Raised in Jerusalem. Then with letters from the high priest, off to Damascus, about 135 miles, roughly a week on foot, to drag believers back in chains.

He was almost there: “come nigh unto Damascus.” God stopped him in the last mile.

A light brighter than the sun

Noon is the brightest hour of the day. Paul later said the light was “above the brightness of the sun” (Acts 26:13).

His companions saw the light, but they “heard not the voice.” In Acts 9 they hear the sound but can't make out the words. Same event: they got the noise, and Saul got the message.

“Why persecutest thou me?”

Saul never laid a hand on Jesus. He went after His people. And Jesus says: that's me. Whatever is done to one of His own, He takes personally.

Then look at what Saul asks, and what he is told. “Who art thou, Lord?” He is answered with a name, Jesus of Nazareth, not a sentence. The Lord he fought has found him, and doesn't destroy him. And the next thing Saul hears is not a punishment but an assignment: “Arise, and go.”

If you've been fighting God, or just running from Him, this is how He meets a man. He calls your name, tells you who He is, and gives you something to do next.

The first thing a believer called him

Saul, the persecutor → Brother Saul A stranger stood by the blind man and the first word was brother.

Ananias was “a devout man according to the law, having a good report.” Saul was the man who had come to arrest people like him. And his first word to Saul was Brother. That's the church at its best: the new man gets a brother before he has earned anything.

The same hour Saul looked up. His sight came back the moment a believer put out a hand. If you're new, or newly fallen, that's what we're for.

“Wash away thy sins”

What takes sin away

Calling on the Lord

Saul had been praying for three days. He called on Jesus, and Jesus saved him. The blood of Jesus cleanses (1 John 1:7).

What shows it

Baptism

A believer's step of obedience that pictures what already happened. It doesn't wash a sin away. It tells everyone whose man you now are.

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

“Why tarriest thou?” means what are you waiting for? If you've trusted Jesus and haven't been baptized, that's the next step. And if you're carrying something you did, don't wait to clean yourself up first. Call on Him now.

Part three

What I am now

Verses 17–21

Back in the temple where it all began, and sent far away.

The coat he never forgot

“And cast him out of the city, and stoned him: and the witnesses laid down their clothes at a young man’s feet, whose name was Saul.”

Acts 7:58

Years later, to the crowd, Paul brings it up himself. He didn't hide it, and he didn't drown in it. Sin named honestly. Regret admitted. And then he keeps going.

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

Paul argued in the temple that he was the perfect man to reach these people: they knew how bad he had been. The Lord said no, and sent him elsewhere. The regret was real. It still wasn't the end of his story.

Far hence

“I will send thee far hence unto the Gentiles.” Three journeys, about AD 46 to 57, and in the end, in chains, Rome. Paul would take the news of Jesus to the whole Roman world.

The man who wrecked the church for a living ended up building it in the places nobody else had gone.

Part four

Paul the Roman

Verses 22–30

One word turns a silent crowd into a mob, and one word from Paul turns soldiers around.

They listened… until he said Gentiles.

They heard him tell about the light, the voice, even the persecution. The word that set them off was God sending him to the outsiders.

Why? Because the same grace that reaches the outsider offends the man who thinks he has earned his place. Throwing off their coats and tossing dust in the air was how a crowd screams. The same crowd wanted the same thing for Jesus.

God's offer is to “whosoever.” It includes the man who thinks he's too far gone, and it includes the man who thinks he doesn't need it. Both are hard to hear.

One question stops the whip

  1. Boundstretched with thongs for scourging
  2. AskedPaul asks the centurion one question
  3. Reportedthe centurion tells the captain: “Take heed what thou doest”
  4. Stopped“Straightway they departed from him”

Roman scourging was brutal: leather whips with bits of metal or bone in them, and men died under it. Roman law said a citizen could never be scourged, and never bound, without a trial. The captain had already done the binding, and he knew it.

Bought, or born

The captain

“With a great sum”

Claudius Lysias (his name tells it: Acts 23:26) had paid for his citizenship. Money bought it, and money could be lost.

Paul

“Free born”

He didn't earn it or buy it. He was born into it. It was his, and nobody could take it away.

“But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his name:”

John 1:12

God's family is the free-born kind. You can't buy your way in, and you can't be thrown out. You are born into it by faith in Jesus, and Paul says our own citizenship “is in heaven” (Philippians 3:20).

Your turn: tell it in three parts

What I was

Say it plainly, no gory details. What was my life like before Jesus?

Paul: “I persecuted this way unto the death.”
What happened

How did I come to Him? Who was there? What did He say to me?

Paul: a light, a voice, “Brother Saul.”
What I am now

What has He changed, even one thing, even a little?

Paul: “his witness unto all men.”

Write three sentences today, one for each part. Don't polish them. Then tell it to one person: a man in this group, a friend, a brother. A story told plainly is hard to argue with.

If you're not sure you have a story yet, that's fine. Part two can start today: “Lord, who art thou?” He answers.

Points to remember

  1. Your story“his witness unto all men of what thou hast seen and heard” 22:15What I was, what happened, what I am now. Tell it plainly.
  2. Honesty“I persecuted this way unto the death” 22:4Name the sin plainly, without excuses or gory details. It makes what Jesus did bigger.
  3. Who Jesus is“Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?” 22:7Jesus takes it personally when His people are hurt, and still meets His enemies with a name and a job, not a sentence.
  4. Calling on the Lord“calling on the name of the Lord” 22:16Sin is washed away by calling on Jesus. Baptism is the believer's step that shows it.
  5. Sent out“I will send thee far hence unto the Gentiles” 22:21Your past doesn't disqualify you. God can send you where you least expect.
  6. Citizenship“But I was free born” 22:28Born into God's family by faith, not bought or earned. It can't be taken away.

Question 1 of 6

Your story

What are the three parts of Paul's story?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

What he was: a zealous man who hunted believers. What happened: Jesus met him on the Damascus road. What he is now: Jesus' witness to all men. Any man can tell his story the same way: before, the meeting, now.

Question 2 of 6

Honesty

Why does Paul tell the crowd the worst about his past?

What verses tell us about this?

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

Answer

Because it's the proof of what Jesus can do. He hides nothing, and he doesn't wallow in it. He says it plainly and moves on to what Jesus did. Your worst chapter, told honestly, is often what helps the next man most.

Question 3 of 6

Who Jesus is

What did Jesus say to the man who was hunting His people?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He called him by name, told him who He was, and told him what to do next. “Why persecutest thou me?” means Jesus takes it personally when His people are hurt. But He answered Saul with a name and a task, not a sentence. That's how He meets a man who has been fighting Him.

Question 4 of 6

Calling on the Lord

What takes sin away: baptism, or calling on the Lord?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Answer

Calling on the Lord. Saul's sins were washed away by Jesus' blood as he called on Him. Baptism is the believer's step of obedience that pictures it, and Ananias says don't put it off. The water doesn't save. Jesus does.

Question 5 of 6

Sent out

Where did the Lord send Paul, and why not Jerusalem?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Far away to the Gentiles. Jerusalem “will not receive thy testimony,” and the Lord had a bigger field for him. It fits the plan of Acts 1:8: the news going to the uttermost part of the earth. God can send even the worst of men a long way.

Question 6 of 6

Citizenship

What did Paul's Roman citizenship do for him?

What verses tell us about this?

“For our conversation is in heaven; from whence also we look for the Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 3:20

Answer

It stopped the scourging, and it kept him alive to reach Rome. He had it by birth, not by money. And his better citizenship is in heaven: given by faith, not bought, and never taken away.
