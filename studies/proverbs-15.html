Part one

Words and the eyes of God

Verses 1–11

What comes out of our mouths, and Who is watching.

Water or gasoline

A soft answer

turneth away wrath

Grievous words

stir up anger

“Grievous” words are words that hurt, words meant to cut. Every man here knows how fast they turn a disagreement into a fight.

A soft answer isn't a weak one. It takes more strength to lower your voice than to raise it. You can still say the true thing, just without the gasoline. Verse 18 says the same: “he that is slow to anger appeaseth strife.”

“Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man.”

Colossians 4:6

In every place

There is nowhere God isn't looking. That's a warning: “beholding the evil.” Nothing done in the dark is hidden from Him (Hebrews 4:13).

It's also a comfort: “and the good.” He sees the temptation you turned down that nobody else knows about. He saw you in that cell, and He never looked away.

“The eyes of the LORD are upon the righteous, and his ears are open unto their cry.”

Psalm 34:15

What God delights in

The wicked

A sacrifice

Religious motions to cover a life he has no plans to change. God calls it an “abomination,” something He can't stand.

The upright

A prayer

No show, no price tag. Just a man talking honestly to God. And God calls it “his delight.”

“And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.”

1 Samuel 15:22

And look at verse 9: God loves the man who “followeth after” righteousness: the one who's chasing it, not the one who's already arrived. That's every one of us.

How much more the heart?

“Hell and destruction” (Hebrew Sheol and Abaddon) are the world of the dead, the deepest, darkest place anyone can imagine. Even that lies open in front of God.

So if He can see all the way down there, how much more can He see inside you? No man fools God about what's in his heart.

That would be terrifying if He didn't love us. But the God who sees everything in your heart is the same God who sent His Son to die for it. He knows the worst, and He still offers you everything.

Part two

Better than treasure

Verses 12–23

A merry heart, a simple meal, a clear road, and good advice.

Salad with love beats steak with hate

A “stalled ox” was fattened in a stall for a feast: the best steak in town. A “dinner of herbs” is a bowl of greens, a poor man's meal.

Hatred ruins the best food. Love makes a plain meal a feast. Some of us had money once, and misery with it. A peaceful home with God in it is worth more than anything we lost.

A continual feast

Verse 13 is honest: “by sorrow of the heart the spirit is broken.” Grief and depression are real, and God doesn't scold us for them.

But verse 15 shows that two men can live through the same hard days and carry them differently. For the afflicted man, every day is evil. The man with a merry heart has a “continual feast”: not because life is easy, but because of what's inside him.

“Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 4:6–7

The lazy road is the hard road

You'd think laziness makes life easier. It does the opposite. Every job put off, every bill ignored, every phone call dodged grows into a thorn bush you have to push through later.

The righteous man's road is “made plain”: built up, level, cleared. Doing today's small duties keeps tomorrow's road open.

Don't plan alone

“Purposes are disappointed” means plans fall apart. Most of our worst decisions were made alone, in our own heads, at the worst possible time. Wise men get advice before they move:

  1. God's wordthe first counsellor
  2. A pastorwho knows the Book
  3. An older believerwho's walked it
  4. An accountability partnerwho knows your story
  5. This groupmen in the same fight

“Where no counsel is, the people fall: but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety.”

Proverbs 11:14

And be a counsellor for someone else. Verse 23: “a word spoken in due season, how good is it!” The right word at the right moment can keep a man from going back.

Part three

The way of life is above

Verses 24–33

God guards the weak, hears the righteous, and lifts up the humble.

God guards the line

In Israel, fields were marked with boundary stones. A proud, greedy neighbor could move them a few feet at night and steal a widow's land. Who was going to stop him?

The LORD. He tears down the proud man's house and puts the widow's border back. God takes the side of people who can't defend themselves.

Verse 27 warns the greedy too: the man who “hateth gifts,” who won't take a bribe or a crooked deal, is the one who “shall live.”

Near or far

God is everywhere (verse 3), so “far” doesn't mean He can't see the wicked. It means He's far from fellowship with a man who wants nothing to do with Him.

So who are “the righteous” that He hears? None of us earned that title. It's a gift: Christ “of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness” (1 Corinthians 1:30). Trust Him, and God hears you as His own son.

Verse 28 is good advice for prayer and for people: the righteous man “studieth to answer”. He thinks before he speaks. The wicked just pours it out.

The road to honour goes down first

Everybody wants the honour. God's order is “before honour is humility.” You go low first: admitting you were wrong, taking correction (verses 31–32), asking for help.

Jesus walked this exact road:

“And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross. Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name:”

Philippians 2:8–9

Points to remember

  1. Words“A soft answer turneth away wrath” 15:1Lower the heat. It takes more strength to answer softly than to fire back.
  2. God sees“The eyes of the LORD are in every place” 15:3He sees the evil and the good. That's a warning and a comfort.
  3. Prayer“The prayer of the upright is his delight” 15:8God isn't after religious show. He delights to hear an honest prayer.
  4. Contentment“Better is little with the fear of the LORD” 15:16A little, with God and love, beats a fortune with trouble.
  5. Counsel“In the multitude of counsellors they are established” 15:22Don't plan alone. Get godly advice before you move.
  6. Humility“Before honour is humility” 15:33The way up is down. Jesus walked that road first.

Question 1 of 6

Words

How can a soft answer turn away wrath?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man.”

Colossians 4:6

Answer

Anger feeds on anger. Grievous words pour fuel on the fire, but a gentle answer gives it nothing to burn. A soft answer can still tell the truth. It just tells it with grace, and that takes more strength than shouting.

Question 2 of 6

God sees

Where are the eyes of the LORD, and why does that matter?

What verses tell us about this?

“The eyes of the LORD are upon the righteous, and his ears are open unto their cry.”

Psalm 34:15

Answer

In every place, even down to the grave and into our hearts. Nothing is hidden, so we can't fool Him. But He also sees the good, and His eyes are on His people to hear their cry. We're never out of His sight, wherever we've been.

Question 3 of 6

Prayer

What prayers does the LORD delight to hear?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The prayers of the upright: men who are honest with Him and are following after righteousness, even if they haven't arrived. God isn't impressed by religious show. Through Jesus, who is our righteousness, even a stumbling prayer is His delight.

Question 4 of 6

Contentment

What is better than great treasure?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A little with the fear of the LORD, and a plain meal where there's love. Treasure that comes with trouble and hatred isn't worth it. The real treasure in a home is righteousness, peace, and love, and no amount of money can buy those.

Question 5 of 6

Counsel

Why does a man need counsellors?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because plans made alone fall apart. None of us sees everything. Wise counsel, and even hard correction, makes a man's plans stand. The man who listens “getteth understanding” and finds himself among the wise.

Question 6 of 6

Humility

What comes before honour?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.”

Luke 14:11

Answer

Humility. God tears down the proud, and He lifts up the humble. The way up starts by going low: admitting we need Him and need help. Jesus humbled Himself all the way to the cross, and God highly exalted Him.
