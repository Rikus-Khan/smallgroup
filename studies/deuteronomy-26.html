Deuteronomy 26

The basket of firstfruits, and “A Syrian ready to perish was my father”

When Israel is in the land, every man is to bring the first of his harvest in a basket and tell his story before the LORD: where he came from, what God did, and what God gave. Then the tithe of the third year, and a people who avouch the LORD as their God, while the LORD avouches them as his own.

Part one

“A Syrian ready to perish was my father”

Verses 1–11

The first of the harvest, and a man telling his story before the LORD.

The first, in a basket

  1. The first“of all the fruit”
  2. A basketcarried by hand
  3. The placewhere his name is
  4. The priest“I profess this day”
  5. The altarset down before the LORD

These are the firstfruits, the first of the harvest, given before the rest was in the barn. It was a man's way of saying the whole crop was the LORD's gift. To profess is to say out loud: “I am come unto the country which the LORD sware unto our fathers.” The promise had come true, and he was standing in it.

“Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase:”

Proverbs 3:9

The confession, in one line

Every man who brought his basket said these words. He started at the bottom, not with his own work: where his family came from, how they cried, what God did. Only then: “now, behold, I have brought.” Evil entreated: treated cruelly. Terribleness: awesome, fearful deeds.

Who was the Syrian?

Jacob. He fled to Syria from his brother with nothing but his staff (Genesis 32:10), and there he served for his wives.

“And Jacob fled into the country of Syria, and Israel served for a wife, and for a wife he kept sheep.”

Hosea 12:12

Ready to perish means about to die, a wanderer with nothing sure. In the famine he went down into Egypt “with a few”: “threescore and ten persons”, as we read in 10:22. Sojourned is lived there as a stranger. And there the few “became there a nation, great, mighty, and populous.”

“And it came to pass in process of time, that the king of Egypt died: and the children of Israel sighed by reason of the bondage, and they cried, and their cry came up unto God by reason of the bondage. And God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob.”

Exodus 2:23–24

Your own confession

You have a story like this one. A man ready to perish was I. Bondage, affliction, a cry from the bottom, and a God who heard.

“That at that time ye were without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from the covenants of promise, having no hope, and without God in the world: But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.”

Ephesians 2:12–13

“Let the redeemed of the LORD say so, whom he hath redeemed from the hand of the enemy;”

Psalm 107:2

Don't be ashamed of where you came from. It is the first line of your thanks. Israel said “my father was ready to perish” in front of the priest, out loud, on purpose. Tell it, as they did, so that God gets the glory for the rest.

The first, and the Firstfruits

The basket ends in joy: “thou shalt rejoice in every good thing… thou, and the Levite, and the stranger that is among you.” The harvest was not eaten alone. The Levite with no land and the stranger far from home sat down at the same feast.

“But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.”

1 Corinthians 15:20

The firstfruits promised the whole harvest was coming. Christ rose first, and every one who has died in him will follow. That is why a man who came out of bondage can rejoice: the best of the harvest is still ahead.

Part two

“The year of tithing”

Verses 12–15

An honest account to God, and a prayer for the whole people.

“I have done according to all”

We met the tithe of the third year in 14:28–29: kept at home, for the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless and the widow. Now Moses gives the words a man said when it was done.

  1. ✓I have brought away the hallowed things out of mine housethe holy share was not kept backverse 13
  2. ✓have given them unto the Levite, and unto the stranger, to the fatherless, and to the widowit went where God saidverse 13
  3. ✓I have not eaten thereof in my mourningnor used it for anything unclean, or for the deadverse 14
  4. ✓I have hearkened to the voice of the LORD my Godhe listened, and did itverse 14

Hallowed means set apart as holy. Ought here means anything at all. Moses does not explain the mourning and the dead; the holy tithe was simply kept clear of them.

An honest account, then a prayer

This is not a man saying he has never sinned. It is a man saying, of one plain duty: I did what you told me with the money. He could say it because he had done it.

Then he prays, and not for himself: “Look down from thy holy habitation, from heaven, and bless thy people Israel, and the land.” Habitation is a dwelling place. He gave to the poor, and asked God to bless everyone.

Could you give God that kind of account for your money? Not perfection, just one plain thing done as he said: the bills paid, the child support sent, something given to a man in need. Start there. It is a good thing to be able to look up and pray with clean hands.

Part three

“Thou hast avouched the LORD this day”

Verses 16–19

Israel claims the LORD, and the LORD claims Israel.

Avouched, both ways

To avouch is to declare openly, to say before witnesses: this one is mine.

Israel→avouched→the LORD, to be their Godto walk in his ways, keep his commandments, and hearken unto his voice (verse 17)
The LORD→avouched→Israel, to be his peculiar peopleas he had promised, to make them high “in praise, and in name, and in honour” (verses 18–19)

Peculiar means his own (we met it in 14:2). What is new here is that it goes both ways, on the same day: Israel says of the LORD, “he is ours”, and the LORD says of Israel, “they are mine”.

Say it out loud

“That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved. For with the heart man believeth unto righteousness; and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.”

Romans 10:9–10

Peter gives Moses' words to everyone who believes in Christ: “a peculiar people” (1 Peter 2:9). You were not saved by keeping Israel's law; Christ “gave himself for us, that he might redeem us… and purify unto himself a peculiar people” (Titus 2:14).

Avouch him today: say plainly, in front of somebody, that the Lord Jesus is yours. And know that he has already said the same of you.

Real world: tell it, then give

  • SinTaking the whole harvest for ourselves, and forgetting where we came from.
  • RegretYears when we were “ready to perish”, and the shame we still carry about them.
  • Grace“The LORD heard our voice” (26:7). He brought us out, and has called us his own (1 Peter 2:9).
  • HopeChrist is “the firstfruits of them that slept” (1 Corinthians 15:20): the rest of the harvest is coming.
  • TodayTell one man your story the way Israel told theirs: where you came from, and what God did. Then give God the first of something, before anything else, and share it.

Points to remember

  1. The basket“thou shalt take of the first of all the fruit of the earth… and shalt put it in a basket” 26:2The first of the harvest, to the place the LORD would choose.
  2. The Syrian“A Syrian ready to perish was my father” 26:5Jacob, who went down with a few and became a great nation.
  3. The cry“the LORD heard our voice, and looked on our affliction” 26:7He heard, he looked, and he brought them out with a mighty hand.
  4. Rejoicing“thou, and the Levite, and the stranger that is among you” 26:11The harvest is a feast, shared with those who have nothing.
  5. The third year“I have hearkened to the voice of the LORD my God” 26:14An honest account of what God said to do, then a prayer for all his people.
  6. Avouched“Thou hast avouched the LORD this day to be thy God” 26:17We claim him, and he claims us as his peculiar people.

Question 1 of 6

The basket

What was the Israelite to put in the basket, and where was he to take it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The first of all the fruit of the land. He took it to the place the LORD would choose to place his name, gave it to the priest, and it was set down before the altar.

Question 2 of 6

The Syrian

Who was the “Syrian ready to perish”, and what became of his family?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Jacob fled into the country of Syria, and Israel served for a wife, and for a wife he kept sheep.”

Hosea 12:12

Answer

Jacob, their father. He went down into Egypt with a few, and there they became a nation, great, mighty and populous.

Question 3 of 6

The cry

What did the LORD do when Israel cried to him in Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He heard their voice and looked on their affliction, labour and oppression, and brought them out of Egypt with a mighty hand and an outstretched arm, with signs and wonders.

Question 4 of 6

Rejoicing

Who was to rejoice together over the good things the LORD gave?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The man and his house, with the Levite and the stranger among them. And in the third year the fatherless and the widow ate and were filled too.

Question 5 of 6

The third year

What was a man to say before the LORD when he had given the third year's tithe?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That he had given the holy tithe where God said and had not misused it, that he had hearkened and done all God commanded; and then he asked God to look down and bless his people and the land.

Question 6 of 6

Avouched

What did Israel avouch that day, and what did the LORD avouch?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Israel avouched the LORD to be their God, to walk in his ways and hearken to his voice. The LORD avouched Israel to be his peculiar people, his own, as he had promised.
