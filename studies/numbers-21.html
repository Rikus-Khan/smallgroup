Numbers 21

Look, and live

A victory at Hormah, where they once ran. A long road round Edom, and the old complaint. Fiery serpents, and a serpent of brass on a pole that Jesus said was about him. Then a well, a song, and two kings who fall.

Part one

“Make thee a fiery serpent”

Verses 1–9

A win, a long road, a bitter mouth, and a remedy nobody could have thought of.

Back at Hormah

Thirty-eight years before

Numbers 14:44–45

“But they presumed to go up unto the hill top: nevertheless the ark of the covenant of the LORD, and Moses, departed not out of the camp. Then the Amalekites came down, and the Canaanites which dwelt in that hill, and smote them, and discomfited them, even unto Hormah.”

Now

Numbers 21:2–3

“If thou wilt indeed deliver this people into my hand, then I will utterly destroy their cities. And the LORD hearkened to the voice of Israel…”

The same south country, the same Canaanites, the same place name. The first time they went up on their own, after Moses told them the LORD was not with them, and they were chased all the way to Hormah. This time they go to the LORD first, and the LORD hands them the victory. A man can win on the very ground where he once fell, if he goes there with the LORD.

Old words: verses 1–3

  • heard tellheard the news
  • the souththe dry south country of Canaan, below the hills
  • by the way of the spiesby the road the spies had taken into the land (13:17, 21)
  • utterly destroygive it wholly up to the LORD, keeping nothing back as spoil
  • Hormahthe name means utter destruction, the same word as “utterly destroyed” in verse 3

Israel's vow was not “give us this, and we'll get rich.” It was the opposite: “give us the victory, and we will keep none of it for ourselves.”

The long way round

Edom had shut the road (20:21). So from mount Hor they went “by the way of the Red sea, to compass the land of Edom”: south, away from the land, then round. Moses says they passed by Edom “from Elath, and from Ezion-gaber” (Deuteronomy 2:8), and Jephthah tells it the same way:

“Then they went along through the wilderness, and compassed the land of Edom, and the land of Moab, and came by the east side of the land of Moab, and pitched on the other side of Arnon, but came not within the border of Moab: for Arnon was the border of Moab.”

Judges 11:18

“Much discouraged because of the way”

The land was right there, and they were walking away from it. Forty years in, they were going backwards. Any man in recovery knows that road: the detour that makes no sense, the months that feel like going in circles.

Then the mouth starts. Read what they said, and set it beside what they had:

What they said

Numbers 21:5

“there is no bread, neither is there any water; and our soul loatheth this light bread.”

What they had

Exodus 16:35; Numbers 20:11

Bread every morning for forty years: “And the children of Israel did eat manna forty years, until they came to a land inhabited; they did eat manna, until they came unto the borders of the land of Canaan.” And not long before, at the rock, “the water came out abundantly.”

“This light bread”

Loatheth is is sick of, can't stand. Light here is worthless, of no weight. They called the bread from heaven worthless. The psalm calls it something else:

“And had rained down manna upon them to eat, and had given them of the corn of heaven. Man did eat angels’ food: he sent them meat to the full.”

Psalm 78:24–25

“For the bread of God is he which cometh down from heaven, and giveth life unto the world.”

John 6:33

The manna was given every day and it was enough every day, and that was the trouble: it was ordinary. A clean, sober, ordinary day can taste like light bread to a man who remembers the old life. It is bread from heaven.

Fiery serpents

They are called fiery serpents, and they bit, and “much people of Israel died.” Paul names the sin behind it, and he names whom they were testing:

“Neither let us tempt Christ, as some of them also tempted, and were destroyed of serpents.”

1 Corinthians 10:9

To tempt here is to try someone's patience, to push to see how far you can go. They were not only grumbling at Moses. They were pushing against the Lord who fed them, and Paul says it was Christ. Moses calls it this: “the people spake against God.”

Two ways to say “We have sinned”

After the spies

Numbers 14:40

“And they rose up early in the morning, and gat them up into the top of the mountain, saying, Lo, we be here, and will go up unto the place which the LORD hath promised: for we have sinned.”

After the serpents

Numbers 21:7

“We have sinned… pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us. And Moses prayed for the people.”

The people say “we have sinned” twice in Numbers (14:40 and here). The first time they said it and went off to fix it themselves, and fell at Hormah. This time they said it and asked for prayer. That is the difference between sorrow that runs and sorrow that comes back:

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Moses prayed for them

They had just spoken against him. “Wherefore have ye brought us up out of Egypt to die?” And the moment they asked, he prayed. No speech, no “I told you so.” Moses doesn't keep score.

Look at what they asked for: “that he take away the serpents from us.” The LORD did not take the serpents away. He gave a remedy instead. Many of us asked God to take the craving away, and the craving is still in the camp. He has not left us without a remedy. Read on.

A look

“Every one that is bitten, when he looketh upon it, shall live.” No medicine, no long walk, no proving he was sorry enough. A bitten man lifted his eyes, and he lived. The worst-bitten man in the camp had the same remedy as the least.

And the thing on the pole was a serpent, in the shape of the very thing that was killing them.

“As Moses lifted up the serpent”

“And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.”

John 3:14–15

We don't have to guess what this picture means. Jesus said it himself, to Nicodemus, one night in Jerusalem. Read it across:

Lifted up from the earth

“And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me. This he said, signifying what death he should die.”

John 12:32–33

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

The brass serpent had no poison in it. It only looked like the thing that killed. Jesus had no sin in him; he “knew no sin.” But on the cross he was made sin for us, lifted up where every bitten man could see him. That is why a look of faith is enough. He did the rest.

Where are you looking?

“Look unto me, and be ye saved, all the ends of the earth: for I am God, and there is none else.”

Isaiah 45:22

“Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God.”

Hebrews 12:2

A bitten man could look at the bite. He could look at the snake, or at the man next to him who was worse. He could go looking for a cure of his own. None of that would save him. The remedy was up on the pole, and he had to look at it.

If you fell yesterday, the bite is real. So is the remedy. Look to Jesus today, not to your failure.

What became of the serpent of brass

“He removed the high places, and brake the images, and cut down the groves, and brake in pieces the brasen serpent that Moses had made: for unto those days the children of Israel did burn incense to it: and he called it Nehushtan.”

2 Kings 18:4

Centuries later, in the days of Hezekiah king of Judah, Israel was burning incense to it. The thing God once used to save them had become an idol. Hezekiah broke it in pieces and called it Nehushtan: a piece of brass.

The power was never in the brass. It was in the LORD who said, “when he looketh upon it, shall live.” A meeting, a program, a church, a preacher can all be used by God. None of them is the remedy. Look to the one they point to: Jesus.

Part two

“Spring up, O well”

Verses 10–20

Camp after camp up the east side, a line the old generation never crossed, and a song.

Across the brook

The valley of Zared looks like one more camp in a list. Moses tells us later it was a line in their history. He calls it the brook Zered:

“And the space in which we came from Kadesh-barnea, until we were come over the brook Zered, was thirty and eight years; until all the generation of the men of war were wasted out from among the host, as the LORD sware unto them.”

Deuteronomy 2:14

Thirty-eight years from Kadesh to this brook, “until all the generation of the men of war were wasted out from among the host.” The men who had said “let us return into Egypt” (14:4) were gone. The men who crossed Zared were their sons, and the LORD kept his word to the sons: “your little ones… them will I bring in” (14:31).

Your father's failures, or your own past, are not the end of the story the LORD is writing.

“The book of the wars of the LORD”

Moses quotes a few lines from a book he names: “the book of the wars of the LORD.” It is not one of the books of our Bible, and Moses doesn't say more about it. He takes from it what the Holy Spirit wanted kept, and no more.

What it remembers is worth seeing: “What he did in the Red sea, and in the brooks of Arnon.” The same LORD at the start of the journey and at the end of it. The wars were his wars.

Arnon is the river that ran down into the Salt Sea, the border between Moab and the Amorites. Ar was a city of Moab. The LORD had told Israel not to touch it: “I have given Ar unto the children of Lot for a possession” (Deuteronomy 2:9).

A well, and a song

“Spring up, O well; sing ye unto it”

This time nobody chode with Moses and nobody struck a rock. The LORD said, “Gather the people together, and I will give them water,” and the princes and nobles dug with their staves, at the word of the lawgiver, Moses, who gave them the law. And Israel sang.

Since the song at the Red sea (Exodus 15:1), the only singing the record mentions in the camp was round the golden calf (Exodus 32:18). Now, near the end of the forty years, the new generation sings over water the LORD gave.

There is a day, after a long dry road, when a man notices he's singing again. Thank God for it, out loud.

What it does fix is the end: the top of Pisgah, over against Jericho (Deuteronomy 34:1), where you can see the land.

Part three

“Fear him not”

Verses 21–35

Words of peace, refused; and a giant the fathers would have run from.

The same request, a different answer from God

Israel asks Sihon almost exactly what it asked Edom (20:17): the king's high way, no fields, no vineyards, no water from the wells. Edom said no, and the LORD had said of Edom, “Meddle not with them” (Deuteronomy 2:5). Sihon said no, and came out to fight. Of Sihon, the LORD had said something else:

“Rise ye up, take your journey, and pass over the river Arnon: behold, I have given into thine hand Sihon the Amorite, king of Heshbon, and his land: begin to possess it, and contend with him in battle.”

Deuteronomy 2:24

“But in the fourth generation they shall come hither again: for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.”

Genesis 15:16

The LORD told Abram the Amorites would have four generations, until their iniquity was full. It was full now. God's patience is long, but it is not endless. And Israel still went with words of peace first.

From Arnon unto Jabbok

Sihon's land ran from the Arnon in the south to the Jabbok in the north, with the Jordan on the west and the children of Ammon on the east, “for the border of the children of Ammon was strong.”

Verse 26 explains why this was not Moab's land any more. Sihon had taken it from “the former king of Moab.” Jephthah argued the same case “three hundred years” later (Judges 11:26):

“And said unto him, Thus saith Jephthah, Israel took not away the land of Moab, nor the land of the children of Ammon:”

Judges 11:15

An old song, turned round

They that speak in proverbs are the men who made the sayings and songs people repeated. This one was the Amorites' song about Sihon's victory over Moab: a fire went out of Heshbon and burned Ar of Moab. Moses quotes it, and now Heshbon itself “is perished even unto Dibon.” The fire came back on the house that lit it.

Chemosh was the god of Moab:

“Then did Solomon build an high place for Chemosh, the abomination of Moab, in the hill that is before Jerusalem, and for Molech, the abomination of the children of Ammon.”

1 Kings 11:7

Look what the song says of him: “he hath given his sons that escaped, and his daughters, into captivity.” The god Moab served could not keep its sons and daughters. Whatever a man serves in place of the LORD ends up handing over his children too. Undone is ruined.

The last of the giants

“For only Og king of Bashan remained of the remnant of giants; behold, his bedstead was a bedstead of iron; is it not in Rabbath of the children of Ammon? nine cubits was the length thereof, and four cubits the breadth of it, after the cubit of a man.”

Deuteronomy 3:11

Thirty-eight years earlier, the spies came back saying “there we saw the giants” and “we were in our own sight as grasshoppers” (13:33). Their sons met a king whose bed was over thirteen feet long, and the LORD said, “Fear him not.”

Who heard about Sihon and Og

“To him which smote great kings: for his mercy endureth for ever: And slew famous kings: for his mercy endureth for ever: Sihon king of the Amorites: for his mercy endureth for ever: And Og the king of Bashan: for his mercy endureth for ever:”

Psalm 136:17–20

Israel sang about these two kings for generations. And the news crossed the river. In Jericho, a harlot named Rahab heard it:

“For we have heard how the LORD dried up the water of the Red sea for you, when ye came out of Egypt; and what ye did unto the two kings of the Amorites, that were on the other side Jordan, Sihon and Og, whom ye utterly destroyed. And as soon as we had heard these things, our hearts did melt, neither did there remain any more courage in any man, because of you: for the LORD your God, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath.”

Joshua 2:10–11

What the LORD did for Israel on the east side of Jordan saved a woman with a past on the west side. Your story of what God has done may be the thing that brings another man home.

Real world: lift up your eyes

This chapter has a win, a fall, a remedy, a song and two giants, all in one stretch of road.

  • SinCalling God's daily bread worthless. Speaking against God when the road is long.
  • Regret“We have sinned.” Say it, and don't run off to fix it alone: ask a brother to pray, as they asked Moses.
  • GraceThe LORD did not leave the bitten to die. He put the remedy up where everyone could see it, and he did the same with his Son.
  • HopeHormah, where they once ran, became a place of victory. The sons crossed the brook the fathers never crossed.
  • TodayWhen the bite comes, look up, not at the bite: pray to Jesus by name. Then call or text a brother and tell him you looked.

Question 1 of 6

Hormah

When king Arad fought against Israel, what did Israel vow, and what did the LORD do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

If the LORD would deliver the Canaanites into their hand, they would utterly destroy their cities and keep nothing. The LORD hearkened and gave them the victory, and the place was called Hormah, where Israel had once been chased (14:45).

Question 2 of 6

The way

Why was the soul of the people discouraged, and what did they say about the bread?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because of the way: the long road round Edom, away from the land. They spoke against God and Moses, and said, “our soul loatheth this light bread”: they called the manna, bread from heaven, worthless.

Question 3 of 6

We have sinned

What did the people say to Moses after the serpents came, and what did Moses do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“We have sinned, for we have spoken against the LORD, and against thee; pray unto the LORD, that he take away the serpents from us.” And Moses prayed for the people, the same people who had just spoken against him.

Question 4 of 6

Look and live

What did the LORD tell Moses to make, and what happened to a bitten man who looked at it?

What verses tell us about this?

“And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up: That whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have eternal life.”

John 3:14–15

Answer

A serpent set upon a pole. Moses made it of brass, and any bitten man who looked at it lived. Jesus said he must be lifted up the same way, so that whosoever believeth in him should not perish.

Question 5 of 6

The well

What did the LORD promise at Beer, and what did Israel do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“I will give them water.” The princes dug the well with their staves, and Israel sang: “Spring up, O well.” No quarrel this time, and no rock struck.

Question 6 of 6

Sihon and Og

What did Israel ask of Sihon, and what did the LORD say to Moses about Og?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To pass through his land by the king's high way, touching nothing. Sihon refused and fought, and fell. Of Og, the giant king of Bashan, the LORD said, “Fear him not: for I have delivered him into thy hand.”
