Leviticus 14

The day of his cleansing

The leper who was shut out of the camp is healed, and the priest goes out to meet him. Two birds, running water, blood and oil, and the road back from the outside to the door of the tabernacle. This is the hope of the whole section.

Part one

“The law of the leper in the day of his cleansing”

Verses 1–9

The priest goes out to the man, and two birds, running water and a washing bring him back to the camp.

The priest goes out to him

Chapter 13 was about finding out whether a man was unclean. Chapter 14 begins with a man who is healed. The law of his cleansing starts with the words “if the plague of leprosy be healed in the leper.”

Ifthe plague be healed in the leper,
→
thenthe priest comes to him, and starts the cleansing.

Look where the priest goes. In chapter 13 the sore was “brought unto the priest.” Here “the priest shall go forth out of the camp.” The man cannot walk into the camp, so the priest walks out. The healing is the LORD's. The priest sees that it is done, and then he brings the man home.

One dies, one goes free

Two clean birds. One is killed over running water. The other is dipped, with the cedar, the scarlet and the hyssop, in its blood, and it is “let loose into the open field.” The man is sprinkled seven times and pronounced clean.

The living bird is set free by the death of the other. Many have read it as a picture of Christ: “delivered for our offences, and raised again for our justification.”

“Who was delivered for our offences, and was raised again for our justification.”

Romans 4:25

Cedar, scarlet and hyssop

  • cedar wooda strong wood from a tall tree
  • scarletred thread or wool
  • hyssopa small plant, used to sprinkle
  • running waterfresh, moving water, not a standing pool
  • seven timescomes four times in this chapter (verses 7, 16, 27 and 51)

The hyssop is the brush that does the sprinkling. David, a man who had done a terrible thing, asked for this very cleansing:

“Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean: wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.”

Psalm 51:7

He knew the leper's law. “Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean.” He did not ask to be excused. He asked to be cleansed, and he asked God to do it.

Back in the camp, but not yet home

  1. the daybirds, wash, shaveverses 4–8
  2. into the campbut not his tentseven days, verse 8
  3. the seventh dayshave, wash againverse 9
  4. the eighth daythe door of the tabernacleverses 10–11

Tarry means stay, wait, and abroad here means outside. He was allowed into the camp, but not yet into his own tent: seven days at the edge of home. Then everything is washed again, “all his hair… his beard and his eyebrows,” and he is clean. It is a slow road back, and every step of it is a step toward home. He is no longer outside.

“Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.”

Hebrews 10:22

“Our hearts sprinkled… and our bodies washed.” The sprinkling and the washing of Leviticus 14 are in that verse.

Part two

“Before the LORD, at the door of the tabernacle”

Verses 10–32

The eighth day, the offerings, the blood and the oil, and the provision for the poor.

  • a meat offeringhere a grain offering: fine flour mixed with oil
  • three tenth dealsthree tenth parts, a measure of flour

Verse 11 puts a man at the door of the tabernacle, in front of the LORD, and verse 13 says the offering is killed “in the holy place.” He had been outside the camp a short time before.

From outside the camp to the door

Picture the whole way. Once he was outside the camp, alone, crying “Unclean, unclean.” On the eighth day he walks in through the gate of the court and stands “at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation… before the LORD,” the very place where the priests stand. The LORD who dwells among his people stands in front of the man who was put out.

Ear, thumb and toe

The priest puts the blood of the trespass offering on three places: the tip of the right ear, the thumb of the right hand, and the great toe of the right foot. Then oil on the same three places, “upon the blood.” The rest of the oil goes on his head.

These are exactly the places Moses marked on Aaron and his sons when they were made priests:

“Then shalt thou kill the ram, and take of his blood, and put it upon the tip of the right ear of Aaron, and upon the tip of the right ear of his sons, and upon the thumb of their right hand, and upon the great toe of their right foot, and sprinkle the blood upon the altar round about.”

Exodus 29:20

Marked like a priest

Moses does not say what each mark means, and we should not invent it. But look at what is plain. The same blood and the same oil that set Aaron apart for the LORD's service are put on a man who a few days earlier was an outcast.

  • the earthe part of a man that hears
  • the handthe part that works
  • the footthe part that walks

Many read the three places as the whole man: hearing God, working for him, and walking with him. Whatever else it says, it says that the man the camp shut out is welcomed back and set apart. The New Testament says this of every believer:

“But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:”

1 Peter 2:9

Four offerings for one man

  • the trespass offeringfirst, with its blood (12–14)
  • the sin offeringfor his uncleanness (19)
  • the burnt offeringgiven wholly to God (19–20)
  • the meat offeringfine flour and oil (20)

“Atonement” comes seven times in this chapter. Even a healed man is not simply waved through: blood is shed, and “the priest shall make an atonement for him before the LORD.” Atonement means a covering, a making peace between a man and God.

“For if the blood of bulls and of goats, and the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean, sanctifieth to the purifying of the flesh: How much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?”

Hebrews 9:13–14

Those offerings had to be brought again and again. Hebrews says what Christ's one offering did:

“By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.”

Hebrews 10:10

Such as he is able to get

If he is rich enough

three lambs

Two he lambs and a ewe lamb, three tenth deals of flour and a log of oil (verse 10).

If he is poor

one lamb, and two doves

One lamb, one tenth deal of flour, a log of oil, and two turtledoves or two young pigeons (verses 21–22). The same oil, the same blood, the same atonement.

“Such as he is able to get” comes twice (verses 22 and 31), and “such as he can get” once more (verse 30). The LORD does not ask a man for what he does not have. The poorest man in the camp is cleansed the same as the richest.

Part three

“A plague of leprosy in a house”

Verses 33–53

A law for the land they have not reached yet.

A law for a house they do not have yet

They are camped at Sinai in tents. This law says “when ye be come into the land of Canaan, which I give to you for a possession.” God is speaking of houses before they have a roof. He has already decided that they will get there.

“I put the plague of leprosy in a house.” Here it is God who says he puts it. We do not have to know why: what matters is what he gives next, a plain way to know when the house is clean.

The house, step by step

  1. empty itbefore the priest comes inverse 36
  2. shut it upseven daysverse 38
  3. take outthe stones with the plagueverse 40
  4. scrape, and plasterother stones, other morterverses 41–42
  5. if it comes backbreak the house downverse 45

Strakes are streaks or hollows, here sunken marks “lower than the wall.” Plaister is plaster. Morter is mortar, the mix that holds the stones. Fretting is eating away. Until the even (verse 46) means until evening. Notice the first step: empty the house first, “that all that is in the house be not made unclean.” The priest guarded the man's belongings before he looked at the walls.

Even the house is cleansed

When the house is pronounced clean, the cleansing is the same as for the man: two birds, cedar, scarlet, hyssop, running water, blood, and the living bird let go (verses 49–53). “Seven times” is sprinkled on the house too. And the verse says it plainly: “make an atonement for the house: and it shall be clean.”

The house had only two roads: cleansed, or broken down. A man's life is not a house, and the Bible says he can be washed. But any man who has had to clear out his own house knows this law: take out what the plague is in, scrape it, replace it, and if it keeps coming back, do not keep living with it.

In real life that may mean clearing out what a man cannot keep beside him: bottles, a phone number, an old stash, a site on his phone. The law does not say that, and we should not put words in it. But it does show that God cares about the house, the walls and what is in them, not only about the man.

Part four

“To teach when it is unclean, and when it is clean”

Verses 54–57

What all this law was for.

The way back in

  1. outsidealone, crying “Unclean”13:45–46
  2. the priest comes outand finds him healed14:3
  3. blood, water, a bird set freeand he is pronounced clean14:4–7
  4. washed, shaved, seven daysin the camp, not yet in his tent14:8–9
  5. the eighth dayblood and oil at the door14:10–20

Chapter 13 ends “This is the law of the plague of leprosy” (13:59), and chapter 14 ends “this is the law of leprosy” (14:57). One chapter tells a man what makes him unclean. The other tells him how to come all the way home.

“The gift that Moses commanded”

When Jesus healed a leper in Matthew 8, he sent him straight to this chapter:

“And Jesus put forth his hand, and touched him, saying, I will; be thou clean. And immediately his leprosy was cleansed. And Jesus saith unto him, See thou tell no man; but go thy way, shew thyself to the priest, and offer the gift that Moses commanded, for a testimony unto them.”

Matthew 8:3–4

The leper was cleansed first, by a touch and a word. Then he was to go to the priest and bring “the gift that Moses commanded,” the very offerings we have just read. Jesus did not skip the law. He is the one the law was pointing to, and he sent the man to do it. On the road to Jerusalem, ten lepers cried for mercy from afar off, and “as they went, they were cleansed.” Only one came back, and he was a Samaritan (Luke 17:12–19).

“And one of them, when he saw that he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God, And fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan.”

Luke 17:15–16

Real world: your day of cleansing

A man who has been outside, ashamed, has a road home. It is not instant, and it is not impossible.

  • SinIt cuts a man off. This chapter is what comes after.
  • RegretThe man cried “Unclean” alone. Then a priest walked out to meet him. You are not too far out.
  • GraceOne bird dies, one goes free; blood, then oil; the same marks as the priests; and the poor man cleansed as the rich.
  • HopeThe chapter ends with a man standing before the LORD at the door, clean.
  • TodayWash, start over, and stay near: do not try to go from the far edge to the front row in one day. Seven days at the edge of the camp is the law's own pace. Call or text a brother and tell him you are walking back in.

“Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits: Who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases;”

Psalm 103:2–3

Question 1 of 6

The priest

Where did the priest go to look at the leper?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Out of the camp, to the man. He was healed, and the priest went out to meet him and begin his cleansing. The leper could not walk in, so the priest walked out.

Question 2 of 6

The birds

What happened to the two birds?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

One was killed over running water. The other was dipped in its blood, with the cedar, scarlet and hyssop, and let loose into the open field. One dies, one goes free: a picture of Christ, delivered for our offences and raised for our justification.

Question 3 of 6

The waiting

Where did the cleansed man wait seven days, and what did he do on the seventh?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In the camp but out of his own tent, seven days. On the seventh day he shaved off all his hair again and washed his clothes and his flesh, and was clean. Then on the eighth day he came to the door of the tabernacle.

Question 4 of 6

The eighth day

Where did he stand on the eighth day, and what was put on his ear, thumb and toe?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Before the LORD, at the door of the tabernacle. Blood of the trespass offering went on the tip of his right ear, the thumb of his right hand and the great toe of his right foot, and then oil on the same places. The blood went where Moses had put it on Aaron and his sons (Exodus 29:20).

Question 5 of 6

The poor man

What if he was too poor to bring so much?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He brought one lamb, a tenth deal of flour, a log of oil, and two turtledoves or two young pigeons, “such as he is able to get.” The poorest man was cleansed as fully as the richest.

Question 6 of 6

The house

What did the priest do when the plague was in a house?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He had the house emptied, shut it up seven days, took out the bad stones, scraped and replastered it. If the plague came back, the house was broken down; if not, he cleansed it with the two birds, and it was clean.
