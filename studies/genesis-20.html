Genesis 20

The same lie, and the God who guards

Abraham moves to Gerar and, afraid, says the same thing he said in Egypt: “She is my sister.” A king takes Sarah, and God steps in with a dream. A chapter about a sin that comes back, and a God who doesn't let go.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

She is my sister

Verses 1–7

The man who pleaded for Sodom is caught in an old habit, and God speaks in the night.

Gerar

Abraham packs up and heads south, to the dry country toward Egypt. “Between Kadesh and Shur” is the edge of the desert. “Sojourned” means lived as a stranger.

Genesis doesn't say why he left Mamre. It just says that he went, and that in a new place, among people he didn't know, the old fear came with him.

“Abimelech” means my father is king. It may be a title, like Pharaoh. There is another Abimelech of Gerar in chapter 26.

Again

“Say, I pray thee, thou art my sister: that it may be well with me for thy sake; and my soul shall live because of thee.”

Genesis 12:13
  1. Egypt, about 2090 BCa famine, fear, and “say thou art my sister” (12:10–13)
  2. Gerar, about 2066 BCfear again: “She is my sister” (20:2)
  3. Same rescuea king is struck by God, and Sarah is sent back (12:17–20, 20:3–7)
  4. Same rebukea heathen king says: “What hast thou done unto me?” (12:18, 20:9)

Twenty-four years apart, and the same sin. Abraham had been through Egypt, through Hagar, through a covenant and a new name. And when he felt cornered, he did what he had always done.

Some of you know this one. You swore never again, and then it was “again”. This chapter isn't here to shame you. It is here to show that God knew this about Abraham, and still stayed.

God speaks to a king in the night

God, in a dreamBehold, thou art but a dead man, for the woman which thou hast taken; for she is a man's wife.

AbimelechLord, wilt thou slay also a righteous nation? … in the integrity of my heart and innocency of my hands have I done this.

Two old words: integrity is a whole, honest heart, and innocency is innocence, clean hands. Abimelech says he did it believing Sarah was single. “Abimelech had not come near her,” verse 4 says.

Notice who is speaking in the night: the LORD of Abraham comes to a man outside the covenant. And Abimelech answers like a man who knows he is talking to a Judge. A heathen king shows more honesty here than the man of God.

Who guarded Sarah?

What Abraham did

He handed her over

To save his own skin, he let another man take his wife. It was exactly what he was supposed to protect her from.

What God did

“I also withheld thee”

God kept Abimelech from touching her. He stopped the sin before it was done, and he had been watching over her from the start.

“He suffered no man to do them wrong: yea, he reproved kings for their sakes; Saying, Touch not mine anointed, and do my prophets no harm.”

Psalm 105:14-15

God guarded the woman who would be the mother of Isaac, the child of promise, even while her husband failed her. This is grace: God protecting what we failed to protect. Some of you have people in your life you put in harm's way. Pray for their protection, and then do the work of making things right.

“He is a prophet”

נָבִיא said navi “prophet”: a man God speaks through, who stands before him for others

This is the first time the word prophet appears in the Bible, and it is used of Abraham, a man who has just lied. The title is not because he is perfect. It is because God has spoken to him, and God hears him when he prays.

“He shall pray for thee, and thou shalt live.” The very man Abimelech has wronged is the one who can pray him out of danger. God doesn't take back his gifts because his servant failed.

Part two

What hast thou done unto us?

Verses 8–13

A pagan king rebukes a prophet, and the prophet has excuses.

“The fear of God is not in this place”

What Abraham expected

“Surely the fear of God is not in this place”

Verse 11. He assumed the people of Gerar were godless and would kill him for his wife.

What he found

“the men were sore afraid”

Verse 8. At the first word from God, Abimelech got up early and told everyone, and they were “sore afraid”, very afraid. He said at once that what Abraham did “ought not to be done”.

Abraham had judged a whole town from the outside, and he was wrong. The fear of God was there. The one who didn't show it was the one who should have.

Sometimes the world sees our hypocrisy more clearly than we do. When someone outside the faith calls out something you did, don't first ask whether they have any right. Ask whether they are right. (“Offended” in verse 9 means wronged.)

Three excuses

  • “I thought, surely the fear of God is not in this place”an assumption, and it was wrong (v. 11)
  • “yet indeed she is my sister”a half-truth, true on paper (v. 12)
  • “when God caused me to wander”a hint that it was partly God's fault (v. 13)
  • “I was afraid, and I lied”the sentence the chapter never records

Abraham explains, and explains. The text records no apology. And that is how we do it, too: we explain before we apologize. The assumption, the technicality and the blame are the three oldest excuses there are. (“Thy kindness” in verse 13 is the loyal favour he asked of Sarah.)

Honest confession sounds different. It sounds like a man in the Psalms: “I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid.” (Psalm 32:5)

Is a half-truth still a lie?

TrueSarah is Abraham's half-sister (v. 12)
→
But he said it so thatAbimelech would think she was not his wife
→
So it wasa lie that wears true words

Sarah really was his father's daughter (20:12). Marrying a half-sister was allowed in that day, and the law of Moses later forbade it. But the aim of “She is my sister” was to make a king believe something false. That is deceit, however true each word was.

Think of the half-truths men tell: “I only had a couple.” “I was at a friend's.” “I didn't say I wasn't going.” A half-truth told to hide something is a whole lie. Say the whole thing, to God and to your brothers in Christ.

The snare

Abraham lied because he was afraid of men: they will slay me for my wife's sake. And his fear made him do the very thing that put his wife in danger.

“The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.”

Proverbs 29:25

Where does the fear of man trap you? A boss, the guys at the job site, a family member, a crowd of old friends. It makes us say “yes” when we mean “no”, or tell a small lie to keep the peace.

The cure isn't to be braver. It is to remember Who is really watching, and Who has already guarded you. Abraham was kept safe by God even in his failure. Trust him with the thing you are afraid of.

Part three

Abraham prayed

Verses 14–18

Sarah comes home, gifts are given, and the man who sinned is the one who prays.

What Abimelech gave

  1. sheep and oxenflocks and herds
  2. menservants and womenservantshousehold workers
  3. Sarah“restored”, untouched
  4. his land“dwell where it pleaseth thee”
  5. a thousand pieces of silvera large sum (not coins as we know them; a weight of silver)

“A covering of the eyes” (verse 16) was a public sign that cleared her name. Everyone could see she had come to no harm. “Reproved” here means set right, cleared: the matter was settled openly.

Abraham came out richer than he went in, and it was the second time (12:16). That doesn't make the lie right. It shows the generosity of God, who is good to people who haven't earned it.

The sinner who prays

Verse 18 explains why: the LORD “had fast closed up all the wombs of the house of Abimelech”. “Fast” means tightly. God had closed the household, and when Sarah was safe and the matter put right, Abraham's prayer opened it again.

Look at who prays. Not a perfect man, but a sinner who has just been shown up, praying for the man he wronged. Honest prayer can start in the middle of a mess.

“My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:”

1 John 2:1

When Abraham failed, God still listened to him. When we fail, we have more than a prophet. We have Jesus Christ the righteous, speaking for us.

Why it comes round again

Egypt, then Gerar: fear, a half-truth, found out, rescued by grace, and then fear again. Rescue without change just resets the circle.

What breaks it isn't a stronger will. It is the sentence Abraham never said, “I was afraid, and I lied”, said out loud to God and to the people wronged. And a few brothers close enough to ask, when you feel afraid, “what are you about to say?”

God did not give up on Abraham

What comes in the very next chapter?

“And the LORD visited Sarah as he had said, and the LORD did unto Sarah as he had spoken.”

Genesis 21:1

“For Sarah conceived, and bare Abraham a son in his old age, at the set time of which God had spoken to him.”

Genesis 21:2

Abraham told the same lie twice, and the promise did not move. Isaac was born “at the set time”, the one God had named. The promise never rested on how well Abraham behaved. It rested on the God who made it.

If you have repeated the same sin, you are not out of the story. Say it plainly, go to your pastor and your brothers, and keep going. His promise to you in Christ doesn't depend on how well you did this month.

Points to remember

  1. The same sin“She is my sister” 20:2Abraham fell again into the old sin. A man can repeat a sin he thought he was done with.
  2. Fear“Surely the fear of God is not in this place” 20:11His fear of men made him judge wrongly, and he was wrong about Gerar.
  3. Protection“I also withheld thee from sinning against me” 20:6God guarded what Abraham failed to guard. That is grace.
  4. Half-truths“And yet indeed she is my sister” 20:12A half-truth told to hide something is still a lie. Say the whole thing.
  5. Prayer“Abraham prayed unto God: and God healed Abimelech” 20:17A sinner can pray, and God hears. We have Jesus Christ the righteous as our advocate.
  6. Hope“the LORD visited Sarah as he had said” 21:1The very next chapter, God kept his promise. He doesn't give up on his own.

Question 1 of 6

The same sin

What did Abraham do here that he had done before?

What verses tell us about this?

“Say, I pray thee, thou art my sister: that it may be well with me for thy sake; and my soul shall live because of thee.”

Genesis 12:13

Answer

He passed Sarah off as his sister, as he had done in Egypt twenty-four years before, and let a king take her. The same fear and the same lie, a second time. It happens to believers. Don't hide it; go to God and to your brothers in Christ.

Question 2 of 6

Fear

Why did Abraham lie, and was he right about the people of Gerar?

What verses tell us about this?

“The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.”

Proverbs 29:25

Answer

He was afraid they would kill him for his wife. He was wrong: the men of Gerar were “sore afraid” of God as soon as they heard. The fear of man is a snare. Trust the LORD with the thing you are afraid of.

Question 3 of 6

Protection

Who kept Sarah safe, and how?

What verses tell us about this?

“He suffered no man to do them wrong: yea, he reproved kings for their sakes; Saying, Touch not mine anointed, and do my prophets no harm.”

Psalm 105:14-15

Answer

God did. He came to Abimelech in a dream, warned him, and stopped him from touching her. Abraham had put her in danger, and God guarded her anyway. That is grace.

Question 4 of 6

Half-truths

Is a half-truth still a lie?

What verses tell us about this?

“I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. Selah.”

Psalm 32:5

Answer

Yes, when it is told to make someone believe something false. Sarah really was his half-sister, but he used it to hide that she was his wife. Say the whole truth, to God and to a brother.

Question 5 of 6

Prayer

What happened when Abraham prayed for Abimelech?

What verses tell us about this?

“My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:”

1 John 2:1

Answer

God healed Abimelech and his household, and they bore children again. A man who had just failed was still the one God heard. And we have an advocate, Jesus Christ the righteous, who speaks for us when we fail.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

Does God give up on a man who repeats the same sin?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD visited Sarah as he had said, and the LORD did unto Sarah as he had spoken.”

Genesis 21:1

“For Sarah conceived, and bare Abraham a son in his old age, at the set time of which God had spoken to him.”

Genesis 21:2

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

No. Abraham told the same lie twice, and God kept the promise at the set time. If you have fallen into the same sin again, confess it, bring in your pastor and your brothers in Christ, and keep going. He is faithful.
