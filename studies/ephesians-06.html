Chapter 5 took the new life home to husbands and wives. Chapter 6 finishes the house: children and fathers, then the workplace. Then Paul pulls back the curtain on the fight behind it all.

It's the last chapter of the letter. At the end of the study we'll look back over all six.

Part one

At home

Verses 1–4

A word for sons, and a harder one for fathers.

The first with a promise

Paul quotes the fifth commandment: “Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee” (Exodus 20:12). It opens the commands about people, and it comes with a promise.

Some of us had parents who drank, hit, or left. Honour doesn't mean pretending it didn't happen. It means no cursing them, no revenge, forgiving them, and caring for them when they're old, if they'll let you.

Fathers

Provoke not to wrath

What makes a kid angry

Broken promises. Yelling. Never pleased. Rules for them, none for you. Showing up drunk, or not showing up.

Nurture and admonition

Train them, and talk to them

“Nurture” is training, the steady kind. “Admonition” is putting it in their mind: warning and teaching with words, gently.

“Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged.”

Colossians 3:21

Rebuilding trust with your kids

Trust breaks in a day and grows back slowly. Don't promise big; promise small, and keep it. Don't demand their trust. Earn it, and let them be angry for a while.

“And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers, lest I come and smite the earth with a curse.”

Malachi 4:6

Part two

At work

Verses 5–9

Whoever signs your paycheck, you work for Christ.

Servants in Ephesus

These “servants” were slaves, and many believers in a city like Ephesus were. Paul isn't defending slavery. He tells believers how to live like Christ inside the world as it was.

And the gospel was already undoing it. A runaway slave named Onesimus was sent back to his master with a letter from Paul: take him back “not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved” (Philemon 16). Over time, that kind of teaching broke the chains.

Most of us aren't slaves. But we have a foreman, a manager, a boss. Paul's words fit the job site just fine.

“Eyeservice” is working for the boss's eyes: busy when he's looking, on the phone when he's not. A believer works the same in every hour, because his real Boss never looks away.

Nobody may thank you for it. Verse 8 says the Lord sees every good thing, and He pays back, in His time. For a man with a record, a steady, honest work life is one of the loudest testimonies there is.

If you're the boss

Some of you will run a crew one day, or already do. “Do the same things unto them”: the same good will you'd want from them.

“Forbearing threatening”: no ruling by fear, no “do it or you're fired” for sport. You have a Master too, and He doesn't play favorites. Boss and worker stand on level ground before Him.

Part three

The whole armour of God

Verses 10–20

The fight behind every other fight, and what God gives you to stand.

Who you're really fighting

  • not the probation officeror the judge
  • not your exor her lawyer
  • not the boss, not the old crewthey're flesh and blood, people Christ died for
  • the wiles of the deviland the powers of darkness behind him

“Wiles” means schemes, tricks, a planned method. The devil doesn't charge in the front door. He waits for the bad day, the lonely night, the payday, the old phone number. That's “the evil day.”

Strong in His strength, standing on your feet

Paul doesn't say be strong. He says be strong in the Lord: in His might, not yours. Willpower alone has lost this fight before. His power hasn't.

And four times he says stand (verses 11, 13, 13, 14). Not charge. Not win the whole war today. The battle was won at the cross. Your job is to hold the ground He gave you: still standing at the end of the day.

“Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.”

James 4:7

“And when we came to Rome, the centurion delivered the prisoners to the captain of the guard: but Paul was suffered to dwell by himself with a soldier that kept him.”

Acts 28:16

Day and night, a Roman soldier guarded him. Paul had time to study the man's gear: belt, breastplate, boots, shield, helmet, sword. He calls himself “an ambassador in bonds” (v. 20). The chain was real.

Three pieces you never take off

Verses 14 and 15 say “having”: these are already on, worn all day, every day.

The belt of truth

“loins girt about with truth”

A Roman's wide belt held his tunic up and his sword on. Nothing stays in place without it.

Today: no lies, not even small ones. A lie is where the old life gets back in.

The breastplate of righteousness

“having on the breastplate”

It covered the heart and lungs, where one blow kills.

Today: Christ's righteousness covers you, so the accuser can't land the killing blow. And a clean life keeps the heart guarded.

Shoes of the gospel of peace

“feet shod with the preparation”

Roman boots had iron studs for grip. A soldier who loses his footing is finished.

Today: you have peace with God; stand on it. And be ready to carry that peace to the next man.

Three pieces you take up

Now the words change to “taking” and “take”: reach for these when the fight comes.

The shield of faith

“quench all the fiery darts”

The big Roman shield covered almost the whole man. Enemies shot arrows wrapped in burning pitch.

Today: the dart is a thought: one won't hurt, you'll never change. Faith believes God over it, and the fire goes out.

The helmet of salvation

“take the helmet of salvation”

It guarded the head. No soldier fought bareheaded.

Today: guard your mind with what's settled: you are saved, sealed, and kept. Shame doesn't get the last word.

The sword of the Spirit

“which is the word of God”

The short Roman sword was for close fighting, face to face. The only weapon in the list.

Today: learn one verse by heart, so it's in your hand when the fight is close.

Put it on, piece by piece

  1. The belt“having your loins girt about with truth”
  2. The breastplate“the breastplate of righteousness”
  3. The boots“your feet shod with… the gospel of peace”
  4. The shield“the shield of faith… quench all the fiery darts”
  5. The helmet“the helmet of salvation”
  6. The sword“the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God”
  7. And over it all, prayer“praying always with all prayer… in the Spirit”

Whose armour is it?

It's called “the whole armour of God”, and it really is His. Isaiah saw the LORD Himself dressed for battle in it:

“For he put on righteousness as a breastplate, and an helmet of salvation upon his head; and he put on the garments of vengeance for clothing, and was clad with zeal as a cloke.”

Isaiah 59:17

You aren't asked to make your own armour out of good intentions. God hands you His. Truth, righteousness, peace, faith and salvation are all gifts of Christ before they are anything you do.

And notice there's nothing for your back. A soldier in this armour doesn't run. He stands, faces it, and keeps his brothers on either side.

It is written

When Satan tempted Jesus in the wilderness, Jesus didn't argue. Three times He answered, “It is written” (Matthew 4:4). That's how the sword works.

the dartOne drink won't hurt.

the sword: Proverbs 20:1“Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging: and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise.”

the dartYou'll never change.

the sword: 2 Corinthians 5:17“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

A sword you don't own can't help you. Pick one verse for the dart that hits you most, and learn it by heart today.

The line back to headquarters

A soldier cut off from his commander is in trouble, however good his armour. Prayer keeps the line open, “always,” not only in the emergency.

“For all saints”: the man next to you is in the same fight. Pray for him by name today, and text him that you did.

Look what Paul asks for himself. Not to get out of the chains, but to speak boldly in them. An ambassador was supposed to be untouchable; Christ's ambassador was in chains, and still on duty.

“Pray without ceasing.”

1 Thessalonians 5:17

Part four

Tychicus

Verses 21–24

The man who carried the letter, and Paul's last word.

The mailman

There was no post office. Tychicus, a believer from Asia (Acts 20:4), carried this letter by hand, hundreds of miles, by road and sea. The way he went isn't known.

He carried Colossians on the same trip, with Onesimus, the runaway slave, going home (Colossians 4:7–9).

Paul calls him “beloved” and “faithful.” No miracles, no sermons we know of. Just a man you could trust to deliver. Years later, Paul sent him to Ephesus again (2 Timothy 4:12).

Peace, love, faith, grace

The letter opened with “Grace be to you, and peace” (1:2), and it closes the same way. Everything in between was grace.

“In sincerity” is literally in incorruption: a love for Jesus that doesn't rot or wear out. Not a love that never stumbles; a love that keeps coming back.

Today: be somebody's Tychicus. Carry a word of comfort to one brother. “That he might comfort your hearts.”

Part five

Looking back

The whole letter

That was the last chapter. Here is where Ephesians has taken us.

Six chapters: sit, walk, stand

  1. 1Every blessing, in Christchosen in Him; sealed
  2. 2Dead, then made aliveby grace, through faith
  3. 3The mystery made knownJew and Gentile, one body
  4. 4Walk worthyput off, put on
  5. 5Walk in love and lightfilled with the Spirit
  6. 6Standthe whole armour

Notice the order. Paul spends three chapters telling you what God has done before he tells you to do anything. You sit in what Christ won, then you walk it out, then you stand your ground. Never the other way round.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

What this letter gave us

  1. Sin“dead in trespasses and sins” (2:1): that's where every one of us started
  2. Regret“ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ” (2:13)
  3. Grace“by grace are ye saved through faith” (2:8)
  4. Hope“exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think” (3:20)
  5. The real world“be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another” (4:32)

If you remember one thing, make it this: you are in Christ. Paul says it, or something like it, again and again from the first chapter to the last. Chosen in Him, forgiven in Him, sealed in Him, and strong in Him.

So wherever you fell yesterday, get up today, put on the armour, and stand. “Be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might.”

Points to remember

  1. Home“Ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath” 6:4Honour your parents, even imperfect ones. Fathers: train gently, and rebuild trust one kept promise at a time.
  2. Work“Not with eyeservice… but as the servants of Christ” 6:6Work the same in every hour. Your real Boss never looks away, and He repays.
  3. The enemy“We wrestle not against flesh and blood” 6:12People aren't the enemy. The devil's schemes are. Be strong in the Lord, and stand.
  4. The armour“Put on the whole armour of God” 6:11Truth, righteousness, peace, faith, salvation, and the word of God: God's own armour, given to you.
  5. Prayer“Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit” 6:18Prayer keeps the line to the Commander open, for you and for every brother in the fight.
  6. The whole letter“Be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might” 6:10In Christ you are chosen, forgiven, sealed and strong. Sit in it, walk it out, and stand.

Question 1 of 6

Home

What does God ask of children, and of fathers?

What verses tell us about this?

“Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged.”

Colossians 3:21

Answer

Children: obey and honour your parents. Fathers: don't push your kids to anger with harshness or broken promises; train them and teach them in the Lord. Trust lost can be rebuilt, slowly.

Question 2 of 6

Work

How should a believer work when the boss isn't watching?

What verses tell us about this?

“And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men;”

Colossians 3:23

Answer

The same as when he is. A believer works from the heart, as for Christ: on time, honest with tools and hours, with good will. The Lord sees it and repays it.

Question 3 of 6

The enemy

Who is our real enemy?

What verses tell us about this?

“Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.”

James 4:7

Answer

Not people. The devil and the powers of darkness, with their schemes. We stand in the Lord's strength.

Question 4 of 6

The armour

What armour has God given us to stand in?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The belt of truth, the breastplate of righteousness, the shoes of the gospel of peace, the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, the word of God. It is God's own armour.

Question 5 of 6

Prayer

Why does Paul finish the armour with prayer?

What verses tell us about this?

“Pray without ceasing.”

1 Thessalonians 5:17

Answer

Because armour is no good to a soldier cut off from his Commander. Prayer keeps us leaning on God's strength, always, and it holds up every brother in the same fight.
