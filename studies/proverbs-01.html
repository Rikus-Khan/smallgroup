The book of Proverbs

Who: mostly Solomon, son of David, king of Israel (1:1). Later chapters add sayings of other wise men, of Agur (ch. 30), and of King Lemuel, taught by his mother (ch. 31).

When: Solomon reigned about 970 to 930 BC, Israel's richest and most peaceful years. He ruled from Jerusalem.

To whom: “my son” (v. 8). A father teaching his boy, and through him every young man, the simple, and the wise.

People came from all over the world to hear Solomon's wisdom, even the queen of Sheba, from far to the south (1 Kings 10).

Where Proverbs fits

  1. about 1010 BCDavid becomes king
  2. about 970Solomon king; Proverbs

The book grew over time. More than two hundred years after Solomon, King Hezekiah had his scribes copy out more: “These are also proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out” (25:1).

God kept these sayings alive through the bad years, when the kingdom split, so they would still be here for a man who needs them today.

Part one

Why this book

Verses 1–9

A king writes down what he wants his son to know.

What is a proverb?

A proverb is a short, sharp saying about how life usually works. Solomon, David's son, was the king God gave unusual wisdom to. The Bible says he spoke three thousand proverbs (1 Kings 4:32).

A proverb

A wise principle

What generally happens when a man lives God's way, or doesn't. True, but not a guarantee for every case.

A promise

God's word to keep

Something God says He will do, like saving everyone who calls on Him. Those never fail.

And here's the hard truth: Solomon knew more wisdom than any man alive, and later in life he didn't follow it. Knowing wisdom isn't the same as walking in it. We need more than good advice. We need the Lord Himself.

Old words, plain meanings

subtilty v. 4
Street smarts, good sense. Here it's a good thing: not getting played.
the simple v. 4
The naive man. Not stupid, just wide open and easy to lead.
discretion v. 4
Thinking it through before you act.
dark sayings v. 6
Riddles. Sayings you have to chew on before they open up.

Notice who this book is for: the simple, the young man, and the wise man (v. 5). Nobody outgrows it.

Where it all starts

“Beginning” means the first step and the foundation. You can't skip it and still build anything that lasts.

The fear of the LORD isn't cowering from a mean boss. It's the respect, awe, and trust a man has when he finally sees how big and how good God is, and lets God be God.

The fool doesn't lack information. He despises it. He looks down on correction because he already knows best.

Wisdom has a name

Proverbs keeps saying wisdom is worth more than anything. The New Testament tells us where to find it:

“But unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God.”

1 Corinthians 1:24

“But of him are ye in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption:”

1 Corinthians 1:30

Proverbs gives the sayings. Jesus is the wisdom of God in person. Fearing the LORD starts the day you stop running your own life and trust Jesus with it.

Worn like a medal

A wreath on the head and a gold chain around the neck were honors, the way a king rewarded a man (Pharaoh put a gold chain on Joseph, Genesis 41:42). Good teaching, taken to heart, looks good on a man.

Some of us didn't have a father or mother worth listening to. That hurts, and it's real. But God steps into that gap. Here in Proverbs He gives you a father's voice, and through Jesus He gives you Himself as your Father.

“A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows, is God in his holy habitation.”

Psalm 68:5

Part two

When the crowd calls

Verses 10–19

“Come with us.” Every man has heard it.

If sinners entice thee, consent thou not.

To entice is to lure, to sweet-talk, to make it sound easy. You can't stop people from asking. You can decide your answer before they ever do.

Three words: consent thou not. No speech, no debate, no “maybe later.” Just no. The longer you stand there talking, the weaker that no gets.

The pitch, and the price

What they sayWhat it really pays
“Come with us”→their feet run to evilv. 16
“We shall fill our houses with spoil”→it takes the life of the ownersv. 19
“Cast in thy lot among us”→they lie in wait for their own bloodv. 18

A lot was like a pair of dice used to decide things. “Cast in thy lot” means throw in with us, share our fate. “One purse” means one bank, one crew. It's the offer of belonging, money, and a family. A gang, a using crowd, a crew that “has your back” sells exactly that.

Don't even start down the road

The father doesn't say “go with them, but be careful.” He says don't take the first step. Refrain thy foot. It's easier to stay off a road than to get off it.

“Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.”

1 Corinthians 15:33

“Evil communications” means bad company. Who you run with will shape who you become. That goes for the old crowd, the old corner, and the old phone numbers.

Dumber than a bird

Even a bird won't walk into a trap it watched you set. That's the picture in verse 17.

But these men can see where their road ends, and they walk it anyway. They think they're setting a trap for somebody else. Verse 18 says the trap is for them.

Verse 19: greed “taketh away the life of the owners thereof.” What you grab ends up owning you.

What did it cost you?

“For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?”

Mark 8:36

Most of us don't need this explained. We've watched it happen: the money, the high, the crew, and then the funerals, the cells, the kids who don't call. The “precious substance” of verse 13 was never worth what it took.

Jesus asks the question so you'll count the cost before you pay it. And if you already paid it, He's the one who can give back what the years took.

Part three

Wisdom calls out

Verses 20–33

The gang calls from the shadows. Wisdom calls from the middle of the street.

She's not hiding

Proverbs paints wisdom as a woman calling out. That's a word-picture: it shows that God's wisdom wants to be found.

She stands at the chief place of concourse (the busiest crossroads) and at the city gates, where business and court cases happened. Out loud, for everybody. God puts His wisdom right where you live.

Three men, one road downhill

The simpleOpen to anything. Easy to talk into stuff.“loves simplicity”
The scornerMocks anything serious. Laughs it off.“delights in scorning”
The foolHas made up his mind. Hates being told.“hates knowledge”

Each one is harder to reach than the last. But look at verse 23: she still calls all three. “Turn you at my reproof.” Turn around, and she promises, “I will pour out my spirit unto you.” The offer comes before the warning.

Missed calls

  1. ✕“I have called”verse 24refused
  2. ✕“I have stretched out my hand”verse 24no man regarded
  3. ✕“all my counsel”verse 25set at nought
  4. ✕“my reproof”verse 25would none of it

Then the storm hits “as a whirlwind” (v. 27). Wisdom “laughing” is a strong word-picture: the man who mocked every warning now finds the warnings were right. It doesn't mean God enjoys anyone's ruin. He is “not willing that any should perish” (2 Peter 3:9).

While the door is open

“To day”
the storm
while she is calling (vv. 20–23)when calamity comes (vv. 26–28)

Verse 29 says they did not choose. It was a real choice, and it was theirs. When trouble came, they cried out to get out of the pain, but they still didn't want wisdom.

“While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation.”

Hebrews 3:15

If you hear Him calling, that's proof the door is open. Anyone who honestly calls on the Lord will be heard. Don't wait for the whirlwind.

Two endings

Verse 32

The one who turns away

“Prosperity” here means careless ease: getting comfortable while life is falling apart. That comfort is what destroys him.

Verse 33

The one who listens

Dwells safely, and is “quiet from fear of evil.” Not a life without trouble, but a man at peace in it.

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Points to remember

  1. The fear of the LORD“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge” 1:7Everything wise is built on respecting and trusting God. Christ is the wisdom of God.
  2. Temptation“Consent thou not” 1:10Decide your no before they ask. Don't take the first step.
  3. The wrong crowd“They lay wait for their own blood” 1:18The crowd promises money and belonging. It pays in blood and loss.
  4. God calls out“I will pour out my spirit unto you” 1:23Wisdom calls everyone, out in the open, and offers to change them from the inside.
  5. Today“They did not choose the fear of the LORD” 1:29Listening is a choice. Make it while He's calling.
  6. God's promise“Whoso hearkeneth unto me shall dwell safely” 1:33The man who listens gets safety and a quiet heart.

Question 1 of 6

The fear of the LORD

Where does real knowledge begin?

What verses tell us about this?

“But unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God.”

1 Corinthians 1:24

Answer

It begins with the fear of the LORD: respecting God, trusting Him, and letting Him be God instead of us. That's the foundation everything else stands on. And the wisdom of God has a name: Jesus Christ.

Question 2 of 6

Temptation

What should you do when sinners entice you?

What verses tell us about this?

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Answer

Say no, and don't stand around negotiating. Don't take the first step down their road. And remember you're not alone in it: God is faithful, and He always makes a way out.

Question 3 of 6

The wrong crowd

What does the crowd promise, and what does it really pay?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It promises money, excitement, and a family to belong to. It pays in blood, prison, and death, and the men who set the trap end up caught in it. Greed takes the life of the man who chases it.

Question 4 of 6

God calls out

Who is Wisdom calling, and what does she offer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Everybody: the simple, the scorner, even the fool. She calls out in public where anyone can hear. And she offers more than advice: “I will pour out my spirit unto you.” God wants to change a man from the inside.

Question 5 of 6

Today

Why shouldn't a man put off listening?

What verses tell us about this?

“Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near:”

Isaiah 55:6

Answer

Because every “no” makes the next one easier, and the storm comes on its own schedule, not ours. Listening is a choice, and it's ours to make. If you hear Him calling today, the door is open. Walk through it now.

Question 6 of 6

God's promise

What does the man who listens get?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

Honor that looks good on him, a safe place to live, and a heart that's quiet instead of always afraid. Jesus offers the same thing to every tired man who comes to Him: rest.
