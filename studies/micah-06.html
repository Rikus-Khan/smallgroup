Part one

A day in court

Verses 1–5

God brings a case against His own people, and He opens with a question.

The LORD's lawsuit

Micah, a country prophet from the town of Moresheth, preached to Judah in the south about 740 to 700 BC. This last part of his book opens like a trial. A “controversy” is a lawsuit, and to “plead” is to argue a case.

  • Bringing the casethe LORD
  • On trialHis people, Israel
  • The witnessesthe mountains, the hills, the foundations of the earth
  • Speaking for GodMicah

Why mountains? They were there when God made His covenant with Israel, and they're still standing. Moses had called the same witnesses:

“I call heaven and earth to record this day against you, that I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live:”

Deuteronomy 30:19

“What have I done?”

The LORDO my people, what have I done unto thee? and wherein have I wearied thee? testify against me.

His people…
no answer

God is the one bringing the case, yet He hands the floor to the accused: “Testify against me.” Tell Me where I failed you. Where I was unfair. Where I wore you out. Nobody says a word, because there's nothing to say.

A lot of us have blamed God for the wreckage our own choices made. Look honestly, and you won't find where He did you wrong.

Remember

Instead of a list of charges, God gives a list of what He has done for them:

  1. Egypt“redeemed thee out of the house of servants”
  2. Moses, Aaron, Miriamleaders, a priest, and a prophetess
  3. Balak and Balaama king hires a prophet to curse themNumbers 22–24
  4. Shittim to Gilgalacross the Jordan and homeJoshua 3–4

Balak, king of Moab, paid Balaam to curse Israel. Every time Balaam opened his mouth, a blessing came out. God wouldn't let anyone curse His people:

“Nevertheless the LORD thy God would not hearken unto Balaam; but the LORD thy God turned the curse into a blessing unto thee, because the LORD thy God loved thee.”

Deuteronomy 23:5

Shittim was their last camp before they crossed the Jordan. Gilgal was their first camp in the promised land. Most of a man's drifting starts when he forgets what God has already done for him.

Part two

What is good

Verses 6–8

A guilty man tries to name his price. God names something else.

Naming a price

Listen to the man bargaining. Every offer is bigger than the last, as if God were a price to be met:

  • burnt offerings, calves of a year oldthe best of the herd
  • thousands of ramsmore than a king could give
  • ten thousands of rivers of oilmore than the world holds
  • my firstbornhis own child

That last one wasn't just talk. In Micah's own day, Judah's king did it:

“But he walked in the way of the kings of Israel, yea, and made his son to pass through the fire, according to the abominations of the heathen, whom the LORD cast out from before the children of Israel.”

2 Kings 16:3, of King Ahaz

God never asked for any of it. You can't buy Him, and you can't pay Him off.

Three things

  • Do justlyBe fair and straight with people. Honest work, honest words, honest weights.
  • Love mercyDon't just show kindness once. Love it. Be loyal to people the way God is loyal to you.
  • Walk humbly with thy GodEvery day, step by step, with Him. Not running ahead, not lagging behind.
חֶסֶד said chesed loyal love; kindness that keeps its promises

“Mercy” here is the word Hosea used for God's own faithful love. Jesus called these things “the weightier matters of the law” (Matthew 23:23), heavier than any offering.

Saved first, then the walk

Verse 8 isn't a way to earn God. Look at the order. He redeemed them first, out of slavery, and only then showed them how to walk.

First, what God did“I … redeemed thee” (6:4)
→
then, how we live“walk humbly with thy God” (6:8)

It's the same with us. Salvation is a gift. The walk is what grows out of it:

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.”

Ephesians 2:8–10

And notice it's a walk. Not a leap, not a one-time decision. One day, one step at a time, with Him.

Part three

Wicked balances

Verses 9–16

God shows what the city does instead: cheating, violence, and lies.

A thumb on the scale

Grain was sold by weight. A crook kept two sets of stones in his bag, and a “scant measure,” a basket a little too small. The buyer always came up short.

The rich got rich this way, then used violence and lies to keep it. God saw every crooked deal. “A false balance is abomination to the LORD” (Proverbs 11:1).

“Hear ye the rod”: the hard times coming had a Sender, and they were meant to be listened to. God disciplines the people He loves (Hebrews 12:6).

Never enough

The workeatsowoliveswine
butbutbutbut
The endnot filledno cropno oilno drink

They got rich by cheating, and God made sure it never filled them. Any man who has chased a high knows this curse from the inside: always more, never enough. Here even the wine is part of the emptiness, not a reward. There is Bread that fills:

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

Copying the worst

Omri and his son Ahab were kings of Israel, the north, about 150 years before Micah. Omri “did worse than all that were before him,” and Ahab outdid him (1 Kings 16:25, 30).

OmribuiltSamariahis sonAhabbrought inBaal worship

Judahwalked in their counsels“an hissing”

An “hissing” is the low whistle people give when they walk past a wreck. Judah watched the north fall, then picked up its habits anyway. We tend to become like the men we walk with. Choose them carefully.

Points to remember

  1. God's faithfulness“O my people, what have I done unto thee?” 6:3God has never done us wrong. The case against Him has no witnesses.
  2. Remembering“O my people, remember now” 6:5Remember what He has done, and you'll know what He is like.
  3. Grace“Shall I give my firstborn for my transgression?” 6:7You can't buy God's favor, and He never asked you to.
  4. Walking with God“Walk humbly with thy God” 6:8Do justly, love mercy, and walk with Him every day.
  5. Honesty“The bag of deceitful weights” 6:11God sees every deal. Honest work is worship.
  6. Sin's emptiness“Thou shalt eat, but not be satisfied” 6:14Sin promises to fill you and never does. Only Jesus satisfies.

Question 1 of 6

God's faithfulness

Has God ever done us wrong?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thus saith the LORD, What iniquity have your fathers found in me, that they are gone far from me, and have walked after vanity, and are become vain?”

Jeremiah 2:5

Answer

Never. God opened the floor, “testify against me,” and nobody could name a single wrong. He set them free, led them, and blessed them when others tried to curse them. When life falls apart, it's tempting to blame God. But an honest look always finds His hands clean and full of kindness.

Question 2 of 6

Remembering

Why does God tell His people to remember?

What verses tell us about this?

“Nevertheless the LORD thy God would not hearken unto Balaam; but the LORD thy God turned the curse into a blessing unto thee, because the LORD thy God loved thee.”

Deuteronomy 23:5

Answer

“That ye may know the righteousness of the LORD.” Remembering what He has done shows us who He is: faithful, loving, a God who turns curses into blessings. When we forget, we drift and start blaming Him. Keep a record of what God has done for you, and go back to it on the hard days.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

Can we buy God's favor?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.”

1 Samuel 15:22

“For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings.”

Hosea 6:6

Answer

No. The man in verse 7 kept raising his offer, all the way up to his own child, and God wanted none of it. God isn't a price to be met. Jesus paid what we owed, and salvation is a gift we receive by faith. What God wants from us is not a payment but a heart that loves Him.

Question 4 of 6

Walking with God

What does the LORD require of us?

What verses tell us about this?

“Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.”

Matthew 23:23

“For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.”

Ephesians 2:10

Answer

To do justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with Him. It isn't a ladder to climb up to God. He redeemed us first. It's the life that grows out of being saved: fair with people, loyal and kind, and walking with God every day, one step at a time.

Question 5 of 6

Honesty

Does God care how we do business?

What verses tell us about this?

“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.”

Proverbs 11:1

Answer

Yes. God brought His case against the city because of crooked scales, short measures, and lies. Every hustle, every padded bill, every shortcut that cheats somebody, He sees. And an honest day's work, a fair deal and a straight answer are things He delights in.

Question 6 of 6

Sin's emptiness

Why does sin never satisfy?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore do ye spend money for that which is not bread? and your labour for that which satisfieth not? hearken diligently unto me, and eat ye that which is good, and let your soul delight itself in fatness.”

Isaiah 55:2

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

Answer

Because we were made for God, and nothing else fits the hole. Sin promises a full plate and leaves you hungrier. Judah ate and wasn't satisfied, and sowed and never reaped. Jesus is the bread that actually fills: come to Him, and you won't have to keep chasing.
