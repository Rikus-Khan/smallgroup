1 Corinthians 2

Weakness, and the Spirit

Paul tells the Corinthians how he came to them: scared, plain, and preaching only Jesus Christ crucified. Then he shows who teaches us the things of God.

Where we are in the letter

Corinth was splitting into teams. Chapters 1–4 are Paul's answer. Chapter 1 ended with “let him glory in the Lord.” Chapter 2 shows how Paul lived that: he came with nothing to boast in.

  1. Chapter 1The cross is God's wisdom, and God picks the ones the world wrote off.
  2. Chapter 2How Paul came, and how the Spirit teaches us God's things.
  3. Chapter 3Babies, and builders on one foundation.
  4. Chapter 4Servants, and who maketh thee to differ.

Fighting over who was wiser was the very thing the Corinthians were doing. Paul says: the wisdom that matters is God's, and it comes as a gift.

Part one

Jesus Christ, and him crucified

Verses 1–5

Paul walked into Corinth with no polish and no plan but one.

What Paul decided to leave out

  • excellency of speechno fancy delivery to impress the crowd
  • man's wisdomno clever philosophy to win the argument
  • anything else“I determined not to know any thing among you”
  • Jesus Christ, and him crucifiedthe one thing he came to say

Paul had just come from Athens, where the thinkers listened politely until he got to the resurrection, and then some of them mocked (Acts 17:32). He could have changed his message for Corinth. He did the opposite. He cut it down to the cross, because the cross is what changes a man.

“In weakness, and in fear, and in much trembling”

The great apostle walked into Corinth scared. He had just been mocked in Athens, and he was working with his hands to pay his way (Acts 18:3). His critics later said his “bodily presence is weak, and his speech contemptible” (2 Corinthians 10:10).

And here is what the Lord told him in the night:

“Then spake the Lord to Paul in the night by a vision, Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace: For I am with thee, and no man shall set on thee to hurt thee: for I have much people in this city.”

Acts 18:9–10

Weak man, strong God

What Paul brought

Weakness, fear, trembling

Plain words and no polish. Nothing for the Corinthians to be impressed by, and nothing for Paul to boast in.

What God brought

The Spirit and power

Men were changed. That left no doubt where the power came from (verse 5).

So that your faith “should not stand in the wisdom of men, but in the power of God.” A faith built on a clever speaker falls when the speaker does. A faith built on God holds.

Today: if you're dreading something, like a hard conversation, a meeting, or telling your story, you're in good company. Paul went in shaking, and God showed up. You don't need to be strong. You need to show up.

Part two

The hidden wisdom of God

Verses 6–9

A plan older than the world, and kept secret from its rulers.

A mystery, and who missed it

μυστήριον said mustērion a secret, now told

In the Bible a mystery isn't something spooky. It's a secret God kept for ages and then made known. Here, the secret is the cross: God's plan to save, planned “before the world.”

The princes of this world were the rulers and powerful men of the day, Pilate and Herod and the council among them. They were the smartest, most powerful men around, and they crucified “the Lord of glory.” Had they known, they never would have. Wisdom that can't recognize God standing in front of it isn't worth much. (“Them that are perfect” in verse 6 means the mature, not a special class of super-believers.)

Eye hath not seen

Paul is echoing the prophet Isaiah (Isaiah 64:4). No eye has seen it, no ear has heard it, and nobody has even imagined it: what God has prepared for “them that love him.”

Many read this as heaven, and it surely includes heaven. But look at the very next verse: “God hath revealed them unto us.” God doesn't keep it all for later. The Spirit is already showing us some of it.

Whatever your life looked like last year, you can't see how far God can take it. He can.

Part three

The Spirit teaches us

Verses 10–16

You can't figure out God from outside. His Spirit shows us from inside.

Who knows what's in a man?

Verse 11, the first half

A man's own thoughts

Nobody can see what you're really thinking. Only the spirit inside you knows. You can say it, or hide it.

Verse 11, the second half

God's thoughts

Only the Spirit of God knows them. So the only way to know God's mind is for His Spirit to tell us.

Verse 12 is the good news: “we have received” that Spirit. Every believer has. The Holy Spirit moves in when you trust Christ, to stay (see also Romans 8:9). You don't need a second experience or a special class. You have the Teacher already, so ask Him.

Today: before you open the Bible, even for five minutes, ask the Spirit to show you what the words mean. Then read one chapter slowly.

The natural man, and the spiritual man

The natural man (verse 14)

Doesn't have God's Spirit

Not necessarily a bad man. He may be smart, decent, hard-working. But the things of God sound like foolishness to him, because they're “spiritually discerned.” He has no way to receive them yet.

The spiritual man (verse 15)

Has God's Spirit

Every believer. He can look at things the way God does and tell what is right. “Judged of no man” doesn't mean nobody can correct him. It means the world can't size up what's going on inside him.

Chapter 3 will show that a believer can still act “carnal,” like a baby. Having the Spirit is not the same as growing up. But the Spirit is the difference between a man God hasn't reached yet and a man He has.

But we have the mind of Christ.

Not perfect thinking, but a new way of looking: the Spirit helping you see sin as God sees it, and see yourself as God sees you. Many of these men thought they were hopeless. The mind of Christ says, “washed, set apart, and wanted.”

Regret tells you your story is over. The Spirit says it isn't. Paul, who once hunted down Christians, wrote this chapter. He knew all about starting over.

Points to remember

  1. What Paul preached“Jesus Christ, and him crucified” 2:2Take away everything else and this is what is left, and it is enough.
  2. Weakness“In weakness, and in fear, and in much trembling” 2:3God uses scared, plain men, so that faith rests on His power and not ours.
  3. Hidden wisdom“They would not have crucified the Lord of glory” 2:8God's plan was a secret from the world's rulers, and now it is told to everyone.
  4. Beyond imagining“Eye hath not seen, nor ear heard” 2:9God has prepared more for those who love Him than we can picture, and He's already begun to show us.
  5. The Spirit“God hath revealed them unto us by his Spirit” 2:10Every believer has received the Spirit, and He is the Teacher who shows us the things of God.
  6. Two kinds of men“But we have the mind of Christ” 2:16The natural man can't take in God's things. The man with the Spirit can, and can grow.

Question 1 of 6

What Paul preached

What did Paul decide to preach when he came to Corinth?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures;”

1 Corinthians 15:3

Answer

Only Jesus Christ, and him crucified. No fancy speech and no clever ideas. The cross is the message that changes a man, so Paul left everything else out.

Question 2 of 6

Weakness

How did Paul come to the Corinthians, and why does it matter?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

Answer

In weakness, in fear and in much trembling. It matters because the Corinthians' faith then rested on God's power, not on Paul's skill. God's strength shows best in weak men, and that is good news for anyone who feels weak today.

Question 3 of 6

Hidden wisdom

What is the hidden wisdom of God, and why did the rulers of this world miss it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It is God's plan to save us through the cross, planned before the world began and kept hidden. The rulers were proud of their own wisdom, so they didn't recognize the Lord of glory when He stood in front of them. That plan is now told to anyone who will hear it.

Question 4 of 6

Beyond imagining

What has God prepared for those who love him?

What verses tell us about this?

“For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known.”

1 Corinthians 13:12

Answer

More than any eye has seen or any heart has pictured. We see only part of it now, and the Spirit has already begun to show us. The rest is waiting. Whatever has happened in your past, God has good things in front of you.

Question 5 of 6

The Spirit

How can a man know the things of God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Only through God's Spirit. Nobody figures out God from outside, just as nobody can read another man's thoughts. But every believer has received the Spirit, so every believer can be taught. Ask Him, and read the Book.

Question 6 of 6

Two kinds of men

What is the difference between the natural man and the spiritual man?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The natural man doesn't have God's Spirit, so the things of God look like foolishness to him and he can't take them in. The spiritual man has the Spirit, and so he can see as God sees. We have the mind of Christ.
