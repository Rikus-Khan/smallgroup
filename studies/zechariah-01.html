Zechariah 1

Turn ye unto me

God calls His people to turn back to Him. Then, in the night, a prophet sees horsemen among the myrtle trees, and hears God say He has come back to Jerusalem with mercy.

The book of Zechariah

Who: Zechariah, grandson of Iddo, from a priestly family that came home from Babylon. Likely a young man.

When: about 520–518 BC, after the exile, under Darius king of Persia. He began two months after Haggai.

To whom: the small remnant back in Jerusalem, rebuilding the temple. Judah was now just a little Persian province, Yehud.

Home lay about 900 miles from Babylon, up the river and around the desert. Ezra's trip, years later, took four months (Ezra 7:9).

Two prophets, side by side

Haggai, the older man, telling them to get building; Zechariah, the younger, showing them why it mattered. His very name tells a discouraged people that God hasn't forgotten them.

Part one

Turn ye unto me

Verses 1–6

Zechariah's first message is short. Don't be like your fathers. Come back.

One word, two directions

You→turn→to MeCome back from the road you've been on.
I→will turn→to youAnd God comes toward the man who comes toward Him.

Verse 2 is honest: God was “sore displeased” with their fathers. But verse 3 is an open door. Notice who God says it to: the same people He was angry with.

“Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.”

James 4:8

Jesus told it as a story. The son only had to start home. The father ran the rest of the way.

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

Where are they now?

The fathers

Gone

The men who ignored the “former prophets” (Isaiah, Jeremiah and the rest) died in the ruin of Jerusalem or in exile.

The prophets

Gone too

Even the faithful men who warned them were dead by now. Nobody lives for ever.

God's words

Still standing

Every word “took hold” of the fathers, just as God said. In the end even they admitted it (v. 6).

Many of these men came from families wrecked by the choices of the generation before. God says: you don't have to repeat it. Learn from it, and turn.

“The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever.”

Isaiah 40:8

Part two

Among the myrtle trees

Verses 7–17

Three months later, in one night, Zechariah sees eight visions. Here is the first.

Eight visions in one night

The month Sebat fell in our February, in 519 BC. Over one night God showed Zechariah eight pictures. Chapters 1 and 2 hold the first three.

  1. 1. The horsemenamong the myrtles (1:7–17)
  2. 2. Four hornsand four carpenters (1:18–21)
  3. 3. The measuring line(2:1–13)
  4. 4. Filthy garmentstaken away (3)
  5. 5. The candlestickand two olive trees (4)
  6. 6. The flying roll(5:1–4)
  7. 7. The woman in the ephah(5:5–11)
  8. 8. Four chariots(6:1–8)

A vision is a picture with a message. Zechariah doesn't pretend to understand it. He asks, “What are these?” and an angel explains. That's a good way to read hard parts of the Bible: ask, and let the Bible explain itself.

Who's who in the vision

  1. The man on the red horsestanding among the myrtles. Verse 11 calls him “the angel of the LORD.”
  2. The horses behind himred, speckled, and white: riders sent out to patrol the whole earth, like a king's scouts
  3. “The angel that talked with me”Zechariah's guide, who explains each vision
  4. The myrtle treeslow, sweet-smelling evergreens, growing down in a shaded hollow

The riders report: “all the earth sitteth still, and is at rest.” Darius had just crushed the revolts in his empire. The nations were quiet and comfortable.

That sounds like good news. It isn't, quite. The world was at ease, and Jerusalem was still in ruins. Nobody out there cared.

Seventy years

Somebody did care. The angel of the LORD himself pleads with God for Jerusalem. He counts the years: “threescore and ten,” seventy. Jeremiah had said it would be seventy.

“For thus saith the LORD, That after seventy years be accomplished at Babylon I will visit you, and perform my good word toward you, in causing you to return to this place.”

Jeremiah 29:10
586 BCthe temple burned
about 516the temple finished
70 yearsalmost to the year

And God answers with “good words and comfortable words.” Comfortable means comforting. The time of anger is nearly over.

Someone pleading for us

In the Old Testament, “the angel of the LORD” often speaks as God Himself. Many Bible teachers believe he is the Son of God, appearing before He was born as a man. Either way, here is someone in heaven standing up for God's hurting people, and God answering kindly.

A believer in Jesus has that every day. When you can't find the words, and when you've fallen again, Jesus is praying for you.

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

“Comfort ye, comfort ye my people, saith your God.”

Isaiah 40:1

A great jealousy

God's jealousy isn't petty. It's the fierce love of a husband for a wife, or a father for his son. He won't share them, and He won't let anyone abuse them.

God was“but a little displeased” with His people
→
the nations“helped forward the affliction”

God used Babylon to correct Judah. But Babylon went far past correction, into cruelty, and enjoyed it. Now they sit “at ease,” and God is very sore displeased with them.

God's discipline of His own has a limit. It is measured, and it ends. Those who pile on will answer to Him.

Returned with mercies

Three times in verse 17 God says “yet”: My cities shall yet spread, the LORD shall yet comfort Zion, and shall yet choose Jerusalem. The fall was real. So is the rise.

These promises belong to Jerusalem, and God will keep them to the letter. They also show us who He is: a God who comes back to the people He disciplined, and comes back with mercy.

Part three

Four horns, four carpenters

Verses 18–21

The second vision: the powers that scattered God's people, and the men God sends to deal with them.

Horns and carpenters

Four horns

The powers that scattered

A horn was a bull's or a ram's weapon, so it stood for strength and power. These are the nations that pushed God's people out of their land, from every side. “No man did lift up his head.”

Four carpenters

The men God sends

Craftsmen: workers in wood, metal, or stone, with the tools to cut a horn right off. For every power that rose against them, God had a power ready to bring it down.

“Fray” is an old word for frighten, terrify. The bullies are about to be the ones afraid.

What scatters a man can look stronger than anything: a habit, a past, a crowd that pulls him under. But no horn is too big for God.

“What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us?”

Romans 8:31

Points to remember

  1. Turning back“Turn ye unto me … and I will turn unto you” 1:3God comes toward the man who comes back toward Him.
  2. The past“Be ye not as your fathers” 1:4We don't have to repeat what wrecked the men before us. Learn from it, and turn.
  3. God's word“My words … did they not take hold?” 1:6Men and prophets pass away. God's word stands, and it always comes true.
  4. Someone pleading“How long wilt thou not have mercy?” 1:12The angel of the LORD pleaded for Jerusalem. Jesus lives to pray for His people.
  5. God's heart“I am returned to Jerusalem with mercies” 1:16God's correction ends. He comes back to His people with comfort and mercy.
  6. The enemy“These are come to fray them” 1:21No power that scatters God's people is too strong for Him.

Question 1 of 6

Turning back

What does God promise the man who turns back to Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.”

James 4:8

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

Answer

That He will turn to him. God said it to people He had been angry with: turn to Me, and I will turn to you. A man doesn't have to clean himself up first. He only has to come home, and the Father runs to meet him.

Question 2 of 6

The past

What can we learn from the men who went before us?

What verses tell us about this?

“Even from the days of your fathers ye are gone away from mine ordinances, and have not kept them. Return unto me, and I will return unto you, saith the LORD of hosts. But ye said, Wherein shall we return?”

Malachi 3:7

Answer

Where their road led. The fathers wouldn't listen, and it cost them everything. In the end even they admitted God had been right. We can learn from their mistakes instead of repeating them. The pattern can stop with us.

Question 3 of 6

God's word

What lasts when everything else passes away?

What verses tell us about this?

“The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever.”

Isaiah 40:8

Answer

God's word. The fathers died, and so did the prophets, but every word God spoke came true. Feelings change, people let us down, and life falls apart, but what God has said will stand. That's solid ground to build on.

Question 4 of 6

Someone pleading

Who speaks up for God's people when they're hurting?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Answer

In the vision, the angel of the LORD pleaded for Jerusalem, and God answered with comforting words. A believer has Jesus Himself, who lives to pray for those who come to God through Him. You are never without someone speaking up for you.

Question 5 of 6

God's heart

How does God feel about His people after He has corrected them?

What verses tell us about this?

“Comfort ye, comfort ye my people, saith your God.”

Isaiah 40:1

Answer

He loves them fiercely. His anger was real, but it was measured, and it ended. Now He is jealous for them, angry at those who hurt them, and coming back to them with mercy and comfort. God doesn't hold a grudge against the people He has brought home.

Question 6 of 6

The enemy

What happens to the powers that scatter God's people?

What verses tell us about this?

“What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us?”

Romans 8:31

Answer

God brings them down. For every horn that scattered His people, He had a carpenter ready to cut it off. Whatever has scattered a man's life, it is not stronger than the God who is for him.
