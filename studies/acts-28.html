Part one

The island

Verses 1–10

A fire on the beach, a snake in the sticks, and a sick man healed.

Melita

Two hundred and seventy-six soaked men crawl up a beach, and only then do they learn where they are: Melita, today's Malta, about 60 miles south of Sicily.

“Barbarous” doesn't mean savage. It's the Greek barbaros, a person who didn't speak Greek, whose speech sounded like “bar-bar.” These islanders spoke their own language.

And they showed “no little kindness.” They lit a fire for “every one” of the strangers, because of the rain and the cold.

The viper

Wet and cold, 276 men need a fire, and Paul pitches in with a bundle of sticks.

A viper, driven out by the heat, fastens on his hand.

He shakes it into the fire and “felt no harm.” The islanders wait for him to swell up or drop dead. He doesn't.

It confirmed God's messenger to people who'd never heard the gospel. It isn't a pattern for the rest of us. Nothing in Scripture tells a believer to prove his faith with snakes.

“A murderer,” then “a god”

  • “No doubt this man is a murderer”trouble must mean guilt, they figured
  • “he was a god”an hour later, the same crowd flipped
  • a man God was keeping alive for Romejust as the angel had said

Both guesses were wrong. People read a man by the first thing they see. Paul knew something about that: he had once stood by while Stephen was killed (Acts 7:58, 8:1). If anyone had a past that sounded like “murderer,” it was him.

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

When people size you up by your past, you don't have to argue. God has dealt with it. Shake it off and do the next right thing.

Making the most of a shipwreck

Publius, the chief manlodged us three daysPaulprayed, laid hands on his fatherhealed
the rest of the islandcame with their sickhealedand sent us off withall we needed

Publius was “the chief man of the island,” and he took in strangers for three days. His father lay sick with “a fever and… a bloody flux” (likely dysentery). Luke, the doctor, notes the symptoms.

Paul prayed first, then laid on his hands. Healings like this marked the apostles' message as it reached new places. But look at the man: a prisoner and a shipwreck survivor, and he was still serving the people in front of him.

Part two

On to Rome

Verses 11–16

Three months of winter, a ship with twin gods on its bow, and brothers on the road.

Winter over, sails up

After three months they leave on another Egyptian grain ship that had wintered on the island. Its figurehead was Castor and Pollux, the twin gods sailors prayed to for safe passage. Paul sailed under them, and the only safe passage was the one God had promised.

Syracuse, three days. Round to Rhegium, then a south wind pushes them to Puteoli in two. Brothers there asked them to stay seven days.

Then the last stretch, on foot along the Appian Way, toward Rome.

They walked out to meet him

  1. Puteoliseven days with the brothersv. 14
  2. Appii Forum43 miles from Rome: the first groupv. 15
  3. Three Taverns33 miles from Rome: more brothersv. 15
  4. Romehanded to the captain of the guardv. 16

Word had gone ahead. Believers from Rome walked a long way out to meet a prisoner. They'd had his letter for about three years (Romans), and now they were seeing the man. And “he thanked God, and took courage.”

Even the strongest man in the book needed brothers to show up: shipwrecked, three months from a hot meal, still in chains, headed for a trial. Be that guy today. Who could use one text, one visit, or one ride to a meeting from you?

Part three

Paul in Rome

Verses 17–31

A chain on his wrist, a house full of visitors, and a story with no last line.

Chained to a guard

Roman house arrest meant a soldier chained to your wrist, in shifts. Paul called it “this chain” (v. 20).

Think of it from Paul's side: a captive audience, and a fresh one every few hours. He'd later write what it did:

“But I would ye should understand, brethren, that the things which happened unto me have fallen out rather unto the furtherance of the gospel; So that my bonds in Christ are manifest in all the palace, and in all other places;”

Philippians 1:12–13

Same room, same message

Some

Believed

They heard it from morning till evening: the kingdom of God, and Jesus, out of Moses and the prophets. They “believed the things which were spoken.”

Some

Believed not

Same room. Same words. They “agreed not among themselves” and walked out.

Paul didn't pressure them or trick them. He laid it out, all day long, and left the choice with them. God's offer goes to everyone, and each man answers it for himself.

If someone you love isn't responding, that isn't your failure. Keep the door open, like Paul.

A door that stays open

a closed eyean open doorIsaiah's words: “their eyes have they closed.” They shut them themselves.
Israel, firstthe Gentiles, too“Be it known therefore unto you.” God's salvation goes to anyone who will hear it.

Paul isn't giving up on his own people. Every city he entered he spoke first in the synagogue, and later in Romans he'll say how his heart aches for them. But when the door is shut from the inside, it doesn't shut the gospel in. It goes on to whoever will listen. That includes most of us.

With all confidence

παρρησίας the word is parrhēsias “boldness”

Speaking freely, nothing held back, nothing to hide. This is a man chained to a soldier, in a rented house, under a charge that could end in his death, and “no man forbidding him.”

“Wherein I suffer trouble, as an evil doer, even unto bonds; but the word of God is not bound.”

2 Timothy 2:9

Paul's body was chained. The message wasn't. Whatever is chaining you today, it doesn't get to decide what God does through you.

What those two years held

  1. about AD 60arrives in Rome
  2. 60 to 62writes from this house: Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, Philemon
  3. about 62Luke finishes Acts
  4. 62 to 64released; 1 Timothy and Titus
  5. about 64Rome burns; Nero turns on Christians
  6. 67 to 68Paul put to death in Rome, by tradition

Luke stops with Paul still in the house, because that's where he was when Acts was finished. The rest we know from Paul's letters and church history. Those two years in chains gave us four of the New Testament's letters.

Looking back

From Jerusalem to Rome

The whole book of Acts

An upper room in Jerusalem, and a rented house in the capital of the world.

Seven steps in about thirty years: from a locked upper room to the capital of the world. And in every one, God used people who weren't ready.

A book of second chances

Saulagreed to Stephen's death, thenturned around on the Damascus roadActs 9
a jailorready to take his own life, thenbelieved, with his whole houseActs 16

Not one of them was ready, and not one was finished by his worst day. That's the book. If you fell yesterday, it was written for you.

There is no “The End”

Luke just stops. The story doesn't.

“For so hath the Lord commanded us, saying, I have set thee to be a light of the Gentiles, that thou shouldest be for salvation unto the ends of the earth.”

Acts 13:47

“The uttermost part of the earth” kept moving. It reached the man who handed you this study. Today it might reach someone at your job.

Your part today: one true sentence about what Jesus has done for you, said to one person. You don't need a pulpit. Paul only had a rented house.

Question 1 of 6

Judging by looks

What did the islanders think when the viper bit Paul, and were they right?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They decided he must be a murderer whom justice wouldn't let live. When he felt no harm, they decided he was a god. They were wrong both times. People judge by the first thing they see. God knows the whole man.

Question 2 of 6

Kindness

How did the people of Melita treat the shipwrecked men?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

With “no little kindness.” They lit a fire for all of them in the cold rain, Publius took them in for three days, and when they left the islanders loaded them with what they needed. Paul prayed for Publius' father, and he was healed.

Question 3 of 6

Brothers

What did Paul do when he saw the brothers who came to meet him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He “thanked God, and took courage.” After a storm, a shipwreck and three months on the road, the sight of believers who'd walked out to meet him lifted him. Even a strong man needs brothers to show up.

Question 4 of 6

The message

What did Paul teach the Jews in Rome?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The kingdom of God, and Jesus, from the law of Moses and the prophets, from morning until evening. His message is the same as Acts 1: Jesus is the hope of Israel. Some believed it and some didn't.

Question 5 of 6

Unbelief

What did Paul say about those who would not believe?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Quoting Isaiah, he said their ears were dull and “their eyes have they closed.” They'd shut them themselves. But the salvation of God was sent on to the Gentiles, “and they will hear it.” The door stays open to anyone who will listen.

Question 6 of 6

The open door

What could Paul still do while he was in chains?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Everything that mattered. He received all who came, preached the kingdom of God, and taught about the Lord Jesus Christ, “with all confidence, no man forbidding him.” His body was chained, but the word of God was not.
