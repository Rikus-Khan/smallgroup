2 Corinthians 12

Grace enough

Paul was taken up to heaven, then given a thorn he could not get rid of. He asked God three times. The answer was not what he wanted, and it was enough.

Back in Corinth, some men who called themselves apostles had been running Paul down: he is weak, he is not impressive, he is not a real apostle. In chapters 10 and 11 Paul answers them, and in chapter 11 he lists his beatings and shipwrecks.

Now, in chapter 12, he does something odd. He tells them about the greatest thing that ever happened to him, and then about the worst thing he carries. And he says the second one taught him more than the first.

Part one

Caught up to heaven

Verses 1–6

The best day of his life, and why he almost never mentions it.

The third heaven

Bible teachers of Paul's day often spoke of heaven in layers: the sky above us, the stars beyond it, and the place where God Himself is. The third heaven is the last: God's own presence. Verse 4 calls it paradise.

Paul says it happened above fourteen years ago, so about AD 42, years before he ever got to Corinth. He doesn't know if his body went too (God knoweth), and he heard things he was not allowed to repeat. He doesn't tell us what he saw.

“And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.”

Luke 23:43

Why he kept quiet for fourteen years

What the other men did

“Look what I've seen”

Visions, credentials, big stories. They wanted the Corinthians to be impressed, and some were.

What Paul did

“Look at my weakness”

He wouldn't let a once-in-a-lifetime experience be his reputation. He wanted people to judge him by his life, what he seeth me to be.

Of such an one will I glory: Paul talks about himself like a stranger, because the day was God's doing, not his. Infirmities are weaknesses, the things that are plainly wrong with us. That is what he will brag about.

Notice what he does not do. He doesn't say never mind my weak side; look at my heaven. He lets people see both.

Part two

A thorn he couldn't pull out

Verses 7–10

The most useful verses in this letter for a man who is still fighting something.

A thorn in the flesh

A thorn is a sharp stake or splinter, something that stays lodged and hurts every time you move. Paul never says what it was. Bible teachers have guessed for two thousand years: an illness, trouble with his eyes, a man who dogged him from town to town. Nobody knows.

That silence may be on purpose. Because Paul doesn't name it, every man who has a thorn can read himself into it: a body that hurts, a craving that won't quit, a memory, a family that won't speak to you.

What the thorn was for

“Lest I should be exalted”

Paul had seen heaven. A man with a story like that can start to think he is somebody. The thorn kept him leaning on God.

Where it came from

“The messenger of Satan”

The thorn came from the enemy's side: it was meant to hurt. But it could only go as far as God allowed, and God turned it to good, as with Job and with Joseph.

Three times he asked

Paul, the first timeLord, take this away from me.

Paul, the second timeLord, please. Take it away.

Paul, the third timeLord, I'm asking again.

The LordMy grace is sufficient for thee.
enough

Thrice means three times. Paul prayed, and prayed again, and again. It is not a failure of faith to keep asking God to take something away. Jesus Himself asked three times in the garden.

God said no to the thing Paul asked, and yes to what Paul needed. That is not the same as being ignored.

Weak, then strong

Most of us run on our own strength as long as we can. Then something breaks, and we find out how little there was.

Made perfect means brought to completion. God's power shows up fully where ours has run out. Paul stopped hiding the weak spot. He was most gladly willing to boast about it, because that was where Christ's power came to rest upon him, like a tent pitched over him.

Enough for today

Sufficient means enough. Not a pile in the bank to spend over the years. Enough for the day in front of you, and then again tomorrow.

“It is of the LORD'S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

If you fell yesterday, you are not starting from nothing. The grace that was enough for Paul's worst day is the same grace you get this morning. Don't wait until you have it together: boldly means come as you are, in your time of need.

Today: name your thorn to God in one plain sentence. Ask Him for what you need to get through today, not for the whole thing to be gone. Then ask one believer to pray with you.

“I take pleasure”

Verse 10 is not Paul enjoying pain. He is listing what he went through for Christ's sake: reproaches (insults), necessities (going without), persecutions, distresses (being squeezed into a corner).

His point is that none of it was wasted. Every weak place became a place where the power of Christ showed.

That is hope for a man with a record. Your worst chapters don't disqualify you. A man who knows how weak he is makes the best witness there is.

Part three

Not yours, but you

Verses 11–19

A man who has been accused of being in it for the money tells them how he really feels.

Sad, and a little sarcastic

Paul hates this boasting. I am become a fool in glorying: ye have compelled me. The Corinthians should have stood up for him. Instead he has to defend himself.

The very chiefest apostles are the ones the Corinthians admired. Paul is no less than they are, and he adds though I be nothing: whatever he is, it is from God.

Forgive me this wrong is a dry little joke. The only way Corinth came off worse than the other churches is that Paul never sent them a bill. He didn't ask them for a penny.

There's a lesson in it for any man who has been hurt by people he helped: you can say plainly that it hurt, without trying to hurt back.

Three visits

  1. about AD 50 to 52First visitthe church begins (Acts 18)
  2. about 55 to 56Second visita painful one (2:1)
  3. about 56 to 57The third time“I am ready to come” (12:14)

Paul is not coming to take something from them. I seek not yours, but you.

Parents lay up for their children, he says, not the other way round. He will spend and be spent: spend his money, and be spent himself, used up, for them.

Then the sad line: the more abundantly I love you, the less I be loved. Some of us have loved people who did not love us back. Paul kept loving. That is how God loved us before we ever turned around.

A man with a past of lying or taking earns trust slowly, and he earns it by being checkable. Paul invites the questions. Today: let one person see something you might be tempted to hide, a receipt, a phone, a schedule. It costs a little pride and buys a lot of trust.

Part four

What Paul is afraid of

Verses 20–21

Not criticism. Not trouble. That they still haven't turned.

Eight ways a church comes apart

  1. Debatesquarrels
  2. Envyingsjealousy
  3. Wrathsangry outbursts
  4. Strifesselfish rivalry
  5. Backbitingstalking behind a man's back
  6. Whisperingssecret gossip
  7. Swellingspride, puffed up
  8. Tumultsdisorder and uproar

These are the everyday, quiet kind of sin, and they have broken more churches than almost anything. Most of us can find ourselves in two or three of them.

The sin, and the turning

Verse 21 names sexual sin plainly: uncleanness (moral filth), fornication (sex outside marriage) and lasciviousness (lust with no restraint). These were people who had sinned already. Paul isn't shocked; he is heartbroken. Bewail means to grieve over, like a father at a bedside.

Look at what he is actually upset about: not that they fell, but that they have not repented. Repenting is turning around: agreeing with God about it, and walking the other way. A man who falls and turns is on the road home.

Sorrow of the world

“I'm a failure.”

It stares at the mud, sinks further, and goes back to the thing it hates.

Godly sorrow

“Lord, I was wrong.”

It sees the sin, and then looks up. It turns, and it is lighter afterward. Nothing left to regret.

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Today: if there is something you have not turned from, tell God, and tell one man you trust. That is repentance, and it is the beginning of getting free.

Points to remember

  1. Humility“Of myself I will not glory, but in mine infirmities” 12:5The best day of his life was not his reputation. His weakness was.
  2. The thorn“For this thing I besought the Lord thrice” 12:8It is all right to keep asking God to take it away.
  3. Grace“My grace is sufficient for thee” 12:9Sometimes God doesn't remove the thorn. He gives what you need to carry it today.
  4. Weakness“When I am weak, then am I strong” 12:10Christ's power shows up where ours has run out.
  5. Love“I seek not yours, but you” 12:14Real love gives, spends, and keeps loving, even when it isn't returned.
  6. Repentance“Have not repented” 12:21The danger isn't the fall. It is staying down. Turn, and be forgiven.

Question 1 of 6

Humility

Why doesn't Paul boast about being caught up to heaven?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The experience was God's doing, not his, and he doesn't want people to think more of him than what they can see in his life. He keeps it quiet for fourteen years, and will boast only in his weaknesses.

Question 2 of 6

The thorn

What did Paul do about his thorn in the flesh?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He prayed about it, three times, asking the Lord to take it away. Paul never says what it was. It's all right to ask God again and again.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

What did the Lord answer when Paul asked?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

Answer

“My grace is sufficient for thee.” God didn't take the thorn away. He gave Paul grace enough to carry it, today and every day, and that grace is there for us too, in our time of need.

Question 4 of 6

Weakness

How can a man be strong when he is weak?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

When he stops leaning on his own strength, the power of Christ rests on him. Strength that comes from God doesn't run out, so the weak man who leans on Him is the strong one.

Question 5 of 6

Love

How did Paul show his love for the Corinthians?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He wouldn't take their money, and he will gladly spend himself for them, even though the more he loves them the less they love him. He wants them, not what they have.

Question 6 of 6

Repentance

What did Paul fear he would find in Corinth?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Quarrels, jealousy, gossip and pride, and some who had sinned sexually and had not repented. Paul's grief is that they have not turned, and the way back is open: confess, and He is faithful and just to forgive.
