Zechariah 2

A wall of fire

A young man sets out to measure Jerusalem's walls. God says the city will overflow its walls, and He Himself will be the wall around it.

Part one

The measuring line

Verses 1–5

The third vision of the night. Zechariah 1 recap: about 519 BC, after the exile, a small remnant is rebuilding the temple in a ruined city.

Too small a plan

In Zechariah's day Jerusalem was a ruin, thinly settled. Its walls had been broken down by Babylon, and they wouldn't be rebuilt for about another seventy years, under Nehemiah.

The young man's plan

Measure the walls

Mark out the old lines and build them back. Sensible. Safe. Small.

God's plan

No walls could hold it

So many people and cattle that the city spills out like open country towns. “Towns without walls.”

The angel is told to run and stop him. God's plans for His city were bigger than anyone's tape measure. A man starting over tends to plan small. God often has more in mind.

God is the wall

A city without walls was helpless in that world. God says: you won't need them. I will be your wall, a ring of fire nobody can get through. And inside, not just safety but His own glory, living among them.

Fire was how God led and guarded Israel out of Egypt:

“And the LORD went before them by day in a pillar of a cloud, to lead them the way; and by night in a pillar of fire, to give them light; to go by day and night:”

Exodus 13:21

When your walls are down

Some men come to Christ with every wall broken: no family left, no money, no good name, no strength to say no. They try to rebuild it all themselves, as fast as they can, before anyone sees.

Walls matter. Nehemiah built them later, and a man in recovery needs good boundaries too. But walls alone never kept anybody safe. The LORD round about His people is what keeps them.

“As the mountains are round about Jerusalem, so the LORD is round about his people from henceforth even for ever.”

Psalm 125:2

Part two

Come forth, and flee

Verses 6–9

Not everyone had come home. God calls to those still sitting comfortable in Babylon.

The land of the north

Babylon lies east of Jerusalem. But nobody crossed that desert. Every army and every caravan came round by the north, so Babylon was “the land of the north.”

“Ho, ho” is a shout: Hey! Get moving! Most Jews had stayed in Babylon. Life was settled there. God tells them to come out before judgment falls on it.

Get out while you can

Babylon wasn't a prison any more. It was comfortable. That made it harder to leave. The old life is often like that: it stops being a chain and starts being a habit, a neighbourhood, a group of friends.

  • it isn't that bad hereBabylon was under judgment
  • I'll go later“Ho, ho, come forth”: now
  • deliver thyselfget up and go home to God

“Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you,”

2 Corinthians 6:17

The apple of His eye

The “apple” of the eye is the pupil, the dark centre. It's the part of you that you protect without thinking. Something flies at your face, and your hand is up and your eyes are shut before you know it.

That's how God says He feels about His people. Hurt them, and you've poked God in the eye. The nations that robbed Israel would become “a spoil to their servants”: the robbers robbed.

“He found him in a desert land, and in the waste howling wilderness; he led him about, he instructed him, he kept him as the apple of his eye.”

Deuteronomy 32:10

“Keep me as the apple of the eye, hide me under the shadow of thy wings,”

Psalm 17:8

Part three

I will dwell in the midst of thee

Verses 10–13

The chapter ends with a song: God Himself is coming to live among His people.

God moving in

They were building a house for God. God says: I'm coming to live in it, and among you. Not a visit. A dwelling.

Listen to who is speaking in verses 9 and 11: the LORD says “the LORD of hosts hath sent me.” The LORD, sent by the LORD, to dwell among His people. The New Testament tells us how God came to live among men:

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

John 1:14

And it tells us how it will end: God living with His people for ever.

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.”

Revelation 21:3

Many nations joined

God always meant to bring the nations in. From the start He told Abraham that all the families of the earth would be blessed through him. Most of us here are Gentiles: we are the “many nations.”

“But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.”

Ephesians 2:13

“Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God;”

Ephesians 2:19

Judah, His portion

The nations are joined in, but Israel isn't pushed out. God calls Judah “his portion,” His own share, and says He will “choose Jerusalem again.” This is the only place in the Bible that calls it “the holy land.”

That promise is Israel's, and God will keep it. What it shows us is God's faithfulness: He doesn't drop the people He chose, even after the exile. That's the same God who holds on to every believer.

Be silent before the LORD.

“He is raised up out of his holy habitation”: God is rising from His throne to act. When He moves, the right thing for everyone, every nation and every man, is to hush.

Not the silence of fear only, but of awe. Stop arguing, stop explaining, and let God be God.

“But the LORD is in his holy temple: let all the earth keep silence before him.”

Habakkuk 2:20

“Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth.”

Psalm 46:10

Points to remember

  1. Protection“A wall of fire round about” 2:5When a man's walls are down, God Himself can be his wall.
  2. God's presence“The glory in the midst of her” 2:5God doesn't only guard His people. He lives among them.
  3. Getting out“Come forth, and flee” 2:6When God says leave the old life, go now, even if it has got comfortable.
  4. God's care“The apple of his eye” 2:8God guards His people the way you guard your own eye.
  5. The nations“Many nations shall be joined to the LORD” 2:11God's door is open to every nation, and through Jesus we have come in.
  6. Reverence“Be silent, O all flesh, before the LORD” 2:13Come before God with awe. Hush, and let Him be God.

Question 1 of 6

Protection

Who protects a man when his walls are down?

What verses tell us about this?

“As the mountains are round about Jerusalem, so the LORD is round about his people from henceforth even for ever.”

Psalm 125:2

Answer

The LORD. Jerusalem had no walls, and God said He would be a wall of fire around it. A man may come to Christ with every defence broken. He still needs wise boundaries, but his real safety is the God who stands round about His people.

Question 2 of 6

God's presence

What does it mean to have God in the midst?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

John 1:14

Answer

It means God is not far off. He doesn't just guard from the outside. He lives among His people. God came and dwelt among us in Jesus, and every believer now has the Holy Spirit living in him. That's reason to sing.

Question 3 of 6

Getting out

What should a man do when God says, “Get out”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you,”

2 Corinthians 6:17

Answer

Go, and go now. Babylon had become comfortable, and that made it dangerous. When God shows a man that a place, a crowd, or a habit is pulling him back, the answer isn't “later.” Come out, and God will receive you.

Question 4 of 6

God's care

How precious are God's people to Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“He found him in a desert land, and in the waste howling wilderness; he led him about, he instructed him, he kept him as the apple of his eye.”

Deuteronomy 32:10

“Keep me as the apple of the eye, hide me under the shadow of thy wings,”

Psalm 17:8

Answer

As precious as the apple of His eye. God guards His people the way a man guards his own eye, by reflex, with everything he has. Whoever hurts them has God to deal with.

Question 5 of 6

The nations

Is God's door open to people outside Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

“But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.”

Ephesians 2:13

“Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God;”

Ephesians 2:19

Answer

Yes. God said many nations would be joined to Him and be His people, while He still keeps His promises to Israel. Through the blood of Jesus, anyone who believes, from any nation, is brought near and made part of God's household.

Question 6 of 6

Reverence

How should we come before the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

“But the LORD is in his holy temple: let all the earth keep silence before him.”

Habakkuk 2:20

“Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth.”

Psalm 46:10

Answer

With awe. God is holy, and He is on the move. The right response is to hush: stop arguing and explaining, and be still before Him. He welcomes us near through Jesus, and He is still God.
