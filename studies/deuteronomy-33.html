Deuteronomy 33

Moses blesses the tribes: “underneath are the everlasting arms”

Before his death, Moses the man of God blesses Israel tribe by tribe. He begins with the LORD who came from Sinai and loved the people, and he ends with the eternal God, their refuge, and a people saved by the LORD.

Part one

“The LORD came from Sinai”

Verses 1–5

Before he blesses the tribes, Moses remembers who came to them.

Like the sun coming up

He came from Sinai, rose up from Seir, shined forth from mount Paran: the words of a sunrise. Seir is the mountain country of Edom; Paran is the wilderness Israel crossed. All the way from the mountain of the law to the edge of the land, the LORD's light went with them.

He came “with ten thousands of saints”, holy ones, and “from his right hand went a fiery law”: the law spoken out of the fire (4:36). This is how Moses begins a blessing, with God, not with the tribes.

“Yea, he loved the people”

The fiery law came out of love. Right after the fire comes “Yea, he loved the people; all his saints are in thy hand.” He did not give the law to crush them. He gave it because they were his, and held in his hand.

And they “sat down at thy feet”, like men at a teacher's feet, to “receive of thy words”. The law was their inheritance, the family treasure handed down. Jeshurun is a name for Israel, as in the song (32:15), and when the heads of the tribes were gathered, they had a King.

That is the right order for a man starting over: first know that he loves you and holds you, then sit at his feet and take his words.

Part two

“Let Reuben live, and not die”

Verses 6–17

Reuben, Judah, Levi, Benjamin and Joseph.

The tribes, in Moses' order

  1. Reuben“live, and not die” (6)
  2. Judah“Hear, LORD, the voice of Judah” (7)
  3. Levi“They shall teach Jacob thy judgments” (10)
  4. Benjamin“The beloved of the LORD” (12)
  5. Joseph“Blessed of the LORD be his land”: Ephraim and Manasseh (13–17)
  6. Zebulun“Rejoice… in thy going out” (18)
  7. Issachar“in thy tents” (18)
  8. Gad“he dwelleth as a lion” (20)
  9. Dan“a lion’s whelp” (22)
  10. Naphtali“satisfied with favour” (23)
  11. Asher“blessed with children” (24)

Like Jacob in Genesis 49, which we saw, Moses blesses the tribes before his death. Simeon is not named in this chapter.

A man with a past, and a man who prays

Reuben

“live, and not die”

The firstborn who lost his place for his sin (Genesis 49:4, which we saw). Moses does not bring it up. He asks that Reuben live, and that his men be not few.

Judah

“Hear, LORD, the voice of Judah”

A blessing that is a prayer: hear him, bring him home, make his hands enough, help him against his enemies.

“For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood.”

Hebrews 7:14

If you have a Reuben's past, here is a man of God praying you will live. And the Lion of the tribe of Judah (Revelation 5:5) is the one who hears.

Levi: chosen to stand

The Thummim and Urim were kept in the high priest's breastplate (Exodus 28:30). Levi had been proved, tested, at Massah and at the waters of Meribah. And when the golden calf was made, Levi stood:

“Then Moses stood in the gate of the camp, and said, Who is on the LORD’S side? let him come unto me. And all the sons of Levi gathered themselves together unto him.”

Exodus 32:26

That is verse 9: they put the LORD's word ahead of father, mother, brother and children. Jesus asks the same: “He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me” (Matthew 10:37).

Jacob had said of Levi's anger, “I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel” (Genesis 49:7). Now the scattering is a blessing: Levi lives in every tribe to teach Jacob thy judgments. God can turn the scar of an old sin into the place he sends a man to serve.

Between his shoulders

“the LORD shall cover him all the day long”

Benjamin, the youngest son, is called “the beloved of the LORD”. He will “dwell in safety by him”, covered all day, and dwell “between his shoulders”, the way a father carries a small boy on his back, or a shepherd carries a lamb:

“And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing.”

Luke 15:5

There are days when you cannot walk it yourself. On those days you are not dropped. You are carried.

Joseph: “the good will of him that dwelt in the bush”

13×1 14×2 15×1 16×1 17 

Precious five times: the dew of heaven, the deep that coucheth (lies) beneath, the sun's crops and the moon's, the old mountains and the lasting hills. But the best of it is in verse 16: “the good will of him that dwelt in the bush”. That is the LORD who spoke to Moses out of the bush that burned and was not consumed (Exodus 3:2), the God who came down to set his people free.

On the head of the brother who was sold, “him that was separated from his brethren”, comes the richest blessing. Joseph's two sons are his strength: horns like the horns of unicorns, beasts too strong for any man to tame; the ten thousands of Ephraim, the younger, and the thousands of Manasseh.

Part three

“Rejoice, Zebulun, in thy going out”

Verses 18–25

Six more tribes, and strength for every day.

The last six

The map shows where these six tribes would later live, when Joshua divided the land (Joshua 13–19). Zebulun and Issachar in the middle, Asher on the coast in the north, Naphtali by the lake, Gad east of Jordan, and Dan, first by the coast and then in the far north.

Moses blesses them before most of them owns a field. A blessing from God comes before you can see where it lands.

Going out, and staying home

Zebulun

“in thy going out”

Rejoice in your going out: the trade, the travel, the work away from home.

Issachar

“in thy tents”

Rejoice in your tents: the home, the family, the quiet work.

Both are to “call the people unto the mountain” and offer sacrifices of righteousness, worship that comes from a right heart. Whatever they gained from “the abundance of the seas, and of treasures hid in the sand” was to bring them to God, not away from him.

Your job and your home are both places to rejoice before the LORD, and both are places to invite other men to him.

Gad: first served, and still faithful

Gad “provided the first part for himself”: he asked for his land east of Jordan before the others had theirs (Numbers 32). It could have made him selfish. But “he came with the heads of the people, he executed the justice of the LORD”: he crossed over armed with his brothers and fought until their land was won.

“Ye have not left your brethren these many days unto this day, but have kept the charge of the commandment of the LORD your God.”

Joshua 22:3

God enlargeth Gad, makes room for him, and he “dwelleth as a lion”. A man who has been blessed first is blessed so he can stand with the men still fighting.

Dan, Naphtali, Asher

  • Dan“a lion’s whelp: he shall leap from Bashan”. A whelp is a young lion, springing out of the hills of Bashan.
  • Naphtali“satisfied with favour, and full with the blessing of the LORD”: a man who has enough, and knows where it came from.
  • Asher“blessed with children… acceptable to his brethren”, with oil so plenty he could dip his foot in it, and shoes of iron and brass for a hard road.

“Acceptable to his brethren”: of all the things to ask for a man, Moses asks that his brothers be glad to have him. That is worth praying for. Then comes the line that has carried believers ever since.

“As thy days, so shall thy strength be”

what the day asks of youthe strength God gives for it

An ordinary day
A hard day
The worst day

Not strength for next year, all at once. Strength for this day, as big as this day. Tomorrow's will come tomorrow.

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Part four

“The eternal God is thy refuge”

Verses 26–29

The blessing ends where it began: with God himself.

Underneath

The God of Jeshurun “rideth upon the heaven in thy help”: high above. And he is also underneath. However far a man falls, the everlasting arms are lower still.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father’s hand.”

John 10:28–29

Rock bottom is not the bottom for a child of God. If you are falling today, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor. The arms hold you; they will help you stand.

A refuge, and a God who fights

“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”

Psalm 46:1

A refuge is a place to run to when you are hunted, like the cities of refuge Moses set apart (4:41–43). Here the refuge is not a city. It is God himself, the eternal God, who was there before your trouble began and will be there after it ends.

Then, “he shall thrust out the enemy from before thee; and shall say, Destroy them”: the LORD would drive out the nations of Canaan before Israel, as he had promised (7:1–2). We do not fight that war. Our enemies are sin and the devil, and the LORD goes before us against them as surely as he went before Israel.

“And even to your old age I am he; and even to hoar hairs will I carry you: I have made, and I will bear; even I will carry, and will deliver you.”

Isaiah 46:4

“O people saved by the LORD”

The last words of Moses' blessing: “Happy art thou, O Israel: who is like unto thee?” Not because of their land, their numbers or their army. Because they were a “people saved by the LORD”. He is their shield and their sword. The fountain of Jacob, his children, would live safe in a land of corn and wine, with dew from heaven.

“Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ:”

Ephesians 1:3

A man saved by the LORD is a happy man in the Bible's sense, even on a hard day: blessed, kept, and on the winning side. That is not a promise of money or an easy life. It is a promise of God himself.

Real world: carried

  • SinReuben's sin and Levi's anger were real (Genesis 49:4–7), and Moses blessed them anyway.
  • RegretReuben is not cast off: “Let Reuben live, and not die.” Levi's scattering became his calling.
  • GraceBenjamin is carried “between his shoulders”, and the good will of him that dwelt in the bush rests on the brother who was sold.
  • Hope“The eternal God is thy refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms.” Rock bottom is not the bottom.
  • TodayAsk for strength for today only, as big as today. And pray for one brother by name, that he be “acceptable to his brethren”.
