Numbers 23

How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed?

Seven altars, then seven more, then seven more. Balak keeps changing the hilltop and Balaam keeps opening his mouth, and every time a blessing comes out. “God is not a man, that he should lie.”

Part one

“From the top of the rocks I see him”

Verses 1–12

The first hilltop, and the first blessing.

You can't buy God's word with oxen

Seven altars, a bullock and a ram on each. Balak pays for the sacrifices, Balaam goes off alone to an high place to see “peradventure” (perhaps) the LORD will meet him. When God comes, the first thing Balaam says is: “I have prepared seven altars.” As if God now owed him one.

God doesn't answer the boast. He puts a word in Balaam's mouth and sends him back. All that meat on all those altars changed nothing. God was not for sale on the high places of Baal, any more than at Pethor. A bullock is a young bull.

“And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.”

1 Samuel 15:22

And notice whose mouth God used. Balaam was a soothsayer for hire (Joshua 13:22 calls him “the soothsayer”), and God still spoke true words through him. God's word does not depend on how good the messenger is. But being used by God and belonging to God are not the same thing, as Balaam's end will show.

The curse that wouldn't come

How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed?

  • took up his parablebegan his prophecy: a parable here is a solemn saying, spoken as poetry
  • defycall down evil on, denounce
  • Aram, the mountains of the eastBalaam's home country, far off by the Euphrates

“As the bird by wandering, as the swallow by flying, so the curse causeless shall not come.”

Proverbs 26:2

Some of us carry curses spoken over us years ago: you'll never amount to anything, you're just like your father. A curse God has not spoken has nowhere to land. What counts is what God has said over you.

A people set apart, too many to count

From the rocks Balaam sees them, and says two things. They “shall dwell alone”: set apart, not counted as one more nation among the rest. And they are past counting: “Who can count the dust of Jacob?” It is the very promise God gave Jacob at Bethel:

“And thy seed shall be as the dust of the earth, and thou shalt spread abroad to the west, and to the east, and to the north, and to the south: and in thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed.”

Genesis 28:14

The man hired to curse ends up repeating, nearly word for word, what God swore to the fathers. The fourth part of Israel is just one quarter of the camp: he can't count even that much.

“Let me die the death of the righteous”

What Balaam wished

23:10

“Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my last end be like his!”

How Balaam died

31:8

“And they slew the kings of Midian, beside the rest of them that were slain; namely, Evi, and Rekem, and Zur, and Hur, and Reba, five kings of Midian: Balaam also the son of Beor they slew with the sword.”

He wanted a righteous man's death without a righteous man's life. He died with the sword, among the Midianites he had helped against Israel.

Lots of men want to die right with God. The time to settle it is while you're alive, not at the end. Today is the day: you don't have to wait to be a better man first. Christ died for the ungodly, and he receives you now.

Part two

“God is not a man, that he should lie”

Verses 13–24

A new hilltop, a narrower view, and a God who does not change his mind.

Show him only part of them

Balak's new idea: take Balaam where he can see “but the utmost part of them”, the edge of the camp, not the whole. Maybe a smaller Israel would be easier to curse.

The place is the field of Zophim, on “the top of Pisgah”. Moses will climb the same height to die, and the Bible tells us where it is:

“And Moses went up from the plains of Moab unto the mountain of Nebo, to the top of Pisgah, that is over against Jericho. And the LORD shewed him all the land of Gilead, unto Dan,”

Deuteronomy 34:1

Yonder means over there.

The enemy wants you looking at the edge

Balak's trick still works. If you look at only part of God's people, the worst part, the edge of the camp, you can talk yourself into anything: church people are hypocrites, nobody there is real, I'm better off on my own.

And the devil does it with you: he shows you only the edge of your own life, the worst week, the old record, so you'll curse yourself. But God saw the whole camp, and he still said, “they are blessed.”

He does not change his mind

God is not a man, that he should lie.

Balak hoped a new hilltop would get a new answer. But God isn't like a man, who says one thing and does another, or regrets a promise and takes it back. Repent here means change his mind. If he said it, he will do it. If he blessed, nobody can reverse it.

“In hope of eternal life, which God, that cannot lie, promised before the world began;”

Titus 1:2

“For I am the LORD, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed.”

Malachi 3:6

That is the ground a believer stands on. God's promises to you in Christ don't depend on how you felt this morning. “He hath blessed; and I cannot reverse it.”

Blessed, and it stays blessed

“Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ:”

Ephesians 1:3

“What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us?”

Romans 8:31

Balaam's words fit every man who has trusted Christ: I have received commandment to bless. No dealer, no old friend, no voice in your own head can undo what God has said over you. They can tempt you. They cannot reverse your blessing.

“He hath not beheld iniquity in Jacob”

Down in the camp

Numbers 21:5

“And the people spake against God, and against Moses, Wherefore have ye brought us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? for there is no bread, neither is there any water; and our soul loatheth this light bread.”

From the hilltop

Numbers 23:21

“He hath not beheld iniquity in Jacob, neither hath he seen perverseness in Israel: the LORD his God is with him, and the shout of a king is among them.”

Israel had just been grumbling against God, and two chapters later they fall into worse. Yet this is what God says over them. Not because they were sinless, but because he had dealt with their sin, by the blood on the altar, and by a serpent of brass lifted on a pole for the bitten to look at and live (21:8–9).

“Saying, Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered. Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin.”

Romans 4:7–8

The shout of a king, and the unicorn

  • the shout of a kingthe cheer of an army with its king among them: the LORD himself is with them
  • unicorna strong horned beast that no man could tame or harness (Job 39:9–10)
  • perversenesscrookedness, wrong

“Will the unicorn be willing to serve thee, or abide by thy crib? Canst thou bind the unicorn with his band in the furrow? or will he harrow the valleys after thee?”

Job 39:9–10

“God brought them out of Egypt”, and the strength he gave them is like that beast's, the kind nobody can yoke. When God looks at a forgiven man, he doesn't see the record. He sees the King in the camp.

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

“What hath God wrought!”

  • enchantmentspells, sorcery, charms
  • divinationreading omens and signs for hidden things
  • wroughtdone, worked

No spell works against a people God has blessed: not Balaam's, not anyone's. Instead, people will look at Israel and say, What hath God wrought! They are a good thing to say over a life God has rebuilt.

“No weapon that is formed against thee shall prosper; and every tongue that shall rise against thee in judgment thou shalt condemn. This is the heritage of the servants of the LORD, and their righteousness is of me, saith the LORD.”

Isaiah 54:17

The lion in verse 24 is the people rising up in strength. Jacob had said it of Judah long before: “he couched as a lion … who shall rouse him up?” (Genesis 49:9). We'll hear it again in the next chapter.

Part three

“Neither curse them at all, nor bless them at all”

Verses 25–30

If he can't have a curse, he'll settle for silence.

Settling for silence

Balak gives up on the curse. Fine. Then just say nothing. “Neither curse them at all, nor bless them at all.”

That is the enemy's fallback with a believer. If he can't get you cursed, he'll be happy with you quiet: saved, but never speaking a word for God, never blessing anyone, sitting in the back with your mouth shut. Balaam's answer is better than Balaam was: “All that the LORD speaketh, that I must do.”

Three hilltops, one answer

0curses

“Peradventure it will please God,” says Balak, as if God could be worn down by a change of scenery. A new hill, a new view, more altars: the same answer. Changing the scenery doesn't change God's word. Jeshimon is the waste land below that Peor looks toward.

Remember the name Peor

On the top of Peor Balaam will bless Israel again. But down in the camp at Shittim, two chapters on, Israel will fall: to the daughters of Moab and their god, Baal-peor. What Balaam could not do with a curse, he did with advice. Jesus himself says so:

“But I have a few things against thee, because thou hast there them that hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balac to cast a stumblingblock before the children of Israel, to eat things sacrificed unto idols, and to commit fornication.”

Revelation 2:14

The enemy who can't curse you will try to invite you. A man God has blessed is rarely brought down by an attack from outside. He is brought down by an invitation he accepts.

Real world: what God says over you

Balak had money, altars, hilltops and a famous prophet. God had one word: blessed. The word won every time.

  • SinIsrael's was real: the grumbling in chapter 21, the fall at Peor to come. So is ours.
  • RegretOld curses still echo: what people said, what we said about ourselves.
  • Grace“He hath not beheld iniquity in Jacob”: the sin covered, not counted (Romans 4:7–8).
  • Hope“He hath blessed; and I cannot reverse it.” God is not a man, that he should lie.
  • TodayWrite down one curse you've believed about yourself. Next to it, write Numbers 23:19–20. Read it out loud to a brother.

Points to remember

  1. The altars“offered a bullock and a ram on every altar” 23:30Three hilltops and twenty-one altars could not buy a curse. God's word is not for sale.
  2. The word in his mouth“the LORD put a word in Balaam’s mouth” 23:5God spoke through a hired soothsayer. Being used by God is not the same as belonging to him.
  3. Not cursed“How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed?” 23:8A curse God has not spoken has nowhere to land.
  4. The last end“Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my last end be like his!” 23:10He wanted a righteous man's death without a righteous man's life, and died by the sword.
  5. God cannot lie“God is not a man, that he should lie … he hath blessed; and I cannot reverse it” 23:19–20What God has said, he will do. What he has blessed, nobody can reverse.
  6. No iniquity beheld“He hath not beheld iniquity in Jacob … the shout of a king is among them” 23:21God sees his forgiven people with their sin covered, and himself in their midst.

Question 1 of 6

The altars

What did Balak and Balaam offer, and how many times did they move?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A bullock and a ram on each of seven altars, on three hilltops: the high places of Baal, the top of Pisgah, and the top of Peor. Twenty-one altars, and not one curse.

Question 2 of 6

The word in his mouth

Whose word came out of Balaam's mouth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD's. God put the word in Balaam's mouth, and Balaam could say nothing else. God used a hired man's mouth, but that didn't make the man his.

Question 3 of 6

Not cursed

Why could Balaam not curse Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

“As the bird by wandering, as the swallow by flying, so the curse causeless shall not come.”

Proverbs 26:2

Answer

Because God had not cursed them; he had blessed them. No spell or omen works against a people God has blessed. The curse causeless shall not come.

Question 4 of 6

The last end

What did Balaam wish for his own death?

What verses tell us about this?

“And they slew the kings of Midian, beside the rest of them that were slain; namely, Evi, and Rekem, and Zur, and Hur, and Reba, five kings of Midian: Balaam also the son of Beor they slew with the sword.”

Numbers 31:8

Answer

To die the death of the righteous. But he would not live the righteous man's life, and he was slain with the sword among the kings of Midian. Settle it with God while you live.

Question 5 of 6

God cannot lie

What does verse 19 say God is not, and what can no man do to his blessing?

What verses tell us about this?

“In hope of eternal life, which God, that cannot lie, promised before the world began;”

Titus 1:2

Answer

God is not a man, that he should lie, or change his mind. What he has said, he will do. What he has blessed, no man can reverse.

Question 6 of 6

No iniquity beheld

What did God not behold in Jacob, and what was among them instead?

What verses tell us about this?

“Saying, Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered. Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin.”

Romans 4:7–8

Answer

Iniquity and perverseness. Instead, the LORD their God was with them, and the shout of a king among them. Blessed is the man whose sins are covered.
