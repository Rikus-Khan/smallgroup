Chapter 13 was dark: a beast out of the sea, a beast out of the earth, a mark, and a world bowing down.

Chapter 14 is God's answer. Before the last judgments fall (the seven bowls of chapters 15 and 16), He lets John see how the story ends. Teachers differ on the exact order of these scenes, but they all agree: this chapter is a look ahead, and it is good news for the faithful.

Part one

The Lamb on mount Sion

Verses 1–5

The beast had the crowd. The Lamb has His people, and they're singing.

Turn the page

Lay chapter 13 and chapter 14 side by side:

Revelation 13

A beast rises

Out of the restless sea. His mark is on hands and foreheads. The world sings, “Who is like unto the beast?”

Revelation 14

The Lamb stands

On solid ground, on mount Sion. “His Father’s name” is on His people's foreheads. They sing a new song.

Sion (Zion) is the hill of Jerusalem where God set His King: “Yet have I set my king upon my holy hill of Zion” (Psalm 2:6). Many read it here as the heavenly Zion, “the city of the living God” (Hebrews 12:22). Either way, the Lamb is the one still standing.

Who are the 144,000?

Good teachers read them two main ways:

Many Baptist teachers

Israel, sealed

The same 144,000 as chapter 7, “of all the tribes of the children of Israel” (7:4): Jewish believers sealed and kept through the time of trouble.

Other teachers

All the redeemed

A picture of God's whole people, counted and complete: twelve times twelve times a thousand, not one missing.

What's plain either way: they were “redeemed from the earth” (14:3), bought. They carry God's name, not the beast's. And not one of them was lost: the number that was sealed in chapter 7 is the number standing here.

Clean before the throne

“These are they which were not defiled with women; for they are virgins” (14:4).

Some read this as men who stayed single to serve God in hard days. Many read it as faithfulness: kept for the Lamb, never chasing the beast, like a church kept “as a chaste virgin to Christ” (2 Corinthians 11:2).

If your past is full of sexual sin, you might think, “That leaves me out.” Look at verse 5: “without fault before the throne of God.” Nobody gets there on his own record. They are redeemed: bought and made clean.

“Now unto him that is able to keep you from falling, and to present you faultless before the presence of his glory with exceeding joy,”

Jude 24

Firstfruits

At harvest, Israel brought the very first sheaf to God before reaping the rest:

“Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When ye be come into the land which I give unto you, and shall reap the harvest thereof, then ye shall bring a sheaf of the firstfruits of your harvest unto the priest:”

Leviticus 23:10

The firstfruits are God's own, and they are a promise: a full harvest is coming. Hold on to that word harvest. It's how this chapter ends.

Part two

Three angels

Verses 6–13

Three messengers fly over the earth: an offer, a fall, and a warning. Then a blessing from heaven.

Three angels, three messages

1 · The offerThe everlasting gospel

“to every nation, and kindred, and tongue, and people”

14:6–7
2 · The fallBabylon is fallen

“is fallen, that great city”

14:8
3 · The warningDon't take the mark

“If any man worship the beast and his image”

14:9–11

Notice the order. Before the warning, before the fall, the very first angel carries the gospel. Even this late in the story, God offers mercy before He judges.

The offer is still open

The beast had power “over all kindreds, and tongues, and nations” (13:7). God's gospel goes to the very same people: every nation, kindred, tongue, people. Nobody is outside its reach. Not the man in a cell, not the man who just relapsed.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

“Fear God” isn't terror. It's taking Him seriously: worship the One who made heaven and earth, not the beast who only borrowed power.

“Babylon is fallen”

Isaiah 21:9

And, behold, here cometh a chariot of men, with a couple of horsemen. And he answered and said, Babylon is fallen, is fallen; and all the graven images of her gods he hath broken unto the ground.

Revelation 14:8

And there followed another angel, saying, Babylon is fallen, is fallen, that great city, because she made all nations drink of the wine of the wrath of her fornication.

Babylon was the proud city that burned Jerusalem and carried God's people away. In the New Testament it became a name for the world's power set against God; Peter even wrote from “Babylon” (1 Peter 5:13), meaning Rome. Teachers differ on what Babylon is at the end: a city, or the whole system of pride, pleasure and money. Chapters 17 and 18 tell its fall. It's said here as already done: “is fallen.”

Two cups

Babylon hands the nations a drink, and they get drunk on her sin. Some of you know that cup: it promises everything and leaves you on the floor.

The man who keeps drinking from her cup ends up drinking from God's. Wine then was usually cut with water. God's wrath is “without mixture”: full strength, nothing watered down.

It's sober. But a warning is mercy. God tells you where the road ends while you can still turn off it.

Why warn at all?

Jesus spoke about hell more than anyone else in the Bible. Not to scare people for fun, but because it's real, and He came to keep people out of it. This warning comes before the end, while the first angel is still preaching.

“Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.”

John 5:24

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

If you belong to Christ, this verse isn't aimed at you. If you're not sure you do, it's an invitation: the door is open today.

No rest, or rest

“No rest day nor night”

Those who worship the beast and take his mark.

14:11
“Rest from their labours”

The dead which die in the Lord.

14:13

Between them stands verse 12: “Here is the patience of the saints.” Keep God's commandments, keep trusting Jesus, and don't quit. The restless way and the restful way are both open to a man today. Only one ends in rest.

For the ones you've buried

Some of you have stood at a grave this year: a mother, a brother, a friend who knew the Lord. Heaven says they are blessed. Their hard days are over, and nothing they did for Christ is lost: “their works do follow them.”

“But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope.”

1 Thessalonians 4:13

Grieve; Jesus wept too. Just not without hope. And if you're unsure where someone stood, leave him with a just and merciful God, and settle where you stand.

Part three

Two harvests

Verses 14–20

Sickles come out. The grain is ripe, and so are the grapes. What God has waited for, He gathers.

One like the Son of man

Daniel 7:13

I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him.

Revelation 14:14

And I looked, and behold a white cloud, and upon the cloud one sat like unto the Son of man, having on his head a golden crown, and in his hand a sharp sickle.

“The Son of man” was Jesus' favorite name for Himself. Here He wears a golden crown, the victor's crown, and He holds the sickle. The harvest of the earth is in His hand, not the beast's.

Grain and grapes

First the grain. The Son of man reaps “the harvest of the earth.” Many see God gathering His people home, like wheat “into my barn” (Matthew 13:30).

Then the grapes, cast “into the great winepress of the wrath of God.” That is judgment on those who would not turn. Some see both harvests as judgment; all agree the grapes are.

And it comes when “the time is come,” not before. God waits long, but the harvest comes.

Without the city

The prophet Joel saw this same harvest: “Put ye in the sickle, for the harvest is ripe… for their wickedness is great” (Joel 3:13). John sees blood “unto the horse bridles” for “a thousand and six hundred furlongs”: about 180 miles. It's a picture of judgment so full and so wide that nobody hides from it. We won't dwell on it. We will notice one phrase:

“trodden without the city”

“Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate.”

Hebrews 13:12

Jesus already went outside the city, and bore God's wrath there, for every man who will come to Him. You don't have to stand in that winepress. He stood in it for you.

Take it to work today

14:6The offer is open

The first angel is still flying. If you've never settled it with Jesus, settle it today. If you have, tell one man the good news.

14:8Put down Babylon's cup

Name the drink, the screen, the habit that promises rest and leaves you restless. Don't pick it up today.

14:13Your work follows you

Nothing you do for Christ is wasted, not the shift you work honestly, not the call you make to a struggling brother.

Points to remember

  1. The Lamb“A Lamb stood on the mount Sion” 14:1The 144,000, redeemed from among men, carry the Father's name, and not one is missing.
  2. The gospel“The everlasting gospel… to every nation” 14:6God offers mercy to every people before He judges; the offer is still open.
  3. Babylon“Babylon is fallen, is fallen” 14:8The world's proud system that makes the nations drunk is as good as gone.
  4. The warning“If any man worship the beast” 14:9Those who worship the beast drink God's wrath without mixture; the warning comes while there's time to turn.
  5. Rest“Blessed are the dead which die in the Lord” 14:13Believers who die rest from their labours, and their works follow them.
  6. Harvest“The harvest of the earth is ripe” 14:15Christ holds the sickle: He gathers His own, and judgment comes on time.

Question 1 of 6

The Lamb

Who stands with the Lamb on mount Sion?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The 144,000, with His Father's name in their foreheads. Teachers differ on whether they are Israel's sealed believers or a picture of all the redeemed, but plainly they were redeemed from among men, they follow the Lamb wherever He goes, and not one is missing.

Question 2 of 6

The gospel

Who is the everlasting gospel preached to?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

To everyone who lives on the earth: every nation, kindred, tongue and people. The same people the beast ruled over are offered the gospel first. Nobody is outside its reach, and the offer is still open.

Question 3 of 6

Babylon

What does the second angel cry?

What verses tell us about this?

“And, behold, here cometh a chariot of men, with a couple of horsemen. And he answered and said, Babylon is fallen, is fallen; and all the graven images of her gods he hath broken unto the ground.”

Isaiah 21:9

Answer

“Babylon is fallen, is fallen.” The great city that made the nations drunk on her sin is finished; God speaks of it as already done. Whatever the world builds against Him comes down.

Question 4 of 6

The warning

What warning does the third angel give?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Whoever worships the beast and takes his mark will drink the wine of God's wrath, full strength, with no rest. It's said plainly so men can turn while the gospel is still being preached. In Christ there is no condemnation.

Question 5 of 6

Rest

What does heaven say about those who die in the Lord?

What verses tell us about this?

“We are confident, I say, and willing rather to be absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord.”

2 Corinthians 5:8

Answer

They are blessed. They rest from their labours, and their works follow them: nothing done for Christ is lost. We grieve the believers we've lost, but not as men without hope.

Question 6 of 6

Harvest

What do the two harvests show us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That Christ holds the sickle and the timing. Many see the grain as God gathering His people and the grapes as judgment on those who would not turn. Both come when “the time is come.” Jesus already bore that wrath outside the city for all who come to Him.
