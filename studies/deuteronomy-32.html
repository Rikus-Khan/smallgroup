Deuteronomy 32

The song of Moses: “He is the Rock”

Moses sings Israel's whole story before it happens: the Rock who found them and carried them like an eagle, the people who waxed fat and forgot him, the judgment, and the mercy at the end. Then he tells them, “it is your life”, and the LORD sends him up mount Nebo to see the land and die.

Part one

“He is the Rock”

Verses 1–14

Who God is, and what he did for a people he found in the desert.

Why a song?

In chapter 31 the LORD told Moses to write a song and teach it to the people, because a song stays in a man's head long after a sermon is gone:

“Now therefore write ye this song for you, and teach it the children of Israel: put it in their mouths, that this song may be a witness for me against the children of Israel.”

Deuteronomy 31:19

“Moses therefore wrote this song the same day” (31:22). Years later, when their children had forgotten God, they would still know the words, and the words would tell them what had happened to them and why.

“And they sing the song of Moses the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying, Great and marvellous are thy works, Lord God Almighty; just and true are thy ways, thou King of saints.”

Revelation 15:3

The song of Moses is still being sung in heaven, beside the song of the Lamb.

The Rock, five times

4×1 15×1 18×1 30×1 31×1

Five times the song calls God “the Rock”: the Rock whose work is perfect (4), the Rock of salvation (15), the Rock that begat thee (18), their Rock (30), and “our Rock” (31). A rock does not move, does not change its mind, and does not give way when you lean on it.

“And did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ.”

1 Corinthians 10:4

Paul tells us who the Rock was. The men who forgot him in the song forgot Christ.

“Do ye thus requite the LORD?”

What he is

“his work is perfect”

“All his ways are judgment”: everything he does is right. “A God of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he.” Nothing crooked in him.

What they became

“a perverse and crooked generation”

“They have corrupted themselves.” Their spot, the mark on them, was not the mark of his children.

Requite means pay back. Moses asks, Is this how you pay back the one who is “thy father that hath bought thee”? He made them, bought them, and set them on their feet. Paul will call the same kind of people “a crooked and perverse nation” and tell believers to shine in the middle of it (Philippians 2:15).

“Ask thy father”

The way to remember is to ask. “Ask thy father, and he will shew thee; thy elders, and they will tell thee.” The old men had seen the sea part and the manna fall. Go and sit with them.

The most High gave every nation its place, and set its bounds, its borders. But of all the earth, he kept one thing for himself: “the LORD’S portion is his people; Jacob is the lot of his inheritance.” The nations had land. The LORD had a people.

You need older men in the faith. Find a man who has walked with God for twenty years, and ask him what the LORD has done. Then one day a younger man will ask you.

As an eagle stirreth up her nest

Five things the eagle does: she stirreth up the nest, fluttereth over her young, spreadeth abroad her wings, taketh them, and beareth them. The nest is stirred; the young are not dropped.

“I bare you on eagles’ wings, and brought you unto myself” (Exodus 19:4). He kept Israel as the apple of his eye, the pupil, the part a man guards first.

Sometimes God stirs a comfortable nest: a job ends, a crutch is taken away. It feels like falling. He is underneath, wings out.

Everything good, and only from him

  • “ride on the high places”set on the heights of the land, over his enemies
  • “honey out of the rock”sweetness where you would expect none
  • “butter of kine, and milk of sheep”the best of the herds; kine are cows
  • “rams of the breed of Bashan”the finest flocks of the rich pasture east of Jordan
  • “the fat of kidneys of wheat”the very best of the wheat
  • “the pure blood of the grape”the juice of the grape, wine

Verse 12 says why: “the LORD alone did lead him, and there was no strange god with him.” No idol helped. Every good thing Israel would have came from one hand. Hold on to that, because the next verse is where it goes wrong.

Part two

“Jeshurun waxed fat, and kicked”

Verses 15–33

Full, then forgetful, then gone after other gods, and the LORD's answer.

Fed, fat, and gone

  1. waxed fatgrew fat on God's gifts (15)
  2. kickedlike a well-fed ox that won't be led (15)
  3. forsook God“which made him” (15)
  4. lightly esteemed the Rockthought little of him (15)
  5. sacrificed unto devils“new gods that came newly up” (17)

Jeshurun is a name for Israel, used three times in Deuteronomy (32:15; 33:5, 26). Moses warned of this in chapter 8: “when thou hast eaten and art full… Then thine heart be lifted up, and thou forget the LORD” (8:12–14). Here the song sings it as if it had already happened.

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

“New gods that came newly up”

Israel did not wake up one day and decide to hate God. They got comfortable, and then something new came along, gods “whom your fathers feared not”. Paul says what was really behind those idols:

“But I say, that the things which the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to devils, and not to God: and I would not that ye should have fellowship with devils.”

1 Corinthians 10:20

A man in recovery knows this. It is rarely the worst week that takes him down. It is the good stretch, when the bills are paid and he stops calling his brothers, and a new thing comes newly up. Unmindful means forgetful: “Of the Rock that begat thee thou art unmindful.”

If you can see yourself on these stairs, you are not on the bottom one. Turn round today and tell a brother which step you're on.

Jealousy for jealousy

Israel→moved me to jealousy→“with that which is not God”idols, their vanities: empty things that can't help
The LORD→will move them to jealousy→“with those which are not a people”the nations, the Gentiles

“But I say, Did not Israel know? First Moses saith, I will provoke you to jealousy by them that are no people, and by a foolish nation I will anger you.”

Romans 10:19

Paul quotes this verse by Moses' name. God would turn to the nations, to people like us, and Israel would see it and want back what they had thrown away. Even the judgment has a door in it: God's jealousy is a husband's, and he means to win them home (Romans 11:11).

The arrows

Hunger, burning heat, wild beasts, serpents, the sword outside and terror inside, from the young man to the man of gray hairs, and the suckling, the baby still at the breast. It is hard to read, and it is meant to be. Sin against this much love is not a small thing.

It all came. The Bible itself tells how Israel was carried away for it: “the children of Israel had sinned against the LORD their God” (2 Kings 17:7), and later Judah (2 Kings 25). The song was a warning sung ahead of time, so that no one could say they were not told.

Mischiefs are harms and disasters; to spend arrows is to use them all up. Hell here, “the lowest hell”, is the deepest place of the dead.

“O that they were wise”

God could have wiped out their very memory. He holds back for his own name, so their enemies cannot behave themselves strangely, mistake it, and boast, “Our hand is high”.

“O that they were wise… that they would consider their latter end!”

Listen to the heart in it: a Father who wishes his sons would see where the road ends before they get there. Their latter end is where their way leads.

Think your road through to the end today. Where does the drink, the old crowd, the lie, end up? God would rather you saw it now.

“Their rock is not as our Rock”

How could one enemy chase a thousand of Israel? Only one way: “except their Rock had sold them, and the LORD had shut them up.” When Israel lost, it was not because the enemy was strong. It was because the LORD had stepped back.

And the enemies themselves knew their gods were no match: “even our enemies themselves being judges.” Their vine came “of the vine of Sodom, and of the fields of Gomorrah”, the cities God burned. Its grapes are grapes of gall, bitter poison.

“The poison of dragons”

Verse 33 is about the wine of Israel's enemies, the fruit of Sodom's vine: what that world pours out looks like wine and works like venom. The asp is a deadly snake, and the verse sets the dragons beside it.

The verse says what it says, and so does the rest of Scripture: wine was on Israel's tables (verse 14). But a man God has set free from the cup has no business going back to it, and nothing here gives him an excuse:

“Look not thou upon the wine when it is red, when it giveth his colour in the cup, when it moveth itself aright. At the last it biteth like a serpent, and stingeth like an adder.”

Proverbs 23:31–32

Part three

“To me belongeth vengeance”

Verses 34–43

The LORD judges, the LORD heals, and the nations are called to rejoice.

His, not yours

Recompence is payback. God says it is “laid up in store with me, and sealed up among my treasures”: he has not forgotten one wrong, and in due time the guilty man's foot will slide.

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

“For we know him that hath said, Vengeance belongeth unto me, I will recompense, saith the Lord. And again, The Lord shall judge his people. It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God.”

Hebrews 10:30–31

Paul quotes this verse twice, in Romans 12:19 and Hebrews 10:30. In Romans he uses it to set us free from getting even. Many of us carry a list: the man who hurt them, the one who lied in court, the one who sold to them. Hand the list to God. It is safe with him, and you don't have to carry it any more.

When their power is gone

Look when God turns toward his people: “when he seeth that their power is gone.” Not when they have pulled themselves together. When there is nothing left. Repent himself here means he turns in pity toward his servants.

If you are at the end of your strength today, that is the very place this verse says God looks. Don't sit there alone. Call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor, and tell them where you are.

“I wound, and I heal”

First the question: where are the gods you fed? Let them get up and help you now. They can't. Then God speaks of himself: “See now that I, even I, am he, and there is no god with me.”

I→kill→and I make aliveLife and death are in one hand.
I→wound→and I healThe hand that wounds is the hand that heals.

“Come, and let us return unto the LORD: for he hath torn, and he will heal us; he hath smitten, and he will bind us up.”

Hosea 6:1

Some of your wounds God allowed, to bring you down to where you would listen. The same hand will heal them. “Neither is there any that can deliver out of my hand”: nothing can pull you out of it either.

“Rejoice, O ye nations, with his people”

God lifts his hand to heaven and swears by the only one greater than any oath: “I live for ever.” He will whet, sharpen, his glittering sword and render, pay back, vengeance to his enemies. Then the song ends, not in judgment, but in joy:

“And again he saith, Rejoice, ye Gentiles, with his people.”

Romans 15:10

Paul quotes the song's last verse to show that the Gentiles were always meant to be brought in. The nations are called to rejoice alongside Israel. That is us. A song that warns Israel of judgment ends by inviting the rest of the world to the celebration, because God “will be merciful unto his land, and to his people.”

Part four

“It is your life”

Verses 44–52

Moses' last charge, and the call up the mountain.

Not a vain thing

Moses does not sing alone. Beside him is Hoshea the son of Nun, Joshua's first name: “Moses called Oshea the son of Nun Jehoshua” (Numbers 13:16). The old leader and the new one teach the song together.

“because it is your life”

A vain thing is an empty one, worth nothing. These words are not that. They are not rules on top of your life; they are your life. Moses said it in chapter 8, and Jesus answered the devil with it: “man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD” (8:3).

“It is the spirit that quickeneth; the flesh profiteth nothing: the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life.”

John 6:63

Two brothers, two mountains

Mount HorAaron “died in mount Hor, and was gathered unto his people” (verse 50; Numbers 20:28)
Mount Nebo“in the land of Moab, that is over against Jericho” (verse 49)

We saw the LORD's word to Moses about this mountain in Numbers 27. What is new is when: “that selfsame day”, the day he finished the song. He has handed on everything, and now he is called up.

“Be gathered unto thy people”: death for God's servant is not the end of him. It is going home to his people, as Aaron did.

“Yet thou shalt see the land”

The reason is given one more time: at the waters of Meribah-Kadesh, “ye sanctified me not”. Moses did not treat the LORD as holy in front of the people (Numbers 20:12). We saw that in Numbers 20. The sin was forgiven; the consequence stood.

But the verse turns on one word: yet. “Yet thou shalt see the land before thee.” The LORD did not send him off to die in a ditch. He took him up high and showed him everything.

“These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off, and were persuaded of them, and embraced them, and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.”

Hebrews 11:13

Real world: sing it on a full stomach

  • SinJeshurun waxed fat and kicked. Comfort, not crisis, is where most men forget the Rock.
  • Regret“O that they were wise… that they would consider their latter end!” Think your road through to the end before you walk it.
  • GraceGod turns to his servants “when he seeth that their power is gone”. The end of your strength is where he looks.
  • Hope“I wound, and I heal.” The hand that brought you low will lift you up, and the song ends with the nations rejoicing.
  • TodayLearn one line of this song by heart: “He is the Rock, his work is perfect.” Say it on the good day, and call a brother before the new thing comes newly up.

Points to remember

  1. The Rock“He is the Rock, his work is perfect: for all his ways are judgment” 32:4Five times the song calls him the Rock, and “that Rock was Christ”.
  2. The eagle“As an eagle stirreth up her nest… beareth them on her wings” 32:11He stirs the nest, and he is underneath.
  3. Waxed fat“But Jeshurun waxed fat, and kicked” 32:15Full, then forgetful: they forsook the God who made them.
  4. Vengeance“To me belongeth vengeance, and recompence” 32:35It belongs to God, so we can lay down getting even (Romans 12:19).
  5. Wounded and healed“I kill, and I make alive; I wound, and I heal” 32:39The hand that wounds is the hand that heals.
  6. Your life“For it is not a vain thing for you; because it is your life” 32:47God's words are not rules on top of life. They are the life.

Question 1 of 6

The Rock

What does Moses call the LORD, and what does he say about his work?

What verses tell us about this?

“And did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ.”

1 Corinthians 10:4

Answer

He calls him the Rock. His work is perfect, all his ways are judgment, and he is a God of truth and without iniquity. Paul says that Rock was Christ.

Question 2 of 6

The eagle

How did the LORD lead Jacob through the wilderness?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He found him in the wilderness, led him, taught him and guarded him like the apple of his eye. Like an eagle, he stirred up the nest and bore them on his wings, and he did it alone, with no strange god.

Question 3 of 6

Waxed fat

What did Jeshurun do when he was full?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He kicked. He forsook God who made him, thought little of the Rock of his salvation, went after new gods and forgot the God who formed him.

Question 4 of 6

Vengeance

To whom does vengeance belong?

What verses tell us about this?

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

Answer

To the LORD. So we do not avenge ourselves: we give place unto wrath and leave the payback to him.

Question 5 of 6

Wounded and healed

What does the LORD say he does in verse 39?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come, and let us return unto the LORD: for he hath torn, and he will heal us; he hath smitten, and he will bind us up.”

Hosea 6:1

Answer

He kills and makes alive, he wounds and he heals, and none can deliver out of his hand. There is no god with him.

Question 6 of 6

Your life

Why did Moses say these words were not a vain thing?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because they were their life. By them they would prolong their days in the land, and they were to command their children to keep them.
