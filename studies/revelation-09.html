Revelation 9

The pit is opened, and still they would not turn

Two trumpets sound, and the darkness gets loose for a while. God's people are kept safe through it. And the chapter ends with men who keep running back to the very things that are killing them, while the door to come home stands open.

In chapter 8 the first four trumpets struck the land, the sea, the rivers and the sky, each only “the third part.” Like the plagues of Egypt, they are warnings with a limit on them, not the end. Then an angel cried out that the last three would be worse:

“And I beheld, and heard an angel flying through the midst of heaven, saying with a loud voice, Woe, woe, woe, to the inhabiters of the earth by reason of the other voices of the trumpet of the three angels, which are yet to sound!”

Revelation 8:13

Part one

The fifth trumpet: the pit is opened

Verses 1–12

Darkness gets loose for a season. Watch who holds the key, who sets the limits, and who is kept safe.

A star with a key

In Revelation a star can stand for an angel (1:20). Some teachers see a fallen angel here (Luke 10:18); others a good angel sent by God, as in 20:1. The bottomless pit is the abyss, where evil spirits are locked up (Luke 8:31).

Either way: “to him was given.” He didn't take the key. Nothing gets out of that pit unless God allows it. The keys that matter are in Jesus' hand:

“I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death.”

Revelation 1:18

Not one of His

Real locusts eat everything green. These must leave it alone and touch only men without God's seal. In chapter 7, God marked His servants first:

“Saying, Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads.”

Revelation 7:3

He did the same in Egypt:

“They saw not one another, neither rose any from his place for three days: but all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings.”

Exodus 10:23

“Men shall seek death, and shall not find it.”

Pain with no way out: hurting so badly a man wants to die, and not even that comes. Some of us know that place, in a cell or at the end of a run. If that's you today, say it out loud to a brother here, or call or text your brothers in Christ. You don't have to sit in it alone.

And the torment has a limit: “five months,” about one locust season. God sets the edges even on judgment. This is not how His story ends for His people:

“And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.”

Revelation 21:4

“Like … as it were …”

2×1 3×1 4  5×1 6  7×4 8×2 9×2 10×1

“As” and “like” twelve times in nine verses. John is reaching for words for something he has never seen. Joel saw a locust army the same way: “The appearance of them is as the appearance of horses” (Joel 2:4).

Some teachers say

Real creatures

Demons let out of the pit, taking a shape men can see, in a time still to come.

Others say

A picture

Evil spiritual forces, drawn with Joel's locusts and Egypt's plague, that torment those who belong to darkness.

Both agree on what matters: it is real evil, it is on a leash, and it cannot touch God's sealed people.

“One woe is past.” Even this woe ends. The Destroyer's season always has an edge on it.

Part two

The sixth trumpet: the Euphrates

Verses 13–19

An answer that starts at the altar of prayer, and an hour that God had already set.

It starts at the altar of prayer

The golden altar stood just outside the holy of holies. It was the altar of incense, the place of prayer, and its four corners had horns. In the last chapter we saw what was offered on it:

“And another angel came and stood at the altar, having a golden censer; and there was given unto him much incense, that he should offer it with the prayers of all saints upon the golden altar which was before the throne.”

Revelation 8:3

So this order comes from the place where His people's prayers are kept. They had cried, “How long?” (6:10), and God heard every one. And these four were “bound” until He said “Loose.” Evil doesn't run on its own clock.

The great river Euphrates

The Euphrates was the far edge of John's world, about six hundred miles east of Patmos. Beyond it lay Rome's feared enemy, the Parthians. Long before, Assyria and Babylon came from beyond it to carry God's people away.

So “the Euphrates” meant the place invasion comes from. Some teachers take it as the real river and a real army still to come; others as a picture of judgment pouring in from that frontier.

To the very hour

Not just a year, or a month. A year, a month, a day, and an hour. These angels were kept waiting for one exact moment that God had marked. Revelation can look like chaos. It is not. Nothing in it is early or late.

That is good news for a man whose life has felt out of control. The same God holds your hours too: the court date, the release, the day the craving finally lets go. He is never late.

An army too big to count

The whole Roman army was a few hundred thousand men. John couldn't count this host; he heard the number. Fire and brimstone (burning sulphur) mark God's judgment from Sodom on.

Some teachers say

A real host

A vast army, demonic or driven by demons, at the end of the age.

Others say

A huge number

“More than anyone can count”: judgment no one can stand against.

Still only “the third part.” God is holding judgment back and giving time.

Part three

Yet they repented not

Verses 20–21

The saddest two verses in the chapter, and the ones that speak most plainly to anyone who has fought an addiction.

You become like what you worship

Psalm 115:4

Their idols are silver and gold, the work of men’s hands.

Psalm 115:8

They that make them are like unto them; so is every one that trusteth in them.

An idol is anything a man runs to instead of God, the thing he trusts to fix how he feels. It can't see you, hear you, or walk with you. And the longer a man serves it, the more he becomes like it: blind to what it's costing, deaf to the people who love him, stuck in one place. “The works of their hands” is what we build our lives around. Behind every idol, verse 20 says, stand “devils.”

“Sorceries”

φαρμακεία said pharmakeia drugs, potions, spells: the word our pharmacy comes from

In John's world, drugs and magic went together: potions to get high, to curse, to control. Paul uses the same word in his list of the works of the flesh, where the KJV reads “witchcraft” (Galatians 5:20). This is not the medicine a doctor gives you. It is a man reaching for a substance or a spell to escape, to control, or to feel like a god.

Read the whole list: murder, drugs, sexual sin, stealing. For many of us it reads like our own record. That's honest, and it's not the end of the sentence.

Through all the pain

These men watched a third of the world die. They were tormented for months. And they went straight back to the same things. Pain alone doesn't change a heart.

Every man who has fought an addiction knows this circle. The thing is killing you, you know it's killing you, and you go back anyway. Rock bottom has a basement. That's not a lecture; it's what verses 20 and 21 describe, and some of us have lived it.

But this is the circle for men who refuse to turn. Turning is still possible.

The door is still open

Why trumpets, and only a third at a time? Because God wants men to turn. The men of verse 20 could have, and wouldn't.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Today, name the thing you keep going back to, out loud, to one brother. Then get rid of what feeds it: the number, the stash, the app, the route home. That's turning around on a workday.

Points to remember

  1. Kept“Only those men which have not the seal of God in their foreheads.” 9:4God marks His own, and the darkness is not allowed to touch them.
  2. Despair“In those days shall men seek death, and shall not find it.” 9:6Pain with no way out is real. Jesus came to give life, and no one has to sit in that place alone.
  3. Two kings“In the Greek tongue hath his name Apollyon.” 9:11Their king is the Destroyer. Ours is the One who holds the keys and gives life.
  4. God's clock“Prepared for an hour, and a day, and a month, and a year.” 9:15Nothing is early or late. Evil is bound until God says loose.
  5. Hard hearts“Neither repented they of their murders, nor of their sorceries, nor of their fornication, nor of their thefts.” 9:21Pain alone doesn't change a heart. Men can run back to what is killing them.
  6. The open door“Yet repented not of the works of their hands.” 9:20The warnings are mercy. God wants every man to turn, and today the door is open.

Question 1 of 6

Kept

Who were the locusts not allowed to hurt?

What verses tell us about this?

“Saying, Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads.”

Revelation 7:3

Answer

God's own people, the ones with His seal. The locusts could hurt only those without it. He marks His own, and He keeps them.

Question 2 of 6

Despair

What does verse 6 say about men who want to die, and who offers life?

What verses tell us about this?

“The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.”

John 10:10

Answer

The pain can get so deep that men want to die and can't. Jesus is the opposite of the Destroyer: He came to give life, more than enough. If you're in that dark place, tell someone today.

Question 3 of 6

Two kings

Who is the king of the bottomless pit, and who is stronger?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death.”

Revelation 1:18

Answer

Abaddon, Apollyon: the Destroyer. But the key to his pit was given, and only for a season. Jesus, who died and rose again, holds the keys of hell and of death.

Question 4 of 6

God's clock

What does “prepared for an hour” tell us about who is in control?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God is. The angels were bound until He said “Loose,” and held for an exact hour He had set. Nothing in Revelation is out of His hands, and nothing in your life is either.

Question 5 of 6

Hard hearts

What did the rest of men refuse to repent of?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Idols and the devils behind them; murder, sorceries (drugs and magic), sexual sin and stealing. They kept going back to what was killing them, even through the pain.

Question 6 of 6

The open door

Why does God send warnings, and is the door still open?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

Because He wants men to turn. He held judgment back to “the third part” and gave time. The men of verse 20 refused, but the door is open today to anyone who turns to Jesus.
