Children of the day

Where we are in 1 Thessalonians

About AD 50 to 51, Paul writes from Corinth to a young church he had to leave too soon. Chapter 4 promised that the believers who have died will rise when the Lord comes. The next question is the one people always ask: when?

Part one

Not in darkness

Verses 1–11

A thief, a night, a soldier on watch, and a promise about wrath.

A thief doesn't call ahead

“The day of the Lord” is the Old Testament's name for the day God steps in to judge the world and set it right. When will it come? Paul has already taught them: like a thief.

  • “Someone has worked out the date”“of that day and hour knoweth no man” (Matthew 24:36)
  • “There'll be plenty of warning”a thief doesn't send word
  • “It's so far off it doesn't matter”they'll be saying “Peace and safety”
  • Be ready every daythen the timing can't catch you out

Like labour pains (verse 3): you know they're coming, and when they come, they come suddenly, and there's no stopping them.

Night people and day people

OF THE NIGHT

  • in darkness v. 4
  • overtaken as a thief v. 4
  • asleep v. 6–7
  • drunken v. 7

OF THE DAY

  • children of light v. 5
  • watching v. 6
  • sober v. 6, 8
  • faith, love, hope v. 8

Look at verse 5: “Ye are all the children of light.” Not try to become. You already are. Every believer belongs to the day, even the one who slipped last week. So live like it: “let us not sleep, as do others.”

Sober, both ways.

Paul's word means clear-headed, alert, not fogged. But verse 7 makes it plain he means the bottle too: “they that be drunken are drunken in the night.” Southern Baptists have long urged believers to leave alcohol alone, and for a man in recovery it isn't a close call.

“The night is far spent, the day is at hand: let us therefore cast off the works of darkness, and let us put on the armour of light.”

Romans 13:12

Watch the hours, too. Most of the trouble in our old lives happened after dark. A man of the day knows where he'll be at midnight before the sun goes down. And if you broke your sobriety yesterday, here is what children of the day do: when morning comes, they get up. Tell someone, and start the count again today.

Dressed for the night watch

A sober man on watch is a soldier in his armour. Faith, love and hope: the same three Paul thanked God for in the first chapter (1:3).

The breastplate covers the heart: trusting God, and loving people.

The helmet covers the head, where the hardest attacks land: “You'll never change. What's the point?” Bible hope isn't wishful thinking. It's a sure thing that hasn't happened yet. Put it on before you go out.

Not appointed to wrath

Here's why the hope is sure: God appointed us “to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ, Who died for us.” Christ took the wrath. It won't come to you.

Teachers differ on whether this “wrath” includes the coming tribulation. What's plain: a believer will never face God's wrath for his sins.

And “whether we wake or sleep”, alive or dead when He comes, we live together with Him. So, for the second time: comfort yourselves together (compare 4:18).

Part two

Life together

Verses 12–22

Short commands for a church full of people who are still under construction.

Four kinds of men, four ways to help

First, honour the ones who lead and teach you, “for their work's sake,” and live at peace. Then Paul names four kinds of people in every church. You'll be each of them some days:

THE UNRULYwarnA soldier out of step, the man drifting off and not working (4:11). Love says something.
THE FEEBLEMINDEDcomfortNot slow: fainthearted, worn down, ready to quit. Speak courage.
THE WEAKsupportHold on to him and hold him up. Give him a ride, a call, a hand.
ALL MENbe patientLong-tempered. People change slowly. So did you.

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Don't mix them up. Comforting the unruly or scolding the fainthearted does real harm. Ask: what does this brother need today?

No payback

“See that none render evil for evil unto any man.” On the street, and inside, payback is the law. You don't let it slide, or you're next. That's how a wrong turns into a feud, and a feud into a funeral.

Jesus breaks the circle. It isn't weakness; it takes more strength to walk away. “Ever follow that which is good,” to the brothers and “to all men.” If someone has done you wrong, decide today you won't settle it yourself. Leave it with God, and tell a brother.

The will of God, twice

Twice this letter says, in so many words, “this is the will of God”:

THE WILL OF GOD

“even your sanctification, that ye should abstain from fornication”

1 Thessalonians 4:3
THE WILL OF GOD

“Rejoice evermore. Pray without ceasing. In every thing give thanks”

1 Thessalonians 5:16–18

Notice: “in” every thing, not “for” every thing. You don't have to thank God for the overdose or the arrest. But in the middle of it there is always something to thank Him for, and finding it changes the man who looks. “Without ceasing” doesn't mean on your knees all day. It means never hanging up: a quick word to God at the job site, in the truck, before you answer that text.

A checklist for the road

Verses 12 to 22 are short enough to carry in your pocket:

  1. Honour those who lead you 12–13
  2. Be at peace among yourselves 13
  3. Warn, comfort, support, be patient 14
  4. No payback, ever 15
  5. Rejoice evermore 16
  6. Pray without ceasing 17
  7. In every thing give thanks 18
  8. Quench not the Spirit 19
  9. Despise not prophesyings 20
  10. Prove all things; hold fast that which is good 21
  11. Abstain from all appearance of evil 22

Don't try to do all eleven by noon. Pick one for today, write it on your hand or your phone, and do that one well.

Quench, prove, abstain

“Quench” is what you do to a fire. The Spirit lives in every believer and can't be lost; you are “sealed unto the day of redemption” (Ephesians 4:30). But you can pour water on what He's doing: ignore the nudge, shrug off the conviction, drown it out.

“Despise not prophesyings”: before the New Testament was written down, God gave messages through believers in the church. Today the plain point is the same: don't tune out when God's Word is preached and taught. And “prove all things”: test what you hear against the Bible. Keep what's good. Drop the rest.

“Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world.”

1 John 4:1

“All appearance of evil” means every form evil takes. Whatever shape it shows up in, the old crowd, the old corner, the old app, stay clear of it.

Part three

He will do it

Verses 23–28

The letter ends with a prayer, a promise, and a handshake.

Holy, all the way through

“Sanctify” is the word from 4:3. There it was a command; here it's a prayer, that God will do it “wholly”: spirit, soul and body, nothing left off-limits.

Then the promise: “Faithful is he that calleth you, who also will do it.” Not you alone, white-knuckling it. Him.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

The last lines

“Brethren, pray for us.” The great apostle asks a little church of new believers to pray for him. Nobody is too far along to need it, and nobody is too new to give it.

“An holy kiss” was how family greeted family in that part of the world. Today it's a handshake or a hug. The word that matters is “all”: greet all the brethren, not just your friends, and not just the ones who look like you.

“Read unto all”: this letter is one of the first books of the New Testament ever written, and it was read aloud to the whole church. Now it's been read to you. It ends where every one of Paul's letters ends, with grace.

Looking back over 1 Thessalonians

Five short chapters to a young church in trouble. Read them again, and you'll find every one looks ahead to the same day:

1Turned from idolsto wait for his Son from heaven1:10
2Like a nurse, like a fatherin the presence of our Lord Jesus Christ at his coming2:19
3Timothy's good newsat the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ with all his saints3:13
4Walk, work, and waitthe Lord himself shall descend from heaven4:16
5Children of the dayunto the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ5:23

That hope is what held a persecuted church together: turn from idols, live clean, work hard, love each other, comfort the grieving, and wait for Him. Waiting, for them, was never sitting still.

If you fell yesterday, you're who this letter was written to. A young church, new believers, still learning. “Faithful is he that calleth you, who also will do it.” Get up, and live today as a child of the day.

Points to remember

  1. The day“the day of the Lord so cometh as a thief in the night” 5:2No one knows the date. Be ready every day, and the timing can't catch you out.
  2. Light“Ye are all the children of light … let us watch and be sober” 5:5–6You already belong to the day. So stay awake, and stay sober, both ways.
  3. Hope“for an helmet, the hope of salvation. For God hath not appointed us to wrath” 5:8–9Put on hope before you go out. Christ took the wrath; it won't come to you.
  4. Together“warn them that are unruly, comfort the feebleminded, support the weak, be patient toward all men” 5:14Give each brother what he needs today. And no payback, ever.
  5. Every day“Rejoice evermore. Pray without ceasing. In every thing give thanks” 5:16–18God's will isn't hidden. Joy, prayer that never hangs up, and thanks in every thing.
  6. Faithful“Faithful is he that calleth you, who also will do it” 5:24God started the work in you, and He will finish it.

Question 1 of 6

The day

How will the day of the Lord come, and who will it catch off guard?

What verses tell us about this?

“Watch therefore: for ye know not what hour your Lord doth come.”

Matthew 24:42

Answer

Like a thief in the night: suddenly, with no date given. It will catch out those saying "Peace and safety," who live in the dark. Believers aren't in darkness, so it needn't overtake us. Be ready every day.

Question 2 of 6

Light

What does Paul call believers, and how should children of the day live?

What verses tell us about this?

“The night is far spent, the day is at hand: let us therefore cast off the works of darkness, and let us put on the armour of light.”

Romans 13:12

Answer

Children of light and of the day, every one of us. So we don't sleep through life as others do: we watch, and we stay sober, clear-headed and off the bottle.

Question 3 of 6

Hope

What is our helmet, and why can we be sure of it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him.”

Romans 5:9

Answer

The hope of salvation. It's sure because God has not appointed us to wrath, but to salvation by Jesus, who died for us. Alive or dead when He comes, we will live together with Him.

Question 4 of 6

Together

How should we treat the unruly, the feebleminded, the weak, and everyone else?

What verses tell us about this?

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Answer

Warn the unruly, comfort the fainthearted, hold up the weak, and be patient with everyone. Never pay back evil for evil; keep doing good, to brothers and to all men.

Question 5 of 6

Every day

What does Paul say is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.”

Philippians 4:6

Answer

Rejoice evermore, pray without ceasing, and in every thing give thanks. Not "for" every thing, but "in" it: there's always something to thank Him for. With 4:3 (our sanctification), it's God's will said out loud.

Question 6 of 6

Faithful

Who will finish making us holy, and how can we be sure?

What verses tell us about this?

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Answer

The very God of peace Himself, in spirit, soul and body. We can be sure because He is faithful: the One who called us "also will do it."
