1 Corinthians 15

Raised, and so shall we be

Jesus really rose. So death is not the end, the past is not the last word, and nothing you do for Him is wasted.

Where we are in the letter

Paul has answered one problem after another. Now he comes to the biggest. Some in the Corinth church were saying there is no resurrection of the dead. If that is true, nothing else in this letter matters. So Paul builds the whole chapter on one fact. (This letter was written about AD 55.)

Part one

The good news, in four lines

Verses 1–11

Paul says what the gospel is, names the men who saw Him alive, and then names himself.

Preached, received, standing on it

“Gospel” means good news. Paul says the Corinthians heard it, took hold of it, and are standing on it. Then comes a hard-sounding phrase: “unless ye have believed in vain.”

Believed “in vain”

said the words, never trusted Him

An empty belief: agreeing with a story without ever putting your weight on Jesus.

Believed

leaning on Him, even wobbly

You may be shaky, and you may have fallen. If you are resting on Jesus' death and rising for you, you are standing on it.

Paul isn't threatening a man who stumbled. He's asking a church: is this real to you? A man who fell yesterday and is sick about it is exactly who this good news is for.

The gospel in four lines

  1. 1Christ died for our sinsNot only died: died for ours. The bill we could never pay, including yesterday's.as the Scriptures said
  2. 2He was buriedProof He was really dead. Nobody buries a man who is only hurt.
  3. 3He rose again the third dayFriday, Saturday, Sunday morning. The tomb was empty.as the Scriptures said
  4. 4He was seenBy named people, in daylight, more than once. That's the next four verses.

Paul didn't invent this. “That which I also received.” It was the message every believer was given from the start. And line one is the one we need most: He died for our sins.

The witnesses, in order

  1. PeterCephas, who had denied Him
  2. The twelvethe men who ran
  3. 500+“at once”
  4. JamesHis own brother, once a doubter
  5. All the apostlesevery man He sent
  6. Paullast of all

Look who is on the list. Peter, who swore he never knew Him. The twelve, who ran. James, who didn't believe in Him while He lived (John 7:5). Jesus didn't wait for them to clean up. He went to the men who had failed Him.

That is what He does with men who have failed Him still.

“Above five hundred brethren at once”

seen by all of them togetherand more

You can fool one man, or twelve, in a dark room. You can't fool five hundred in the open. And Paul adds: “the greater part remain unto this present.” Most were still alive when he wrote, about AD 55. Anyone in Corinth who doubted could have gone and asked them.

“Fallen asleep” is how the Bible talks about believers who have died. It sounds like rest, because for those in Christ it is.

Paul does not hide his past

  1. last of allseen by the risen Lord
  2. born out of due timelate, like a child born after the family had grown
  3. the leastof the apostles
  4. not meetto be called one: I persecuted the church

Paul had dragged believers off to prison, and he approved of Stephen's death (Acts 7–9). He says it out loud, in a letter read to a whole church, about twenty years later. He doesn't pretend it away.

And he doesn't live there either.

“I am what I am”

the man who persecuted → by the grace of God “His grace which was bestowed upon me was not in vain.” Then he worked harder than any of them, and says: “yet not I, but the grace of God which was with me.”

He keeps both truths. I did that, and grace made me this. Without the first you are pretending. Without the second you are stuck. Say what you did, plainly, to God:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Then get up and work, like Paul. Regret is meant to turn you back to Him. It was never meant to be where you live.

Part two

If Christ is not raised

Verses 12–34

Paul follows the idea of “no resurrection” all the way down, to see where it ends.

Follow it down

  1. no resurrectionof the dead
  2. Christ not raisedso He is still dead
  3. preaching vainand the preachers liars
  4. faith vaintrusting a corpse
  5. still in your sinsnothing paid
  6. most miserablethe dead in Christ lost

Paul is not doubting. He is showing what is at stake. If the tomb is not empty, forgiveness is a story and there is no hope. Every promise in this book stands on this one fact.

Notice the fifth step: “ye are yet in your sins.” Your forgiveness doesn't rest on how a service felt or how the last few days went. It rests on an empty tomb. That is why it can't be taken from you.

Firstfruits

At harvest, a farmer cut the very first sheaf and brought it to God. It was a promise: the rest is coming. Israel did this every spring, in Passover week (Leviticus 23). Paul says Jesus is that first sheaf.

He didn't rise alone. He rose first, and the harvest follows: all who belong to Him. Believers who have died aren't lost. They are the part of the field that hasn't ripened yet.

Two families

In Adam

everyone dies

The first man sinned, and death came to the whole family. Everyone is born into it. There is nothing you did to earn it, and nothing you can do to leave it.

In Christ

everyone in Him lives

Not everyone, automatically: “they that are Christ's” (verse 23). But it is offered to whosoever. You change families by trusting Him, and you can do that today.

“Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:”

Romans 5:12

Every enemy, one by one

All rule, all authority, all powereverything that sets itself against Godunder His feet

The last enemy: deaththe one nobody has ever beatendestroyed

Paul gives no dates and draws no chart, and we won't either. Jesus said no man knows the day (Matthew 24:36). What is plain: Christ reigns now, and the story ends with death beaten and “God all in all.”

“But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only.”

Matthew 24:36

When the Son is “subject” to the Father (verse 28), He isn't getting smaller. A Son hands a finished job to His Father.

A hard verse: “baptized for the dead”

What nobody can say for sure

what that practice was

Good Bible teachers have offered many explanations over the centuries. Paul says “they,” not “we.” He never says whether it was right, and the Bible never mentions it again.

What is plain

they believed the dead rise

Whatever they were doing, it only makes sense if the dead do rise. That's Paul's point. Baptism is a believer's own step of obedience, picturing his own salvation. It doesn't save, and nobody is baptized in another's place.

When a verse is unclear, hold to the clear ones around it. Here the clear message is verse 20: “now is Christ risen from the dead.”

“I die daily”

Paul is writing from Ephesus (16:8), and it was a dangerous place for him. He says he “fought with beasts” there. Most Bible teachers take that as a picture of savage opposition, like the riot in the theatre (Acts 19), though some think he meant it more literally.

Either way: why would a man put himself in danger every day for a Saviour who stayed dead? Paul's life only makes sense if Jesus is alive.

Two ways to live

If death is the end

“let us eat and drink; for to morrow we die”

A famous saying of the world, and it's in the prophets too (Isaiah 22:13). It is the voice in the head of every last binge: nothing matters, so why not?

Because He is risen

it all counts

What you do with this day matters, because there is a tomorrow, and a Judge who is also a Saviour. And the next verses say what to do with those who drag you back.

“And behold joy and gladness, slaying oxen, and killing sheep, eating flesh, and drinking wine: let us eat and drink; for to morrow we shall die.”

Isaiah 22:13

Be not deceived

The old crowdold friends, old places, the old phone number
The new crowdbrothers who know your story, a church, a group like this one

“Evil communications” means bad company: the talk and time you share. “Manners” means habits, what you're really like. Paul quotes a proverb his readers knew. You become who you spend your time with. Nobody drifts that way on purpose.

So “awake to righteousness, and sin not.” That's getting up. You fell, so you get up, and you walk toward the men who will pull you the other way.

Do one thing today. Pick one name in your phone, or one place you pass on the way home, that drags you back. Decide today what you'll do about it.

Part three

How are the dead raised?

Verses 35–49

Someone will ask: with what kind of body? Paul answers with a seed, the sky, and two men.

“Thou fool”: look at a seed

Paul isn't being unkind. He's saying: you see this every spring. A bare grain goes into the ground. The grain itself is gone. Up comes something that looks nothing like it.

“Quickened” just means made alive. A seed must “die” first, and the farmer doesn't call that the end.

God gave it a body “as it hath pleased him.” Nobody can see the plant in the seed. If He does that to wheat every year, a risen man is not hard for Him.

A body fitted for its place

Fish, birds and animals each have the body that suits where they live. The sun, moon and stars each shine their own way. God doesn't make one kind of thing.

So a body made for heaven can be quite different from the one we bury, and no less real. We aren't told much about it, and we don't have to know. Enough to know the One who makes it.

Sown, and raised

sown (buried)raised
corruption→incorruption
dishonour→glory
weakness→power
a natural body→a spiritual body

A body put in the ground is “sown” like a seed. Every word on the left is true of the body you have now: it breaks, it's worn out, and many of us know what we've done to ours. None of that is the last word. A “spiritual” body isn't a ghost. It's a body fully led by the Spirit.

“Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.”

Luke 24:39

Two men, two images

The first man, Adam

“of the earth, earthy”

Made from dust, and to dust he returned. We were born carrying his likeness: weak, mortal, and bent toward sin.

The second man, Christ

“the Lord from heaven”

He rose, and He is what we shall be. The same Jesus who ate fish by the lake, with a body that will never die again.

“As we have borne the image of the earthy, we shall also bear the image of the heavenly.” The first is done. The second is promised.

“Who shall change our vile body, that it may be fashioned like unto his glorious body, according to the working whereby he is able even to subdue all things unto himself.”

Philippians 3:21

Part four

Changed

Verses 50–58

A secret, a sound, a shout, and then Paul sends everyone back to work.

A mystery, and a blink

In the Bible a “mystery” is a secret God now makes known, not a puzzle. The secret: flesh and blood can't live in God's kingdom as it is. So everyone who belongs to Christ is changed: those who have died, and those still alive.

Bible teachers differ on the order of the end times, and we won't argue it here. What is plain: it is sudden, and it reaches every believer.

Two groups, one change

those who have died: “raised incorruptible”those still alive: “we shall be changed”

“But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.”

1 Thessalonians 4:13–14

Paul doesn't say don't grieve. He says grieve differently, “not as others which have no hope.” If you've buried someone, mother, brother or friend, who died knowing Jesus, you will see them again.

Put on, swallowed up

“Put on” is clothing. What wears out and dies is exchanged for what can't: incorruption for corruption, immortality for what is mortal. That is the old promise of Isaiah (Isaiah 25:8), and Paul sets it next to Hosea, where God Himself speaks to death:

“I will ransom them from the power of the grave; I will redeem them from death: O death, I will be thy plagues; O grave, I will be thy destruction: repentance shall be hid from mine eyes.”

Hosea 13:14

The sting of death

  1. Death stings
    It frightens because there's something behind it.
  2. The sting is sin
    The guilt: what we did, and what's waiting because of it.
  3. Sin gets its strength from the law
    The law shows us what's wrong, and can't make us right. It leaves us guilty.
  4. But thanks be to God
    He “giveth us the victory” through Jesus. Our sins went on Him. The sting went into Him, and He rose.

A snake with no fangs can still hiss. Death can still frighten us, and grieve us. But it can't do what it was made to do. “The wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life.” (Romans 6:23)

If you fell yesterday, the sting is not on you. It's on Him. Confess it, take the victory, and keep walking.

“Therefore”: so go to work

The whole chapter turns on one word. Because He is risen, and because you will be, here is what to do on an ordinary Tuesday.

  1. ✓stedfastDon't be talked off the facts. Stay planted on the four lines of the gospel.
  2. ✓unmoveableNot dragged back by the old crowd (verse 33). KJV spells it “unmoveable”: it won't be shifted.
  3. ✓always abounding in the work of the LordMore than the minimum. Show up on time, do the job well, and help the man next to you.
  4. ✓your labour is not in vainNothing done for Him is wasted. Not the shift, not the phone call to a brother, not the day you got up again.

Points to remember

  1. The gospel“Christ died for our sins… he was buried… he rose again the third day” 15:3–4Died for our sins, buried, raised the third day, and seen. That's all of it, and it's enough.
  2. A man with a past“By the grace of God I am what I am” 15:10Paul named what he'd done, and wouldn't live there. His past is real, and grace is more real.
  3. The empty tomb“If Christ be not raised, your faith is vain; ye are yet in your sins” 15:17Your forgiveness stands on an empty tomb, not on how the last few days went.
  4. Bad company“Evil communications corrupt good manners” 15:33You become who you spend time with. Get up, and walk toward better men.
  5. The new body“It is sown in corruption; it is raised in incorruption” 15:42A seed dies and a plant rises. God can do the same with a body, and He will.
  6. Victory“Thanks be to God, which giveth us the victory… your labour is not in vain” 15:57–58Death has lost its sting. Meanwhile, stand firm and work hard: none of it is wasted.

Question 1 of 6

The gospel

What four things does Paul say the gospel is?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Christ died for our sins, was buried, rose again the third day, and was seen by many. All according to the Scriptures. He died for our sins and rose to give us life.

Question 2 of 6

A man with a past

How did Paul describe himself, and what made him what he was?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

The least of the apostles, who persecuted the church. What made him what he was: the grace of God. He admitted the past, and he didn't live in it.

Question 3 of 6

The empty tomb

Why does everything depend on Christ being raised?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:”

John 11:25

Answer

If Christ is not raised, preaching is empty, faith is empty, and we are still in our sins. But He is raised, the first of a harvest, so our forgiveness and our hope are certain.

Question 4 of 6

Bad company

What does Paul say about the company we keep?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Bad company ruins good habits: you become like the people you spend your time with. So wake up, get out of it, and keep company that pulls you the right way.

Question 5 of 6

The new body

How can a dead body be raised?

What verses tell us about this?

“Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.”

Luke 24:39

Answer

The way a seed dies and a plant comes up. God gives it a body as it pleases Him: a real body, no longer weak or dying, fitted for the life to come.

Question 6 of 6

Victory

What is the answer to death, and what do we do in the meantime?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Jesus took the sting of death, which is sin, and gives us the victory. Meanwhile we stand firm, never moved, always working for Him, knowing our labour is not in vain.
