James 1

Faith that does something

A letter from the Lord's own brother, the Proverbs of the New Testament. It is about what believing looks like on a Tuesday, in a hard week, when the temptation comes: “be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only.”

The letter of James

“James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes which are scattered abroad, greeting.”

Written from
Jerusalem, where James led the church (Acts 15)
When
about AD 45 to 50, perhaps the earliest letter in the New Testament
To whom
Jewish believers scattered by persecution, “throughout the regions of Judaea and Samaria” and on to Phenice, Cyprus and Antioch (Acts 8:1; 11:19)
Why
to show hurt, scattered people what real faith looks like in everyday life: in trouble, in temptation, with money, with their mouths

“Now they which were scattered abroad upon the persecution that arose about Stephen travelled as far as Phenice, and Cyprus, and Antioch, preaching the word to none but unto the Jews only.”

Acts 11:19

The skeptic in the family

This James is the brother of Jesus. He grew up in the same house, ate at the same table, and for years did not believe a word of it.

John 7:5Not a believer

“For neither did his brethren believe in him.” Thirty years of watching Jesus up close, and still no.

1 Corinthians 15:7He saw the risen Lord

“After that, he was seen of James; then of all the apostles.” Something happened in that meeting that changed everything.

Acts 15:13A leader at Jerusalem

At the great council it is James who sums up: “my sentence is…” Paul calls him one of the pillars (Galatians 1:19; 2:9).

Now look at how he introduces himself: “a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ.” Not “the Lord's brother.” He had the best family connection in the whole church, and he calls himself a servant. A man who was once the family doubter knows what it is to be wrong about Jesus, and what it is to be forgiven for it.

If somebody in your family has written you off, or you wrote them off, remember James. The brother who did not believe became the man who wrote this letter. Early writers say that he died for Jesus at Jerusalem, about AD 62.

If James is early, his readers were very new believers, many of them thrown out of their homes and jobs, and a few years from the cross. No New Testament yet. Just the Old Testament, a handful of stories about Jesus, and each other.

So he does not write theory. He writes the way a wise old man talks to younger men in trouble: short, plain, and a little blunt. That is why people call James the Proverbs of the New Testament.

James has no long argument. He gives five short sermons on one question: what does real faith look like at work, at home, in a temptation and in a bad week? Chapter 1 sets out all of it in seed form.

Part one

Count it all joy

Verses 1–12

James starts with the hard stuff: trouble, and what it is for. And a promise about what to do when you do not know what to do.

Why would anyone count it joy?

James does not say feel happy. “Count” is a bookkeeping word: work it out. Hard times are not wasted. The fire does not make the gold; it burns off what was never gold.

“That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ:”

1 Peter 1:7

“Patience” is staying power under pressure, and you get it by getting through today. God's aim is “perfect and entire”: whole, grown up, nothing missing.

Asking without being told off

YouGod, I did it again. I do not know what to do, and I do not know how to start.

God, giving to all men liberallyHere is wisdom. Take it.
and not one word of scolding

“Upbraideth not.” It means He does not throw it back in your face. A lot of us are slow to ask God for help because we expect the lecture a person would give. James says it plainly: God gives liberally, with an open hand, and with no lecture attached.

Wisdom is not being clever. It is knowing what to do next, in the real situation, today. Ask for it in plain words, and then watch for it: a verse, a friend's advice, a door that closes.

Two minds

δίψυχος said dipsychos di = two  +  psychē = soul. Two-souled.

The man James has in mind asks God for help, and all the while has one foot out the door. “Like a wave of the sea driven with the wind and tossed.” Up one minute, down the next, because he has not decided who is steering.

This is not a man with doubts. Every believer has doubts, and Jesus took doubting men in His hands. It is a man who asks for help while holding back the very thing he is asking about. Today: is there one thing you ask God to fix while still keeping a back door open? Name it to Him, and shut the door.

Money fades. A crown does not.

Two men in the church: one poor, one rich. The poor man can be glad: in Christ he is “exalted.” The rich man must remember he is the flower, and the heat is coming for every flower.

Then the promise, for the man who stays on his feet in a hard week: “Blessed is the man that endureth temptation: for when he is tried, he shall receive the crown of life, which the Lord hath promised to them that love him.” Notice: when he is tried, not if.

Part two

Where temptation comes from

Verses 13–18

The same word means a test and a temptation. James now draws the line between God's tests and our own wants, and shows how one becomes the other.

One word, two directions

πειρασμός said peirasmos a test, a trial, or a temptation. Only the situation tells you which.

From God (verses 2–4, 12)

A test

The hard thing that shows what is real and builds staying power. God allows it, and He is with you in it. “Count it all joy.”

From my own wants (verses 13–15)

A temptation

The pull toward sin. “God cannot be tempted with evil, neither tempteth he any man.” He never baits the hook.

The same Tuesday can hold both. The loss of a job is a test. What you do with the free evening is where the temptation starts. God stands behind the first, never the second.

Whose fault is it, then?

  • “God put this in front of me”He does not tempt anyone. Hard situations come, but the pull to sin is not His doing.
  • “It came out of nowhere”It rarely does. James says it grows from something already in us: “his own lust.” It has a start, and a start can be found.
  • “That's just who I am”It is what you are drawn to, not who you have to stay. The next verse shows how fast it grows, and how early it can be stopped.
  • “It started in me, so I can deal with it in me, with God's help”That is honest, and it is hopeful. If it begins in the wanting, it can be cut in the wanting.

None of this is meant to make you feel low. A man who knows where it starts is a man who can stop it earlier next time.

The anatomy of a relapse

OneDesiredrawn away of his own lust

A want, a craving, a thought that will not go. Not yet a sin.

TwoBaitand enticed

A fishing word. The hook hides inside something that looks good. I start looking at the bait.

ThreeConceptionwhen lust hath conceived

I say yes in my head. I make a plan. I tell myself no one will know.

FourSinit bringeth forth sin

It is born. I do the thing I swore I would not.

FiveDeathwhen it is finished, bringeth forth death

Full grown, it kills: marriages, jobs, trust, a man's life.

James picks a picture of pregnancy: it grows in secret, then it is born, then it grows up. The scary part is not the first link. It is how ordinary the first link looks. Every man here has been at link one on a normal Tuesday.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Every link has a door out, and the cheapest door is the first. Pick yours today: the person you will call, the place you will not go, the thing you will delete.

And if you are already at link four

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

Regret is what you do with the past. It is allowed, and it can turn you around. But regret is not supposed to be a place to live. Confess it, tell one man who can help carry it, and start again with the day in front of you. James is not warning you that you are finished. He is showing you where to cut next time.

One more thing. A believer is held by Christ (John 10:28), and no link in this chain can pull him out of those hands. But sin can still cost him his peace, his family and his health. That is why we cut it early.

He does not change

The sun moves across the sky, the moon waxes and wanes, and shadows turn through the day. “The Father of lights” made all of them. “With whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.”

So “every good gift and every perfect gift” comes from Him, and temptation never does. He is the same this morning as He was before the worst thing you ever did.

“Of his own will begat he us with the word of truth.” That is the new birth, and it was His idea, His will. You did not talk Him into it, and you cannot talk Him out of it.

Part three

Hearers and doers

Verses 19–27

Now the practical part: how to listen, how to read the Bible, and what the real thing looks like.

Fast, slow, slow

Swift to hear
Slow to speak
Slow to wrath

Anger feels like strength. James says it never gets you the life God wants. Most of us have burned something down in a rage that we could not rebuild. The fix is not to feel nothing. It is the gap: hear the whole thing first, wait before you answer, and cool down before you act.

Verse 21 is about clothes. “Lay apart” is how you take off dirty clothes: filth and leftover wickedness go on the floor. Then you “receive” the word like something planted in you, and it grows. The same Word that began the new life keeps it going.

The man in the mirror

A mirror tells you the truth about your face: the smudge, the mess. A man glances in it, walks away and “straightway forgetteth what manner of man he was.” The mirror did nothing wrong. He did nothing about it.

That is how a man hears a sermon: that was good, and off to his truck. Nothing changed because nothing was done: “deceiving your own selves.”

The cure is to do the one thing you saw: make the call, say sorry, skip the bar, pay the bill. God's word is “the perfect law of liberty”: it frees the man who keeps looking and acts. Today: what have you heard and not yet done?

What the real thing looks like

  1. A bridled tongueHe keeps a rein on what he says (verse 26). It comes back in chapter 3.
  2. Visit the fatherless and the widowsLook after people who cannot pay you back, in their “affliction.”
  3. Keep unspotted from the worldStay clean from the way the world does things.

“Religion” here means the outward practice of faith. James is not knocking church. He is asking what it looks like when it is real, and his answer is simple, and it costs something: care for the people nobody else will, and keep yourself clean.

The fatherless and the widows were the people with nobody: no income, no one to speak for them. Many of the men in this room know what it is to be that person, and God calls looking after them pure religion, “undefiled before God and the Father.” And He does not ask perfect men to do it. He has always used men with a past.

Today: one concrete visit. A man in a bad place, a widow in your street, a kid with no dad who needs a lift. And when you are tempted to talk someone down, keep it behind your teeth.

Points to remember

  1. Trials“Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience.” 1:3Hard times are the fire that burns off what is not real, and builds staying power in what is.
  2. Wisdom“If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not” 1:5Ask. He gives freely, with no lecture, and no one is too much of a mess to ask.
  3. Temptation“Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.” 1:15It starts in me, so I can cut it early: desire, bait, conception, sin, death. The first door out is the cheapest.
  4. The Father“Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights” 1:17Every good thing comes from Him, and He does not change. He is the same today as before your worst day.
  5. Doing“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.” 1:22Do the one thing you saw in the mirror. Hearing alone changes nothing.
  6. Real religion“Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world.” 1:27Real faith looks after the people nobody else will, and keeps itself clean.

Proverbs 2:6

Ask God, in faith, and not with a back door open. He gives liberally, to all, and He does not throw it back at you.

Question 3 of 6

Temptation

Where does temptation come from, and where does it lead?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Not from God. It starts in my own wanting, grows from desire to bait to sin, and ends in death. Break it at the first link, and if you have fallen, confess it and start again.

Question 4 of 6

The Father

What kind of gifts does the Father of lights give?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Every good gift and every perfect gift, from above. He does not change, and He chose to give us new life through His word of truth.

Question 5 of 6

Doing

What does James say we must be, and not hearers only?

What verses tell us about this?

“And why call ye me, Lord, Lord, and do not the things which I say?”

Luke 6:46

Answer

Doers of the word. A man who only hears is like a man who looks in a mirror and forgets his own face. The man who keeps looking and does it is blessed in what he does.
