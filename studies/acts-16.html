Acts 16

Midnight in Philippi

The second journey begins. Doors close, a call comes from Macedonia, and in a Roman prison at midnight a jailor asks the question every man eventually asks.

Part one

Closed doors, then a call

Verses 1–10

A young man joins the team, and God says no, and no, and then yes.

Timothy: a faith passed down

Lois, his grandmotherpassed on her faith toEunice, his motherwho raisedTimothywho joinedPaul

“When I call to remembrance the unfeigned faith that is in thee, which dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois, and thy mother Eunice; and I am persuaded that in thee also.”

2 Timothy 1:5

His mother was a Jewish believer. His father was a Greek, and he isn't named. A man doesn't need a perfect home to be useful to God. And if you're a father, you are a link in a chain like this one.

Timothy, but not Titus

Titus

Pressed to be circumcised

Some insisted it was required. Paul said no: “neither Titus… was compelled to be circumcised” (Galatians 2:3).

Timothy

Chose it, to reach people

Nobody said it saved him. Paul did it so that Jews who knew Timothy's Greek father would hear him.

This is what Acts 15 settled: circumcision can't save anyone, so it's never required. But a man who is free can also give up a right out of love. Verse 4 says they carried the council's letter to the churches as they went.

No, and no again

They set out from Antioch with Silas, and Timothy joined them at Lystra.

Then two closed doors. The Holy Ghost forbade them to preach in Asia, and when they tried for Bithynia, “the Spirit suffered them not.” We aren't told how.

So they walked on northwest to Troas, by the sea. God steers a man who is already moving, and a closed door is sometimes God being kind.

The gospel steps onto a new continent

A man of Macedonia begged for help in Paul's dream, and they sailed at once: Troas, in Asia, to Philippi, in Europe.

Look at the little word “we.” From verse 10 the writer, Luke the physician, is in the boat, telling it as an eyewitness.

Part two

Philippi

Verses 11–24

A businesswoman, a slave girl, and a beating they didn't deserve.

Lydia: the Lord opened her heart

Lydia sold “purple,” costly dyed cloth, and had come from Thyatira. She already worshipped God and came to the riverside to pray.

“The Lord opened” her heart, and she “attended”: she listened and believed. Then she was baptized, a step that pictures her faith but doesn't save her.

No earthquake, no fear. If your story is a quiet one, it is the same grace.

A girl somebody else owned

The girl, for many daysThese men are the servants of the most high God, which shew unto us the way of salvation.

Paul, grievedI command thee in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her.
not a thank-you

She was a slave, and her owners made money from her bondage. What she shouted was true, but from the wrong source. Paul was grieved, not angry. He saw a person being used.

Some of us know being used by someone who profits while we stay stuck. In Jesus' name she was free “the same hour.” Nothing that owns you is stronger than He is.

All for doing the right thing

  1. draggedinto the marketplace (19)
  2. accusedbefore the magistrates (20)
  3. the crowdrose up against them (22)
  4. strippedclothes torn off (22)
  5. beatenmany stripes (23)

Paul and Silas had freed a girl and preached the gospel. For that they were beaten with rods, with no trial, and chained in the darkest cell with their feet locked wide apart in the stocks.

Sometimes doing right costs you. The job, the friends, the old crowd. They weren't being punished by God. They were exactly where He sent them, and it was dark.

Part three

Midnight

Verses 25–34

Chains, a song, an earthquake, and a question.

A song in the inner prison

Their backs were cut open, their feet were locked in wood, and it was midnight. And they prayed, and sang praises unto God. Loud enough that “the prisoners heard them.”

Then “suddenly there was a great earthquake.” Every door opened and every man's chains came loose.

Somebody is always listening to how you handle your night: your kids, your coworkers, the guy two cells down.

They sang before the shaking.

“I waited patiently for the LORD; and he inclined unto me, and heard my cry. He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings. And he hath put a new song in my mouth, even praise unto our God: many shall see it, and fear, and shall trust in the LORD.”

Psalm 40:1–3

It isn't a magic trick: sing and the doors open. Paul spent years in prison cells and some doors stayed shut. The point is where their eyes were. Praying and singing in the dark, they were trusting a God who was bigger than the stocks.

Years later Paul wrote this very church from another cell: “Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, Rejoice” (Philippians 4:4). Start with one honest prayer today.

The jailor's night

  1. EveningThe jailor locks them in the inner prison (23–24)
  2. MidnightPrayer and praise (25)
  3. The quakeDoors open, chains loose (26)
  4. The swordHe thinks they've escaped, and draws it (27)
  5. “Do thyself no harm”Paul cries out (28)
  6. DaybreakThe magistrates send word (35)

He believed the prisoners had fled, and Roman guards who lost prisoners were put to death (see Acts 12:19). In one second he saw his whole life as over, and he reached for the sword.

Paul had every reason to let him. Instead: “Do thyself no harm: for we are all here.” A man in the dark can be wrong about how it ends. If you ever stand where he stood, tell someone today. In the US you can call or text 988.

The question every man asks

The jailor, tremblingSirs, what must I do to be saved?

Paul and SilasBelieve on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house.

Note his word, “Sirs,” or “Lords.” A moment ago he had them in the stocks. Now he's on his knees. He asks what he must do, and the answer isn't a task. It's a Person.

He had no clean record to bring, nothing to offer, a sword in his hand a minute ago. The answer was the same as for Lydia, and for you.

Ifyou believe on the Lord Jesus Christ,
→
thenthou shalt be saved.

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Whosoever. The jailor, the slave girl, you. Trust Jesus Christ as your Lord and Saviour, and you are saved: not “maybe,” not “for now.” Thou shalt be saved is a promise from the One who can keep it.

And “thy house.” It doesn't mean his faith saves his family. Verse 32 says they all heard the word, and verse 34 says they all believed. But a believing man can be the doorway for his whole household.

Two nights, one grace

Lydia

  • Where: a riverbank
  • When: a Sabbath morning
  • How: quietly, “the Lord opened” her heart
  • Then: baptized, and opened her home

The jailor

  • Where: a prison
  • When: midnight
  • How: earthquake, a sword, terror
  • Then: baptized “the same hour,” and set a table

Same Lord. Same grace. Same way: believe.

Look at the first thing the jailor did: he washed their stripes, tending the wounds of men he had chained. Then they baptized him, a believer's step that pictures what had already happened. And he “rejoiced.”

Part four

Morning

Verses 35–40

The magistrates want it all quietly forgotten. Paul says no.

“Being Romans”

Paul“let them come themselves”the serjeantstoldthe magistrateswho feared, and came and besought them

Roman law didn't allow a citizen to be beaten without a trial. Paul and Silas were Roman citizens, and the magistrates had broken the law in front of everyone. Now they were the frightened ones.

Scripture doesn't say why Paul waited until now. It very likely protected the young church left behind. He didn't take revenge, though. He made them come and walk the two men out themselves, and he left a town that knew they'd been wronged.

The church at Philippi begins

They went back to Lydia's house, “comforted” the believers, and left. Philippi now had a church.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Paul later wrote that to this church. Next stop: Thessalonica. God finishes what He starts, in you too.

Points to remember

  1. Guidance“The Spirit suffered them not” 16:7A closed door isn't God gone. He steers men who keep walking, and He often says yes after no.
  2. Salvation“Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved” 16:31Not something to do. Someone to trust. It's for whosoever, and it's a promise.
  3. A new heart“Whose heart the Lord opened” 16:14God opens the heart. You listen and believe. Quiet stories count just as much.
  4. Trouble“Paul and Silas prayed, and sang praises unto God” 16:25Pray and praise in your dark hour. People are listening, and God is bigger than the stocks.
  5. Despair“Do thyself no harm: for we are all here” 16:28When it feels like the end, you are wrong about the ending. Tell someone today.
  6. A new life“Rejoiced, believing in God with all his house” 16:34One night turned a hard man into a washed, baptized, rejoicing believer with a table full of family.

Question 1 of 6

Guidance

How did God steer Paul when his plans were blocked?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He closed two doors, Asia and Bithynia, and then opened the right one with a vision of a man of Macedonia. Paul didn't sit still. He kept moving, and God steered him where He wanted him. The closed doors were part of the guidance.

Question 2 of 6

Salvation

What must a man do to be saved?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ. That's it. The jailor asked what he must do, and the answer was to trust a Person, not to perform a task. It is for whosoever, and it comes with a promise: “thou shalt be saved.”

Question 3 of 6

A new heart

Who opened Lydia's heart?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The Lord did. She was already worshipping and praying, and when Paul spoke, the Lord opened her heart so that she attended to the message, believed, and was baptized. It was a quiet conversion, and just as real as the jailor's.

Question 4 of 6

Trouble

What did Paul and Silas do at midnight?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They prayed and sang praises to God, beaten, bleeding and in the stocks, while the other prisoners listened. God shook the prison. The point isn't a formula. It's that they trusted God in the dark, and everyone around them saw it.

Question 5 of 6

Despair

What did Paul say to the jailor who thought it was all over?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Do thyself no harm: for we are all here.” The jailor thought his life was finished, but he was wrong about the ending. Paul stopped him with a loud voice, and that same night he was saved. If you are in that dark place, tell someone today.

Question 6 of 6

A new life

What happened to the jailor and his house?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They all heard the word, and they all believed. The jailor washed the wounds of the men he'd chained, was baptized that same hour, set a meal before them, and rejoiced with his whole house. One night changed a man and his entire family.
