Genesis 16

The God who sees

Abram and Sarai get tired of waiting and make a plan of their own. It goes badly for everyone. Then a runaway slave woman meets God in the desert, and gives him a name nobody had used before.

Sarai's words have two halves. “The LORD hath restrained me from bearing” is true. “Go in unto my maid” is where she goes wrong. She was right about God and wrong about what to do about it.

To be fair to her, God had said the heir would come from Abram's “own bowels” (15:4), but he had not yet said the child would be Sarai's. Her plan did not flatly contradict his words. It just wasn't his way, and she never asked him.

The custom, and the plan

The custom of the day

Give your maid to your husband

A childless wife could give her servant to her husband, and count the baby as her own. It was accepted where Abram came from. It made good sense.

God's plan from the beginning

One man, one wife

“They shall be one flesh.” The promise was going to come through Sarai, in God's time.

“Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.”

Genesis 2:24

Name it plainly: Abram took a second woman, because the world around him said that was how it was done. The Bible does not say God approved. The rest of the chapter shows what it cost. A man can do what everyone around him is doing, and still be wrong.

“Abram hearkened to the voice of Sarai”

“And unto Adam he said, Because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of thy wife, and hast eaten of the tree, of which I commanded thee, saying, Thou shalt not eat of it: cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of thy life;”

Genesis 3:17

Those are the same words God used against Adam: hearkened unto the voice of thy wife. In both stories a man was meant to listen to God first, and the voice nearest him won. Abram doesn't argue and doesn't pray. The Bible does not tell us he asked God anything at all.

This isn't about blaming wives. It is about a man who let someone else make the decision he was supposed to take to God. Any man can do that: with a friend, a boss, an old crowd, a craving.

Verse 3 is the first time Hagar is called Abram's “wife”. It means a lesser wife, with a wife's duties and none of her standing. And the moment Hagar was expecting, she looked down on the woman who had owned her. Pride in a person who has just been used is not hard to understand. It is still sin, and it made things worse.

  2. Going along“Abram hearkened”
  3. Contempt“her mistress was despised in her eyes”

A shortcut around God rarely ends where it starts. Look how fast the home goes from a sensible plan to a runaway in the desert. Any man who has taken a shortcut (a lie to smooth things over, a drink to get through it, a woman who was not his) knows how this staircase feels.

The good news starts at the bottom of the stairs, in the next verse.

Part three

The God who sees

Verses 7–14

A woman at the bottom of the stairs, alone in the desert, is found.

The road to Shur

Hagar was an Egyptian. When she ran, she ran toward home: south from Abram's camp, into the wilderness of Shur, the dry country on the road to Egypt. Egypt was about two hundred miles away.

She was pregnant, alone, a slave with no one to speak for her, and nothing but what she was wearing. That country is hard on anyone who is alone.

“The angel of the LORD found her by a fountain of water.” Found her. She wasn't looking for him. He was looking for her.

Where from? Where to?

The angel of the LORDHagar, Sarai's maid, where did you come from? And where are you going?

HagarI am running away from my mistress Sarai.

God knew the answers. He asks so she can say it out loud. And listen to her answer: she tells where she came from, and she has no answer to the second question. She had nowhere to go.

That is the question every man asks himself on the way out of a hard place. Where are you headed? The bar. The old friends. The dealer. The old life. The easy lie. It is worth asking before you take a step: where does this road end?

This is the first time the angel of the LORD appears in the Bible. He says “I will multiply”, which only God can say, and Hagar calls him the LORD himself. This is the Son of God, before he became a man. The text does not name him, but it fits what the Bible later says: Jesus came to look for those who are lost.

“Return”

What God asked

Go back

Back to the hard house, back under Sarai. It was not what she wanted, and God did not pretend it was easy.

What God gave

A promise, a son, a name

She went back knowing that God had seen her, had promised her descendants, and had given her son a name. She no longer went back alone.

This is one word to one woman, from the One who knew her whole story. It is not a rule that anyone must stay where they are being harmed. If you are ever in danger, tell your pastor and your brothers in Christ, and get safe.

But often, what God asks is not run but go back and face it, with Me: back to the job, the family, the person you owe. He does not send a man back empty-handed.

Ishmael: “God hears”

יִשְׁמָעֵאל said Yishma‘el “God hears”

“Because the LORD hath heard thy affliction.” Every time she called her boy by name, she said it: God hears. Her son would walk around for the rest of his life with the proof that somebody had listened.

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Psalm 34:18

The LORD hears the broken-hearted. That is true of a runaway slave, and it is true of you at two in the morning.

A wild man

his hand against every man,

and every man's hand against him;

The Hebrew picture is a wild donkey of a man: free, untamed, hard to hold. God was honest with her about what her son would be like. His descendants, the Ishmaelites, became tribes of the desert lands to the east and south (Genesis 25:12–18).

But it was never a curse. In chapter 17 God will say of Ishmael, “I have blessed him” (17:20). God can tell you what is hard about the child you are raising, or about yourself, and still hear, bless, and love.

This is the first time anyone in the Bible gives God a name of their own. It is not Abram, a priest or a prophet. It is a runaway slave woman, a foreigner, pregnant, alone. She is the first to say it, and what she says is: You see me.

Her second sentence, “Have I also here looked after him that seeth me?”, is pure astonishment: she had seen God, and she was still alive, and she was looked after. The well was named so no one would forget.

When you are ready to run

“Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence? If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there. If I take the wings of the morning, and dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea; Even there shall thy hand lead me, and thy right hand shall hold me.”

Psalm 139:7-10

“Whither shall I go?” is Hagar's question, and the answer is: wherever you run, he is already there. That is the scary part, and it is the best news of all.

  1. 1Say where you areBefore you go anywhere, call or text a brother in Christ: “I'm about to run.” Then tell your pastor. You are not meant to carry this alone.16:8
  2. 2Thou God seest meSay it out loud, wherever you are. He saw Hagar at a well in the middle of nowhere. He sees you.16:13

Part four

A son called God-Hears

Verses 15–16

She went back, and the boy was born.

Thirteen quiet years

one dot for every year until God spoke again

“Fourscore and six” is four twenties and six: eighty-six. Abram will be ninety-nine when God next speaks to him (17:1). The Bible records no word from God in the thirteen years between.

Think what those years looked like. Abram has a boy growing up in his tent, and it would be easy to think the plan had worked. And God is silent. Silence is not abandonment. In chapter 17 he comes back, and he does not come to scold.

The slave woman and the free woman

“For it is written, that Abraham had two sons, the one by a bondmaid, the other by a freewoman. But he who was of the bondwoman was born after the flesh; but he of the freewoman was by promise.”

Galatians 4:22-23

Hagar's son

Born “after the flesh”

The child of a human plan: a good idea, executed without God.

Sarah's son, still to come

Born “by promise”

The child of God's word, given when no one could have done it themselves.

Paul uses the two sons as a picture of two ways to try to be right with God: your own effort, or God's promise. This is the chapter where that picture begins.

Question 1 of 6

Waiting

What do we do when God seems slow?

What verses tell us about this?

“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.”

Proverbs 3:5-6

Answer

Wait on him, and ask him. Sarai heard God's promise, saw the delay, and decided she had to fix it. Nobody in these verses prays. Before you act on a clever plan, ask: is this his way, or only a sensible way? And bring it to your pastor and your brothers in Christ before you move. They can see what a tired man can't.

Question 2 of 6

Sin

Who was at fault in this home: Sarai, Abram, or Hagar?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All three, in different ways. Sarai planned it and blamed Abram. Abram went along, then passed the problem back. Hagar looked down on Sarai. Sin in a home spreads, and every person had a part. The honest thing is not to find who was worst. It is to own your own part.

Question 3 of 6

Consequences

Does a quick fix make the problem go away?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

Answer

No. It trades the first problem for a worse one. The plan to get a child brought contempt, blame, cruelty, a runaway, and a rivalry that lasted for generations. But there is a way out. “Whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.” Own it, turn from it, and God is faithful to forgive.

Question 4 of 6

God's heart

Does God see a person nobody else cares about?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Psalm 34:18

Answer

Yes. Hagar had the least say in the whole story. She was a slave, a foreigner, pregnant, alone, running. And the angel of the LORD went looking for her, called her by name, and told her he had heard her. If you feel like the person nobody sees, you are the kind of person he goes to find.

Question 5 of 6

God's name

What did Hagar call the LORD, and why does it matter?

What verses tell us about this?

“O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me. Thou knowest my downsitting and mine uprising, thou understandest my thought afar off. Thou compassest my path and my lying down, and art acquainted with all my ways.”

Psalm 139:1-3

Answer

She called him El Roi: “Thou God seest me.” It is the first name anyone in the Bible gives him, and a slave woman gave it. It matters because being seen is what a man in trouble most wants and most fears. God sees all of it, and he comes close anyway.

Question 6 of 6

Running

What should a man do when he is ready to run?

What verses tell us about this?

“Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence? If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there. If I take the wings of the morning, and dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea; Even there shall thy hand lead me, and thy right hand shall hold me.”

Psalm 139:7-10

Answer

Stop, and say where you are. Hagar had no answer to “whither wilt thou go?” Before you run, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor, and tell them the truth. Then face the hard thing with God, and with them. Wherever you run, he is already there, and it is far better to run to him and to the men he has put around you.
