Part one

Abraham believed God

Verses 1–8

Paul has said a man is justified by faith, not works. Now he calls two witnesses every Jew respected: Abraham and David.

Who was Abraham?

About 2000 BC, God called Abraham out of Ur, a city far down the Euphrates. He went up to Haran, and at seventy-five he set out for Canaan, a land God promised to show him (Genesis 12:4). He lived there in tents, near Shechem and Hebron.

Every Jew called him “our father.” If any man could boast of his works, it was Abraham. But Paul says he had nothing to boast of “before God.”

Counted

Abraham's account

Earned by his worksnothing to glory of “before God”
Counted to him by Godrighteousness“And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness.” Genesis 15:6

Counted is a bookkeeping word: to put something on a man's account. Abraham didn't earn righteousness. He believed God's promise, and God wrote righteousness into his account. Paul uses the word eleven times in this chapter, as counted, reckoned and imputed.

A paycheck, or a gift?

To him that worketh

Wages

Owed to you. You earned it, so you can brag about it. If heaven were a paycheck, God would be in your debt.

To him that worketh not, but believeth

A gift

Grace: given, not owed. All you can do with a gift is take it and say thank you.

He “justifieth the ungodly.”

Not the cleaned-up. Not the ones who got their act together first. The ungodly. That's the only kind of man God has ever justified, so you qualify.

Whose sins are covered

“When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day long.”

Psalm 32:3

“I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid… and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin.”

Psalm 32:5

David wrote this after adultery and murder. When a man covers his own sin, it eats him alive. When he uncovers it to God, God covers it, for good.

Part two

The father of us all

Verses 9–17

Was this blessing only for the circumcised Jew? Paul checks the dates, and the answer opens the door to everyone.

Check the dates

God counted Abraham righteous in Genesis 15. Ishmael was born when Abraham was 86 (Genesis 16:16). Abraham wasn't circumcised until he was “ninety years old and nine” (Genesis 17:24). So he was right with God for at least fourteen years before circumcision.

A ritual didn't make Abraham righteous. Faith did. So the door can't be marked “Jews only.”

A sign and a seal

A seal doesn't write the letter. It's pressed on afterward to show the letter is real. Circumcision didn't give Abraham righteousness. It marked the righteousness “which he had yet being uncircumcised.”

Baptism works the same way for us: a picture of what Christ has already done, not the thing that saves. And Abraham is “the father of all them that believe,” Jew or Gentile. Anyone who walks “in the steps of that faith” is his son.

“And this I say, that the covenant, that was confirmed before of God in Christ, the law, which was four hundred and thirty years after, cannot disannul, that it should make the promise of none effect.”

Galatians 3:17

The law came centuries after the promise, and it can't cancel it. If the promise depended on keeping the law, nobody would get it, because “the law worketh wrath”: it shows how far we fall short. The promise rests on faith.

Sure to all

Abram → Abraham Abram means high father. Abraham means father of a multitude. “for a father of many nations have I made thee” (Genesis 17:5).

God renamed him “father of many nations” while he still had no son by Sarah. God “calleth those things which be not as though they were.” He calls you by what He's making you, not by what you were. And because it rests on grace, not your performance, the promise is sure.

Part three

Against hope

Verses 18–25

An old man, an old wife, and a promise of more children than stars. Abraham believed it, and it was written down for us.

Against hope, in hope

God took Abraham outside at night and told him to count the stars. That's how many children you'll have, He said, to a man with none. Every human hope said no. Abraham hoped anyway, because of who had promised.

Faith isn't positive thinking. It's taking God at His word when nothing you can see backs it up.

He looked at the facts, and past them

about 100Abraham's age (4:19)

25 yearswaiting, from Haran to Isaac

He knew his body was as good as dead, and Sara's too. He didn't pretend otherwise. He just didn't let those facts have the last word. God did.

You've got facts too: a record, a debt, a family that doesn't trust you yet. Don't lie about them. But don't let them outvote God.

How God tells the story

What Genesis records

A man who stumbled

Twice he lied that Sarah was only his sister (Genesis 12, 20). He took Hagar to get a son his own way (Genesis 16). When God repeated the promise, he fell on his face and laughed (17:17).

What Romans records

“He staggered not”

“Strong in faith, giving glory to God… fully persuaded.” Not one word about the lies or Hagar.

Genesis tells the truth about Abraham's failures. But when God sums up a justified man, He writes what He counted to him. Abraham wobbled; his faith in the promise held. If you fell yesterday, your story isn't over, and God isn't telling it by your worst day.

Delivered, and raised

On Friday Jesus was delivered, handed over to die, for our offences. On Sunday He was raised. The empty tomb is God's receipt: the payment was accepted, and the account is settled.

That's why it was “not written for his sake alone, But for us also.” Righteousness is counted to anyone who believes on the God who raised Jesus. Anyone. “Whosoever” (Romans 10:13).

Question 1 of 6

Abraham

How was Abraham made right with God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He believed God, and God counted his faith as righteousness. He didn't earn it by works; before God he had nothing to boast of. God put righteousness on his account as a gift.

Question 2 of 6

Wages

What's the difference between wages and a gift?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Wages are owed: you earned them, and you can brag about them. A gift is grace: given freely to someone who didn't earn it. God justifies the ungodly who believe, not the ones who work for it.

Question 3 of 6

Covered

What blessing did David describe?

What verses tell us about this?

“I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. Selah.”

Psalm 32:5

Answer

The happiness of a man whose sins are forgiven and covered, and whom the Lord will never charge with sin. David knew it firsthand: when he hid his sin it ate at him; when he confessed it, God forgave him.

Question 4 of 6

Before

Why does it matter when Abraham was circumcised?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because God counted him righteous years before he was circumcised. Circumcision was only a seal of the faith he already had. So righteousness doesn't come by a ritual, and Abraham is the father of all who believe, Jew or Gentile.

Question 5 of 6

Hope

What did Abraham do when things looked hopeless?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He believed in hope against hope. He faced the facts about his age and Sara's, but he didn't stagger at God's promise. He gave glory to God, fully persuaded that God could do what He had promised.

Question 6 of 6

For us

Who else was this written for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For us. Righteousness will be counted to anyone who believes on the God who raised Jesus from the dead. Jesus was delivered for our offences and raised for our justification.
