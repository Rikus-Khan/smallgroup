Part one

Before the king

Verses 1–10

Kings, courts, and knowing your place.

Who were “the men of Hezekiah”?

Solomonreigned about 970–930 B.C.speaks the proverbs
more than 200 years
Hezekiahreigned about 715–686 B.C.his men copy them out

Solomon “spake three thousand proverbs” (1 Kings 4:32). Long after he died, King Hezekiah of Judah had his scribes and wise men gather more of them into a new collection. Chapters 25 through 29 are that collection.

“And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, according to all that David his father did.”

2 Kings 18:3

Hezekiah cleaned out the temple and brought his people back to God. Part of that revival was getting God's wisdom back into their hands. The book you're holding is here partly because a king cared enough to have it copied.

God's secrets and a king's job

The glory of God

to conceal a thing

God doesn't owe us every answer. He is bigger than our questions, and some of His ways stay hidden. That's part of what makes Him God.

The honour of kings

to search out a matter

A good leader digs for the truth before he acts. He doesn't decide on rumors or first impressions.

Verse 3 adds that you can't measure the sky, the deep, or a king's heart. Only God sees all the way down. But what He wants us to know, He has made plain in His word and in His Son, “who of God is made unto us wisdom” (1 Corinthians 1:30).

Skim off the dross

When silver ore is melted, the junk in it floats to the top as dross. The silversmith skims it off. Until he does, he can't make anything worth using.

Verse 5 makes the point: a king who clears wicked men out of his court will have a throne built on righteousness.

It works in a man's life too. God is the refiner. He's not throwing you away. He's taking out what doesn't belong so He can make something useful out of you.

Jesus told this proverb as a story

Proverbs 25:7

For better it is that it be said unto thee, Come up hither; than that thou shouldest be put lower in the presence of the prince whom thine eyes have seen.

Luke 14:10

But when thou art bidden, go and sit down in the lowest room; that when he that bade thee cometh, he may say unto thee, Friend, go up higher: then shalt thou have worship in the presence of them that sit at meat with thee.

At a dinner, Jesus watched guests grab the best seats and taught them this very proverb. Pushing yourself forward is a gamble: somebody may tell you to move. Taking the low seat costs nothing, and lets the one in charge lift you up.

When you have a beef with somebody

  • Rush into a fight“Go not forth hastily to strive.” You may not like how it ends.
  • Tell everybody else“Discover not a secret to another.” That can shame you for good.
  • Go to him yourself“Debate thy cause with thy neighbour himself.”

Jesus taught the same order in Matthew 18:15: go to your brother alone first. Most fights get worse the more people you drag in, and better when two men sit down face to face.

Part two

Words like gold

Verses 11–20

What we say can be beautiful, refreshing, or deadly.

Apples of gold in silver

The “pictures of silver” were probably carved silver settings. Picture golden fruit in a silver bowl: beautiful, and placed just right.

A word “fitly spoken” fits the moment: true, and said at the right time, in the right way.

Verse 12: a rebuke is like gold jewelry on “an obedient ear”. Wise words need a man willing to hear.

A cold drink, or an empty cloud

Verse 13

Snow at harvest

Not a snowstorm, which would ruin the crop. This is probably snow carried down from the mountains to cool drinks for workers in the hot fields. A man who does what he's sent to do is that refreshing.

Verse 14

Clouds without rain

A farmer sees clouds roll in and gets his hopes up, and not a drop falls. That's the man who brags about what he's going to give or do, and never does it.

Jude says the same about false teachers: “clouds they are without water” (Jude 12). Be the man whose word holds water. Show up when you say you will.

Good, then too much

sufficient“filled therewith, and vomit it”

Honey is a gift from God. The problem isn't the honey. It's not knowing when to stop.

Verse 17 says the same about people: even a good friend can wear out his welcome. Good things turn sour when there's no limit.

Every man in recovery knows this one. And some things, for some of us, have no safe “little bit.” For those, sufficient means none. Wisdom is knowing which is which, and being honest with yourself about it.

A soft tongue breaketh the bone.

Bone is the hardest thing in the body, and a gentle word breaks it. Patience and a calm voice can get through to a hard man when shouting never will.

Most of us learned to win by being louder or harder than the other guy. God's way looks weaker, and it works better.

Lies are weapons

A maula heavy war club. It crushes, up close.
A swordit cuts at arm's length.
An arrowit hits from far off. You never see it coming.

False witness does all three: to your face, behind your back, and from across town. A lie about a man can wreck his name, his job, and his family.

Verse 19: trusting an unfaithful man is like biting down on a broken tooth or stepping on a foot out of joint. It gives way right when you put your weight on it.

Vinegar on soda

Nitre here is a natural soda. Pour vinegar on it and it fizzes up, and both are spoiled. Taking a man's coat on a cold day is just as unhelpful.

That's what cheerful songs do to a man whose heart is heavy. Pep talks and “cheer up” don't help someone who is grieving. Sitting with him does.

“Rejoice with them that do rejoice, and weep with them that weep.”

Romans 12:15

Part three

Coals and walls

Verses 21–28

How to treat an enemy, and how to guard your own spirit.

Coals of fire?

This sounds like revenge, but it isn't. Kindness he doesn't deserve makes a man's face burn. It puts his wrong in front of him, and it may be the thing that softens him.

Some think it also points to an old custom of carrying hot coals in a pan on your head to show you were sorry. Either way, the goal is to win your enemy, not to hurt him.

What it doesn't mean, and what it does

  • kindness as a sneaky weaponbeing nice so it hurts him more
  • so he'll owe youkeeping score is still keeping a grudge
  • overcome evil with goodPaul quotes this proverb in Romans 12:20

“Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.”

Romans 12:21

That's exactly what God did for us. “While we were yet sinners, Christ died for us” (Romans 5:8). He fed His enemies. And “the LORD shall reward thee”: you leave the results with Him. Your enemy may never change. You still did right.

Better alone on the roof

Houses in Israel had flat roofs that people used like a porch. A man would rather sit out in a cramped corner of the roof than stay in a big house full of quarreling.

It's said about a “brawling woman”, but it cuts both ways. Ask yourself the hard question: am I the one people want to get away from? Peace at home starts with the man.

Good news from far away

No phones, no mail trucks. News from family in another land could take weeks or months, if it came at all. When good news finally arrived, it hit like cold water on a dry throat.

The best news ever came from the farthest country there is. Heaven sent word that God has made a way back to Him through His Son.

“But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life.”

John 4:14

A muddy spring

A spring is where a whole village gets its water. Trample through it and the water goes muddy for everyone downstream.

When a godly man caves in to wicked men, going along to get along, it isn't only his problem. Everyone who was drinking from his example gets mud.

Verse 27 ties back to the honey: chasing your own glory is like eating too much honey. It looks sweet and ends up making you sick.

A city with no walls

In Bible times, walls were a city's only defense. Without them, any raider could walk in and take whatever he wanted.

That's a man who can't rule his own spirit: his temper, his appetites, his moods. Anything that comes along can walk right in.

Self-control isn't something you work up on your own. It's “temperance,” fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23). God rebuilds the walls one stone at a time, as you walk with Him, day by day.

Points to remember

  1. God's word“Which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out” 25:1God kept His wisdom for us through people who cared about it. Don't leave it on the shelf.
  2. Humility“Come up hither” 25:7Take the low seat. Let God, not your own pushing, lift you up.
  3. Our words“A word fitly spoken” 25:11True words, said at the right time and the right way, are beautiful.
  4. Knowing when to stop“Eat so much as is sufficient for thee” 25:16Even good things turn bad without a limit. For some things, enough is none.
  5. Enemies“If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread” 25:21Overcome evil with good, and leave the results with the LORD.
  6. Self-control“A city that is broken down, and without walls” 25:28Without self-control, anything can walk in. The Spirit rebuilds the walls.

Question 1 of 6

God's word

Why were these proverbs copied out and kept for us?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he did that which was right in the sight of the LORD, according to all that David his father did.”

2 Kings 18:3

Answer

Hezekiah was a king who turned his people back to God, and part of that was gathering God's wisdom so it wouldn't be lost. God hides some things, but what we need He has given us. These words were kept for hundreds of years so men like us could read them now.

Question 2 of 6

Humility

Why is it better to be called up than to push yourself forward?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.”

Luke 14:11

Answer

The man who pushes himself up risks being put back down in front of everybody. The man who takes the low seat can only be lifted. Jesus taught this proverb at a dinner table. Chasing your own glory isn't glory at all. Let God do the lifting.

Question 3 of 6

Our words

What makes a word “fitly spoken”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It's true, and it fits the moment: said at the right time, in the right way, to someone ready to hear it. A gentle word can do what shouting never will. And the same chapter warns us about lies, empty boasting, and gossip.

Question 4 of 6

Knowing when to stop

What does honey teach us about how much is enough?

What verses tell us about this?

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Answer

A good thing becomes a bad thing when we don't stop. Honey, a friend's hospitality, anything. Wisdom knows the limit, and for some things the honest limit is none. When the pull is strong, God is faithful: He always gives a way to escape.

Question 5 of 6

Enemies

How does God tell us to treat an enemy?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.”

Romans 12:21

“But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you;”

Matthew 5:44

Answer

Meet his need. Feed him if he's hungry, give him water if he's thirsty. Not to hurt him or make him owe you, but to overcome evil with good. That's how God treated us when we were His enemies. Leave the results to Him.

Question 6 of 6

Self-control

What is a man like when he has no rule over his own spirit?

What verses tell us about this?

“But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law.”

Galatians 5:22–23

“For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind.”

2 Timothy 1:7

Answer

He's like a city with its walls knocked down. Anger, cravings, and moods walk right in and take over. Willpower alone won't hold the wall. Self-control is fruit the Holy Spirit grows in every believer, and He rebuilds the walls as we walk with Him day by day.
