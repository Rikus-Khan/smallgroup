Scattered, and sent

Part one

A great persecution

Verses 1–4

The church buries Stephen, Saul tries to wipe it out, and the plan backfires.

Grief, and a hunter

Devout men carried Stephen to his burial and made great lamentation: they wept out loud. Nothing in the Bible says a believer shouldn't grieve. These men loved him and buried him properly.

  1. consentingto Stephen's death (8:1)
  2. made havockof the church (8:3)
  3. every househe entered (8:3)
  4. men and womendragged to prison (8:3)

Havock means wreck. Haling is dragging. Saul watched one killing and agreed with it, and it grew from there. A man doesn't usually fall in one step. He agrees with something small, and it gets easier each time.

He was sure he was serving God. He wasn't. The good news is that Jesus isn't done with him yet. We'll see that in Acts 9.

Scattered like seed

“But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.”

Acts 1:8

For seven chapters the church stayed in Jerusalem. Then persecution scattered them, “except the apostles,” and the plan started moving.

Who went every where preaching the word? Ordinary believers. Saul tried to stamp the church out and scattered it like seed.

Part two

Samaria

Verses 5–25

The gospel crosses its first border, and runs into a man who thinks God's power is for sale.

Philip goes down to Samaria

This is Philip the evangelist, one of the seven chosen to serve in Acts 6, the same group as Stephen. He isn't one of the twelve apostles. He's a man who was handed a job and kept going when it got dangerous.

Samaria was the district just north of Judaea. Philip went down, away from Jerusalem, and preached Christ. People were freed of unclean spirits and the lame were healed. “There was great joy in that city.”

A wall comes down

JewsSamaritans“preached Christ unto them”

Jews and Samaritans had been enemies for hundreds of years. The Samaritans were descendants of mixed Israelite and foreign settlers, and they had their own temple on Mount Gerizim. Respectable Jews walked the long way around Samaria.

Jesus had already gone through it and talked to a Samaritan woman (John 4). Now Philip goes in, and the whole city comes. The people everyone had written off were the first to hear. The gospel doesn't ask who's respectable.

The biggest man in town

“This man is the great power of God”

Simon had been in town a long time. Sorcery means magic: tricks and spells, with the people's wonder as his income. He bewitched them (amazed and controlled them), and he gave out that himself was some great one. Everyone “from the least to the greatest” followed him.

Plenty of us know a man like that: the one with the show, the big talk, the people who need him. Some of us were him. The thing to see is what he was selling: himself.

Then Philip arrives and talks about Someone else entirely. Hold on to that difference. It's the whole story.

Believed, then baptized

Philip preachedthe kingdom of God, the name of Jesus Christthey believedthenthey were baptized

Look at the order: they believed, and then were baptized, “both men and women.” Baptism doesn't make anyone right with God. It's the step a believer takes to say, in front of everybody, I belong to Jesus.

Even Simon believed also and was baptized. He stuck close to Philip, amazed at the real miracles. Keep an eye on him. The next verses show what was in his heart.

The Spirit, in steps

  1. JerusalemJewsActs 2
  2. SamariaSamaritansActs 8
  3. CaesareaGentiles, Cornelius' houseActs 10
  4. EphesusJohn's followersActs 19

Verse 16 is odd: they believed and were baptized, but the Spirit “was fallen upon none of them.” Why wait for Peter and John? Because the apostles needed to see it, and so did the Samaritans. The Spirit coming through Jerusalem's own apostles showed everybody that these Samaritans are fully in: one church, not a rival one.

Not a pattern to repeat

Acts is a book of transition, and each new group gets this sign: Jews, Samaritans, Gentiles, and John's followers. It's God proving to everyone that each one belongs. It is not a pattern you have to repeat.

“Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his.”

Romans 8:9

So if you've trusted Jesus, you have the Holy Spirit. Today, now, with no second step, no waiting, and nobody's hands needed. You don't have to chase a feeling to know it.

You can't buy it

  • Money“Thy money perish with thee” (8:20)
  • Power over people“Give me also this power” (8:19)
  • A big showhe'd already tried that
  • Being good enough“Not of works, lest any man should boast”
  • “The gift of God”Romans 6:23

Simon watched the apostles lay hands on people, and he saw an opportunity. He had been the big man in town, and he wanted that power too. He didn't want God. He wanted what God could do for him.

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

Peter's answer is blunt because Simon is about to make the biggest mistake of his life. A gift can't be bought. Trying to pay for it insults the One who paid all of it.

Repent, and pray

“The gall of bitterness, and the bond of iniquity”

Gall is a bitter poison. Bond is a chain. Peter looks at a man who wants control and says he's already in chains himself. Plenty of us know what it is to chase power or a high and find out we're the ones being run.

But Peter doesn't write Simon off. “Repent therefore... and pray God, if perhaps the thought of thine heart may be forgiven thee.” There's a way out: turn around, and ask God yourself.

Sorry, or just scared?

Simon's answer is “Pray ye to the Lord for me, that none of these things... come upon me.” Asking others to pray is a good thing. But he asks them to keep the consequences off him. He doesn't say I was wrong. Luke doesn't tell us what happened next.

Regret says

“Keep it off me”

I hope nothing bad happens. It's about the cost to me.

Repentance says

“I was wrong”

I'm turning around, and I'm asking You to forgive me.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Was Simon saved?

The text says Simon believed and was baptized (verse 13). It also says his heart is not right in the sight of God (verse 21). Christians have read this two ways: a man who never really trusted Christ but followed the crowd, or a real believer who fell badly. The text doesn't settle it, so we won't pretend it does.

Simon wanted

What God can do for him

Power, a name, the next show. He offered money for it.

A believer wants

God Himself

Forgiveness he can't buy, and a Saviour he can't earn.

This isn't a reason to doubt your own salvation. If you've trusted Jesus for forgiveness, you're not Simon. It's a reason to ask what you want from God: the gift, or the Giver.

Peter and John go home by way of Samaria

fire on a Samaritan village → the gospel in many villages Verse 25 says Peter and John “preached the gospel in many villages of the Samaritans.” Years earlier, John had asked Jesus to call fire down on a Samaritan village that wouldn't welcome them (Luke 9:54). Now he's preaching to them.

Grace changes the thing a man hates most. If John can go from wanting to burn Samaria to preaching in it, there's room for the rest of us to change too.

Part three

The desert road

Verses 26–40

In the middle of a revival, an angel sends Philip away to meet one man in the desert.

A man from the far south

The angel says go toward the south, down the road from Jerusalem to Gaza, which is desert. Philip is in the middle of a city-wide revival, and he's told to leave it for an empty road.

Ethiopia in the Bible is the land of Cush, up the Nile, south of Egypt: more than a thousand miles from Jerusalem. Candace was the title of Ethiopia's queen, like Pharaoh. This man was her treasurer, a man of great authority. He's reading Esaias (Isaiah) in his chariot.

A man the temple kept at a distance

He had come to Jerusalem for to worship. He was an eunuch and a foreigner. By the law, a man like him couldn't fully enter the assembly of Israel (Deuteronomy 23:1). He may have made the long trip and been turned back at the gate.

So he was reading Isaiah on the road home, probably from a scroll he'd paid good money for. And the same prophet had written, hundreds of years before, a promise with his name on it:

“For thus saith the LORD unto the eunuchs that keep my sabbaths, and choose the things that please me, and take hold of my covenant; Even unto them will I give in mine house and within my walls a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters: I will give them an everlasting name, that shall not be cut off.”

Isaiah 56:4–5

Some of us have been told, one way or another, that we're not welcome. God sent a man across a desert for one who'd been kept out.

“Understandest thou?”

The Spirit, to PhilipGo near, and join thyself to this chariot.

Philip, running upUnderstandest thou what thou readest?

The EthiopianHow can I, except some man should guide me?

Philip ran. The Spirit pointed him, and he moved. And he asked a simple question without any lecture or a speech. The man was reading, which means he was already looking.

How can I, except some man should guide me? That's an honest answer, and a humble one. He was a rich, powerful man, and he asked a stranger to sit beside him. It's the reason a group like this exists. None of us understands it alone.

He took our place

Our roadgone astray

His roadto the slaughter

Us“every one to his own way”
Jesus“wounded for our transgressions”

He asked: of himself, or of some other man? Philip began at the same scripture, and preached unto him Jesus. The swap is the whole good news.

“But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.”

Isaiah 53:5–6

“If thou believest”

The question“What doth hinder me to be baptized?”
→
The answer“If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest.”

The condition is faith. Not good works, not a clean record, not a long class. And he answers plainly: “I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God.”

Notice who the question came from: the man who'd been told he couldn't enter. What doth hinder me? The answer is nothing. Not his skin, not his past, not his body.

Down, and up

He commanded the chariot to stand still. They went down both into the water, and he baptized him. Right there, no waiting for a church service.

“Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.”

Romans 6:4

Baptism doesn't save anyone. It pictures what already happened: the old life buried, a new one raised. If you've believed and haven't taken that step, ask what hinders you.

Found at Azotus

The moment he comes up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord caught away Philip, and the man saw him no more. And he went on his way rejoicing. He didn't need Philip anymore. He had Jesus.

Azotus is the old Philistine city of Ashdod, about twenty miles up the coast. Philip passing through preached in all the cities, as far as Caesarea. He just kept going, one town after another.

Two men, two roads

Simon, in the city

Wanted the power

Offered money. Had a heart “not right in the sight of God.” Asked to be kept from the consequences, and Luke doesn't say he repented.

The Ethiopian, in the desert

Asked, and believed

Admitted he needed a guide. Heard about Jesus, believed with all his heart, and “went on his way rejoicing.”

One was the biggest man in the city and the other was an outsider on a desert road. God doesn't care how big we are. He asks one question: do you believe?

“He went on his way rejoicing”

That's the end of the chapter, and it's where a believer ends up: not fixed in a day, but rejoicing on the road home.

Today: two things from this chapter. First, if something in the Bible doesn't make sense, ask a man who knows, the way the Ethiopian did. Second, you don't need a title to tell one person what Jesus did for you. The scattered ones were ordinary people, and they changed a country.

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Points to remember

  1. Scattered“Went every where preaching the word” 8:4What was meant to stop the church spread it. God can use your worst season.
  2. Samaria“There was great joy in that city” 8:8The people everyone wrote off were the first to hear and believe.
  3. The gift“The gift of God may be purchased with money” 8:20Grace can't be bought or earned. It's a gift.
  4. Repent“Repent therefore of this thy wickedness” 8:22Turn around, and ask God yourself. The door is open.
  5. Understanding“Understandest thou what thou readest?” 8:30He was reading Isaiah 53, and Philip showed him it was Jesus.
  6. Baptism“If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest” 8:37Believe, then take the step. Nothing hinders a man who believes.

Answer

It scattered the believers across Judaea and Samaria, and everywhere they went they preached the word. Saul meant to wipe the church out, and he spread it instead.

Question 2 of 6

Samaria

What happened in Samaria when Philip preached Christ?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The people gave heed, the sick were healed, evil spirits came out, and there was great joy in that city. Men and women believed and were baptized.

Question 3 of 6

The gift

What did Simon think he could buy?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The power to give the Holy Ghost, the gift of God. Peter told him it can't be bought, because God gives His gifts freely. Salvation works the same way.

Question 4 of 6

Repent

What did Peter tell Simon to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To repent of his wickedness and pray to God himself for forgiveness. Peter saw a man in the grip of bitterness and chains, and still pointed him to the way out.

Question 5 of 6

Understanding

What was the man from Ethiopia reading, and who explained it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He was reading Isaiah 53, about the sheep led to the slaughter. Philip, sent by the Spirit, sat beside him, started at that same passage, and preached Jesus.

Question 6 of 6

Baptism

What happened once the man from Ethiopia believed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He said, “I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God,” and Philip baptized him on the spot. He came up out of the water and went on his way rejoicing.
