Micah 2

Plotting in the dark

Powerful men lie awake planning how to take their neighbors' land, and they want a preacher who will tell them it's fine. God has a word for them, and a promise for His flock.

Part one

Upon their beds

Verses 1–5

Micah, the country preacher from chapter 1, speaks up for the small farmers losing everything.

The night shift

These were rich, powerful men in Judah, in Micah's day about 700 years before Christ. They didn't sin by accident. They lay in bed working it out, and at first light they went and did it. Why? “Because it is in the power of their hand.” Because they could.

  1. devise iniquitylying awake (v. 1)
  2. practise itat first light (v. 1)
  3. covetfields and houses (v. 2)
  4. take them by violencea man and his heritage (v. 2)

Every relapse has a night before it. The plan gets drawn up in the dark, long before the hands move. That's the place to fight it: when the thought first shows up in bed, bring it into the light. Call a brother. Pray out loud. Don't let it sit there till morning.

Not just dirt

When Israel came into the land under Joshua, every family got its own piece, and it was meant to stay in that family for good. A man's field was his living, his children's future, and his share in God's promise. That's why the Bible calls it his heritage.

God's rule

“The land shall not be sold for ever: for the land is mine; for ye are strangers and sojourners with me.”

Leviticus 25:23

One poor man's answer to a king

“And Naboth said to Ahab, The LORD forbid it me, that I should give the inheritance of my fathers unto thee.”

1 Kings 21:3

King Ahab wanted Naboth's vineyard so badly that his wife had Naboth killed for it. In Micah's day, men were doing the same thing on a bigger scale: loans, courts, and plain force. Coveting is the last of the Ten Commandments, and it's often the first step toward breaking the others.

Two kinds of plans

The rich men→devise→iniquityThey plot to take from the poor (v. 1).
The LORD→devise→an evil (a disaster)He plans their downfall, with a yoke they can't slip (v. 3).

Same Hebrew word, two directions. They planned in bed. God was planning too. The men who took other men's fields would watch foreign soldiers divide up their own (v. 4). Assyria, and later Babylon, did exactly that.

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

A cord by lot

Land in Israel was first handed out by casting lots, and each share was measured with a cord, like a surveyor's line. “The congregation of the LORD” is His people gathered together.

thenyou'll have no one to measure out yours.

These men grabbed everything, and in the end they'd have nothing to pass down. Greed always promises more and leaves you with less. The man who stops grabbing finds out what God gives freely.

Part two

Prophesy ye not

Verses 6–11

The people don't want to hear it. They'd rather have a preacher who talks about the good times.

“Stop preaching that”

The people, to the prophetsProphesy ye not… they shall not take shame.

The LORDIs the spirit of the LORD straitened? are these his doings? do not my words do good to him that walketh uprightly?
My word only hurts the crooked

“Straitened” means cut short, held back. Their idea was, God's too kind to do anything like this. Quit with the doom. God answers: My word is good news to the man who walks straight. It only feels like an attack if you're walking crooked.

“Which say to the seers, See not; and to the prophets, Prophesy not unto us right things, speak unto us smooth things, prophesy deceits:”

Isaiah 30:10

“My people is risen up as an enemy”

God's own people were acting like an invading army against their neighbors. Peaceful travelers, men who wanted no trouble, got robbed of the coat off their back. Widows were thrown out of their homes. Children lost the inheritance God meant them to have.

This is not your rest

So God says: get up and go. The land was supposed to be their rest, but they'd polluted it, and it would throw them out. A life built on what we took from other people can't give us peace. It looks like rest, but it's poison.

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

The preacher they wanted

“Walking in the spirit and falsehood” means a man who claims to speak for God, and lies. God says, if a man stood up and promised them all the wine and liquor they could want, he'd be their favorite preacher.

The voice they wanted

“Wine and strong drink”

Good times, no cost, no change. Never mentions sin.

The voice they needed

“My words do good”

Hard truth from a God who wants them whole (v. 7).

Every man in recovery has heard that first voice. It doesn't sound like a liar. It sounds like a friend: one won't hurt, you've earned it, you've got this handled. It promised the good life, and you know what it delivered.

“For the time will come when they will not endure sound doctrine; but after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears;”

2 Timothy 4:3

Part three

The breaker

Verses 12–13

Out of nowhere, a promise. God will gather His flock and lead them out Himself.

One flock, gathered

After all the warnings, God turns and promises the opposite of scattering. He will gather all of Jacob. The “remnant” is the part left after judgment, the ones who survive and come back to Him.

Bozrah was a city in Edom, sheep country, famous for its huge flocks. Picture a fold so packed with sheep you can hear the noise from far off.

That promise was made to Israel, and God will keep it. But it shows us who He is. He doesn't just scatter the proud. He goes looking for the stragglers and brings them home.

“Behold, at that time I will undo all that afflict thee: and I will save her that halteth, and gather her that was driven out; and I will get them praise and fame in every land where they have been put to shame.”

Zephaniah 3:19

The breaker goes first

“The breaker” is the one who breaks a way through the wall or the gate. The sheep don't break out on their own. Someone goes ahead and opens the way.

Then the flock pours out, the king in front and the LORD at the head, like a new Exodus out of captivity.

“And when he putteth forth his own sheep, he goeth before them, and the sheep follow him: for they know his voice.”

John 10:4

Points to remember

  1. Temptation“Work evil upon their beds” 2:1Sin gets planned in the dark. Fight it at the first thought, in the light.
  2. Greed“They covet fields, and take them” 2:2Coveting starts in the heart and ends with taking from others.
  3. God's justice“Against this family do I devise an evil” 2:3A man reaps what he sows. God sees the plans made in bed.
  4. Truth“Do not my words do good?” 2:7God's word only wounds what needs to change. The voice promising good times with no cost is lying.
  5. Hope“The breaker is come up before them” 2:13God gathers His scattered sheep and goes ahead of them to open the way.

Question 1 of 5

Temptation

Where does sin start before it ever reaches the hands?

What verses tell us about this?

“Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.”

James 4:8

Answer

In the mind, often at night. These men planned their sin in bed and did it in the morning. Our worst days usually start with a thought we let sit and grow. The time to deal with it is when it first shows up: draw near to God, bring it into the light, and tell a brother before the hands move.

Question 2 of 5

Greed

Why is coveting such a serious sin?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour’s house, thou shalt not covet thy neighbour’s wife, nor his manservant, nor his maidservant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any thing that is thy neighbour’s.”

Exodus 20:17

“And he said unto them, Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man’s life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth.”

Luke 12:15

Answer

Because it's where the other sins start. Coveting is wanting what God gave someone else so badly that you'll take it. In Micah's day it led to theft, lies in court, and violence. Jesus says a man's life isn't the stuff he has. Contentment is trusting that God gave you what you need.

Question 3 of 5

God's justice

Does a man reap what he sows?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Answer

Yes. The men who divided up other people's fields lived to see their own fields divided by enemies. God isn't fooled, and He isn't mocked. But the cross means a man doesn't have to reap the eternal harvest of his sin. Jesus took that for everyone who trusts Him.

Question 4 of 5

Truth

Why do people want preachers who tell them what they want to hear?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the time will come when they will not endure sound doctrine; but after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears;”

2 Timothy 4:3

Answer

Because the truth would mean changing, and they didn't want to change. So they shut down Micah and would have cheered a man who promised more wine. We all have that pull. A good friend, a good sponsor, a good preacher will sometimes say what we don't want to hear. God's words “do good to him that walketh uprightly.”

Question 5 of 5

Hope

What does God promise His scattered flock?

What verses tell us about this?

“And when he putteth forth his own sheep, he goeth before them, and the sheep follow him: for they know his voice.”

John 10:4

Answer

To gather them, all of them, and to lead them out Himself. He breaks open the way, and He walks in front. God made that promise to Israel and will keep it. It shows us His heart: the Shepherd doesn't wait for lost sheep to find their own way home. He goes ahead and opens the gate.
