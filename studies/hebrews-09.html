The writer of Hebrews isn't named. He is writing, about AD 64 to 68, to Hebrew believers who were tempted to slip back into the old ways. In Jerusalem the temple was still standing, the priests still busy at the altar.

Chapter 7 gave us a better Priest. Chapter 8, a better covenant. Now chapter 9 walks us through the old tent, room by room, to show what Jesus did when He went in.

Part one

A tent with a veil

Verses 1–10

Two rooms, a curtain between them, and a door to God that stayed shut all year but one day.

The tabernacle, from above

God gave Moses this plan on the mountain (8:5). The first room, the sanctuary, held the bread and the light. Behind a heavy curtain, the veil, was a perfect cube: the Holiest of all, where God met His people (Exodus 25:22).

Notice what's missing: no chair anywhere. Hold on to that for chapter 10.

Bread, light, and a curtain

  1. The candlestickA golden lampstand with seven lamps burning olive oil. The only light in the room.
  2. The tableOf wood covered in gold, for the bread.
  3. The shewbreadTwelve loaves, one for each tribe, set out before God and changed every sabbath.
  4. The veilA thick curtain of blue, purple and scarlet, with cherubims woven in. It said: this far and no farther.

“And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.”

Exodus 25:8

God wanted to live among His people. But a holy God and sinful men can't simply sit down together. The veil stood in between, and every man who saw it knew why.

What was in the ark

The ark was a gold-covered chest. Inside: a pot of the manna God fed them with in the desert, Aaron's dead stick that sprouted overnight (Numbers 17), and the stone tables of the Ten Commandments.

The lid was the mercyseat, with two cherubims of gold above it. Between them God said He would meet with them. Right under it lay the law they had broken.

Once a year the high priest sprinkled blood on that lid. God looked down at His broken law and saw blood between it and Him. That is mercy: the sin not ignored, but covered by a death.

One man, one day, never without blood

This was the Day of Atonement. The high priest washed, went in with coals and incense so the smoke hid the mercy seat, and sprinkled blood on it, first for his own sins, then for the people's.

“For on that day shall the priest make an atonement for you, to cleanse you, that ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD.”

Leviticus 16:30

Even the best man needed blood

The holiest man in Israel couldn't walk in on his own record. He had to bring blood for himself first. Sin isn't just a problem for the worst of us. It's in all of us.

“And the LORD said unto Moses, Speak unto Aaron thy brother, that he come not at all times into the holy place within the vail before the mercy seat, which is upon the ark; that he die not: for I will appear in the cloud upon the mercy seat.”

Leviticus 16:2

Errors means sins done in ignorance or weakness, the things a man does without fully meaning to. God made a way even for those. He's not looking for a reason to shut you out. He's been building a way in since the beginning.

From the tent to the torn veil

  1. Moses at SinaiThe tabernacle, built to God's patternExodus 25–40
  2. Temples in stoneSolomon's, then Herod's: same rooms, same veil1 Kings 6
  3. The crossThe veil rent from top to bottomabout AD 30–33
  4. Hebrews writtenThe temple still standingabout AD 64–68
  5. The temple fallsNo altar, no priest, no veilAD 70

“And, behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom; and the earth did quake, and the rocks rent;”

Matthew 27:51

For about fifteen hundred years the curtain preached one sermon: the way in is not open yet. The Holy Ghost was saying it through the furniture. Then Jesus died, and God tore it from the top down.

Clean outside, guilty inside

What the old way could reach

The outside

Meats and drinks, and divers washings: food rules, washing rules, rules for the body. A man could keep every one and walk home clean on paper.

What it couldn't reach

The conscience

The voice inside that keeps replaying what you did. No washing could get to it. That was waiting for the time of reformation: the day God would set it all right.

A lot of us know this one. You can get clean, get a job, get your hair cut and your record sealed, and still lie awake hearing the old tapes. Religion can fix the outside. Keep reading: verse 14 goes inside.

Part two

By his own blood

Verses 11–22

The real High Priest goes into the real holy place, and what He brings out is a clean conscience.

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Redemption is buying a slave or a prisoner free. Eternal means it doesn't run out at the end of the year. He paid once, and you are free for good.

How much more

A red heifer (a young cow) was burned, and its ashes mixed with water. A man who had touched a dead body was sprinkled with it and counted clean again (Numbers 19).

If animal blood and ashesmade the body clean on the outside
→
how much more will the blood of Christmake the conscience clean inside

“And a man that is clean shall gather up the ashes of the heifer, and lay them up without the camp in a clean place, and it shall be kept for the congregation of the children of Israel for a water of separation: it is a purification for sin.”

Numbers 19:9

The writer argues from the small to the great. If God honored a cow's ashes, what will He do with the blood of His own Son, who offered himself without spot?

A conscience made clean

Purge means scrub clean. Dead works are everything a man does to try to make up for it: the promises, the penance, the beating himself up. They can't give life. The blood of Christ goes where nothing else reaches.

And He cleans you to serve the living God. Guilt keeps a man staring at the past. A clean conscience frees him to get up and work for God today.

When the old tapes play

Your conscience will bring it up again: at 3 a.m., at work, when you see that face. Don't argue with it using your own good deeds. Point it to the blood.

“Come now, and let us reason together, saith the LORD: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool.”

Isaiah 1:18

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Regret is honest; you did those things. But a believer's regret goes to the cross, not round and round in his head. If something can be made right with a person, make it right. Then let the blood have the last word.

A will is read after a death

The same word means covenant and testament, like a man's last will and testament. A will does nothing while the man who made it, the testator, is alive. It takes force when he dies.

A will is readonly after the testator dies
→
so Jesus diedand everything He left is yours

“Likewise also the cup after supper, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you.”

Luke 22:20

His death put the will in force, and the inheritance is eternal. And who's named in it? They which are called: everyone who answers the gospel's call. A mediator stands between two sides to make peace. Jesus stands between you and God.

Without shedding of blood is no remission.

“For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.”

Leviticus 17:11

Why blood?

Remission means sent away: a debt cancelled, a charge dropped. The wage of sin is death (Romans 6:23). So sin is never just shrugged off. Either the sinner dies, or a death is given in his place.

“And Moses took the blood, and sprinkled it on the people, and said, Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD hath made with you concerning all these words.”

Exodus 24:8

At Sinai Moses sprinkled the book, the people, the tent and every cup and bowl. It looked hard. It was meant to. Sin is that serious, and God's answer to it is that costly.

That's why being sorry, however sincere, can't pay for sin, and neither can doing better. Only a death can. Jesus gave His. Your part is to trust Him for it.

Part three

Once

Verses 23–28

He went into heaven itself, He did it once, and He's coming back.

Every year, or once

EVERY YEAR, FOR ABOUT 1,500 YEARS

Each dot is ten Days of Atonement.

Once “by the sacrifice of himself” (9:26)

If His sacrifice worked like theirs, He would have to die over and over, since the foundation of the world. But it doesn't. One death, one time, to put away sin. Not cover it for a year: put it away.

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

He appears three times

  1. He appearedTo put away sin, at the cross9:26 · past
  2. He appearsIn the presence of God for us, right now9:24 · today
  3. He shall appearThe second time, unto salvation9:28 · to come

Notice the middle one. Right now, today, while you sit here and while you're at work, Jesus stands before God for us. He's not there to accuse you. He's there to plead for you.

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Two appointments

every manonce to dieafter this the judgment
once for oncejudgment, or salvation
soChrist was once offered to bear the sins of manyHe shall appear the second time unto salvation

Every man has two dates he didn't choose: one death, then the judgment. No second run at life. That's sobering, and it should be.

But look how the sentence turns: so Christ. He bore the sins, and the judgment they deserved, for everyone who trusts Him. For a believer, the next time he sees Jesus isn't a trial. It's a rescue.

“Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.”

John 5:24

Men who look for Him

The first time He came to deal with sin. The second time He comes without sin: that business is finished. He's coming to bring His people home.

“Looking for that blessed hope, and the glorious appearing of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ;”

Titus 2:13

A man who is looking for someone keeps his eyes up. Today, when guilt pulls your eyes back to yesterday, lift them. Your sins were borne once. Your Saviour is coming. Go to work like a man who's expecting Him.

  1. The tabernacle“The high priest alone once every year, not without blood” 9:7One man, one day a year, with blood. The veil said the way in wasn't open yet.
  2. His blood“By his own blood he entered in once” 9:12Jesus went into heaven itself with His own blood and obtained eternal redemption.
  3. Conscience“Purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God” 9:14His blood reaches the guilt inside, and frees you to serve God today.
  4. Remission“Without shedding of blood is no remission” 9:22Sin costs a death. Jesus gave His, so your sin can be sent away.
  5. Once“Now once … to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself” 9:26Not every year. Once. It never needs doing again.
  6. He's coming“Shall he appear the second time without sin unto salvation” 9:28Every man dies once, then judgment. Christ bore the sins, and He's coming to bring His people home.

Question 1 of 6

The tabernacle

Who could go into the holiest of all, and how often?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Only the high priest, alone, once a year, and never without blood for himself and the people. The veil showed the way to God wasn't open yet. When Jesus died, God tore it from top to bottom.

Question 2 of 6

His blood

Where did Christ go with His own blood, and what did He get for us?

What verses tell us about this?

“But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:19

Answer

Not into a tent made with hands, but into heaven itself, into the presence of God for us. He went once, with His own blood, and obtained eternal redemption: we are bought free for good.

Question 3 of 6

Conscience

What can the blood of Christ do for a guilty conscience?

What verses tell us about this?

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Answer

What animal sacrifices and washings never could: purge the conscience. It cleans out the guilt and the dead works we tried to pay with, and sets us free to serve the living God.

Question 4 of 6

Remission

Why is there no remission without the shedding of blood?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.”

Leviticus 17:11

Answer

Because the wage of sin is death, and the life is in the blood. Sin is only sent away when a life is given in the sinner's place. Like a will that takes force at a death, the new testament took force when Jesus died.

Question 5 of 6

Once

How many times did Christ have to offer Himself?

What verses tell us about this?

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

Answer

Once. The high priest went in every year with the blood of others. Christ appeared once, at the end of the ages, and put sin away by the sacrifice of Himself. It never has to be done again.

Question 6 of 6

He's coming

What waits for every man after death, and what will Christ bring when He appears the second time?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Every man dies once, and after that comes the judgment. But Christ was offered once to bear the sins of many, and to those who look for Him He will appear the second time, not to deal with sin, but to bring salvation.
