Part one

Where wisdom starts

Chapters 1, 2, 9, 15, 21, 30

Before it's anything else, wisdom is a gift from God.

A craftsman's word

חָכְמָה said chokmah skill, know-how, the ability to do a thing well

The Bible uses the same word for the skill of a builder. When God picked a man to build the tabernacle, He said:

“See, I have called by name Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah: And I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship,”

Exodus 31:2–3

So wisdom in Proverbs isn't being book-smart. It's skill for living: knowing how to handle your money, your mouth, your temper, and your family the way God designed them to work.

The first stone

The fear of the LORD isn't being scared of God the way you'd be scared of a violent man. It's taking Him seriously: awe, respect, and trust that shape how you live.

“Beginning” means the starting place and the most important part. You can't build the roof first. A smart man who doesn't fear God is building on sand.

And 15:33 adds: “before honour is humility.” The road up starts by bowing down.

Three words that travel together

דַּעַת
da'ath

Knowledge

Knowing the facts. What is true.

A mechanic knows the parts.

בִּינָה
binah

Understanding

Seeing how the facts fit together. Telling one thing from another.

He understands how the engine runs.

חָכְמָה
chokmah

Wisdom

The skill to do the right thing with what you know.

He can actually fix your truck.

A man can know drinking is killing him, and understand exactly why he reaches for it, and still not have the skill to walk away. Proverbs wants all three, but wisdom is where it all gets lived out.

Where does wisdom come from?

  • being smartplenty of brilliant men wreck their lives
  • getting oldergray hair doesn't guarantee it
  • hard knocks alonea fool can take the same hit ten times
  • the LORD giveth wisdom“out of his mouth,” through His word

Even Solomon, who wrote most of Proverbs, got his wisdom as a gift: “God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding exceeding much” (1 Kings 4:29). And 2:10 says it has to get past your head and into your heart.

An honest man's question

Chapter 30 comes from a man named Agur. He admits he can't climb up to heaven and figure God out on his own. Nobody can. 21:30 says no wisdom of man can stand against the LORD.

What is his name, and what is his son's name, if thou canst tell?

Agur couldn't answer that. We can: His Son's name is Jesus. And Jesus said of Himself, “a greater than Solomon is here” (Matthew 12:42). Hold that thought. We'll come back to it in part three.

Part two

Wisdom calls out

Chapters 1–8, 16–24

She isn't hiding. She's out in the street, calling your name.

Why is Wisdom a woman?

Chokmah is a feminine word in Hebrew, so the poet pictures wisdom as a noble woman: an idea described as a person (personification).

She calls in the street and at the gate, where you live. Her rival is the “strange woman” of chapters 5–7. Every man decides whose voice he'll follow.

Who is she calling?

  • the simple1:22, 8:5, 9:4
  • fools8:5
  • scorners1:22
  • men“Unto you, O men, I call” (8:4)

“Simple” doesn't mean stupid. The Hebrew word (pethi) comes from a word for open: a man who is open to anything, easily talked into things, untrained.

Wisdom isn't calling the men who already have it together. She's calling the ones who don't. If you've been a fool, good news: you're on her list.

Old words, plain meanings

prudence
Thinking ahead. Seeing trouble coming and stepping around it.“A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself” (22:3).
witty inventions
In 1611 “witty” meant clever, skillful, not funny. Wise plans and good ideas.
sound wisdom
Hebrew tushiyyah: wisdom that actually works. Good judgment that holds up.Also in 2:7, 3:21, 8:14.
counsel
Advice. A plan you can lean on.

And 8:14 ends with “I have strength.” Wisdom isn't weak or soft. It's the strength to do the right thing when everything in you wants the wrong one.

A father talking to his son

23times “my son” in Proverbs
4:7“the principal thing”

Over and over, Proverbs sounds like a dad sitting a young man down. A lot of men never had a father who did that. Some had one who taught them the opposite.

Here, God gives you that talk. “My son, attend unto my wisdom.” He's the Father who stays.

7:4 says to treat wisdom like family: “Thou art my sister.” Keep her close, and the very next verse says why: to “keep thee from the strange woman” (7:5).

With all thy getting

  1. Money 23:4
  2. Respect 13:10
  3. A woman 29:3
  4. The next high 9:17
  5. Wisdom 4:7

“Principal” means first, number one. Most of us had that list upside down. Wisdom says: get this first, and whatever else you go after, go after understanding too.

“Get” is an active word. You don't drift into wisdom. You lean your ear toward it (2:2) and go after it.

Put it on the scale

Rubies, gold, silver: the most valuable things anybody in Solomon's day could hold in his hand.

Pile them all on one side. Wisdom still outweighs them. “All the things that may be desired are not to be compared to it” (8:11).

Money can disappear in one bad weekend. Wisdom keeps you from having that weekend.

You can't buy it with cash

17:16

Money in a fool's hand

He can pay for the class. He can buy the books. But “he hath no heart to it.” Wisdom isn't for sale to a man who doesn't want it.

23:23

“Buy the truth, and sell it not”

The price is paid in other ways: humility, listening, letting go of your old way. Once you have it, don't trade it back for anything.

19:8: the man who gets wisdom “loveth his own soul.” Going after wisdom isn't selfish. It's finally treating yourself the way God does.

And for any man who thinks his future is already ruined, 24:14: “when thou hast found it, then there shall be a reward, and thy expectation shall not be cut off.” With wisdom, there is still hope ahead of you.

Part three

Wisdom and the Maker

Chapters 3, 8, 9, 24

The wisdom that built the world, and two houses with their doors open.

The same tools, two building jobs

Proverbs 3:19

The LORD by wisdom hath founded the earth; by understanding hath he established the heavens.

Proverbs 24:3

Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established:

God used wisdom and understanding to build the whole universe. He offers you the same two tools to build your house: your marriage, your kids, your recovery, your life.

If it held up the heavens, it can hold up your house.

Does 8:22 say Jesus was created?

The claim

“Wisdom is Jesus, and God made Him”

Jehovah's Witnesses use this verse to teach that Jesus was the first thing God created. It's an old argument, going back to a teacher named Arius in the 300s.

The passage

A poem about God's own wisdom

The speaker is the same “she” who cries in the streets (8:1) and builds a house with seven pillars (9:1). It's a picture, not a birth record.

God has never been without His wisdom. If wisdom had a start date, there was a time God was foolish. Verse 23 says it plainly: “I was set up from everlasting.”

“Possessed” (Hebrew qanah) means had, owned. It's the same root used of God as “possessor of heaven and earth” (Genesis 14:19). God owned the heavens and earth; He didn't come from them.

“I was there”

Proverbs 8:27, 30

When he prepared the heavens, I was there… Then I was by him, as one brought up with him: and I was daily his delight, rejoicing always before him;

John 1:1, 3

In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God… All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.

Proverbs pictures God's wisdom at His side while He made the world, rejoicing in it, with its “delights… with the sons of men.” John shows us that wisdom in a Person: the Word, who was with God, was God, and made all things.

“As one brought up with him” (amon) may also mean master workman. Either way, it's the craftsman's word again.

Christ, the wisdom of God

“But unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God.”

1 Corinthians 1:24

“But of him are ye in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption:”

1 Corinthians 1:30

“In whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.”

Colossians 2:3

Creator, not creature

“All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.”

John 1:3

“For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him: And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.”

Colossians 1:16–17

If Jesus made every thing that was made, He can't be one of the made things. The Maker isn't part of what He made. Wisdom says, “whoso findeth me findeth life” (8:35). That life is found in Him.

So Proverbs 8 doesn't teach that Jesus had a beginning. It's poetry about God's wisdom, which God has always had. And Jesus is that wisdom in the flesh: eternal God, who “was in the beginning with God.”

Two houses, same invitation

9:4 and 9:16 open with the same words. Both houses call the same man. Folly's offer is “stolen” and “in secret,” and her guests never see the pit until they're in it (9:18).

Sweet now, or good at the end?

Folly's table

the first tastethe end

Wisdom's table

the first step“and live”

Every addiction makes the same pitch: it's sweet, it's secret, nobody has to know. That's folly's house. The first taste is the best it will ever be.

Wisdom's “bread” and “mingled wine” in 9:5 are a picture of a feast, not a call to drink. Her table costs you your old way, but it gets better the longer you stay. “Forsake the foolish, and live.”

Part four

An ordinary day

Chapters 10–31

Your mouth, your pride, your eyes, your friends, your money.

Wisdom comes out of your mouth

What's deep in a man's heart comes up through his mouth. When wisdom is the source, his words are like a running stream: clean, fresh, and good for whoever drinks.

“The lips of the righteous feed many” (10:21). Your words can feed the man next to you. And 31:26 shows the best kind of wise words: they carry “the law of kindness.”

Knowing when to keep quiet

11:12 · 23:9

Hold your peace

The man without wisdom runs his neighbor down. The wise man lets it go. And some arguments aren't worth having: a fool “will despise the wisdom of thy words.”

24:7

“In the gate”

The town gate was where the elders sat to settle disputes and make decisions (see Ruth 4:1–2). When it counts, the fool has nothing to say.

Wisdom isn't always saying the smart thing. A lot of the time it's not saying the dumb one.

A full cup can't be filled

The scorner “seeketh wisdom, and findeth it not.” He won't be taught. Pride already filled his cup.

Wisdom is “with the well advised” (13:10): the man who asks for advice and takes it. And “reproof,” correction, gives wisdom (29:15). Can you take it from God's word, a pastor, or a brother?

Where are your eyes?

The fool's eyes are always out on the horizon: the next score, the next scheme, the next place where life will finally be different. Meanwhile wisdom is sitting right in front of him.

To a fool, mischief is “sport” and folly is “joy” (10:23, 15:21). Wisdom “understands his way” (14:8): it asks, where does this road actually go?

And it “resteth” in the heart (14:33). A wise man doesn't have to show off. It just settles in and stays.

Alone is dangerous

This verse is hard in the old English. “Intermeddleth” means breaks out against, picks a fight with. Many read it this way: the man who cuts himself off is chasing his own desire, and he fights against every bit of sound wisdom.

Isolation is where relapse starts. A man pulls away, stops answering his phone, and nobody is close enough to tell him the truth. Stay in the circle. Answer the phone. Come back if you've drifted.

Money, and who you spend it on

23:4

“Cease from thine own wisdom”

Working hard is good (Proverbs says so again and again). Wearing yourself out to get rich is not. Stop trusting your own angle on getting money fast. That “wisdom” has burned a lot of men.

29:3

“Spendeth his substance”

“Substance” is everything he owns. The company you keep decides where your paycheck goes. A man who loves wisdom makes his father glad. A man who keeps company with harlots wastes it all.

Think of the prodigal son, who “wasted his substance with riotous living,” and then came home to a father who ran to meet him (Luke 15). However much you've spent, the Father's door is open.

So how do I get it?

“If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.”

James 1:5

“Upbraideth not” means He doesn't throw your past in your face when you ask. He gives freely, to anyone.

“But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy.”

James 3:17

Start with the fear of the LORD. Come to Jesus, who is “made unto us wisdom.” Then ask, every day. That's a prayer God always answers.

Points to remember

  1. The fear of the LORD“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom” 9:10Wisdom starts by taking God seriously. That's the foundation stone.
  2. Wisdom's call“Wisdom crieth without” 1:20She calls the simple and the fools, right in the street. Nobody is too far gone to be called.
  3. What it's worth“Wisdom is better than rubies” 8:11More than gold, silver, or anything you could want. Make it number one.
  4. Who Jesus is“Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God” 1 Cor. 1:24Proverbs 8 is poetry about God's eternal wisdom. Jesus is that wisdom in the flesh, the Creator, not a creature.
  5. Two houses“Forsake the foolish, and live” 9:6Both call the same man. Folly is sweet at first and ends in death. Wisdom ends in life.
  6. Daily life“With the lowly is wisdom” 11:2Watch your mouth, drop your pride, stay with your brothers, and ask God for wisdom every day.

Question 1 of 6

The fear of the LORD

Where does wisdom begin?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

With the fear of the LORD: taking God seriously, with awe and trust. That's the first stone, and everything else is built on it. Wisdom isn't something we invent. The LORD gives it, and the way in is humility.

Question 2 of 6

Wisdom's call

Who is Wisdom calling out to?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Everybody, and especially the ones who don't have it together: the simple, the fools, the men who've been easily led. She calls in the streets and at the gates, right where people live. If you've been a fool, you're exactly who she's calling.

Question 3 of 6

What it's worth

What is wisdom worth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

More than rubies, gold, silver, or anything else a man could want. It's “the principal thing,” number one on the list. You can't buy it with cash, but getting it is the best way to love your own soul.

Question 4 of 6

Who Jesus is

Was Jesus created, or is He the wisdom of God?

What verses tell us about this?

“But unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God.”

1 Corinthians 1:24

“In whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.”

Colossians 2:3

“All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.”

John 1:3

Answer

He was never created. Proverbs 8 is a poem picturing God's wisdom, which God has always had, “from everlasting.” Jesus is that wisdom in the flesh. And He made all things, so He can't be one of the things that were made. He is God.

Question 5 of 6

Two houses

What is the difference between Wisdom's house and folly's?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Both call out to the same man with the same words. Folly offers stolen, secret pleasure that tastes sweet and ends in death. Wisdom sets a real table, costs you your old foolish way, and ends in life. And real life is found in Jesus.

Question 6 of 6

Daily life

What does wisdom look like on an ordinary day?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Words that build people up, and knowing when to keep quiet. Humility instead of pride. Thinking about where a road goes before you take it. Staying close to your brothers instead of pulling away. And asking God for wisdom every day.
