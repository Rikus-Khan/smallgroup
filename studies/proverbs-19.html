Part one

Poor, but honest

Verses 1–10

Integrity is worth more than money, and money buys a lot of fake friends.

Wrecking it, then blaming God

His own foolishness“perverteth his way”a wrecked lifeand thenhe's mad at God

“Fretteth” means rages, is furious. A man makes his own choices, they blow up on him, and then he shakes his fist at heaven: “Why did God let this happen?”

Owning our part is where change starts. God isn't the one who wrecked it. He's the One who can rebuild it. And verse 1 says an honest poor man is better off than a rich man with a lying mouth.

Count your friends

When the money's flowing

“Every man is a friend to him that giveth gifts” (v. 6).

When it's gone

“He pursueth them with words, yet they are wanting to him” (v. 7). He calls; nobody answers.

Proverbs isn't telling us to be like this. It's telling us the truth about people, so we're not surprised. Plenty of us lost every “friend” when the money or the dope ran out.

So be the other kind of man: the friend who answers the phone when there's nothing in it for you.

Said twice, on purpose

Proverbs 19:5

A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he that speaketh lies shall not escape.

Proverbs 19:9

A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he that speaketh lies shall perish.

Four verses apart, almost the same words. When the Bible repeats itself, it's underlining. A lie may work for a while, but it catches up. The second time, it goes from “not escape” to “perish.”

Verse 8 turns it around: getting wisdom is how a man “loveth his own soul.” Learning to tell the truth is a way of taking care of yourself.

Part two

A long fuse

Verses 11–19

Anger, home, laziness, and what God does with a gift to the poor.

Lengthen the fuse

“Discretion” is good sense. “Deferreth” means puts off, slows down. A wise man doesn't stop feeling anger. He just doesn't let it go off right away.

And it's his glory, his honor, to let an offense go. The world says a real man gets even. God says a real man can let it pass.

That's how God treats us: “slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy” (Psalm 103:8).

A leak in the roof

Old houses had flat clay roofs, and when it rained they leaked. Drip, drip, drip, all night. That's what constant fighting at home feels like: you can't get away from it.

Verse 13 isn't a joke about wives. It names two heartbreaks at home: a foolish son and a quarreling marriage. Verse 14 says a sensible, wise wife is a gift “from the LORD,” something no inheritance can buy.

Verse 15: laziness puts a man to sleep while life goes on without him. An idle soul ends up hungry.

A loan to God

You help a poor man→God counts it as a loan to Himself→He pays it backGod never forgets what's done for the least.

“And the King shall answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.”

Matthew 25:40

This isn't a get-rich scheme. God doesn't promise cash back. He repays in His own way and time. The point is, kindness to the poor is never wasted, because God takes it personally.

The rescue loop

Rescue a hot-tempered man from the consequences, and you'll have to do it again. Rescuing doesn't change him.

Many of us know this loop from both sides. Sometimes love lets a man feel the weight of his choices, while staying close and pointing him to the Lord.

Verse 18: correct a child early, “while there is hope.” Loving discipline, never abuse.

Part three

The counsel of the LORD

Verses 20–29

Our plans are many. His is the one that stands.

Many plans, one that stands

“I'll quit on my own.” “Just one more time.” “I'll get rich quick.” “I'll get even.” “Nobody needs to know.” “Next year I'll get serious.” the counsel of the LORD shall stand

“Devices” are plans and schemes. A man's head is full of them. Some are good, some are crooked, and many never happen.

God's purpose is the one thing that will stand. That's good news: His plan for the people who trust Him is good. “The counsel of the LORD standeth for ever” (Psalm 33:11). So verse 20 says: listen to counsel now, so you're wise “in thy latter end.”

The road that leads to life

“The fear of the LORD” is deep respect for God, taking Him seriously. It's where wisdom starts (1:7). Verse 23 says it tendeth to life: it leans that way, it heads that direction.

“Shall not be visited with evil” is a wise saying, not a promise that a believer never suffers. Good men get sick and get hurt. But a man who walks with God rests satisfied, and no evil can ever take him out of God's hand.

Verse 22: what people really want in a man is kindness and faithfulness. A poor man who's honest beats a liar every time.

Too lazy to lift the fork.

The word translated “bosom” here likely means a dish. People ate by hand from a shared bowl. The sluggard gets his hand into the food, then can't be bothered to bring it back up to his mouth.

It's meant to make you laugh, and then think. Laziness doesn't just skip the hard jobs. It even skips the good things right in front of you.

Verse 25: when a scorner gets punished, the simple man watching learns to be careful. But a man with understanding only needs a word.

Stop listening to that

Not all teaching is good. Some “instruction” pulls you away from the truth: the old crowd's advice, the voice that says you'll never change, preachers who twist the Bible for money.

A wise man learns to tell the difference, and he turns that voice off. Measure what you hear against God's Word.

Verses 26–29: the son who robs his father and drives out his mother brings shame. And “stripes” (beatings) wait for fools. Mockery and lies end in judgment.

Points to remember

  1. Blame“His heart fretteth against the LORD” 19:3Own your choices. God didn't wreck it, and He can rebuild it.
  2. Honesty“He that speaketh lies shall not escape” 19:5Lies catch up. Truth is how you take care of your own soul.
  3. Anger“The discretion of a man deferreth his anger” 19:11Lengthen the fuse. It's a man's glory to let an offense go.
  4. Kindness“He that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto the LORD” 19:17Kindness is never wasted. God takes it personally.
  5. Who God is“The counsel of the LORD, that shall stand” 19:21Our plans come and go. His purpose stands, and it's good.
  6. Listening“Hear counsel, and receive instruction” 19:20Listen now, so you're wise later. Turn off the voices that lead you astray.

Question 1 of 6

Blame

Who do we blame when our own choices wreck our lives?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Too often, God. A man's own foolishness twists his road, and then he rages against the LORD. The way out is the opposite of blame: own it and confess it. God is faithful to forgive and to clean us up.

Question 2 of 6

Honesty

What will happen to a liar?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He won't escape. God says it twice, four verses apart. A lie may seem to work for a while, but it catches up. And a poor man who tells the truth is better off than a liar with money.

Question 3 of 6

Anger

What does a wise man do with his anger?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Answer

He slows it down. He uses good sense to put it off instead of going off, and he counts it an honor to let an offense pass. A man of great wrath keeps landing in trouble. We forgive because God, for Christ's sake, has forgiven us.

Question 4 of 6

Kindness

What happens when you help someone who is poor?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the King shall answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.”

Matthew 25:40

Answer

You lend to the LORD, and He repays, in His own way and time. Most people walk away from the poor. When we stop and help, Jesus counts it as done to Him. It's not a money-back scheme. It's a promise that kindness is never wasted.

Question 5 of 6

Who God is

Whose plans stand in the end?

What verses tell us about this?

“The counsel of the LORD standeth for ever, the thoughts of his heart to all generations.”

Psalm 33:11

Answer

The LORD's. We have plenty of plans, and many of them fall apart. His counsel stands forever. That's good news for anyone who trusts Him: His plan for you leads toward life.

Question 6 of 6

Listening

Why should we take counsel and correction?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So we'll be wise in our latter end, instead of learning everything the hard way. A man with understanding takes a word of correction and grows. Getting wisdom is how you love your own soul. And part of wisdom is shutting off the voices that lead you away from the truth.
