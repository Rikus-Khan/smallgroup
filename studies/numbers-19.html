Numbers 19

The red heifer, and the water of separation

A heifer without spot is burned outside the camp, and its ashes cleanse the man who has touched the dead. Hebrews asks what that points to: “How much more shall the blood of Christ… purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?”

Part one

“A red heifer without spot”

Verses 1–10

A death outside the camp, so that the unclean could come back in.

What the LORD asked for

  • a red heifera young cow. The LORD says red, and Moses doesn’t say why
  • without spot, no blemishnothing wrong with her: no flaw, no mark
  • never came yokea yoke is the wooden beam laid on an ox’s neck to pull a plough or a cart. She had never worked
  • brought by the children of Israelthe people supplied her (verse 2); Eleazar the priest saw to her (verse 3)

Eleazar was Aaron’s son, and chapter 20 puts Aaron’s garments on him (20:26–28). The text doesn’t say why Eleazar and not Aaron does it here, and we won’t guess.

“But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:19

“Without spot” is the same call as every offering since Leviticus 1. The one who purges us is without blemish and without spot.

The red heifer, step by step

Everything is done outside the camp except the sprinkling, and that is aimed at the tent. “Seven” comes four times in the chapter: for the blood (verse 4), then for the days of uncleanness (verses 11, 14 and 16).

Cedar, hyssop and scarlet

These three things go into the fire together with the heifer. Moses doesn’t say what each means. He names the same three in Leviticus 14:4, in the cleansing of a leper, and David knew what hyssop was for:

“Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean: wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow.”

Psalm 51:7
  • hyssopa plant with soft stems, used as a brush for sprinkling (verse 18)
  • scarletred wool or cloth
  • cedar woodthe strong, sweet-smelling wood of the cedar tree

“Dung” is the same plain word it is today: even the dung is burned. Nothing of the animal is kept back, and nothing is left for anyone to point at. The whole thing is given up.

The one who handles it is made unclean

“Unclean until the even” (until the evening) comes five times in this chapter. Three are for the men who prepare the ashes: the priest (verse 7), the man who burns her (verse 8), and the man who gathers the ashes (verse 10). The other two are for the man who touches the water of separation (verse 21) and the soul that touches what the unclean man has touched (verse 22). Even the man who sprinkles the water must wash his clothes (verse 21).

The clean man who works with the ashes

Unclean until the even

He washes his clothes and bathes in water, and waits for evening. He did nothing wrong. This was the cost of helping.

The unclean man the ashes are used on

Clean at even

“And on the seventh day he shall purify himself, and wash his clothes, and bathe himself in water, and shall be clean at even.” (verse 19)

The same ashes make the clean man unclean and the unclean man clean. Someone takes on the uncleanness so that someone else can be clean.

“Much more the blood of Christ”

“For if the blood of bulls and of goats, and the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean, sanctifieth to the purifying of the flesh: How much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?”

Hebrews 9:13–14

The New Testament names it: “the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean.” They cleaned the flesh. The blood of Christ cleans the conscience, and from “dead works,” the things we did that led nowhere but to death.

“For the bodies of those beasts, whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin, are burned without the camp. Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate.”

Hebrews 13:11–12

Hebrews 13 is about the sin offering burned “without the camp,” like the heifer: Jesus suffered “without the gate” of Jerusalem. The one who makes the unclean clean is the one who stood outside.

Old words in this chapter

  • yokethe wooden beam on an ox’s neck
  • directly beforestraight in front of
  • the eventhe evening. “Unclean until the even” means until sunset
  • water of separationwater that separates a man from his uncleanness, so he can come back to the camp
  • purification for sinwhat makes a man clean again
  • perpetualnever ending

A heifer is a young cow. And whosoever toucheth means, plainly, anyone who touches.

Part two

“Whosoever toucheth the dead”

Verses 11–22

What the ashes were for.

Why the dead?

“Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:”

Romans 5:12

Death is not part of what God made. It came in by sin. So the great uncleanness in Israel was not a disease or a dirty dish: it was touching a dead man. A person who touched one had met what sin does.

And this camp was about to meet it again and again. The LORD had said of the generation that murmured, “Your carcases shall fall in this wilderness” (14:29), and in one plague alone “fourteen thousand and seven hundred” died (16:49). The water of separation was not a rare thing in this camp. It was the way back.

Seven days, and two sprinklings

  1. The deadhe touches oneunclean seven days · 11
  2. Third daysprinkled, and purifies himself12, 19
  3. Seventh daysprinkled again19
  4. Washes and batheshis clothes, himself19
  5. At evenclean19

“But if he purify not himself the third day, then the seventh day he shall not be clean” (verse 12). The two days go together. And the man did not purify himself alone: a clean person sprinkled him with the water (verse 19).

Where death was

  • In a tentevery one who goes in, and everything in it, unclean seven days (verse 14)
  • An open vessela pot with “no covering bound upon it” is unclean. A pot with its lid tied on was safe (verse 15)
  • In the open fielda man slain with a sword, or a dead body, or even “a bone of a man”
  • A grave(verse 16)

Whatever death touched was unclean, and whatever was open to it. Moses doesn’t explain the lid. But a man can see it: what is left open to death takes it in, and what is kept covered does not.

The water, and who used it

  1. The asheskept for the congregation9, 17
  2. Running waterput to them in a vessel17
  3. A clean persondips the hyssop18
  4. Sprinkledon the tent, the vessels, the persons18

Running water is water that flows, fresh from a spring or a stream, not water standing in a pool. And it was always there: the ashes were “kept for the congregation of the children of Israel” (verse 9). Nobody had to go and find the cure after the death.

The only way to stay unclean

Read verses 13 and 20 together. A man who touched the dead was unclean, and that was not his sin. What cut him off was one thing: “and purifieth not himself”, “and shall not purify himself.” The water was ready, and he would not use it.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

The cleansing is ready for every man, and what keeps him unclean is staying away from it: hiding, waiting until he feels better, thinking he is too far gone for the water. “Cleanseth us from all sin.” All of it.

Real world: when death has touched you

Many of us have buried friends. A needle, a bottle, a crash, a bad night. Chapter 19 does not tell a man he did wrong by being near death. It tells him how to come back.

  • SinDeath came into the world by sin (Romans 5:12), and some of us walked straight into it. The chapter doesn’t shame the man; it calls what touched him unclean, and then it gives the water.
  • RegretWe carry the dead: the friend we left, the one who is gone, what we did that led there. Hiding it keeps it on us.
  • GraceA heifer without spot was burned outside the camp, so that any man who touched death could be made clean. The blood of Christ purges the conscience from dead works.
  • HopeUnclean for seven days, not for ever. “And shall be clean at even.”
  • TodayDon’t sit out the seven days alone. Say it to a brother in Christ and to your pastor, and confess it to the Lord. Go to the water today.

Points to remember

  1. The heifer“a red heifer without spot, wherein is no blemish, and upon which never came yoke” 19:2The offering had to be whole and unworked. The one who purges us was without blemish and without spot.
  2. Outside the camp“bring her forth without the camp, and one shall slay her before his face” 19:3She was slain and burned outside the camp, with cedar wood, hyssop and scarlet. Jesus also “suffered without the gate.”
  3. The ashes“it shall be kept for the congregation of the children of Israel for a water of separation: it is a purification for sin” 19:9The cleansing was kept ready for the congregation, so no one had to look for it afterward.
  4. Death“He that toucheth the dead body of any man shall be unclean seven days.” 19:11Death is the great uncleanness, because it came by sin.
  5. The two days“the clean person shall sprinkle upon the unclean on the third day, and on the seventh day” 19:19Two sprinklings, and at even he was clean.
  6. The refusal“the man that shall be unclean, and shall not purify himself, that soul shall be cut off from among the congregation” 19:20What cut a man off was not touching the dead, but refusing the water.

Question 1 of 6

The heifer

What kind of heifer did the LORD say to bring?

What verses tell us about this?

“But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:19

Answer

A red heifer without spot, with no blemish in her, and one upon which never came yoke. The one who purges us is “without blemish and without spot.”

Question 2 of 6

Outside the camp

Where was the heifer slain and burned, and what was cast into the fire?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate.”

Hebrews 13:12

Answer

Without the camp. Cedar wood, hyssop and scarlet were cast into the midst of the burning. Jesus also “suffered without the gate.”

Question 3 of 6

The ashes

What was done with the ashes, and what were they for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A clean man gathered them and laid them up without the camp in a clean place, kept for the congregation “for a water of separation: it is a purification for sin.” Running water was put to the ashes in a vessel.

Question 4 of 6

Death

What made a man unclean for seven days?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Touching the dead: a dead body, a man slain in the open field, a bone, a grave, or going into a tent where a man had died (verse 14). “Death by sin” (Romans 5:12), and death made a man unclean.

Question 5 of 6

The two days

On which days was the unclean man sprinkled?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On the third day and on the seventh. On the seventh he washed his clothes and bathed in water, and was clean at even.

Question 6 of 6

The refusal

What became of the man who would not purify himself?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

That soul was “cut off” from the congregation (verses 13 and 20), because he had defiled the sanctuary of the LORD. The water was ready. He would not use it.
