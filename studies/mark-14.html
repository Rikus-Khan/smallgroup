Part one

She hath done what she could

Verses 1–11

Two days before the Passover. A plot, a broken box of perfume, and a man who sells his Master.

The last week

  1. Sunday–Tuesdayrides in, clears the temple, teaches11–13

Mark slows down here. Most Bible teachers lay out the week like this. The Passover remembered the night God brought Israel out of Egypt. Jerusalem filled with huge crowds of pilgrims, so the leaders wanted Jesus taken quietly: “Not on the feast day.”

They picked their time. God had already picked His. The Lamb would die at Passover.

Very precious

Alabaster is a soft white stone made into sealed jars. Spikenard was a perfumed oil from a plant that grows far away in the mountains of India.

She “brake the box.” You can't save any for later once the neck is snapped. She held nothing back. Some at the table called it a waste.

What she could

John tells us who she was: Mary of Bethany, the sister of Lazarus (John 12:3). She seems to have understood what the twelve didn't. He was going to die, and she anointed Him ahead of time.

“Ye have the poor with you always” isn't Jesus shrugging at the poor. He's echoing the law, which goes on to command a man to give:

“For the poor shall never cease out of the land: therefore I command thee, saying, Thou shalt open thine hand wide unto thy brother, to thy poor, and to thy needy, in thy land.”

Deuteronomy 15:11

Jesus didn't ask her to do what she couldn't. She gave what she had. And we're telling her story today, just as He said.

The woman

“she hath wrought a good work on me” (v. 6)

Judas

Judas was “one of the twelve.” He had walked with Jesus for about three years. Being close to Jesus isn't the same as belonging to Him.

Then he “sought how he might conveniently betray him.” He waited for the right moment. Sin usually does.

Part two

This is my body

Verses 12–31

The last supper in an upper room: a traitor at the table, the bread and the cup, and a promise Peter couldn't keep.

Making ready the passover

  1. The lambkilled that afternoon at the temple
  2. Unleavened breadno time for the dough to rise when they left Egypt
  3. Bitter herbsthe bitter years of slavery
  4. The cuppassed around the table as they remembered

“And the blood shall be to you for a token upon the houses where ye are: and when I see the blood, I will pass over you, and the plague shall not be upon you to destroy you, when I smite the land of Egypt.”

Exodus 12:13

Carrying water was usually women's work, so a man with a pitcher was easy to spot. Jesus knew who they'd meet and what room was ready. Nothing that night caught Him by surprise.

The twelve, one by oneIs it I? … Is it I?

JesusIt is one of the twelve, that dippeth with me in the dish.

To share a man's bread was to be his friend. David had felt the same knife: “mine own familiar friend … which did eat of my bread, hath lifted up his heel against me” (Psalm 41:9).

It was “written,” and still Jesus says, “woe to that man.” God knew it ahead of time, and Judas chose it. Both are true.

The blood of the covenant

“And Moses took the blood, and sprinkled it on the people, and said, Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD hath made with you concerning all these words.”

Exodus 24:8, at Mount Sinai

A testament is a covenant, a binding agreement. The old one was sealed with animal blood. The new one is sealed with His own.

The Lord's Supper is a memorial: “this do in remembrance of me” (1 Corinthians 11:24). The bread and the cup don't save, and don't turn into something else. They point to the One whose body and blood were given for us.

“The fruit of the vine”

The Passover cup was wine, and Jesus calls it “the fruit of the vine.” But what mattered was whom it pointed to: His blood, shed for us.

Many Baptist churches use grape juice at the Lord's Table, so a man in recovery can take it with a clear conscience. If it's ever a stumbling block for you, tell the pastor ahead of time.

And the promise: He'll drink it “new in the kingdom of God.” He's coming back. Each time we take the Supper we “shew the Lord’s death till he come” (1 Corinthians 11:26).

The hymn was likely Psalms 113–118, sung at every Passover. Jesus walked out into that night singing.

Before they fell

“Awake, O sword, against my shepherd, and against the man that is my fellow, saith the LORD of hosts: smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered: and I will turn mine hand upon the little ones.”

Zechariah 13:7

“Offended” means tripped up, falling away. Jesus tells them they'll all run. Then, in the same breath, He tells them where He'll meet them afterward.

He planned the comeback before the fall. If you've fallen, He's not done with you. He had already said where He'd be waiting.

Peter meant it. He just didn't know himself. “I'll never” may be the most dangerous words a man can say.

Part three

Not what I will

Verses 32–52

In an olive garden at night, Jesus prays alone while His closest friends sleep. Then the torches come.

The oil press

From the upper room they go out of the city and across the Kidron to an olive grove. Gethsemane means “oil press,” where olives were crushed.

There Jesus was “sore amazed” and “very heavy,” crushed under the weight of what was coming.

Before morning He'll be led back, arrested, to the high priest's house (v. 53).

Abba said AH-bah Father: the word a son at home called his dad

“This cup” is the cup of God's judgment against sin. The prophets used that picture. Jesus, who had no sin, was about to drink it for us.

He told His Father exactly what He wanted, and then gave it over: “not what I will, but what thou wilt.” That's the prayer of surrender, and every man can pray it, today.

Ready, and weak

The spirit

truly is ready

Peter really did want to stand by Jesus.

The flesh

is weak

He couldn't keep his eyes open for one hour.

Every man in recovery knows this. You can mean it with your whole heart and still go down. The answer isn't trying harder on your own. It's what Jesus said: watch, stay awake to where trouble comes from, and pray, lean on God before the moment hits.

It's the same word He used at the end of chapter 13: “Watch.” Before the night was over, they'd find out why.

Just as He said

  1. ✓One of you which eateth with me shall betray mev. 18“and kissed him” (v. 45)
  2. ✓the sheep shall be scatteredv. 27“they all forsook him, and fled” (v. 50)
  3. ✓the hour is comev. 41“they laid their hands on him” (v. 46)

A disciple greeted his teacher with a kiss. Judas turned a sign of love into the signal. The sword was Peter's, and Jesus healed the servant's ear (John 18:10, Luke 22:51).

Nobody trapped Jesus. Every piece of that night happened the way He had said it would.

A young man runs

Only Mark tells us this. Many believe the young man was Mark himself, quietly signing his work: I was there, and I ran too.

And they all forsook him, and fled.

Every last one, down to a boy who left his clothes behind. Jesus went on alone, to save the very men who ran.

Part four

He wept

Verses 53–72

Upstairs, Jesus stands before the council and tells the truth. Downstairs by the fire, Peter doesn't.

Afar off, by the fire

The high priest that year was Caiaphas (Matthew 26:57). The council was the Sanhedrin, the ruling court of the Jews, about seventy men, called together in the middle of the night.

Give Peter credit: he followed when the others ran. But he followed “afar off,” and he ended up sitting with the servants of the men who arrested Jesus, warming himself at their fire.

A man can drift that way without ever deciding to. A little distance from Jesus, a seat by the wrong fire, and he's in trouble before he knows it.

Witnesses that didn't agree

“One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity, or for any sin, in any sin that he sinneth: at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established.”

Deuteronomy 19:15

God's law needed two or three witnesses whose stories matched. They had plenty of liars, and not two who could get their stories straight.

The temple charge twisted something Jesus had said about His own body: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up” (John 2:19).

“I am”

Through all the lies, “he opened not his mouth” (Isaiah 53:7). Then the high priest asked the one question that mattered, and Jesus answered it plainly. “The Blessed” was a way of saying God without saying His name.

Psalm 110:1

“The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool.”

Daniel 7:13

“… behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven …”

He's telling the men judging Him that one day He will judge them.

His northern accent gave him away. “To curse and to swear” means he called down curses on himself if he was lying. He wasn't just cussing; he was swearing an oath.

At that very hour the guards upstairs were mocking Jesus: “Prophesy” (v. 65). Downstairs, His prophecy was coming true word for word.

Upstairs and downstairs

Jesus, before the high priest

Peter, by the fire

Mark puts the two side by side on purpose. Jesus stood firm for Peter while Peter fell apart. That's the gospel: He kept faith when we didn't.

Not the end of Peter

Judas gave up hope. Peter wept and came back. When Jesus rose, the angel said:

“But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee: there shall ye see him, as he said unto you.”

Mark 16:7

Points to remember

  1. Giving all“She hath done what she could” 14:8Jesus doesn't ask what you can't give. He honours the one who gives Him what she has.
  2. The Supper“This is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many” 14:24The bread and the cup are a memorial of His body and blood, given for us, till He comes.
  3. Prayer“Not what I will, but what thou wilt” 14:36Tell the Father plainly what you want, then hand it over to Him.

Question 1 of 6

Giving all

What did Jesus say about the woman who poured out the ointment?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That she had done a good work on Him, that she had done what she could, and that she had anointed His body ahead of His burial. Wherever the gospel goes, her story is told.

Question 2 of 6

The Supper

What do the bread and the cup point to?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To His body given and His blood shed for many, the blood of the new covenant. We take them in remembrance of Him. They don't save us; His death does.

Question 3 of 6

Prayer

How did Jesus pray when the cup was in front of Him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On His face, honestly, calling God “Abba, Father.” He asked for the cup to pass if it were possible, and then surrendered: “not what I will, but what thou wilt.”

Question 4 of 6

Temptation

What did Jesus tell His sleepy men to do about temptation?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Watch and pray. The spirit may be willing, but the flesh is weak. A man doesn't beat temptation by meaning well. He stays awake to it, and leans on God in prayer before the moment comes.

Question 5 of 6

Who He is

What did Jesus answer when the high priest asked if He was the Christ?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“I am.” He said plainly He is the Christ, the Son of God, and that they would see Him at God's right hand, coming in the clouds. The prisoner on trial is the Judge of all.

Question 6 of 6

Failure

How did Peter fall, and what did he do when he remembered?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Step by step: sure of himself, following afar off, sitting at the wrong fire, then three denials. When the cock crew he remembered Jesus' word and wept. His tears turned him back, and Jesus sent for him by name.
