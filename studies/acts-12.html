Acts 12

Peter in prison, and the church on its knees

A king kills one apostle and chains another, the church prays all night, and the chains fall off. A hard chapter, told honestly.

Part one

Herod strikes

Verses 1–5

A king who wants to be liked hurts the church, and the church does the only thing it can.

James, and a question with no answer

James and his brother John were two of the first men Jesus called. Years earlier Jesus had told them what lay ahead:

“And they said unto him, We can. And Jesus said unto them, Ye shall indeed drink of the cup that I drink of; and with the baptism that I am baptized withal shall ye be baptized:”

Mark 10:39
James · verse 2the swordThe first of the twelve to die for Jesus.
Peter · verse 7the angelSet free the night before his trial.
Same Lord. Same church. Same prayers (verse 5).

Why James died and Peter lived, the chapter doesn't say. Luke tells it in one short sentence and moves on, and we should be honest and not make up an answer. This is a hard thing. But James didn't die forgotten: “Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of his saints” (Psalm 116:15). And a man in recovery knows life doesn't hand out explanations. We trust God anyway.

Three Herods

“Herod” was a family name, like “Caesar.” This one was Herod Agrippa I, king over the Jews from about AD 41 to 44. His family had a long record:

Herod the Greattried to kill the baby Jesus (Matthew 2)
his son Herod Antipasbeheaded John the Baptist
his grandson Herod Agrippakilled James, arrested Peter

Look at his reason: “because he saw it pleased the Jews.” Not a conviction, only a popularity poll. A man who lives to please the crowd will do whatever the crowd wants, and that's a dangerous way to run a life, a country, or a life.

Sixteen soldiers for one man

four squads (“quaternions”) of four soldiers each, one squad for each of the four night watches

Herod took no chances. Two soldiers were chained to Peter and two more stood at the door, with fresh squads every watch. After James, he knew exactly what he was doing, and the plan was a public trial after the feast.

One word needs a fix. The King James says “Easter,” but this is the Jewish feast of Passover (verse 3 says “the days of unleavened bread”). The Greek word is pascha. Herod was waiting for the festival to end so he could put Peter on display.

Peter, then and now

Afraid of a servant girl → Asleep in chains Peter in the courtyard (Luke 22:56–57) and Peter in the prison (Acts 12:6).

This is the same Peter who denied Jesus. Whatever happened between that courtyard and this cell, it changed him: restored, forgiven, and handed a job to do. A man who's been forgiven can sleep, even in chains.

“It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep.”

Psalm 127:2

And note who was awake: the church. Prayer was made “without ceasing.” Peter slept while others prayed. You don't always have to carry it yourself. Some nights somebody else will carry you.

Part two

The night of the chains

Verses 6–19

A light in a cell, an iron gate that opens by itself, and a knock at the wrong door.

Chains, light, and freedom

Verse 6Dark

Asleep, chained to two soldiers, with guards at the door.

Verse 7Light

“A light shined in the prison.” The angel strikes Peter on the side. The chains fall off.

Verse 11Free

“Now I know of a surety, that the Lord hath sent his angel.”

The angel doesn't hurry him. “Gird thyself,” belt your robe. “Bind on thy sandals.” “Cast thy garment about thee.” Get dressed, Peter. God breaks the chains, and then He expects the man to stand up and walk.

Nobody wakes the soldiers. Nobody rushes. The One who opened Peter's chains is the One who knew exactly how long it would take a half-asleep man to find his shoes.

Out past every guard

Peter, chained between two soldiers, had a ward on either side and an iron gate beyond them. The prison was built to keep a man in.

“Wist not that it was true.” Peter thought he was dreaming. The gate opened “of his own accord,” by itself, and one street later the angel was gone and Peter was alone in the dark, wide awake, free.

“Peter was come to himself.” It dawned on him: this is real. Some of the best news a man can get seems too good to be true. It isn't. “The Lord hath sent his angel, and hath delivered me.”

Prayer, and the answer at the door

Verse 5The church prays without ceasing
Verse 7God sends the angel
Verse 12Peter walks to where they're praying
Verse 13He knocks

The believers met at the home of Mary, the mother of John Mark. John Mark, by long tradition, wrote the Gospel of Mark. We'll see him again.

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.”

James 5:16

Nobody there was a great pray-er. They were a house full of frightened people doing the one thing they could. Today, pick a name and pray for him.

“Thou art mad”

RhodaPeter is standing at the gate!
so glad she never opened it

The churchThou art mad.
praying for exactly this

The churchIt is his angel.
anything but Peter, though she constantly affirmed it

They'd been praying for this man, and when he showed up they didn't believe it. Only Rhoda, a servant girl, did. God answered a prayer its own people couldn't quite believe, and that's grace. He doesn't wait for perfect faith. He hears the shaky kind, too.

“It is his angel” fit what people believed then, that God sends angels to look after His own (Psalm 34:7). Peter just kept knocking, and when they opened the door they were astonished. Then he sent word to James, the Lord's brother, who led the church in Jerusalem, and slipped away to “another place.”

The cost nobody counted

Peter walked free. His guards did not.

Herod searched, found nothing, and had the guards killed. That's the other side of a rescue. A cruel man answered failure with execution, and men who'd only been doing their job paid for it.

Luke doesn't dress it up, and neither should we. This isn't God's doing. It's what Herod does, and it's why a king like this ends the way he does. God can free a man from a prison and still not undo every wrong that a wicked ruler does. That's why the Bible keeps pointing us toward a day when He will.

Part three

Herod's last day

Verses 20–25

A hard text, plainly told: what happens to a man who takes the glory that belongs to God.

Why Tyre and Sidon came begging

Tyre and Sidon were rich port cities with little farmland of their own. Their food came from the king's country. When Herod got angry with them, they had good reason to be afraid.

They won over Blastus, the king's chamberlain, and asked for peace. Herod received them at Caesarea, his seaside capital. With famine on the way (Acts 11:28), the king held the grain.

“The voice of a god”

Herod put on royal robes and gave a speech, and the crowd began to chant. A man killed an apostle, locked up another, and then took worship. Compare what others did when they were offered it:

  1. Peter“Stand up; I myself also am a man.” Acts 10:26
  2. Paul and Barnabastore their clothes: “We also are men of like passions with you.” Acts 14:14–15
  3. Herodtook the shout, and “gave not God the glory.” Acts 12:23

The Jewish historian Josephus tells much the same story: a festival at Caesarea, a robe that shone in the sun, the crowd calling him a god, and a painful death within days, about AD 44. “Eaten of worms” means a slow, painful, wasting death. Luke doesn't say more, and we don't need to.

“I am the LORD: that is my name: and my glory will I not give to another, neither my praise to graven images.”

Isaiah 42:8

A hard text, told honestly

Herod

A king who took the glory

He killed James, jailed Peter, executed the guards, and took the worship that belongs to God. Nobody pushed him into it, and nothing in the chapter says he stopped.

A believer who stumbles

A man who falls and gets up

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” (1 John 1:9). “There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus” (Romans 8:1).

If you're afraid this verse is about you because of yesterday's mistake, it isn't. God doesn't strike a believer dead for stumbling. Herod's story is about pride at its worst: a man who let himself be called God.

“Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?”

Ezekiel 33:11

God is not eager to judge. He said so. The cure for pride is to say, “I can't do this alone,” and to give God the glory. That is what a man in recovery has already learned to say.

Kings die. The word grows.

Herod's name ends. The word of God grew.

A king with an army and a prison couldn't stop it. He killed an apostle, and the church grew anyway. In a few verses he's gone, and “the word of God grew and multiplied.” Whatever is pressing on your life today, it's smaller than it looks.

Barnabas and Saul came back from Jerusalem with the relief money from Acts 11:30, “when they had fulfilled their ministry,” and brought a young man with them: John Mark.

John Mark: a second chance

  1. Acts 12:12his mother's house, full of people praying
  2. Acts 12:25goes along with Barnabas and Saul
  3. Acts 13:13turns back and goes home to Jerusalema failure
  4. 2 Timothy 4:11Paul asks for him by namerestored

“Only Luke is with me. Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry.”

2 Timothy 4:11

Mark quit on the first trip, and Paul refused to take him on the second (Acts 15:38). But near the end of his life Paul asked for him by name. By long tradition this is the same Mark who wrote the Gospel.

You can walk away, fail, quit, and still be a man God uses. Don't stay where you stopped.

Points to remember

  1. Hard things“He killed James the brother of John with the sword.” 12:2Some things have no explanation. God is still good, and He still counts His saints precious.
  2. Prayer“Prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him.” 12:5Pray for somebody by name today. It does something.
  3. Peace“Peter was sleeping between two soldiers.” 12:6A forgiven man can sleep, even in chains. Somebody else is awake praying.
  4. God's power“His chains fell off from his hands.” 12:7God breaks the chains and the gates, and then He says, “Get up and follow me.”
  5. Weak faith“They said unto her, Thou art mad.” 12:15God answers shaky faith. He doesn't wait for perfect.
  6. Pride“Because he gave not God the glory.” 12:23Give God the glory, and say “I can't do this alone.” That's the opposite of Herod.

Question 1 of 6

Hard things

What happened to James, and what did the church do about Peter?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Herod killed James with the sword, then arrested Peter. The church did the one thing it could: they prayed, without ceasing. The chapter never explains why James died and Peter lived. It doesn't have to. The Lord's saints are precious to Him either way.

Question 2 of 6

Prayer

What was the church doing while Peter sat in prison?

What verses tell us about this?

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.”

James 5:16

Answer

Praying, together, at night, at Mary's house. They weren't a powerful group. They had no way to get Peter out. All they could do was ask God, and God did the rest.

Question 3 of 6

Peace

How could Peter sleep with his trial the next morning?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep.”

Psalm 127:2

Answer

The chapter doesn't tell us what Peter was thinking. But this is a man who had once denied Jesus, been forgiven, and been given a job. He was in God's hands, and the church was praying. The Lord gives His beloved sleep.

Question 4 of 6

God's power

Who opened Peter's chains and the iron gate?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The Lord, by His angel. The chains fell off, two wards and an iron gate were no barrier, and Peter said it himself: “Now I know of a surety, that the Lord hath sent his angel, and hath delivered me.” Nothing a man can build will hold out against God.

Question 5 of 6

Weak faith

What did the church do when their prayer was answered?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

At first, they didn't believe it. They told Rhoda she was mad, and then guessed it was his angel. When they finally opened the door they were astonished. God answered anyway. He hears the shaky kind of faith, too.

Question 6 of 6

Pride

Why did God strike Herod down?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The verse says why: “because he gave not God the glory.” Herod took the worship that belongs to God alone, after killing one apostle and jailing another. This is judgment on a proud tyrant, not a warning to a struggling believer. Meanwhile the word of God grew and multiplied.
