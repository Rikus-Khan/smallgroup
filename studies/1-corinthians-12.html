1 Corinthians 12

One body, many members

A church that showed off its gifts learns where they come from, what they're for, and why the weakest man in the room can't be spared.

Where we are in 1 Corinthians

Paul wrote this letter to fix real problems, one at a time. Chapters 1–6 answer what he'd heard. From chapter 7 he answers what they had asked, and each new question starts the same way: “Now concerning…”

We're in the middle of a question about worship. Chapter 11 dealt with how they came together. Now: the gifts of the Spirit, and the showing off that came with them.

A question crosses the sea

The church at Corinth had written Paul a letter of questions. He was across the Aegean at Ephesus, about AD 55, and he works through them one by one.

“Now concerning the things whereof ye wrote unto me…”

1 Corinthians 7:1

Chapter 12 opens with the next one: “Now concerning spiritual gifts.” Their meetings were loud, and some men treated the flashiest gift as proof they were the most spiritual. Paul's answer sails back.

Three chapters, one answer

Chapters 12, 13 and 14 are one argument. Read today's chapter knowing where it's going.

chapter 12 · todayEvery man has a giftgiven to every man to profit withal
chapter 13Love is greater than any giftthe greatest of these is charity
chapter 14Use them in orderLet all things be done decently and in order.

one answer to one question: “Now concerning spiritual gifts” (12:1)

Paul isn't against the gifts. God gave them. He is against a church using God's gifts to rank its people. Chapter 12 levels the room: one Spirit gave every gift, and every man got one.

Part one

Who has the Spirit?

Verses 1–3

Before Paul talks about gifts, he settles who the Giver lives in.

Carried away, as ye were led

What you were

Led around by dumb idols

Dumb means unable to speak. Corinth was full of temples, and their gods never said a word. Worship there could be wild and frenzied, and a man got swept along with the crowd.

What you are now

Led by the Spirit to say “Jesus is the Lord”

Not dragged, not swept away. The Spirit of God leads a man to Christ, with his mind awake.

Most of us know what it is to be carried away: a bottle, a pipe, a crowd, a habit that led us wherever it wanted. That's the old life. Something that runs you and never once speaks a true word to you. You are not that man anymore.

The test is Jesus

The Corinthians wanted to measure the Spirit by noise and show. Paul gives a different test: what does a man say about Jesus? Nobody speaking by God's Spirit curses Him. And nobody can truly say “Jesus is the Lord”, and mean it, unless the Holy Ghost is at work in him.

“But ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his.”

Romans 8:9

So if you have turned to Jesus as your Lord, the Spirit of God lives in you. Right now, today, on a bad day too. You don't need a louder experience to prove it. Every believer in that messy church had Him, and so do you.

Part two

Same Spirit, many gifts

Verses 4–11

Different gifts, one Giver. And not one man left out.

Many gifts, one God

Three times Paul says “different,” and three times “the same.” Spirit, Lord and God: the whole Trinity stands behind every gift.

Administrations are ways of serving. Operations are workings: what actually gets done. Nobody's gift is his own invention, so nobody's gift is a reason to look down on another man.

Given to every man, for the good of all.

  • a trophysomething to show you're more spiritual
  • a rewardsomething you earned by getting clean first
  • for a special fewthe preacher, the talented, the ones with a clean past
  • a tool, for the good of the others“to profit withal”: to be useful with it

A manifestation is a showing: the Spirit shows Himself through what He does in you. Withal means “with it.” He gives you something so the rest of the body can profit by it.

The nine gifts, plainly

Knowing

  • The word of wisdomknowing what to do, and saying it at the right time
  • The word of knowledgeunderstanding God's truth, and passing it on
  • Discerning of spiritstelling what is from God from what is not

Power

  • Faitha special trust for great things. Not saving faith: every believer has that.
  • Gifts of healingthe sick made well by God's power
  • Working of miraclesacts of power only God can do

Speaking

  • Prophecyspeaking a message from God that builds up, urges on and comforts (14:3)
  • Divers kinds of tonguesspeaking a real language never learned, as at Pentecost (Acts 2:6–8)
  • Interpretation of tonguesmaking that language plain to the hearers

This is not every gift. Romans 12 and Ephesians 4 name others, like teaching, giving, showing mercy and leading. Notice where tongues come: near the end. The Corinthians had put it first.

Where good teachers differ

Where they differ

Some gifts here, like miracles, healings and tongues, were signs. Many Baptists believe these sign gifts confirmed the apostles' message in the church's first days and passed away once the New Testament was complete. Other good Bible teachers believe God may still give them. Godly men stand on both sides.

What is plain

  • Every believer has the Spirit, from the day he believed.
  • Every believer has a gift: “to every man.”
  • No gift makes a man more spiritual than his brother.
  • God still hears and answers prayer for the sick.
  • Every gift is for the good of others, used in love (chapter 13) and in order (chapter 14).

Don't let this question split a group of brothers. The Corinthians fought over gifts, and that fight was the very thing Paul was writing to stop.

As He will

Severally means one by one, to each man his own. And “as he will”: you don't pick your gift or earn it. You can't lose your place in line by your past, because there was never a line. The Spirit chose what to hand you.

“But unto every one of us is given grace according to the measure of the gift of Christ.”

Ephesians 4:7

Part three

One body, many members

Verses 12–20

The foot that wanted to quit

Picture it: the foot looks at the hand, which gets to shake hands and do fine work, and decides, “I am not of the body.”

Then the ear looks at the eye and says the same thing. Paul's answer both times: “is it therefore not of the body?” Feeling left out doesn't make it so.

This is the man who says, “I'm not like those guys. I can't pray like him or read like him. I don't really belong here.” Brother, you're still of the body. Comparing yourself doesn't cut you off. God put you there.

Set there on purpose

If the whole body were an eye“where were the hearing?”
→
if they were all one member“where were the body?”

A body that's all eye is not a body. It's a giant eyeball that can't walk, hear or eat. A church where everybody has the same gift is just as useless. God wants the differences.

“God set the members… as it hath pleased him.” Where you are in the body is not an accident and not second best. It pleased God to put you there. That includes your story, the parts you're ashamed of too, which may be exactly what makes you useful to the next man coming in.

Part four

I have no need of thee?

Verses 21–31

The proud member's mistake, and the part of the body you can't live without.

Two lies a member can tell

The foot · verse 15“I am not of the body”

The lie of the man who feels like nothing. He thinks he's too far down to matter.

The eye · verse 21“I have no need of thee”

The lie of the man who thinks he's something. He thinks he's too far up to need anybody.

One looks down on himself, the other looks down on his brother. Both are pride turned a different way, and both are answered the same way: God put the body together, and He doesn't make spare parts.

Sound familiar? Some days a man swings between the two: “I'm worthless” in the morning and “I don't need anybody” by night. Neither one is true of a man in Christ.

The parts nobody sees

Everybody sees the eye and the hand. A man can lose an eye or a hand and live.

Now look inside. The heart and lungs are hidden, soft, easy to hurt. They “seem to be more feeble.” And you can't live one minute without them.

No man in this group is useless. The brother who never speaks but is always here, who sets out the chairs, who prays where nobody hears: he's the heart of this thing. God “tempered” (blended) the body to give the hidden parts the most honour.

One hurts, all hurt

Stub one toe and the whole man doubles over. Nobody says, “That's the toe's problem.” A schism is a split, a tear down the middle.

It works the other way too. When one brother gets a year sober, a job, his kids back, the whole body gets to rejoice. His win isn't a threat to you. It's yours too.

When a brother falls, the body hurts. So don't gossip about him. Go and get him. “Ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular”: not the church in general. You, by name.

Are all…? No.

“Are all apostles?”No
“are all prophets?”No
“are all teachers?”No
“are all workers of miracles?”No
“Have all the gifts of healing?”No
“do all speak with tongues?”No
“do all interpret?”No

In Paul's Greek every question expects “No.” No one gift belongs to everybody, so no one gift proves a man has the Spirit. Apostles were men sent out by the risen Christ Himself. Helps are helping hands. Governments is a word for steering, like a ship's pilot: men who guide the church. Helps sit right in the list beside the miracles.

A more excellent way.

Covet earnestly here means want it with all your heart. Want the gifts that do the most good for others, not the ones that get the most attention. And then Paul says there's something better than any gift, and he spends the whole next chapter on it: love.

Today: ask God plainly, “What have You given me for these men?” Then do one useful thing for a brother before the day is out: a call, a ride, a word, a hand. That's the body working.

Points to remember

  1. The Spirit“No man can say that Jesus is the Lord, but by the Holy Ghost” 12:3If Jesus is your Lord, the Spirit lives in you. No louder proof is needed.
  2. Every man“Given to every man to profit withal” 12:7Every believer has a gift, chosen by the Spirit, for the good of the others.
  3. One Spirit“By one Spirit are we all baptized into one body” 12:13Every believer, at salvation. Not a second experience for a few.
  4. Not of the body?“Is it therefore not of the body?” 12:15Feeling left out doesn't cut you off. God set you in the body on purpose.
  5. The feeble“Which seem to be more feeble, are necessary” 12:22The hidden, quiet members are the ones the body can't live without.
  6. One care“All the members suffer with it” 12:26When one brother hurts, we all hurt. When one is honoured, we all rejoice.

Question 1 of 6

The Spirit

How can a man tell that the Spirit of God is in him?

What verses tell us about this?

“But ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his.”

Romans 8:9

“The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God:”

Romans 8:16

Answer

By what he says about Jesus. Nobody can truly call Jesus Lord except by the Holy Ghost, and every man who belongs to Christ has the Spirit of Christ. It isn't measured by noise or feelings.

Question 2 of 6

Every man

Who gets a gift from the Spirit, and what is it for?

What verses tell us about this?

“But unto every one of us is given grace according to the measure of the gift of Christ.”

Ephesians 4:7

Answer

Every believer, each one his own, as the Spirit chooses. It isn't earned and it isn't a trophy. It is given “to profit withal”: so the rest of the body is helped by it.

Question 3 of 6

One Spirit

When was every believer baptized by the Spirit into the body?

What verses tell us about this?

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

Answer

At salvation, the moment he believed. Paul says “we all,” about a church full of immature believers. It is not a second blessing for a few, and not the water of baptism, which pictures it. Every believer is in the body of Christ.

Question 4 of 6

Not of the body?

What do the foot and the ear say, and how does Paul answer them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They compare themselves with the hand and the eye and decide they don't belong. Paul answers: is it therefore not of the body? Of course it is. God set every member in the body as it pleased Him. Comparing yourself doesn't cut you off.

Question 5 of 6

The feeble

Which members of the body does Paul call necessary?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The ones that seem more feeble. The proud member can't say “I have no need of thee,” because God put the body together and gave the most honour to the parts that lacked it. Like the heart and lungs, the quiet, hidden members are the ones the body can't live without.

Question 6 of 6

One care

What happens to the whole body when one member suffers, or is honoured?

What verses tell us about this?

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Answer

All the members suffer with it, or all rejoice with it. God made the body that way so there would be no split, and every member would care for the others. A brother's fall is our pain, and his win is our joy.
