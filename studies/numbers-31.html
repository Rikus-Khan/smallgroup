Numbers 31

Not one man lacking

Moses' last battle. The LORD's judgment falls on Midian for the snare at Peor, Balaam dies by the sword, the men of war are made clean, the spoil is shared, and the captains count their men and find every one of them alive.

Part one

“Avenge the LORD of Midian”

Verses 1–12

Twelve thousand men go out, and Balaam is found among the dead.

One more command, and then home

This war was not Israel's idea. After Peor the LORD had already said it:

“Vex the Midianites, and smite them: For they vex you with their wiles, wherewith they have beguiled you in the matter of Peor, and in the matter of Cozbi, the daughter of a prince of Midian, their sister, which was slain in the day of the plague for Peor’s sake.”

Numbers 25:17–18

Now it is time, and it is Moses' last piece of work. “Afterward shalt thou be gathered unto thy people.” He has already been told he will see the land from the mountain and not go in (27:12–13). He doesn't argue, and he doesn't drag his feet. The next verse, he is giving orders.

Gathered unto thy people is not the language of a man who simply stops. It is the language of a man going to join those who went before. For a believer, the end of the work is not the end of the man.

12,000out of 601,730 men of war just numbered (26:51): about one man in fifty

The LORD says, “Avenge the children of Israel.” Moses says, “avenge the LORD.” It is the same war, and Moses names it rightly: Midian's sin at Peor was first against God. Avenge means to see a wrong put right by judgment. This was the LORD's quarrel, carried out at his word, not a grudge.

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

A priest goes with the army

Moses doesn't send a general at the head. He sends Phinehas, the priest's son, the man who stood up for the LORD at Peor and stopped the plague (25:7–8, 11). The holy instruments go with him (Moses doesn't list them), and the silver trumpets, as the LORD had ordered back at Sinai:

“And if ye go to war in your land against the enemy that oppresseth you, then ye shall blow an alarm with the trumpets; and ye shall be remembered before the LORD your God, and ye shall be saved from your enemies.”

Numbers 10:9

The message to every man in the ranks: this is God's battle, fought in God's presence. Twelve thousand were enough, because they weren't the ones who would win it.

Balaam's last end

What Balaam wished, Numbers 23:10

“Let me die the death of the righteous, and let my last end be like his!”

How Balaam died, Numbers 31:8

“Balaam also the son of Beor they slew with the sword.”

Balaam went home after the blessings (24:25), but he did not stay home. He was found among the Midianites, beside five kings: Evi, Rekem, Zur, Hur and Reba. Zur was the father of Cozbi, the woman slain at Peor (25:15). Balaam had thrown in his lot with Israel's enemies, and he fell with them.

“Which have forsaken the right way, and are gone astray, following the way of Balaam the son of Bosor, who loved the wages of unrighteousness;”

2 Peter 2:15

Wanting to die the death of the righteous is not enough. You have to live on the LORD's side to die on it.

Brought back to the camp

  • spoilgoods taken in war
  • preywhat was captured, of men and of beasts
  • castleshere, their walled strongholds, the fortified camps they lived in

Nothing was kept back in a tent. Everything taken was brought “unto Moses, and Eleazar the priest, and unto the congregation”, to the camp in the plains of Moab, across the Jordan from Jericho, where Israel had been since 22:1. What happened to it next was not up to the men who took it. The LORD would say.

Part two

“Abide without the camp seven days”

Verses 13–24

Moses is angry, and then the soldiers are made clean before they come home.

What Balaam couldn't curse, he tried to corrupt

Now we learn what Balaam did after he “returned to his place.” He couldn't curse Israel; God turned every curse into a blessing. So he gave Balak another plan: don't fight them, draw them in. Send the women of Midian and Moab, and invite Israel to the feasts of their gods.

“And Israel abode in Shittim, and the people began to commit whoredom with the daughters of Moab. And they called the people unto the sacrifices of their gods: and the people did eat, and bowed down to their gods.”

Numbers 25:1–2

“But I have a few things against thee, because thou hast there them that hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balac to cast a stumblingblock before the children of Israel, to eat things sacrificed unto idols, and to commit fornication.”

Revelation 2:14

It worked where the curses failed. Twenty and four thousand died in the plague (25:9). That is why Moses was wroth (very angry) to see the same women brought back into the camp. Many of us know this plan. The enemy that can't take you by force will try to take you by invitation.

A hard sentence

This is one of the hardest commands in the Bible to read, and we shouldn't pretend otherwise. It was a sentence of judgment from the LORD on one people, at one time, for a sin that had already killed twenty and four thousand in Israel. The grown women, by whom the snare at Peor was laid, were put to death, and the boys with them; the young girls were spared and brought into Israel.

“That be far from thee to do after this manner, to slay the righteous with the wicked: and that the righteous should be as the wicked, that be far from thee: Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?”

Genesis 18:25

God is the judge, and his judgments are right even when they are heavy. But this was never handed to us as a way to fight. A Christian's war is a different kind:

“For though we walk in the flesh, we do not war after the flesh: (For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds;)”

2 Corinthians 10:3–4

The war was the LORD's own command, and still every man who had killed or touched the dead was unclean. Death defiles. They waited outside the camp seven days, washed on the third day and the seventh with the water of separation made from the red heifer's ashes (19:9, 12), and only then came home. Abide the fire means bear it without burning up.

“That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ:”

1 Peter 1:7

Coming home clean

These men came back from a hard place with things on them that didn't belong in the camp. They weren't sent away for good. They were given a way to be clean, and a day when they could walk back in.

Some of you have come back from years in places you'd rather not name, and you carry them. The Bible doesn't tell you to pretend you weren't there. It tells you there is a washing that reaches all the way down:

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

And notice where it puts you after the washing: in fellowship, “one with another”, back inside the camp with your brothers.

Part three

“Divide the prey into two parts”

Verses 25–47

The fighters and the people share alike, and the LORD has his part from both.

Half and half

  • levy a tributeraise a set share, a tax
  • one soul of five hundredone out of every five hundred
  • beevesoxen, cattle
  • heave offeringa portion lifted up to the LORD, then given to the priest

Twelve thousand fighting men took half. The whole congregation, who stayed in the camp, took the other half. The LORD took his part from both: one in five hundred from the soldiers' half for Eleazar the priest, one in fifty from the people's half for the Levites.

The men who went out didn't keep it all, and the men who stayed home didn't go without. David made the same rule after a battle of his own:

“For who will hearken unto you in this matter? but as his part is that goeth down to the battle, so shall his part be that tarrieth by the stuff: they shall part alike.”

1 Samuel 30:24

Work it out and every figure Moses wrote agrees: the two halves make the whole, and each tribute is exactly one in five hundred. (The Levites' column in italics is our own arithmetic; Moses gives the rule, “one portion of fifty”, not the totals.)

Four times in this chapter: “as the LORD commanded Moses” (31:7, 31, 41, 47). The plunder of a war was handled to the last ass by God's word. Nothing about a man's money or goods is outside it.

Part four

“There lacketh not one man of us”

Verses 48–54

The captains count their men, and come to Moses with gold.

Twelve thousand out, twelve thousand home

Judah1,000Issachar1,000Zebulun1,000 Reuben1,000Simeon1,000Gad1,000 Ephraim1,000Manasseh1,000Benjamin1,000 Dan1,000Asher1,000Naphtali1,000

The officers took the count and it came out the same: “there lacketh not one man of us.” Lacketh: is missing. They didn't boast about it. They knew who had kept them, and they brought gold to say so.

Why “an atonement for our souls”? The captains don't explain. But they had just numbered their men, and the law said that when Israel was numbered, every man was to give “a ransom for his soul unto the LORD … that there be no plague among them” (Exodus 30:12). After Peor's plague, no one wanted to take that lightly.

“That the saying might be fulfilled, which he spake, Of them which thou gavest me have I lost none.”

John 18:9

A memorial before the LORD

  • oblationa gift offered to God
  • tabletshere, gold ornaments worn on the body
  • wrought jewelsgold worked into jewellery
  • memorialsomething kept so a thing is not forgotten

16,750 shekels of gold, from the captains alone. The common soldiers kept what they took (verse 53). The officers, who answered for other men's lives, gave theirs back to God. And Moses didn't melt it into something for himself. He put it in the tabernacle, “for a memorial for the children of Israel before the LORD.”

A memorial is for the forgetful, and that's all of us. The day God brought you through, the day nobody was lost, is worth marking.

“Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits:”

Psalm 103:2

Real world: the snare and the count

Midian never came at Israel with swords. It came with an invitation. The chapter that closes that story closes with every man accounted for.

  • SinPeor: a trap laid by a man who couldn't curse Israel, so he tempted them. The enemy still works that way.
  • RegretTwenty and four thousand died for one season of giving in. Sin always costs more than it shows.
  • GraceA way to be clean and come back into the camp; a ransom for the soul; “I have lost none.”
  • HopeThe war is the LORD's. Twelve thousand went, and twelve thousand came home.
  • TodayName your Peor: the place, the people, the invitation that gets you. Tell a brother today, and stay out of it.

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall. There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:12–13

Question 1 of 6

Moses' last work

What did the LORD tell Moses would come after this war?

What verses tell us about this?

“And when thou hast seen it, thou also shalt be gathered unto thy people, as Aaron thy brother was gathered.”

Numbers 27:13

Answer

That afterward he would be gathered unto his people: this was his last work before he died. He obeyed at once.

Question 2 of 6

The LORD's quarrel

Why did the LORD send Israel against Midian?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For Peor. By Balaam's counsel, Midian drew Israel into whoredom and idolatry, and twenty and four thousand died in the plague. It was the LORD's judgment, at his command.

Question 3 of 6

Balaam's end

How did Balaam die, and what had he done to Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

“But I have a few things against thee, because thou hast there them that hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balac to cast a stumblingblock before the children of Israel, to eat things sacrificed unto idols, and to commit fornication.”

Revelation 2:14

Answer

He was slain with the sword among the Midianites. He could not curse Israel, so he taught Balak to lay a stumblingblock: idol feasts and fornication.

Question 4 of 6

Clean again

What did the men of war have to do before they came back into the camp?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Stay outside the camp seven days, be purified on the third and seventh day, put metal through the fire and everything through the water, wash their clothes, and then come in.

Question 5 of 6

The LORD's share

How was the prey divided, and what went to the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Half to the men who fought, half to the congregation. One in five hundred of the soldiers' half went to Eleazar the priest, and one in fifty of the people's half to the Levites.

Question 6 of 6

Not one man

What did the captains find when they counted their men, and what did they bring?

What verses tell us about this?

“That the saying might be fulfilled, which he spake, Of them which thou gavest me have I lost none.”

John 18:9

Answer

Not one man was missing. They brought their gold, 16,750 shekels, as an offering to the LORD, and Moses laid it up in the tabernacle as a memorial.
