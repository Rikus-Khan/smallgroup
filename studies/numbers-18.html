Numbers 18

“I am thy part and thine inheritance”

The people have just cried, “we die, we perish.” The LORD answers with men to stand between them and the holy things: priests and Levites, given as a gift. And he tells the men who get no land what they get instead: himself.

Things to watch for

Part one

“Bear the iniquity of the sanctuary”

Verses 1–7

The people were afraid to come near. The LORD names the men who will stand in between.

After “we die, we perish”

In chapter 17 Aaron’s rod budded among the twelve rods (17:8), and the people were frightened. This is what they said:

“And the children of Israel spake unto Moses, saying, Behold, we die, we perish, we all perish. Whosoever cometh any thing near unto the tabernacle of the LORD shall die: shall we be consumed with dying?”

Numbers 17:12–13

Chapter 18 is the LORD’s answer. He does not say, “Stay away, then.” He says, “I have given you men.” Verse 5 gives the purpose: “that there be no wrath any more upon the children of Israel.” A holy God, a people who could not safely come near, and a way provided between them.

The tent, and who stood where

The tabernacle of witness, in its court, with the camp pitched round it. The altar stood in the court. “Within the vail” (verse 7) is the most holy place at the far end, where the ark was.

Three kinds of men, three places

Aaron and his sons

17

“Every thing of the altar, and within the vail.” The priests’ office, “as a service of gift.”

The Levites

234

“Joined unto thee”: they keep the charge of the tabernacle and its service, but “not come nigh the vessels of the sanctuary and the altar, that neither they, nor ye also, die.”

The stranger

47

“Shall not come nigh.” The man outside the tribe and the family the LORD set apart, “shall be put to death.”

Charge here means what a man is put in charge of: his duty. The Levites carried and guarded the tent. They did not touch its holiest furniture, and the priests did not hand their work to anyone else. Everyone had a place, and the place kept him alive.

Who bears it?

The word bear comes five times in this chapter (twice in verse 1, then verses 22, 23 and 32), and every time it is about sin.

  • Verse 1Aaron, his sons and his father’s house “shall bear the iniquity of the sanctuary”, and he and his sons “the iniquity of your priesthood.” If a fault was made in the holy service, it fell on them.
  • Verse 22The children of Israel must not come nigh “lest they bear sin, and die.”
  • Verse 23“The Levites shall do the service of the tabernacle… and they shall bear their iniquity.”
  • Verse 32The Levites “shall bear no sin by reason of it” if they do the tithe the way the LORD said.

Iniquity is sin. These men stood where a mistake could cost a life, and the LORD set it on them to answer for it, so that the rest of Israel would not have to.

The one who bore it all

“All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.”

Isaiah 53:6

“Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed.”

1 Peter 2:24

The priests bore the sin of the sanctuary, and could fail. Christ bore ours, and did not. Whatever you carry from yesterday, he has already carried it to the tree.

Stay back, or come boldly

Under the law

Numbers 18:22

“Neither must the children of Israel henceforth come nigh the tabernacle of the congregation, lest they bear sin, and die.”

In Christ

Hebrews 10:19, 22

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus… Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.”

The priests and Levites were a mercy, and they still kept the people at a distance. The priests were men with failings of their own. Ours opened the way into the holiest itself, by his own blood, and bids us draw near. A man who fell yesterday can come to him today.

Part two

“Every thing devoted in Israel shall be thine”

Verses 8–19

The priests had no land. They were fed from the altar.

Fed from the altar

  • The most holy thingsWhat was not burned: the meat offering, the sin offering, the trespass offering. Only the males of the priests’ house ate them, “in the most holy place” (verses 9–10).
  • The heave and wave offeringsThe priests’ whole household, sons and daughters, if “clean in thy house” (verse 11).
  • The best of the oil, the wine and the wheatThe firstfruits, “the best” of every harvest (verse 12).
  • Whatsoever is first ripeThe first of the land (verse 13).

The word given comes ten times in this chapter. The Levites are given, the priest’s office is given, the offerings are given, the tithe is given. Not one of these men earned his place by his own work. It was all a gift, from the LORD’s own hand.

Old words in verses 8–16

  • heave offeringa part “heaved,” lifted up, and given to the LORD, and by him to the priest
  • wave offeringa part waved to and fro before the LORD, then given to the priest. The “wave breast” and “right shoulder” of verse 18 are the same
  • reserved from the firekept back, not burned on the altar
  • devotedgiven up wholly to the LORD
  • matrixthe womb. “That openeth the matrix” is the firstborn
  • estimationvaluation, the price the priest sets

Some of these you met in Leviticus 27. Meat offering means a grain offering: meat is old English for food.

Five shekels for a son

5shekels of silver for the firstborn of man
20gerahs in the shekel of the sanctuary
1 month“from a month old shalt thou redeem”

A man’s firstborn son was the LORD’s, as every firstborn was from the Passover night, and the father bought him back for five shekels. The firstling of an unclean beast was bought back too, and the firstling of a cow, a sheep or a goat was not: “they are holy,” the blood went on the altar and the fat was burned (verse 17). The flesh was the priest’s.

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Five shekels bought a son back. The price of every son of God was not silver at all.

A covenant of salt

Leviticus 2:13

And every oblation of thy meat offering shalt thou season with salt; neither shalt thou suffer the salt of the covenant of thy God to be lacking from thy meat offering: with all thine offerings thou shalt offer salt.

2 Chronicles 13:5

Ought ye not to know that the LORD God of Israel gave the kingdom over Israel to David for ever, even to him and to his sons by a covenant of salt?

Moses doesn’t explain the phrase, and we won’t guess past the text. The same words come in 2 Chronicles for a promise given “for ever,” and salt was put on every offering. The LORD’s word to Aaron’s house, by this phrase, was a covenant “for ever.”

Part three

“I am thy part and thine inheritance”

Verses 20–32

The tribe with no land, and the God who was their land.

No share on the map

When Joshua divided the land, the shares went to the other tribes. Levi has no colour on this map.

“For the children of Joseph were two tribes, Manasseh and Ephraim: therefore they gave no part unto the Levites in the land, save cities to dwell in, with their suburbs for their cattle and for their substance.”

Joshua 14:4

The Levites got cities to live in, forty and eight of them (Numbers 35:7), named in Joshua 21. But no field of their own.

The LORD is the portion

“The LORD is the portion of mine inheritance and of my cup: thou maintainest my lot. The lines are fallen unto me in pleasant places; yea, I have a goodly heritage.”

Psalm 16:5–6

“The LORD is my portion, saith my soul; therefore will I hope in him.”

Lamentations 3:24

A man with no land could have felt like a man with nothing. The LORD called it the best share there was. Many of us have lost a lot: a home, a marriage, years, money. The LORD is still the portion of every man who will have him.

A tenth of the tenth

100measures: the tithes Israel brings to the Levites
10“a tenth part of the tithe,” to Aaron the priest
90the Levites keep, “as the increase of the threshingfloor”

The numbers are an example. What the LORD says is the rule: the people tithed to the Levites, and the Levites, who lived on tithes, tithed again. The man who received still gave. And he gave “the best thereof” (verse 29), not what was left over.

The ones who minister

“Do ye not know that they which minister about holy things live of the things of the temple? and they which wait at the altar are partakers with the altar? Even so hath the Lord ordained that they which preach the gospel should live of the gospel.”

1 Corinthians 9:13–14

Paul draws the same line. The Levites’ “reward for your service” (verse 31) becomes a pastor’s living. Thank the men who serve you. Pray for them. And if the church is taking care of you, remember that someone gave it first.

Real world: when you have no portion

Most of us here know what it is to have lost our share. Chapter 18 does not say that man has nothing.

  • SinGrabbing for security: taking what is not ours, hoarding, living as if everything depends on us.
  • RegretHome, family, years, money: the inheritance we threw away. The LORD does not pretend it was nothing.
  • Grace“I am thy part.” He gives himself. And he gave us a great high priest who bore our sin.
  • HopeA man with nothing in his hand can still say, “The LORD is my portion” (Lamentations 3:24).
  • TodayGive the best of something first, even a small thing: an hour, a kind word, a few dollars. And say Psalm 16:5 to a brother when you are afraid of what you have lost.

Points to remember

  1. The burden“thou and thy sons with thee shall bear the iniquity of your priesthood” 18:1The men who stood nearest took the weight of the sin, so Israel would not. Christ bore ours.
  2. The gift“to you they are given as a gift for the LORD” 18:6The Levites were a gift, and so was the priest’s office. Nothing in the service was earned.
  3. The priests’ share“Every thing devoted in Israel shall be thine” 18:14The priests had no land, so the LORD fed them from the altar.
  4. The firstborn“the firstborn of man shalt thou surely redeem” 18:15Five shekels bought a son back. We were bought with the precious blood of Christ.
  5. The inheritance“I am thy part and thine inheritance among the children of Israel” 18:20The tribe with no land had the LORD himself.
  6. The tenth of the tenth“then ye shall offer up an heave offering of it for the LORD, even a tenth part of the tithe” 18:26Even the man who lives on gifts gives, and gives the best.

Question 1 of 6

The burden

What were Aaron, his sons and the Levites to bear?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The iniquity of the sanctuary and of their priesthood: Aaron and his sons for the priestly work, the Levites for their service. They answered for the faults of the holy service, “that there be no wrath any more upon the children of Israel.” Christ “bare our sins in his own body on the tree” (1 Peter 2:24).

Question 2 of 6

The gift

What did the LORD call the Levites he gave to Aaron?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A gift: “to you they are given as a gift for the LORD, to do the service of the tabernacle.” The priest’s office was “a service of gift” too. The word given comes ten times in the chapter.

Question 3 of 6

The priests’ share

What did the LORD give Aaron and his sons out of what Israel brought him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The most holy things not burned, the heave and wave offerings, the best of the oil, wine and wheat, the firstfruits, and every thing devoted. The priests had no land, so the LORD fed them from the altar: “a covenant of salt for ever” (verse 19).

Question 4 of 6

The firstborn

What was paid for the firstborn of man, and what was done with the firstling of a cow, a sheep or a goat?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The firstborn of man was bought back for five shekels of silver, “after the shekel of the sanctuary.” The firstling of a cow, a sheep or a goat was not redeemed: it was holy, its blood was sprinkled on the altar and its fat burned.

Question 5 of 6

The inheritance

What did the LORD say was Aaron’s part and inheritance?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is the portion of mine inheritance and of my cup: thou maintainest my lot.”

Psalm 16:5

Answer

The LORD himself: “I am thy part and thine inheritance among the children of Israel.” No land for Aaron, and the best share there was.

Question 6 of 6

The tenth of the tenth

What did the Levites give out of the tithes they received?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A tenth part of the tithe: “the LORD’S heave offering,” given to Aaron the priest, and out of the best of it. The man who lived on gifts gave a gift.
