Numbers 16

“Ye take too much upon you”

Korah, a Levite, and Dathan and Abiram of Reuben rise up against Moses and Aaron with two hundred and fifty princes. The earth opens, fire comes out from the LORD, and the next day the whole camp murmurs. Then Aaron runs with a censer and stands between the dead and the living.

“Ye take too much upon you”

Korah was Moses' and Aaron's cousin: Amram and Izhar were brothers (Exodus 6:18, 21). Dathan and Abiram were of Reuben (26:8–9), and both families camped on the south side of the tabernacle (2:10; 3:29): neighbours. On, the son of Peleth, is never named again.

A half-truth

“All the congregation are holy, every one of them, and the LORD is among them.” That sounds right, and it is half right. God had said it himself:

“And ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation. These are the words which thou shalt speak unto the children of Israel.”

Exodus 19:6

But God had also chosen who would come near to the altar and burn the incense, and it was Aaron and his sons. Korah took a true word and used it to tear down what God had set up. A Psalm names the root of it:

“They envied Moses also in the camp, and Aaron the saint of the LORD. The earth opened and swallowed up Dathan, and covered the company of Abiram. And a fire was kindled in their company; the flame burned up the wicked.”

Psalm 106:16–18

“For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.”

James 3:16

Thrown back

Korah→“Ye take too much upon you”→to Moses and Aaronverse 3: you set yourselves up over us.
Moses→“Ye take too much upon you”→to the sons of Leviverse 7: you are reaching for what God has not given you.

Before he said a word, Moses fell on his face (verse 4). He did not argue for his own place. He put it in the LORD's hands: “to morrow the LORD will shew who are his, and who is holy”. Each man would bring a censer, a small pan of burning coals with incense laid on them, and God would choose.

“Nevertheless the foundation of God standeth sure, having this seal, The Lord knoweth them that are his. And, Let every one that nameth the name of Christ depart from iniquity.”

2 Timothy 2:19

Paul's words carry both halves of this chapter: the LORD knows who are his (verse 5), and those who are his depart from the tents of the wicked (verse 26).

“Seemeth it but a small thing unto you?”

Look at what Korah already had. God had separated the Levites “to bring you near to himself”, to serve in his tabernacle. The Kohathites carried the holiest things of all on the march:

“And when Aaron and his sons have made an end of covering the sanctuary, and all the vessels of the sanctuary, as the camp is to set forward; after that, the sons of Kohath shall come to bear it: but they shall not touch any holy thing, lest they die. These things are the burden of the sons of Kohath in the tabernacle of the congregation.”

Numbers 4:15

It was not enough. “Seek ye the priesthood also?” And Moses says who they were really fighting: “thou and all thy company are gathered together against the LORD: and what is Aaron, that ye murmur against him?”

Most of us know this one. A man is given a place, a job, a second chance, a seat in the group, and it is a gift from God. Then he looks at the man beside him and it starts to feel small. Envy does not say “I want more”. It says “he's no better than me”. That is how Korah started.

They renamed Egypt

Egypt: “the house of bondage” → “a land that floweth with milk and honey” Dathan and Abiram took the name God gave the promised land and gave it to the place of the whips and the brick kilns. And the land God was giving them? “Thou hast not brought us into a land that floweth with milk and honey.”

“Wilt thou put out the eyes of these men?” is an angry way of saying, Do you think you can blind us? And “We will not come up,” twice: they would not even come and talk.

Every addiction does this to the memory. The old life gets renamed: the good times, the freedom. The chains drop out of the story, and the new life with God gets called a wilderness that is killing you. When you hear yourself calling Egypt a land of milk and honey, remember the whips.

Even the meekest man

“(Now the man Moses was very meek, above all the men which were upon the face of the earth.)”

Numbers 12:3

And here that same man is very wroth, very angry. Being lied about will do that. But watch where Moses takes it: he says it “unto the LORD”. He does not strike out at them. He asks the LORD not to accept their offering, and he lays out his own record: not one ass taken, not one man hurt.

Anger is not always sin; what we do with it can be. When someone accuses you falsely, tell it to God before you tell it to them.

Part two

“The earth opened her mouth”

Verses 16–35

Two hundred and fifty censers at the door, a prayer for the guilty, and a new thing.

Praying for the men against them

Korah had gathered “all the congregation against them”. The LORD told Moses and Aaron to step aside so he could consume the people. And the two men who had just been accused fell on their faces and prayed for them.

3times Moses, or Moses and Aaron, fell on their faces (verses 4, 22, 45)

“The God of the spirits of all flesh”: the God who made every man's spirit and knows every heart. He knows the leaders from the led. Moses pleads that the many not perish for the one.

That is a hard prayer to pray: for the people who are tearing you down. It is the kind Jesus prayed on the cross.

Get away from those tents

God answered the prayer, and the answer was: move. The congregation was not swallowed up, because they “gat up from the tabernacle of Korah, Dathan, and Abiram, on every side”. Their safety was a matter of where they stood.

“Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you,”

2 Corinthians 6:17

“Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.”

1 Corinthians 15:33

For a man in recovery that means people and places: the friend who keeps calling you back, the house where it always starts. You can love them and pray for them, as Moses did, and still not stand in their door. “Touch nothing of theirs.”

“A new thing”

  • “the common death of all men”if they died of age or sickness, Moses was a fraud
  • “a new thing”something the LORD would create that had never been
  • quick into the pitquick means alive: down into the grave alive
  • appertainto belong to: all that belonged to them
  • clave asundersplit apart

Moses staked everything on it, and before he had finished speaking the ground split. This is not a story about an earthquake that happened to come along. It is the LORD answering the question “Did God send this man?” in front of the whole camp. The Bible tells it again as plain history:

“And the earth opened her mouth, and swallowed them up together with Korah, when that company died, what time the fire devoured two hundred and fifty men: and they became a sign. Notwithstanding the children of Korah died not.”

Numbers 26:10–11

Verse 27 says their wives and little ones stood with them in the door of their tents. It is a hard verse, and we read it as it stands. Yet Moses tells us the line of Korah did not end: “the children of Korah died not”. Keep that; we'll come back to it.

Fire from the LORD, again

“And Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, took either of them his censer, and put fire therein, and put incense thereon, and offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not. And there went out fire from the LORD, and devoured them, and they died before the LORD.”

Leviticus 10:1–2

The two hundred and fifty princes had done what Aaron's own sons did: taken censers and offered incense the LORD had not given them to offer. And the same fire came out. Nearness to God is never something a man takes. It is something God gives.

That is good news for us, not bad. Nobody can push his way to God, and nobody has to. The way near has been opened by the one Priest God chose, and it is open to every man who comes through him.

Part three

“Between the dead and the living”

Verses 36–50

Censers beaten into plates, a whole camp murmuring the next morning, and a high priest who runs.

Plates on the altar

Eleazar the priest took the brasen censers out of the ashes. The fire in them was to be scattered yonder, away over there, but the pans themselves were hallowed, set apart to the LORD, because they had been offered before him. So they were beaten into broad plates to cover the brasen altar, the first thing a man saw inside the gate.

Every time an Israelite brought an offering, he saw them: “a memorial … that no stranger, which is not of the seed of Aaron, come near to offer incense before the LORD”. A stranger here means any man not of Aaron's line, even a Levite. The chapter's warning was hammered into the altar itself.

On the morrow

The very next day. The ground had closed, the fire had fallen, the plates were still being hammered, and the whole congregation blamed Moses and Aaron: “Ye have killed the people of the LORD.” They called the rebels the LORD's people, and the LORD's servants murderers.

“Neither murmur ye, as some of them also murmured, and were destroyed of the destroyer.”

1 Corinthians 10:10

“Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come.”

1 Corinthians 10:11

Paul says these things were written for us. Murmuring looks small: grumbling, blaming, the running complaint about the people over us. Paul puts it beside idolatry and fornication in that chapter. It is a heart that has decided God is the problem.

He stood between

Moses told him to go quickly, and the old high priest ran: not away from the plague but into it, into the midst of the people who had just accused him, carrying fire from the altar and incense. He made an atonement for them, and he stood still. On one side, the dead. On the other, the living. Where he stood, the plague stopped.

One who stands between

Aaron is a picture of the High Priest God would send. He did not stand at a safe distance. He came into the midst of us, where death was already at work, and stood in the gap, and he is still there:

“For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;”

1 Timothy 2:5

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Maybe yesterday you were among the murmurers. Maybe you have felt the plague of your own sin moving through your life, taking ground. Run to the One who stands between. He makes intercession for you today.

“The gainsaying of Core”

“Woe unto them! for they have gone in the way of Cain, and ran greedily after the error of Balaam for reward, and perished in the gainsaying of Core.”

Jude 11

Core is Korah, as the name is spelled in the New Testament, and gainsaying is speaking against. Jude puts Korah beside Cain and Balaam, in a warning about “certain men crept in unawares” among believers (Jude 4). Korah's way is still a danger in the church.

But remember: “the children of Korah died not” (26:11).

The sons of Korah

Generations later, Korah's family kept the gates of the house of God:

“And Shallum the son of Kore, the son of Ebiasaph, the son of Korah, and his brethren, of the house of his father, the Korahites, were over the work of the service, keepers of the gates of the tabernacle: and their fathers, being over the host of the LORD, were keepers of the entry.”

1 Chronicles 9:19

And several Psalms carry the heading “for the sons of Korah” (Psalm 42 and Psalm 84 among them). Psalm 84 says this:

“For a day in thy courts is better than a thousand. I had rather be a doorkeeper in the house of my God, than to dwell in the tents of wickedness.”

Psalm 84:10

Their father fought to come nearer than God allowed, and died at the door. His sons were glad to keep the door, and wrote that they'd rather be there than in “the tents of wickedness”. Your father's sin, or your own, does not have to be the end of your family's story.

Real world: your place, and his

  • SinEnvy of the man God put over you or beside you; renaming the old life; murmuring about the people God gave you.
  • RegretKorah's company could not take back the censers they lit. Some things we said and did can't be unsaid.
  • GraceThe high priest they had accused ran into the plague for them, and it stopped where he stood.
  • HopeThe sons of Korah lived, and sang. Your family's story is not finished.
  • TodayThank God out loud for the place he has given you, small as it seems. Don't stand in the door of the old tents. And if someone has set himself against you, pray for him before you answer him.

Points to remember

  1. The charge“Ye take too much upon you, seeing all the congregation are holy” 16:3A half-truth used to tear down what God set up. The root was envy.
  2. The Levites“Seemeth it but a small thing unto you … and seek ye the priesthood also?” 16:9–10They had been brought near to serve. It felt small next to someone else's place.
  3. Dathan and Abiram“thou hast brought us up out of a land that floweth with milk and honey” 16:13They gave Egypt the promised land's name. Sin rewrites the memory of the old life.
  4. The sign“if the LORD make a new thing, and the earth open her mouth” 16:30The LORD answered “Did God send him?” with a new thing before the whole camp.
  5. The censers“broad plates for a covering of the altar … To be a memorial” 16:39–40Nearness to God is given, never taken. The warning was hammered into the altar.

Question 1 of 6

The charge

What did Korah and his company say against Moses and Aaron?

What verses tell us about this?

“They envied Moses also in the camp, and Aaron the saint of the LORD.”

Psalm 106:16

Answer

“Ye take too much upon you”: the whole congregation is holy, so why do you lift yourselves up over us? It was half true, and it came from envy of Moses and of “Aaron the saint of the LORD”.

Question 2 of 6

The Levites

What did Moses say Korah already had, and what was he seeking besides?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God had separated the Levites and brought them near to serve in the tabernacle and minister to the congregation. Korah wanted the priesthood too, and in fighting Aaron he was fighting the LORD.

Question 3 of 6

Dathan and Abiram

What did Dathan and Abiram call the land they had left?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They called Egypt, the house of bondage, “a land that floweth with milk and honey”, the very name of the land God promised, and said Moses had brought them out to kill them. Sin rewrites the memory of the old life.

Question 4 of 6

The sign

How did the LORD show that he had sent Moses?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By a new thing: as soon as Moses finished speaking, the ground split, the earth opened her mouth and swallowed them, and they went down alive into the pit. Then fire from the LORD consumed the two hundred and fifty.

Question 5 of 6

The censers

What were the censers made into, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Broad plates to cover the altar, a sign and a memorial to Israel that no man outside Aaron's line should come near to offer incense, lest he be as Korah and his company.

Question 6 of 6

Between

What did Aaron do when the plague began?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Answer

He took a censer with fire from the altar, ran into the midst of the congregation, made an atonement, and stood between the dead and the living, and the plague stopped. Christ ever lives to make intercession for us.
