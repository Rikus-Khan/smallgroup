Acts 21

Bound for Jerusalem

Paul sails toward Jerusalem knowing chains are waiting, and the riot in the temple proves it.

Part one

Warned, and still going

Verses 1–16

Port after port, friend after friend: don't go. Paul keeps sailing.

The last leg of the third journey

Paul was racing to reach Jerusalem for Pentecost (20:16). They island-hopped to Patara, took a bigger ship across open sea south of Cyprus to Tyre (about 600 miles), then the coast and about 65 miles uphill to Jerusalem.

He carried a gift: a collection from the churches of Macedonia and Achaia for the poor believers in Jerusalem (Romans 15:25–26). A gift for the city that would put him in chains.

Everywhere · 20:23“The Holy Ghost witnesseth in every city, saying that bonds and afflictions abide me.”Paul told the Ephesian elders

Three warnings. One answer: I'm going.

This is a hard passage. How can the Spirit say “don't go,” and Paul go anyway? The simplest reading: the Spirit showed them what was coming, bonds and trouble, and their love for Paul drew the conclusion “so stay away.” Paul had already been told to go (20:22, and see Luke 9:51, where Jesus “stedfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem”).

Knowing a hard road is ahead is not the same as being told to avoid it. When people who love you urge the easy way, ask: is this God's word to me, or their fear for me?

Sleeping under the roof of the men he hunted

Paul had been the man dragging believers like Philip off to prison. And here is Philip, feeding him and giving him a bed. Nobody in this story is holding a grudge. That is what grace looks like between two men.

Verse 9 tells us his four daughters “did prophesy”: they spoke God's word. Luke mentions it in passing, the way you mention a family's good name.

A sermon without words (v. 11)

The girdle was a long cloth belt that held the robe in. Agabus, the prophet who warned of a famine in Acts 11:28, picks up Paul's and acts out his message.

Notice what he says: the Jews will bind Paul and hand him to the Gentiles. He doesn't say don't go.

It came true. A Jewish mob seized Paul, and the Romans bound him with two chains (v. 33). Better to be warned than ambushed: a man who knows what the road costs can walk it with his eyes open.

He had already decided.

Settled beforehand“None of these things move me” (20:24)

so when it cameno argument. Just “I am ready.”

Paul wasn't gritting his teeth in the moment. His friends' tears broke his heart, but they didn't change his answer, because the decision was made long before.

Most of us know the other way: the craving hits late at night, the decision gets made then, tired and alone, and it goes wrong. Decide today, in daylight, with God and a friend, what you'll do when it comes. Then you're not arguing with the craving. You're just keeping your word.

When his friends saw he was settled, they stopped arguing: “The will of the Lord be done.” Sometimes love means praying and letting a man go.

Part two

A plan to keep the peace

Verses 17–26

Paul reaches Jerusalem. The elders have a rumor to calm down, and an idea.

Glad to see him, worried about the talk

What Paul reported · v. 19

What God had done

He told them “particularly,” one thing after another, what God had done among the Gentiles. They “glorified the Lord.”

What they had heard · v. 21

A rumor about Paul

That he taught Jews everywhere to “forsake Moses,” and not to circumcise their children.

“Thousands” here is really myriads, tens of thousands, believing Jews, all still keeping the law of Moses out of love for it. Nothing wrong with that. The rumor was just not true: Paul taught that Gentiles didn't have to become Jews, not that Jews had to stop being Jews. He even circumcised Timothy himself (Acts 16:3).

The plan, and what it was not

What it was

A gift and a bridge

Four poor men had finished a vow. Paul would pay their costs, join them in the temple, and show the whole city he still honored his own people.

What it was not

A way to be saved

Nobody needed a vow for that. The Gentiles' answer stays as the council settled it (v. 25; Acts 15).

“But we believe that through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved, even as they.”

Acts 15:11

Was it wise? Good men have disagreed. What is clear is Paul's reason: “unto the Jews I became as a Jew, that I might gain the Jews” (1 Corinthians 9:20). He never added a thing to the gospel. And notice the Gentile list includes “fornication.” Grace frees us from keeping the law to be saved, never from living clean.

Part three

Dragged out of the temple

Verses 27–40

Seven days of peace, and then one lie sets the whole city running.

Where it happened

Paul is in the Court of Women, finishing the seven days of the vow with four men.

Stone warnings told any non-Jew to keep out on pain of death. Men from Asia had seen Trophimus, a Gentile, with Paul in the city and “supposed” he'd taken him in.

They drag Paul out, and the doors are shut behind him: “Men of Israel, help!”

Soldiers from the fortress run down its stairs, and the beating stops.

All it took was a guess

  • “teacheth all men every where against the people”he had just come with a gift for their poor
  • “against the law”he was in the temple keeping a vow, in public
  • “brought Greeks also into the temple”he hadn't. They supposed it.
  • What he was really doingpaying for four poor men's offerings, so as to keep the peace

A lie never needs proof. It only needs a crowd that already wants to believe it. The accusers were “Jews which were of Asia,” the same kind of men who had chased Paul from town to town for years.

If you have a past, you know this. People decide who you are from what they've heard. Don't answer it with a fight. Live so the guess doesn't fit, and be patient. And be careful what you suppose about another man.

The same cry, on a different stair

Jesus“Away with him, away with him, crucify him” (John 19:15)
Paul“Away with him” (Acts 21:36), on the castle stairs

Paul isn't being compared to Jesus. Jesus died for sins, and Paul didn't. But Paul is walking where his Lord walked: the same city, the same kind of crowd, and the same cry. He would later say he wanted to share Christ's sufferings.

Before he left, Paul had asked the Romans to pray “that I may be delivered from them that do not believe in Judaea” (Romans 15:31). Look how it was answered. Not by a miracle, and not by a friendly crowd, but by a Roman officer and a company of soldiers on the run. God picks His own rescuers. Sometimes they're the last men you'd choose.

Three languages, one man

Latinthe army and the law
Greekthe street and the marketplace. The officer asks, and Paul answers.
Hebrewthe heart language of the temple crowd, the people who wanted him dead

The officer thought he had caught a notorious Egyptian rebel, a man who had led a wild crowd out to the wilderness (Josephus, the Jewish historian, tells of him). Paul quietly corrects him: a Jew of Tarsus, “a citizen of no mean city.” Tarsus was a famous university town.

Then he asks a favor: “suffer me to speak unto the people.” Bleeding and chained, he doesn't ask for a lawyer. He asks for a microphone. He speaks in their own tongue, and a roaring mob goes silent. Chapter 22 is what he says.

What this arrest sets going

  1. about AD 57Paul arrested in Jerusalem, at Pentecost
  2. about AD 57 to 59Two years in prison at Caesarea
  3. about AD 59 to 60The voyage and shipwreck
  4. about AD 60 to 62House arrest in Rome

Paul wanted to reach Rome, and he did, but not the way he planned. The road to Rome ran through a Jerusalem riot, two years in a cell, and a shipwreck. God's plan for a man can go straight through the place he dreaded.

“And the night following the Lord stood by him, and said, Be of good cheer, Paul: for as thou hast testified of me in Jerusalem, so must thou bear witness also at Rome.”

Acts 23:11

If you've fallen, if you feel locked in by what you've done, this is for you too: the chains are not the end of the story.

Points to remember

  1. Being ready“I am ready not to be bound only, but also to die” 21:13Decide ahead of time, in daylight, what you will do when the pressure comes.
  2. Friends“The will of the Lord be done” 21:14When you can't talk a man out of it, pray for him, kneel on the shore, and let him go.
  3. Law and grace“we have written and concluded that they observe no such thing” 21:25Saved by grace through faith, not by keeping rules. Grace never means living dirty.
  4. Winning people“Then Paul took the men” 21:26Paul gave up his rights to win people. He never gave up the gospel.
  5. Rumors“whom they supposed that Paul had brought into the temple” 21:29A guess is not a fact. Don't spread one, and don't be crushed by one.
  6. God's hand“Who immediately took soldiers and centurions” 21:32God rescues by His own means, and He isn't done with you.

Question 1 of 6

Being ready

What had Paul already settled before he reached Jerusalem?

What verses tell us about this?

“Save that the Holy Ghost witnesseth in every city, saying that bonds and afflictions abide me.”

Acts 20:23

Answer

That he would go, whatever it cost. Told about the chains, he still said, “I am ready.” He'd decided before the pressure came, so there was nothing left to argue about. Settle it ahead of time, and the hard moment is just keeping your word.

Question 2 of 6

Friends

What do friends do when they can't talk a man out of a hard road?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They love him, say so, and cry. When he's settled, they stop arguing and pray: “The will of the Lord be done.” The families at Tyre knelt on the beach with him. You don't have to agree with the road to stand beside the man on it.

Question 3 of 6

Law and grace

Does a believer in Jesus have to keep the law of Moses to be saved?

What verses tell us about this?

“But we believe that through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved, even as they.”

Acts 15:11

Answer

No. The church had settled it years before: Gentiles are saved by the grace of the Lord Jesus, and that's all. Jewish believers could still keep their customs out of love for them, but no one is saved by them. Salvation is by grace through faith, and a saved man wants to live clean.

Question 4 of 6

Winning people

Why did Paul agree to the elders' plan?

What verses tell us about this?

“And unto the Jews I became as a Jew, that I might gain the Jews; to them that are under the law, as under the law, that I might gain them that are under the law;”

1 Corinthians 9:20

Answer

To remove a roadblock, not to add to the gospel. He was free in Christ and used that freedom to serve the Jews he wanted to win. Good men have argued whether it was wise. The reason is clear: love for people.

Question 5 of 6

Rumors

What happens when people jump to conclusions about a man?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A guess becomes a charge, and a charge becomes a riot. They “supposed” Paul had brought a Gentile in, and the whole city ran. If people judge you by your past, answer with patience and a changed life. And hold back your own suppositions about other men.

Question 6 of 6

God's hand

Who stepped in when the mob was about to kill Paul?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the night following the Lord stood by him, and said, Be of good cheer, Paul: for as thou hast testified of me in Jerusalem, so must thou bear witness also at Rome.”

Acts 23:11

Answer

The Roman officer and his soldiers, who ran down and stopped the beating. And behind them, the Lord, who had said Paul would bear witness at Rome. God rescues in His own way, sometimes through people we would never have chosen. His plan for you isn't finished either.
