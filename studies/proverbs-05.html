Honey now, wormwood later

Part one

Honey and wormwood

Verses 1–6

Sin never shows you the end of the road at the beginning.

Who is the “strange woman”?

זָרָה said zarah an outsider; one who isn't yours

“Strange” here doesn't mean odd. It means she belongs to somebody else, or to nobody: another man's wife, a prostitute, any woman who isn't your wife.

Let's be plain about it. For us she might be an affair, a hookup, a strip club, an app, or a screen at 2 a.m. Different packaging, same road.

Sweet at the top, bitter at the bottom

  1. Her lips drop as an honeycomb
  2. Her mouth is smoother than oil
  3. Her end is bitter as wormwood
  4. Sharp as a twoedged sword
  5. Her feet go down to death
  6. Her steps take hold on hell

Wormwood is a plant so bitter it became a byword for misery. Temptation always sells the first taste and hides the last one. That's how it worked with the bottle and the pipe too.

Play the tape forward

To “ponder” is to weigh it, to think it through. Her ways are “moveable”: always shifting, so you never stop long enough to think.

Temptation needs you in the moment. It doesn't want you asking, “Where does this end? Who gets hurt? How will I feel tomorrow?”

That's why “play the tape forward” works so well in recovery. Before you take the first step, watch the whole movie to the end. The end is where the truth is.

Part two

What it costs

Verses 7–14

The bill always comes. It's just mailed later.

Don't go near the door

The father doesn't say, “Be strong when you get there.” He says, don't go there. Most battles are won at a distance.

Joseph knew it: “he left his garment in her hand, and fled, and got him out” (Genesis 39:12). Running isn't weakness. It's wisdom.

For us: delete the number. Lose the app. Take a different way home. Get rid of what leads you to her door.

The bill comes due

Ask any man who's lost a marriage, his kids' trust, his savings, or his health this way. It always costs more than it promised.

“How have I hated instruction”

This is the speech of a man at the end of the road. Notice what he regrets most. Not getting caught. Not listening. He had teachers. He heard the warnings. He just wouldn't take them.

Verse 14: he was “almost in all evil in the midst of the congregation.” He nearly lost everything, in front of everybody.

Here's the good news: you're hearing the warning now, while there's still time to listen. And if you've already lived that speech, your story isn't over. Keep reading.

Part three

Your own well

Verses 15–23

God isn't against desire. He made it, and He gave it a home.

Drink from your own well

A cistern was a tank cut in the rock to keep rainwater. Here the water pictures sex inside marriage.

Many read verse 16 as a question: should your springs run out into the streets? Verse 17 answers: “Let them be only thine own.”

God doesn't tell a thirsty man to stop being thirsty. He shows him where the good water is.

One word, two roads

“Ravished” is the Hebrew shagah: carried away, swept off your feet, even staggering like a drunk man. Watch where Solomon uses it.

Verse 19→“be thou ravished always with her love”Swept away by your own wife. God says yes to this, “always.”
Verse 20→“ravished with a strange woman”Same word, wrong woman.
Verse 23→“he shall go astray”Same word again. That's where the wrong road ends: lost.

“Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled” (Hebrews 13:4). If you're single right now, faithfulness starts today. You're keeping the well for the one God may give you.

Tied up with his own cords

Nobody else ties him up. His own sins do it, one loop for every yes. That's addiction from the inside.

She keeps you from pondering (v. 6). God pondereth every road you take (v. 21).

And the God who sees is the God who sets men loose: “If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed” (John 8:36).

Question 1 of 6

Temptation

Why does sin look so good at the start?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because it only shows you the beginning. Her lips are honey and her mouth is smooth, but her end is bitter. Temptation keeps you in the moment so you never ponder where the road goes. Think it through to the end before you take the first step.

Question 2 of 6

The cost

What does sexual sin take from a man?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

His honor, his years, his money, the work of his hands, and his health. Other people end up with what he spent his life building. And it ends with mourning. It always costs more than it promised.

Question 3 of 6

Listening

What does a man say at the end if he won't listen?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“How have I hated instruction!” His biggest regret isn't getting caught. It's that he had teachers and warnings and wouldn't listen. The way to avoid that speech is to listen now, while you still can.

Question 4 of 6

Faithfulness in marriage

What does God give a man instead?

What verses tell us about this?

“Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled: but whoremongers and adulterers God will judge.”

Hebrews 13:4

Answer

His own wife. God isn't against desire. He made it and gave it a home. A man is told to rejoice with the wife of his youth and be swept away by her love, always. That's not a consolation prize. That's God's best.

Question 5 of 6

Who God is

Who sees every road a man takes?

What verses tell us about this?

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.”

Hebrews 4:15

Answer

The LORD. Every way a man goes is before His eyes, and He weighs all of it. Nothing is hidden, not even what happens in the dark. But the One who sees us also understands temptation from the inside, and He's the one we can run to.

Question 6 of 6

A way out

Is there hope for a man who is already caught?

What verses tell us about this?

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Answer

Yes. Sin ties a man up with his own cords, and he can't untie them himself. But Jesus sets men free. Bring it into the light: confess it to God, and He forgives and cleans you up. Then tell a brother you trust. And every time temptation comes, God has built a way out. Take it.
