Exodus 26

The tent, and the vail

Ten linen curtains with angels woven in, covered by three more layers; a frame of gold-covered boards standing on silver; and the vail, a curtain that divides the room where God meets his people from everything else.

Part one

Ten curtains and four coverings

Verses 1–14

The roof and walls of God's tent were cloth, and the best of it was on the inside, where the priests could see it.

The measurements, in feet

A cubit is about 18 inches. Here is what the verse says, in numbers you can picture:

28 × 4 cubitsone curtain, which is about 42 × 6 feet
5 curtains joinedone piece about 42 × 30 feet
2 pieces joinedone roof-cloth about 60 feet long, 42 feet across
  • fine twined linenlinen thread twisted together for strength
  • cunning workskilled design. In the old English, cunning meant clever with the hands, not sly
  • coupledjoined together, stitched or clasped into one piece

Blue, purple and scarlet again, and this time with cherubims woven into the cloth: angels, in thread, covering the ceiling. A priest who looked up inside the tent looked up at a sky of angels in royal colours, in a tent in the desert.

Fifty loops, fifty clasps, one tabernacle

Two sets of five curtains

Fifty blue loops on each edge

Selvedge is the finished edge of cloth. The loops of blue wool run along it, loop facing loop across the gap.

Fifty taches of gold

“It shall be one tabernacle”

A tache is a clasp or hook. Fifty gold clasps catch the loops, and two pieces of cloth are one roof.

Two halves, held together by gold. God did not want two tents. He wanted one, and he put the joining in the most precious metal there was.

The tent, cut through, from the end

The linen is 28 cubits wide: ten across the roof and nine down each side, a cubit short of the ground. The goats' hair is 30, so it hangs a cubit lower and covers the seam (verse 13). A half curtain hangs over the back, and the sixth one is doubled over the front (verses 9 and 12).

Plain on the outside, glorious within

What a man outside saw

Dark leather

A low, plain tent. The outermost layer is a rough, weatherproof skin. Nothing to look at.

What a priest inside saw

Gold, blue, purple, scarlet

Gold-covered walls. A ceiling of fine linen with angels woven in. The best of it was kept for the inside.

Many who walked past the tabernacle would have seen only a tent. John writes of Jesus in a way that sounds like it: a man who looked like any other man, among men who did not see anything. But those who did look could say:

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

John 1:14

Part two

The boards and the bars

Verses 15–30

A frame of gold-covered wood, standing in sockets of silver, that could be taken down and carried through the wilderness.

The floor plan, from above

Twenty boards on the south side, twenty on the north, six across the west end, and two corner boards: forty-eight in all. Each is 10 cubits high (15 feet) and 1½ cubits wide (27 inches), so twenty side by side make a wall 45 feet long.

Standing on silver, held with bars

  • tenonstwo pegs at the foot of each board
  • socketssunken stands of silver: each board stands with its two pegs in two sockets. Forty, forty, and sixteen at the back make ninety-six
  • barslong rails of gold-covered wood slid through gold rings along each wall. Five on a side; the middle bar runs the whole length
  • rear upto raise, set up. The tent could be taken down and carried, then raised again

The silver for these sockets was not bought with extra money. It is the ransom money, the same amount for every man in the camp, rich or poor, counted in the census (30:11–16; 38:25–27). The tent of God stands on the price paid for the lives of his people.

Wood, covered with gold, on a foundation of silver. Everything came from what they had been given, and he told them exactly how to put it together.

Part three

The vail

Verses 31–37

One curtain divides the holy place from the most holy, and a second closes the door.

Two rooms, one curtain

The tent is 30 cubits long and 10 wide (about 45 by 15 feet). The vail cuts it into two rooms. The inner one is a perfect cube, about 15 feet each way: the most holy place, with the ark. The outer, 20 cubits long, is the holy place, with the table on the north side and the candlestick on the south (verse 35).

Priests served in the first room. In the second, no one came in but the high priest, once a year.

“From the top to the bottom”

“Jesus, when he had cried again with a loud voice, yielded up the ghost. And, behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom; and the earth did quake, and the rocks rent;”

Matthew 27:50–51

The same curtain, the same colours, in the temple that came after the tent. It tore at the moment Jesus died, and it tore from the top. Nobody on earth could reach that high. God himself tore it.

The way in is open

The vail kept sinful men out of the place where God sat, and the high priest could go in only once a year, with blood. Hebrews says what the tearing means:

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh; And having an high priest over the house of God; Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.”

Hebrews 10:19–22

“Through the veil, that is to say, his flesh.” The curtain was a picture of Jesus' body, broken on the cross, and now the way through is a person, not a curtain. “Boldness to enter,” said the writer, to men who had every reason to feel unfit.

Three doors, one set of colours

  1. the gateof the court (27:16)20 cubits wide
  2. the doorof the tent (26:36)five pillars, “wrought with needlework”
  3. the vailof the most holy place (26:31)with cherubims woven in

Every way in is made of the same blue, purple and scarlet. A man had to pass the gate, then the door, then the vail, one at a time. Each one is a screen that says you cannot just walk in, and each one has a place to go through.

Wrought with needlework means embroidered. The door's five pillars stand in sockets of brass (verse 37), not silver: the outside is bronze, and the silver is saved for the frame of the tent itself.

Real world: when shame keeps you outside

After a fall, the feeling is: I can't go in there. You stay away from the brothers, from your Bible, from your God. That is the old vail talking, and the New Testament says it has been torn.

  • You did not tear itIt tore from the top. God did it, at the cross. You cannot make the way more open by cleaning up first.
  • Go in the same dayPray now, with the failure still fresh. Hebrews says “boldness,” not “after you feel better.”
  • Take a brother with youCall or text one of the men, and your pastor. Say what happened. Draw near together.

“Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith.” Draw near, and bring the real you.

Points to remember

  1. The curtains“ten curtains of fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet: with cherubims of cunning work” 26:1The best of it is inside: angels woven in the roof. God does not cut corners.
  2. The coverings“a covering for the tent of rams’ skins dyed red, and a covering above of badgers’ skins” 26:14Four layers. Plain outside, glory within, like Jesus among men (John 1:14).
  3. The frame“forty sockets of silver under the twenty boards” 26:19The tent stands on silver, the ransom for every man.
  4. The vail“the vail shall divide unto you between the holy place and the most holy” 26:33It kept sinners out, and it tore from the top to the bottom when Jesus died.
  5. Within the vail“bring in thither within the vail the ark of the testimony” 26:33The ark within, the table and lamp outside. Now we have “boldness to enter into the holiest.”
  6. The door“an hanging for the door of the tent, of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen” 26:36Every way in is in the same colours: gate, door, vail. One way, made by God.

Question 1 of 6

The curtains

What were the ten inner curtains made of, and what was worked into them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Fine twined linen in blue, purple and scarlet, with cherubims woven in. Two sets of five curtains, coupled by fifty gold clasps so that it is one tabernacle.

Question 2 of 6

The coverings

What was spread over the linen curtains, layer by layer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

First curtains of goats' hair, then a covering of rams' skins dyed red, and on top a covering of badgers' skins. Plain outside, glorious inside.

Question 3 of 6

The frame

What did the boards of the tabernacle stand in?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Sockets of silver, two under every board, one for each tenon. The silver is the ransom money paid for every man (30:11–16). The tent of God stands on the price paid for lives.

Question 4 of 6

The vail

What did the vail divide, and what was worked into it?

What verses tell us about this?

“And, behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom; and the earth did quake, and the rocks rent;”

Matthew 27:51

Answer

It divided the holy place from the most holy, and cherubims were worked into it. When Jesus died it was torn from the top to the bottom, and the way is open to every man who believes.

Question 5 of 6

Within the vail

What was brought in within the vail, and where were the table and the candlestick set?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The ark of the testimony, with the mercy seat on it, went within the vail in the most holy place. The table stood outside the vail on the north side and the candlestick across from it on the south.

Question 6 of 6

The door

What was hung at the door of the tent?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A hanging of blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen, embroidered, on five gold-covered pillars. The same colours as the vail and the gate: every way in is one way, made by God.
