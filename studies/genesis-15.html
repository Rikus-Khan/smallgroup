Genesis 15

Counted righteous

Abram is old, childless, and afraid. God answers his questions with a sky full of stars and a covenant that God keeps alone. Here a man is counted right with God by believing, not by earning.

Part one

Fear not

Verses 1–6

A frightened old man, an empty house, and a sky full of stars.

A shield, and a reward

What Abram needed first

“Fear not, Abram: I am thy shield,”

Genesis 15:1

What Abram wanted most

“and thy exceeding great reward.”

Genesis 15:1

“After these things” points back to chapter 14. Abram had just fought four kings to rescue his nephew Lot, and then turned down the king of Sodom's offer of the loot (14:21–24). The Bible does not say what Abram feared that day. Maybe revenge from the men he beat. Maybe he wondered if he had thrown away his pay by refusing the spoils. God's answer covers both.

Look at what God does not say. He doesn't say I will give you a reward. He says I am your reward. What a man like Abram needs most is not a thing. It is God himself.

“But thou, O LORD, art a shield for me; my glory, and the lifter up of mine head.”

Psalm 3:3

A man with no son

AbramLord GOD, what can You give me? I have no child. My house will go to a servant.

What it cost him to say itAbram had been promised a great nation (12:2). Ten years or so on, he had nothing to show for it. honest

In Abram's world, a man with no son could make a trusted servant his heir. That was Eliezer of Damascus, the manager of his household, a man born in his house. It was the sensible plan. It was also a man quietly giving up on God's promise.

But notice what Abram did with his doubt. He did not hide it, and he did not walk away. He said it to the LORD, politely and plainly: Lord GOD, here is what I see. That is a prayer. God does not scold him for it.

Old words in verses 2–4

  1. stewardthe manager of a household; the servant who runs the place
  2. heirthe one who receives everything when the owner dies
  3. seedchildren, descendants, as a farmer talks of seed that grows into a crop
  4. bowelsthe inner body; “out of thine own bowels” means a son of your own flesh

So God's answer in verse 4 is plain: not the servant. A son from your own body.

Count them if you can

God took Abram outside, and let him see the sky. Out in the open country, with no city lights, a man can see thousands of stars with his own eyes. That many, God said, is how many children you will have. And Abram could not point to a single son.

“He telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names.”

Psalm 147:4

And God knows every one of those stars by name. He is not short of attention for one worried man. He knows what you are called, too.

Three words that carry the weight

הֶאֱמִין
he'emin
“believed”: leaned his whole weight on God. Same root as Amen.
וַיַּחְשְׁבֶהָ
vayachshveha
“counted”: an account-book word. Put it to his credit.
צְדָקָה
tzedaqah
“righteousness”: being right, in the right with God.

Put it together. Abram trusted God, and God wrote right with Me on his account. Nothing is said about what Abram did to earn it. In the same chapter he is full of questions. God counted him righteous anyway, because he took God at his word.

This is the first time in the Bible the word “believed” is joined to “righteousness”. It is the heart of the gospel, and it is here in Genesis, more than six hundred years before Moses' law was written.

What Paul does with verse 6

A man who works

Gets wages

What he is paid is not a gift. His boss owes it to him. “Not reckoned of grace, but of debt.”

A man who believes

Gets counted righteous

He brings nothing but trust. God “justifieth the ungodly”, and puts it to his account as a free gift.

“For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness. Now to him that worketh is the reward not reckoned of grace, but of debt. But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness.”

Romans 4:3-5

So when you read “counted it to him for righteousness”, read credited. Paul quotes this verse twice, in Romans 4 and Galatians 3, to show that this is how every man has ever been made right with God.

Counted, not earned

“Even as Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness. Know ye therefore that they which are of faith, the same are the children of Abraham. And the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the heathen through faith, preached before the gospel unto Abraham, saying, In thee shall all nations be blessed. So then they which be of faith are blessed with faithful Abraham.”

Galatians 3:6-9

Whatever you did last night, or last year, your standing with God does not rest on your record. It rests on whether you trust him. If you have trusted Jesus, you are counted righteous, the same way Abram was. You can't earn more of it, and you can't lose it by stumbling.

And a man counted righteous gets a title as well:

“And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God.”

James 2:23

Part two

Whereby shall I know?

Verses 7–11

Abram asks for something to hold on to, and God gives it in blood.

“Out of Ur of the Chaldees”

God reminds Abram where he started: Ur, a great city of southern Mesopotamia. Abram was called out, went up the river to Haran, and then down into Canaan. That is roughly a thousand miles of road, with no map in his hand.

“To give thee this land to inherit it.” Inherit means to receive what is handed down to a son. Abram has no son, and is standing in a land that belongs to other people.

So the question in verse 8 is a fair one: whereby (by what, how) shall I know? God does not call it unbelief. He gives him a sign.

What God asked for

  1. heifera young cow, three years old
  2. she goatthree years old
  3. rama male sheep, three years old
  4. turtledovea small wild dove (nothing to do with turtles)
  5. young pigeona baby pigeon

Three of them were cut in half and laid out in two rows, “each piece one against another”, with a lane between. The two birds were laid down whole. Later, in the law of Moses, these same five kinds of animal are the ones offered on God's altar. A life given in place of a life.

“Carcases” are dead bodies, and “fowls” are birds. When the birds of prey came down, Abram drove them away. That is faith with work to do: the promise is God's, but the man still stands guard over what God has given him, against whatever swoops in to spoil it.

Part three

A horror of great darkness

Verses 12–16

Before the promise comes the dark, and God tells Abram how long the wait will be.

A deep sleep

Genesis 2:21Adam's sleep

The LORD God made a deep sleep fall on Adam. While Adam slept, God built something new, and gave it to him.

Genesis 15:12Abram's sleep

The same word. While Abram sleeps, God will make the covenant. The man is not asked to do a thing.

Romans 5:6A man at rest

“For when we were yet without strength, in due time Christ died for the ungodly.”

The “horror of great darkness” is a real fear. Faith does not feel like sunshine. Sometimes the promise comes with dread, and God is as near as ever.

God told him the whole schedule

  1. NowAbram, with no son
  2. soonIsaac is born
  3. about 1875 BCJacob's family goes down to Egypt
  4. about 1446 BCThe Exodusout “with great substance”
  5. about 1406 BCInto the land“in the fourth generation”

“Four hundred years” is a round number, and Bible teachers count the span a few different ways. Exodus 12:40 says four hundred and thirty. The point is not the arithmetic. God told Abram, before he had a single child, how long his family would wait, how they would suffer, and how they would leave.

“Now the sojourning of the children of Israel, who dwelt in Egypt, was four hundred and thirty years. And it came to pass at the end of the four hundred and thirty years, even the selfsame day it came to pass, that all the hosts of the LORD went out from the land of Egypt.”

Exodus 12:40-41

“The selfsame day.” When God sets a time, he keeps it to the day. Waiting is not the same as being forgotten.

Old words in verses 13–16

  1. suretycertainty. “Know of a surety” means know for certain.
  2. afflictto oppress, to treat harshly
  3. substancewealth, possessions
  4. iniquitysin, wrongdoing: the “unequal”, crooked thing
  5. hitherhere, to this place

“Fourth generation” fits a lifespan of about a hundred years: four generations is about four hundred years.

“Not yet full”

What God is doing in verse 16

Waiting before he judges

The people of Canaan, here called the Amorites, were doing evil. God did not judge them at once. He waited for centuries, until the cup was full.

What it means for you

His patience is a door

When God doesn't strike at once, it isn't because he approves. It is so a man can turn around.

“Or despisest thou the riches of his goodness and forbearance and longsuffering; not knowing that the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance?”

Romans 2:4

Be honest with yourself about this one. A man can read God's patience as permission: nothing happened last time, so it must be okay. It isn't permission. It is time to repent. Do not spend it.

“Thou shalt go to thy fathers in peace”

Verse 15 is for Abram himself. He will die at peace, in a good old age. He will not see his children enslaved, and he will not see them freed. He will not see the land his family finally owns.

Many Bible teachers hear more than a funeral here. “Go to thy fathers” and “be buried” are two different things in the verse, and his fathers were not buried anywhere near where he will be. They take it as a sign that Abram knew death was not the end.

“These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off, and were persuaded of them, and embraced them, and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.”

Hebrews 11:13

He died trusting. If you have trusted Christ, there is peace waiting for you at the end of the road, and it does not depend on how tidy your life looks on the way.

Part four

The furnace and the lamp

Verses 17–21

The covenant is cut, and only God walks.

One walker

After sunset, in the dark, a smoking pot of fire and a burning torch pass down the lane between the pieces. In the Bible, fire is the way God shows himself (the burning bush, Exodus 3:2; the pillar of fire, Exodus 13:21).

Where is Abram? Asleep. In a covenant like this, both sides normally walked between the pieces. Here, God walks alone.

That is the whole point of the chapter. God binds himself. He is the only one who can break it, and he will not.

What walking between the pieces meant

כָּרַת said karat “cut”: in verse 18, “made a covenant” is literally cut a covenant

Cutting a covenant meant cutting animals, and walking between the pieces. It was a way of swearing: may this happen to me, if I break my word. Jeremiah 34:18–19 describes this same custom, when people “cut the calf in twain, and passed between the parts thereof.”

So when God, and only God, walks between the pieces, he is saying: if this promise fails, let it fall on Me.

Who paid

Christians have long seen the cross behind this scene. The promise was never going to rest on Abram's strength. God put it on himself, and in Jesus he took the full weight of what it cost.

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

“Wherein God, willing more abundantly to shew unto the heirs of promise the immutability of his counsel, confirmed it by an oath: That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation, who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us:”

Hebrews 6:17-18

“Two immutable things”: God's promise, and his oath. It is impossible for God to lie. If your standing with him depended on your grip, you would worry. It depends on his.

The land, and ten nations

  1. Kenites
  2. Kenizzites
  3. Kadmonites
  4. Hittites
  5. Perizzites
  6. Rephaimsa tribe of very tall men, called giants
  7. Amorites

The promise has a boundary: “from the river of Egypt unto the great river, the river Euphrates.” That is far bigger than the land Israel ever lived in during the judges. It was reached in Solomon's day (1 Kings 4:21).

None of this had come true when Abram heard it. He owned no land but a grave (Genesis 23). God gave a promise first, and the land later. That is how it works with him: I have given comes before you can see it.

Question 1 of 6

God's heart

What does God say to a man who is afraid?

What verses tell us about this?

“But thou, O LORD, art a shield for me; my glory, and the lifter up of mine head.”

Psalm 3:3

Answer

He says Fear not, and then he tells the man why: I am thy shield. God didn't hand Abram a list of steps. He put himself between Abram and whatever he feared. If you are afraid today (of a relapse, a debt, a person), the answer is the same: you have a shield, and his name is the LORD.

Question 2 of 6

Honest questions

Can a man ask God hard questions and still be his friend?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God.”

James 2:23

Answer

Yes. Abram asked twice, honestly and respectfully (Lord GOD), and God answered with a sign and a covenant, never a scolding. Honest questions brought to God in prayer, and to your pastor, can be an act of faith. What God warns against is walking away from him while you ask.

Question 3 of 6

Salvation

How did Abram become right with God?

What verses tell us about this?

“But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness.”

Romans 4:5

“Even as Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness.”

Galatians 3:6

Answer

By believing. Abram trusted God's word, and God counted that trust as righteousness, before he had done anything to earn it. Paul says this is how every person is saved: not by wages, but by believing the One “that justifieth the ungodly”. For us the promise is Jesus. Trust him and he counts you righteous.

Question 4 of 6

God's word

Who keeps the promise, Abram or God?

What verses tell us about this?

“That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation, who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us:”

Hebrews 6:18

Answer

God. Abram was asleep, and the smoking furnace and burning lamp passed between the pieces alone. Both sides normally walked; here God took the whole thing on himself. That is why the promise cannot fail, and why a believer can be sure: it never depended on our grip.

Question 5 of 6

God's patience

Why did God make Abram's family wait four hundred years?

What verses tell us about this?

“Or despisest thou the riches of his goodness and forbearance and longsuffering; not knowing that the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance?”

Romans 2:4

Answer

Because God does not judge in a hurry. The people in the land had not yet filled up their sin, and God waited, even though the wait cost his own people. His patience is meant to lead to repentance. It is never permission to carry on. Read it as a warning, and as an open door.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

Did Abram live to see the promise come true?

What verses tell us about this?

“These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off, and were persuaded of them, and embraced them, and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.”

Hebrews 11:13

Answer

No. He had one promised son, and a grave, and a promise that outran his lifetime. But he “died in faith”, and every word God gave him came true, even the Exodus “the selfsame day”. If God can be trusted across four hundred years, he can be trusted with what you are waiting on today. Hold on, and keep your brothers close while you wait.
