Chapters 2 and 3 were on earth: seven struggling churches. Chapter 3 ended with a promise to sit with Christ “in his throne.”

Now John is taken up to see that throne. Before the storms of chapters 6 to 16, God shows him who is in charge.

Part one

A door opened in heaven

Verses 1–3

The first thing John sees in heaven isn't a calendar of the end. It's a throne, and Someone on it.

Two doors

At the end of chapter 3, Christ stood outside a man's door and knocked: “if any man… open the door, I will come in.”

Now a door opens the other way: heaven's door, and a voice like a trumpet: “Come up hither.”

Open your door to Him, and one day He opens His to you. The man who lets Christ in will be brought in.

“Come up hither”

Christ had told John to write “the things which thou hast seen, and the things which are, and the things which shall be hereafter” (1:19). Chapters 2–3 were “the things which are.” Now: “things which must be hereafter.”

many teachers

A picture of the church caught up

After chapter 3 the church isn't named on earth again until the last chapter. They see John's call as a picture of believers taken up before the troubles.

others

John's own call

They read it simply as John being shown heaven in a vision, with no timetable in it.

Good Baptist teachers hold both. Plain either way: the One who says “come up” is in control of everything that follows.

One sat on the throne

John doesn't describe a face or a body. He can only reach for jewels: jasper, brilliant and clear as crystal (21:11), and sardine, a deep blood-red stone.

Many teachers point out that a sardius was the first stone on the high priest's breastplate and a jasper the last (Exodus 28:17–20): God, from first to last.

And round the whole throne, a rainbow, green like an emerald.

A throne wrapped in a promise

“I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth.”

Genesis 9:13

After the flood, God hung His bow in the sky as a promise: He would never again destroy the earth by water. Ezekiel saw the same bow around God's glory (Ezekiel 1:28).

This is the throne that will judge the world in the chapters ahead, and it is circled by a promise. God's judgment never cancels His faithfulness. Mercy goes all the way around.

“No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.”

John 1:18

Part two

Round about the throne

Verses 4–8

Elders, fire, a sea like glass, and four living creatures who never stop saying one word: holy.

Twenty-four elders

Seated, in white, crowned with gold, all around the throne.

Who are they? Teachers differ. Many Baptist teachers see the redeemed, the church in heaven: white raiment, crowns and thrones are just what Christ promised the overcomers (2:10; 3:5; 3:21). Others see angels, or the twelve tribes and twelve apostles together (21:12–14).

Either way, what they do is plain, and we'll see it at the end: they worship.

Lightning, and seven lamps

“Lightnings and thunderings and voices”: the sound of Sinai, when God came down to give the law (Exodus 19:16). This is a holy throne.

The seven lamps “are the seven Spirits of God.” Seven is the number of fullness. Many teachers see the one Holy Spirit in all His fullness (Isaiah 11:2; Zechariah 4), burning before the throne.

That same Spirit lives in every believer from the day he is saved. The fire before the throne burns in you.

A sea of glass, and four beasts

Before the throne, a sea as still and clear as crystal. Nothing stirs it. Nothing troubles the peace around God.

And four “beasts.” In 1611 a beast meant any living creature, not a monster. These are living ones, full of eyes: nothing escapes God's sight.

Lion, calf, man, eagle: many teachers see the best of creation around its Maker: the strongest, the most patient worker, the wisest, the swiftest.

John wasn't the first to see them

Ezekiel 1:10

As for the likeness of their faces, they four had the face of a man, and the face of a lion, on the right side: and they four had the face of an ox on the left side; they four also had the face of an eagle.

Isaiah 6:2–3

Above it stood the seraphims: each one had six wings; with twain he covered his face, and with twain he covered his feet, and with twain he did fly. And one cried unto another, and said, Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory.

Ezekiel saw their faces; Isaiah heard their song. John sees and hears both. Many teachers take them to be the highest angels, the cherubims and seraphims nearest to God. The Bible explains its own pictures.

“Holy, holy, holy”

Day and night, without a break, the creatures closest to God say one thing about Him. Not mighty first. Holy: pure, set apart, wholly other than us.

  1. “which was”your pastHe was there
  2. “and is”todayHe is here
  3. “and is to come”what's aheadHe is already there

There's no part of your story He wasn't there for, and no part of your future He won't be in.

“Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever.”

Hebrews 13:8

Part three

Thou art worthy

Verses 9–11

The elders take off their crowns and lay them down. Everything they have, He gave.

They cast their crowns

When the four beasts give glory, the elders fall down too.

And they throw their crowns at His feet. The crowns were real; they'd been given them. But before Him they don't want to wear anything that makes them look big.

A man fresh out of the old life often wants a crown: respect, control, his name back. Here's the freedom: you don't have to run the world. He's worthy; you don't have to be.

You were made on purpose

Why is He worthy? “For thou hast created all things.” Everything exists because He wanted it to. “Pleasure” here means His will, what He chose.

That includes you. You are not an accident, a mistake, or a case number. Whatever your parents, your record or your past says, God wanted you here.

“I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well.”

Psalm 139:14

The throne is occupied

Next come seals, trumpets, war, famine and falling stars. But before John sees one seal broken, God shows him the throne, and Someone on it.

Whatever comes, it doesn't come from an empty throne. Nothing in these chapters catches God by surprise, and nothing in your life does either.

“The LORD hath prepared his throne in the heavens; and his kingdom ruleth over all.”

Psalm 103:19

Look up today

Points to remember

  1. Come up“A throne was set in heaven, and one sat on the throne” 4:2The first thing heaven shows John is a throne, occupied.
  2. The throne“Like a jasper and a sardine stone” 4:3Too bright to describe, and circled by a rainbow of promise.
  3. Elders“And cast their crowns before the throne” 4:10They lay every honour at His feet and worship.
  4. Beasts“Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty” 4:8Day and night: He is holy; He was, He is, and He is to come.
  5. Worthy“For thou hast created all things” 4:11He made everything, you included, on purpose.
  6. In charge“His kingdom ruleth over all” Psalm 103:19Before any trouble, the throne. Someone is in charge.

Question 1 of 6

Come up

What did John see when a door was opened in heaven?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A throne set in heaven, and One sitting on it. Before anything else, before any of the troubles to come, John is shown that God is on His throne.

Question 2 of 6

The throne

What does the One on the throne look like?

What verses tell us about this?

“No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.”

John 1:18

Answer

John can only describe Him with jewels: brilliant like jasper, red like sardine stone, with an emerald rainbow round the throne. He is too glorious to picture, but Jesus has made Him known.

Question 3 of 6

Elders

What do the twenty-four elders do with their crowns?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They fall down before Him, worship Him, and cast their gold crowns before the throne. Every honour they have, they give back to Him.

Question 4 of 6

Beasts

What do the four beasts say day and night?

What verses tell us about this?

“And one cried unto another, and said, Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory.”

Isaiah 6:3

Answer

“Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come.” They never stop praising His holiness, His power, and that He lives for ever.

Question 5 of 6

Worthy

Why is the Lord worthy to receive glory and honour and power?

What verses tell us about this?

“I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well.”

Psalm 139:14

Answer

Because He created all things, and everything exists because He willed it. That includes us: we were made on purpose, by Him.

Question 6 of 6

In charge

Why does John see the throne before the troubles that follow?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD hath prepared his throne in the heavens; and his kingdom ruleth over all.”

Psalm 103:19

Answer

So that whatever comes next, we know the throne is not empty. God is in charge of everything that follows, and nothing catches Him by surprise.
