Acts 24

Go thy way for this time

A lawyer flatters, a prisoner tells the truth, and a governor trembles. Then he says the most dangerous words in the Bible: not yet.

Part one

The accusation

Verses 1–9

Five days later, the high priest arrives in Caesarea with a lawyer.

Flattery first

An orator was a hired professional speaker, a lawyer. Tertullus opens by buttering up the governor:

he said“great quietness”“very worthy deeds”“most noble Felix”
not quitenot quitenot quite
butunrest, put down harda taste for bribes (v. 26)a freed slave with a governor's power

Roman writers remember Felix as cruel and greedy. Tertullus was paid to say otherwise. He also leaves out that Lysias rescued Paul from a mob that wanted him dead. He calls it “great violence.”

A man who flatters wants something from you. Watch for it in the voices around you, and watch for it in yourself. Compare how Paul opens his answer in verse 10: respectful, and true.

Four charges, one true

  • “a pestilent fellow”a plague, a troublemaker. Nobody could name what he'd done
  • “a mover of sedition”stirring up riots worldwide. No witness stood up
  • “gone about to profane the temple”he was found there quietly purified, not causing a scene
  • “the sect of the Nazarenes”followers of Jesus of Nazareth. That one he'd never deny.

The only charge that stuck was the best one to be guilty of. Peter says it for every believer:

“But let none of you suffer as a murderer, or as a thief, or as an evildoer, or as a busybody in other men’s matters. Yet if any man suffer as a Christian, let him not be ashamed; but let him glorify God on this behalf.”

1 Peter 4:15–16

Part two

Paul's defence

Verses 10–23

No flattery, no panic. Just what happened, what he believes, and a clean conscience.

Twelve days

  1. Day 1Reaches JerusalemActs 21:17
  2. About day 7Seized in the templeActs 21:27
  3. ThenCouncil, plot, night rideActs 22–23
  4. Day 12On trial before FelixActs 24:11

Paul just states the facts, and they're easy to check. In twelve days, spread over a trip to the temple, an arrest and a night ride, when was he supposed to start a worldwide uprising?

He also puts the weight where it belongs: “Neither can they prove” it. The accusers had to prove their charges, and they had no witnesses. An honest man can stand on what he really did.

“The way, which they call heresy”

αἵρεσις said hairesis a choice, a party, a sect

In the King James, “heresy” just means a sect or party. It's the same word used for the Sadducees and the Pharisees. They called following Jesus “a sect.” Paul answers: it's the Way, and it's not new. It's the same God of his fathers, “believing all things which are written in the law and in the prophets.”

And the hope is shared: “a resurrection of the dead, both of the just and unjust.” Everyone rises, and everyone answers to God. For the one who trusts Jesus, that's good news, not fear.

“And as it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment:”

Hebrews 9:27

A conscience void of offence

The old way

Dull it

Bury it, drink it quiet, keep busy, argue with it. The guilt just waits for the next 2 a.m.

The way out

Clean it

Tell God the truth and be forgiven: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9).

“Void of offence” means nothing left that accuses you. Paul isn't claiming he never failed. He says “herein do I exercise myself”, which is training language, and “always.” He works at it daily, the way you train a muscle.

And notice the two directions: toward God, and toward men. One thing for today: if there's somebody you owe an apology or a repayment, that's the next step, and it's how a conscience gets quiet for real. Hebrews 9:14 says the blood of Christ can “purge your conscience from dead works.”

He came to give

After years away, Paul came to Jerusalem carrying alms, a gift of money from the churches of Greece and Macedonia for the poor believers there.

That's his answer to “troublemaker”: a man who crossed the sea to bring a gift. The Jews from Asia who started the trouble found him “purified in the temple, neither with multitude, nor with tumult.”

He had nothing to hide, and he told it plainly. A life that's been put right can say what it did, without spin.

What the case was missing

  1. ✗Who ought to have been here before thee, and object, if they had ought against me.The men from Asia who began it never camev. 19
  2. ✗found me purified in the temple, neither with multitude, nor with tumultNo riot, no crowdv. 18
  3. ✗Except it be for this one voice, that I cried standing among themThe only “crime” before the council was one sentence about the resurrectionv. 21

A fair judge would have let him go. Felix didn't. We'll see why.

Knowing more is not deciding

Felix knew. He stalled.

Felix had “more perfect knowledge of that way.” His wife was Jewish, and he'd been governor for years. He knew enough to see Paul was no criminal. And he “deferred” the case: postponed it, waiting for Lysias, who as far as we read never came.

Meanwhile, Paul got “liberty”, with his friends free to come and look after him. God gave him people even in a cell. (Philip the evangelist lived in Caesarea, 21:8, and Luke was likely nearby.)

It's one thing to know about Jesus. It's another to decide. Felix is about to show us the difference.

Part three

Felix trembled

Verses 24–27

The governor and his wife send for the prisoner, and hear more than they bargained for.

A governor, his wife, and a prisoner with the truth

Herod the Greather great-grandfather, who tried to kill the baby Jesus (Matthew 2)
Herod Agrippa Iher father, who killed James and was struck down (Acts 12)
Drusillanow sitting in a room, hearing about Christ from a prisoner

Drusilla was a Jewish princess, very young, and by the Jewish historian Josephus she had left her first husband to marry Felix. Felix had been a slave, freed, and risen to rule Judaea.

Think what it took for Paul to speak. He was a prisoner. Felix could free him or have him killed. He still told the truth, because that's what these two needed to hear.

Three things Felix didn't want to hear

Righteousness

Living right with God and men. Felix wanted bribes (v. 26).

Temperance

Self-control, mastery over appetites. Felix was ruled by his. This is the word in Galatians 5:23, a fruit of the Spirit.

Judgment to come

One day everyone answers to God. Felix had a lot to answer for.

Felix trembled.

“Trembled” means he became afraid. That's conviction: God's Spirit pressing on a man's conscience. Jesus said He would “reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment” (John 16:8). Conviction is a gift. It points to a door and says this way. Shame only says hide. Paul didn't call Felix names. He told him the truth and where it ends.

“When I have a convenient season”

Today Tomorrow Next week After things calm down A convenient season

The convenient season never comes. There is always one more reason: work, a bad week, a better time. “For this time” is a polite way to say no.

Every man here has said it. “I'll deal with God once things settle.” “I'll quit Monday.” “I'll make that call after the holidays.” Felix felt it, and he put it off. Trembling doesn't save anyone. Believing does.

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

How it ended for Felix

  1. The dayFelix tremblesv. 25
  2. Next“Go thy way for this time”v. 25
  3. OftenSends for Paul, hoping for a bribev. 26
  4. Two yearsPaul in custodyv. 27
  5. The endFestus takes over. Paul left bound.v. 27

Felix did send for Paul “the oftener”, but not to hear about Christ. He “hoped… that money should have been given him.” He talked with Paul, and wanted a bribe. When he left office, he “left Paul bound” to please the Jews. His parting favor to the Jews cost an innocent man his freedom.

We aren't told that Felix ever believed. The record just ends. That's the danger of not yet: nobody announces the last chance. Is there something you've been putting off with God? Today is the day.

Two kinds of waiting

Felix put God off, and nothing came of it. Paul waited on God for two years in Caesarea, about AD 57 to 59, and the road led to Rome, just as the Lord had said (23:11).

From the outside they look alike: two years go by. But Paul wasn't running out the clock. He was trusting a promise he'd been given.

If you're waiting in a hard place, a program, a job, a courtroom, a long recovery, you can wait like Paul. Not “not yet”, but “Lord, I'm here. I'm Yours. Take me where You said.”

Points to remember

  1. Flattery“Most noble Felix” 24:3Smooth words usually want something. Be wary of flattery, and tell the truth kindly.
  2. Honesty“Neither can they prove the things whereof they now accuse me” 24:13Say what really happened, plainly. A life put right can stand on the facts.
  3. Conscience“A conscience void of offence toward God, and toward men” 24:16Don't dull it. Confess, be cleansed, and make it right with people today.
  4. Hope“A resurrection of the dead, both of the just and unjust” 24:15There is a day ahead. In Christ it is hope, not dread.
  5. Conviction“Felix trembled” 24:25If God is pressing on you, that's a door opening, not a judge's finger.
  6. Delay“Go thy way for this time” 24:25There is no convenient season. “Now is the day of salvation.”

Question 1 of 6

Flattery

How did Tertullus try to win over the governor?

What verses tell us about this?

“A man that flattereth his neighbour spreadeth a net for his feet.”

Proverbs 29:5

Answer

With flattery: “great quietness,” “very worthy deeds,” “most noble Felix.” A hired lawyer, he buttered up the governor before he made his charges. The Bible says flattery is a net for someone's feet.

Question 2 of 6

Honesty

How did Paul answer the charges against him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Plainly, with the facts and no flattery. Twelve days in Jerusalem, no riot, no crowd, and no witnesses who could prove a thing. Paul admitted the one true charge, that he followed Jesus, and held to it.

Question 3 of 6

Conscience

What did Paul say he worked at keeping?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“How much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?”

Hebrews 9:14

Answer

“A conscience void of offence toward God, and toward men.” He worked at it always, daily, like training. A clean conscience comes from confessing and being forgiven through Christ's blood, and from making things right with people.

Question 4 of 6

Hope

What hope did Paul say he shared with his accusers?

What verses tell us about this?

“And as it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment:”

Hebrews 9:27

Answer

The resurrection of the dead, both the just and the unjust. Paul's whole case came down to that one hope. Everyone will rise and answer to God, and for the man who trusts Jesus, that day is hope, not dread.

Question 5 of 6

Conviction

What made Felix tremble?

What verses tell us about this?

“And when he is come, he will reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment:”

John 16:8

Answer

Paul reasoned about righteousness, self-control and the judgment to come, and Felix's own life was in every one of them. That fear was conviction, God's Spirit pressing on a conscience. It is meant to lead a man to Christ.

Question 6 of 6

Delay

What did Felix say instead of believing?

What verses tell us about this?

“Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth.”

Proverbs 27:1

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

Answer

“Go thy way for this time; when I have a convenient season, I will call for thee.” Not no, just not yet. The Bible doesn't record that he ever called. Now is the accepted time. Don't wait for a convenient season, because tomorrow is not promised.
