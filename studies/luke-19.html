Luke 19

To seek and to save

A hated tax man up a tree, ten servants and ten pounds, a King riding a colt down the mount of Olives, and Jesus weeping over Jerusalem.

Part one

Come down

Verses 1–10

The richest crook in Jericho climbs a tree, and Jesus stops right under it.

Jericho

Jesus is on His last trip to Jerusalem, a few days before the Passover. Jericho sat near the Jordan, the last town on the road before the long climb up to the city.

It was a rich town: palm trees, balsam groves, a winter palace, and trade coming through from the east. Where goods move, somebody collects the tolls. In Jericho, the man at the top of that business was Zacchaeus.

The chief publican

Publicans were Jews who collected taxes and tolls for Rome. A chief publican probably held the contract for a whole district, with other collectors working under him.

Romewants its moneyZacchaeushires collectorspublicans
publicanstake Rome's share, and morehis own peoplepay, and hate him for itZacchaeus

Whatever they squeezed out above what Rome asked, they kept. So Zacchaeus was rich, and everybody knew how he got that way. To his neighbors he was a traitor working for the occupiers, and a thief.

Up a tree

He sought to see Jesus who he was. Something in him wanted more than money. But he was short, and the crowd wasn't going to make room for the tax man.

So he ran ahead and climbed a sycomore, a fig tree with a short, thick trunk and low, spreading branches. Rich men in robes didn't run, and they didn't climb trees. He didn't care how it looked.

A man who's tired of the life he's in will do things he'd have been too proud to do once. That's not foolish. That's hungry.

He knew his name

זַכַּי said Zakkai “pure, clean”

His name likely comes from the Hebrew Zakkai. The whole town would have laughed at that: Mr. Clean, the biggest crook in Jericho. Jesus stopped under the tree, looked up, and called him by it anyway.

Zacchaeus thought he was the one looking for Jesus. It turns out Jesus came to that tree looking for him.

Make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house.

No lecture first. No “clean up and then we'll talk.” Jesus invites Himself to dinner, in front of everybody, at the house of the most hated man in town.

I must: this was no accident. It was why He came through Jericho. And to day: not when Zacchaeus had his life together. Today.

He came down fast and received him joyfully. That's what it looks like to receive Christ: you hear Him call, and you let Him in.

“Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me.”

Revelation 3:20

Two speeches

The crowd, to each otherHe is gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner.

Zacchaeus, standing upBehold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold. nobody asked him to

The crowd was right about one thing: he was a sinner. What they missed is that sinners are exactly who Jesus eats with (15:2). The ones murmuring had nothing to give away. The man they despised was giving away half of everything.

Jesus never told him to do it. Nobody made a rule. When Jesus comes into a man's house, the money starts to let go of him.

Making it right

100taken by fraud
120what the law asked: pay it back, and add a fifth
400what Zacchaeus paid: fourfold, the price for a stolen sheep

“If a man shall steal an ox, or a sheep, and kill it, or sell it; he shall restore five oxen for an ox, and four sheep for a sheep.”

Exodus 22:1

The law asked a fraud to pay back what he took plus a fifth (Numbers 5:7). Zacchaeus called it what it was, plain stealing, and paid the thief's price. Making amends was hard and costly, and he did it gladly.

Which came first?

  1. he sought“to see Jesus”
  2. Jesus called“Zacchaeus, … come down”grace first
  3. he received Him“joyfully”
  4. he made it right“fourfold”
  5. salvation“This day”

Zacchaeus didn't fix his life so Jesus would come. Jesus came, and Zacchaeus received Him, and then his life started to change. The giving was the fruit, not the price.

“Or despisest thou the riches of his goodness and forbearance and longsuffering; not knowing that the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance?”

Romans 2:4

A new name for him

a man that is a sinner → a son of Abraham What the crowd called him (19:7), and what Jesus called him (19:9).

He had always been a Jew by birth. Now he was a son of Abraham the way that counts: a man who believed God, like Abraham did. Last chapter it was a daughter of Abraham set free (13:16). Now a son comes home.

He was chief of the publicans. Paul later called himself chief of something else:

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

The Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.

This is the whole gospel of Luke in one line. Lost isn't an insult here. It's what Jesus goes looking for: the sheep, the coin, the son in chapter 15, and a little man up a tree.

God promised long before that He would come looking Himself:

“I will seek that which was lost, and bring again that which was driven away, and will bind up that which was broken, and will strengthen that which was sick: but I will destroy the fat and the strong; I will feed them with judgment.”

Ezekiel 34:16

If you've ever felt too far gone, that's the kind of man He came for. He's not waiting for you to find your way back. He's out looking.

Part two

Occupy till I come

Verses 11–27

A king goes away, leaves his servants one coin each, and comes back to see what they did with it.

A story they'd seen happen

The crowd expected Jesus to walk into Jerusalem and take the throne that week. So He told a story the people of Jericho knew from real life. When Herod the Great died, his son Archelaus had to go to Rome to be made king.

  1. about 4 BCHerod the Great dies
  2. a far countryArchelaus sails to Rome
  3. a message after him“we will not have this man”
  4. he returnswith powerhis enemies pay

Jesus is the true Nobleman. He was about to go away, to the cross, the tomb, and back to the Father. He would receive the kingdom, and He would come back. In between, His servants have work to do.

One pound each

1 pounda mina: 100 pence, about a hundred days' wages
10 servantsten pounds handed out
1 eachthe same for every man

Occupy meant “do business with it.” Put it to work. Every servant got the same amount, and the same order. What God has given every believer, His word, His Spirit, the good news, and today, isn't meant to sit.

Meanwhile, the citizens weren't servants at all. They hated him. That's the world's answer to Jesus: We will not have this man to reign over us.

Faithful in a very little

One pound wasn't much for a man about to rule cities. That was the point. The king was finding out who could be trusted with more.

Notice the servants' words: thy pound hath gained. Not “my hard work.” It was his pound all along.

And the third man? We'll hear his excuse next.

“He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much: and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much.”

Luke 16:10

A very little, today

Most of us don't get handed anything big at first. We get one day. One honest conversation. One shift worked. One call to a brother instead of a call to the old crowd.

That's the pound. Being faithful in that very little is not small to the King. He sees it, He remembers it, and He rewards it.

Nobody here is saved by his trading. These are servants already. The question for a servant is what he did with what his Lord put in his hands.

The third man's excuse

The servantLord, behold, here is thy pound, which I have kept laid up in a napkin: for I feared thee, because thou art an austere man.

The noblemanOut of thine own mouth will I judge thee. Wherefore then gavest not thou my money into the bank? not even that

Austere means harsh and hard. The servant had a wrong picture of his master, and the wrong picture froze him. He did nothing and called it being careful. He didn't lose the pound. He just buried it in a napkin and blamed the master.

A man who thinks God is out to get him will hide. But the God of this chapter climbs down the road to a crook's house and calls him by name. Don't let fear of failing keep you from trying.

When the King comes back

His servants

Judged by what they did

Given something to use. The faithful get more to rule over. The one who hid his pound loses it. Use what He gives, and He gives more.

His enemies

Judged by whom they refused

Never His servants at all. Their crime was simple: would not that I should reign over them. They are brought and slain.

That last line is sober, and Jesus means it to be. Every man will one day face the King he either served or refused. Today there's still time to stop sending messages after Him that say “not this man,” and bow to Him instead.

Part three

Blessed be the King

Verses 28–40

A borrowed colt, a road paved with coats, and a crowd that can't keep quiet.

Ascending up

From Jericho to Jerusalem the road climbs about 3,300 feet in about 15 miles. He went before: Jesus walked at the front.

He knew what was waiting at the top. He climbed anyway.

Bethany and Bethphage were villages on the mount of Olives, the ridge just east of the city.

A colt, tied up

A colt no man had ever ridden: young, untrained, and set apart for this one use. Jesus told them exactly where it would be and what the owners would say, and it was even as he had said.

Kings went to war on horses. A king who came in peace rode a donkey. Matthew and John both point back to this promise:

“Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion; shout, O daughter of Jerusalem: behold, thy King cometh unto thee: he is just, and having salvation; lowly, and riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass.”

Zechariah 9:9

One more thing: loose him. A tied-up animal nobody had ever used, untied because the Lord hath need of him. He can use a man like that too.

The King that cometh

Spreading coats on the road was how Israel crowned a king (Jehu, 2 Kings 9:13). The crowd sang Psalm 118, the song pilgrims sang on the way up to the feast, but they added a word: King. And their last line sounds like another song Luke has already given us:

Luke 2:14, the angels at His birth

Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men.

Luke 19:38, the disciples on the road

Blessed be the King that cometh in the name of the Lord: peace in heaven, and glory in the highest.

The angels sang of peace on earth when He was born. Now He's riding toward the cross, where He would make peace between God and men by His blood (Colossians 1:20).

If these should hold their peace

Some PhariseesMaster, rebuke thy disciples.

JesusI tell you that, if these should hold their peace, the stones would immediately cry out. praise will happen

The Pharisees heard the crowd calling Him King, and they wanted it stopped. Jesus wouldn't stop it. This day was coming one way or another. If men kept quiet, the rocks along the road would shout it.

“For the stone shall cry out of the wall, and the beam out of the timber shall answer it.”

Habakkuk 2:11

If a man has seen what Jesus has done for him, it's hard to keep quiet about it. Don't let anybody talk you out of thanking Him out loud.

Part four

He wept over it

Verses 41–48

At the top of the ridge, with the crowd still cheering, Jesus looks at the city and cries.

Tears in the parade

Everyone around Him is singing. He's crying, and not quietly: the word means He wept out loud. From the mount of Olives you look straight across the valley at the whole city and the gold and white temple.

The things which belong unto thy peace. Jerusalem's own name sounds like peace in Hebrew. The Prince of peace was riding in, and the city couldn't see Him.

He wasn't weeping for Himself, days from the cross. He was weeping for them. That's the heart of God toward people who are turning Him down.

About forty years later

Jerusalem rose up against Rome. In about AD 70, Roman armies under Titus built siege works and a wall all the way around the city so no one could get in or out. Thousands starved inside.

Then they burned the temple and pulled it down, stone by stone, just as Jesus said.

He didn't say it gladly. He said it crying.

This thy day

The time of thy visitation was the time God Himself came to see them. Zacchaeus knew his day when it came: he climbed, he came down, he said yes. The city missed its day.

Some of us have missed days before. If you're reading this, you haven't missed this one.

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

A house of prayer, a den of thieves

He rides into the city and goes straight to the temple. The outer court, the only place a Gentile could come to pray, was full of sellers and money changers, and the prices were a racket. Jesus throws them out with two lines from the prophets:

Isaiah 56:7

… for mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people.

Jeremiah 7:11

Is this house, which is called by my name, become a den of robbers in your eyes? Behold, even I have seen it, saith the LORD.

A den is where robbers hide out after the job. They thought the temple made them safe. God's house was supposed to be where any man could come and meet Him, not where religious men cashed in.

ἐξεκρέματο said exekremato “hung on to Him”

Very attentive is one Greek word: the people were hanging on His every word. The chief priests and scribes wanted Him dead, but they couldn't touch Him in front of a crowd like that. Not yet.

Two kinds of people in the temple: men looking for a way to get rid of Jesus, and men who couldn't stop listening. Be the second kind. Keep coming back, every day, to hear Him.

Points to remember

  1. Seen“Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down” 19:5He climbed to see Jesus. Jesus already knew his name and was coming to his house.
  2. Made right“I restore him fourfold” 19:8Grace came first; making things right followed, gladly.
  3. The lost“To seek and to save that which was lost” 19:10Nobody is too far gone. Jesus is out looking.
  4. Faithful“Faithful in a very little” 19:17Use what He's given you today. The King notices.
  5. The King“Blessed be the King that cometh in the name of the Lord” 19:38He came in peace, on a colt, to make peace at the cross.
  6. Tears“He beheld the city, and wept over it” 19:41God grieves over those who miss their day. Today is yours.

Question 1 of 6

Seen

What did Zacchaeus do to see Jesus, and what did Jesus say to him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He was too short to see over the crowd, so he ran ahead and climbed a sycomore tree. Jesus stopped, looked up, called him by name, and said, “make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house.”

Question 2 of 6

Made right

When Jesus came to his house, what did Zacchaeus promise to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To give half his goods to the poor, and to pay back anyone he had cheated four times over, far more than the law required. Nobody made him. He had received Jesus joyfully, and making it right came out of that.

Question 3 of 6

The lost

Why did Jesus say the Son of man came?

What verses tell us about this?

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

Answer

To seek and to save that which was lost. He goes looking for sinners, and He saves the ones who receive Him. That day salvation came to the house of a man everyone else had written off.

Question 4 of 6

Faithful

What did the nobleman say to the servant who was faithful in a very little?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much: and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much.”

Luke 16:10

Answer

“Well, thou good servant: because thou hast been faithful in a very little, have thou authority over ten cities.” The King rewards faithfulness with small things by giving much more.

Question 5 of 6

The King

What did the disciples shout as Jesus came down the mount of Olives?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Blessed be the King that cometh in the name of the Lord: peace in heaven, and glory in the highest.” When the Pharisees wanted them silenced, Jesus said the stones would cry out instead.

Question 6 of 6

Tears

Why did Jesus weep over Jerusalem?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the city didn't know the things that belonged to its peace. God had come to visit them, and they missed it, and He could see the destruction that was coming. He wept because He loved them.
