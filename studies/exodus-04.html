Exodus 4

I will be with thy mouth

Moses keeps finding reasons it should be someone else. The LORD answers every one: a rod, a hand, a brother, and a promise. Then Moses goes home to Egypt, and the people bow down and worship.

Part one

“What is that in thine hand?”

Verses 1–9

Moses says they will not believe him. God begins with what Moses is already holding.

“What is that in thine hand?”

Moses held a shepherd's rod: a plain stick for walking and for the sheep. “A rod.” That is the whole of his answer. The LORD does not ask for something better. He asks for what is already in his hand.

Moses has just said: they will not believe me. The LORD had already told him the opposite:

“And they shall hearken to thy voice”

Exodus 3:18

“God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty;”

1 Corinthians 1:27

Real world: what is in your hand today? A phone to call a brother. A story you have been ashamed of. A job that needs doing. Give God that, not the thing you wish you had.

By the tail

Moses had just run from the snake (verse 3). Now the LORD says: take it by the tail. That is the one way no man ever picks up a snake: the end that is hardest to control. Moses did it anyway, and it “became a rod in his hand.”

Notice when the sign happened. It happened as Moses reached out, not before. God asked for the frightening step first, and the power was there when he took it.

That they may believe (verse 5) is what a sign is for: a mark that points to the One who sent it. The LORD was not showing off. He was making it easier for tired slaves to believe that he had really come.

A word for the men here: often the very thing we ran from is the thing he asks us to take hold of, a fear, a debt, a phone call, a conversation. He does not ask us to do it alone.

Three signs

  • A roda stick becomes a serpent, and back again (3–4)
  • A handclean, then leprous as snow, then clean again (6–7)
  • The riverwater poured on dry ground turns to blood (9)
  • leprousa skin disease that made a man “unclean” and put him outside the camp (Leviticus 13). Not exactly the leprosy of today.
  • bosomthe front fold of a robe, against the chest, where a man tucked his hand
  • latterthe later one, the second

Verse 8 calls the first sign a voice: “neither hearken to the voice of the first sign.” A sign speaks. Only God could make a hand sick and whole again in a moment, and nobody in the story can do that.

The third sign foreshadows the first plague. The Nile was Egypt's life and food. When the river turns to blood, in chapter 7, Moses will already have heard it from God here.

Part two

“I will be with thy mouth”

Verses 10–17

Five times in two chapters Moses finds a reason it should be someone else.

“Who hath made man’s mouth?”

MosesO my Lord, I am not eloquent, neither heretofore, nor since thou hast spoken unto thy servant: but I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue.

the LORDWho hath made man’s mouth? … have not I the LORD? Now therefore go, and I will be with thy mouth, and teach thee what thou shalt say.

Eloquent means a fluent speaker. Heretofore means up until now. Dumb in verse 11 means unable to speak.

The LORD does not say, “You are wrong about your speech.” He says, I will be with thy mouth. God's answer to our weakness is not a better us. It is his presence.

The man who used to talk

“And Moses was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and was mighty in words and in deeds.”

Acts 7:22

Forty years earlier, in Pharaoh's house, he had been a man of words. The wilderness years, and what he had done before them (2:11–15), took the swagger out of him and left him sure he was no use to God.

Many men carry an old failure as if it were their name. “I am not eloquent” can be the voice of a man who once was, and stopped believing it. God heard the failure and the fear, and still said go.

Five ways of saying “not me”

  • “Who am I?”3:11, answered: Certainly I will be with thee
  • “What is his name?”3:13, answered: I AM THAT I AM
  • “They will not believe me.”4:1, answered with three signs
  • “I am not eloquent.”4:10, answered: I will be with thy mouth
  • “Send somebody else.”4:13, and here the LORD's anger is kindled
  • “Go, and I will be with thee.”the same answer every time

Every excuse had some truth in it: he was nobody, he was slow of speech. And every excuse got the same reply: I will be with you. The last one was different. It was not a question. It was a man saying, “I would rather not.”

Anger, and a brother

“The anger of the LORD was kindled.” God's patience is long, and he still corrects his own. And in the very same verse, he sends help: Is not Aaron the Levite thy brother?

He did not drop Moses. He gave him what he was afraid to go without.

“My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

A brother already on the road

the LORDwordsMoses“put words in his mouth”Aaronspokesmanthe people

Wherewith (verse 17) means with which. A spokesman is a man who speaks for another. Moses was to Aaron “instead of God”: the one Aaron listened to for God's words. It does not make Moses a god. It describes who spoke first, and who repeated it.

Look at verse 14 again: Aaron “cometh forth to meet thee: and when he seeth thee, he will be glad in his heart.” The helper was already walking before Moses asked. God had prepared a brother, and the brother would be glad to see him.

You are not meant to do this by yourself. Say it to your brothers in Christ: what you are afraid of, what you cannot do. Let one of them speak with you.

Part three

“Go in peace”

Verses 18–31

A man goes back to the place he ran from, and a brother comes out to meet him.

“Go, return into Egypt”

Moses asks his father-in-law Jethro's leave: Let me go… and see whether they be yet alive. “Go in peace.”

Then the LORD, in Midian, gives the reason it is safe: “all the men are dead which sought thy life.” The thing Moses ran from (2:15) was over, and he had stayed in the desert anyway.

Regret: the fear that sent you away may have ended without your knowing. He sets out with his wife and sons “upon an ass” (a donkey).

“I will harden his heart”

  1. 4:21God tells Moses firstso Moses is not surprised
  2. 8:15, 8:32, 9:34Pharaoh hardens his own heartagain and again
  3. 9:12, 10:20the LORD confirms himin what he chose

Read it in the order the book gives it. God knows Pharaoh, and tells Moses ahead, so that when Pharaoh says no, Moses will not think he has failed.

How a heart hardens

“when Pharaoh saw that there was respite, he hardened his heart, and hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had said.”

Exodus 8:15

First, Pharaoh does it to himself. The LORD's own hardening comes only after that:

“And the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh”

Exodus 9:12

A heart hardens one “not yet” at a time. Each time Pharaoh said no after the pain eased, the next no came easier. The warning for every man here: do not put off for one more day what you know he is asking.

“Israel is my son”

Israel is my son, even my firstborn.

A nation of slaves, and God calls them his son. The firstborn is the son with the place of honour. And he wants them freed to serve him, not freed to nothing: Let my son go, that he may serve me.

“Out of Egypt have I called my son.”

Matthew 2:15

Matthew says that was fulfilled again when Jesus came back out of Egypt as a child. The warning in verse 23 comes early, before any plague: mercy gives a long time to turn around.

At the inn

A hard few verses, so here they are plainly. An inn was a place to stop for the night on the road. The LORD “met him, and sought to kill him”: Moses was in danger of death, and the text does not say how.

The reason is the one thing in the story left undone. Circumcision was the sign God gave Abraham for his covenant:

“that soul shall be cut off from his people; he hath broken my covenant.”

Genesis 17:14

Moses' son had not been circumcised. The man about to tell Pharaoh “Israel is my son” was neglecting God's sign in his own house.

What happened, and what it means

Zipporah, his Midianite wife, acted fast with a sharp stone, and “he let him go.” Her words, “a bloody husband”, are sharp, and the text does not explain them. Teachers differ, so do not build on them.

What you can build on: a man who leads cannot leave God's plain word undone at home. And when it was put right, the danger stopped. God did not throw Moses away. He dealt with him, and let him go on.

Real world: is there one thing God has already told you to do that you have put off? Do it today, before you carry it into tomorrow.

A brother meets him

The LORD tells Aaron to go out into the wilderness, and Aaron finds Moses at “the mount of God”, the same mountain, Horeb, where Moses met him at the bush. And he kissed him, as brothers did. Moses was eighty and Aaron eighty-three (7:7).

Then Moses told Aaron all the words of the LORD… and all the signs. All of it. Moses did not carry this alone or keep it secret. He told his brother everything.

That is what a brother is for. If you have a man in your life who is glad to see you and knows the whole story, thank God for him today. If you do not, ask the Lord for one, and ask your pastor.

“The people believed”

What Moses feared in verse 1 did not happen. The elders gathered, Aaron spoke, the signs were done, and “the people believed.” What turned them was not the signs alone. It was what they heard: the LORD had visited… and… had looked upon their affliction.

“I die: and God will surely visit you, and bring you out of this land unto the land which he sware to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.”

Genesis 50:24

Joseph said it more than three hundred years before. Visit means God coming in person to help. Now it was true, and they “bowed their heads and worshipped.” When you hear that God has seen your affliction, bow first.

Believing is not the end of trouble. The next chapter will show how hard it gets.

Question 1 of 6

Signs

What three signs did the LORD give Moses, and what were they for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A rod that became a serpent and back, a hand that went leprous and was healed, and river water that would turn to blood, all “that they may believe.” God started with what Moses held. Give him what is in your hand.

Question 2 of 6

Excuses

What was Moses' next excuse, and how did the LORD answer it?

What verses tell us about this?

“My grace is sufficient for thee”

2 Corinthians 12:9

Answer

He said he was no speaker. The LORD did not argue. He said: who made your mouth? I will be with it. God's answer to our weakness is his presence, not a better version of us.

Question 3 of 6

Anger and grace

How did the LORD answer Moses' last “send somebody else”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God was angry, and still he gave Moses a brother who was already walking to meet him and would be glad to see him. His patience is long, he still corrects his own, and his mercy is already on the road. Do not wait for a perfect moment; go with the brother he gives you.

Question 4 of 6

Pharaoh

What did the LORD tell Moses about Pharaoh before he even went?

What verses tell us about this?

“he hardened his heart, and hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had said.”

Exodus 8:15

“And the LORD hardened the heart of Pharaoh”

Exodus 9:12

Answer

He told Moses ahead that Pharaoh would not let them go, so Moses would not take it for his own failure. Then the book shows Pharaoh hardening his own heart first, and the LORD confirming him afterward. A heart hardens one “not yet” at a time.

Question 5 of 6

The inn

Why did the LORD meet Moses at the inn?

What verses tell us about this?

“that soul shall be cut off from his people; he hath broken my covenant.”

Genesis 17:14

Answer

Because Moses had left God's covenant sign undone on his own son, while he was on his way to lead God's people. When it was put right, he was let go. Do the thing he has already told you, beginning at home, and do not wait.

Question 6 of 6

Belief

How did Israel answer when they heard that God had visited them?

What verses tell us about this?

“I die: and God will surely visit you”

Genesis 50:24

Answer

They believed, and they bowed and worshipped, because God had visited them and looked on their affliction, just as Joseph had said. When you hear that he has seen what you are carrying, bow first, and then tell your brothers.
