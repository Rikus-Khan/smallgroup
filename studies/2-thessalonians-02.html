2 Thessalonians 2

Don't be shaken

Someone told the church the day of Christ had already come. Paul calms them down: it hasn't, and when it does come, Christ wins.

Chapter 1 promised rest “when the Lord Jesus shall be revealed from heaven.” Now Paul gets to the reason he wrote: a false word had the church in a panic that the day had already come and they'd missed it.

This is one of the hardest chapters in the New Testament. We'll go slowly, say plainly what's plain, and be honest where good Bible teachers disagree.

Part one

Don't be shaken

Verses 1–5

A scary rumor is going round the church. Paul answers it with the facts.

Where the panic came from

“By spirit”

v. 2

Somebody stood up claiming God had shown him something. It sounded spiritual. It wasn't true.

“By word”

v. 2

A teaching, a sermon, a report passed along: “Paul says…”

“By letter as from us”

v. 2

Maybe even a forged letter with Paul's name on it. That's why he signs this one with his own hand (3:17).

“At hand” here means already here. They'd been told the day of the Lord had come, and with all the persecution of chapter 1, it felt true. Had they missed Jesus? Was this the wrath?

Steady

Shaken is the word for a boat torn loose from its mooring and tossed by the waves. Troubled means alarmed, the jolt you feel when you hear a siren. Paul's answer starts with what they already knew: Jesus is coming for His own, and they'll be gathered together unto him.

“Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord.”

1 Thessalonians 4:17

“Let not your heart be troubled: ye believe in God, believe also in me.”

John 14:1

There are still people who sell fear about the end: a date, a video, a sign in the news. Don't be shaken by any of it. Open the Book, check what it actually says, and go do the next right thing.

Paul's answer, in one line

Ifthe man of sin has not been revealed,
→
thenthe day of Christ has not come. You haven't missed it.

Two things come first, Paul says. “A falling away”: a great turning away from the truth. And “that man of sin” shown for who he is. Neither had happened, so the rumor was false.

Paul isn't giving them a date to watch for. He's giving them a reason to calm down.

The man of sin

He's called “the son of perdition,” a man headed for ruin. Jesus used the same words for Judas:

“While I was with them in the world, I kept them in thy name: those that thou gavest me I have kept, and none of them is lost, but the son of perdition; that the scripture might be fulfilled.”

John 17:12

What makes him so evil? He “exalteth himself above all that is called God,” and sits in God's place. It's the oldest lie there is, the serpent's line in the garden:

“For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.”

Genesis 3:5

We know a small version of it. Every addiction puts something else on God's throne, and every “I'll run my own life” puts me there. The man of sin is that same sin, full grown. The cure is the same for him as for us: get off the throne.

How to read a hard chapter

Paul had taught them this in person. We only have the letter, so we should hold our ideas humbly.

What's plain

v. 2, 8, 15

Don't be shaken. The day hadn't come. Evil will grow proud, and the Lord Jesus will destroy it with a word. Hold on to what you were taught.

Where good teachers differ

v. 3–7

Who or what holds evil back, whether the church is caught up before the man of sin appears, and what the “temple” is. Godly Baptists land in different places.

So no charts, no dates and no guessing which famous person he is. Men have named a dozen “antichrists” and been wrong every time.

Part two

Held back, then brought down

Verses 6–12

Evil is busy, but it's on a leash. And its end takes one breath.

let old English: hinder “hold back, restrain”

In 1611 let meant the opposite of what it means now. Paul uses it in Romans: he had wanted to visit Rome, “but was let hitherto” (Romans 1:13), held back until now. And verse 6's “withholdeth” means the same.

Who's the one holding back? Many Baptist teachers say the Holy Spirit, working through His people. Others say law and government. Paul's readers knew (“ye know”), and he didn't write the name down. What is plain: evil runs only as far as God allows, and only “in his time.”

One breath

All that pride, all those “signs and lying wonders,” and how does it end? No battle worth describing. The Lord Jesus breathes, and he's gone. He shows up, and that's the end of it.

Paul is echoing Isaiah's promise about the coming King:

“But with righteousness shall he judge the poor, and reprove with equity for the meek of the earth: and he shall smite the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips shall he slay the wicked.”

Isaiah 11:4

Evil is already at work. It will not win.

“The mystery of iniquity doth already work.” The spirit of the man of sin was around in Paul's day, and John said the same thing about forty years later:

“Little children, it is the last time: and as ye have heard that antichrist shall come, even now are there many antichrists; whereby we know that it is the last time.”

1 John 2:18

So you don't have to be naive about evil, in the world or in your own heart. But you don't have to be afraid of it either. It's on a leash, and its end is already written. If the worst man in history falls with one breath, the thing that's beating you up today isn't bigger than Jesus.

How a man ends up believing a lie

It starts with what he loves. They “received not the love of the truth”; they “had pleasure in unrighteousness.” A man who wants the lie gets better and better at believing it.

Anybody who's been in addiction knows this circle: “I can handle it. Just one. Nobody's getting hurt.”

The way out is to love the truth, even when it hurts: “And ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free” (John 8:32).

“Strong delusion”: does God trick people?

  • not “God fools honest seekers”He “will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth” (1 Timothy 2:4)
  • not “you might be too far gone”a man worried that he's lost the truth is a man who still wants it
  • but God gives men over to the lie they insist onthey turned the truth away, and in the end He lets them have what they chose

“And even as they did not like to retain God in their knowledge, God gave them over to a reprobate mind, to do those things which are not convenient;”

Romans 1:28

Here's something to do today: name one lie you've been telling yourself, and say it out loud to a brother you trust. Lies lose their grip in the light.

Part three

Chosen, called, comforted

Verses 13–17

“But.” After the darkness, Paul turns to the people he loves.

Chosen, and how

  1. from the beginningGod planned to savev. 13
  2. “by our gospel”He called youv. 14
  3. “belief of the truth”you believedv. 13
  4. “sanctification of the Spirit”He set you apartv. 13
  5. “the glory”where it endsv. 14

Look at the word through. God chose them to salvation through the Spirit's work and through believing the truth. Put it beside verse 10: those who perish “received not the love of the truth.” The difference is the truth, received or refused.

And the call came “by our gospel,” preached in the open to anybody who'd listen. “Whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved” (Romans 10:13). If you've believed, God wanted you, and planned for you, before you ever knew Him.

From shaken to stablished

How the chapter began (v. 2)

“Shaken in mind… troubled”

Pushed around by every rumor, every scary voice, every forged letter.

How it ends (v. 15–17)

“Stand fast… stablish you”

Feet planted, holding on to what the apostles taught, and comforted by God Himself.

Traditions here isn't man-made church rules. It's what the apostles handed down, “by word, or our epistle.” We have it today in the Bible. When you're shaken, that's the anchor: not the latest voice, but the Word that was handed down.

“Everlasting consolation and good hope through grace.”

Look who's doing the giving: “our Lord Jesus Christ himself, and God, even our Father, which hath loved us.” The comfort isn't something you scrape together. It's been given, and it's everlasting: it doesn't run out on a bad day.

And notice where Paul ends this hard chapter. Not with a chart of the future, but with “every good word and work.” The best way to be ready for His coming is to be faithful at whatever is in front of you today: an honest word, a day's work, a kindness to the man next to you.

Points to remember

  1. Shaken“That ye be not soon shaken in mind, or be troubled” 2:2Don't let a rumor, a voice or a fake letter steal your peace. Check it against the Word.
  2. First“Except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed” 2:3The day hadn't come. Paul gave them a reason to calm down, not a date to watch.
  3. Held back“Only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way” 2:7Evil is busy, but it's on a leash. It runs only as far as God allows.
  4. Christ wins“Whom the Lord shall consume with the spirit of his mouth” 2:8The worst man in history falls with one breath. Jesus wins.
  5. The truth“Chosen you to salvation through sanctification of the Spirit and belief of the truth” 2:13Some refuse the love of the truth; believers received it. Love the truth, even when it hurts.
  6. Stand fast“Everlasting consolation and good hope through grace” 2:16Stand fast in what you were taught. God has already given the comfort and the hope.

Question 1 of 6

Shaken

What did Paul not want the Thessalonians to be shaken by?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By any spirit, word or letter claiming to be from him that said the day of Christ had already come. He told them not to let anyone deceive them by any means.

Question 2 of 6

First

What did Paul say must happen before the day of Christ comes?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A falling away must come first, and the man of sin, the son of perdition, must be revealed: a man who sets himself above God. Since that hadn't happened, the day hadn't come.

Question 3 of 6

Held back

What is happening to the mystery of iniquity for now?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It is already at work, but it is being held back until the restrainer is taken out of the way. The man of sin can only be revealed in God's time.

Question 4 of 6

Christ wins

How will the Lord deal with that Wicked?

What verses tell us about this?

“But with righteousness shall he judge the poor, and reprove with equity for the meek of the earth: and he shall smite the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips shall he slay the wicked.”

Isaiah 11:4

Answer

He will consume him with the spirit of His mouth and destroy him with the brightness of His coming. It takes one breath. Jesus wins.

Question 5 of 6

The truth

What is the difference between those who perish and those God chose to salvation?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The truth. Those who perish received not the love of the truth. Believers were chosen to salvation through the Spirit's work and belief of the truth. And God wants all men to come to the knowledge of the truth (1 Timothy 2:4).

Question 6 of 6

Stand fast

What did Paul tell them to do, and what has God already given them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Stand fast and hold on to what the apostles taught. God has already given everlasting consolation and good hope through grace, and He comforts and establishes them in every good word and work.
