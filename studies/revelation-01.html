Revelation 1

Alive for Evermore

An old man in exile sees Jesus as he has never seen Him: shining, strong, walking among His churches. John falls down as dead. Jesus lays His hand on him and says, “Fear not.”

The Revelation, from Patmos

“John to the seven churches which are in Asia: Grace be unto you, and peace”

Written from
“the isle that is called Patmos, for the word of God” (1:9): a rocky island about 60 miles off the coast from Ephesus.
When
about AD 95, under the emperor Domitian.
To whom
seven real churches in Asia (today western Turkey), named in the order a messenger would walk them (1:11).
Why
Small churches under pressure, some growing cold, some giving in. Jesus shows them who He is, what He sees, and how the story ends.

By now Paul and Peter were dead, and by tradition John was the last of the twelve still living: an old man, the disciple “whom Jesus loved” (John 13:23), pastoring the churches around Ephesus.

Domitian liked to be called “Lord and God.” A Christian who would only call Jesus Lord could lose his job, his freedom, or his life. John was sent to Patmos for it. That is where Jesus met him.

Part one

The Revelation of Jesus Christ

Verses 1–8

Before a single beast or trumpet, the book opens with a Person, a blessing, and a song about what He has done for us.

An unveiling, not a puzzle

ἀποκάλυψις said apokalypsis an uncovering; the veil pulled back
Godgave itJesus Christsent it byHis angeltoJohntoHis servants: us

People treat Revelation like a locked box of riddles. The title says the opposite: something hidden is being shown. And above all, it shows a Person. It is the revelation of Jesus Christ: from Him, and about Him.

Seven blessings

The letter was read aloud in church: one man read, the rest heard. This is the only book in the Bible that opens with a blessing on the man who reads it, and it holds seven of them:

  1. “Blessed is he that readeth”1:3
  2. “Blessed are the dead which die in the Lord”14:13
  3. “Blessed is he that watcheth”16:15
  4. “Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb”19:9
  5. “Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection”20:6
  6. “blessed is he that keepeth the sayings of the prophecy of this book”22:7
  7. “Blessed are they that do his commandments”22:14

Notice: keep those things. This book is not for arguing over charts. It is for living differently today.

Three names for hard days

The faithful witness

He told the truth before Pilate and died for it. These churches were being pushed to deny Him. He went first.

The first begotten of the dead

The first to rise from death, never to die again. Where He went, His people follow.

The prince of the kings of the earth

Domitian called himself lord. Jesus outranks him, and every boss, judge and warden on earth.

The greeting comes from the Father (“which is, and which was, and which is to come”), from the seven Spirits, and from Jesus. Seven is the Bible's number for complete: the seven Spirits are the Holy Spirit in all His fullness (compare Isaiah 11:2). Father, Spirit, Son: the whole of God sends you grace and peace.

“Unto him that loved us, and washed us”

Before one word about judgment, John breaks into praise.

He loved us. He washed us from our sins in his own blood: not covered up, not excused, washed. Whatever you did, and whatever you did yesterday, “the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin” (1 John 1:7).

Then He made us kings and priests: men with a place in His kingdom and open access to God. Not probation. Not the back row. That is who you are now.

John puts two prophets together

Daniel 7:13

I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him.

Zechariah 12:10

… and they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourneth for his only son …

Jesus is coming back, and it won't be secret: every eye. Some Baptists teach that He first comes for His church and later returns with it; others see one coming at the end. All agree on this: He is coming, everyone will see Him, and He will set things right.

The first letter and the last

ΑΒΓΔΕΖΗΘΙΚΛΜΝΞΟΠΡΣΤΥΦΧΨΩ
Alpha: the firstOmega: the last

Alpha and Omega are the first and last letters of the Greek alphabet: our A to Z. God said it long before: “I am the first, and I am the last; and beside me there is no God” (Isaiah 44:6). At the end of this book Jesus takes the same words for Himself (22:13).

He was there before your story started, and He will be there at the end of it. Your worst chapter is not the last letter. He is.

Part two

On Patmos

Verses 9–11

Exiled, alone, and still worshipping on the Lord's day. Then a voice like a trumpet behind him.

Your brother, in exile

The last apostle doesn't pull rank. He writes as “your brother”, in the same trouble they're in. He was on Patmos “for the word of God”: banished for preaching Jesus.

“In the Spirit on the Lord's day”: Sunday, the day Jesus rose. No church building, no friends, no freedom. John worshipped anyway, and that is where Jesus met him.

Some of you know what it is to be locked away from everyone. God is not locked out of any place. A cell, a rehab bed, a lonely room: He can meet you there.

Part three

Among the lampstands

Verses 12–20

John turns to see the voice, and sees the Jesus he once leaned on, in all His glory.

Seven lampstands, and Someone in the middle

A candlestick here is a lampstand that holds an oil lamp, like the golden lampstand in the tabernacle. Verse 20 tells us what these are: the seven churches. A church is meant to hold up light in a dark town.

And Jesus is in the midst of them, dressed like the high priest in a long robe and a golden girdle (sash). The priest's job was to tend the lamps and keep them burning. That is what He is doing. Your small group, in a chapel, a halfway house or a cell block, is one of His lampstands, and He walks there.

What John saw

  1. 1
    Hair white like wool, as snowDaniel's “Ancient of days” (Daniel 7:9): eternal, pure and wise.
  2. 2
    Eyes as a flame of fireHe sees straight through: He “searcheth the reins and hearts” (2:23).
  3. 3
    Feet like fine brassRefined in the furnace: strong, unshaken, treading evil down (Daniel 10:6).
  4. 4
    A voice as many watersGod's own voice (Ezekiel 43:2). It drowns out every other voice.
  5. 5
    Seven stars in His right handThe churches' messengers (1:20), held where no one can pluck them out.
  6. 6
    A sharp twoedged swordHis word (Hebrews 4:12). He fights with what He says (2:16).
  7. 7
    A face as the sunThe glory three disciples saw on the mountain (Matthew 17:2), full strength.

John had read this before

Daniel 7:9

… the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool: his throne was like the fiery flame …

Daniel 10:5–6

… a certain man clothed in linen, whose loins were girded with fine gold of Uphaz: … his eyes as lamps of fire, and his arms and his feet like in colour to polished brass, and the voice of his words like the voice of a multitude.

Revelation explains itself from the Old Testament. Daniel saw God on His throne with hair like pure wool; John sees the same in Jesus. The Son of man has everything that belongs to God.

And the white He wears, He offers to you: “though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool” (Isaiah 1:18).

The hand that holds the stars

John

Fell down as dead

Sixty years before, he leaned on Jesus' chest at supper (John 13:23). Now, seeing His glory, he can't stand.

Jesus

Laid His hand on him

The right hand that holds the seven stars reaches down to a man on the ground. First word: “Fear not.”

Some of you are on the floor this morning: you fell, and you're sure that when Jesus looks at you He'll be done with you. Look what He does with a man face down at His feet. He doesn't kick him. He touches him, and tells him not to be afraid.

Who holds the keys

Whoever holds the keys decides who goes in and who comes out. Hell here is the place of the dead, the grave. Jesus went in, and came out holding the keys.

So death doesn't hold them. The devil doesn't. Your past doesn't. Jesus came to “deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage” (Hebrews 2:15).

If you belong to Him, the One who holds the keys has already decided where you are going.

Jesus explains His own pictures

seven golden candlesticks→the seven churches
seven stars in His right hand→the angels of the seven churches

Angel means messenger. Some think each church had an angel set over it; many think it means each church's pastor, the messenger who would read the letter aloud. Either way, Jesus holds them in His right hand.

This is how to read the whole book: let the Bible explain the pictures. When it doesn't tell us, we hold our guesses loosely and hold Jesus tightly.

Many Bible teachers see the whole book laid out in this verse. Next come seven letters, from this same Jesus, to seven churches a lot like ours.

“Fear not; I am the first and the last.”

Revelation does not start with monsters. It starts with Jesus: loving us, washing us, walking among His churches, holding the stars, holding the keys, and laying His hand on a man who can't get up.

Something to do today: write “Fear not; I am the first and the last” where you'll see it (on your phone, your hand, your lunch box). When fear or shame hits at work, read it, and remember whose hand is on you.

Question 1 of 6

The unveiling

Whose revelation is this book, and who is it about?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It is “The Revelation of Jesus Christ.” God gave it to Jesus, and Jesus sent it to John for His servants. It comes from Jesus and shows us Jesus.

Question 2 of 6

Loved and washed

What has Jesus done for us, according to verses 5 and 6?

What verses tell us about this?

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Answer

He loved us, washed us from our sins in His own blood, and made us kings and priests unto God. We are clean, and we belong.

He is coming

Every eye, even those who pierced Him. His coming will not be hidden; the whole earth will see Him come with clouds.

Question 4 of 6

The Son of man

What did John see when he turned to see the voice?

Answer

Seven golden lampstands, and in the middle of them Jesus in glory: white hair, eyes like fire, feet like brass, a voice like many waters, seven stars in His hand, a sword from His mouth, and a face like the sun.

Question 5 of 6

Fear not

What did Jesus do when John fell at His feet as dead?

What verses tell us about this?

He laid His right hand on him and said, “Fear not; I am the first and the last.” He lifts the man who falls before Him.

Question 6 of 6

The keys

What does Jesus have the keys of?

What verses tell us about this?

“O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory?”

1 Corinthians 15:55

Answer

The keys of hell and of death. He died, rose, and lives for evermore, so death and the grave answer to Him, not the other way round.
