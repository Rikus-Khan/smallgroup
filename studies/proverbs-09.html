Proverbs 9

Two houses, two invitations

Wisdom and Folly both set a table and call out to the same man walking by. Only one of those meals ends in life.

Part one

Wisdom's feast

Verses 1–6

The house is built, the meal is ready, and the door is open.

A house built to hold a crowd

Wisdom throws a party. Seven pillars (seven is the Bible's number for complete) means a big, finished hall, built for guests.

Meat (rare and costly), spiced wine, a full table: everything is ready before anyone is invited.

Then she calls from the highest spot in town. Wisdom doesn't hide.

Who gets an invitation?

Not the smart guys. Not the ones who have it together. Wisdom calls “whoso is simple” and the man who “wanteth” (lacks) understanding. In Proverbs, “the simple” is the naive man, wide open, easily led. That's most of us when we came in the door.

And the meal is free. You just turn in. God makes the same call all through the Bible:

“Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath no money; come ye, buy, and eat; yea, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price.”

Isaiah 55:1

“And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him that heareth say, Come. And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.”

Revelation 22:17

Wisdom has a name

Proverbs paints wisdom as a woman calling in the street. The New Testament shows us who wisdom really is:

“But unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God.”

1 Corinthians 1:24

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

“Come, eat of my bread” finds its fullest answer in Jesus. But notice verse 6: you can't sit at both tables. “Forsake the foolish, and live.” Coming in means walking away from something.

Part two

The teachable man

Verses 7–12

Between the two feasts, a word about who can actually learn.

Same correction, two different men

A scorner is the mocker who sneers at anything serious; he won't be helped. A wise man isn't the one who never needs correcting. He's the one who thanks you for it. The test is which of these two men shows up when you're corrected.

Where wisdom starts

יִרְאָה yir-AH fear, awe, reverence

This isn't the fear of a man who hides from a cruel boss. It's the awe of a son who knows exactly how great and holy his Father is, and takes Him seriously.

The whole first section of Proverbs is framed by this one idea. It opened with it, and now it closes with it:

“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.”

Proverbs 1:7

Build from the bottom up

a fuller life“thy days shall be multiplied” (9:11)
understanding“the knowledge of the holy”
wisdomknowing how to live
The fear of the LORDthe beginning, the foundation

“The holy” in Hebrew is plural, and most readers take it as a way of saying the Holy One. Understanding isn't just knowing facts. It's knowing God Himself.

Plenty of men are smart. Wisdom starts on your knees, before a holy God.

Thou alone shalt bear it.

Wisdom pays the man who has it. Scorning costs the man who does it. Nobody else can be wise for you, and nobody else can carry the weight of your mocking either.

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

That sounds hard, but it's also good news. Your father's choices, the crowd you ran with, the things done to you: those are real, but they don't decide your future. Your choice to come to Wisdom's table counts.

Part three

Folly's feast

Verses 13–18

Another woman, another door, and almost the same words.

She copies Wisdom's lines

Folly is “clamorous”: loud, restless, pushy. She didn't build anything. She just sits at her door. And look at what she says:

Wisdom, 9:4

Whoso is simple, let him turn in hither: as for him that wanteth understanding, she saith to him,

Folly, 9:16

Whoso is simple, let him turn in hither: and as for him that wanteth understanding, she saith to him,

Word for word, nearly the same. Temptation rarely shows up looking evil. It uses the same language as the good thing. And she calls out to men “who go right on their ways.” She's looking for the man who is doing okay, to pull him off the road.

Sweet because it's stolen

Wisdom offered a feast. Folly offers water and bread. Its only selling point: it's stolen, secret.

Every man who has hidden a habit knows it. Half the pull is the sneaking.

But “the dead are there.” “Hell” here is sheol, the place of the dead. Her guests only came for a drink. Nobody walks in planning to die.

Every day both women call out to you, and from the street their voices sound almost the same. The only way to tell them apart is to look at where each house leads.

“The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.”

John 10:10

Points to remember

  1. Who God is“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom” 9:10Wisdom starts with taking a holy God seriously. Everything else is built on that.
  2. God's invitation“Whoso is simple, let him turn in hither” 9:4The invitation is for people who know they lack. The meal is free. Just turn in.
  3. Correction“Rebuke a wise man, and he will love thee” 9:8A scorner hates correction. A wise man thanks you and grows.
  4. Temptation“Stolen waters are sweet” 9:17Sin borrows Wisdom's words, and sells the thrill of the secret.
  5. Where it ends“The dead are there” 9:18Nobody goes in planning to die. Look at where the house leads, not how the door looks.
  6. Owning it“Thou alone shalt bear it” 9:12Nobody can be wise for you. Your choice to come to the table counts.

Question 1 of 6

Who God is

Where does real wisdom begin?

What verses tell us about this?

“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.”

Proverbs 1:7

“If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.”

James 1:5

Answer

With the fear of the LORD: awe and respect for who God is. Wisdom isn't about being clever. It starts with knowing the Holy One and taking Him seriously. And if you lack it, ask Him. He gives it freely, without putting you down for asking.

Question 2 of 6

God's invitation

Who is invited to Wisdom's table, and what does it cost?

What verses tell us about this?

“Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath no money; come ye, buy, and eat; yea, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price.”

Isaiah 55:1

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

Answer

The simple, the ones who lack understanding: men who know they need help. It costs nothing to come; the meal is already prepared. Jesus, the wisdom of God, says the same thing: come to Me and you'll never hunger. The only thing you leave behind is the foolish way (9:6).

Question 3 of 6

Correction

How can you tell a wise man from a scorner?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Watch what happens when they're corrected. The scorner gets offended and hates the man who told him the truth. The wise man is grateful, and he gets wiser. If you want to know which one you are, notice how you react the next time someone calls you out.

Question 4 of 6

Temptation

Why does Folly's offer sound so good?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

She uses Wisdom's own words, so it sounds like the same invitation. And she sells the thrill of the forbidden: the sneaking, the secret. What she offers is plain water and bread. The sweetness is all in the stealing, and that's the trap.

Question 5 of 6

Where it ends

What's waiting at the end of Folly's party?

What verses tell us about this?

“The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.”

John 10:10

Answer

Death. Her guests are “in the depths of hell,” and they never saw it coming. Sin steals, kills and destroys. Jesus came to give life, and more of it. Turning away from Folly isn't losing out. It's choosing to live.

Question 6 of 6

Owning it

Who pays for the choices I make?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Answer

You do. Wisdom blesses the man who has it, and scorn falls on the man who scorns. That's sobering, but it's hopeful too: whatever was handed to you, your own choice to come to Wisdom's table really matters, and the life she offers is yours to take.
