Part one

An empty vine

Verses 1–8

The more God gave Israel, the more altars it built to other gods.

Where we are in Hosea

Hosea preached to the northern kingdom, Israel. He often calls it Ephraim, after its biggest tribe. Judah, with Jerusalem, was the southern kingdom. Chapters 10–14 are among his last messages, while the empire of Assyria closed in.

  1. About 790–750 BCJeroboam II: Israel is rich
  2. About 755 BCHosea begins to preach
  3. About 733 BCAssyria takes Galilee and Gilead
  4. About 722 BCSamaria falls; Israel is carried away

A vine is planted to bear fruit for its owner. Israel bore fruit “unto himself.” God sent the harvests, and Israel kept the credit.

You'd expect a blessed man to be a thankful man. Israel did the opposite: every good harvest paid for another altar. The blessing became the budget for the idols.

“Their heart is divided.” They never quit the LORD outright. They just added other gods. From the inside, a divided heart doesn't feel like rebellion. It feels like keeping your options open.

“A double minded man is unstable in all his ways.”

James 1:8

Kings who didn't last

Israel's last years were chaos. Here are its last six kings (2 Kings 15–17):

  1. Zechariahkilled after six months
  2. Shallumkilled after one month
  3. Menahempaid Assyria to stay king
  4. Pekahiahkilled by his captain
  5. Pekahkilled by Hoshea
  6. Hosheajailed by Assyria

Verse 4 says why: “swearing falsely in making a covenant.” Promises made to be broken. And broken promises don't just go away. They come up later “as hemlock in the furrows,” poison weeds in a plowed field.

A lot of us know that field. But a field can be plowed again. Hold on to that until verse 12.

House of God, house of nothing

About two hundred years earlier, Jeroboam, the first king of the north, set up golden calves at Bethel and Dan, so his people wouldn't go to Jerusalem to worship.

“Whereupon the king took counsel, and made two calves of gold, and said unto them, It is too much for you to go up to Jerusalem: behold thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.”

1 Kings 12:28
Beth-el → Beth-aven house of God became house of nothing: Hosea's nickname for a town full of idols

And the calf they bowed to? It will be loaded up and carried to Assyria as a gift for “king Jareb,” probably a nickname for Assyria's king. A god that its enemies can pack up and haul away was never a god at all.

“Cover us”

Samaria's king will vanish like foam on a stream: there one second, gone the next. The high places, hilltop shrines to idols, will stand so empty that thorns and thistles grow on the altars.

Then the saddest line in the chapter: men begging the mountains to fall on them. When a man has run from God long enough, he'd rather be buried than be seen.

Jesus quoted these words on His way to the cross, warning of the judgment coming on Jerusalem:

“Then shall they begin to say to the mountains, Fall on us; and to the hills, Cover us.”

Luke 23:30

But He was walking to that cross so that men wouldn't have to hide. You don't need a mountain to cover you. Come to Him, and your sin is covered.

Part two

Hard ground

Verses 9–15

An old sin, a hard field, and an invitation: it is time to seek the LORD.

Ever since Gibeah

Gibeah was a town in the tribe of Benjamin. In Judges 19–20 its men raped and killed a traveling Levite's concubine, and the crime set off a civil war. It was one of the lowest points in Israel's history, and Hosea says Israel never really left it.

Then verse 10: “It is in my desire that I should chastise them.” That sounds hard. But chastising is what a father does to a son he means to keep. Nobody disciplines a stranger's kid.

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

From the easy job to the plow

Treading out grain was the easy job for a young cow. She walked in circles on the threshing floor and ate as she worked; the law said nobody could muzzle her (Deuteronomy 25:4).

Ephraim loved the easy life. So God says He'll put a yoke on that “fair neck” and set her to plowing, alongside Judah. The hard work of turning back to God is coming.

Break up your fallow ground

Fallow ground is a field left unplowed so long it has gone hard. Seed bounces off. It has to be broken open first.

“Sow to yourselves in righteousness.” Start doing what's right, one seed at a time.

“Reap in mercy.” Not reap what you earned. You plant, and God sends the rain: “till he come and rain righteousness upon you.”

Hearts go hard too. God isn't asking you to make anything grow. Break the ground open and seek Him. “It is time”: today.

Same farm, two harvests. What you plant comes up. That isn't God being harsh. It's the way He made the world.

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Then the war comes (vv. 14–15). Shalman and Beth-arbel were a massacre everyone then remembered; we don't know the details now. And just as Hosea said, Israel's last king, Hoshea, was “cut off”: Assyria took him, and the kingdom with him, about 722 BC.

Points to remember

  1. A divided heart“Their heart is divided” 10:2A heart that keeps God and its idols both ends up empty, like a vine with no fruit for its owner.
  2. False trust“Thou didst trust in thy way” 10:13Altars, kings, and armies all failed Israel. Only the LORD holds up.
  3. Sowing and reaping“Ye have plowed wickedness, ye have reaped iniquity” 10:13What you plant comes up. So plant righteousness, and trust God for the rain.
  4. Seeking God“For it is time to seek the LORD” 10:12Break up the hard ground. The time is now.
  5. God's discipline“It is in my desire that I should chastise them” 10:10God disciplines the sons He means to keep.

Question 1 of 5

A divided heart

What happens to a heart that is divided?

What verses tell us about this?

“A double minded man is unstable in all his ways.”

James 1:8

Answer

It goes empty. Israel kept the LORD's name and added idols on the side, and the more God blessed them, the more idols they bought. A divided heart is unstable: it can't stand on anything. God doesn't want a share of a man's heart. He wants the whole thing, because He gave His whole Son for it.

Question 2 of 5

False trust

What did Israel lean on instead of God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.”

Psalm 20:7

Answer

Golden calves, a string of kings, deals with other nations, and its own army. Every one of them failed. The calf got carried off, the kings got killed, the soldiers got beaten. We all have our own list of things we lean on. The only one who never gives way is the LORD.

Question 3 of 5

Sowing and reaping

What do we reap from what we sow?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Answer

What you plant comes up. Lies grow more lies, and wickedness grows trouble. But it works the other way too. Start sowing what's right, and God promises a harvest of mercy, more than the seed deserves. You can't undo yesterday's planting, but you can choose today's.

Question 4 of 5

Seeking God

When is it time to seek the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

“For thus saith the LORD to the men of Judah and Jerusalem, Break up your fallow ground, and sow not among thorns.”

Jeremiah 4:3

“Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near:”

Isaiah 55:6

Answer

Now. Hosea said it to a nation on the edge of ruin: it is time. Don't wait until you feel ready or the ground feels softer. Break it open by being honest with God, and seek Him while He is near. He promises to come and rain righteousness on the man who does.

Question 5 of 5

God's discipline

Why does God chastise His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

Answer

Because He loves them and means to keep them. A father disciplines his own son, not a stranger. Hard times aren't proof that God has given up on you. Often they're the plow breaking up hard ground, so that something good can finally grow.
