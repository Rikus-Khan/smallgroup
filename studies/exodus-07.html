Exodus 7

The rod, and the river of blood

Moses is eighty and Aaron eighty-three when they stand before Pharaoh. Aaron's rod swallows the magicians' rods, and Pharaoh will not listen. Then the first of the plagues: the river turns to blood.

Part one

“I have made thee a god to Pharaoh”

Verses 1–13

Two old men, one shepherd's rod, and a king who has already said no.

“A god to Pharaoh”

Moses is not being told he is divine. Back at the burning bush the LORD had already explained what this meant:

“And he shall be thy spokesman unto the people: and he shall be, even he shall be to thee instead of a mouth, and thou shalt be to him instead of God.”

Exodus 4:16

Aaron speaks for Moses. Moses speaks for the LORD. A prophet is a man who speaks for someone else, and when Moses stands before Pharaoh he carries God's authority, not his own.

Think of who this is. Forty years before, Moses killed a man and ran. He told God he was no speaker. God did not wait for a better man. He gave Moses a brother to stand beside him, and sent him. A bad past and a weak voice do not disqualify a man whom God has called.

Foretold: “I will harden Pharaoh's heart”

This is the second time God says it. The first was before Moses ever left Midian:

“And the LORD said unto Moses, When thou goest to return into Egypt, see that thou do all those wonders before Pharaoh, which I have put in thine hand: but I will harden his heart, that he shall not let the people go.”

Exodus 4:21

Read it slowly, because it is easy to get wrong. God is telling Moses what is coming so he will not lose heart when Pharaoh says no. Pharaoh shall not hearken unto you is a forecast, so that Moses does not think he has failed when it happens.

As we read through the plagues, keep a careful eye on the words. Who does the Bible say hardened Pharaoh's heart, and when? We will keep a running tally.

Harden is a picture of a muscle going stiff, or clay going hard. In Hebrew three different words stand behind it: to make firm, to make heavy, and to make stiff. The Bible uses all three of Pharaoh.

We will watch this unfold in chapters 7 to 14. A man says no, and says no, and says no. And one day God lets him have what he chose. The lesson for us is not “God made him do it.” It is: this is what a heart does when it keeps putting God off.

Eighty and eighty-three

  1. a babyMoses bornExodus 2
  2. age 40flees to MidianActs 7:23
  3. age 80stands before Pharaoh7:7
  4. age 120dies on NeboDeuteronomy 34:7

Fourscore is four twenties: eighty. Stephen later says the forty years in Egypt, the forty in Midian and the forty in the wilderness (Acts 7:23, 30, 36). The work God gave Moses began at eighty.

Half a lifetime in a palace, half a lifetime in a pasture, and then God used him. Whatever your age, and however many years you think you have wasted, the years behind you are not the end of what God can do through you.

“Shew a miracle for you”

Shew is the old way of spelling show. Pharaoh will ask for a proof, and God has already told them what to do. The rod is a shepherd's staff: the plainest thing a poor man owns.

It was this same rod that Moses threw down in front of the burning bush (4:3). The word for the creature is a different one this time. It is a bigger word, like a great serpent, and Pharaoh wore a cobra on the front of his crown as a sign of his power.

So the first sign is aimed straight at the throne: a shepherd's stick becomes a snake, in the king's own hall. And note that Moses and Aaron did so as the LORD had commanded. They did exactly what they were told.

The magicians

  • wise menthe court's learned men
  • sorcerersmen who deal in spells and spirits
  • magiciansthe priests with the secret arts
  • enchantmentsspells and secret tricks

Paul gives the names of two of them:

“Now as Jannes and Jambres withstood Moses, so do these also resist the truth: men of corrupt minds, reprobate concerning the faith.”

2 Timothy 3:8

The text does not say how they did it, and we do not need to guess. What it does say is that their rods were all swallowed up by Aaron's. They could make more snakes. They could not stop one. Anything a man can do for himself, God's rod swallows.

One more thing. Spells and secret arts are no toy. Dabbling in them opens a door to what you do not want to meet, and God later forbids them to Israel. Leave them shut.

He saw it, and said no

Pharaoh watched a dead stick become a snake, and then watched that snake swallow all his men's snakes. He had every reason to listen. And verse 13 says:

Hearkened means listened, and acted on it. Notice as the LORD had said: this is exactly what God warned Moses would happen. Verse 14 states it flatly:

Verse 13 says “he hardened Pharaoh's heart, that he hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had said.” What God told Moses before he ever reached Egypt (4:21) is coming to pass. Watch what comes next.

Part two

“The waters were turned to blood”

Verses 14–25

The first plague falls on the river that all of Egypt lived by.

By the river, in the morning

Egypt had almost no rain. It lived on one river, the Nile: its water, fish and crops all came from it. The LORD sent Moses to the heart of the country.

Against he come means before he arrives: Moses is to stand waiting, at the river's brink, its edge, with the rod in his hand. One more warning, given before any harm is done.

The first of ten

Why the river?

Everything: their streams, their rivers, their ponds, all their pools, in vessels of wood and of stone. The curse reaches every drop, even the water already in the pots in the house. (Lothe, in verse 18, is the old spelling of loathe: they could not stand to drink it.)

Egypt worshipped many gods, and the Nile was counted holy as the giver of life. God does not strike a lifeless thing. He strikes the thing they trusted instead of him, and says: in this thou shalt know that I am the LORD.

“And Pharaoh charged all his people, saying, Every son that is born ye shall cast into the river, and every daughter ye shall save alive.”

Exodus 1:22

Many teachers also see this: Pharaoh had ordered Hebrew baby boys thrown into this very river. The river that had been used for that was the first thing to be turned. The Bible does not make the link for us, so we say it gently. But the God who sees is not blind to what is done by the water.

“The magicians did so”

Aaron lifts the rod in front of Pharaoh and his servants, and all the water turns. And Egypt's magicians do it too.

The magicians

Make it worse

They turned yet more water into blood. They added to the trouble. They did nothing for a single thirsty man.

The LORD

Will end it

The one who sent the plague is the only one who can lift it. Pharaoh will have to ask Him, in the next chapter.

Anyone can copy a problem. Anyone can make a mess bigger. When a man's life is full of blood and thirst, the question is not who can make more of it. It is who can take it away.

He turned and went into his house

Neither did he set his heart to this also means he did not take it to heart. The Egyptians dug round the river for water, for they could not drink it. Pharaoh had a house with the door shut.

days with a bloody river

Seven days. A whole week for a man to think, to repent, to send for Moses. He used it to do nothing. Nobody forced him back into the house. He turned and went in.

Real world: “lest any of you be hardened”

Pharaoh's trouble was not a lack of evidence. A river of blood is hard to argue with. His trouble was that he would not set his heart to it. For a man in recovery, that is the daily danger: knowing, and not setting your heart to it.

“But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

Hebrews 3:13

Exhort one another daily: that is what this group is for. Hardening does not happen in a day. It comes one small “not yet” at a time, and it is much harder to do alone. A brother who asks you straight questions is one of God's means of keeping a heart soft.

Today: tell one brother something true that you would rather keep shut up in the house.

“That ye may know that I am the LORD”

Pharaoh once said, “Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice?” (5:2). This is the answer. Not a lecture: a series of signs, each saying I am the LORD.

God's aim in these plagues is that they would know him. And it was never his wish that anyone be lost along the way.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Every sign here is a door held open a little longer. Longsuffering means long patience. If you have been putting God off, he has been waiting, and he is not finished waiting.

Points to remember

  1. Calling“Moses was fourscore years old, and Aaron fourscore and three years old” 7:7God called two old men, and one of them a man who had once run. Your past and your age do not decide what God can do.
  2. A warning“Pharaoh shall not hearken unto you” 7:4God told them beforehand so they would not lose heart. He tells the truth in advance.
  3. A sign“Aaron’s rod swallowed up their rods” 7:12They could copy, but not conquer. What a man does for himself, God's rod swallows.
  4. A hard heart“Pharaoh’s heart is hardened, he refuseth to let the people go” 7:14He saw the sign and said no. Hearts go hard when we keep saying not yet.
  5. The first plague“all the waters that were in the river were turned to blood” 7:20God struck what Egypt trusted instead of him, and every Egyptian felt it.
  6. The LORD's purpose“the Egyptians shall know that I am the LORD” 7:5The plagues are answers to “Who is the LORD?”, and doors held open by a patient God.

Question 1 of 6

Calling

How old were Moses and Aaron, and what job did the LORD give each of them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Eighty and eighty-three. Moses was to speak all that the LORD commanded, with God's authority, and Aaron was to be his prophet, his spokesman. God used two old men, one with a past. He can use you.

Question 2 of 6

A warning

What did the LORD say beforehand about Pharaoh?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD said unto Moses, When thou goest to return into Egypt, see that thou do all those wonders before Pharaoh, which I have put in thine hand: but I will harden his heart, that he shall not let the people go.”

Exodus 4:21

Answer

That he would not listen, and that God would harden his heart. It was told in advance so Moses would not be discouraged. We will watch who does the hardening, and when, as the plagues go on.

Question 3 of 6

A sign

What happened to Aaron's rod, and to the rods of the magicians?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Aaron's rod became a serpent, and so did theirs, but Aaron's swallowed all of theirs. They could copy the sign. They could not stand against it.

Question 4 of 6

A hard heart

How did Pharaoh answer the sign of the rod?

What verses tell us about this?

“But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

Hebrews 3:13

Answer

He did not listen. His heart was hard, and he refused to let the people go, even after seeing the rod swallow the rods of his own men. Hearts harden a little at a time. That is why we exhort one another daily.

Question 5 of 6

The first plague

What happened to the river, and who was hurt by it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The river turned to blood, the fish died, the river stank, and the Egyptians could not drink. They dug for water. The people of the land felt what their king's refusal cost them.

Question 6 of 6

The LORD's purpose

What did the LORD want Egypt to know?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

That he is the LORD. Every plague is a sign pointing at him, and each one is also a patient chance to turn. He is not willing that any should perish.

Looking ahead: Exodus 8

The river was only the start. Next come frogs, lice and flies, and a Pharaoh who learns how to say to morrow.

After seven days of blood the river runs clean again, and nothing has changed. Pharaoh still has the same heart. What does a man do when the trouble ends and nothing has changed? That is the next chapter.
