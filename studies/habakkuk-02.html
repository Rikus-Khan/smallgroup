Habakkuk 2

The just shall live by his faith

Habakkuk climbs his watchtower and waits. God tells him to write the answer down plainly: the proud will fall, and the just will live by faith.

Part one

Write the vision

Verses 1–3

Habakkuk has asked his hard question. Now he goes up where he can see, and waits.

Up on the wall

Quick reminder: Habakkuk lives in Judah, about 610–600 BC. In chapter 1 he asked God why He let wrong go on, and God said He was sending Babylon. Habakkuk asked again: how can a holy God use men like that?

Then he does a wise thing. He stops talking and climbs up, like a watchman on the city wall, to look out for God's answer. He even expects to be corrected (“when I am reproved”).

  1. askhonestly (chapter 1)
  2. stand and watchquiet, looking for God
  3. listenready to be set straight

“Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for him: fret not thyself because of him who prospereth in his way, because of the man who bringeth wicked devices to pass.”

Psalm 37:7

Write it plain.

God says: write it on “tables,” that is, tablets of clay, stone, or wood. Make the letters big and clear, so anyone can read it at a glance and run with the news.

God's answer wasn't a secret for one prophet. It was for everybody, and it was meant to last. We're reading it today, about 2,600 years later.

There's a lesson in that for a man in recovery too: when God shows you something, write it down. On the hard days, you'll want to read it again.

Slow, but never late

“Tarry” means to linger, take a long time. It may seem slow to us. But God has set the time, and His word will arrive right on it, not a minute late.

Hebrews uses this verse about Jesus coming again:

“For yet a little while, and he that shall come will come, and will not tarry. Now the just shall live by faith: but if any man draw back, my soul shall have no pleasure in him.”

Hebrews 10:37–38

Part two

By his faith

Verses 4–5

Two kinds of men: one puffed up and never satisfied, one who lives by trusting God.

Two men

The proud man

“Lifted up”

Puffed up, trusting himself and his own strength. Babylon is the picture. Something is crooked inside him, and he won't last.

The just man

“Shall live by his faith”

He leans on God, not on himself. He takes God at His word and keeps trusting while he waits. He will live.

“Faith” here means trusting God and staying with Him: faithfulness. It isn't a feeling. It's a man leaning his whole weight on God and not getting off.

Quoted three times

The New Testament quotes this one short line three times. Each time, it shines a light on a different part of it:

  1. Romans 1:17“The just”: how a sinner is made right with God
  2. Galatians 3:11“shall live by faith”: not by keeping the law
  3. Hebrews 10:38“shall live”: keep trusting; don't draw back

“For therein is the righteousness of God revealed from faith to faith: as it is written, The just shall live by faith.”

Romans 1:17

“But that no man is justified by the law in the sight of God, it is evident: for, The just shall live by faith.”

Galatians 3:11

Nobody earns a place with God. Not by cleaning up first, not by enough good days. A man is made right with God by trusting Jesus, and then he goes on living the same way: by faith.

Never enough

Verse 5 describes the proud man, Babylon's king and every man like him. He's undone by wine. He can't stay home. His appetite is like the grave, which takes and takes and never says “enough.” He swallowed nation after nation.

Plenty of us know that hunger. One more never does it. That isn't because you're worse than other men. It's because nothing but God can fill the space that was made for Him.

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Part three

Five woes

Verses 6–20

The nations Babylon crushed get the last word: a song mocking the bully, five verses long.

Woe one: taking what isn't yours

  1. Woe 1stealing (v. 6)
  2. Woe 2greed (v. 9)
  3. Woe 3blood (v. 12)
  4. Woe 4drink (v. 15)
  5. Woe 5idols (v. 19)

“Woe” is a cry of grief over someone headed for disaster. A “taunting proverb” is a mocking song. Listen to what the victims sing: “how long?” That's Habakkuk's own question (1:2), and here God promises it has an end.

“Thick clay” most likely means a heavy load of pledges: things taken from poor people as security on loans. Babylon piled it up, and it would crush him. The ones he robbed would rise up and rob him (“booties” means plunder).

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Woe two: the house that talks

  1. Woe 1stealing (v. 6)
  2. Woe 2greed (v. 9)
  3. Woe 3blood (v. 12)
  4. Woe 4drink (v. 15)
  5. Woe 5idols (v. 19)

Babylon's king grabbed and grabbed to build his house up high, like an eagle's nest out of reach, so nobody could ever touch him. But he “sinned against his soul.” What he did to others, he did to himself.

The house built with stolen money becomes a witness against him: the stones in the wall cry “guilty,” and the beams answer “guilty.” A man can hide what he did from people. The walls still know.

Woe three: a city built on blood

  1. Woe 1stealing (v. 6)
  2. Woe 2greed (v. 9)
  3. Woe 3blood (v. 12)
  4. Woe 4drink (v. 15)
  5. Woe 5idols (v. 19)

Babylon built its great city with the forced labor of the peoples it conquered. God says all of it is fuel for the fire. They wore themselves out for “vanity,” for nothing. Babylon fell to Persia in 539 BC, about sixty years later.

Some of us spent years working ourselves to the bone for something that went up in smoke. It's not too late to build on something that lasts.

As the waters cover the sea.

Right in the middle of the woes, God lifts Habakkuk's eyes. Babylon wanted to fill the world with its own glory. It won't. One day the whole earth will know the glory of the LORD, as completely as water fills the sea.

“They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain: for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the LORD, as the waters cover the sea.”

Isaiah 11:9

Empires come and go. The bully on the block comes and goes. God's plan for His world doesn't.

Woe four: getting a man drunk

  1. Woe 1stealing (v. 6)
  2. Woe 2greed (v. 9)
  3. Woe 3blood (v. 12)
  4. Woe 4drink (v. 15)
  5. Woe 5idols (v. 19)

This woe is about a man who keeps pouring for his neighbor on purpose, to get him drunk and helpless and then use him and shame him. That's what Babylon did to whole nations: got them reeling, then stripped them bare.

“giveth his neighbour drink”→“drink thou also”the cup he forced on others comes back to him

“The cup of the LORD's right hand” is a picture of God's judgment, poured out to the man who poured for others. He wanted glory. He'll get shame.

Both sides of the bottle

Men in recovery may have stood on either side of verse 15. God speaks to both.

If you were the one used

God saw it

He calls it wrong, and He puts the woe on the man who did it. What was done to you is not the last word about you.

If you poured for others

There is forgiveness

Jesus washes that too. And now you can be the man who helps another man stay sober.

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

“It is good neither to eat flesh, nor to drink wine, nor any thing whereby thy brother stumbleth, or is offended, or is made weak.”

Romans 14:21

Woe five: gods that can't talk

  1. Woe 1stealing (v. 6)
  2. Woe 2greed (v. 9)
  3. Woe 3blood (v. 12)
  4. Woe 4drink (v. 15)
  5. Woe 5idols (v. 19)

A “graven image” is carved; a “molten image” is cast from melted metal. The man makes it, then trusts it, then begs it for answers. Habakkuk calls them “dumb idols”: they can't speak.

  • to the wood, “Awake”it doesn't
  • to the stone, “Arise”it can't
  • gold and silver on the outside“no breath at all” on the inside
  • “But the LORD is in his holy temple”alive, on His throne, and He speaks

Let all the earth keep silence.

The idol worshipper begs his idol to speak. The true God is the One who speaks, and the right answer to Him is to go quiet.

Look how far Habakkuk has come. In chapter 1 he did all the talking: how long? why? Now God has answered, and the whole world, the prophet included, falls silent before Him. In chapter 3, Habakkuk will pray again, a very different prayer.

“Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth.”

Psalm 46:10

Points to remember

  1. Waiting“I will stand upon my watch” 2:1Ask honestly, then get quiet and watch for God's answer, ready to be set straight.
  2. God's timing“Though it tarry, wait for it” 2:3It may seem slow, but God is never late. His word comes right on time.
  3. Faith“The just shall live by his faith” 2:4A man is made right with God by trusting Him, not by earning it, and goes on living that way.
  4. Never enough“Cannot be satisfied” 2:5Pride and appetite always want more. Only God fills what's empty.
  5. Reaping“All the remnant of the people shall spoil thee” 2:8Nobody gets away with it. What a man does to others comes back on him.
  6. God's glory“The LORD is in his holy temple” 2:20Empires fall and idols are dumb. God reigns, and His glory will fill the earth.

Question 1 of 6

Waiting

How do we wait on God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Rest in the LORD, and wait patiently for him: fret not thyself because of him who prospereth in his way, because of the man who bringeth wicked devices to pass.”

Psalm 37:7

“Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the LORD.”

Psalm 27:14

Answer

Like a watchman: awake, quiet, and looking for Him. Habakkuk asked his question, then stopped talking and watched for God's answer, ready to be corrected. Waiting on God isn't sitting around. It's staying at your post, trusting Him, and not running off to fix it your own way.

Question 2 of 6

God's timing

Is God ever late?

What verses tell us about this?

“For yet a little while, and he that shall come will come, and will not tarry. Now the just shall live by faith: but if any man draw back, my soul shall have no pleasure in him.”

Hebrews 10:37–38

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

No. His word has “an appointed time,” and it “will surely come.” It may seem to tarry, but that's our clock, not His. When God seems slow, it's often patience: He's giving people time to turn to Him.

Question 3 of 6

Faith

How is a man made right with God?

What verses tell us about this?

“For therein is the righteousness of God revealed from faith to faith: as it is written, The just shall live by faith.”

Romans 1:17

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

By faith, not by works. The proud man trusts himself. The just man trusts God. Paul took Habakkuk's words and showed that anyone, whatever he's done, is made right with God by trusting Jesus. Salvation is a gift, and once it's received, a man goes on living by that same faith.

Question 4 of 6

Never enough

Why can't pride and appetite ever be satisfied?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

Because they're trying to fill with things a place made for God. Babylon swallowed nations and still wanted more, like the grave. Wine, money, power, or praise always leave a man hungrier. Jesus offers the rest that the next one never gives.

Question 5 of 6

Reaping

Does anyone really get away with it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Answer

No. Babylon looked unstoppable, but every woe came true: the robber was robbed, and the cup came back around. God sees, even when nobody else does. That's a warning for every man, and a comfort for every man who's been wronged. And for anyone who turns to Jesus, there is forgiveness for what he's sown.

Question 6 of 6

God's glory

Who is really in charge of the world?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth.”

Psalm 46:10

Answer

The LORD. Babylon thought it ran the world, and its idols couldn't even breathe. But “the LORD is in his holy temple.” He's on the throne, and one day His glory will fill the earth like water fills the sea. That's why we can be still.
