In chapter 1, Paul told them what he saw in them. Now he tells them what they saw in him. After Paul left town in a hurry, some people were probably saying he was a traveling talker who took the money and ran when trouble came. Many Bible teachers think so. Paul never repeats the charge; he just answers it, with his life.

Part one

Straight talk

Verses 1–6

Before Paul says anything warm, he clears his name. He lets them check him against what they saw.

Fresh from a Philippian jail

“And when they had laid many stripes upon them, they cast them into prison, charging the jailor to keep them safely: Who, having received such a charge, thrust them into the inner prison, and made their feet fast in the stocks.”

Acts 16:23–24
  1. PhilippiBeaten and locked up, feet in the stocksActs 16:22–24
  2. MidnightAn earthquake, and the jailer believesActs 16:25–34
  3. MorningAsked to leave the cityActs 16:39–40
  4. Thessalonica“bold in our God to speak”1 Thess. 2:2

Paul wasn't fearless. His boldness was “in our God”: borrowed, not his own. “Much contention” means a real struggle. If you start a new life with fresh bruises, you're in good company.

A con man's toolkit

  • flattering wordstelling you what you want to hear, so he gets what he wants
  • a cloke of covetousnessreligious talk with a hand reaching for your wallet
  • glory of menpreaching to get a name, to be looked up to
  • God, which trieth our heartsPaul spoke to an audience of One, who sees the heart

Some of the men in this room have been the con man, or been conned by one. You know the signs. Paul says to the Thessalonians: “as ye know.” Check me. A real teacher can stand to be checked.

He also says (v. 6) he might have been burdensome: as an apostle he had every right to be paid. He gave that up so nobody could call him a taker.

Part two

A nurse and a father

Verses 7–12

Gentle and firm. The two things a young believer needs most.

Not only the message

Paulgave themthe gospel of God
Pauland also gave“our own souls”because“ye were dear unto us”

“Cherisheth” means keeps warm, holds close, the way a mother nursing her baby does. This was the man who had just been beaten in a jail, and he describes himself as gentle. Gentle is not weak.

Notice what Paul says he gave: not just advice, but himself. His time, his tears, his life. That's what a good friend or sponsor does, and it's what this group does for each other.

Night and day, with his own hands

IfPaul had lived off them,
→
thensomeone could say, “He's in it for the money.”

“And because he was of the same craft, he abode with them, and wrought: for by their occupation they were tentmakers.”

Acts 18:3

So he worked at his trade, tentmaking: heavy, hard craft. “Labour and travail” means toil and hardship.

If you're going to work today, this is for you. Honest work is not second-class; Paul did it every day he preached. And if you've taken what wasn't yours or leaned on people, that's the old side of the line. Being no burden to anybody is a good thing to aim at now.

What a father does

  1. exhortedurged us on, like a coach on the side of the track
  2. comfortedcame close to the one who was hurting, and spoke gently
  3. chargedgave a solemn word and expected it to be heard

Nurse and father. Tender and firm. A new believer needs both, and a good mentor knows which one a man needs today. And Paul did it for “every one of you,” one man at a time.

Look at the order in verse 12. First, God “hath called you unto his kingdom and glory.” Then, “walk worthy.” You don't walk worthy to earn the call. You walk worthy because of it.

Part three

The word, and the cost

Verses 13–16

What they did with the message, and what it cost them.

More than good advice

  • as the word of menone more opinion, one more voice, easy to argue with
  • as it is in truth, the word of Godand it keeps working in you that believe

“For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart.”

Hebrews 4:12

“Effectually worketh” means it is at work, getting things done. You don't have to feel something to start reading. Read it, and let it do its job.

The same road

The churches in Judaeawere hurt by their own countrymenYou in Thessalonicaare hurt by yours, and follow the sameLord

“Your own countrymen” are the people you grew up with. Sometimes the hardest pushback comes from home: old friends who liked you better the old way, family who doesn't believe the change is real.

Paul doesn't tell them it will go away. He tells them they're not alone, and that other believers have been walking this exact road. You're in good company.

A hard few lines

Verses 15–16 sound harsh. Paul is a Jew, writing about the particular men who killed Jesus and drove the church out of Judaea. He isn't speaking against the Jewish people: Jesus, the apostles and the first believers were all Jews.

“Brethren, my heart’s desire and prayer to God for Israel is, that they might be saved.”

Romans 10:1

Paul had been one of those men himself (1 Timothy 1:13), so he writes from the inside. On “the wrath is come upon them,” Bible teachers differ about exactly what he points to. What's plain: judgment belongs to God, and the door is open to everyone. Never use these verses to look down on anyone.

“Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do. And they parted his raiment, and cast lots.”

Luke 23:34

Part four

Torn away, still close

Verses 17–20

Paul wanted to be back. Something stopped him. Here is what he felt, and what he saw in them.

“Satan hindered us”

The word Paul uses for “taken from you” means made orphans. A father torn from his children. He tried to come back “once and again.”

Paul names a real enemy. Not every closed door is Satan, but when a man is doing right and something keeps blocking the way, he's not imagining it.

“Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.”

James 4:7

Submit first, resist second: you don't fight him alone. And Paul finds another way to reach them (chapter 3).

“For ye are our glory and joy.”

A crown of rejoicing is the wreath given to the winner of the games. Paul's prize isn't a thing. It's people. A man who was once a violent persecutor now says the best thing he has is a church of new believers.

Somebody will look at you one day and say, “That's my joy.” It may be a sponsor, a pastor, a wife, a kid, or a man in this group. Be that for someone, and let someone be that for you.

One thing to do today: tell one man in this group one thing you see growing in him. That's exactly what Paul did in chapter 1.

Points to remember

  1. Pleasing God“not as pleasing men, but God, which trieth our hearts” 2:4Preach, work and live for an audience of One.
  2. Like a nurse“not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls” 2:8Real care gives the person, not only the advice.
  3. Like a father“we exhorted and comforted and charged every one of you, as a father doth his children” 2:11Urge, comfort, and speak plainly, one man at a time.
  4. Honest work“labouring night and day, because we would not be chargeable unto any of you” 2:9Honest work is holy, and being no burden is worth aiming at.
  5. The word of God“ye received it not as the word of men, but as it is in truth, the word of God” 2:13Scripture is God's word, and it keeps working in you that believe.
  6. Glory and joy“For ye are our glory and joy.” 2:20People are what matter most. Be someone's joy.

Question 1 of 6

Pleasing God

Whom was Paul trying to please when he preached?

What verses tell us about this?

“For do I now persuade men, or God? or do I seek to please men? for if I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ.”

Galatians 1:10

Answer

God, who tries the heart. Paul used no flattery and no cover for greed, and he sought no glory from men. If you try to please everybody, you end up as nobody's servant.

Question 2 of 6

Like a nurse

What did Paul give them besides the gospel?

What verses tell us about this?

“He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom, and shall gently lead those that are with young.”

Isaiah 40:11

Answer

His own soul: his time, his love, his life. He cared for them as a mother keeps her children warm, the way the Lord Himself carries the lambs.

Question 3 of 6

Like a father

What three things did Paul do for each of them, as a father does for his children?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

Answer

He exhorted, comforted and charged every one of them. Tender and firm, the way a good father is, and the way the Father in heaven is to a son who comes home.

Question 4 of 6

Honest work

Why did Paul work night and day?

What verses tell us about this?

“I have shewed you all things, how that so labouring ye ought to support the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he said, It is more blessed to give than to receive.”

Acts 20:35

Answer

So he would not be chargeable to any of them, and no one could say he preached for money. Honest work was part of how he showed the gospel was real.

Question 5 of 6

The word of God

How did they receive what Paul preached?

What verses tell us about this?

“So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

Romans 10:17

“For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart.”

Hebrews 4:12

Answer

Not as the word of men but as the word of God, which is what it is. It keeps working in those who believe.

Question 6 of 6

Glory and joy

What did Paul call the Thessalonians when he thought of Jesus coming back?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore, my brethren dearly beloved and longed for, my joy and crown, so stand fast in the Lord, my dearly beloved.”

Philippians 4:1

Answer

His hope, joy and crown of rejoicing, his glory and joy. For Paul the people were the prize.
