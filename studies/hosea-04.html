Hosea 4

The LORD's case against Israel

Hosea moves from his own home to the courtroom. God brings charges against a nation that has forgotten Him, starting with the men who were supposed to teach it.

Part one

A controversy

Verses 1–3

Chapters 1–3 were Hosea's marriage. From here on, it's his preaching to Israel, the northern kingdom, in its last years before Assyria.

The charges

A “controversy” is a lawsuit. God takes His people to court. First, what's missing: truth, mercy, and knowing God. Then, what has taken its place:

The LORD's charges (Hosea 4:2)

  • swearingcommandment 3
  • lyingcommandment 9
  • killingcommandment 6
  • stealingcommandment 8
  • committing adulterycommandment 7
The resultblood toucheth blood

Read down the list and you're reading the Ten Commandments, broken one after another. “Blood toucheth blood” means one killing after another, until the blood runs together.

And it spreads. In verse 3 even the land, the animals, the birds and the fish suffer for it. Sin is never as private as we tell ourselves.

Part two

Like people, like priest

Verses 4–10

The men who should have taught God's ways had forgotten them.

Lack of knowledge

In verses 4–6, God turns to the priests. Their job was to teach the people God's law. Instead they had “forgotten the law of thy God.” So the people never learned, and the priests couldn't be told anything either.

דַּעַת said da‘at knowing, up close and personal

This isn't head knowledge, like knowing facts about the president. It's the word family Hebrew uses when it says Adam “knew” his wife. Israel knew about God. They didn't know Him.

And notice: they didn't just lack knowledge. They rejected it. The Bible was there, and they set it aside. That's still how men are destroyed today.

Never enough

The priests got a share of every sin offering the people brought. So more sin meant more meat for them. They “eat up the sin of my people,” and they wanted the people to keep sinning.

That's the way of every appetite you feed apart from God. You eat and you're still hungry. You chase it and it gives back less each time. And the leaders were no better than the led: “like people, like priest.”

Part three

A spirit of whoredoms

Verses 11–19

When the heart is gone, a man will take advice from a stick of wood.

They take away the heart

What they chasedwhoredom, wine, and new wine
→
what it tookthe heart

In the Bible, the “heart” is more than feelings. It's your mind, your judgment, your conscience, your will. Sex outside of God's way and drink both promise to make a man feel alive. Followed far enough, they take the very part of him that could say no.

Then verse 12 shows where that leads: “their stocks,” a carved block of wood, and “their staff,” a stick thrown down to tell the future. Men who couldn't hear God took advice from a piece of wood.

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

God's answer isn't just “stop.” It's to be filled with something better. A heart that's been taken can be given back.

The weight falls on the men

The idol shrines sat on hilltops in the shade of big trees. Part of the worship was sex with “harlots” kept at the shrine. The men went. Then their daughters and wives followed.

God says something surprising: “I will not punish your daughters… nor your spouses.” Not because what they did was fine, but because the men led the way. The men were the ones He held to account.

A man sets the course for his home. That's a heavy word, and a hopeful one: when a man turns around, his house can turn with him.

Don't follow them

Hosea warns Judah, the southern kingdom: don't copy the north. Gilgal and Bethel were just across the border, and both had become places to worship idols.

Bethel means house of God. It's where Jacob saw the ladder to heaven. But Israel's first king set up a golden calf there (1 Kings 12:28–29). So Hosea gives it a new name: Beth-aven, house of nothing.

Don't go there, and don't stand in that place and swear “The LORD liveth” while you worship something else.

A backsliding heifer

Picture a young cow that won't take the yoke. Put a rope on her and she plants her feet and pulls backward. You can't lead her anywhere. That was Israel with God.

“A lamb in a large place” sounds pleasant, but a lamb alone in wide-open country has no fold and no shelter. Many take it as a warning: since they won't be led, they'll be scattered out in the open, the way the exile scattered them.

Backsliding is real. Believers can dig in and pull against God. But listen to what He says to backsliders:

“Return, ye backsliding children, and I will heal your backslidings. Behold, we come unto thee; for thou art the LORD our God.”

Jeremiah 3:22

Let him alone

Ephraim was the biggest tribe in the north, so Hosea often uses its name for the whole northern kingdom. “Joined to idols” means glued fast to them. And God's word is the most frightening in the chapter: let him alone.

The party has gone sour (verse 18). And a wind is coming to sweep them away (verse 19). That wind was Assyria.

Hosea 4:17

“Ephraim is joined to idols: let him alone.”

Hosea 14:4, the same book

“I will heal their backsliding, I will love them freely: for mine anger is turned away from him.”

Hosea doesn't end at “let him alone.” It ends with God calling Ephraim home. Judgment is real, and His mercy is greater. While a man has breath, the door is open.

  2. Sin's spread“Therefore shall the land mourn” 4:3Sin is never private. It spills onto home and land.
  3. Leaders“Like people, like priest” 4:9When the leaders forget God, the people follow them down.
  4. Wine“Take away the heart” 4:11They steal the very judgment a man needs to say no.
  5. Backsliding“As a backsliding heifer” 4:16Pulling against God is real, and He still says, “Return.”

Question 1 of 6

Knowing God

Why does God say His people are destroyed for lack of knowledge?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings.”

Hosea 6:6

Answer

Because they didn't know God Himself, and they didn't want to. The priests forgot His law and the people never learned it. Without knowing Him, everything else fell apart: truth, mercy, families. God wants more than rituals. He wants us to know Him, and He has given us His word so we can.

Question 2 of 6

Sin's spread

How does sin spread from a man to his home and his land?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Answer

It runs downhill. One sin leads to the next until blood touches blood. The fathers go to the shrines and the daughters follow. Even the land suffers. What a man sows, he reaps, and so does everyone close to him. That's sobering, but it works the other way too: a man walking with God blesses his whole house.

Question 3 of 6

Leaders

What happens when the leaders go wrong?

What verses tell us about this?

“My brethren, be not many masters, knowing that we shall receive the greater condemnation.”

James 3:1

Answer

The people go wrong with them. Israel's priests forgot God's law and even profited from the people's sin. “Like people, like priest.” Anyone who leads, a pastor, a sponsor, a father, carries more weight before God. And every one of us leads somebody.

Question 4 of 6

Wine

What do whoredom and wine do to a man's heart?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging: and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise.”

Proverbs 20:1

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

Answer

They take it away: the judgment, conscience and will a man needs to choose rightly. They promise life and end sour. Willpower alone can't win the heart back. God can. He fills believers with His Spirit, and He gives a new heart to anyone who turns to Him.

Question 5 of 6

Backsliding

What does a backslider look like, and is there hope for him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Return, ye backsliding children, and I will heal your backslidings. Behold, we come unto thee; for thou art the LORD our God.”

Jeremiah 3:22

“I will heal their backsliding, I will love them freely: for mine anger is turned away from him.”

Hosea 14:4

Answer

Like a stubborn heifer that plants its feet and pulls back against the rope. A believer can do that, and it costs him. But God doesn't say, “You're finished.” He says, “Return, and I will heal.” A believer who slides back is still His child, and the way home is still open.

Question 6 of 6

Let alone

What happens when God lets a man have his own way?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore God also gave them up to uncleanness through the lusts of their own hearts, to dishonour their own bodies between themselves:”

Romans 1:24

“O Israel, return unto the LORD thy God; for thou hast fallen by thine iniquity.”

Hosea 14:1

Answer

He gets what he insisted on, and it ruins him. That's one of God's hardest judgments: stepping back and letting the idol have him. But the same book ends with God calling, “O Israel, return.” If you can still hear Him calling, it isn't too late. Come back today.
