Proverbs 26

Fools, sluggards, and talebearers

Three kinds of men we don't want to be, painted plainly, with a way out for every one of us.

Part one

The fool

Verses 1–12

Twelve sayings, one portrait.

Things that don't fit

“Snow in summer”Wrong timeIsrael's summers are hot and dry. Snow then would be strange, and could ruin a crop.
“Rain in harvest”Ruins itRain at harvest could rot the grain in the field (see 1 Samuel 12:17–18).
“Honour for a fool”Doesn't fitPut a fool in charge, or praise him, and it only feeds his folly.

In Proverbs, a fool isn't a man with a low IQ. He's a man who won't listen: to God, to correction, to anybody. “The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God” (Psalm 14:1). Words don't reach him, so verse 3 says he learns the hard way, from the rod.

A curse that won't land

A swallow darts and wanders all day and never seems to land. A curse you didn't earn is the same. It has nowhere to come down.

Maybe you had things spoken over you for years: “You'll never amount to anything.” If it isn't true, it doesn't get to land.

And in Christ, the one verdict that matters has already been read: “There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus” (Romans 8:1).

Answer him, or don't?

Proverbs 26:4

Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him.

Proverbs 26:5

Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit.

These sit side by side on purpose. It's not a mistake; it's a test. Both are true. Wisdom is knowing which one fits the moment.

Verse 4: keep quiet when

he wants a fight

If answering means getting down in the mud with him, shouting, cussing, matching insult for insult, don't. You'll just become him.

Verse 5: speak up when

a lie would stand

If staying silent lets him think he's right, or lets others be hurt by his folly, answer him. Calmly, plainly, and then be done.

Jesus did both

When Herod mocked Him and only wanted a show, Jesus said nothing at all:

“Then he questioned with him in many words; but he answered him nothing.”

Luke 23:9

When the religious leaders tried to trap Him with trick questions, He answered so clearly that they had nothing left to say:

“And no man was able to answer him a word, neither durst any man from that day forth ask him any more questions.”

Matthew 22:46

Before you answer, ask: Will this help, or am I just trying to win?

Four pictures of a fool

  1. Sending a foolTrusting him with a message is like cutting off your own feet. The job won't get done, and you'll pay for it. (v. 6)
  2. A lame man's legsA wise saying in a fool's mouth limps. He can quote it, but he can't walk in it. (v. 7)
  3. A stone tied in a slingA tied stone can't fly; it just swings back. Honoring a fool is just as useless. (v. 8)
  4. A thorn in a drunk's handHe waves it around and hurts himself and everybody near him, and hardly feels it. (v. 9)

Notice verses 7 and 9: a fool can quote the Bible. Knowing the words isn't the same as being changed by them. That's a warning for all of us who sit in a Bible study.

Back to the vomit

It's a gross picture on purpose. The dog throws up what made him sick, then goes right back and eats it.

Most of us know this loop from the inside. The old thing, the crash, “never again,” the pull, and back around. That's what relapse looks like, and naming it honestly is the first step out.

The loop has a door. You don't have to break it alone, and you don't have to hide when you've slipped.

If you've gone back

“But it is happened unto them according to the true proverb, The dog is turned to his own vomit again; and the sow that was washed to her wallowing in the mire.”

2 Peter 2:22

Peter quotes our verse about false teachers who never really changed. But a believer who stumbles isn't a dog. In Christ, “he is a new creature” (2 Corinthians 5:17). A slip doesn't undo what Jesus did. It does mean it's time to get up, tell the truth, and let your brothers help.

“For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.”

Proverbs 24:16

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

No shame in this room. Just get up one more time than you fall.

There is more hope of a fool than of him.

After eleven verses about fools, here's someone worse: the man who thinks he already knows it all. A fool might still learn the hard way. A man who's “wise in his own conceit” won't even listen.

“Be not wise in your own conceits.”

Romans 12:16

About verse 10: the Hebrew is hard, and translations differ a lot. The KJV reads it as God, the Maker of all things, giving fools and sinners what's coming to them.

Part two

The sluggard

Verses 13–16

Four snapshots of a man who won't get up.

Turning on his hinges

A door swings back and forth all day and never leaves the doorway. The sluggard rolls over and over in bed the same way. Lots of motion, no progress.

Verse 15 is almost funny: his hand is already in the dish, and lifting it to his mouth is too much work. He'll go hungry with food in his hand.

Sometimes what looks like laziness is really fear, or a heaviness that's hard to name. If that's you, say so. Either way, the answer starts with one small step today.

The sluggard's excuses

  • “There is a lion in the way”a danger so dramatic nobody can argue with it
  • “A lion is in the streets”just to be sure, there's one out front too
  • “I know better than you”“wiser in his own conceit than seven men” (v. 16)
  • Get up and gothe lion is almost never really there

The sluggard is never short of reasons. Seven wise men could give him good answers, and he'd still think he's smarter. Be honest about your own “lions.” What is the excuse you keep using?

Part three

Fires and pits

Verses 17–28

Meddlers, pranksters, gossips, and liars.

Grabbing a dog by the ears

Jumping into a fight that isn't yoursBittenDogs back then were mostly wild street dogs, not pets. Grab a passing one by the ears and you can't hold on, and you can't let go without getting bit.
“Am not I in sport?”Not a jokeVerses 18–19: the man who lies to his neighbor or hurts him, then says “I was only kidding,” is like a madman throwing flaming arrows.

“Just joking” doesn't pull the arrow back out. If you hurt someone, own it.

No wood, no fire

A fire only lasts as long as somebody feeds it. Strife is the same. The talebearer, the man carrying stories from one person to the next, is the wood.

Stop passing it on, and the fire gets smaller.

Starve it completely, and it goes out. “Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth!” (James 3:5).

Verse 22: gossip tastes good going down, and it wounds deep. Be the place where a story stops.

Shiny on the outside

A potsherd is cheap broken pottery. Coat it with the shiny leftover scum from melting silver, and from across the room it looks like silver.

That's smooth talk covering a hateful heart. Up close, it's cracked clay.

Verse 25: “When he speaketh fair, believe him not.” Most of us have been played by a smooth talker, and some of us have been the smooth talker. Watch what a man does, not just what he says.

It comes back on you

Dig a pit to trap somebody and you'll be the one who falls in. Push a stone uphill to drop on someone, and it rolls back down on you.

Hatred hidden behind lies gets found out, often in front of everyone (v. 26). Sin has a way of coming home.

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Points to remember

  1. The fool“A rod for the fool’s back” 26:3A fool won't listen, so he learns the hard way. Be a man who listens.
  2. Wise answers“Answer not… Answer” 26:4–5Don't stoop to his level, and don't let a lie stand. Wisdom knows which moment it is.
  3. Going back“As a dog returneth to his vomit” 26:11Name the loop honestly. If you fall, confess, get up, and let your brothers help.
  4. Pride“More hope of a fool than of him” 26:12The man who thinks he knows it all is worse off than a fool.
  5. The sluggard“There is a lion in the way” 26:13The lion is usually an excuse. Take one small step today.
  6. The tongue“Where no wood is, there the fire goeth out” 26:20Gossip and lies feed the fire. Be the place where the story stops.

Question 1 of 6

The fool

What makes a man a fool in God's eyes?

What verses tell us about this?

“The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God. They are corrupt, they have done abominable works, there is none that doeth good.”

Psalm 14:1

Answer

Not a lack of brains, but a refusal to listen. The fool leaves God out and won't take correction, so he only learns from pain. He can even quote wise sayings without living them. The cure is a humble heart that listens to God.

Question 2 of 6

Wise answers

When should you answer a fool, and when should you keep quiet?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then he questioned with him in many words; but he answered him nothing.”

Luke 23:9

Answer

Keep quiet when answering would drag you down to his level. Speak up when silence would let a lie stand or let him think he's wise. Jesus did both. Ask yourself: will this help, or am I just trying to win?

Question 3 of 6

Going back

What does the dog going back to his vomit teach us, and where is the hope?

What verses tell us about this?

“For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.”

Proverbs 24:16

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Folly is a loop: we go back to the very thing that made us sick. Naming that honestly matters. But the hope is that in Christ we are new men, and a fall doesn't have to be the end. Confess it to God, tell a brother, and get back up. He is faithful to forgive.

Question 4 of 6

Pride

Who is in worse shape than a fool?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not wise in your own conceits.”

Romans 12:16

Answer

The man who is wise in his own eyes. A fool might still learn, but a know-it-all has shut the door on learning. The first step to wisdom is admitting you don't have it all figured out.

Question 5 of 6

The sluggard

What excuses does the sluggard make?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Dramatic dangers that aren't really there: “a lion in the way.” And he's sure he's smarter than everyone telling him to get moving. The way out is to be honest about our own “lions” and take the next small step anyway.

Question 6 of 6

The tongue

How do gossip and lies set fires?

What verses tell us about this?

“Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth!”

James 3:5

Answer

Gossip is the wood that keeps a fight burning, and lies are flaming arrows. Words go deep and wound. Take away the wood, by refusing to carry the story, and the fire goes out.
