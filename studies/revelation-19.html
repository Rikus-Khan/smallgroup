In chapters 17 and 18 John watched Babylon fall: the great city of the world that sold pleasure, money and power, and even bought and sold “slaves, and souls of men” (18:13). On earth, the merchants wept.

Now we hear what heaven says about it. Then the door of heaven swings all the way open, and the King Himself rides out. This is the chapter the whole book has been waiting for.

Part one

Alleluia

Verses 1–6

Babylon is gone. Heaven doesn't weep. It shouts, four times over.

Alleluia hal-leh-LOO-yah “Praise ye the LORD”

It's a Hebrew word: hallelu, praise ye, and Yah, short for the LORD's own name. The Psalms are full of it; in our King James it's printed “Praise ye the LORD.” The last psalm ends:

“Let every thing that hath breath praise the LORD. Praise ye the LORD.”

Psalm 150:6

In the whole New Testament the word Alleluia turns up only four times, and all four are right here, in six verses:

1Alleluia 2  3Alleluia 4Alleluia 5  6Alleluia

God settles the account

Babylon “did corrupt the earth”: it made men drunk on what it sold and chewed up people, God's people too. Heaven isn't cheering cruelty. It's glad the wrong is finally put right, by God, not by men.

Maybe somebody did you wrong, and you've replayed the payback in your head for years. God says: hand it over.

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

And the wrong you did? The same God forgives every man who comes to Him and confesses it (1 John 1:9). Babylon never asked.

The Lord God omnipotent reigneth.

Omnipotent means all-powerful: there is nothing He can't do. John hears it like the roar of a stadium, like Niagara Falls, like thunder right overhead. One sound, from “a great multitude.”

Look at the invitation in verse 5: “all ye his servants… both small and great.” In heaven's choir there's no back row for the small. The man who was somebody and the man who was nobody sing the same song.

And notice the tense. Not will reign some day: reigneth. Babylon looked like it was in charge. It never was. On your worst day, at your job, in your house, the Lord God omnipotent still reigns.

Part two

The marriage of the Lamb

Verses 7–10

The great city was a harlot. The Lamb has a bride, dressed in white.

Clean and white

The bride is the church (Ephesians 5:25). The key word in verse 8 is “granted”: she doesn't sew her own dress. It's given to her.

Our own righteousnesses are “as filthy rags” (Isaiah 64:6). But the Lamb's people “have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb” (7:14).

Then a saved man starts living clean, one right thing at a time, with the Spirit's help. Many teachers say that's “the righteousness of saints,” and even that is His gift.

A wedding, the old way

A Jewish wedding then came in stages. Many Bible teachers see Christ and His church in it:

  1. The promisethe couple pledged, the price paidthe cross
  2. The waithe goes to get a place readyJohn 14:2
  3. He comesthe groom comes for his brideJohn 14:3
  4. The supperthe wedding feastRevelation 19:9

“I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also.”

John 14:2–3

We live in stage two: the price paid, the Groom coming back. “His wife hath made herself ready”: a bride who knows he's coming lives like it.

Blessed are the called

Called here means invited. And Jesus told a story about who gets the invitation:

“Go ye therefore into the highways, and as many as ye shall find, bid to the marriage.”

Matthew 22:9

Men off the street, men out of the cell block, men out of rehab. The invitation has your name on it, and the angel says, “These are the true sayings of God.” You can take it to the bank.

Even John got it wrong

The beloved apostle, an old man who had walked with Jesus, falls at the feet of an angel. And he does it again in 22:8–9. Peter had to stop Cornelius the same way: “Stand up; I myself also am a man” (Acts 10:26).

  • the messengerthe preacher, the sponsor, the man who got you clean
  • the experiencethe feeling, the miracle, the vision
  • “worship God”

Thank the people God used, but don't put any of them on the throne. And look how the angel corrects John: no shame, no lecture. “I am thy fellowservant.” Get up off the floor and point your worship the right way. That's all.

Part three

The Rider on the white horse

Verses 11–16

In chapter 4 a door opened in heaven. Now heaven itself opens, and Jesus comes back.

Faithful and True

His first name in this chapter is a promise kept. Forty days after He rose, the angels told the disciples:

“Ye men of Galilee, why stand ye gazing up into heaven? this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven.”

Acts 1:11

You've known men who weren't faithful or true. Maybe you've been one: a promise to your kids you didn't keep. He has never once broken His word. He said He'd come back, and He does. And He makes war only “in righteousness”: never for pride, never wrong.

Many teachers see the first rider as conquest, or a false christ who looks like the real thing. Either way, the world has had a lot of men on white horses promising to fix everything. Only one of them is Faithful and True.

His names and His marks

John 1:1

In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

Isaiah 63:2–3

Wherefore art thou red in thine apparel, and thy garments like him that treadeth in the winefat? I have trodden the winepress alone;

The Word of God: John gives Him the same name as in his Gospel. God's last word to the world is a Person. The vesture dipped in blood is Isaiah's Warrior, stained like a man treading grapes: the winepress of verse 15, judgment on those who fight Him to the end.

The sword is His word (1:16): He speaks, and it's done. The rod of iron is from Psalm 2:9, a rule nobody breaks. And a name… that no man knew: there's always more to Him than we can know.

Who rides behind Him?

Their clothes are fine linen, white and clean, like the bride's in verse 8. Many teachers say these are the saints, with the angels (Matthew 25:31). John already told us who rides with the Lamb:

“These shall make war with the Lamb, and the Lamb shall overcome them: for he is Lord of lords, and King of kings: and they that are with him are called, and chosen, and faithful.”

Revelation 17:14

And the armies never lift a hand. Their robes stay white; His is the one dipped in blood. He fights, and they follow. That's the gospel: He does the work, and we share His victory.

KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS.

Written “on his vesture and on his thigh”, where a rider's robe falls across his leg, where everyone can read it as He comes.

Every king, president, warden and judge answers to Him. So does every king you served yourself: the bottle, the pipe, the money, your temper.

“That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.”

Philippians 2:10–11

One day every knee bows. A man who bows today isn't a loser; he's on the winning side early.

Part four

The end of the beast

Verses 17–21

The armies of the world gather against Him. The battle is over before it starts.

Two suppers

This chapter has two suppers in it. The picture of birds called to a battlefield comes from the prophet Ezekiel (Ezekiel 39:17). It's meant to be sober, not to be enjoyed.

Verse 9

The marriage supper of the Lamb

For everyone who answers the invitation. Dress provided, cost paid, a seat with your name on it.

Verse 17

The supper of the great God

The end of those who gather to fight the Lamb to the last: “both free and bond, both small and great.”

Both lists say “small and great” (19:5, 19:18). Rank doesn't decide where a man ends up. What he does with the Lamb does. And the invitation to the first supper is still open today.

No battle at all

The world's armies gather, and John doesn't describe a fight. The beast “was taken.” One word from the mouth of the King, and it's finished.

The beast and the false prophet are the great deceivers of chapter 13: power that demanded worship, and religion that sold the lie with miracles. They are the first ones into the lake of fire. Every lie that ever told a man “you'll never be free” ends here.

This is sober, and it's meant to be. But for a believer it's hope: evil doesn't get the last word. “For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil” (1 John 3:8).

Take it to work today

19:6He reigns

Say it once out loud today, when things go wrong: the Lord God omnipotent reigneth.

19:2Hand over the score

Name the man you want to pay back, and give him to God. Vengeance is His job, not yours.

19:8Wear the linen

You've been given a clean robe. Do one right thing today that fits it.

Question 1 of 6

Alleluia

Why does heaven shout Alleluia?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because God has judged Babylon, the great city that corrupted the earth and killed His servants. His judgments are true and righteous, and the wrong is finally put right. Above all, they praise Him because the Lord God omnipotent reigns.

Question 2 of 6

Fine linen

What is the bride's fine linen, and where does she get it?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Answer

It's “the righteousness of saints,” clean and white, and it is “granted” to her: a gift. Our own goodness is filthy rags, but Christ gives His righteousness to every believer, washes us in His blood, and then helps us live right. Even the good we do is His gift.

Question 3 of 6

Invited

Who is blessed, according to verse 9?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him that heareth say, Come. And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely.”

Revelation 22:17

Answer

Those who are called, that is, invited, to the marriage supper of the Lamb, and who come. The invitation goes out to the highways, to whosoever will. The dress is provided and the cost is paid.

Question 4 of 6

Worship

Why did the angel tell John not to worship him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then saith Jesus unto him, Get thee hence, Satan: for it is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve.”

Matthew 4:10

Answer

Because the angel was only a fellowservant, one of God's servants like John. Worship belongs to God alone. The angel corrected John gently and pointed him back to God, and to Jesus, who is what all prophecy is about.

Question 5 of 6

The Rider

Who is the Rider on the white horse?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Jesus Christ, coming back as He promised. He is called Faithful and True and The Word of God. His eyes are a flame of fire, He wears many crowns, His robe is dipped in blood, and His name is KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS.

Question 6 of 6

The end of evil

How does the war against the Rider end?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

There is hardly a battle. The beast and the false prophet are taken and cast alive into the lake of fire, and the rest fall by the sword from the King's mouth: His word. Evil doesn't get the last word. Jesus does.
