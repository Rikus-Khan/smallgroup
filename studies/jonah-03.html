Jonah 3

The second time

God gives Jonah another chance. Jonah preaches one short warning, and a violent city falls on its face before God.

Things to watch for

Jonah ran from God's call to Nineveh, the great city of Assyria, Israel's enemy. God brought him back up out of the sea. Keep these questions in mind as we read.

Part one

The second time

Verses 1–4

Same God, same call, same man. This time he goes.

Same call, new answer

The first time (1:2–3)

“Arise, go to Nineveh”

“But Jonah rose up to flee unto Tarshish.”

The second time (3:2–3)

“Arise, go unto Nineveh”

“So Jonah arose, and went unto Nineveh.”

God didn't hand Jonah a smaller job because he'd failed. He gave him the same call again. A man who has blown it is not benched for life.

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

“Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown.”

In Hebrew that's just five words. No miracle recorded, no long sermon. Nineveh was so big, with its surrounding towns, that it took about three days to cover on foot. Jonah was only a day in when the city started to turn.

forty days: a warning, and time to turn

Why warn at all, if God meant to destroy them? Because the warning was the mercy. Forty days was room to turn around.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Part two

A city on its knees

Verses 5–10

From the king to the cattle, Nineveh turns, and God turns too.

Off the throne

Notice the order: first they believed God, then they fasted. Then the most powerful man in the city came down, one step at a time:

  1. arosefrom his throne
  2. laidhis robe from him
  3. covered himwith sackcloth
  4. satin ashes

Sackcloth was rough cloth, like burlap, worn for mourning. Ashes meant deep grief and shame. From the greatest to the least, nobody was too big to get low before God.

“Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up.”

James 4:10

Two turns

The king's order covered everybody, even the herds and flocks: no food, no water, sackcloth on man and beast. But look past the sackcloth to the heart of it:

Nineveh→turn→from his evil wayAnd they named it: “the violence that is in their hands.”
God→turn→from his fierce anger“Who can tell?” They hoped He might.

Repentance isn't just feeling bad. It's turning: from the specific thing, with your hands. And notice, they had no promise, only a warning, and they still threw themselves on God's mercy. We have a promise:

“Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord;”

Acts 3:19

“God repented”?

God doesn't sin, so He has nothing to repent of in our sense. Here “repented” means He relented: He turned from the disaster He had warned about. And that's exactly what He had always said He would do, as He later told Jeremiah:

Ifthat nation, against whom I have pronounced, turn from their evil,
→
then (Jeremiah 18:8)I will repent of the evil that I thought to do unto them.

God didn't change. Nineveh did. God stayed exactly who He is: just toward the proud, merciful toward the man who turns.

A greater than Jonas

“The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: because they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here.”

Matthew 12:41

Jesus held Nineveh up as an example. A violent pagan city heard five words from a reluctant prophet and turned. People in Jesus' day had the Son of God Himself standing in front of them, and many wouldn't.

Nineveh's turning didn't last forever. About 150 years later the prophet Nahum announced its end, and it fell in 612 BC. Every generation has to turn for itself. Every man does too.

We've heard more than Nineveh ever did. We've heard about a cross and an empty tomb. The question is the same one they faced: will we turn?

Points to remember

  1. Second chances“The word of the LORD came unto Jonah the second time” 3:1God gives a failed man the same call again. You're not benched for life.
  2. God's word“So the people of Nineveh believed God” 3:5A short, plain word from God, believed, can turn a whole city.
  3. Repentance“Let them turn every one from his evil way” 3:8Repentance is turning from the specific thing, not just feeling bad.
  4. Humility“From the greatest of them even to the least of them” 3:5Nobody is too big to get low before God, not even a king.
  5. God's mercy“God saw their works, that they turned” 3:10God warns so that people can turn, and He spares those who do.
  6. Jesus“A greater than Jonas is here” Matthew 12:41We've heard far more than Nineveh did. Will we turn?

Question 1 of 6

Second chances

Does God give a man a second chance?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Answer

Yes. Jonah ran, and God came to him “the second time” with the same call. God didn't shelve him or give him a lesser job. His mercies are new every morning. If you've blown it, His word to you today is the same as it was to Jonah: arise, and go.

Question 2 of 6

God's word

What happens when God's word is preached?

What verses tell us about this?

“So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

Romans 10:17

Answer

God uses it. Jonah's message was five words in Hebrew, and he didn't even want them to listen. But it was God's word, and the people believed God. The power isn't in the preacher. It's in what God says. That's why we keep reading it together, every day.

Question 3 of 6

Repentance

What does real repentance look like?

What verses tell us about this?

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

“Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord;”

Acts 3:19

Answer

It starts with believing God, and it shows up as turning. Nineveh didn't just cry. They named their sin, “the violence that is in their hands,” and walked away from it. God “saw their works.” Being sorry you got caught isn't repentance. Turning from the thing is.

Question 4 of 6

Humility

Who needs to humble himself before God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up.”

James 4:10

Answer

Everybody, from the greatest to the least. The king of the most powerful city on earth got off his throne, took off his robe, and sat in the ashes. If he could get that low before God, so can we. And God lifts up the man who humbles himself.

Question 5 of 6

God's mercy

Why did God spare Nineveh?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Because they turned, and because God is merciful and doesn't want anyone to perish. He sent the warning so they could turn. When they did, He did just what He always said He would: He relented. God's warnings are never His last word to the man who listens.

Question 6 of 6

Jesus

What did Jesus say about the men of Nineveh?

What verses tell us about this?

“The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: because they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here.”

Matthew 12:41

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

That they repented at Jonah's preaching, and that a greater than Jonah had come. Nineveh turned after five words from an unwilling prophet. We have Jesus Himself, His cross, and His resurrection. If they turned with so little, we have every reason to turn today. And whosoever calls on Him will be saved.
