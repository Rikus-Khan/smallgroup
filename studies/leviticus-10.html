Leviticus 10

Strange fire

On the day the glory of the LORD appeared, two of Aaron's sons offered fire the LORD had not commanded, and fire from the LORD devoured them. Aaron held his peace. Then comes a command about wine, and a father's grief that Moses did not argue with.

Chapter 9 ended with the best day Israel had seen since the sea. Aaron offered for himself and for the people, Moses and Aaron came out and blessed them, and the glory appeared:

“And there came a fire out from before the LORD, and consumed upon the altar the burnt offering and the fat: which when all the people saw, they shouted, and fell on their faces.”

Leviticus 9:24

Chapter 10 happens on the same day. Aaron says so in verse 19: “this day.” The shouting had hardly stopped.

Part one

“Strange fire before the LORD”

Verses 1–7

Two priests, one day into the work, bring a fire God did not ask for.

Two fires from the LORD

The same fire, from the same LORD, on the same day. First it took the offering God asked for, and the people fell on their faces with joy. Then it took two men who came near with an offering he had not asked for. The holiness that blesses is the holiness that burns.

Nadab was Aaron's firstborn, Abihu the next (Numbers 3:2). These were not strangers to God. They had gone up the mountain with Moses:

“Then went up Moses, and Aaron, Nadab, and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel: And they saw the God of Israel: and there was under his feet as it were a paved work of a sapphire stone, and as it were the body of heaven in his clearness.”

Exodus 24:9–10

What was strange about the fire?

  • censera small pan for carrying burning coals, with incense laid on them
  • strangenot the one God gave: foreign, other
  • either of themeach of them
  • devouredate up, consumed

Moses tells us one thing about it, and it is enough: “which he commanded them not.” The LORD had already said what was not to go on his altar of incense:

“Ye shall offer no strange incense thereon, nor burnt sacrifice, nor meat offering; neither shall ye pour drink offering thereon.”

Exodus 30:9

And later in this book he says where the fire for the incense must come from: “a censer full of burning coals of fire from off the altar before the LORD” (16:12), the altar where the blood was shed. Moses doesn't tell us what was in their hearts. He tells us they came near God their own way.

“I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me”

Sanctified means treated as holy. The closer a man comes to God, the more it matters how he comes. Priests stood nearer than anyone in Israel, so the word fell on them first.

“Wherefore we receiving a kingdom which cannot be moved, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear: For our God is a consuming fire.”

Hebrews 12:28–29

“God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth.”

John 4:24

God has not changed. We come near him now more freely than Aaron ever did, but never casually, and never our own way. There is one way, and he gave it.

“And Aaron held his peace”

Five words for a father who has just lost two sons. He did not shout at God, and he did not make excuses for his boys. He said nothing.

That is not the silence of a man who feels nothing. We will hear from Aaron's heart at the end of the chapter. It is the silence of a man who knows God is right, even when it breaks him. Some of you have stood at a grave where a man's own choices put him. You know that silence.

Holding your peace is not the same as holding it in. Aaron still had his brother next to him. Don't carry a loss like that by yourself: call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor.

Out through the gate

They died “before the LORD,” at the door of the tent. Mishael and Elzaphan, Aaron's cousins, sons of his uncle Uzziel, carried them out past the altar, through the gate, and out of the camp, still in the priests' coats Moses had put on them a week before (8:13). Brethren here means kinsmen.

Somebody had to do the hard carrying, and Moses called on family. When a man falls, his brothers are the ones who come near.

Not now, not you

  • uncover your headslet the hair hang loose, a sign of mourning
  • rendtear. Men tore their clothes in grief
  • bewailweep aloud over

The whole house of Israel could weep for them. But Aaron, Eleazar and Ithamar had the anointing oil on them, and they were still on duty at the door. To mourn the dead openly, in the middle of their service, would set them against what the LORD had done, and verse 6 says it would bring wrath “upon all the people.”

It sounds hard, and it was. But it was not because their grief did not matter: the whole nation did the weeping for the priests' family. There are days a man has to keep his post while others do his weeping.

Part two

“Do not drink wine nor strong drink”

Verses 8–11

The LORD speaks to Aaron himself.

Right after the fire

Almost everywhere in Leviticus the LORD speaks to Moses, or to Moses and Aaron together. Verse 8 is the only place in the book where the LORD speaks to Aaron alone. And the first thing he says to him, on the day his sons died, is this: no wine and no strong drink when you go in.

Moses doesn't say that Nadab and Abihu had been drinking. He puts this command right after their death and lets it stand there. Many have read the two together. Read it slowly and draw the line yourself.

Strong drink is any drink stronger than wine. A statute for ever is a standing rule, not for one day only.

A clear head for holy work

No wine when you go in,so that you can tell holy from unholy, clean from unclean,
→
andteach Israel what the LORD has said.

A priest had to make calls all day long: this is clean, that is not; this is holy, that is common. Drink blurs that line in any man. The same rule shows up for kings:

“It is not for kings, O Lemuel, it is not for kings to drink wine; nor for princes strong drink: Lest they drink, and forget the law, and pervert the judgment of any of the afflicted.”

Proverbs 31:4–5

And Ezekiel saw it again for the priests: “Neither shall any priest drink wine, when they enter into the inner court,” and “they shall teach my people the difference between the holy and profane” (Ezekiel 44:21, 23).

You are a priest too

Every believer is one of God's priests now (1 Peter 2:9). You go near him every day, and you have people looking to you to tell right from wrong: your kids, a newer man in the group, a brother who is wobbling.

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

Many of us know from the inside what drink does to the line between holy and unholy. It does not move it. It just stops you seeing it. The answer Paul gives is not only “don't.” It is “be filled”: something better has to fill the place the bottle took. That is the Spirit's work, not willpower. Ask him for it, and let your brothers hold you to it.

Part three

“Such things have befallen me”

Verses 12–20

The day's work still has to be finished, by a father with two empty places beside him.

Fed at God's table

  • meat offeringthe grain offering: fine flour and oil (chapter 2)
  • without leavenwithout yeast
  • duerightful share, what is owed
  • wave breastthe breast of a peace offering, waved before the LORD, then given to the priests (7:30–34)

Moses calls them “his sons that were left.” Then he tells the three of them to sit down and eat. The most holy part, beside the altar; the wave breast and heave shoulder, with their families, in a clean place.

On the worst day of Aaron's life, God still set a meal in front of him. The LORD who judged was the same LORD who fed the ones who were left.

Moses and Aaron

MosesWherefore have ye not eaten the sin offering in the holy place… ye should indeed have eaten it in the holy place, as I commanded.

AaronBehold, this day have they offered their sin offering… and such things have befallen me: and if I had eaten the sin offering to day, should it have been accepted in the sight of the LORD?

The goat of the people's sin offering (9:15) was to be eaten by the priests in the holy place, since its blood had not been carried into the tent (6:26, 30). They had burned it instead, on a day that had already cost two lives.

Aaron's answer: on a day like this, would the LORD have accepted the most holy meal from a man in my state? (Befallen: happened to.) Moses heard it, and “he was content.” He did not press the rule against a broken heart.

A high priest who knows

In verse 3 Aaron held his peace. In verse 19 he finally speaks, and what comes out is grief. God did not ask him to pretend he was fine, and Moses did not either.

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:15–16

Aaron could only come near once his own sins were covered (9:7), and he lost sons to the fire. Our high priest never offered strange fire, and he still knows grief from the inside: “Jesus wept” (John 11:35). He took the fire of judgment on himself so that we could come near and live. So come boldly, and come his way.

Real world: come near his way

“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6
  • SinNadab and Abihu's sin was doing God's things their own way. Ours can look religious too.
  • RegretAaron's grief was real, and so is yours. Say it to God out loud, as Aaron did to Moses.
  • GraceThe fire that should fall on us fell on Christ. We come near by his blood, not our own fire.
  • HopeAaron kept his post, ate the meal God gave, and went on serving. A terrible day did not end his story.
  • TodayKeep a clear head for the people who look to you. If drink is your line, hold it, and tell a brother where you've drawn it.

Points to remember

  1. The fire“offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not” 10:1The same fire that accepted the offering devoured the two priests who came near their own way.
  2. The silence“I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me… And Aaron held his peace” 10:3The nearer we come, the more it matters how. Aaron did not argue with God.
  3. The brothers“Come near, carry your brethren from before the sanctuary out of the camp” 10:4Aaron's cousins carried them out; Aaron and his sons kept their post with the oil on them.
  4. The wine“Do not drink wine nor strong drink… when ye go into the tabernacle” 10:9The command comes right after the judgment. Draw the line, and be filled with the Spirit.
  5. The difference“that ye may put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean” 10:10A clear head to tell holy from common, and to teach others what God said.
  6. The grief“such things have befallen me… And when Moses heard that, he was content” 10:19–20Aaron's grief was honest, and Moses did not press the rule against it.

Answer

Strange fire, “which he commanded them not.” Fire went out from the LORD and devoured them, the same day his fire had accepted the offering on the altar. Come near God his way, not ours.

Question 2 of 6

The silence

What did the LORD say through Moses, and what did Aaron do?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore we receiving a kingdom which cannot be moved, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear: For our God is a consuming fire.”

Hebrews 12:28–29

Answer

“I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me, and before all the people I will be glorified.” Aaron held his peace: he did not argue with God or excuse his sons. God is holy, and those nearest him must treat him so.

Question 3 of 6

The brothers

Who carried the dead out of the camp, and who was told to stay at the door?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Mishael and Elzaphan, Aaron's cousins, carried them out. Aaron, Eleazar and Ithamar stayed at the door with the anointing oil on them, while the whole house of Israel did the mourning.

Question 4 of 6

The wine

What did the LORD tell Aaron about wine and strong drink?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

Answer

Not to drink wine or strong drink, he or his sons, when they went into the tabernacle, “lest ye die,” a statute for ever. It comes right after the fire. For us the answer is to be filled with the Spirit.

Question 5 of 6

The difference

What were the priests to tell apart, and what were they to teach?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is not for kings, O Lemuel, it is not for kings to drink wine; nor for princes strong drink: Lest they drink, and forget the law, and pervert the judgment of any of the afflicted.”

Proverbs 31:4–5

Answer

Holy from unholy, and unclean from clean, and they were to teach Israel all the statutes the LORD had given by Moses. That takes a clear head, for them and for us.

Question 6 of 6

The grief

Why was Moses angry about the sin offering, and why was he content?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The goat should have been eaten in the holy place, and it had been burnt. Aaron answered from his grief: “such things have befallen me.” Moses heard him and was content. Our high priest is touched with the feeling of our infirmities.
