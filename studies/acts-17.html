Part one

Thessalonica

Verses 1–9

Three sabbaths in the synagogue, and then a mob.

A hundred miles on foot

Paul and Silas had just been beaten and jailed at Philippi (Acts 16). Their backs were still raw, and they walked on west along the Roman road, through Amphipolis and Apollonia, to Thessalonica.

Thessalonica was the big port and capital of Macedonia, and it had a synagogue. Paul always started there. He had been hurt, and he went right back to work.

Timothy was with them too (verse 14). That is how Paul's team grew: one man at a time.

What Paul proved from the scriptures

“Opening” means unrolling the scroll and explaining it. “Alleging” means laying the evidence out, one piece at a time. For three sabbaths he used only the Old Testament:

  1. ✓The Christ must sufferthe prophets had said so (Isaiah 53; Psalm 22). The cross was the plan, not a defeatverse 3
  2. ✓And rise again from the deaddeath was not the last word (Psalm 16)verse 3
  3. ✓This Jesus… is Christthe One who did exactly this is the One God promisedverse 3

Some believed, “of the devout Greeks a great multitude, and of the chief women not a few” (verse 4). The good news was never for one kind of man. Notice who believed: people who already worshipped, people with money, people with none.

“The world upside down”

The Jews who wouldn't believe were “moved with envy”: Paul's crowd was bigger than theirs. So they hired some marketplace loafers, “lewd fellows of the baser sort,” and started a riot. Not finding Paul, they dragged his host, Jason, before the rulers.

“These that have turned the world upside down.” They meant it as an insult. It was the best review the early church ever got.

But that world was already upside down: greed, idols, cruelty. Jesus turns it right way up, one life at a time.

A charge with a little truth in it

What they said

“They defy Caesar's decrees”

Calling someone else king sounded like treason. Caesar was the only king the empire allowed.

What was true

Jesus is King

But His kingdom doesn't take over Caesar's throne. It starts in the heart, and it makes better neighbors and better workers, not rebels.

Jason put up “security”, a kind of bail, and the rulers let them go. Jason took a real risk for men he'd known a few weeks. That night the brethren sent Paul and Silas away.

Doing right can still get you slandered. If someone twists your new life into something ugly, you don't have to fight it. Keep showing up, keep working, and let your life answer.

Part two

Berea

Verses 10–15

A town of men who checked everything themselves.

What “noble” means here

The word means well-born, and here it means fair-minded. The Bereans weren't better people by birth. They were better listeners by choice.

  • swallow it because a preacher said soeven an apostle got checked, and he was glad of it
  • shut it out because it was newthat is pride dressed up as caution
  • hear it gladly, then check it“received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily”

“Prove all things; hold fast that which is good.”

1 Thessalonians 5:21

Daily

✓Mon✓Tue✓Wed✓Thu✓Fri✓Sat✓Sun

They didn't hear Paul on Saturday and forget it by Monday. Every day they opened the scrolls and asked, “Is that really what it says?” The result: “Therefore many of them believed” (verse 12), Greek women of standing and men, not a few.

That is why this group meets daily. A man in recovery lives one day at a time, and he eats one day at a time too. Today, read one chapter yourself, even a short one, and ask the Bereans' question: “Is that so?”

Chased out, and sent south

The Thessalonian Jews came about fifty miles to stir up the Berean crowd.

The believers rushed Paul out “as it were to the sea,” then down the coast to Athens. Silas and Timothy stayed to steady the church.

Alone in a strange city, Paul would later say he came to Corinth “in weakness, and in fear, and in much trembling” (1 Corinthians 2:3). Even an apostle gets worn down.

Part three

Athens

Verses 16–34

The smartest city in the world, and a hole in the middle of it shaped like God.

Two ways Athens lived without God

Paul's spirit was “stirred”, provoked and grieved, to see a city packed with idols. But he didn't just fume. He argued in the synagogue and talked in the marketplace every day. Then two schools of thinkers took him on:

The Epicureans

“Get all the pleasure you can”

Life is short, the gods are far away and don't care, so chase what feels good and avoid what hurts.

The Stoicks

“Grit your teeth and take it”

Fate runs everything. Don't feel too much, don't hope too much, just do your duty and endure.

Every man in recovery knows both. One says chase the feeling. The other says just try harder and stop feeling. Neither one has a God who is near and who helps. Paul is about to introduce Him.

A “babbler”

σπερμολόγος said spermologos “seed-picker”

A street word for a bird pecking up scattered seed, then for a man who picks up scraps of other people's ideas and repeats them. They figured Paul was passing off odd bits of religion.

Their other guess: “a setter forth of strange gods.” They heard Jesus and the resurrection (Anastasis) and may have taken them for two new gods. Being misunderstood didn't make Paul quit.

Mars' hill and an honest hole

Mars' hill is the Areopagus, the rocky hill by the Acropolis where Athens' council heard new ideas. Mars is the Roman name for the Greek god Ares.

Paul doesn't open by attacking. He opens with what he saw: a very religious city, and an altar “TO THE UNKNOWN GOD.”

They built a spare altar in case they'd missed one. Paul says: I can tell you who He is. Every man has that hole. Some fill it with a bottle, some with money, some with a woman. It was made for God.

Gods that need us, and the God who doesn't

The gods of Athens

Made, carried, fed, housed

A man carved the statue, built the temple, brought the food, and polished the god every morning. The god needed him.

The God who made everything

Gives life, breath, all things

He doesn't need a thing from you. He gives you every breath you've ever taken, and He gave you the next one too.

One more thing, in verse 26: “hath made of one blood all nations of men.” Every man on earth came from one family, and no race, class, or past puts anyone outside His reach.

Not far from every one of us

Not far. Feel after him.

“Feel after him” is a man groping in the dark, hands out, for a door. Paul says the door isn't far.

He even quotes their own poet, Aratus: “we are also his offspring.” So a stone statue can't be what God looks like.

“Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near: Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts: and let him return unto the LORD, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon.”

Isaiah 55:6–7

Tired, ashamed, unsure He'd want you? He's near, and He will “abundantly pardon.”

Before, now, and the day appointed

  1. before“the times of this ignorance”God “winked at” them: He overlooked, and held back
  2. now“commandeth all men every where to repent”everyone, in every place
  3. a day“he will judge the world in righteousness”by the Man God has chosen
  4. the proof“raised him from the dead”given “unto all men”

“Winked at” doesn't mean they were innocent. It means God was patient and didn't wipe them out. That patience has a purpose: now He asks every man to repent, which is to change your mind and turn around. It isn't a punishment. It is the way back.

And the Judge is the same Jesus who died for sinners and then rose. The One who will judge you is the One who already took your place. Turn to Him today, and the judgment is already settled.

Three answers

“Some mocked”

32

They laughed at a man who rises from the dead. Mockery is the easiest way to avoid thinking.

“We will hear thee again”

32

Polite, and gone. Later is how most men miss it. Nobody's later is promised.

“Clave unto him, and believed”

34

A judge from the council, Dionysius, and a woman, Damaris, by name. Not a crowd, but real.

A small result, but not a failure: Luke names them, and God knows every one. Paul did his part and left the rest to God.

If something in this chapter stirred you, don't say “later.” Pray it, or tell somebody, today.

  1. God's Word“Searched the scriptures daily” 17:11Listen gladly, then check it against the Bible yourself, every day.
  2. Standing out“Turned the world upside down” 17:6Following Jesus will look strange to some. It's the world that's upside down.
  3. Who God is“He giveth to all life, and breath, and all things” 17:25The God who made everything needs nothing from you and gives you everything.
  4. Close to God“He be not far from every one of us” 17:27Whoever is groping for Him will find Him close.
  5. Repentance“Commandeth all men every where to repent” 17:30Turn around today. The Judge is the One who already died for you.
  6. Hearing the news“We will hear thee again of this matter” 17:32Don't say “later.” Some clave to Him, and God knows each by name.

Question 1 of 6

God's Word

How did the Bereans test what Paul taught?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They listened with open minds, and then they went to the Scriptures every day to see whether what Paul said was so. They were not gullible and not stubborn. The result was that many believed, and Luke calls them “more noble.”

Question 2 of 6

Standing out

Why were Paul and Silas called men who “turned the world upside down”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because they preached that Jesus is the Christ, and people believed. The jealous crowd called it trouble. In truth, they were setting a crooked world right. Following Jesus still looks odd to people who are upside down.

Question 3 of 6

Who God is

What does Paul say about the God who made the world?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He made the world and everything in it, He is Lord of heaven and earth, and He doesn't live in buildings made by hand. He needs nothing from us, and He gives every one of us life and breath. He made every nation from one blood.

Question 4 of 6

Close to God

How near is God to every man?

What verses tell us about this?

“Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near.”

Isaiah 55:6

Answer

Not far at all. We live and move and have our being in Him. A man who is groping for God, however lost, finds Him near, and He will abundantly pardon.

Question 5 of 6

Repentance

What does God now command all men everywhere to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To repent: to change your mind about sin and God, and turn around to Him. God was patient with the old ignorance, but now He calls every man, everywhere. The reason to trust Him is the resurrection: the Judge is the Man God raised from the dead.

Question 6 of 6

Hearing the news

What did the men of Athens do when they heard about the resurrection?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Three things. Some mocked, some put it off, and some believed, including Dionysius and a woman named Damaris. The message is the same for everyone. What changes is what each man does with it, and today is the day to answer.
