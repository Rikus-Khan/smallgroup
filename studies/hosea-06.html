Hosea 6

Come, and let us return

A beautiful call to come back to the LORD, a goodness that burns off like morning fog, and the words Jesus would quote twice: “I desired mercy, and not sacrifice.”

Part one

Let us return

Verses 1–3

The hand that wounded is the hand that heals.

The same hand

He willheal usbind us up

Remember the end of chapter 5: the lion tore, then went back to his place “till they acknowledge their offence.” Here are the words God was waiting for. “Come, and let us return.”

They don't run from the God who wounded them. They run to Him, because the hand that tore is the only hand that can heal. A doctor who sets a broken bone hurts you too. He does it so you can walk again.

“After two days ... in the third day” was a way of saying “soon, in a short time.” God's restoring doesn't have to take forever.

As sure as sunrise

Our partFollow on

“if we follow on to know the LORD”

Keep going. Knowing God isn't one moment. It's a road.

His comingAs the morning

“his going forth is prepared as the morning”

Nobody has to talk the sun into rising.

His giftAs the rain

“he shall come unto us as the rain”

Rain on dry ground: life where nothing was growing.

On a long night, dawn can feel like it'll never come. But it always does. God's coming to the man who keeps seeking Him is that certain.

The farmer's year

  1. Former rainfall, about Octobersoftens the ground to plow and sow
  2. Winterthe seed is in the ground
  3. Latter rainspring, about March–Aprilfills out the grain
  4. Harvestbarley, then wheat

Israel had no big rivers to water its fields. Everything depended on two rains, one to start the crop and one to finish it. Miss either, and there was no harvest.

So God is saying: I'll be there at the beginning, and I'll be there at the end. He doesn't just get you started and leave.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Part two

A morning cloud

Verses 4–6

Good intentions that burn off by noon.

Gone by mid-morning

The sun comes up, and the dew is gone. Every drop.

Hear God's heart: “What shall I do unto thee?” Like a father who has tried everything. Israel's repentance was real enough at sunrise. Then the day got hot, and it was gone.

We know this one. The morning after: never again, I mean it this time. By afternoon the feeling has burned off. Feelings are like dew. What lasts is a turning that keeps following on (v. 3), one day at a time, with God and with brothers who'll hold you to it.

Words that cut

“Hewed” means chopped, like a man shaping timber with an axe. God sent prophet after prophet, and their words cut deep. That was His mercy: He kept telling them the truth.

And His judgments are “as the light that goeth forth”: as plain as daylight. Nobody could say they weren't warned.

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

There's a sorrow that's only sorry about the consequences. It fades like dew. And there's a godly sorrow that turns a man around. That's the kind that lasts.

What God weighs

חֶסֶד said chesed loyal love, kindness that keeps its promises

God commanded the sacrifices. He wasn't throwing them out. But Israel was bringing animals to the altar while lying, stealing, and running after idols the rest of the week. God wanted the heart, and loyal love toward Him and toward people, more than any ritual.

Jesus quoted this verse twice

When the Pharisees complained that Jesus ate with tax collectors and sinners:

“But go ye and learn what that meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice: for I am not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.”

Matthew 9:13

And when they condemned His hungry disciples for picking grain on the sabbath:

“But if ye had known what this meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice, ye would not have condemned the guiltless.”

Matthew 12:7

Religious people have always been tempted to keep the rules and lose the heart. Jesus sat down at the table with men like us. That's Hosea 6:6 with skin on. And it's what He asks of us now: mercy toward the next man who walks in broken.

Part three

Robbers on the road

Verses 7–11

A broken covenant, a bloody town, and priests lying in wait.

Ambush on the road

Gilead was Israel's rugged land east of the Jordan. A few years before this, a band of fifty men of Gilead helped an army officer murder Israel's king (2 Kings 15:25). “Polluted with blood” was no figure of speech.

Worse: the priests had become like a gang of highway robbers, waiting by the road for travelers, “by consent”, all of them agreeing to it.

If a pastor, a priest, or a church leader ever hurt you, God saw it, and He calls it what it is: “an horrible thing” (v. 10). What they did wasn't God. Don't let the robbers on the road keep you from the One they were supposed to point you to.

Two harvests

What Judah sowed

A harvest of judgment

The southern kingdom wasn't any better. It watched Israel fall and kept on in the same sins. Its own reaping day was set too.

What God promised

“I returned the captivity”

Even here, God looks past the judgment to the day He would bring His people home. He kept that promise: Judah came back from Babylon about 70 years after the exile began.

That promise belonged to Israel, and God kept it. What it shows us is who He is: He doesn't give up on His people. Judgment is never His last word for those who come back to Him.

“It is of the LORD'S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Question 1 of 6

Returning

What does it look like to come back to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts: and let him return unto the LORD, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon.”

Isaiah 55:7

“Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.”

James 4:8

Answer

It looks like turning around and running toward God, not away from Him, even when His discipline hurt. We admit we were wrong, trust that the hand that wounded will heal, and draw near. He draws near to us, and He abundantly pardons.

Question 2 of 6

God's faithfulness

How sure is God to come to those who seek Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

“It is of the LORD'S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Answer

As sure as the sunrise and the rains. Our part is to “follow on to know the LORD,” to keep going. His part is to come, and He never fails. He's there at the start like the former rain and at the finish like the latter rain.

Question 3 of 6

Shallow repentance

Why did Israel's goodness keep vanishing?

What verses tell us about this?

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Answer

Because it was a feeling, not a turning. It showed up in the cool of the morning and burned off when the day got hot. Sorrow over consequences fades. Godly sorrow changes direction. That kind of turning lasts because it keeps leaning on God, day after day.

Question 4 of 6

What God wants

What does God want more than sacrifice?

What verses tell us about this?

“But go ye and learn what that meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice: for I am not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.”

Matthew 9:13

“And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.”

1 Samuel 15:22

Answer

Mercy, loyal love, and really knowing Him. Ritual without the heart means nothing to God. Jesus quoted this verse to defend eating with sinners. He came for people like us, and He wants us to show that same mercy to others.

Question 5 of 6

Broken trust

What happens when the men who should lead us to God become robbers?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God sees it, and He calls it “an horrible thing.” Leaders who prey on people will answer to Him. Their sin isn't His character. If you've been hurt by someone who claimed to speak for God, bring that hurt to God Himself. He is nothing like the robbers on the road.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

Does God give up on His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is of the LORD'S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Answer

No. Even while He warned of judgment, God spoke like a heartbroken father, “what shall I do unto thee?”, and promised to bring His people back from captivity. He did it, too. His mercies are new every morning, and that's good news for every man who has to start over today.
