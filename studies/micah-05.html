Micah 5

Out of Bethlehem

Jerusalem's king is struck in the face. Then God points to a little town and promises a Ruler who never had a beginning.

Part one

Little Bethlehem

Verses 1–4

A king humbled, a town too small to count, and a Ruler older than time.

A king slapped in the face

Micah preached in Judah, the southern kingdom, about 740 to 700 BC, while Assyria was swallowing up the north. Here he tells Jerusalem, the “daughter of troops,” to call up her soldiers. An enemy is coming to surround her.

  1. about 740 BCMicah starts to preach
  2. 722Samaria falls to Assyriathe north is gone
  3. 701Assyria surrounds JerusalemGod turns them back
  4. 586Babylon takes Jerusalemthe king led off in chains

To strike a man on the cheek was to shame him in public. The “judge of Israel” is the man on the throne, and he can't even protect his own face. Bible teachers differ on which siege Micah meant. Both came, and in the second the shame was far worse than a slap:

“And they slew the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes, and put out the eyes of Zedekiah, and bound him with fetters of brass, and carried him to Babylon.”

2 Kings 25:7

Too small to count

Judah's families were counted by “thousands,” clans big enough to send a thousand men to war. Bethlehem was a farm village a few miles south of Jerusalem, too small to make the list.

Ephratah was the old name of the area, and it marks which Bethlehem (there was another one up north). It was David's hometown, where a shepherd boy was picked out to be king.

The capital is under siege and its king is shamed. And God says, “But thou, Bethlehem.” He passes over the palace and picks the little town down the road.

Seven hundred years ahead

About seven hundred years later, wise men from the east came to Jerusalem asking where the King of the Jews had been born. King Herod asked the chief priests and scribes. They didn't have to look far:

Micah, about 710 BC

“But thou, Bethlehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel…”

Micah 5:2

The scribes, answering Herod

“And they said unto him, In Bethlehem of Judaea: for thus it is written by the prophet, And thou Bethlehem, in the land of Juda, art not the least among the princes of Juda: for out of thee shall come a Governor, that shall rule my people Israel.”

Matthew 2:5–6

Notice how the scribes put it: Bethlehem is not the least any more, because of who came out of it. Even ordinary people in Jesus' day knew this verse:

“Hath not the scripture said, That Christ cometh of the seed of David, and out of the town of Bethlehem, where David was?”

John 7:42

Older than Bethlehem

  1. from everlastingHis goings forthbefore anything was made
  2. about 1000 BCDavid, born in Bethlehem
  3. about 710 BCMicah names the town
  4. about 5 BCJesus, born in BethlehemMatthew 2, Luke 2

Read it slowly. This Ruler will come forth from Bethlehem, but His goings forth have been from everlasting. A baby born in a town who is older than the town. No mere man fits that sentence.

“Jesus said unto them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Before Abraham was, I am.”

John 8:58

Jesus' life didn't begin at Bethlehem. That's where the eternal Son of God stepped into our world.

A King who shepherds

Verse 3 says God will “give them up,” leave His people to hard times, until a woman in labor gives birth. Micah has just used that picture for Jerusalem's pain (4:9–10). Here the pain ends in a birth: the Ruler comes, and the scattered come home.

The judge of Israel (5:1)

Struck and shamed

Besieged, slapped, helpless. He can't save the city, or himself.

The Ruler from Bethlehem (5:4)

Stands and feeds

“Feed” is a shepherd's word: He leads, feeds, and guards. His people “abide,” safe at last, and His name reaches the ends of the earth.

Even Matthew's “shall rule my people” uses a Greek word that means to shepherd. This King doesn't use His sheep. He lays down His life for them.

“I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.”

John 10:11

Part two

This man shall be the peace

Verses 5–9

Assyria is the terror of the age. God says the Ruler from Bethlehem is bigger.

This man shall be the peace

Not “bring” peace. Be it. When the enemy is in the palace, peace isn't a feeling or a treaty. It's a Person.

“Seven shepherds, and eight principal men” is a Hebrew way of saying enough, and then more than enough. God will raise up all the leaders needed. The Bible uses the same pattern elsewhere:

  1. Six … yea, sevenProverbs 6:16
  2. Three … and fourAmos 1:3
  3. Seven … and eightMicah 5:5

“The land of Nimrod” means Assyria and the lands around it, where the ancient hunter-king Nimrod once ruled (Genesis 10). And Paul says of Jesus:

“For he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us;”

Ephesians 2:14

Dew and a lion

The “remnant of Jacob” is the part of Israel that survives, scattered among the nations. Micah gives two pictures of them:

Verse 7

As dew

Quiet, fresh, and from the LORD. Dew doesn't wait for a man to call it down. It just comes and the grass lives.

Verse 8

As a lion

Strong and unafraid. The people who were hunted won't be prey any more.

That promise is Israel's, and God will keep it. But we've already tasted the dew: the Saviour, the apostles, and the Bible all came to the nations through Jacob's family. Jesus said:

“Ye worship ye know not what: we know what we worship: for salvation is of the Jews.”

John 4:22

Part three

I will cut off

Verses 10–15

God clears out everything His people leaned on instead of Him.

Five times: “cut off”

9×1 10×1 11×1 12×1 13×1 14  15 

Verse 9 cuts off their enemies. Then, in verses 10 to 13, God turns and cuts off things from His own people. “In that day,” the day the Ruler reigns, He removes everything they trusted in His place.

  • horses and chariotsthe army
  • cities and strong holdsthe walls
  • witchcrafts and soothsayersfortune-tellers, charms, spells
  • graven images, standing imagesidols of wood and stone
  • the LORDHe alone is left to lean on

When God takes the crutch

A “soothsayer” told fortunes. Judah's kings paid for them, and King Manasseh, Hezekiah's son, went deep into it (2 Kings 21:6). A “standing image” was a stone pillar set up for worship. And the “work of thine hands” is any idol a man makes and then bows to.

Hosea, who preached in these same years, put the right words in Israel's mouth:

“Asshur shall not save us; we will not ride upon horses: neither will we say any more to the work of our hands, Ye are our gods: for in thee the fatherless findeth mercy.”

Hosea 14:3

Most of us have leaned on something that wasn't God: a bottle, a pill, money, a woman, our own toughness. When He takes it away, it feels like loss. It's mercy. He's clearing room for Himself.

“Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen.”

1 John 5:21

Peace, and judgment

The “groves” were wooden poles set up for Asherah, a Canaanite goddess. God pulls them up by the roots. Then He turns to the “heathen,” the nations that set themselves against Him, with a judgment such as they have never heard of.

The Ruler from Bethlehem→is the peace→for all who come to Himverse 5
The same Ruler→executes vengeance→on all who refuse Himverse 15

The same chapter holds both. Judgment is real. But God waits a long time before it falls, because He wants men to come home:

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Question 1 of 6

Christ foretold

Where was the Saviour to be born, and how did they know?

What verses tell us about this?

“And they said unto him, In Bethlehem of Judaea: for thus it is written by the prophet, And thou Bethlehem, in the land of Juda, art not the least among the princes of Juda: for out of thee shall come a Governor, that shall rule my people Israel.”

Matthew 2:5–6

“And Joseph also went up from Galilee, out of the city of Nazareth, into Judaea, unto the city of David, which is called Bethlehem; (because he was of the house and lineage of David:)”

Luke 2:4

Answer

In Bethlehem, David's little hometown. They knew because God said so through Micah about seven hundred years before. Mary and Joseph lived up north in Nazareth, yet God moved an emperor's census to put them in Bethlehem at the right time. When God says it, it happens.

Question 2 of 6

Who Jesus is

Did Jesus' life begin in Bethlehem?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus said unto them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Before Abraham was, I am.”

John 8:58

“And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.”

Colossians 1:17

Answer

No. His earthly life began there, but He Himself has no beginning. Micah says His goings forth are “from everlasting,” and Jesus said, “Before Abraham was, I am.” The baby in the manger was the eternal Son of God, who holds all things together. That's why He can save us.

Question 3 of 6

The Shepherd

What kind of ruler is the One from Bethlehem?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.”

John 10:11

Answer

A shepherd-king. Earthly kings get besieged and shamed, and they can't save anyone. This King stands firm in the LORD's own strength, feeds His people, and keeps them safe. And unlike the kings who used their people, He gave His life for His sheep.

Question 4 of 6

Peace

Where does real peace come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us;”

Ephesians 2:14

“Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid.”

John 14:27

Answer

From a Person. Micah says “this man shall be the peace,” even with the enemy in the palace. Jesus makes peace between us and God by the cross, and then gives His own peace to us. The world's peace depends on how things are going. His doesn't.

Question 5 of 6

Small things

Does God use what the world overlooks?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise; and God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty; And base things of the world, and things which are despised, hath God chosen, yea, and things which are not, to bring to nought things that are:”

1 Corinthians 1:27–28

Answer

All the time. He passed over Jerusalem and chose Bethlehem. He passed over David's big brothers and chose the shepherd boy. If you feel like a nobody with a past nobody would pick, you're exactly the kind of man God likes to use, so the glory goes to Him.

Question 6 of 6

Crutches

Why does God cut off the things we lean on?

What verses tell us about this?

“Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.”

Psalm 20:7

Answer

Because anything we lean on in His place will fail us, and it keeps us from leaning on Him. Judah trusted horses, walls, fortune-tellers and idols. God cut them all off so His people would trust Him alone. When He takes something away from us, it isn't cruelty. It's room being made for the only One who can hold us up.
