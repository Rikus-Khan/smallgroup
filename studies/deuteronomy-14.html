Deuteronomy 14

An holy people, at the LORD's table

“Ye are the children of the LORD your God.” So they are not to mourn like the nations, nor eat what he called unclean. And every year they bring a tenth of all their increase, and eat it with joy before the LORD, and every third year the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless and the widow eat and are satisfied.

Part one

“Ye are the children of the LORD your God”

Verses 1–2

Who they are comes first. What they do follows.

Children grieve, but not like that

The nations mourned their dead by cutting their bodies and shaving the front of the head, baldness between your eyes. We saw the cuttings forbidden in Leviticus 19:28. Here Moses gives the reason first: “Ye are the children of the LORD your God.” The prophets of Baal show what it looked like:

“And they cried aloud, and cut themselves after their manner with knives and lancets, till the blood gushed out upon them.”

1 Kings 18:28

A child of God still grieves. But he doesn't tear himself apart, because his Father hasn't left him alone with it.

“But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope.”

1 Thessalonians 4:13

Some men punish their own bodies when grief or shame comes: the bottle, the needle, the fist through the wall, or worse. If that's you, don't carry it alone today. Call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor.

“A peculiar people unto himself”

bondmen in Egypt → a peculiar people Peculiar here means his own: a people belonging to him, “a peculiar treasure unto me above all people” (Exodus 19:5). Not odd; owned, and treasured.

Why them? Not for their size or their goodness: “The LORD did not set his love upon you, nor choose you, because ye were more in number than any people… But because the LORD loved you” (7:7–8). Peter gives the same names to everyone who belongs to Christ:

“But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:”

1 Peter 2:9

Part two

“Thou shalt not eat any abominable thing”

We saw the two marks of a clean beast in Leviticus 11. Moses adds something new: seven wild beasts by name, the game of the hills and fields of a land they had not yet entered. Abominable means detestable, a thing to turn from. Cleaveth the cleft into two claws: the hoof split right through.

The same lines, one new name

The camel, the hare, the coney and the swine; fins and scales; the birds of prey and the bat. It is the list we read in Leviticus 11, with one name added: Leviticus names twenty birds, and Deuteronomy twenty-one. The new one is the glede, a bird of prey of the kite kind.

“Every creeping thing that flieth” means winged insects. Leviticus 11:21–22 had already set apart the ones with “legs above their feet, to leap withal”, the locust and the grasshopper, as clean.

Why say it all again? Because a new generation was listening. Forty years earlier their fathers had heard it at Sinai. Now Moses tells it to the children, at the edge of the land, so they would know it for themselves.

Framed by one reason

Verse 21“for thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God”

The whole menu sits between the same words, twice. A beast that dieth of itself died on its own and was never bled; the stranger and the alien, not under this law, might have it. Seethe means boil. “Thou shalt not seethe a kid in his mother’s milk” is the third time in the law (Exodus 23:19; 34:26). Moses doesn't say why, and we don't need to guess. The reason he does give is the one on both sides: whose they were.

From the plate to the heart

In Leviticus 11 we heard Jesus' word “purging all meats” (Mark 7:19) and Peter's sheet (Acts 10). Jesus didn't stop at the food. He went on to say where uncleanness really lives:

“And he said, That which cometh out of the man, that defileth the man.”

Mark 7:20

“I know, and am persuaded by the Lord Jesus, that there is nothing unclean of itself: but to him that esteemeth any thing to be unclean, to him it is unclean.”

Romans 14:14

The food laws are fulfilled in Christ. The holiness they pictured is not done away. Israel was “an holy people” at every meal; a believer is called to be holy in every thought, word and deed, because Christ has made him clean: “What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common” (Acts 10:15).

Part three

“Thou shalt truly tithe”

Verses 22–29

A tenth of everything, and a feast with it.

A tenth, eaten before the LORD

“all the increase of thy seed… year by year”one in ten

To tithe is to set apart a tenth. In Numbers 18 we saw the tithe given to the Levites for their service. Here Moses speaks of a tithe the household itself was to eat, with the firstlings of the herd and flock, “in the place which he shall choose to place his name there”. That was Shiloh at the first (Jeremiah 7:12), and later Jerusalem, “the city which I have chosen me to put my name there” (1 Kings 11:36).

And the reason: “that thou mayest learn to fear the LORD thy God always.” They learned the fear of the LORD at a feast, sitting down to eat in his presence. Fearing him and rejoicing before him go together.

When the way is too long

  1. At homethe tithe of the harvesttoo far to carry
  2. On the road“turn it into money”“bind up the money in thine hand”
  3. The place“which the LORD thy God shall choose”verse 25
  4. The feastbuy, eat, rejoice“thou, and thine household”

God made a way for the man who lived far off. The tithe didn't have to be carried the whole way as grain and wine and oil. To bestow the money is to spend it. Whatsoever thy soul lusteth after means whatever you long for; there is no sin in the word here. Three times in these verses comes “the place which the LORD… shall choose” (23, 24, 25). The meal was not just anywhere: it was “before the LORD”.

“Wine, or… strong drink”

Say it plainly: verse 26 lets the tithe money buy “wine, or… strong drink” for the feast before the LORD. That is what it says. The same book calls the son who is “a glutton, and a drunkard” rebellious (21:20).

And say this just as plainly: a verse about Israel's feast is no ticket back to the cup for a man God has set free from it. The feast was never about the drink. It was about eating “before the LORD” and rejoicing with your household, and you can do every bit of that with a clear head.

“It is good neither to eat flesh, nor to drink wine, nor any thing whereby thy brother stumbleth, or is offended, or is made weak.”

Romans 14:21

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

A table for the least

the Levite“no part nor inheritance with thee”
the strangerfar from his own people
the fatherlessno father to provide
the widowno husband to provide

Every third year the whole tithe stayed home, “within thy gates”, and four kinds of people with nothing of their own came and “shall eat and be satisfied”. The Levite had no land, for the LORD was his part (Numbers 18:20), so Israel was told: “thou shalt not forsake him.”

“Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world.”

James 1:27

“That the LORD thy God may bless thee” was his word to Israel in the land, not a money formula for us. Believers give as Paul says: “Every man according as he purposeth in his heart… for God loveth a cheerful giver” (2 Corinthians 9:7).

Real world: whose you are

  • SinGrief and shame can turn a man against his own body. That is how the nations mourned, not the children of the LORD.
  • RegretYou may have taken years of your family's bread for yourself. The third-year table shows God's heart for those left with nothing.
  • Grace“What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common.” He calls you his peculiar people, his own.
  • HopeGod put the fear of him at a feast table. Walking with him is not grim; it is rejoicing “before the LORD”.
  • TodaySet something apart for God before you spend on yourself, and share a meal with someone who has less than you: a new man, a widow, a brother just out.

Points to remember

  1. The children“Ye are the children of the LORD your God: ye shall not cut yourselves” 14:1Who they were came first; so they would not mourn like the nations.
  2. The people“an holy people… a peculiar people unto himself” 14:2Set apart and owned, because the LORD loved them; Peter says the same of us.
  3. The table“That which cometh out of the man, that defileth the man” Mark 7:20The food laws are fulfilled in Christ; holiness now lives in the heart.
  4. The tithe“that thou mayest learn to fear the LORD thy God always” 14:23They learned the fear of the LORD by feasting in his presence.
  5. The feast“Then shalt thou turn it into money… and thou shalt rejoice” 14:25–26God made a way for the man far off; the joy was “before the LORD”.
  6. The third year“the stranger, and the fatherless, and the widow… shall eat and be satisfied” 14:29God sets a table for those with nothing, and the Levite is not forsaken.

The tithe

What was eating the tithe before the LORD meant to teach them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To fear the LORD their God always. They learned it year by year, eating a tenth of all their increase in his presence, in the place he chose.

Question 5 of 6

The feast

What could a man do if the place was too far to carry his tithe?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Turn it into money, carry the money to the place the LORD would choose, buy food there for the feast, and eat and rejoice before the LORD with his household.
