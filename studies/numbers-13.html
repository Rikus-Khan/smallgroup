Numbers 13

Twelve men, one cluster, and the giants

The LORD sends twelve rulers to search the land he is giving. For forty days they walk it, and they carry back a cluster of grapes on a staff between two men. Then ten of them say, “We be not able,” and one man stills the people: “Let us go up at once.”

Part one

“Send thou men”

Verses 1–16

Twelve rulers, one from every tribe, and a land already promised.

“Which I give”

Look at the LORD's words in verse 2: the land of Canaan, which I give unto the children of Israel. The question was never whether they would have it. God had already said it was theirs. The men were sent to see what he was giving, not to vote on it.

Moses tells the other side of it later, at the end of the forty years. The people came to him and asked for the spies, and he agreed:

“And ye came near unto me every one of you, and said, We will send men before us, and they shall search us out the land, and bring us word again by what way we must go up, and into what cities we shall come.”

Deuteronomy 1:22

Both are true: the people asked, and the LORD said, “Send thou men.” Searching out the road is wise. The trouble came later, when what they saw became a reason not to go.

Where they stood

  1. out of Egyptthe first month33:3
  2. at Sinaithe law and the tabernacleExodus 19–40
  3. the cloud liftsthe second year, second month, twentieth day10:11
  4. the wilderness of Parantwelve men sent13:3

It is a little over a year since the Red sea. Behind them: bread from heaven, water from the rock, the law, the tabernacle, the cloud going before them. In front of them: the land. Moses says it was a short road: “eleven days’ journey from Horeb by the way of mount Seir unto Kadesh-barnea” (Deuteronomy 1:2).

They were at the border. Everything God had done so far had brought them to this one step.

These were not boys or nobodies. Each was “a ruler”, one of the “heads of the children of Israel” (verses 2–3). Levi sends no man, since the Levites had no inheritance in the land; Joseph's two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh, each send one, so there are still twelve.

Ten of these names are remembered only for what they said in this chapter. Two are remembered for ever. The same trip, the same land, the same giants: what made the difference was not the eyes, but the heart.

Oshea becomes Jehoshua

Oshea → Jehoshua Oshea means salvation. Moses set the LORD's own name on the front of it: Jehoshua, the LORD is salvation. We know him as Joshua.

It is the same name the angel gave the Son of Mary: “thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins.” The New Testament even calls Joshua by it, in the passage about this very chapter:

“For if Jesus had given them rest, then would he not afterward have spoken of another day.”

Hebrews 4:8

There, “Jesus” is Joshua, who later led Israel into the land. Paul's point: Joshua gave them a rest in Canaan, but a greater rest still remained, and a greater Jesus gives it. We'll come back to that in chapter 14.

Part two

“See the land, what it is”

Verses 17–25

Forty days in the land, and fruit to carry home.

What to look for

  • strong or weak, few or manythe people: how many, and how hard to beat
  • tents, or strong holdsopen camps, or walled forts
  • fat or leanof the soil: rich and fruitful, or thin and poor
  • wood therein, or nottrees, for timber and for fruit
  • firstripe grapesthe first grapes to ripen, early in the grape harvest

It is an honest list, and Moses wanted honest answers. Faith does not shut its eyes. It looks at the walls and counts the people, and then it looks at God.

And before they left: be ye of good courage. Moses knew they would see things that would scare them. He told them to be brave before they saw anything at all.

The length of the land

They went “from the wilderness of Zin”, the desert on the south, “unto Rehob, as men come to Hamath”, the far north, on the road to Hamath. The line shows how far they walked, not the road they took.

In the south they came to Hebron, where Abraham, Isaac and Jacob were buried. Living there now: three sons of Anak, by name. Moses adds that Hebron was built “seven years before Zoan in Egypt”: older than Zoan, where God did “Marvellous things… in the field of Zoan” (Psalm 78:12).

The fathers' graves were in the hands of giants. That is where the fear would start.

One cluster, two men

One bunch of grapes, so heavy that they bare it (carried it) between two men on a pole. They brought pomegranates and figs too. They named the brook for it: Eshcol, “because of the cluster of grapes.”

God let them taste the land before they had to fight for it. He does that still: a clean week, a peaceful morning, a meal with your children without shame. That is the fruit of the land. It is a promise in your hands that there is more where it came from.

Part three

“We be not able”

Verses 26–33

The same facts, and two different reports.

“Nevertheless”

They came back to Kadesh, in the wilderness of Paran. The report starts well, and every word of it is true: the land “floweth with milk and honey; and this is the fruit of it.” Then one word turns the whole thing: Nevertheless.

  • in the souththe Amalekites, who had fought them at Rephidim
  • in the mountainsthe Hittites, the Jebusites, the Amorites
  • by the sea and by Jordanthe Canaanites (the coast of Jordan is its side, its border)
  • everywherewalled cities, “very great”, and the children of Anak

Notice what is missing. Not one word about the LORD who had sent them and promised the land. The facts were right. The report was wrong, because God was left out of it.

Same land, two reports

The ten

“We be not able”

They measured the giants against themselves: “they are stronger than we.” And they were right. On their own, they were not able.

Caleb

“We are well able”

He saw the same walls. He measured them against the LORD who gave the land. “Let us go up at once.”

Caleb stilled the people: he hushed a crowd that was already rising. One man against ten, and against a nation's fear. He did not say the giants were small. He said God was bigger.

“What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us?”

Romans 8:31

“In our own sight as grasshoppers”

“We were in our own sight as grasshoppers.” They decided how small they were first, and then they were sure the giants saw them the same way. Fear always starts at home, in a man's own eyes.

The evil report did not stay with the facts. “A land that eateth up the inhabitants thereof” is fear talking: a land that would swallow them. The same men had just carried a cluster home on a pole.

Who are the grasshoppers?

“It is he that sitteth upon the circle of the earth, and the inhabitants thereof are as grasshoppers; that stretcheth out the heavens as a curtain, and spreadeth them out as a tent to dwell in:”

Isaiah 40:22

From where the LORD sits, the sons of Anak are grasshoppers too. The ten measured themselves against giants. Caleb measured the giants against God. That is the whole difference between the two reports.

Every man here has a giant of his own: a habit years deep, a debt, a record, a temper, a family that does not trust him yet. And every man has heard the evil report in his own head: You'll never stay clean. You're not able. On your own, that report is true. With God, it is not the last word.

“Ye are of God, little children, and have overcome them: because greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world.”

1 John 4:4

Real world: which report?

  • Look honestlyFaith counts the walls. Name your giant out loud to a brother.
  • Taste the fruitRemember what God has already put in your hands, and thank him for it.
  • Put God in the reportWhen you tell your story, say what he has promised, not only what you are up against.
  • Be a CalebWhen the room fills with fear, one steady voice can still it.

If you fell yesterday, the evil report is loud this morning. Don't let it be the only voice. Call or text a brother in Christ today and tell him the truth: the giant is real, and so is the LORD who gave you the land.

Question 4 of 6

Nevertheless

What did the men say about the people and the cities of the land?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That the people were strong, the cities walled and very great, and the children of Anak there: “We be not able.” Every fact was true, but the LORD was left out, and that made it an evil report.
