Matthew 17

Jesus only

On a mountain, three men see who Jesus really is. Down in the valley, a desperate father brings Him a son nobody else could help.

Part one

On the mountain

Verses 1–13

For a few minutes, the curtain is pulled back.

An high mountain apart

“After six days”: six days after Peter said, “Thou art the Christ,” and Jesus promised some would see Him “coming in his kingdom” (16:28).

Matthew doesn't name the mountain. Many think it was Mount Hermon, the high peak above Caesarea Philippi, where they had just been. An old tradition says Mount Tabor in Galilee. The place matters less than what they saw.

Peter, James, and John were the three Jesus kept closest: at the bedside of Jairus' daughter (Mark 5:37), here, and later in Gethsemane (26:37).

Transfigured

metamorphoō met-a-mor-FO-o (where we get metamorphosis) “changed in form”

No spotlight hit Him from outside. The glory came from inside. For thirty-some years, the Son of God had looked like an ordinary working man from Nazareth. On the mountain, for a few minutes, they saw what had been there all along.

“For he received from God the Father honour and glory, when there came such a voice to him from the excellent glory, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. And this voice which came from heaven we heard, when we were with him in the holy mount.”

2 Peter 1:17–18

Peter never forgot it. More than thirty years later, near the end of his life, he was still writing about it.

Moses and Elias

Moses

the law

God gave the law through him on Mount Sinai. He died, and God Himself buried him (Deuteronomy 34:6).

Elias (Elijah)

the prophets

The great prophet who never died. He went up by a whirlwind into heaven (2 Kings 2:11).

The whole Old Testament, the law and the prophets, stood on that mountain beside Jesus. And Luke tells us what they talked about:

“Who appeared in glory, and spake of his decease which he should accomplish at Jerusalem.”

Luke 9:31

Peter wanted to build three tabernacles, shelters of branches like the booths at the Feast of Tabernacles, and stay up there. Good intentions. But it put Jesus on the same level as two of His servants.

Hear ye him

Deuteronomy 18:15

The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken;

Matthew 3:17, at His baptism

And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.

Moses himself had said God would send a Prophet like him, and “unto him ye shall hearken.” Now, with Moses standing right there, the Father points at Jesus: this is the one. The same words He spoke at the Jordan, with three more added: “hear ye him.”

Peter was busy talking. The Father interrupted him. Sometimes the most spiritual thing a man can do is be quiet and listen to Jesus.

Save Jesus only

A bright cloud covered them, the cloud of God's presence, like the cloud over the tabernacle in Moses' day. The voice came out of it, and the three men fell flat on their faces, terrified.

Then Jesus came, touched them, and said, “Arise, and be not afraid.” When they looked up, Moses and Elias were gone. “They saw no man, save Jesus only.”

If you're face down today, in fear or in shame, He doesn't stand over you and lecture. He comes close, puts a hand on you, and says, Get up. Don't be afraid. Then look up. He's still there.

Elias is come already

Malachi 4:5

Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD:

Luke 1:17, the angel about John

And he shall go before him in the spirit and power of Elias, to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children, and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just; to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.

The scribes taught from Malachi that Elijah had to come before the Messiah. If this is the Messiah, the disciples wondered, where's Elijah? Jesus says: he came. John the Baptist came in Elijah's spirit and power, and they killed him (14:10). “Listed” means wanted. They did whatever they wanted with him.

And “likewise shall also the Son of man suffer.” Even coming down from the glory, Jesus talks about the cross. The glory they saw would only make sense after He rose from the dead.

Part two

Down in the valley

Verses 14–21

From the mountaintop straight into a father's worst nightmare.

A father on his knees

“Lunatick” comes from the word for the moon. People then blamed the moon for fits and seizures. Matthew lists “possessed with devils” and “lunatick” separately (4:24), so not every seizure is a demon. But here the text says plainly it was one (v. 18). It was trying to destroy the boy, throwing him into fires and into water.

Some of you know this father. Maybe you've been him: watching your child walk into the fire, again and again, and nothing you do stops it. Or maybe you were the son, and somebody was on their knees for you.

Mark tells us what this father cried out, and it's a prayer any of us can pray:

“And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.”

Mark 9:24

Bring him hither to me

the fatherbrought his son tothe disciples“they could not cure him”
the fatherbrought his son toJesus“cured from that very hour”

Jesus' sigh, “how long shall I suffer you?”, was aimed at the unbelief all around Him, His own disciples included. Not at the father, and not at the boy. Then one word from Him, and the boy was free.

People will let you down. Programs, preachers, even good friends can come up short. Don't stop there. Take it to Jesus. “Bring him hither to me” still stands.

Faith as a grain of mustard seed

These nine had cast out devils before. Jesus had given them that power (10:1). Maybe they started to treat it like something they owned, and stopped leaning on God. “Because of your unbelief.”

A mustard seed is about the size of a pinhead. Jesus' point: it isn't how big your faith is. It's how big the God it rests on is. A little faith in a great God moves mountains.

This isn't a promise to get whatever we want. It's a promise that nothing God asks of us is too big when we trust Him. And some battles only go out “by prayer and fasting”: on your knees, depending on Him, not on yourself.

Part three

The children are free

Verses 22–27

The cross again, and a coin in a fish's mouth.

He kept telling them

  1. Caesarea Philippi“be killed, and be raised again”16:21
  2. Galilee“betrayed … raised again”17:22–23
  3. going up to Jerusalem“crucify … rise again”20:18–19
  4. the empty tomb“risen, as he said”28:6

This time Jesus adds a new word: betrayed. One of their own would hand Him over.

“They were exceeding sorry.” They heard kill and stopped listening. They missed “the third day he shall be raised again.” When bad news hits, don't stop listening before God finishes the sentence.

The temple tax

This tribute wasn't Rome's tax. It was the yearly temple tax every Jewish man paid for the upkeep of God's house, going back to Moses:

“This they shall give, every one that passeth among them that are numbered, half a shekel after the shekel of the sanctuary: (a shekel is twenty gerahs:) an half shekel shall be the offering of the LORD.”

Exodus 30:13
½ shekeleach man, every year, about two days' pay
× 2“for me and thee”
1 cointhe “piece of money” in the fish's mouth, worth exactly a shekel

“Prevented him” means got there first: before Peter could bring it up, Jesus already knew.

Free, and paying anyway

His right

“the children are free”

Kings don't tax their own sons. The temple was His Father's house. The Son of God owed it nothing.

His choice

“lest we should offend them”

He paid anyway, so nobody would stumble over a small thing and miss the big one.

Being free in Christ doesn't mean insisting on every right we have. Sometimes love lays a right down for someone else's sake.

And look who provided the money: the One who knows which fish will bite first. He paid for Peter too, “for me and thee.” A bigger debt was coming, and He would pay that one for Peter as well, and for us.

Points to remember

  1. His glory“His face did shine as the sun” 17:2For a moment they saw who He had been all along: God's own Son in glory.
  2. Listen“This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him” 17:5Stop talking and listen to Jesus.
  3. Fear“Arise, and be not afraid” 17:7He comes close, touches us, and lifts us up. Then: Jesus only.
  4. Faith“If ye have faith as a grain of mustard seed” 17:20It's not the size of our faith but the size of our God. Pray, and lean on Him.
  5. The cross“The third day he shall be raised again” 17:23He told them again and again: the cross, and then the empty tomb.
  6. Freedom“Notwithstanding, lest we should offend them” 17:27Free men can lay down a right out of love, and He pays what we owe.

Question 1 of 6

His glory

What did Peter, James, and John see on the mountain?

What verses tell us about this?

“For we have not followed cunningly devised fables, when we made known unto you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, but were eyewitnesses of his majesty.”

2 Peter 1:16

Answer

They saw Jesus' glory shine out from inside Him, His face like the sun and His clothes white as light, with Moses and Elias talking with Him about His death at Jerusalem. They saw who He really is: the Son of God, Lord over the law and the prophets.

Question 2 of 6

Listen

What did the Father tell the disciples to do?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken;”

Deuteronomy 18:15

Answer

“Hear ye him.” Peter was full of plans, and the Father interrupted to point to His Son. Jesus is the Prophet Moses promised, greater than Moses and Elias. Our job is to listen to Him and do what He says.

Question 3 of 6

Fear

What does Jesus do when His people are afraid?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He comes to them, touches them, and lifts them up: “Arise, and be not afraid.” He doesn't leave His people face down. And when they look up, He's the one still standing there. Jesus only.

Question 4 of 6

Faith

Why couldn't the disciples help the boy?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because of their unbelief. They had leaned on what they'd done before instead of on God. Even a tiny faith is enough when it rests on Him, and some battles are only won through prayer and fasting, depending on Him and not on ourselves.

Question 5 of 6

The cross

What did Jesus keep telling His disciples about what was ahead?

What verses tell us about this?

“He is not here: for he is risen, as he said. Come, see the place where the Lord lay.”

Matthew 28:6

Answer

That He would be betrayed, suffer, and be killed, and that the third day He would be raised again. The disciples heard the dying and missed the rising. But it happened exactly as He said.

Question 6 of 6

Freedom

Why did Jesus pay a tax He didn't owe?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As God's Son, He owed nothing to His Father's house. But He paid anyway, so He wouldn't put a needless stumbling block in anyone's way. He laid down His right out of love, provided the money Himself, and paid for Peter too.
