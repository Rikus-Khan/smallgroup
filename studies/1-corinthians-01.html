1 Corinthians 1

The foolishness of God

Paul writes to a gifted, noisy, divided church in a wild port city. He starts with grace, then goes straight to the cross.

The first letter to the Corinthians

“Paul, called to be an apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God, and Sosthenes our brother, Unto the church of God which is at Corinth”

Written from
Ephesus, in Asia, during Paul's third journey (Acts 19; 1 Corinthians 16:8)
When
about AD 55
To whom
the church at Corinth, in Greece, which Paul had started a few years before (Acts 18)
Why
to answer bad news from Chloe's household and a list of questions the church had written. Most think Stephanas and two others carried their letter to him (16:17).

Where 1 Corinthians fits in Paul's life

  1. about AD 34 to 35Saul turned aroundActs 9

  3. about 49 to 52Second journeyActs 15–18

  5. about 53 to 57Third journey, three years at EphesusActs 19–20
  6. about 551 Corinthians, from Ephesus“Is Christ divided?”
  7. about 56 to 572 Corinthians, then Romansfrom Macedonia, then Corinth

Paul spent about a year and a half in Corinth (Acts 18:11), then left. The church was only about three years old when this letter was written, and already in a mess.

Paul did not give up on them. He wrote this letter, and at least one more after it. A young church that fails is not finished. Neither is a young believer.

Corinth: rich, rough, and on the move

Corinth sat on a neck of land about four miles wide, with a sea on each side. Ships dreaded the stormy trip around the south of Greece, so small ships were dragged across a stone track, ship and all.

Money, sailors, slaves and gods all passed through. It was known for wealth and loose living; to “live like a Corinthian” was not a compliment.

Into that city God sent Paul: “I have much people in this city” (Acts 18:10). This letter is written to them.

The whole letter on one page

Paul wrote this letter to fix real problems, one at a time. Chapters 1–6 answer what he'd heard. From chapter 7 he answers what they had asked. If you read one chapter alone, this is where it sits.

Chapter 16 closes with the collection for poor believers and goodbyes. A church with this many problems still gets called “the church of God” and “saints.” That's where Paul starts.

Part one

Saints, with problems

Verses 1–9

Before one word of correction, Paul tells them who they are in Christ.

Called saints, in Corinth

Sanctified means set apart for God. Saints means holy ones. Paul gives both words to the Corinthians, the church with the worst record in the New Testament. They are not saints because they behaved. They are saints because they are “in Christ Jesus.” God made that call first, and the behaving is supposed to follow.

Notice who is writing with Paul: Sosthenes our brother. A man of that name was the ruler of the synagogue in Corinth who got beaten in front of the court (Acts 18:17). If it's the same man, he went from the crowd that opposed Paul to Paul's partner in the gospel. Nobody is too far gone.

“Grace be unto you, and peace.” Grace first: God's favor you didn't earn. Then peace. Paul never gets the order backward.

What God had already done

What God had done (verses 4–9)

Given, enriched, confirmed, called

Grace given. Every kind of gift. The message of Christ proven true in them. And a promise to keep them to the end.

What they were doing (verses 10–13)

Quarrelling over who was best

Teams, rivalry, pride in the gifts God gave them for free.

God had not waited for them to clean up. He gave first. And look at the end of it: “God is faithful.” He will “confirm you unto the end,” and the day He finishes the job they will be blameless. That's not because they've got it together. It's because He is faithful. A believer is kept, even on a bad week.

Part two

Is Christ divided?

Verses 10–17

A church picks teams. Paul asks who died for them.

Picking teams

I am of Paulthe man who started the church
I am of Apollosthe polished speaker from Alexandria (Acts 18:24)
I am of CephasPeter, the famous apostle. Cephas is his Aramaic name.
I am of Christsounds the holiest, but used as a badge to look down on the rest

Paul and Apollos were on the same side. Nobody in this fight was fighting for the teachers. They were fighting for themselves: my guy is better than your guy, so I am better than you. A few people from Chloe's household had the nerve to tell Paul. He names them openly, because he means to deal with the problem honestly.

Who died for you?

Christ is not cut into piecesHe is one, and so is the body that belongs to Him.

Three questions, each with the same answer. Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Were you baptized in Paul's name? No, no, and no. Only one Man hung on a cross for you. Whoever follows Him belongs to the same family.

Anyone who's been around churches or meetings knows it: my preacher, my group, my way. It starts small and it feels like loyalty. Paul calls it a division, and he doesn't let it slide.

Why Paul is glad he baptized so few

What baptism is not

A team badge, or what saves

Nobody gets into Paul's club by being baptized by Paul. And nobody is saved by the water. “Preach the gospel” is what saves.

What baptism is

A believer's step of obedience

Jesus told His followers to do it. It's a public picture of what He did for you, and what He's done in you. Paul isn't against it.

The people Paul did baptize were real men: Crispus and Gaius, and the household of Stephanas (verses 14–16). Paul didn't even remember if there were others. That's the point. If the water were the center, he'd have kept count. The center is the cross.

Part three

The word of the cross

Verses 18–25

What sounds like foolishness to one man is the power of God to another.

Foolishness, or power

To them that perish

“Foolishness”

A God who dies on a Roman cross, the death for slaves and rebels? That sounds like a joke to a man who thinks he has it handled.

To us which are saved

“The power of God”

To a man who has run out of his own answers, it's the only thing that ever worked. It's not advice. It is power.

Paul puts himself in the second group: unto us. Most of the men reading this once thought the cross was foolish, or never thought about it at all. Then something in them gave out. The man who has run out of answers is closer to the cross than the man who still has them all.

Where are the clever ones now?

  1. The wisethe Greek thinker, with big ideas
  2. The scribethe Jewish Bible expert
  3. The disputerthe sharp debater, who always wins the argument

Learning isn't bad. God made the mind. Paul's point is that no amount of cleverness ever found Him: “the world by wisdom knew not God.” God chose to save by something that looks foolish, “the foolishness of preaching,” so that nobody can say he figured it out and got himself saved.

That's good news for the man who never finished school, and for the man who thought he was too smart to need it. The way in is the same for both.

One cross, three reactions

The JewsA stumblingblock

They wanted a sign, a mighty Messiah. A Messiah on a cross tripped them up.

The GreeksFoolishness

They wanted wisdom, a clever philosophy. A crucified God sounded absurd.

The called, of both kindsGod's power and wisdom

The same message, the same cross. To the man God has called, it is exactly what he was looking for.

“The foolishness of God is wiser than men; and the weakness of God is stronger than men.” Even God's “weakness,” a dying Savior, beats the best plan of the strongest man. If you've lost a fight with something you couldn't beat, that's worth hearing today.

Part four

Look at who God called

Verses 26–31

Look around your own church, Paul says. God picks the ones the world wrote off.

Not many. But God hath chosen…

  • not many wisethe ones with the degrees and the answers
  • not many mightythe ones with the power and the money
  • not many noblethe ones born into the right family
  • but the foolish, the weak, the base, the despisedthe ones everybody else wrote off

Notice, not many, not none. God's call goes out to everyone: “both Jews and Greeks” (verse 24). The point is who tends to answer it. The proud don't. The ones who have been knocked flat do. And when they answer, God does not hold their record against them. He chose them.

Such were some of you

“Base” means low-born, from the gutter. “Things which are not” means nobodies: people who don't count. God takes the ones who don't count and uses them to put the proud to shame. This is exactly who was in the Corinth church. Paul says so himself, later in this letter:

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

“Such were some of you.” Were. Past tense. Some of them had been thieves, drunks and men who wrecked their families, and God washed them, set them apart, and declared them right. Whatever you were, that's the past tense. Today you are a man God chose.

Everything we need, in one Person

  1. WisdomGod's plan, in a Person. You don't need to figure it out.
  2. RighteousnessRight with God, as a gift. The record is cleared.
  3. SanctificationSet apart for God, and being made clean from the inside, day by day.
  4. RedemptionBought back. A slave's price paid, and the man set free.

Notice “of him are ye in Christ Jesus.” It's God's doing from start to finish. That's why Paul says no flesh should glory: nobody gets to point at his own résumé. Redemption was the word for buying a slave out of slavery. If you've felt like a slave to something, that word is for you.

Let him glory in the Lord.

To glory is to boast. A man who boasts about his thirty days is standing on thin ice. But a man who says, “God did this, and I'm still on my knees about it,” is standing on something solid. Paul asks the Corinthians the same thing in chapter 4:

“For who maketh thee to differ from another? and what hast thou that thou didst not receive? now if thou didst receive it, why dost thou glory, as if thou hadst not received it?”

1 Corinthians 4:7

Today: pick one person you've been measuring yourself against. Pray for him by name. Then thank God for one thing you've been given that you didn't earn.

Points to remember

  1. Grace first“God is faithful” 1:9God gave first, and promised to keep them to the end. He started with grace, not a lecture.
  2. Divisions“Is Christ divided?” 1:13Teams and rivalry have no place in the family of the One who died for all.
  3. The cross“Unto us which are saved it is the power of God” 1:18Foolishness to a man who has it all handled. Power to a man who has run out.
  4. The world's wisdom“The world by wisdom knew not God” 1:21Cleverness never found God. He came looking for us.
  5. Who God chooses“God hath chosen the foolish things of the world” 1:27He picks the ones everybody wrote off. That includes you.
  6. Boasting“He that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord” 1:31Christ is our wisdom, righteousness, sanctification and redemption. Boast in Him.

Question 1 of 6

Grace first

What had God already done for the Corinthians before Paul said a word about their problems?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He had given them grace, enriched them with every gift, proven the message of Christ in them, and promised to keep them to the end. Paul calls them saints before he says anything about their sin. God gives first. He is faithful, even when we are not.

Question 2 of 6

Divisions

What was splitting the church, and what did Paul ask them to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They were lining up behind men: Paul, Apollos, Cephas, even “Christ” as a badge. Paul asks them to speak the same thing, drop the teams, and be joined together in one mind. Only Christ was crucified for them.

Question 3 of 6

The cross

Why does the message of the cross sound foolish to some, and what is it to those who are saved?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A God who dies on a cross doesn't fit what proud men expect, so it sounds foolish to them. But to those who are saved, it is the power of God and the wisdom of God. Even God's “weakness” is stronger than the best plan of man.

Question 4 of 6

The world's wisdom

Why did the wisdom of the world never find God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because God is not found by cleverness. He chose to save by what looks foolish, the preaching of the cross, so that no one can say he figured it out and saved himself. The way in is open to every man who believes.

Question 5 of 6

Who God chooses

Who does God choose, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not many wise, mighty or noble, but the foolish, the weak, the base and the despised: the ones the world wrote off. God does it so that no man can boast. If you feel like a nobody, you are exactly the kind of man God picks.

Question 6 of 6

Boasting

Who has Christ become for us, and who should we boast in?

What verses tell us about this?

“For who maketh thee to differ from another? and what hast thou that thou didst not receive? now if thou didst receive it, why dost thou glory, as if thou hadst not received it?”

1 Corinthians 4:7

Answer

He has become our wisdom, our righteousness, our sanctification and our redemption: everything we need. So we boast in the Lord, not in ourselves. Everything good in us was a gift.
