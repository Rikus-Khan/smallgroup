Genesis 28

A stone for a pillow, and a ladder to heaven

Jacob leaves home with his father's blessing and his brother's anger behind him. Alone on the road, with nothing, he lies down on the ground. That is where God meets him.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

Sent away

Verses 1–9

Isaac sends Jacob off with a blessing, and Esau tries to make things right.

The blessing, given on purpose

In chapter 27 Isaac blessed Jacob without knowing who he was. Now he knows, and he blesses him again with his eyes open. He calls him, charges him, and says it all over again: the blessing of Abraham.

Chapter 27

Got by a trick

Jacob wore his brother's clothes and lied to a blind old man. It cost him his home.

Chapter 28

Given freely

Isaac sends him with the same blessing, and God Almighty (El Shaddai, as in chapter 17) is the one who will carry it out.

Jacob sinned to get what God had already promised (25:23). The sin did its damage. But God does not take the promise back from a man who grabbed at it the wrong way. He finishes what he said.

Five hundred miles on foot

Jacob leaves Beer-sheba in the far south and heads for Haran, where his mother's brother Laban lives. By the old roads it is five hundred miles or more: weeks of walking, alone.

Notice what is missing. No servants, no camels, no gifts for a bride (compare Isaac's servant in chapter 24). He leaves with a staff, and a name he has not yet earned.

Rebekah told him to go “a few days” until Esau cooled down. It would be twenty years.

What Esau did about it

Esau saw that his father did not like the Canaanite wives he had. So he went and added one more wife: Mahalath, a daughter of Ishmael, Abraham's other son.

What he got right

He wanted to please his father

Mahalath was from Abraham's own family. It was a real attempt to fix something.

What he missed

He added, he didn't turn

He kept the wives that grieved his parents, and put another beside them. The new wife was a patch on the old trouble.

Plenty of us have tried this: do one good thing to cover the bad thing, and hope it adds up. Esau's story is a warning about regret that never turns into a change of direction. Real repair means turning, not just adding.

Part two

The dream

Verses 10–15

The lowest night of Jacob's life becomes the night God speaks to him.

Old words in verses 10–22

  1. lighted uponcame upon, happened to reach
  2. tarriedstayed
  3. pillowssomething to rest his head on (here, stones)
  4. dreadfulawesome, full of awe and fear
  5. pillara standing stone, set up as a marker
  6. raimentclothing

“he took of the stones of that place”: it was a rocky hillside with nothing soft on it. A stone under his head, the sky over him.

Where God found him

  1. liedto his father
  2. ranfrom his brother
  3. lefthis mother behind (Genesis never records them meeting again)
  4. lay downalone, on the ground, with a stone for a pillow

Genesis doesn't tell us what Jacob was thinking that night. A man who has made a mess of things, with the sun gone down and nobody around, has plenty to think about.

Here is the surprise. God did not wait for Jacob to clean himself up and start praying. He came to him in the dark, in the middle of the run. Before Jacob said a word, God spoke.

The ladder

סֻלָּם said sullam a stairway or ramp (the word is found only here)

Not a ladder a man climbs to reach God. Look at what is moving on it: angels going up and angels coming down, and “the LORD stood above it.” Heaven comes down to meet the man on the ground.

Jesus said he is the ladder

Nathanael was a man the Lord called “an Israelite indeed, in whom is no guile.” Guile means deceit. Jacob, who became Israel, had plenty. Then Jesus told Nathanael:

“Jesus saw Nathanael coming to him, and saith of him, Behold an Israelite indeed, in whom is no guile!”

John 1:47

“And he saith unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Hereafter ye shall see heaven open, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man.”

John 1:51

Jesus took Jacob's dream and put his own name on it: the place where heaven and earth meet is the Son of man. Not a ladder we build. A person God sent down.

“For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;”

1 Timothy 2:5

Every promise, from the LORD above the ladder

  1. The land“to thee will I give it, and to thy seed” (13)
  2. Many children“as the dust of the earth” (14)
  3. A blessing for all“in thee and in thy seed shall all the families of the earth be blessed” (14)
  4. His presence“I am with thee” (15)
  5. His keeping“will keep thee in all places whither thou goest” (15)
  6. His return“will bring thee again into this land” (15)

Almost every line is God saying I: I will give, I am with thee, I will keep, I will bring. Nothing in these promises depends on Jacob getting better first, and not one of them says “if”.

“Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.”

Galatians 3:16

“All the families of the earth” reach as far as you. The One in Jacob's seed who blesses them all is Christ.

Part three

The house of God

Verses 16–22

Jacob wakes up afraid, builds a marker, and makes a promise of his own.

“Surely the LORD is in this place; and I knew it not.”

God had been in that place all along. Jacob had walked into the middle of nowhere, and God was already there. The only thing that changed was that Jacob's eyes were opened.

“Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence? If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there.”

Psalm 139:7-8

Think of the places you have gone where you were sure God wasn't: the bar, the motel room, the bad night. He was there, and he was calling. Think of today, too: the job site, the truck, the kitchen table. The LORD is in this place, and today you know it.

From Luz to Bethel

Luz → Bethel Beth-el means “house of God.” Luz was the name of the town nearby. After that night the place had a new name.

He set up the stone he had slept on and poured oil on it. That was an old way of marking a place and setting it apart for God. The stone was a reminder, not a god. Jacob was not worshipping a rock; he was saying: something happened here.

Have a Bethel of your own. Where were you when God first got hold of you? Remember it. When the old shame says nothing ever changed, point to the stone.

Jacob's bargain

Jacob says “If”God will be with me, keep me, give me bread and clothes, bring me home safe
→
Thenthe LORD will be my God, and I will give a tenth

Be honest about it: that is a young believer's prayer. God had just said I am with thee, and Jacob answers if. He was still trying to set terms.

And God took it. He didn't scold him. Notice that every “if” Jacob asked for was something God had already promised. God knew exactly what kind of man he was talking to, and he kept every promise anyway. He is patient with beginners.

“And blessed be the most high God, which hath delivered thine enemies into thy hand. And he gave him tithes of all.”

Genesis 14:20

Jacob's grandfather Abraham had already given a tenth. Jacob promises the same: God gets the first share, and he'll start there.

What to do with Genesis 28

  1. 1I am with theeIf you are running from something right now, you are not running from him. Stop and tell him where you are. He knows already.28:15
  2. 2the angels of God ascending and descendingHeaven came down to Jacob; he didn't climb up. Say it out loud: Jesus is the way to the Father, and I do not have to earn my way up.28:12
  3. 3I knew it notLook at the day you have ahead. Where is the place you assumed God wasn't? Ask him to open your eyes there.28:16
  4. 4this is none other but the house of GodSet up a stone: write down the day God got hold of you, and keep it where you will see it on a bad day.28:17
  5. 5of all that thou shalt give me I will surely give the tenthGive God the first part of what he gives you, and don't wait until you feel you've earned the right.28:22

Question 1 of 5

Grace

Did God meet Jacob while he was running away?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Yes. Jacob had lied to his father and was running from his brother, and God came to him that very night. He did not wait for Jacob to clean up. If you have fallen or you are on the run, God is not waiting for you to get your act together before he speaks. Call your brothers in Christ, and your pastor, and come back into the light.

Question 2 of 5

The promise

What did God promise Jacob at Bethel?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The land, a great family, a blessing for every family on earth, and his own presence: “I am with thee”, with his keeping and a way home. It was all I will. None of it depended on Jacob being good first.

Question 3 of 5

The ladder

What does Jacob's ladder point to?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he saith unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Hereafter ye shall see heaven open, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man.”

John 1:51

“For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;”

1 Timothy 2:5

Answer

To Jesus. Jesus himself said heaven would be opened over the Son of man. The ladder is God coming down to us, not us climbing up to him. There is one mediator between God and men, and he is the way to the Father.

Question 4 of 5

His presence

Can a man be somewhere God is not?

What verses tell us about this?

“Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence? If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there.”

Psalm 139:7-8

Answer

No. Jacob thought he was out in the middle of nowhere, and the LORD was there. There is nowhere you can go that he is not. For a believer that is a comfort and not a threat: he goes with you into the day, and he is keeping you.

Question 5 of 5

The vow

What did Jacob promise God, and what does it show?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That if God kept him, fed him, clothed him and brought him home, the LORD would be his God, and he would give a tenth. It shows a young believer with a bargaining prayer, still trying to set terms. God took it anyway, and kept every promise. He is patient with beginners, so don't stop praying because your prayers sound small.
