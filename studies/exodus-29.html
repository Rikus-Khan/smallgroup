Exodus 29

Set apart by blood, and God comes to live among them

Aaron and his sons are washed, dressed, anointed and covered with blood. Two lambs go on the altar every day. And the LORD says what the whole tabernacle is for: “I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God.”

Part one

“To hallow them”

Verses 1–9

Washed, dressed and anointed: how a man is made a priest.

What was brought

  • bullocka young bull
  • ramstwo male sheep, “without blemish”
  • unleavenedmade without yeast, plain and fresh
  • temperedmixed (with oil)
  • wafersthin flat bread
  • hallowmake holy, set apart for God

Everything is the best and the cleanest: a young bull, two rams with no defect, bread made of wheat flour. God's servants are not set apart with leftovers.

“Without blemish” points past the animals. The One who would finally stand for us is called a “lamb without blemish and without spot” (1 Peter 1:19).

Washed, dressed, anointed

  1. washedwith water“thou shalt wash them”
  2. dressedin the holy clothescoat, robe, ephod, breastplate, mitre
  3. anointedoil on his head“pour it upon his head”
  4. sacrificethree offeringsthe rest of the chapter

Notice who does what. Aaron does not wash himself, dress himself, or anoint himself. Moses does it all. The priest is made a priest by what is done to him.

Anoint means to pour oil on a person to set him apart. The Hebrew word for “anointed one” is Messiah, and the Greek is Christ. When we call Jesus “Christ,” we call Him the Anointed One.

He did it the same way for you. You did not wash yourself clean or dress yourself in righteousness. It was done to you, by Him.

Part two

Three sacrifices

Verses 10–28

A sin offering, a burnt offering, and a ram of consecration: sin, surrender and belonging.

Hands on the head

Aaron and his sonsguilty, and priests→the bullock“without blemish”
their sinleaned on its head→its bloodon the horns of the altar

Before a priest could serve other sinners, he was first made right himself. A man cannot carry others' sins into God's presence until his own are dealt with. The hands on the head say: this one stands in my place.

The fat was burned on the altar. The rest of the bull, with its skin, was burned “without the camp: it is a sin offering.” Outside, away from the tents.

“For the bodies of those beasts, whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin, are burned without the camp. Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate.”

Hebrews 13:11–12

Jesus “suffered without the gate.” The sin offering was taken outside the camp, and so was He. He was driven out, so that we could be brought in.

All of it to God

The bullock

A sin offering

My sin needs to be dealt with.

The first ram

A burnt offering

The whole animal goes up. Everything is given to God.

Savour means smell. A “sweet savour” is a pleasing smell, God being pleased with the gift. First the sin is dealt with, then the man gives himself, all of him, to God.

“And walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour.”

Ephesians 5:2

The order matters, and you can see it in the chapter: first the sin, then the surrender. We do not give ourselves to God to earn the cleansing. Christ's gift came first, and our giving follows it (Romans 12:1).

Blood on the ear, thumb and toe

right earwhat he hears
right thumbwhat he does
right great toewhere he walks

The text puts the blood on the tip of the right ear, the thumb of the right hand, and the big toe of the right foot, and sprinkles the rest on the altar, then on the men and their clothes with the anointing oil. It does not explain each place. The plain sense is the whole man: hearing, doing, and walking, all under the blood.

“And he shall be hallowed.” The blood came first, before any service. Hearing God, working for God, and walking with God all start there.

For a man in recovery, the three are real. What do you listen to? What do your hands do? Where do your feet go? You don't clean these up first and then come to God. You come, and the blood covers them, and then He starts to change them.

Wave and heave

Wave offering

The breast

Lifted and moved toward the altar, held out before the LORD, and and this time it was Moses' part: “it shall be thy part” (verse 26). After this, the wave breast and heave shoulder belong to Aaron and his sons “by a statute for ever” (verses 27–28).

Heave offering

The shoulder

Lifted up high before God, and then it belongs to Aaron and his sons “by a statute for ever.”

Other words: caul is the fatty covering over the liver, the rump is the fat tail of the sheep, and the inwards are the inner parts.

Look at what happens. The portion is first given to God, held up to Him, and God gives it back to the priests for their food. God provides for the men who serve Him. Nobody goes hungry in His house.

Part three

“Seven days shalt thou consecrate them”

Verses 29–37

The meal, the leftovers, and a week of sacrifice, day after day.

A meal at the door

After the sacrifice came a meal. Seethe means boil, and ought in verse 34 means anything. Aaron and his sons ate the meat of the ram and the bread from the basket, at the door of the tabernacle, in front of God.

  • “those things wherewith the atonement was made”they ate what was given for them
  • “a stranger shall not eat”for the priests only: “because they are holy”
  • nothing left overwhat remained until the morning was burned

They ate from the sacrifice made for them. At the Last Supper Jesus took the bread and the cup and said they were His body and His blood (Matthew 26:26–28), and those who follow Him still eat and drink in remembrance of what He did.

Nothing left till morning: what is holy is not kept for another day, as if it were ordinary. Each day gets its own bread (Matthew 6:11).

Seven days, seven bullocks

one dot for each day of the consecration

Every one of the seven days, a bullock was killed “for atonement,” and the altar itself was cleansed and anointed, till it was “most holy.” The priests did not get through in one afternoon. It took a week of blood.

“And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins: But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God; From henceforth expecting till his enemies be made his footstool. For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.”

Hebrews 10:11–14

That is the difference. Aaron's priests stood “daily,” and the sacrifices could never take away sins. Jesus offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, and then sat down. There was nothing left to add.

Part four

“I will dwell among the children of Israel”

Verses 38–46

Two lambs a day, and the reason for it all: God coming to live with His people.

Every morning, every evening

  1. morningone lambflour, oil and wine
  2. eveningthe other lambthe same offering
  3. next morningand again“day by day continually”

First year means a lamb a year old, and at even means in the evening. Deal is a part: a tenth deal of flour is a tenth of the standard measure, about two quarts. A hin is a liquid measure of about a gallon, so a fourth part is about a quart, for the oil and the wine poured out.

Nothing was done once and forgotten. The day began with an offering to God, and it ended with one. The worship rose before the work and again before the dark.

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

The daily lamb was always in front of the people. When John pointed at Jesus and said, “Behold the Lamb of God,” every man who knew the daily offering knew what he meant.

Real world: begin and end with God

A daily lamb for the whole nation, morning and evening. You can do something small and daily too. It is not a sacrifice for sins, because that is finished. It is a way to keep the day tied to Him.

  • MorningBefore the phone and the news. A verse, a prayer, say it out loud: “Today, Lord, I am Yours.”
  • EveningThank Him for the day. Confess what went wrong, and ask Him for tomorrow.
  • If you missed oneThe lamb did not wait for a perfect man. You can start again at the next morning or the next evening.

Text a brother the first thing you read, or tell him you missed. Two men doing it together do it longer than one.

“Where I will meet you”

The altar and the daily lamb stand at the door, because the meeting happens there.

Why He brought them out

Read verse 46 again: “that brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them.”

Out of Egypt

From slavery

The plagues, the Passover, the sea. God broke the chains.

To dwell with them

Into His presence

That was the goal. Not freedom alone, but a God who lives with His people.

Freedom was not the end. God did not rescue them from Egypt to leave them alone in the desert. He rescued them so He could live among them. He has done the same for you: He brought you out so that He could be near.

He dwelt among us, and He dwells in you

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

John 1:14

“Dwelt” means “pitched his tent,” the same picture. The tent in the wilderness was the first step, and Jesus was the next.

“What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own?”

1 Corinthians 6:19

And now God does not live in a tent, but in every believer. The Holy Spirit lives in you from the day you trusted Christ.

The end of the story

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.”

Revelation 21:3

And the end of the story is the same promise, made perfect: “he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people.”

The tabernacle was a start. The whole Bible is the story of God coming to live with the people He made.

Real world: you are not alone today

Every one of us has had the feeling that God is far off, and the loneliest hour is the one before a fall.

  • He lives with youThis is God's own stated purpose: “that I may dwell among them.”
  • He lives in youIf you have trusted Christ, the Spirit is in you now, on the worst day too.
  • Say itWhen temptation comes, say out loud: “God is with me. I am not alone.” Then call a brother.

“I am the LORD their God.” He said it at the start and at the end of the promise. It does not depend on how today goes.

Points to remember

  1. The washing“thou shalt bring unto the door of the tabernacle… and shalt wash them with water” 29:4The priests were washed, dressed and anointed by someone else. So were we.
  2. The hands“Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the bullock” 29:10The animal stood in their place. Jesus stood in ours.
  3. The blood“put it upon the tip of the right ear… the thumb of their right hand, and… the great toe of their right foot” 29:20The whole man under the blood: hearing, doing and walking.
  4. Seven days“seven days shalt thou consecrate them” 29:35A week of blood for the priests. Jesus did it once and sat down.
  5. Every day“two lambs of the first year day by day continually” 29:38One in the morning and one at evening. Begin and end the day with God.
  6. The dwelling“I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God” 29:45He brought them out so that He could live with them. He brought you out for the same reason.

Question 1 of 6

The washing

What was done to Aaron and his sons at the door of the tabernacle before they were dressed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They were brought to the door and washed with water, then dressed in the holy garments, and Aaron was anointed with oil. All of it was done to them, not by them.

Question 2 of 6

The hands

What did Aaron and his sons do to the bullock and the rams before they were killed?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Answer

They laid their hands on its head: the animal stood in their place. “he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

Question 3 of 6

The blood

Where was the blood of the ram of consecration put on Aaron and his sons?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On the tip of the right ear, the thumb of the right hand and the great toe of the right foot, then sprinkled on the men and their clothes. The whole man, hearing, doing and walking, is covered by the blood.

Question 4 of 6

Seven days

How long were Aaron and his sons consecrated, and what was offered every day?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Seven days, with a bullock for a sin offering for atonement every day. Aaron's priests stood “daily.” Jesus offered one sacrifice for sins for ever and sat down.

Question 5 of 6

Every day

What was offered on the altar every day, and when?

What verses tell us about this?

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

Answer

Two lambs of the first year, one in the morning and one at evening, with flour, oil and wine, every day. John called Jesus “the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

Question 6 of 6

The dwelling

What did the LORD say He would do among the children of Israel, and why did He bring them out of Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

John 1:14

Answer

“I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God.” He brought them out of Egypt for that: “that I may dwell among them.” In Jesus He came and lived among us, and now He lives in every believer.
