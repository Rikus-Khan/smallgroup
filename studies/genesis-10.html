Genesis 10

The table of nations

About seventy names, and every people on earth traced back to one family that came off one boat. God knows every one of them by name.

One family, three branches

  • Noah
    • Japhethverses 2–5 · 14 names
    • Hamverses 6–20 · 30 names
    • Shemverses 21–31 · 26 names

Genesis 10 is a family tree of the whole world. It tells Japheth's family first and saves Shem for last, because Shem's line is the one the rest of the Bible will follow.

Count the families and you get seventy: fourteen from Japheth, thirty from Ham, twenty-six from Shem. Many of these names became the names of nations you'll meet all through the Bible.

Isles means the coastlands and islands across the sea. Gentiles simply means the nations, and a tongue is a language. Most think Madai became the Medes, Javan the Greeks, and Kittim the people of Cyprus.

Noah had said, “God shall enlarge Japheth” (9:27). Here it starts: his families spread the farthest. Centuries later the gospel would go out to these same “Gentiles”, the nations, and reach them.

Mizraim is the Hebrew name for Egypt to this day. Cush is the land up the Nile beyond Egypt, which the KJV often calls Ethiopia. Phut is most often placed in Africa too, west of Egypt.

Every one of these was a real nation that Israel would one day deal with: Egypt, which would enslave them; Canaan, whose land they would be given; and the Philistines, Goliath's people (v. 14).

Nimrod

Begat means became the father of. Nimrod is the first man the Bible calls “mighty,” and the first with a kingdom. He was famous enough to become a saying: “Even as Nimrod.”

“Before the LORD” can be read two ways. It may mean in the LORD's sight: God saw it all. Many, from old Jewish teachers on, read it as in the LORD's face, in defiance. Either way, look where his kingdom began: Babel, the city where men will soon say, “let us make us a name” (11:4).

Nimrod built his name by strength: hunting, fighting, ruling. Plenty of men still try it that way, on the street or at work. The Bible will show us a different kind of mighty man: one who knows he's weak and leans on God.

Babel and Nineveh

Shinar is the flat land between the rivers, later called Babylonia. Builded is built. Verse 11 can be read Asshur went out, or he (Nimrod) went out into Asshur; either way, the cities of the north rose too.

Babel became Babylon. Nineveh became the capital of Assyria. Those two cities would one day carry God's people away into exile.

But even Nineveh wasn't beyond mercy. When Jonah preached there, it turned:

“And God saw their works, that they turned from their evil way; and God repented of the evil, that he had said that he would do unto them; and he did it not.”

Jonah 3:10

The land of Canaan

“As thou comest” means as you go toward. Canaan's families filled the land from Sidon on the coast down to Gaza, and east to the cities by the Salt Sea.

Remember those names: the Jebusite (Jerusalem's people before David), the Amorite, the Hivite. This is the very land God will promise to Abraham in chapter 12.

God didn't hand it over right away. He waited about four hundred years, giving these peoples time to turn:

“But in the fourth generation they shall come hither again: for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.”

Genesis 15:16

Of all Shem's descendants, verse 21 names just one: Eber. Many think the word Hebrew comes from his name; Abram is the first man called “the Hebrew” (14:13). The chapter is already pointing ahead to Israel.

Peleg: “division”

Peleg means division. Most Bible teachers connect his name with the next chapter, when God divided men by their languages at Babel and scattered them over the earth:

“Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the LORD scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth.”

Genesis 11:9

Joktan's thirteen sons settled in Arabia; Sheba and Ophir later became famous for gold and spices. But the Bible follows Peleg. From him come Abram, Israel, and in the end Jesus. Luke traces Jesus' family right back through these names:

“Which was the son of Saruch, which was the son of Ragau, which was the son of Phalec, which was the son of Heber, which was the son of Sala,”

Luke 3:35 (Phalec is Peleg, Heber is Eber, Sala is Salah)

Where they went

From the mountains where the ark came to rest, Noah's grandsons spread out, “every one after his tongue, after their families.” These are the usual places Bible maps give them:

Japheth went north and west, to Asia Minor and the islands, and east to the Medes.

Ham went south: Canaan, Egypt, and up the Nile to Cush.

Shem stayed in the middle: Assyria, Elam, Aram, and Arabia.

Of one blood

Every nation, every color, every language on earth comes from one family. Nobody's people are better by birth. Paul said the same to the proud Greeks of Athens:

“And hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth, and hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation; That they should seek the Lord, if haply they might feel after him, and find him, though he be not far from every one of us:”

Acts 17:26–27

God put each nation where it is so that men would seek the Lord. And He means to bring men back from every one of them:

“After this I beheld, and, lo, a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues, stood before the throne, and before the Lamb, clothed with white robes, and palms in their hands;”

Revelation 7:9

Why a chapter of names?

one dot for every family named

Most of these names mean nothing to us. They meant something to God. He kept track of every family on earth, and He still knows every man by name, the forgotten ones most of all.

“To him the porter openeth; and the sheep hear his voice: and he calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out.”

John 10:3

Nobody's name is lost in the crowd with Him. Not the man with the record, not the man whose own family stopped calling. He calls His own sheep by name.

Question 1 of 4

One family

Where did all the nations of the world come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“And hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth, and hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation;”

Acts 17:26

Answer

From Noah's three sons after the flood. Every people on earth is one family, made of one blood. No man is better than another by birth, and God placed every nation where it is so that men would seek Him.

Question 2 of 4

A mighty one

What kind of man was Nimrod, and what did he build?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A powerful hunter and the first kingdom builder, famous for his strength. His kingdom started at Babel, and the cities of the north like Nineveh rose from the same land. Those cities became Babylon and Assyria, great empires that later fought against God's people. Yet even Nineveh found mercy when it repented.

Question 3 of 4

The line to Jesus

Which family does the Bible follow from here, and where does it lead?

What verses tell us about this?

“Which was the son of Saruch, which was the son of Ragau, which was the son of Phalec, which was the son of Heber, which was the son of Sala,”

Luke 3:35

Answer

Shem's, through Eber and Peleg. From Peleg the line runs to Abram in the next chapter, then to Israel, to David, and to Jesus. God was keeping His promise to bless the world through Shem's family, one generation at a time.

Question 4 of 4

Every name

Why would God fill a whole chapter with names?

What verses tell us about this?

“To him the porter openeth; and the sheep hear his voice: and he calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out.”

John 10:3

“After this I beheld, and, lo, a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues, stood before the throne, and before the Lamb, clothed with white robes, and palms in their hands;”

Revelation 7:9

Answer

Because every family matters to Him. These names are real people and real nations, and God kept track of them all. He still knows every man by name, and He means to have people from every one of these nations standing before His throne.
