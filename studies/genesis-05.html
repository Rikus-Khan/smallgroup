Genesis 5

And he died

Ten generations from Adam to Noah. The same sad line closes one life after another, until one man walks with God and the line breaks.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

The book of the generations of Adam

Verses 1–5

Made in the likeness of God. Then a son in the likeness of Adam.

Two likenesses

Verse 1

Adam, made by God

“In the likeness of God made he him.” Made to know God, to reflect Him, and to rule the earth under Him.

Verse 3

Seth, born to Adam

“In his own likeness, after his image.” Still a man made in God's image (9:6), but born to a fallen father, with a fallen nature, and under death.

Every man since has been born in Adam's likeness. That is why the sin you fight didn't start with you, and why you can't beat it alone.

“For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive.”

1 Corinthians 15:22

Old words: begat = became the father of; begotten = fathered. An hundred = a hundred.

Part two

And he died

Verses 6–20

The same three lines, again and again. Listen for the rhythm.

Two families

Chapter 4 traced Cain's family. Chapter 5 traces Seth's. Both had sons, both lived long, both built a life. The difference was Who they called on.

Cain's line (4:17–24)

Making a name

A city named after a son. Herds, harps, bronze and iron. And Lamech, bragging to his wives that he had killed a young man.

Seth's line (4:25–26)

Calling on His name

Seth was God's gift “instead of Abel.” When his son Enos was born, something began:

“And to Seth, to him also there was born a son; and he called his name Enos: then began men to call upon the name of the LORD.”

Genesis 4:26

A life in three lines

  1. He lived“And Cainan lived seventy years…”
  2. He begat“…and begat sons and daughters”
  3. He died“…and he died”

Hundreds of years each, and God's book sums them up in three lines. No riches listed, no fights won, no regrets explained. Only: he lived, he had children, he died.

That isn't cold. It's honest. Death came into the world through sin, just as God said it would (2:17), and it came for every one of these men. One day the line will be written about each of us. The question is what comes before it.

“So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom.”

Psalm 90:12

Part three

Enoch walked with God

Verses 21–24

Listen for the word that changes, and the line that is missing.

Walked, not just lived

Verse 21→“Enoch lived sixty and five years”
Verse 22→“Enoch walked with God … three hundred years”Every other man in the list “lived.” Twice the Bible says Enoch “walked with God.”

The text marks the time from the birth of his son. Whatever came before, from there on Enoch walked with God, and kept walking for three hundred years.

Walking is plain and daily: one step, then the next, going the same way as the One beside you, talking as you go. Enoch did it while raising sons and daughters in a world going bad around him.

“Can two walk together, except they be agreed?”

Amos 3:3

The line that's missing

Every man so far ends with “and he died.” Enoch's verse doesn't. He was not means he was no longer there, because God took him.

“By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death; and was not found, because God had translated him: for before his translation he had this testimony, that he pleased God. But without faith it is impossible to please him: for he that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him.”

Hebrews 11:5–6

Translated = carried across. Enoch went from walking with God on earth to being with God, without dying. In the middle of a chapter full of graves, God shows that death does not get the last word.

“Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live: And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die. Believest thou this?”

John 11:25–26

Part four

This same shall comfort us

Verses 25–32

The oldest man who ever lived, and a tired father who names his son for hope.

What the numbers hide

Add up the ages and Methuselah, the oldest man in the Bible, dies in the year the flood comes. His father Enoch walked with God; Methuselah lived nearly a thousand years in a world getting worse, and the flood came only in the year his life ran out. God is patient a long time before He judges.

And Adam was still alive when Lamech, Noah's father, was born. These men could hear about Eden from the man who had been there.

Noah: rest

נֹחַ said Noach “rest”; it sounds like the Hebrew for “comfort”

Lamech was worn out. Hard work on hard ground, cursed since Adam's sin, just as God had said: “cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it” (3:17). He named his boy for the rest he longed for.

God did bring rest through Noah: a family carried through the judgment. But the rest a tired man needs most is the one Jesus offers:

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Eight times: “and he died.” That is what sin brought into the world, and no man in this list outlived it by living longer.

“Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:”

Romans 5:12

But one stone stands open. Enoch is God's early word that a man who walks with Him belongs to Him, in this life and after it. In Jesus that open road is for every man who believes.

Why a list of names?

Most of these men we know only by name and age. Nobody remembers what Cainan did, or what Mahalaleel was afraid of. God wrote their names down anyway.

God doesn't save “people” in general. He knows men one by one: who your father was, how long you've lived, what you've done, and what you've lost. Your life isn't a number to Him.

“But now thus saith the LORD that created thee, O Jacob, and he that formed thee, O Israel, Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.”

Luke 10:20

Points to remember

  1. Death“And he died” 5:5Sin brought death to every son of Adam. Long lives still ended.
  2. Walking with God“Enoch walked with God” 5:24Not a feeling, a daily walk: same direction, step by step, for life.
  3. Hope“This same shall comfort us” 5:29A tired man hoped for rest. Jesus gives it.
  4. Names“I have called thee by thy name” Isaiah 43:1God knows every man by name, and writes it down.

Question 1 of 4

Death

Why does almost every man in this chapter end with “and he died”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:”

Romans 5:12

“For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive.”

1 Corinthians 15:22

Answer

Because God told Adam the truth: sin brings death. Each man was born in Adam's likeness, and even nine hundred years couldn't outrun it. But the same Bible that says “in Adam all die” says “in Christ shall all be made alive.” The line doesn't have to end your story.

Question 2 of 4

Walking with God

What does it mean that Enoch “walked with God”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Can two walk together, except they be agreed?”

Amos 3:3

“By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death; and was not found, because God had translated him: for before his translation he had this testimony, that he pleased God.”

Hebrews 11:5

Answer

It means going God's way, beside Him, every day, by faith. Enoch did it for three hundred years with a family to raise and a world going bad around him. You don't have to walk perfectly to walk with God. You take today's steps with Him, and if you stumble, you get up and keep walking beside Him.

Question 3 of 4

Hope

What hope did Lamech hang on his son Noah?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

Rest and comfort from the hard, cursed ground. Lamech didn't have all the answers, but he trusted God to send relief, and God did save the world's future through Noah. The deeper rest he longed for comes in Jesus. If you're worn out today, that invitation is for you.

Question 4 of 4

Names

Why would God fill a chapter of His book with names?

What verses tell us about this?

“But now thus saith the LORD that created thee, O Jacob, and he that formed thee, O Israel, Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.”

Isaiah 43:1

“Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.”

Luke 10:20

Answer

Because these were real men, and God keeps track of real men. The list carries His promise from Adam to Noah, and on to Jesus (Luke 3). And it shows that no man is lost in the crowd to God. He knows your name, and in Christ it is written in heaven.
