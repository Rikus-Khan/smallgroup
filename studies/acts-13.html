Part one

The Spirit sends

Verses 1–12

A church on its knees, two men sent, and a sorcerer in a governor's house.

A church from everywhere

Antioch, in Syria, was one of the biggest cities in the Roman world. Its church was the first to take in Jews and Gentiles side by side.

Barnabas came from Cyprus. Simeon was called Niger, Latin for “black”: he may have been African. Lucius was from Cyrene, in North Africa. Manaen was raised alongside Herod the tetrarch. And Saul of Tarsus used to drag believers off to prison.

Nobody would have picked this team. The Spirit did. In Jesus' church the walls are down, and a man's past doesn't disqualify him.

While they were serving

the church worships and faststhenthe Holy Ghost speakssothey fast, pray, lay hands on themandsend them out

“Ministered to the Lord” means they were serving and worshipping Him. They weren't running a program or waiting on a feeling. God spoke to men who were already busy doing the next faithful thing. The Holy Ghost is a Person who speaks and sends, not a force.

“Laid their hands on them” was the church saying: we're with you. Barnabas and Saul were already leaders (verse 1). This was a send-off, not a ceremony that gave them some new power.

You don't need a burning bush to start. What's the next faithful thing in front of you today?

Across the sea to Cyprus

From Antioch they went down to the port of Seleucia and sailed for Cyprus, the home island of Barnabas, landing at Salamis. Then they crossed the island on foot to Paphos, preaching in the synagogues as they went.

Notice “John to their minister.” That's John Mark, Barnabas's relative (Colossians 4:10: “sister's son to Barnabas”), along as their helper. Remember him. We'll see him again in a few verses.

A sorcerer in the governor's house

Bar-jesus → Elymas “son of Jesus,” a very common Jewish name (it means the LORD saves). Elymas is the name he used: “the sorcerer,” as verse 8 says “by interpretation.”

A “deputy” was a Roman governor, and Sergius Paulus was “a prudent man”: sensible, and curious. He sent for Barnabas and Saul because he wanted to hear for himself. The man at his elbow, a sorcerer, wanted to keep it that way: a man who couldn't find the truth himself, and wouldn't let anyone else.

“False prophet” is the right name for anyone who stands between a seeker and Jesus. And there are plenty of them today, in every size and shape.

Two names, one man

Saul → Paul His Hebrew name, and his Roman one. From here on, Luke calls him Paul.

This isn't a new name from God, the way Jesus renamed Simon. Saul had both names all his life, like a man with a Hebrew name at home and an English one at work. Now he is on the road to the Gentile world, and Luke switches to the name they'd use. Some think it's because of the governor's name, Sergius Paulus, but nobody knows.

One more thing to notice: verse 2 said “Barnabas and Saul.” By verse 13 it's “Paul and his company.” Without any announcement, Paul has become the leader. And Barnabas, who brought Saul to Antioch, is fine with that.

Darkness for a season

Acts 9:9Saul, three days blind

Paul knew this darkness from the inside. After the Damascus road he sat three days without sight. Then mercy came.

Acts 13:11Elymas, “for a season”

A hard word, but with a limit on it. “For a season” means this wasn't the end. The door was still open to turn around.

Acts 13:12The governor sees

He “believed, being astonished at the doctrine of the Lord.” The miracle got his attention. It was the teaching that won him.

Paul wasn't showing off. A man was actively working to keep another man from Jesus, and Paul named it for what it was. If you ever dabbled in spells, horoscopes, or spirits, you don't have to be afraid of that world now. Nothing in it can stand against the Lord.

Part two

Perga, and the road up

Verses 13–25

The journey turns north, one man turns around, and Paul gets up to preach.

Perga, and the road up

From Paphos they sailed north to Perga, a coastal city in Pamphylia. This is where John Mark left them and went home to Jerusalem.

Ahead of them, the road climbs through the Taurus mountains to a high plateau, about 3,600 feet above the sea, where Antioch in Pisidia sat. It's a long, hard climb, away from the warm coast and away from home.

John Mark turns back

The Bible doesn't say why he left. Fear, sickness, homesickness, a disagreement: we aren't told. We just know he quit, and Paul remembered it (Acts 15:38).

“Only Luke is with me. Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry.”

2 Timothy 4:11

Years later, in his last letter, Paul asks for him by name. The man who quit became the man Paul wanted at his side. Early church tradition says this same John Mark wrote the Gospel of Mark.

Where did you turn back?

Quitting once is not the same as being finished.

A job, a program, a church, a friend, a marriage, a step of recovery. Everyone here has a Perga. It's a real failure, and it cost something. It is not the last word.

Notice who made the difference. Barnabas, whose name means “son of encouragement,” refused to write Mark off (Acts 15:37). Every man who comes back needs a Barnabas. And every man who has come back can become one.

Today: pick the one thing you walked away from, and take one step back toward it. Make the call, show up, send the text.

Inside a synagogue on the sabbath

  1. Prayersand the great “Hear, O Israel”
  2. The Lawa reading from Moses

Two strangers walked in, and the leaders gave them the floor. Paul had been trained as a Pharisee, so he knew exactly how this service worked, and he'd been waiting for this chance.

Starting with a story they loved

  1. EgyptGod chose our fathersv. 17
  2. 40 yearsin the wildernessv. 18
  3. Canaanthe land divided by lotv. 19
  4. about 450 yearsof judgesv. 20
  5. 40 yearsKing Saulv. 21

Paul tells five hundred years of history in a few lines, because every man in the room already knew the story. He doesn't open with “you're sinners.” He opens with God chose us, God carried us, God was patient with us. Then he walks them toward Jesus.

Notice who does everything in this story: God. “Chose.” “Exalted.” “Brought he them out.” “Suffered” (put up with) “their manners.” That's grace long before anyone used the word.

A man after His own heart

the mana king who fell hard (2 Samuel 11–12)
→
God's word on him“a man after mine own heart”

“But now thy kingdom shall not continue: the LORD hath sought him a man after his own heart, and the LORD hath commanded him to be captain over his people, because thou hast not kept that which the LORD commanded thee.”

1 Samuel 13:14

God said this about David before David ever failed, and David failed badly. But what made him a man after God's heart wasn't that he never fell. It was that when he did, he came back to God, broken and honest.

If you fell yesterday, that's not where your story ends. The question isn't whether you've fallen. It's whether you'll come back.

“A Saviour, Jesus”

יֵשׁוּעַ said Yeshua “the LORD saves”

The sorcerer was “Bar-jesus,” a man with the name but no salvation to give. Here is the real thing: not a son of a Jesus, but the Saviour Jesus Himself, from David's seed, as God promised.

And John the Baptist, the most famous preacher in the country, said he wasn't “worthy to loose” His sandals. The man everyone came out to hear pointed away from himself. That's what a true messenger does.

Part three

Justified

Verses 26–41

Paul's sermon: the best news a guilty man can hear.

They read it, and still missed Him

every sabbaththe leaders heard the prophets read
→
and yetthey fulfilled them by condemning Jesus

“They knew him not.” It's possible to hear the Bible every week and miss Jesus completely. Being near the Word isn't the same as meeting Him.

“They found no cause of death in him.” Even Pilate couldn't find a charge. “The tree” is the cross. They did it in ignorance, and still, “when they had fulfilled all that was written of him,” God's plan went straight through it.

That's the same cross that carried your sin and mine. It was no accident. It was the plan.

“But God”

Two words turn the whole story. Men condemned Him, killed Him, and buried Him. But God raised him from the dead.

“He was seen many days” by the men and women who had walked with Him from Galilee. They became His witnesses, and they staked their lives on what they saw.

This is the proof. If Jesus is alive, then everything He said about sin and forgiveness is true, and the grave doesn't get the last word on anybody, including you.

Three promises kept

  1. ✓Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee.Psalm 2:713:33
  2. ✓I will give you the sure mercies of David.Isaiah 55:313:34
  3. ✓Thou shalt not suffer thine Holy One to see corruption.Psalm 16:1013:35

“Glad tidings” is the word gospel: good news. The promise made to the fathers has been kept, and the resurrection is the Father's public yes: this is My Son. “Sure mercies” means promises God can't fail on. “Corruption” means decay, the grave.

“Incline your ear, and come unto me: hear, and your soul shall live; and I will make an everlasting covenant with you, even the sure mercies of David.”

Isaiah 55:3

Two graves

David

Served, and “saw corruption”

The greatest king Israel ever had. He “served his own generation by the will of God,” then “fell on sleep,” and his body decayed like everyone's.

Jesus

Raised, and “saw no corruption”

God raised Him again, so His body never decayed. David's tomb still holds David. Jesus' tomb is empty.

Every great man you've ever looked up to has died. There's only One who didn't stay dead, and He's the One who can do something about yours.

Justified

“Justified” is a courtroom word. The Judge looks at you and says: not guilty, and right with Me. Not “forgiven a few things.” All things.

“Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:”

Romans 5:1

“All that believe” is anyone who trusts Him. That's the whole requirement.

What the law couldn't do

Through Moses

The law

Shows God's standard, and shows us where we fall short. It could cover some sins with sacrifices, but it could never make a man right with God for all of them.

Through Jesus

Forgiveness, and justified

“Through this man is preached unto you the forgiveness of sins.” Everyone who believes is declared right with God from all things, as a gift.

“For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God; Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus:”

Romans 3:23–24

That's not a license to sin. It's the reason a man who's blown it can stand up again today: his standing with God doesn't depend on how this morning went.

A warning, and a day

Habakkuk 1:5

“Behold ye among the heathen, and regard, and wonder marvellously: for I will work a work in your days, which ye will not believe, though it be told you.”

Acts 13:41

“Behold, ye despisers, and wonder, and perish: for I work a work in your days, a work which ye shall in no wise believe, though a man declare it unto you.”

Paul ends with a warning from Habakkuk. Men heard that God was doing something, and shrugged. “Despisers” are the ones who shrug at good news, and the shrugging is what costs them.

God is working in your days too. Today is a day He is working. Don't let it go by while you wait for a better one.

The whole sermon at a glance

verses 16–25Their storyGod chose, carried and was patient with Israel, and promised a Saviour.
verses 26–37Jesus' storyRejected, killed, buried, and raised, just as Scripture said.
verses 38–41Your moveBelieve, and be justified.

Notice the order: the story they knew, then the story they hadn't heard, then the question. A good sermon doesn't leave a man with only information. It asks him for an answer.

Part four

Turning to the Gentiles

Verses 42–52

Half the town begs for more. The leaders burn with envy.

The week between

  1. First sabbathPaul preachesv. 14–41
  2. The days between“Many” follow Paul and Barnabas, and the Gentiles beg for morev. 42–43
  3. Next sabbath“Almost the whole city”v. 44

Paul's advice to the new believers is short: “continue in the grace of God.” They'd just heard that forgiveness is free. Now keep living in it. Grace isn't only how you get started. It's where you stay.

Pushed away, or taken in

The leaders saw the crowd Paul drew and were “filled with envy.” They'd lost their spot at the center, so they “spake against” the message.

The leaders

“Ye put it from you”

They shoved it away with both hands, and so they “judge yourselves unworthy of everlasting life.” God didn't judge them. They did it themselves.

The Gentiles

“They were glad”

They heard it, and they were glad, and they “glorified the word of the Lord.” Nobody was kept out of everlasting life except by pushing it away.

“It was necessary that the word of God should first have been spoken to you.” Israel came first in God's plan. Paul always starts in the synagogue. But when the door was shut, he went on to everyone else.

To the ends of the earth

Paul quotes Isaiah's promise (49:6): a light for the Gentiles, salvation “unto the end of the earth.” Jesus is that light, and these two men are carrying it. The grey line shows where it goes next: Ephesus, Corinth, Rome. Paul will walk most of it.

“Ordained to eternal life”

This is a verse some people stumble over, so let's take it slowly. “Ordained” means set in place, appointed, lined up. Does it mean God picked certain Gentiles and shut out the rest? Look at what Luke shows us in the same passage:

  • “to you is the word of this salvation sent”v. 26, to everyone in the room
  • “whosoever among you feareth God”v. 26, anyone
  • “all that believe are justified”v. 39, all
  • the Jews “put it from you,” the Gentiles “were glad”v. 46 and 48, each side chose its answer

God planned that the Gentiles would be included (verse 47 says He commanded it), and the Gentiles who heard it were glad and believed. The text doesn't say anyone who wanted Christ was turned away. If you want Him today, that wanting is where it starts. Come.

Run out of town, full of joy

Paul and Barnabasstirred against, expelled from the region
→
the disciples left behind“filled with joy, and with the Holy Ghost”

“Shook off the dust of their feet” was a Jewish way of saying: we're leaving this with God. Jesus told His men to do it when a town refused the message (Matthew 10:14). They didn't sulk or stay to argue. They shook it off and walked the next miles to Iconium.

Now think of the new believers they left behind: a few weeks old, no Paul, no Barnabas, the town against them. And they were “filled with joy.” Joy doesn't depend on a good leader or an easy town. The Holy Ghost lives in every believer from the moment he believes, and that's what keeps a new Christian going when the teacher has gone.

Points to remember

  1. Sent“Separate me Barnabas and Saul for the work whereunto I have called them.” 13:2God sends out men who are already serving Him right where they are.
  2. False power“Then the deputy, when he saw what was done, believed, being astonished at the doctrine of the Lord.” 13:12Nothing false stands up to the Lord, and even His hard word leaves a door open: “for a season.”
  3. Failure“Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry.” 2 Tim. 4:11Quitting once is not the same as being finished. Come back.
  4. Salvation“And by him all that believe are justified from all things.” 13:39Not by keeping the law. By Jesus, through faith: forgiven, and declared right with God.
  5. The resurrection“But God raised him from the dead:” 13:30The empty tomb is the proof. Jesus is alive, so what He says about sin and forgiveness is true.
  6. For everyone“For so hath the Lord commanded us, saying, I have set thee to be a light of the Gentiles, that thou shouldest be for salvation unto the ends of the earth.” 13:47Everyone. No one is too far, too far gone, or too late. The word of this salvation is sent to you.

Question 1 of 6

Sent

What was the church at Antioch doing when the Holy Ghost sent out Barnabas and Saul?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They were serving the Lord and fasting. While they worshipped, the Holy Ghost told them to set Barnabas and Saul apart, and the church fasted, prayed, laid hands on them, and sent them. God calls men who are already serving.

Question 2 of 6

False power

What happened when Elymas the sorcerer tried to turn the deputy away from the faith?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Elymas fought the message, and Paul, filled with the Holy Ghost, told him the hand of the Lord was on him. He was blinded “for a season.” The governor saw it and believed, astonished at the teaching. No counterfeit power can stand against the real thing, and even the judgment had a limit.

Question 3 of 6

Failure

Was John Mark's turning back at Perga the end of his story?

What verses tell us about this?

“Only Luke is with me. Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry.”

2 Timothy 4:11

Answer

No. John Mark left them at Perga and went home. Paul wouldn't take him on the next trip, but Barnabas stood by him. Years later, from a Roman prison, Paul asked for him by name: “he is profitable to me for the ministry.” The man who quit became a man Paul wanted at his side.

Question 4 of 6

Salvation

How does Paul say a man is made right with God?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Through Jesus, by believing. Forgiveness of sins is preached through Him, and everyone who believes is justified, declared right with God, from all the things the law of Moses could never clear. It isn't earned. It's a gift, received by faith.

Question 5 of 6

The resurrection

What proof does Paul give that Jesus is the Saviour?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God raised Him from the dead. Many people saw Him alive over many days, and they became His witnesses. And Scripture said it would happen: David died and his body decayed, but God's Holy One saw no corruption.

Question 6 of 6

For everyone

Who is the good news for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Everyone. “To you is the word of this salvation sent”: Jews, and “whosoever among you feareth God.” God set His light for the Gentiles too, and when they heard it, they were glad and believed.
