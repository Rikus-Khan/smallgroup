1 Peter 2

Living stones and a Shepherd

Once you were “not a people.” Now you are “the people of God.” Peter tells the scattered strangers who they are, how to live among people who watch them, and who is looking after their souls.

Part one

Newborn babes

Verses 1–3

You were just born again. Here is what to put down, and what to pick up.

Five things to lay down

Malice

wanting to hurt someone back, keeping score

Guile

the angle, the lie, working people

Hypocrisies

acting one way here and another way there

Envies

resenting what the other man has

Evil speakings

running people down behind their backs

None of these is the headline sin. They are the ones that live quietly in a man and wreck his friendships, his family and his work. Laying aside is what you do with a coat: you take it off. You do not win a fight with it.

Hungry like a baby

A newborn does not decide to be hungry. He cries for milk until he gets it. “Sincere” means pure, with nothing added. “Of the word”: God's own Word, straight from the page.

“That ye may grow thereby.” Nobody expects a baby to be a grown man by Friday. A year from now you will be taller, by small feedings: a chapter, a prayer, a sentence you carry to work.

And why would a man want it? “If so be ye have tasted that the Lord is gracious.” Once you have tasted His kindness, the other stuff starts to taste like what it is.

Part two

A living stone, a living house

Verses 4–10

Men threw Jesus away like a bad stone. God made Him the stone the whole building rests on. And He builds His house out of people like us.

Built out of people

The corner stone was the first stone laid. Every wall was lined up against it. If it was true, the building was true. Peter says that stone is Jesus.

“Ye also, as lively stones.” You are not a spectator. You are one of the stones, set beside others. A stone on its own is just a rock in a field.

A “holy priesthood” means you can go to God yourself, any hour, with Jesus as the only go-between. “Spiritual sacrifices” are your thanks, your prayers and a life lived for Him.

One stone, two reactions

To those who believe

“He is precious”

The man who puts his weight on Him finds Him solid. He is the foundation, and “he that believeth on him shall not be confounded”: he will not be put to shame.

To those who will not

“A stone of stumbling”

The same stone, in the middle of the road. A man who will not look at it, or will not go round it in humility, trips on it. “Which stumble at the word, being disobedient.”

“The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner.”

Psalm 118:22

Jesus did not change. People either build on Him or fall over Him. Some of us fell over Him for years before we built on Him. Most of the men who stumbled are still invited.

Four names for you

  1. A chosen generationa family of people God picked out, not a club you joined
  2. A royal priesthoodbelonging to the King, and free to come to God for yourself
  3. An holy nationset apart, with citizens from every background, every country and every record
  4. A peculiar peoplean old word for His own possession, the way a man keeps something close that is his

These words were first said to Israel (Exodus 19:5–6). Peter says them to the church, and he says them to Gentiles who never had any of it. And why? “That ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light.” You are chosen so that other men can see what He does.

From nobody to somebody

“not a people”
“had not obtained mercy” → “the people of God”
“have obtained mercy” Two small words are doing all the work: “but now.” Everything before them is the old story. Everything after them is the new one. Peter does not tell them the old story never happened. He says it is behind them.

Mercy, from God's own word

“And I will sow her unto me in the earth; and I will have mercy upon her that had not obtained mercy; and I will say to them which were not my people, Thou art my people; and they shall say, Thou art my God.”

Hosea 2:23

This is for the man who had no one, whose name meant trouble in every house in his town. Mercy is not getting what you had coming. You did not earn it by being good enough. You obtained it. It was given to you.

Part three

Strangers and pilgrims

Verses 11–20

You belong to God, and you still have to go to work, obey the law and put up with unfair bosses. Here is how.

A war inside the man

What the lust says

“One time won't hurt”

It sounds like an appetite you can feed and leave. It does not feel like an enemy. It feels like a friend you have known a long time.

What Peter says it is doing

“War against the soul”

It is not standing still. It is a campaign for the whole man: his family, his money, his freedom, his peace. “Abstain” means hold yourself back from it.

Many Baptists urge believers to leave alcohol alone, and a man who has fought drink knows why. Peter calls us “strangers and pilgrims”: this world's appetites are not our home. If you fell yesterday, you are still a pilgrim. Stand up, tell God, tell one brother, and keep walking.

Let them watch

They say“He's an evildoer. He always was.”
→
They see, over timehonest work, a kept promise, a man who is on time

The neighbours already have a story about you. Peter does not tell you to argue with it. He tells you to outlive it. “Having your conversation honest” means: let your way of life be clean enough that the story stops fitting.

Today: do one thing that someone who doubts you will see. Show up on time. Pay what you owe. Tell the truth when a lie would be easier.

Four short commands

  1. Honour all menthe guy you can't stand, the cop, the clerk. Everybody
  2. Love the brotherhoodthe family of believers, which is closest to home
  3. Fear Godawe, not dread. He gets the top place
  4. Honour the kingrespect the one in charge, even if you did not vote for him

The “king” when Peter wrote was the Roman emperor, and the emperor was Nero, who would soon turn on the Christians. Peter still says honour the office. And look at the order: fear God comes third, with the king after it. When the two ever collide, the apostles themselves said: “We ought to obey God rather than men” (Acts 5:29).

Free men who keep the rules

Free, and not a cloke.

A cloke is a cover. Some people say I'm free in Christ to cover the thing they want to do anyway. Peter says your freedom is not a cover for bad behaviour. You are free so that you can serve God.

For you that may look like the probation officer, the court date, the fine, the child support, the speed limit. A man with a record who keeps the law is a quiet witness.

Two kinds of suffering

Because you did wrong

“Buffeted for your faults”

Peter says there is no glory in that. If you lose a job because you stole, or a licence because you drank, take it and own it. Say that was me. That is honesty, not holiness.

Because you did right

“Ye do well, and suffer for it”

The foreman who punishes you for not joining the theft. The old friends who drop you. Peter says God sees that, and calls it “acceptable with God.”

Peter's “servants” were household slaves, many of them Christians. He is not saying slavery is good. He is telling people who had no way out that they still had dignity, and that Jesus was with them in it. For us the lesson is the boss who is “froward” (crooked, unfair): do your work as for God. And if the work itself is a sin, or the boss is hurting you, that is a different matter. Talk to a brother or your pastor.

Part four

The Shepherd of your souls

Verses 21–25

Jesus suffered wrongly, and He did not answer back. And it was for us.

What Jesus did with an insult

When He was reviledHe did not revile back
→
When He sufferedHe did not threaten

So what did He do? “Committed himself to him that judgeth righteously.” He handed the whole thing to the Father. He did not have to win the argument, because the right Judge was watching.

Next time someone disrespects you in the car park or at work: the old you had a fist or a sentence ready. Jesus left “an example, that ye should follow his steps.” One step: say nothing for ten seconds, and hand it over in your head.

He carried it Himself

  1. Who his own selfnot an angel, not a stand-in: Jesus Himself
  2. Bare our sinsyours and mine, in His own body, up on the tree: the cross
  3. Dead to sins, alive to righteousnessthe sins are dealt with, so we can walk away from them
  4. By whose stripes ye were healedthe whip marks of Jesus. The healing of the soul

“But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.”

Isaiah 53:5

Whatever you did, it was already carried. You do not have to carry it round for the rest of your life as if it were still on you. Hand it over and live.

Home to the Shepherd

Sheep wander because they follow the next patch of grass. That was you: “as sheep going astray.”

“But are now returned.” The Shepherd came after you. Bishop here means overseer: the one who watches over the flock and does not let go.

“I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.”

John 10:11

If you wandered off this month, you are still His sheep. Come back today.

Points to remember

  1. The word“As newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that ye may grow thereby” 2:2Feed on God's Word like a hungry baby, a little every day, and you will grow.
  2. Living stones“Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house” 2:5Christ is the chief corner stone. You are a living stone, laid beside other believers.
  3. Who you are“Which in time past were not a people, but are now the people of God” 2:10A chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, God's own people. The old story is behind you.
  4. Pilgrims“Abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul” 2:11The appetites are a campaign against you. Hold yourself back, and live honestly where they can see.
  5. The example“Christ also suffered for us, leaving us an example, that ye should follow his steps” 2:21When He was reviled He did not revile back. He gave it to the Father, who judges rightly.
  6. The Shepherd“Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree… ye were as sheep going astray; but are now returned” 2:24–25He carried your sins Himself, and the Shepherd brought you home.

Question 1 of 6

The word

What are newborn babes to desire, so that they may grow?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The sincere milk of the word. A new believer grows by feeding on God's Word, a little at a time, and by tasting that the Lord is gracious.

Question 2 of 6

Living stones

What are believers built up into, and who is the chief corner stone?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A spiritual house and a holy priesthood. Jesus Christ is the chief corner stone, elect and precious, and the one who believes on Him will not be put to shame.

Question 3 of 6

Who you are

What are believers called in verse 9, and what has changed since time past?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people. Once they were not a people and had not obtained mercy. Now they are the people of God, and they have obtained mercy.

Question 4 of 6

Pilgrims

What are strangers and pilgrims to abstain from, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Fleshly lusts, because they war against the soul. A believer holds himself back from them and lives honestly, so those who watch him can see the good and glorify God.

Question 5 of 6

The example

What has Christ left us, and how did He answer being reviled?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

An example to follow in His steps. When He was reviled He did not revile again, and when He suffered He did not threaten. He committed Himself to the Father, who judges righteously.

Question 6 of 6

The Shepherd

What did Christ bear in His own body, and to whom have we returned?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He bore our sins in His own body on the tree. By His stripes we were healed, and we have returned to the Shepherd and Bishop of our souls.
