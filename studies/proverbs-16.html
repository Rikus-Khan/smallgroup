Proverbs 16

The LORD directeth his steps

Our plans and God's hand, the pride that comes before a fall, and the man who rules his own spirit.

Things to watch for

Part one

Man plans, God directs

Verses 1–9

We make our plans. The LORD has the last word.

Your eyes, His scales

How we see it

“Clean in his own eyes”

Every man has a good reason for what he does. We can explain almost anything to ourselves.

How God sees it

“The LORD weigheth the spirits”

He doesn't just watch what we do. He weighs why we do it, the motive underneath.

“But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

Verse 1 says it too: a man can line up his thoughts, but the final answer comes from the LORD.

Roll it onto Him

The Hebrew word for “commit” means to roll, like rolling a heavy stone off your back and onto someone strong enough to carry it.

Notice it's your works, your real job, your real plans. You still do the work. You just roll the weight of the outcome onto Him.

“Commit thy way unto the LORD; trust also in him; and he shall bring it to pass.”

Psalm 37:5

A hard verse, read carefully

  • God made some men to be wickedthe Bible never says God causes anyone to sin
  • evil is out of God's controlnothing is outside His hand
  • everything serves His purposeeven a wicked man who refuses God will meet a day of reckoning

God made everything for His own glory. Men choose wickedness; God doesn't push them into it. But He still rules over them, and their end, “the day of evil,” is judgment. And He'd rather they turned:

“Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?”

Ezekiel 33:11

How sin gets washed out

“Purged” means cleaned out, covered over. Not by working harder, but “by mercy and truth.” Mercy that forgives, and truth that doesn't pretend the sin was small.

Those two meet in one place: the cross. There God told the full truth about our sin, and poured out full mercy on sinners at the same time.

“Mercy and truth are met together; righteousness and peace have kissed each other.”

Psalm 85:10

Verse 7 is a principle, not a promise: living to please God often calms even our enemies down. But Jesus pleased the Father perfectly and still had enemies. Do what's right anyway.

You plan the trip. He directs the steps.

Planning is good. God gave you a mind to use. But almost nobody's life has gone the way he drew it up, has it?

For a believer, that's good news. The detours, the delays, even the places we didn't want to go: the LORD was directing the steps the whole way.

“O LORD, I know that the way of man is not in himself: it is not in man that walketh to direct his steps.”

Jeremiah 10:23

Part two

Weights, kings, and a fall

Verses 10–20

Honest scales, a righteous King, and the pride that goes before destruction.

Honest scales

Merchants weighed goods against stones they carried in a bag. A crook kept two sets: a heavy one for buying and a light one for selling.

God says the weights are His. Honest business, a fair day's work, a straight deal: that's worship. Cheating on the scale is cheating Him.

Remember verse 2? He weighs our spirits too. And He never uses a crooked scale.

The King's face

Verse 14

The king's wrath

“As messengers of death.” In the ancient world, an angry king could end your life with a word.

Verse 15

The king's favour

“A cloud of the latter rain”: the spring rains, about March and April, that filled out the grain just before harvest. It meant a good year was coming.

Verses 10–15 describe what a king ought to be: just, truthful, never twisting judgment. Solomon's line fell far short. Only one King fits every word: Jesus.

We all deserved the King's wrath. Through Christ, we get His favour instead, and in the light of His face there is life.

Wisdom is worth more than gold.

Verse 16 says it plainly. Money can be lost in a night. Wisdom keeps paying out for the rest of your life.

A “highway” in Bible times was a raised, built-up main road, not a dirt track. The upright man's main road, the way he travels every day, is “to depart from evil.” Turning away from sin isn't a side trip. It's the road itself.

“He that keepeth his way preserveth his soul.” Stay on it.

Pride goes first

Pride climbs. Every step up feels like strength: “I've got this.” “I don't need a meeting.” A haughty spirit looks down on everybody from up there.

But the higher it climbs, the harder the fall. Most relapses start right here, the day a man decides he's strong enough on his own.

“But he giveth more grace. Wherefore he saith, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.”

James 4:6

Better to be low with the lowly

“To divide the spoil” is to split up the loot after a raid. Running with proud men can pay well for a while. Verse 19 says it's still better to be humble and poor among humble people.

Chapter 15 ended with “before honour is humility.” Chapter 16 shows the other side of the same coin: before destruction is pride. Same hill, two directions.

And verse 20 gives the way to stay low and safe: “whoso trusteth in the LORD, happy is he.”

Part three

Sweet words and crooked men

Verses 21–33

Words that heal, words that burn, a ruled spirit, and a God who isn't left to chance.

Words like honey

I'm sorry Thank you I forgive you I'm proud of you Welcome back I'll pray for you

Honey was the sweetest thing they had, and it was used as medicine. Kind words work the same way: “sweet to the soul, and health to the bones.”

Verse 23 shows where they come from: “the heart of the wise teacheth his mouth.” Fix the heart, and the mouth follows.

Said twice, on purpose

Proverbs 14:12

There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.

Proverbs 16:25

There is a way that seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.

One word different, “which” and “that.” Otherwise the same warning, word for word, two chapters apart. When God says something twice, He means for us to hear it.

Here it sits right after pride (v. 18) and right before troublemakers (vv. 27–30). The road that seems right is usually the one our pride picked, or the one our old friends invited us down.

Four troublemakers

  1. The digger“diggeth up evil”: goes looking for dirt, then spreads it like fire (v. 27)
  2. The sower“soweth strife”: plants a fight wherever he goes (v. 28)
  3. The whisperer“separateth chief friends”: one bit of gossip can split best friends (v. 28)
  4. The enticer“enticeth his neighbour”: talks you into what's not good (v. 29)

Verse 30 even shows the body language: eyes half shut, scheming; lips moving, making trouble happen. Learn to spot these men. Then make sure you're not one of them.

Rule your own spirit

Taking a city was the biggest thing a warrior could do. God says the man who can control his own temper and cravings is stronger than that.

“He that hath no rule over his own spirit is like a city that is broken down, and without walls.”

Proverbs 25:28

A man with no self-control is wide open: every craving, every insult, every temptation walks right in. We don't build those walls by willpower alone. Self-control (“temperance”) is “the fruit of the Spirit” (Galatians 5:22–23), grown in us by God.

No such thing as luck

The “lot” was something like drawing straws or rolling dice, often used to make a decision. It was tossed into the lap, the fold of a man's robe. It looks like pure chance.

But “the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD.” The chapter opens and closes on the same truth:

  1. 16:1the answer of the tongue is from the LORD
  2. 16:9the LORD directeth his steps
  3. 16:33the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD

And verse 31: gray hair is “a crown of glory” when it's found in righteousness. Whatever age you came to Christ, the years you have left can be a crown.

Points to remember

  1. God's direction“The LORD directeth his steps” 16:9Make your plans, then trust Him with the steps. Nothing is left to chance.
  2. Trust“Commit thy works unto the LORD” 16:3Do the work, and roll the weight of it onto Him.
  3. Pride“Pride goeth before destruction” 16:18The day you think you don't need help is the day before the fall.
  4. Self-control“He that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh a city” 16:32Ruling yourself takes more strength than conquering a city, and the Spirit grows it in us.
  5. The heart“The LORD weigheth the spirits” 16:2We see clean hands. God weighs the heart and the motive.
  6. Words“Pleasant words are as an honeycomb” 16:24Kind words heal. Gossip burns and splits friends.

Question 1 of 6

God's direction

Who is really directing your steps?

What verses tell us about this?

“For that ye ought to say, If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that.”

James 4:15

Answer

The LORD. We plan, and we should. But He directs the steps, and even what looks like chance is in His hand. For a believer that's a relief: the detours of our lives weren't wasted. We hold our plans loosely and say, “If the Lord will.”

Question 2 of 6

Trust

What does it mean to commit your works unto the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

“Commit thy way unto the LORD; trust also in him; and he shall bring it to pass.”

Psalm 37:5

Answer

To roll it onto Him. We still do the work, but we hand Him the weight of how it turns out, and we trust Him with it. He gives our plans solid footing, and the man who trusts Him is called happy.

Question 3 of 6

Pride

What comes right before a fall?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Pride, a haughty spirit that thinks it's above needing God or anyone else. Pride climbs, then it falls hard. God resists the proud but gives grace to the humble, so the safest place for a man is low, near the lowly, trusting the LORD.

Question 4 of 6

Self-control

Why is ruling your own spirit better than taking a city?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that hath no rule over his own spirit is like a city that is broken down, and without walls.”

Proverbs 25:28

“But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law.”

Galatians 5:22–23

Answer

Because the hardest enemy to beat is the one inside. A man who can't rule his temper or cravings is a city with no walls. Conquering yourself takes more strength than conquering a city, and God gives that strength: self-control is fruit the Holy Spirit grows in every believer.

Question 5 of 6

The heart

How does the LORD see our ways differently than we do?

What verses tell us about this?

“But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

Answer

We judge by how things look and how they feel, so our own ways always seem clean and right to us. God weighs the spirit, the heart and motive underneath. That's why we need His word and His Spirit, not just our gut, to tell us which road we're on.

Question 6 of 6

Words

What can our words build up, and what can they tear down?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.”

Ephesians 4:29

Answer

Pleasant words build up the soul and even help the body heal. Gossip and strife burn like fire and split the closest friends. Every time we open our mouths, we are either handing someone honey or striking a match.
