Genesis 43

Judah puts himself on the line

The grain runs out, and Simeon is still in an Egyptian cell. Jacob cannot let Benjamin go, until Judah says the sentence that changes the story: “I will be surety for him.” Then a second journey, a house with a feast, and a ruler who has to leave the room to weep.

Part one

Judah steps forward

Verses 1–14

An old man says no until the bread runs out.

Waiting until the bread is gone

  1. “the famine was sore”sore means heavy, severe
  2. “they had eaten up the corn”a hard thing put off until the last handful
  3. “Go again, buy us a little food”Jacob says it as if nothing stands in the way
  4. Nobody says Simeonin a prison since chapter 42

Notice what the family had been doing: nothing. The only thing they were waiting for was for hunger to be worse than their fear. Everybody knew the condition: no Benjamin, no grain (42:20). They would rather run out of bread than have the argument.

What are you putting off until it hurts more not to? A phone call, an apology, a visit to your pastor, a step in your recovery. Hunger will force it eventually, but it is cheaper to go early.

A family argues over whose fault it was

Jacob (Israel)Why did you ever tell the man you had another brother?

The sonsHe asked us, sharply, about our father and our family. How could we know he would say, Bring him down? “straitly” means strictly

Everyone is looking for someone to blame, and nobody is going to be Simeon's rescuer. A family under pressure does this: it fights over the past while the grain is running out. Blame feels like action. It is not.

Then one man stops arguing and starts to carry something.

Two pledges

Reuben (42:37)

“Slay my two sons”

A big offer, paid in someone else's coin. Two boys who were not asked. Jacob would not take it.

Judah (43:9)

“I will be surety for him”

“Of my hand shalt thou require him.” Surety means a guarantee: a man who signs for another and pays if he fails. Judah puts himself on the line: “let me bear the blame for ever.”

Jacob accepts Judah and not Reuben. And look who is speaking. This is the Judah who said “What profit is it if we slay our brother?” and sold Joseph for silver (37:26–27). It is the Judah who, in chapter 38, was caught in his own sin and said of Tamar, “She hath been more righteous than I” (38:26).

Something happened to this man between the pit and the famine. He was broken, and he was rebuilt. If you have been broken, hold on to that: the man who steps up in chapter 43 is the same man who failed in chapter 37.

A present from the land

  1. balma healing resin, famous from Gilead
  2. honeysweet, rare, and prized
  3. spicesgum from desert shrubs, burned as incense
  4. myrrha sweet-smelling resin, for perfume and burial
  5. nutsprobably pistachios, nuts from Canaan's trees
  6. almondsfrom the almond tree, a Canaan fruit

Read that list again. Balm, spices and myrrh are the very goods the Ishmeelite caravan was carrying when it took Joseph down to Egypt (37:25). Jacob's sons are now carrying the same kind of goods down the same road, to the same brother, and again they do not know it.

“Take double money” and return the money from the sacks: “peradventure it was an oversight.” Jacob, an honest man, wants nothing in his hands that is not his. Then he prays: “God Almighty give you mercy before the man.” He has given up the arguing, and he has begun to pray. “If I be bereaved of my children, I am bereaved” means: if I must lose them, I must. He lets go.

PAID“I had your money”

“Peace be to you, fear not: your God, and the God of your father, hath given you treasure in your sacks: I had your money.”

Genesis 43:23

The steward says it plainly: it was already paid. The debt they feared had been settled before they arrived, and the man who settled it said nothing. Then he brought out Simeon, alive and free.

Paid before they asked

Notice who the steward credits: “your God, and the God of your father.” An Egyptian household servant speaks of the God of Israel. Joseph's house has been taught who the LORD is.

And that is the gospel in a small bag: the account was paid already by Someone else, and the right answer is not to run, but to come in.

“Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross;”

Colossians 2:14

Nothing is left on the bill. If you carry a debt of shame you can never pay, hear the steward: fear not. It is paid.

Water for the men who left him with none

The pit (37:24)

“There was no water in it”

Joseph, at seventeen, shut in a dry cistern while his brothers sat down to eat.

The house (43:24)

“Gave them water, and they washed their feet”

The brothers, tired from the road, are given water to wash and feed for their donkeys. They are made welcome.

The first courtesy of a house was water for dusty feet. Nobody here holds the old wrong over them: mercy before anyone has asked.

“Then came Peter to him, and said, Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times? Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven.”

Matthew 18:21-22

Joseph is already living that. If someone has wronged you, you can begin to treat them well before they say sorry.

Part three

The feast

Verses 26–34

The man holds it together until he sees his mother's son.

Eleven stars, eleven brothers

The first time, ten bowed (42:6). Now Simeon is free, and they bow again. “Made obeisance” means bowed low in respect.

Count them. In Joseph's dreams there were eleven sheaves and eleven stars (37:7, 9). On the first trip, Benjamin stayed home, so only ten bowed. Now Benjamin is here, and the picture is finally complete.

God keeps his word down to the number. And Joseph asks, “Is your father well?” The dream had a father in it too, and he is still to come (46:29).

“his bowels did yearn”

רחמים said rachamim “bowels” in the KJV: the deep gut feeling of compassion, a mother's tenderness

In the Bible, “bowels” are the place of deep feeling, like we'd say “my heart went out to him.” “Yearn” means to ache for someone. Joseph looks at Benjamin, the only brother with the same mother, and something breaks. He “sought where to weep.”

He goes into a private room, cries, washes his face, holds himself together (“refrained himself”), and says “Set on bread.”

A man may weep

There is no shame in a man's tears. The Bible shows a ruler of a nation doing it, and the Lord himself:

“Jesus wept.”

John 11:35

“Thou tellest my wanderings: put thou my tears into thy bottle: are they not in thy book?”

Psalm 56:8

If you have been taught that a man never cries, you were taught wrong. God keeps count of every tear. And if the heaviness is more than tears can fix, call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor. You do not have to carry it in a back room alone.

Seated by age, and a portion five times as large

Three tables: Joseph alone, the Egyptians, and the Hebrew brothers. Egyptians would not eat with Hebrews: an “abomination” to them, a thing they found disgusting.

The brothers are seated eldest to youngest, and “the men marvelled one at another” (looked at each other, amazed). How could a stranger know their ages?

Each of the ten

One portion

A plate from Joseph's table, the same for each brother.

Benjamin

Five times as much

“Benjamin’s mess was five times so much as any of theirs.”

A “mess” is a portion sent from the table. A coat of many colours once made ten men hate a brother. This time: “They drank, and were merry with him.” No sneer, no silence. Something has changed, and the next chapter tests it harder.

Question 1 of 6

Delay

Why did the brothers wait until the grain was gone?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Going back meant taking Benjamin, and Jacob would not hear of it. They waited until the hunger was worse than the fear. A hard thing put off gets harder. Do the thing you have been putting off today, and tell a brother in Christ you are doing it.

Question 2 of 6

Responsibility

What did Judah put on the line for Benjamin?

What verses tell us about this?

“A friend loveth at all times, and a brother is born for adversity.”

Proverbs 17:17

Answer

Himself. “Of my hand shalt thou require him… let me bear the blame for ever.” Reuben had offered his two sons. Judah offered his own life and name. Be the man who stands in for a brother, not the man who puts someone else's life on the table.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

What did the brothers think the returned money meant, and what did the steward say?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They thought it was a trap, a reason to take them as slaves. The steward said, “Peace be to you, fear not… I had your money.” It was paid already, by someone else. That is the gospel in a small bag (Colossians 2:14): the debt is settled, so stop running and come in.

Question 4 of 6

God's word

How was Joseph's dream still coming true?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he dreamed yet another dream, and told it his brethren, and said, Behold, I have dreamed a dream more; and, behold, the sun and the moon and the eleven stars made obeisance to me.”

Genesis 37:9

Answer

The dream had eleven bowing. On the first trip Benjamin stayed home and only ten came. Now all eleven bow, and God has kept his word down to the number. What the LORD has said, he does, in his time.

Question 5 of 6

Tears

What did Joseph do when he saw Benjamin?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus wept.”

John 11:35

Answer

He hurried out, went into his private room, and wept, then washed his face and came back to serve the meal. A strong man may cry, and the Lord himself did. If it is more than you can carry, call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor.

Question 6 of 6

Change

Did the brothers envy Benjamin's five times as much?

What verses tell us about this?

“For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.”

James 3:16

Answer

Not that we are told. “They drank, and were merry with him.” The men who once hated a brother for his coat now sit by a favoured one without a sneer. Something changed in them, and chapter 44 will test it harder. A man can change, and so can you.
