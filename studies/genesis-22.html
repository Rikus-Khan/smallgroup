Genesis 22

The Lamb God provided

God tests Abraham with the hardest request in the Bible: give back Isaac, the son of the promise. Abraham obeys, God stops the knife and provides a ram, and the whole mountain points ahead to the Lamb of God.

Part one

Take now thy son

Verses 1–8

Three days of walking, and a question from a boy.

“Tempt” means test

נִסָּה said nissah “tested, tried, proved”

Hebrews 11:17 puts it with another old word: “when he was tried”. A test shows what is in a man. And remember what we know about God from James:

“Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God: for God cannot be tempted with evil, neither tempteth he any man: But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed.”

James 1:13-14

So God was never trying to trip Abraham into sin. He was proving a faith that was already real (15:6), the way fire proves gold. The Bible tells us the end before the story starts: it is a test. Abraham did not know that.

God says it slowly

  1. “thy son”not a servant, not a stranger
  2. “thine only son”the one of the promise, the one Sarah bore
  3. “Isaac”“he laughs”, the name God had chosen
  4. “whom thou lovest”the first time “love” appears in the Bible

God did not say it quickly or lightly. He narrows it down, one word at a time, to the one thing Abraham loved most. He knew exactly what He was asking.

The test was not whether Abraham loved Isaac. It was whether he loved Isaac more than he trusted God.

Did God want a child killed?

What the false gods wanted

A child, burned

In the countries around, some gave their children to fire. The LORD later said of it, “which I commanded them not, neither came it into my heart.”

What the LORD did here

Asked for his heart, and stopped the knife

He asked for Abraham's trust, stopped the knife before it fell, and put a ram in Isaac's place. In the end He did not take Isaac. He kept him.

“And they have built the high places of Tophet, which is in the valley of the son of Hinnom, to burn their sons and their daughters in the fire; which I commanded them not, neither came it into my heart.”

Jeremiah 7:31

This is a hard chapter, and we won't pretend it isn't. But the story is told so that we see what God is like: He never wanted Isaac's death. The whole point of Moriah is the stop, and the provision.

“The land of Moriah”

From Beer-sheba (21:33) to Moriah is about fifty miles: three days on foot, with a donkey carrying the wood. Genesis says only, “one of the mountains which I will tell thee of”. The name next appears here:

“Then Solomon began to build the house of the LORD at Jerusalem in mount Moriah, where the LORD appeared unto David his father, in the place that David had prepared in the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite.”

2 Chronicles 3:1

Many hold it was the same ridge at Jerusalem, where God's house would stand.

Three days to turn back

  1. morningRose up earlysaddled the donkey, split the wood (v. 3)
  2. day oneWalkingIsaac beside him
  3. day twoWalkingstill a whole day to turn back
  4. day three“The place afar off”“I and the lad will go yonder” (v. 5)

It was not a rush. Abraham had three days to argue, to run, to change his mind. He did not. He got up early, did the practical things, and kept walking.

Look at what he says to the young men: “we will worship, and come again to you.” He told his servants they would both be back. Hebrews 11:19 explains why: Abraham reckoned that God could raise the dead.

Old words in verses 1–8

  1. temptto test, to put to proof (here it is not tempting to sin)
  2. clavesplit (this old word can also mean “stuck to”)
  3. assdonkey
  4. young menservants, young workmen
  5. lada boy or young man
  6. abidestay, wait
  7. burnt offeringan offering burned whole on the altar, wholly given to God

Lamb for a burnt offering: a young sheep, offered whole. Keep that in mind for verse 8, because it is the question of the whole chapter.

“Where is the lamb?”

IsaacMy father.

AbrahamHere am I, my son.

IsaacBehold the fire and the wood: but where is the lamb for a burnt offering?

AbrahamMy son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering. provide

It is the question that runs through the whole Bible, asked by a boy on a hill: we have the fire and the wood, where is the lamb? Sin needs a sacrifice, and nobody can bring one of his own. Abraham's answer is faith. He did not know how. He knew Who.

They went both of them together.

The words come twice, in verse 6 and again in verse 8. A father with the fire and the knife, a son with the wood on his back. Neither could do it for the other, and neither walked it alone.

There is nothing in the text to say Isaac fought. He asked his one question, got his father's answer, and went on walking. That is trust.

Some of you are about to walk a road you dread: a hearing, a funeral, a hard talk, a day without a drink. Don't walk it alone. Go together. Call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor, and let them come with you.

Part two

The knife, and the ram

Verses 9–14

The hardest verses in Genesis, and the moment everything turns.

What was Abraham thinking?

God had promised“in Isaac shall thy seed be called” (21:12)
→
God then commanded“offer him there for a burnt offering”
→
So Abraham reasonedGod is able to raise him up, even from the dead

Slay means kill. Genesis tells us what Abraham did. Hebrews tells us what was in his heart: he could not make the two words of God fit, so he held on to both, and trusted God to make them fit.

What Hebrews says

“By faith Abraham, when he was tried, offered up Isaac: and he that had received the promises offered up his only begotten son, Of whom it was said, That in Isaac shall thy seed be called: Accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead; from whence also he received him in a figure.”

Hebrews 11:17-19

“Tried” here means tested, as with “tempt” in verse 1, and “accounting” means counting it as certain. His faith had a reason: the God who gave Isaac when it was impossible could give him back.

Don't read this as God asking a man today to hurt his child. God has told us in His Word what He wants, and He never asks us to sin. Read it as an answer to this: when obeying God costs you the thing you love most, is He still good? Abraham's answer was yes.

Here am I

  1. 22:1, to God“Behold, here I am.”
  2. 22:7, to Isaac“Here am I, my son.”
  3. 22:11, to the angel“Here am I.”

Three times Abraham says it. To God when He called, to his son when he asked, and now to the angel of the LORD who calls from heaven, “Abraham, Abraham”. The double name is urgent: stop.

He is a man who is present to whoever calls him, and God stops the knife while his hand is still lifted. God did not need to see the cut. He saw the heart. “Now I know that thou fearest God” means now it is shown: to Abraham, to Isaac, and to everyone who reads this. To fear God here means to honour Him above everything, even His dearest gift. Withheld means held back.

Faith that shows

Genesis 15:6Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness
→
Genesis 22that faith is tested, and acts
→
James 2:22faith made perfect: completed, shown real

Paul says Abraham was counted right with God by faith (Romans 4). James says he was justified by works. They are not fighting. Abraham was counted righteous in Genesis 15, years before the mountain. On the mountain his faith was shown to be real. A man is not saved by his works, but a real faith always does something.

“The Friend of God”

“Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar? Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect? And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God.”

James 2:21-23

That is the name Abraham ends up with: not “the man who lied about his wife”, not “the man who tried Hagar”, but “the Friend of God”. A man in recovery can have that name too.

“In the stead of his son”

On the altarunder the knife

In the thicketcaught by his horns

Isaacbound on the wood, with no way out
The ram“behind him”, there all along

They trade places. Isaac walks free, and the ram goes on the altar. Stead is an old word for place: the ram died in his place.

This is the gospel, in a single verse of the Old Testament. Somebody has to die, and God provides a substitute. Like Isaac, we are bound, with no way out of sin and its wages. The difference is the substitute: for Isaac it was a ram, for us it was the Lamb of God.

And notice that the ram was “behind him”. The provision was there the whole time. He only had to lift up his eyes and turn around.

Jehovah-jireh

יְהוָה יִרְאֶה said Yahweh yireh “the LORD will see to it”, “the LORD will provide”

The word for provide in verse 8 and the word for seen in verse 14 are the same Hebrew verb: to see. Our own word provide comes from the Latin for “see ahead”. God sees what you will need before you need it, and has it ready.

And the saying looks forward: “as it is said to this day, In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen.” It was a promise that was still waiting. Jesus said of Abraham:

“Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad.”

John 8:56

Genesis does not tell us how much Abraham saw. But Jesus Himself connects this man's joy to His own day.

The lamb that God provided

Genesis 22:8

And Abraham said, My son, God will provide himself a lamb for a burnt offering: so they went both of them together.

John 1:29

The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.

Abraham said it in faith, not knowing how. About two thousand years later John the Baptist pointed at a man walking toward him, and said, there is the lamb. God provided Himself the lamb: not just a ram, but His own Son.

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18-19

“Without blemish and without spot”: nothing wrong with Him, so He could take the blame for men who had plenty wrong.

Spared, and not spared

Genesis 22:12

And he said, Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou any thing unto him: for now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me.

Romans 8:32

He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?

Paul uses the same picture. The Father gave His Son “for us all”, and Paul draws the conclusion: if God did not hold back His own Son, “how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?” The cross is the proof that He will provide the rest.

That is where a man in despair over his sin, or his future, can rest. The hardest thing was already given.

Isaac and Jesus

The only son

Isaac, “thine only son” (22:2). Jesus, “his only begotten Son” (John 3:16).

The wood

Isaac carried the wood up the hill (22:6). Jesus carried His cross (John 19:17).

The father

Abraham did not withhold his son (22:12). God “spared not his own Son” (Romans 8:32).

The third day

Isaac was as good as dead, and Abraham received him back “in a figure” (Hebrews 11:19). Jesus rose on the third day.

But one thing is different, and it matters most. Isaac was spared, because a substitute took his place. Jesus was the substitute. “In a figure” means it was a picture, and the picture points to Him.

Part three

In thy seed

Verses 15–24

God swears an oath, and the blessing reaches every nation.

“By myself have I sworn”

“For when God made promise to Abraham, because he could swear by no greater, he sware by himself, Saying, Surely blessing I will bless thee, and multiplying I will multiply thee.”

Hebrews 6:13-14

The promise was already made (12:2–3, 15:5). Now God seals it with an oath. When a man swears, he swears by something greater than himself. God has nothing greater, so He swears by Himself.

That is where a man who knows his own record can stand. The promise does not rest on how well you perform. It rests on the name of the One who swore it. Abraham's obedience was the fruit of a promise, not the price of it.

In thy seed shall all the nations be blessed.

Stars and sand: too many to count. And “thy seed shall possess the gate of his enemies”: the gate was the seat of a city's strength, so it means victory over every enemy.

“Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.”

Galatians 3:16

The word is seed, singular, and Paul says that Seed is Christ. All the nations means Egypt and Babylon and Rome, and every man who ever struggled with sin. The blessing that came down that mountain was for you.

Back to Beer-sheba.

Genesis does not describe the walk down. It says only that Abraham went back to his servants, and home. The hardest day of his life is over, and now it is the next day, with tents and flocks and a well to look after.

After a great day, or a terrible one, you go back to work. The God who met Abraham on the mountain was waiting at Beer-sheba too.

Question 1 of 6

The test

Why did God ask this of Abraham, and did He ever mean for Isaac to die?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God: for God cannot be tempted with evil, neither tempteth he any man:”

James 1:13

Answer

It was a test, not a temptation to sin: God never tempts anyone to evil (James 1:13). He was proving Abraham's faith, to Abraham himself and to us. He never meant Isaac to die: He stopped the knife in time, and gave Isaac back.

Question 2 of 6

Faith

What did Abraham believe on the way up the mountain?

What verses tell us about this?

“Accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead; from whence also he received him in a figure.”

Hebrews 11:19

Answer

That God would keep His word. God had said, “In Isaac shall thy seed be called”, and now He asked for Isaac. Abraham reasoned that God was able to raise him from the dead, so he told his servants, “we will come again to you”. When what God asks seems to cut against what He promised, keep walking, and trust that He is good.

Question 3 of 6

The lamb

Who provided the lamb, and in whose place did it die?

What verses tell us about this?

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

Answer

God provided it, and it died in Isaac's place: a ram, “in the stead of his son”. The full answer came about two thousand years later, when John the Baptist saw Jesus and said, “Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.” Whatever your sin is, the Lamb has already died for it.

Question 4 of 6

The only son

How does Abraham's only son point to God's only Son?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?”

Romans 8:32

Answer

Both are only sons, loved by their fathers, and both carried the wood up a hill. But Isaac was spared, because a ram took his place. God's Son was not spared: “He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all.” Isaac had a substitute. Jesus is the substitute.

Question 5 of 6

The name

What does Jehovah-jireh mean?

What verses tell us about this?

“Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad.”

John 8:56

Answer

“The LORD will see to it”, or “the LORD will provide”. The Hebrew for provide is see: He sees ahead, and has it ready. The ram was in the thicket before Isaac asked his question. And “it shall be seen” looks forward: Jesus said Abraham “rejoiced to see my day.” Your need is already known to the One who sees ahead.

Question 6 of 6

The promise

What did God add after Abraham obeyed?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.”

Galatians 3:16

Answer

God swore by Himself, since nobody was greater to swear by: countless descendants and, best of all, “in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed.” Paul says that Seed is Christ. So the blessing that came down this mountain reaches every man who trusts Him, including you.
