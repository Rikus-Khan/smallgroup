Part one

Peace and the heart

Verses 1–9

Quiet bread beats a fighting feast, and God sees what the fire sees.

How to read this chapter

From chapter 10 on, Proverbs is mostly two-line sayings. Each verse stands on its own, like a punch that lands and is done. The second line usually repeats the first in other words, or flips it to show the opposite.

These are wise sayings about how life usually works. They aren't promises that things will always go a certain way. They're God's wisdom for daily life.

We'll read every verse in order, then gather the sayings by theme as we go.

Bread and peace, or a feast and a fight

Option one

A dry crust, and quiet

Not much on the table. But nobody's yelling, and you can rest.

Option two

A full house, and strife

“Sacrifices” here means the meat left over from offerings, the makings of a big family feast. Plenty of food, and constant fighting.

Peace is worth more than plenty. A lot of men have had the money and the stuff and found it wasn't worth the fighting that came with it.

The fire shows what's real

A metalworker heats silver and gold until the junk (the dross) floats up and can be skimmed off. Heat doesn't ruin the metal. It shows what's in it.

God does that with hearts. Hard seasons bring out what's really in us, so He can clean it out. “The trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold” (1 Peter 1:7).

What you listen to, and who you laugh at

“Naughty” in the KJV is much stronger than today: worthless, wicked. Verse 4 says a crooked man likes to hear crooked talk. What we enjoy listening to says something about us.

Verse 5: making fun of a poor man is an insult to the God who made him. Every man you'll ever meet carries his Maker's fingerprints, the guy under the bridge included. So do you.

Cover it, or repeat it

A wrong is done→covered→love growsForgive it, deal with it privately, and let it go.
A wrong is done→repeated→friends splitBring it up again, tell it around, keep score.

“Covering” isn't hiding crime or excusing abuse. It means not dragging an old offense back out. “Charity shall cover the multitude of sins” (1 Peter 4:8). That's what God did for us in Christ.

Part two

Fools and friends

Verses 10–20

Some men learn from one word. Some never learn. And a true friend stays.

One word, or a hundred blows

The wise man

One reproof. He hears it and changes.

The fool

A hundred stripes. Still the same.

Some of us learned everything the hard way: jail, rehab, a wrecked marriage. The wise man learns from a word. Today you can choose to be the man who listens the first time.

And verse 12 is no joke: a mother bear who's lost her cubs is less dangerous than a fool in the middle of his foolishness. Don't get in the car with him.

A crack in the dam

A fight starts like a little leak in a dam. At first it's a trickle: one sharp word, one look.

But water widens every crack it goes through. Soon nobody can stop it.

So “leave off contention, before it be meddled with.” Walk away while it's still a trickle. That isn't weakness. It's wisdom.

You can't buy what you don't want

Picture a fool with money in his hand to pay a teacher. He could buy lessons, books, a program. But he “hath no heart to it.” He doesn't really want to change.

Wisdom isn't bought. It starts with wanting it. And the good news is, God gives it freely to anyone who asks Him (James 1:5).

Verse 15 adds: calling a guilty man innocent, or an innocent man guilty, is “abomination,” something God hates. God cares about fair judgment.

A friend loveth at all times.

Not just when you're sober, or doing well, or fun to be around. All times. And a brother is “born for adversity”: hard times are what brothers are for.

Most of us have had “friends” who were only around for the party. When the money or the high ran out, so did they. Verse 17 describes a different kind of man.

“Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.”

John 15:13

Jesus is that Friend. And He makes us into that kind of friend for each other.

Striking hands

Your neighbor“I need a loan”You shake on it“I'll stand good for it”His debt is now yours

A “surety” promises to pay if the borrower doesn't. A handshake in front of witnesses sealed it. Today we'd call it co-signing. Proverbs warns against it again and again (6:1–5, 11:15, 22:26). Generosity is good. Promising what you can't cover is not.

Verse 19: “exalteth his gate” likely means building a big, showy entrance. A man who loves to fight and loves to show off is asking for trouble. “Froward” (v. 20) means twisted, crooked.

Part three

A merry heart, a quiet mouth

Verses 21–28

Joy that heals, bribes that rot, and the wisdom of saying less.

Doctor's orders

℞

Takea merry heart

Effectdoes good, like medicine

Warninga broken spirit dries the bones

God made us so that joy really does help body and mind. A crushed spirit wears a man down to the bone. A lot of using started as a way to numb a broken spirit.

Real joy isn't faking a smile. It comes from the Lord: “the joy of the LORD is your strength” (Nehemiah 8:10). And He's near to the broken: “He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds” (Psalm 147:3).

A gift under the table

The “bosom” was the fold of a robe at the chest, where a man carried things out of sight. A gift slipped out of there is a bribe.

17:8→how a bribe looks to the man who has itLike a lucky stone. It seems to open every door.
17:23→what a bribe really doesIt twists justice. Proverbs reports what people think, then tells the truth.

Verse 24: a fool's eyes are “in the ends of the earth.” Always looking somewhere else for the answer, when wisdom is right in front of him.

The power of saying less

A man of understanding has “an excellent spirit,” a cool, steady spirit. He doesn't have to win every argument or fill every silence.

Verse 28 has a smile in it: even a fool looks wise as long as he keeps his mouth shut. Most of our worst moments started with words we couldn't take back.

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:”

James 1:19

Points to remember

  1. Who God is“The LORD trieth the hearts” 17:3God looks at the heart. Hard times show what's there, so He can clean it out.
  2. Strife“Leave off contention, before it be meddled with” 17:14Walk away while it's still a trickle. Cover offenses instead of repeating them.
  3. Correction“A reproof entereth more into a wise man” 17:10Learn from a word, not a hundred blows.

Question 1 of 6

Who God is

What is the LORD looking at when He tests a man?

What verses tell us about this?

“That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ:”

1 Peter 1:7

Answer

The heart. People look at the outside, but God tests what's inside, like fire tests silver and gold. Hard seasons aren't God trying to destroy us. He's bringing the junk to the surface so He can clean it out, and what's left is worth more than gold.

Question 2 of 6

Strife

How do you stop a fight before it gets out of hand?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Stop it early, while it's still a trickle. Walk away before it gets going. Don't keep bringing up old offenses; cover them with love. And remember that peace is worth more than winning, or even than a full table.

Question 3 of 6

Correction

How does a wise man take correction?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He lets one word sink in. He doesn't need a hundred hard lessons. He wants wisdom, so he listens when it comes, instead of always looking somewhere else for the answer. The fool takes beating after beating and stays the same.

Question 4 of 6

Friendship

What does a real friend do when trouble comes?

What verses tell us about this?

“Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.”

John 15:13

Answer

He stays. He loves at all times, not just the good ones. Hard times are what brothers are for. He covers your faults instead of spreading them. Jesus is that kind of Friend: He laid down His life for us. Now we can be that kind of friend for each other.

Question 5 of 6

Joy

What does a merry heart do for a man?

What verses tell us about this?

“He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.”

Psalm 147:3

“Then he said unto them, Go your way, eat the fat, and drink the sweet, and send portions unto them for whom nothing is prepared: for this day is holy unto our Lord: neither be ye sorry; for the joy of the LORD is your strength.”

Nehemiah 8:10

Answer

It does him good, like medicine. A broken spirit dries a man out. We can't fake joy, but the Lord gives it: He heals the broken-hearted, and His joy becomes our strength.

Question 6 of 6

Our words

When is it wise to say nothing at all?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:”

James 1:19

Answer

When you're angry, when you don't know the whole story, and when your words would only add fuel. A wise man spares his words and keeps a cool spirit. Listening first is almost never a mistake.
