Genesis 19

Out of Sodom

Two angels reach Sodom, and we see what the city has done to Lot. God's judgment falls, and a man who lingered is pulled out by the hand. It is the story of a believer who drifted, and of mercy that went and got him.

Part one

Two angels at the gate

Verses 1–11

Evening in Sodom: a good man's house, and a city at his door.

How a man ends up in the gate

  1. tent toward Sodom(13:12)
  2. dwelt in Sodom(14:12)
  3. sat in the gate(19:1)
  4. sons in law of Sodom(19:14)

“Abram dwelled in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelled in the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom.”

Genesis 13:12

“The gate” was the city's meeting place, where the leaders sat and the judges heard cases. Lot had gone from a tent toward Sodom, to living in it, to a seat among its leaders. No single step looked like a big one.

Every relapse has stairs like this. It starts with a place you “just drive past”. Stop at the top step, and bring a brother in Christ with you.

“Nay; but we will abide in the street.” The angels first said no, and Lot “pressed upon them greatly”. Lot knew what a night in the open square would bring.

What the city wanted

“Know” here has its plain Bible meaning: to have sex. The whole city, “both old and young, all the people from every quarter”, wanted to use two strangers. It wasn't one bad man. It was a city that had stopped treating sin as sin.

“Even as Sodom and Gomorrha, and the cities about them in like manner, giving themselves over to fornication, and going after strange flesh, are set forth for an example, suffering the vengeance of eternal fire.”

Jude 1:7

Add what Ezekiel said last time (pride, plenty, idleness, no help for the poor), and you have a city where nothing was holy and everything was for the taking.

That is where unchecked lust ends: other people become things to use. If that is part of your own story, don't hide it. Bring it into the light with your pastor and your brothers in Christ, where it loses its power.

Lot's worst moment

What Lot did right

He stood in the door

He went out alone to face the whole city, shut the door behind him to protect his guests, called them “brethren”, and said plainly: “do not so wickedly.”

What went terribly wrong

He offered his daughters

The Bible reports it and does not praise it. A father is supposed to protect his daughters, not trade them.

Years in Sodom had bent Lot's sense of right and wrong. In the East a guest under your roof was sacred, and that was all he could see. Compromise doesn't only affect what you do. It dulls what you can see.

“And delivered just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked: (For that righteous man dwelling among them, in seeing and hearing, vexed his righteous soul from day to day with their unlawful deeds;)”

2 Peter 2:7-8

Here is the surprise: Peter calls Lot a righteous man. He was saved by faith, as Abraham was, and tormented by what he saw every day. (“Conversation” there means way of life.) A believer can drift, and it will cost him, but it does not cancel what God has done for him.

“He will needs be a judge”

LotI pray you, brethren, do not so wickedly. one word

The crowdThis one fellow came in to sojourn, and he will needs be a judge: now will we deal worse with thee, than with them.

Lot said one honest sentence, and the city turned on him: who are you to judge? (“Sojourn” means to live somewhere as an outsider.) If you have stopped drinking, using or gambling, you have already met this crowd. You didn't even say anything, and they felt judged.

And watch the angels. They reach out, pull Lot in and shut the door, and the mob, struck blind, “wearied themselves to find the door”. Sin makes a man blind, and he keeps groping for what he wants anyway. Lot didn't save himself. The visitors he took in saved him.

Part two

Escape for thy life

Verses 12–23

Morning comes. A man who would not leave has to be led out by the hand.

He seemed as one that mocked.

Lot ran out and told his sons-in-law the plain truth: Up, get you out of this place; for the LORD will destroy this city. They thought he was joking.

Why? Because of how he had lived. For years Lot had lived exactly as they did, so when he finally said something serious, it carried no weight. A father who talks one way and lives another loses his voice.

You can't undo years in a day, but you can start. Live it quietly in front of your children and your friends, and keep at it. Honest years add up, and they speak.

While he lingered, they laid hold upon his hand.

At dawn the angels hurried Lot, and he stalled. He wasn't dragging his feet out of defiance. It was home. His house, his things, his life. So the men took him, his wife and his two daughters by the hand and led them out, “the LORD being merciful unto him.”

“And the LORD said unto Satan, The LORD rebuke thee, O Satan; even the LORD that hath chosen Jerusalem rebuke thee: is not this a brand plucked out of the fire?”

Zechariah 3:2

“And others save with fear, pulling them out of the fire; hating even the garment spotted by the flesh.”

Jude 1:23

Lot didn't walk out on his own strength. He was pulled, and mercy did it. When you are stalling at the edge of something you know you must leave, let your brothers in Christ take you by the hand. Call one now.

The commands

  1. Escape for thy lifedon't stay to save your things
  2. look not behind theedon't turn around
  3. neither stay thou in all the plaindon't stop in the same country
  4. escape to the mountaingo to the high ground

“Lest thou be consumed” (verse 17): lest means “for fear that”, and consumed means wiped out. These are the instructions of a rescuer, not a judge. Notice that every one of them points away from something and toward somewhere safe.

That is the shape of recovery too. It isn't only "stop". It is: leave that place, don't look back, and go somewhere safe, which for you is your brothers in Christ and your church.

“Is it not a little one?”

צֹעַר said Tso'ar “small”: Zoar, the little one

The angels said “escape to the mountain”. Lot argued: “I cannot.” He asked for the nearby little town instead, and the LORD said yes: “I have accepted thee concerning this thing also.” God is patient with a man's weak faith.

But the place Lot begged for wasn't safe in his own head. Verse 30 says he “feared to dwell in Zoar”. Half-measures don't give peace.

Real judgment, real patience

The fire

Jesus treated it as real

“Brimstone” is burning sulphur. Jesus spoke of it as plain history, and Peter calls Sodom “an ensample”, an example for the ungodly.

The patience

The angels got Lot out first

The fire fell only when Lot was safe in Zoar: “I cannot do any thing till thou be come thither.” God took the righteous out before he judged.

“Likewise also as it was in the days of Lot; they did eat, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they builded; But the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed them all.”

Luke 17:28-29

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Ordinary life, right up to the day. Don't put off settling things with God. He is patient, and the door is open to anyone who comes to Jesus.

Part three

Fire from the LORD

Verses 24–29

A woman looks back, a man looks on from a hilltop, and God remembers a prayer.

Remember Lot's wife.

“Remember Lot’s wife.”

Luke 17:32

The Bible gives her one line: “his wife looked back from behind him.” She was behind him, lagging. Most teachers read this as a heart still tied to Sodom, and Jesus uses it in a warning about the day of his return, not to go back for the things we left behind.

If you have left a life behind (the bar, the dealer's number, the old crowd), the old life will call you by name. Don't go back for a look. Jesus said the man who tries to hold on to his old life loses it. Face forward, with your brothers in Christ beside you.

And remember who Lot is in this verse: he was pulled by the hand while she was lagging. The hand is still out. Take it.

So why was Lot saved? Not because his life earned it. Verse 16 says the LORD being merciful, and verse 29 says God remembered Abraham. Another man's prayer, and God's own mercy.

Abraham went early to the place where he had stood and looked down on the smoke “as the smoke of a furnace”. He didn't know who had got out. He only knew what he had prayed. We often don't see the answer to a prayer for years.

Somebody prayed for you before you knew you needed it: a mother, a grandmother, a pastor, a friend. And you can be that man for someone else.

Part four

The cave

Verses 30–38

What compromise cost Lot, told in a few plain lines.

  4. brimstoneburning sulphur
  5. gat upgot up

“After the manner of all the earth” (verse 31) means the way marriage normally works.

From the garden plain to a cave

  1. the garden plain“as the garden of the LORD” (13:10)
  2. a house in Sodomand a seat in the gate
  3. little Zoar“he feared to dwell” (19:30)
  4. a cavein the mountain he had refused (19:30)

Lot chose the best-looking land, and years later he was living in a cave in the very mountain he had said he couldn't reach. His wife was gone, his sons-in-law were gone, his home and herds were gone.

That is what Lot's choice cost. And yet he is alive, because God was merciful. A cave is not the end of a story that God is still in.

Two plain lines

The Bible tells this in a few short sentences and does not dress it up, and we will not either. Lot's daughters had grown up in Sodom, and they had just watched their world burn. They believed there was “not a man in the earth” left, and they chose a terrible, sinful way to keep the family going. Scripture reports it and does not approve it.

Two things stand out for us. The first is the wine: Lot “perceived not”. Drink took him where he would never have gone sober, and he had no control over what happened while he was under it. Many of you know exactly what it is to lose hours, or a whole night, or worse.

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

The second is what a house takes in. The family had brought something out of Sodom in their heads. Children carry what the home carried. That is a reason to clean the house now, for the sake of your own kids.

Moab and Ammon

  • LotAbraham's nephew
    • Moab“from the father”; the Moabites
      • Rutha Moabite woman, centuries later
    • Benammi“son of my people”; the Ammonites

Two nations came from this, east of the Dead Sea, and they were often trouble to Israel. But look at Ruth.

“And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth; and Obed begat Jesse;”

Matthew 1:5

Ruth was a Moabite who trusted the God of Israel. She became the great-grandmother of King David, and so an ancestor of Jesus. God was kind to Lot's children, too (Deuteronomy 2:9). A shameful beginning did not keep God out of the story.

Points to remember

  1. Drifting“Lot sat in the gate of Sodom” 19:1Nobody falls in one step. Catch it at the top of the stairs.
  2. Sin“do not so wickedly” 19:7Sodom made sin normal, and years of living there dulled Lot's sense of right.
  3. Mercy“the LORD being merciful unto him” 19:16Lot was pulled out by mercy, and because God remembered Abraham's prayer.
  4. Judgment“the LORD rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah brimstone and fire” 19:24The judgment was real, and God took his own out first. Come to Jesus while the door is open.
  5. Looking back“his wife looked back from behind him” 19:26Don't go back for one look at the old life. Remember Lot's wife.
  6. The cost“he dwelt in a cave, he and his two daughters” 19:30Compromise costs a family dearly, but God's grace was still working: Ruth came from Moab.

Question 1 of 6

Drifting

How did Lot end up sitting in the gate of Sodom?

What verses tell us about this?

“Abram dwelled in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelled in the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom.”

Genesis 13:12

“And they took Lot, Abram’s brother’s son, who dwelt in Sodom, and his goods, and departed.”

Genesis 14:12

Answer

One step at a time. First a tent pitched toward Sodom, then a house in it, then a seat among its leaders. No step looked big. If you are drifting, stop at the top of the stairs, and bring a brother in Christ with you.

Question 2 of 6

Sin

What was Sodom like, and what had it done to Lot's thinking?

What verses tell us about this?

“that righteous man dwelling among them, in seeing and hearing, vexed his righteous soul from day to day with their unlawful deeds”

2 Peter 2:8

Answer

The whole city wanted to abuse strangers and told the one man who objected to mind his own business. Years there dulled Lot's judgment until he made a terrible offer at his own door. Yet Peter calls him a righteous man, tormented by what he saw. A believer can drift, and it costs him.

Question 3 of 6

Mercy

Why was Lot saved?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not because he earned it. The LORD was merciful, and God remembered Abraham's prayer. Lot lingered and was pulled out by the hand. When you stall, let your brothers in Christ take you by the hand.

What verses tell us about this?

Yes. Jesus and Peter both point back to it as a warning. But the same God took Lot out first, and today he is patient, not willing that any should perish. Don't put off coming to Jesus. The door is open now.

Question 5 of 6

Looking back

What happened to Lot's wife, and what does Jesus say about her?

What verses tell us about this?

“Remember Lot’s wife.”

Luke 17:32

Answer

She looked back from behind her husband, with her heart still in Sodom, and was caught in the judgment. Jesus says: remember her. Don't go back for the things of the old life. Face forward, and stay close to your brothers in Christ.

Question 6 of 6

The cost

What did Lot's compromise cost his family in the end?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth; and Obed begat Jesse;”

Matthew 1:5

Answer

Nearly everything: his wife, his sons-in-law, his home, and a cave for a house. Wine took away his control, and his daughters carried Sodom in their thinking. Yet Ruth, a woman from Moab, became an ancestor of Jesus. Shame at the start doesn't keep God out of the story.
