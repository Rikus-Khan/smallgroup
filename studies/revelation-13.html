In chapter 12 John saw the dragon, “that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan” (12:9), thrown down to the earth, angry, “because he knoweth that he hath but a short time” (12:12).

He can't touch the throne, so he goes after God's people. Chapter 13 shows the two helpers he raises up to do it.

Read this one calmly

No chapter in the Bible has drawn more guesses, charts and fear. So here's how we'll read it:

  • no chartsthe pictures aren't a puzzle to solve on paper
  • no dates“of that day and hour knoweth no man” (Matthew 24:36)
  • no names off the newswe won't pin the beast on any man, nation or thing
  • what is plainlet the Old Testament explain the pictures, and hold on to what is clear

Good Bible teachers differ widely here. Many see a last world ruler still to come before Christ returns; others see the Rome of John's day; many see both, Rome then and a final beast to come. All of them agree on what matters most: the beast loses, and the Lamb wins.

Part one

The beast out of the sea

Verses 1–4

John stands on the shore and watches something terrible climb out of the water. The whole world follows it.

Daniel saw it first

Six hundred years before John, Daniel saw “four great beasts” come up from the sea (Daniel 7:3): a lion, a bear, a leopard, and a fourth with ten horns. He was told:

“These great beasts, which are four, are four kings, which shall arise out of the earth.”

Daniel 7:17

John sees one beast with all of Daniel's in it. Every proud kingdom that ever set itself against God, rolled into one. And the sea? “The waters… are peoples… and nations” (17:15): it rises out of restless mankind.

Whose power is it?

Look at the end of verse 2. Nothing the beast has is its own: the dragon gave it all. And the dragon of chapter 12 has the same “seven heads and ten horns” (12:3). The beast is the devil's image, walking on the earth.

In Daniel a horn is a king: “the ten horns… are ten kings” (Daniel 7:24). Heads, horns and crowns mean great, wide, worldly power. Teachers differ on which kings are meant; you don't need to know that to see the point.

Here is the point: the devil has never made anything. He can only borrow, bend and copy. Keep your eye on that word gave. It's going to keep showing up.

“Who is like unto the beast?”

Exodus 15:11, Moses' song

Who is like unto thee, O LORD, among the gods? who is like thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?

Revelation 13:4, the world's song

And they worshipped the beast, saying, Who is like unto the beast? who is able to make war with him?

The world takes Israel's song to God and sings it to the beast. And the wound “wounded to death” and healed is a copy of the Lamb “as it had been slain” who stands alive (5:6). The devil even fakes a resurrection.

Why do they worship? Not because the beast is good, but because it looks unbeatable: “who is able to make war with him?” Men have always bowed to whatever looks strongest: the gang, the money, the bottle, the crowd. That's the sin under this chapter.

Part two

Given, for a time

Verses 5–10

The beast speaks big and makes war. But every line has a leash on it, and one book it can never touch.

On a leash

Count the times the beast's power is gave or given in verses 1 to 8:

1  2×1 3  4×1 5×2 6  7×2 8 

Borrowed power, and only for “forty and two months.” Daniel's last king also had a set time, “a time and times and the dividing of time” (Daniel 7:25), which many read as the same three and a half years. Some read it as three and a half literal years; others as a picture of a short, fixed season. Either way, God set the clock, and the beast can't add a day to it.

The devil is loud, but he is not free. He has “but a short time” (12:12), and he knows it.

The safe place

The whole world worships the beast, except those written in “the book of life of the Lamb.” He can make war on them. He can't take a name out of that book.

Jesus promised: “I will not blot out his name out of the book of life” (3:5).

And how does a name get there? Not by being good enough. It's the Lamb's book, and the Lamb was slain. His door is open: “whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely” (22:17).

Rescue planned before the fall

Before the first man ever sinned, God had already planned the Lamb. Your sin didn't catch Him by surprise, and neither did yesterday.

“But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot: Who verily was foreordained before the foundation of the world, but was manifest in these last times for you,”

1 Peter 1:19–20

“Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.”

Luke 10:20

Here is the patience of the saints

Verse 7 is sobering: the beast is allowed “to make war with the saints, and to overcome them.” Believers may lose jobs, freedom, even their lives. God doesn't hide that. So what does He ask?

What the beast can do

Hurt the body

Make war. Take your freedom. Take your living. Even take your life, for a short time.

What he can't do

Touch the soul

Blot out a name. Add a day to his clock. Pluck a man out of Christ's hand (John 10:28).

Patience here means endurance: staying put under the weight and not quitting. Faith means trusting God with the outcome. And verse 10 says leave the sword alone: God will repay. “All they that take the sword shall perish with the sword” (Matthew 26:52).

Who really overcomes?

Revelation 13:7

And it was given unto him to make war with the saints, and to overcome them: and power was given him over all kindreds, and tongues, and nations.

Revelation 12:11

And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.

“And fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell.”

Matthew 10:28

On earth it can look like the beast won. In heaven's record, the man who stayed faithful is the overcomer. You may feel beaten today. You are not defeated.

Part three

The beast out of the earth

Verses 11–17

The second beast doesn't roar. It looks gentle, does wonders, and talks people into worship.

Looks like a lamb

The first beast came out of the sea, roaring. This one comes up quietly, with two little horns “like a lamb.” Later chapters call him “the false prophet” (16:13). His whole job is to point people to the first beast.

But listen to him talk, and it's the dragon's voice. Jesus warned about this very thing:

“Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep’s clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves.”

Matthew 7:15

The copycat

Put the dragon and his two beasts side by side with God, and you see what they are: a fake of the real thing.

The devil can't create. He can only copy, and a copy always looks close enough to fool a man who isn't looking at the real thing.

Gentle voices

Temptation rarely looks like a dragon. It looks like a friend, and it sounds kind:

the lamb's voice“Just one won't hurt. You've earned it. Nobody's going to know.”

what it really saysBow down to the old master again. dragon

So don't judge a voice by how soft it sounds. Judge it by where it's leading you: to worship God, or to bow to something else. “Try the spirits whether they are of God” (1 John 4:1).

A miracle is not proof

Fire from heaven was Elijah's sign on Mount Carmel (1 Kings 18:38). The false prophet copies it. Paul warned of the same thing:

“Even him, whose coming is after the working of Satan with all power and signs and lying wonders,”

2 Thessalonians 2:9

So the test of a teacher is never “Can he do wonders?” The test is “Does he lead me to worship the Lamb?” A man who can make fire fall and leads you away from Jesus is a liar with a good trick.

Bow or burn: it's happened before

Daniel 3:6, Nebuchadnezzar's golden image

And whoso falleth not down and worshippeth shall the same hour be cast into the midst of a burning fiery furnace.

Daniel 3:18, three young men answer

But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up.

Babylon's king set up an image and said: worship or die. Shadrach, Meshach and Abed-nego said no. And when they were thrown in, the king saw four men walking loose in the fire (Daniel 3:25). God didn't keep them out of the furnace. He walked in it with them.

Whose name do you wear?

In John's world, slaves and soldiers were often marked with their owner's or general's name. A mark said: this one belongs to me. God told Israel to bind His word “for a sign upon thine hand… as frontlets between thine eyes” (Deuteronomy 6:8): the hand for what you do, the forehead for what you think.

Revelation 7:3; 14:1

Sealed by God

His servants carry “his Father’s name written in their foreheads.” They belong to the Lamb.

Revelation 13:16–17

Marked by the beast

The beast's people carry his mark. They belong to him, and they've traded worship for a paycheck.

Notice the pressure: “that no man might buy or sell.” Most men don't sell out for a crown. They sell out to keep the job and pay the bills. Bowing to keep your living is an old temptation, and it didn't start here.

Part four

Here is wisdom

In the Bible seven is the number of completeness, God's number. Many teachers see six as man's number: made on the sixth day, always one short. Six, six, six is man at his very best, three times over, and still short of God.

Two thousand years of guesses

In Greek and Hebrew, letters also served as numbers, so a name could be added up. That's why people have tried to “count” the number into a name ever since John wrote it.

Every confident guess so far has been wrong.

Emperors, kings, generals, inventions: for two thousand years men have been sure, and every one of them died or faded and the world went on. We won't add another guess. “Here is wisdom” isn't a call to decode headlines. It's a call to see through the beast: impressive, but only a man.

Can a believer take it by accident?

  • something that happens to you while you aren't looking
  • the sin you fell into yesterday
  • a rumor to lose sleep over
  • a choice of whom to worshipthe mark goes with worship (13:8, 15; 14:9)

The mark comes to those who worship the beast, and verse 8 says plainly who won't: everyone whose name is in the Lamb's book. A believer isn't tricked out of that book. “They shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand” (John 10:28).

Question 1 of 6

Borrowed

Where does the beast get his power?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

From the dragon, the devil. And even that is only allowed: over and over the chapter says “it was given unto him,” and only for forty-two months. His power is borrowed and his time is limited.

Question 2 of 6

Daniel

What do the leopard, the bear and the lion tell us?

What verses tell us about this?

“These great beasts, which are four, are four kings, which shall arise out of the earth.”

Daniel 7:17

Answer

They come from Daniel 7, where four beasts out of the sea were four kingdoms. John's beast has all of them in it: every proud power that sets itself against God, rolled into one. Scripture explains its own pictures.

Question 3 of 6

The book

Who will not worship the beast?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that overcometh, the same shall be clothed in white raiment; and I will not blot out his name out of the book of life, but I will confess his name before my Father, and before his angels.”

Revelation 3:5

Answer

Everyone whose name is written in the book of life of the Lamb. The beast can make war on them, but he can't take their names out of that book. It's the safe place, and the Lamb's door is open to whosoever will.

Question 4 of 6

Patience

What does God ask of His people when the beast makes war on them?

What verses tell us about this?

“And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.”

Revelation 12:11

Answer

Patience and faith: to endure under the weight without quitting, and to trust God with how it ends. Not to grab the sword, but to hold on. The beast can hurt the body; he can't touch the soul.

Question 5 of 6

Deceived

Why does the second beast look like a lamb?

What verses tell us about this?

“Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep’s clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves.”

Matthew 7:15

Answer

Because deception works best when it looks gentle. He looks harmless, does wonders, and sounds reasonable, but he speaks for the dragon and leads people to worship the beast. Judge a voice by where it leads, not how it looks.

Question 6 of 6

The mark

Can a believer take the mark by accident?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Answer

No. The mark goes with worshipping the beast, a choice of whom to serve. Those whose names are in the Lamb's book do not worship him, and nobody can pluck them out of Christ's hand. A believer doesn't need to fear it; he needs to keep worshipping the Lamb.
