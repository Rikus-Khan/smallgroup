Proverbs 30

The words of Agur

A humble man asks who made the world, trusts God's pure Word, prays for just enough, and learns from ants and lizards.

Part one

Who is God?

Verses 1–9

A man admits how little he knows, asks the biggest question there is, and prays a short, honest prayer.

Honestly, nobody knows who Agur was. He appears nowhere else in the Bible. Some old Jewish teachers guessed he was Solomon under another name, but there's no evidence for it. Some think “the prophecy” (Hebrew massa) names a place, so he may not even have been an Israelite.

What we do know: God put his words in the Bible. You don't have to be famous for God to use what you say.

Wisdom starts with “I don't know”

“Brutish” means like a dumb animal. Agur isn't putting on a show of humility. He's honest: compared with God, what he knows is nothing.

Look at his words, “the knowledge of the holy.” They're the same words from earlier in Proverbs:

“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: and the knowledge of the holy is understanding.”

Proverbs 9:10

Every man in recovery knows this moment: “I can't figure this out on my own.” That isn't weakness. It's the doorway to wisdom.

Questions no man can answer

Who went up into heaven and came back down?

Who holds the wind in his fists?

Who wraps the waters up like a bundle in a cloth?

Who set the ends of the earth in place?

The answer to all four is the same: only God. Then Agur pushes one step further. If you know who did all this, “what is his name?” God isn't a nameless force. He is Someone.

“What is his son's name?”

“if thou canst tell?” → Jesus

Honestly, Agur probably couldn't fill in that blank himself. Some Jewish readers took “his son” to mean Israel: “Israel is my son, even my firstborn” (Exodus 4:22).

But the New Testament answers his riddle word for word:

“And no man hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven, even the Son of man which is in heaven.”

John 3:13

Agur asked the question. God answered it out loud at the Jordan: “This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (Matthew 3:17). We can tell. His name is Jesus.

Pure, like refined silver

“Pure” here is a metalworker's word: refined, tested in the fire. A silversmith melted silver again and again, skimming off the junk each time.

“The words of the LORD are pure words: as silver tried in a furnace of earth, purified seven times.”

Psalm 12:6

There's no junk left in God's Word. No lie, no trick, no mistake. People have tested it for thousands of years. You can put your whole weight on it. And He is “a shield” to the man who does.

Don't add to it

If God's Word is already pure, anything we add to it only makes it less pure. Moses said the same thing long before Agur:

“Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it, that ye may keep the commandments of the LORD your God which I command you.”

Deuteronomy 4:2

Watch for groups that bring a “new book,” a “new prophet,” or a list of extra rules a man must keep to be saved. And watch yourself: it's easy to say “God told me” when He didn't. Agur says that man will be “found a liar.”

A ditch on both sides

Agur only asks God for two things. First, keep me honest. Second, give me just enough.

Too little, and he might steal and drag God's name into the mud.

Too much, and he might get comfortable and say, “Who needs God?”

He knows his own heart. So he asks to stay on the road in the middle.

Just enough for today

“Food convenient for me” means my portion, the bread I need. Jesus taught us to pray the same way:

“Give us this day our daily bread.”

Matthew 6:11

“Not that I speak in respect of want: for I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content.”

Philippians 4:11

Plenty of us know both ditches. Broke and desperate, we did things we're ashamed of. Flush with cash, we forgot God and fed the habit. Contentment is a prayer you pray one day at a time. “Godliness with contentment is great gain” (1 Timothy 6:6).

Part two

Never enough

Verses 10–20

Proud eyes, endless appetites, and sins that try to leave no trace.

Four kinds of people

“Generation” here means a kind of people, the sort you'll find in every age:

  1. The ungratefulcurse their father, won't bless their mother (v. 11)
  2. The self-righteousclean in their own eyes, still dirty (v. 12)
  3. The proud“how lofty are their eyes!” (v. 13)
  4. The greedyteeth like knives, chewing up the poor (v. 14)

Number two is the most dangerous, because that man can't see it. You can't wash yourself clean by deciding you're clean.

“If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:8–9

Verse 10 adds a warning: don't run down a working man to his boss to get ahead. That kind of thing comes back on you.

“Give, give”

A horseleach is a leech, a bloodsucker. Its “two daughters” are probably its two suckers, one at each end. All it knows how to say is “Give, give.”

Then four things that are never full: the grave, a childless woman's longing, dry ground, and fire.

Any man who's fought an addiction knows that voice. One is too many, and a thousand is never enough. It never says, “That's enough.”

The only thing that fills

Feeding a craving never satisfies it. It only makes it hungrier. Jesus offers something that actually fills:

“Jesus answered and said unto her, Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again: But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life.”

John 4:13–14

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

And verse 17 is a sober warning in the middle of all this: the man who mocks his father and despises his mother ends up like a body left in a field for the birds. Disrespect is a road that ends badly.

“Three things, yea, four”

Agur loves numbered lists. It's a Hebrew way of teaching: name three, then add one more to say “and the list could keep going.” The last one is often the punch line.

30:15never satisfied
30:18too wonderful for me
30:21the earth cannot bear
30:24little, but exceeding wise
30:29comely in going

The prophet Amos uses the same pattern: “For three transgressions of Damascus, and for four, I will not turn away the punishment thereof” (Amos 1:3).

No trace left behind

Agur marvels at four things that move without leaving a track: a bird in the sky, a snake on bare rock, a ship on open water, and the mystery of how love grows between a man and a young woman.

Then he turns it into a warning (v. 20). The adulterous woman acts the same way. She “wipeth her mouth” like she just finished a meal, and says, “I have done no wickedness.”

Wiping your mouth doesn't make it clean

This is how sin talks after it's done: delete the texts, clear the history, wipe your mouth, and act normal. Do it long enough and you start believing you've done nothing wrong.

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

The way out isn't hiding better. It's telling God the truth, and usually a trusted brother too. Covered sin keeps its grip. Confessed sin loses it.

Part three

Small and stately

Verses 21–33

What shakes the earth, what little creatures know, and how to stop a fight before it starts.

More than they can carry

A fool when he is filled with meat.

All four of Agur's earth-shakers are people who suddenly got something their character couldn't carry: a servant made king, a fool with plenty, a bitter woman who finally got married, a maid who took her mistress's place.

Power, money, or position doesn't change a man's heart. It shows what's already in it. A settlement check, a new job, getting out: all good things, but they can shake a man's life if his character hasn't grown to match.

Ask God to grow the man first. Then whatever He gives you won't shake the ground under you.

Small, but wise

Ants

“not strong”

They store up food in summer. They plan ahead.

Conies

“a feeble folk”

Rock badgers. They can't fight, so they live in the rocks.

Locusts

“no king”

No leader, yet they move together, in bands.

The spider

small enough to hold

Yet she gets into kings' palaces. She hangs on.

Each one is weak, and each one makes up for it with wisdom. (The Hebrew word for “spider” probably means a small lizard, like a gecko. Either way, the lesson is the same.)

Weak men can be wise men

Not one of these creatures wins by being strong. Neither will we. Look at what they do:

  • Prepare aheadlike the ant: plan your day before temptation shows up
  • Hide in the rocklike the coney: run to the Lord, not to your own strength
  • Move togetherlike the locusts: you weren't made to fight alone. That's why this group exists
  • Hold onlike the lizard: keep showing up, keep hanging on

“The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer; my God, my strength, in whom I will trust; my buckler, and the horn of my salvation, and my high tower.”

Psalm 18:2

Walking tall

Four things with a confident walk: the lion that backs down from nothing, the “greyhound” (the Hebrew is unclear, maybe a war horse or a strutting rooster), the billy goat at the front of the herd, and a king with his army behind him.

They walk that way because of who they are, not because they're trying to look tough. A man who belongs to Christ doesn't have to strut. He can walk steady because of whose he is.

Squeeze it and something comes out

milkchurnedbutter
a nosewrungblood
angerforceda fight

In Hebrew, “churning,” “wringing,” and “forcing” are all the same word: pressing, squeezing. And the word for “wrath” is built from the word for “nose,” like flared nostrils. Keep pressing on anger and you'll get a fight just as sure as you'll get a bloody nose.

So verse 32 says: if you've been foolish or thought up something evil, “lay thine hand upon thy mouth.” Stop. Shut it. Walk away.

“Set a watch, O LORD, before my mouth; keep the door of my lips.”

Psalm 141:3

Points to remember

  1. Humility“I neither learned wisdom, nor have the knowledge of the holy” 30:3Wisdom starts when a man admits he can't figure it out on his own.
  2. Who God is“What is his name, and what is his son’s name?” 30:4Only God made it all. The Son who came down from heaven and went back up is Jesus.
  3. God's Word“Every word of God is pure” 30:5God's Word is refined like silver, with nothing false in it. Don't add to it. Trust it.
  4. Contentment“Give me neither poverty nor riches” 30:8Both ditches can pull us from God. Ask for daily bread, and be content.
  5. Cravings“Four things say not, It is enough” 30:15A craving is never satisfied by feeding it. Only Jesus fills a man.
  6. Small but wise“Little upon the earth, but they are exceeding wise” 30:24Weak men can be wise: prepare ahead, hide in the Rock, stick together, hang on.

Question 1 of 6

Humility

Why does Agur start by admitting what he doesn't know?

What verses tell us about this?

“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: and the knowledge of the holy is understanding.”

Proverbs 9:10

Answer

Because that's where real wisdom starts. Compared with the holy God, any man's understanding is small. The man who admits it and turns to God is the one who begins to learn. Pride shuts the door to wisdom; humility opens it.

Question 2 of 6

Who God is

Who is the Son that Agur asks about?

What verses tell us about this?

“And no man hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven, even the Son of man which is in heaven.”

John 3:13

“And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

Matthew 3:17

Answer

Agur may not have known the answer himself. But the New Testament gives it: Jesus is the One who came down from heaven and went back up, and the Father called Him “my beloved Son.” Only God made the world, and God has a Son whose name we can tell.

Question 3 of 6

God's Word

What does it mean that every word of God is pure?

What verses tell us about this?

“The words of the LORD are pure words: as silver tried in a furnace of earth, purified seven times.”

Psalm 12:6

“As for God, his way is perfect: the word of the LORD is tried: he is a buckler to all those that trust in him.”

Psalm 18:30

Answer

It's like silver that has been refined over and over until there's no junk left. God's Word has no lies or mistakes in it, so we can trust it completely, and God shields the man who does. Because it's already pure, we must not add our own words to it.

Question 4 of 6

Contentment

Why would a man pray for neither poverty nor riches?

What verses tell us about this?

“Give us this day our daily bread.”

Matthew 6:11

“But godliness with contentment is great gain.”

1 Timothy 6:6

Answer

Because he knows his own heart. Too much, and he might forget he needs God. Too little, and he might steal and shame God's name. So he asks for daily bread, just enough, and learns to be content with it.

Question 5 of 6

Cravings

What do the horseleach and the grave teach us about cravings?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus answered and said unto her, Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again: But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life.”

John 4:13–14

Answer

Some appetites are never satisfied. The more you feed them, the louder they cry “Give, give.” An addiction never says “That's enough.” Feeding it is a losing game. Only Jesus gives water that ends the thirst for good.

Question 6 of 6

Small but wise

What can a man learn from the ants, the conies, the locusts, and the spider?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer; my God, my strength, in whom I will trust; my buckler, and the horn of my salvation, and my high tower.”

Psalm 18:2

Answer

None of them is strong, but all of them are wise. Prepare ahead like the ant. Run to the Rock like the coney. Stick together like the locusts. Hang on like the lizard in the king's palace. Weak men can win with wisdom.
