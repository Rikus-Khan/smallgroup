Mark 12

Render to God the things that are God's

In the temple, Jesus tells of a vineyard and a murdered son, answers three hard questions, asks one of His own, warns about showy religion, and praises a poor widow's two mites.

Part one

They will reverence my son

Verses 1–12

Still in the temple, still the same day, Jesus tells the leaders a story. By the end they know it's about them.

A vineyard everyone knew

The owner built everything a vineyard needs. The winefat is the pit where juice ran from the press. Then he rented it to husbandmen, tenants who owed him a share of the crop.

The owner went away and trusted them with it. All he asked was his share of the fruit.

A story they already knew

Isaiah 5:2

And he fenced it, and gathered out the stones thereof, and planted it with the choicest vine, and built a tower in the midst of it, and also made a winepress therein: and he looked that it should bring forth grapes, and it brought forth wild grapes.

Isaiah 5:7

For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant: and he looked for judgment, but behold oppression; for righteousness, but behold a cry.

Isaiah sang this song about 700 years before. The leaders knew it by heart: the vineyard is Israel, the owner is God. So they're listening closely for who the husbandmen are.

Servant after servant

  1. beatensent away empty (v. 3)
  2. stonedwounded in the head (v. 4)
  3. killedthe third servant (v. 5)
  4. many othersbeating some, killing some (v. 5)

The servants are the prophets God sent to His people, one after another, for centuries. Israel's own history says it plainly:

“But they mocked the messengers of God, and despised his words, and misused his prophets, until the wrath of the LORD arose against his people, till there was no remedy.”

2 Chronicles 36:16

Notice who the patient one is. Any landlord would have sent soldiers after the first beating. This owner keeps sending messengers. That's the patience of God.

Last of all, the Son

The servants

Sent for the fruit

Messengers who spoke for the owner. Many came, and the tenants treated them worse and worse.

The son

The heir, the only one

Not one more messenger. The owner's own “wellbeloved.” The last one he had to send.

At the Jordan the Father had said, “Thou art my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (1:11). Jesus is telling the leaders, to their faces, who He is, and what they are planning to do to Him (11:18).

“Cast him out of the vineyard”: He would die outside the city walls. “Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate” (Hebrews 13:12). And the Father sent Him knowing all of it, for these very men too.

The stone they threw away

Jesus quotes Psalm 118, the same psalm the crowd sang the day before (11:9). The builders are the leaders. They looked Him over and tossed Him aside.

But God set Him as the head of the corner, the stone the whole building lines up on. They would reject Him at the cross. God would raise Him from the dead.

They knew

God builds with rejected stones.

They understood the story perfectly. It didn't change them. They only wanted Him gone faster, and the crowd was all that held them back.

Thrown away by people, written off by family, locked up? Jesus knows how that feels. His own nation's leaders said, “Not this one.” God made Him the cornerstone.

“This is the stone which was set at nought of you builders, which is become the head of the corner. Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.”

Acts 4:11–12

Part two

Two traps

Verses 13–27

First a question about taxes, then a riddle about heaven. Each one is built to catch Him.

Pharisees hated Roman rule; Herodians backed Herod's family, who ruled for Rome. Enemies, joined against Jesus. Tribute was the head tax every man paid Caesar.

Their flattery was true, and none of it meant. Jesus saw through it: “Why tempt ye me?”

Whose image?

A penny was a silver denarius, a day's wage. This one bore Tiberius Caesar's face, and a superscription (writing) calling him “son of the divine Augustus.”

His picture is on it, so give Caesar his coin. Christians pay their taxes (Romans 13:7).

But whose image is on you? “God created man in his own image” (Genesis 1:27). Caesar gets the coin. God gets the man, all of him.

Who were the Sadducees?

  1. Priests and rich menthe party of the high priest's family. They ran the temple.
  2. Moses firstthey held above all to the five books of Moses
  3. No resurrectionno angels, no spirits, no life after death

“For the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, neither angel, nor spirit: but the Pharisees confess both.”

Acts 23:8

They quote Moses' law about a man who died with no son. His brother was to marry the widow, so the dead man's name and land would stay in the family (Deuteronomy 25:5–6). This mattered for a widow: it kept her from being left with nothing.

A riddle to make heaven look silly

  1. died
  2. died
  3. died
  4. died
  5. died

  8. the womandied also

Seven brothers, one wife, no children. It's a made-up story, and an old one: the kind of puzzle men tell to win an argument. The point was to make the resurrection sound ridiculous: if there's life after death, she'll have seven husbands!

They didn't want an answer. They wanted a laugh at the Pharisees' expense, and maybe at His. But Jesus takes the question seriously, because death is serious.

Two mistakes

They didn't know

The power of God

They pictured the next life as this one carried on. But the God who made life can make it new. No more death means no more need for heirs: “they neither marry, nor are given in marriage.”

They didn't know

The scriptures

They trusted Moses most. So Jesus answers them from Moses, at the burning bush (Exodus 3).

“As the angels” doesn't mean we become angels. It means, like them, we'll never die again. Heaven isn't less than the best of this life. It's more.

“I am,” not “I was”

  1. Abrahamdied
  2. Isaacdied
  3. Jacobdied
  4. The bushcenturies later: “I am”Exodus 3:6
  5. Jesus“the God of the living”12:27

“Moreover he said, I am the God of thy father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. And Moses hid his face; for he was afraid to look upon God.”

Exodus 3:6

Abraham had been dead for centuries. God didn't say, “I was his God.” He said, “I am.” Abraham, Isaac and Jacob were still alive to God, waiting for the day He raises the dead.

If you've buried someone who knew the Lord, hold on to this. They are not lost. God is still their God.

Part three

The first of all the commandments

Verses 28–34

One scribe has been listening, and he likes what he hears. His question is an honest one.

The words every Jew knew

שְׁמַע said shema hear, listen, and do it

Later rabbis counted 613 commands in the Law and argued over which ones mattered most. Jesus answered with the Shema, the words a faithful Jew said every morning and evening (Deuteronomy 6:4–5). It starts with who God is: one Lord. Then it says what He wants: all of you.

  1. heartwhat you want
  2. soulyour life itself
  3. mindhow you think
  4. strengthwhat you do

Up, then out

Deuteronomy 6:4–5

Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD: And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.

Leviticus 19:18

Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the LORD.

He was asked for one commandment and gave two, because they can't be pulled apart. Love for God that never reaches people is a lie (1 John 4:20). Love for people with no love for God runs dry.

Look at the verse He picked for the second: “not … bear any grudge.” Loving your neighbor starts with letting go.

Not far

Not far is still not in.

This scribe “answered discreetly”, wisely. Love beats sacrifices, as Samuel said long before (1 Samuel 15:22).

But knowing the right answer isn't coming in. A man can know every slogan in the room and never surrender. This scribe didn't need a better answer. He needed the One standing in front of him.

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

John 3:16

Three questions, three answers

“Is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar?”RenderThe Pharisees and Herodians: a trap. Give Caesar his coin, and give God yourself (v. 17).
“Whose wife shall she be?”Ye errThe Sadducees: a riddle. God is the God of the living, and He raises the dead (v. 27).
“Which is the first commandment?”LoveA scribe: an honest question. Love God with everything, and your neighbor as yourself (vv. 30–31).

Durst means dared. After that, nobody dared ask Him anything. So He asks them one.

Part four

David's Lord, and a widow's two mites

Verses 35–44

A question nobody can answer, a warning about men who love to be seen, and a poor woman nobody else noticed.

David's son, and David's Lord

the LORD (God)“Sit thou on my right hand”my Lord (the Christ)
King Davidcalls Him“my Lord”
Everyone agreedthe Christ isDavid's son

“The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool.”

Psalm 110:1

No king calls his own descendant “my Lord.” Unless the Son is more than a man. The Christ is David's son by birth, and David's Lord because He is God. Jesus says David wrote it “by the Holy Ghost.” After He rose, Jesus sat down at that right hand (16:19).

Dressed for the part

  1. long clothingflowing robes that said “scholar”
  2. salutationsbeing greeted by title in the market
  3. chief seatsthe front bench of the synagogue, facing the people
  4. uppermost roomsthe places of honor at a feast
  5. devour widows' housescheated the women who trusted them, then made long prayers for a pretence: for show

Jesus isn't saying every religious man is a fraud. One scribe just came close to the kingdom. He's warning about men who love the look of holiness. More light means more responsibility, and so “greater damnation.”

“For the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

Two mites

The treasury was the offering chests in the Court of the Women; Jewish writings describe thirteen, shaped like trumpets. Big gifts clattered loudly.

2 mites1 farthing
64 farthings1 penny
1 pennya day's wage

A mite was the smallest coin. Her gift was a few minutes' wages. Nobody heard it land.

How God counts

Men count what you give. God counts what you keep back. The rich gave what they'd never miss. She gave what she had to live on.

Just before, Jesus warned about men who “devour widows' houses.” Here a widow gives God everything they had left her. Jesus saw it when no one else did.

He sees what nobody else sees

This isn't a sales pitch for giving to get. She went home that day with nothing anyone could see. But Jesus called His men over just to point her out.

Some of us feel we've got nothing left to give: no money, no reputation, years wasted. Two mites from a man who has nothing is worth more to God than a rich man's show. Your time, your honesty today, a phone call to a brother who's struggling. He sees it.

“For if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath, and not according to that he hath not.”

2 Corinthians 8:12

And it points ahead. In a few days Jesus Himself would give “all that he had, even all his living”: His own life, for us.

Points to remember

  1. The Son“This is the heir; come, let us kill him” 12:7God sent prophet after prophet, and last of all His Son. The stone they rejected became the head of the corner.
  2. Caesar“Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s” 12:17Caesar's image is on the coin. God's image is on you. Give Him yourself.
  3. Life“He is not the God of the dead, but the God of the living” 12:27God said “I am” the God of Abraham. Those who belong to Him are alive to Him, and He will raise the dead.
  4. Love“Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart” 12:30Love God with all you are, and your neighbor as yourself. Not far is still not in.
  5. David's Lord“David therefore himself calleth him Lord” 12:37The Christ is David's son by birth, and David's Lord because He is God.
  6. Giving“She of her want did cast in all that she had” 12:44God doesn't count the size of the gift. He sees the heart, and He sees what nobody else does.

Question 1 of 6

The Son

What did the husbandmen do with the owner's son?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They said, “This is the heir; come, let us kill him,” and they killed him and threw him out of the vineyard. Jesus was telling the leaders what they would do to Him, God's beloved Son. But the stone they rejected, God made the head of the corner.

Question 2 of 6

Caesar

What belongs to Caesar, and what belongs to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.”

Genesis 1:27

Answer

The coin with Caesar's image belongs to Caesar, so pay what you owe. But we bear God's image, so we belong to God, all of us.

Question 3 of 6

Life

How did Jesus show from Moses that the dead rise?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

At the burning bush, long after Abraham, Isaac and Jacob had died, God said, “I am the God of Abraham,” not “I was.” He is the God of the living. They are alive to Him, and He has the power to raise the dead.

Question 4 of 6

Love

Which commandment did Jesus call the first of all?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Hear, O Israel; The Lord our God is one Lord: and thou shalt love the Lord thy God” with all your heart, soul, mind and strength. The second is like it: love your neighbor as yourself. No commandment is greater than these.

Question 5 of 6

David's Lord

Why did David call his own son Lord?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore let all the house of Israel know assuredly, that God hath made that same Jesus, whom ye have crucified, both Lord and Christ.”

Acts 2:36

Answer

Because the Christ is more than a man. He is David's son by birth, and David's Lord because He is God. Speaking by the Holy Ghost, David saw Him seated at God's right hand.

Question 6 of 6

Giving

Why did the poor widow give more than all the rich?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They gave out of their abundance, what they'd never miss. She gave out of her want, all she had to live on. God measures the heart and what it costs, not the size of the gift.
