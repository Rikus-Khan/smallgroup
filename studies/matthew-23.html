Matthew 23

They say, and do not

Jesus' last public sermon in Matthew. He warns the crowds about religion that is all show, speaks eight woes over the scribes and Pharisees, and ends by grieving over Jerusalem.

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

Moses' seat

Verses 1–12

Jesus turns to the crowd and His own men: listen to the teaching, but don't copy the teachers.

The teacher's chair

Same day, same temple courts as chapter 22. The questions are over. Now Jesus speaks to “the multitude, and to his disciples” about the men who had just tried to trap Him.

“Moses' seat” was the place of the man who taught the law. Stone seats from old synagogues have been dug up, one at Chorazin in Galilee, where a teacher sat to explain the Scriptures.

Their words

“observe and do”

When they read and taught God's law, it was still God's law. Listen to it.

Their works

“do not ye after”

Don't follow their example. “They say, and do not.”

Two kinds of load

Matthew 23:4

For they bind heavy burdens and grievous to be borne, and lay them on men’s shoulders; but they themselves will not move them with one of their fingers.

Matthew 11:28, 30

Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. … For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.

They piled their own rules on top of God's law, then wouldn't lift a finger to help. “Grievous” means crushing. A heavier list can't set a man free. Jesus carries the load with us, and He carried our sin to the cross.

Dressed to be seen

“phylacteries”made broadSmall leather boxes of Scripture tied on the forehead and arm (Deuteronomy 6:8). They made theirs bigger.
“the borders of their garments”made longerTassels the law told Israel to wear, to remember God's commands (Numbers 15:38). They wore theirs long.
“the uppermost rooms… the chief seats”best seatThe places of honour at feasts and up front in the synagogue.
“Rabbi, Rabbi”the title“My master,” teacher. They loved hearing it in the market.

Most of us know how to put on a front. The trouble isn't the tassel. It's doing God's things to get men's eyes.

One Master, one Father

not “Rabbi”

8

One is your Master, even Christ.

not “father”

9

One is your Father, which is in heaven.

not “masters”

10

One is your Master, even Christ.

Jesus isn't banning the word “father” for your dad, or the word “teacher.” Paul called himself a father to the believers he led to Christ (1 Corinthians 4:15). He's banning titles that put a man between you and God, so that other men owe you a bow.

In His church nobody outranks anybody. “All ye are brethren.” The new believer and the old preacher stand on the same level ground.

The way up is down

exalt himself→abasedAbased means brought low. The man who climbs over others gets taken down.
humble himself→exaltedThe man who takes the low place gets lifted up, by God.

“Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up.”

James 4:10

Two days later, Jesus would kneel and wash His disciples' feet. The greatest Man who ever lived took the servant's towel.

Part two

Woe unto you

Verses 13–28

Eight times Jesus says it. Shut doors, loophole oaths, a swallowed camel, a dirty cup, and painted tombs.

Eight woes

13woe 14woe 15woe 16woe 17 18 19 20 21 22  23woe 24  25woe 26  27woe 28  29woe

“Woe” is a cry of grief over coming disaster, like “alas.” It's a warning, and it's sorrow too. This chapter ends with Jesus longing to gather these very people (v. 37).

A “hypocrite” was a stage actor, playing a part behind a mask. Jesus was gentle with sinners who knew they were sinners. His hardest words were saved for religious men who wore a mask and shut the door on everyone else.

“And the Pharisees and scribes murmured, saying, This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them.”

Luke 15:2

The first three

“ye shut up the kingdom”blocking the doorThey wouldn't come to Jesus, and they stood in the way of anyone who tried.
“ye devour widows' houses”robbing the weakThey took advantage of widows who trusted them, then covered it with long prayers. “Pretence” means for show.
“to make one proselyte”making it worseA proselyte was a Gentile who converted to the Jewish faith. They'd go anywhere to win one, then make him twice as lost as they were.

“The greater damnation”: the more light a man has, and the more he uses God's name to hurt people, the heavier his judgment.

Loophole oaths

They had a system. Swear “by the temple” and it didn't count. Swear “by the gold of the temple” and you were “a debtor,” bound to keep it. Same with the altar and the gift on it.

It was crossing your fingers behind your back, with a rulebook to back it up. And it had things upside down: the temple is what made the gold holy. “Sanctifieth” means makes holy, set apart for God.

Every word is said before God

Every oath goes back to God, whatever you swear by: the altar, the temple, heaven itself. You can't find a promise that God isn't in on.

He'd said it plainly on the mountain:

“But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil.”

Matthew 5:37

A lot of us lived on promises we never meant to keep: to wives, to kids, to judges, to ourselves. “I swear, this is the last time.” A new man in Christ doesn't need to swear. He just needs his yes to mean yes, one kept word at a time.

Counting leaves

Mint, anise, and cummin are kitchen herbs. A tithe is a tenth. They counted out every tenth leaf for God, and felt good about it.

“judgment”doing rightJustice and fair dealing.
“mercy”kindnessTreating people as God treats you.
“faith”faithfulnessTrusting God, and being trustworthy.

Jesus doesn't sneer at the tithe: “not to leave the other undone.” Small things matter. They just don't replace the big ones (Micah 6:8).

A gnat and a camel

Gnats and camels were both unclean under the law (Leviticus 11:4). A careful Pharisee strained his drink through a cloth so he wouldn't swallow a gnat.

Then he gulped down a whole camel, hump and all. Careful about tiny rules, blind to huge sins.

It's easy to fuss over another man's language while ignoring our own pride, or how we treat our families. Jesus calls that “blind.”

Start on the inside

“Platter” is a dish. “Extortion” is taking from people by pressure. “Excess” means no self-control, grabbing more than enough.

We know this cup: say the right things, look sober, act fine. Inside, nothing changed.

Jesus starts where we can't reach. Clean the inside, and the outside follows.

“Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.”

Psalm 51:10

Painted tombs

Touching a grave made a man unclean for seven days (Numbers 19:16). So each spring before Passover, tombs by the roads were whitewashed to warn pilgrims away. This was Passover week: the men listening had just walked past them, freshly painted and bright in the sun.

Beautiful outside. Full of death inside.

“…for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

Part three

O Jerusalem

Verses 29–39

The last woe, the blood of the prophets, and the saddest words in the chapter: “ye would not.”

“We'd never have done that”

The scribes and PhariseesIf we had been in the days of our fathers, we would not have been partakers with them in the blood of the prophets.

JesusWherefore ye be witnesses unto yourselves, that ye are the children of them which killed the prophets.
already planning it

They built and decorated monuments to the dead prophets. “Garnish” means adorn. Dead prophets are easy to honour; they can't tell you anything you don't want to hear. Meanwhile these men were plotting to kill the greatest Prophet of all.

“I'll never be like my old man” is a hard promise to keep without God. Only a new heart breaks the pattern.

All eight woes

  1. Shutting the door23:13
  2. Robbing widows23:14
  3. Making converts worse23:15
  4. Loophole oaths23:16
  5. Herbs, not mercy23:23
  6. A clean cup, a dirty inside23:25
  7. Painted tombs23:27
  8. Tombs for the prophets23:29

Run your eye down that list. Every one of them is about the gap between the outside and the inside, between saying and doing. That gap is what “hypocrite” means. And none of us is a stranger to it.

The hardest words

“Fill ye up then the measure”: their fathers had filled the cup of guilt partway by killing the prophets. Now they would fill it to the top by killing Him.

“Generation of vipers” means brood of snakes. John the Baptist had said the same to the Pharisees and Sadducees (3:7). Hell is real, and Jesus doesn't soften it. These are the hardest words He ever spoke.

But even these men weren't beyond reach. Nicodemus and Joseph of Arimathaea were rulers of the Jews, and Paul was a Pharisee. And soon after the cross:

“And the word of God increased; and the number of the disciples multiplied in Jerusalem greatly; and a great company of the priests were obedient to the faith.”

Acts 6:7

All the righteous blood

  1. Abelthe first murderGenesis 4
  2. prophetsstoned and killedthrough the centuries
  3. Zachariasbetween temple and altar
  4. Johnbeheaded14:10
  5. Jesuscrucifiedin a few days
  6. AD 70the temple fallsabout 40 years on

Abel was murdered by his own brother. Zacharias was killed in the temple courts themselves. From first to last, the blood cried out.

“And he said, What hast thou done? the voice of thy brother’s blood crieth unto me from the ground.”

Genesis 4:10

“This generation”: the people alive then. The men He sent were scourged and killed (Acts 5, 7, 12), and about AD 70 Rome destroyed Jerusalem and its temple.

Under His wings

After all the woes, this: open arms. A hen spreads her wings over her chicks when danger comes, and takes the hit herself.

“How often would I… and ye would not!” Jesus wanted them; they refused. God doesn't drag anyone in, and a man can say no.

But the wings are still open today. Whatever you've done, there's room under them.

He walks out

“Your house” is the temple. Not “My Father's house” any more (John 2:16), but yours. Desolate means left empty. In the very next verse, He leaves it:

“And Jesus went out, and departed from the temple: and his disciples came to him for to shew him the buildings of the temple.”

Matthew 24:1

His last public teaching in Matthew, but not a slammed door. “Blessed is he that cometh” is from Psalm 118:26, the welcome the crowds shouted on Sunday (21:9). Jesus gives no date. He says a day is coming when His people will welcome Him.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Points to remember

  1. Leaders“All therefore whatsoever they bid you observe, that observe and do; but do not ye after their works” 23:3Keep God's Word, even from imperfect teachers. Don't copy what they do.
  2. Show“All their works they do for to be seen of men” 23:5Doing God's things for men's eyes misses God.
  3. Greatness“He that is greatest among you shall be your servant” 23:11The way up is down. God lifts the humble.
  4. Weightier matters“Judgment, mercy, and faith” 23:23Don't strain at gnats and swallow camels. Justice, mercy, and faithfulness come first.
  5. Inside“Cleanse first that which is within the cup and platter” 23:26God starts on the inside. A clean heart changes the outside.
  6. His heart“How often would I have gathered thy children together… and ye would not!” 23:37Jesus wanted to gather them, and they refused. His wings are still open to anyone who will come.

Question 1 of 6

Leaders

What did Jesus tell the people to do with what the scribes and Pharisees taught?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

When they taught the law of Moses, the people were to observe and do it, because it was God's Word. But they were not to follow their example, because they said and did not.

Question 2 of 6

Show

Why did the scribes and Pharisees do their works?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To be seen of men. Big phylacteries, long tassels, the best seats, and being called Rabbi in the market. Their religion was a show for other people's eyes, not service to God.

Question 3 of 6

Greatness

Who does Jesus say is greatest among us?

What verses tell us about this?

“Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up.”

James 4:10

Answer

The servant. The man who exalts himself will be brought low, and the man who humbles himself will be lifted up by God. Jesus showed it by washing His disciples' feet.

Question 4 of 6

Weightier matters

What weightier matters of the law had they left undone?

What verses tell us about this?

“He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”

Micah 6:8

Answer

Judgment, mercy, and faith: doing right by people, showing kindness, and being faithful. They tithed their garden herbs but ignored these. Jesus said to do both, without leaving the weightier things undone.

Question 5 of 6

Inside

Which must be cleansed first, the inside or the outside?

What verses tell us about this?

“Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.”

Psalm 51:10

Answer

The inside. A polished outside can hide a heart full of greed. When God cleans the heart, the outside is made clean too. Only He can do that work.

Question 6 of 6

His heart

What did Jesus long to do for Jerusalem, and why didn't it happen?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

He longed to gather them like a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, again and again. It didn't happen because they would not come. God isn't willing that any should perish, but He doesn't force anyone. Today His wings are still open.
