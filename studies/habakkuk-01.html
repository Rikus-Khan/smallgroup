Habakkuk 1

How long, LORD?

A prophet looks at the wrong all around him and asks God why He does nothing. God's answer is harder than the question.

The book of Habakkuk

Who: Habakkuk, “the prophet.” Little else is known. His name seems to mean “embrace,” or “cling.”

When: about 610–600 BC, before the exile. Assyria had fallen. Babylon, the land of the Chaldeans, was rising fast.

To whom: Judah, the southern kingdom, with its capital, Jerusalem.

Most prophets speak to the people for God. Habakkuk speaks to God, and argues with Him.

  1. 722 BCAssyria takes Israel
  2. 612Nineveh falls to Babylon
  3. about 610–600Habakkuk
  4. 605Babylon wins; first captives taken
  5. 586Babylon burns Jerusalem

Part one

How long?

Verses 1–4

A man of God looks at his own country and can't understand why God lets it go on.

A heavy word

מַשָּׂא said massa a load, something lifted and carried; a message from God

A “burden” was a prophet's message, often a hard one. Habakkuk carried it. And the first thing he does with it is take it back to God with a question. Here is how the whole book goes:

  1. Habakkuk“How long?”1:2
  2. The LORD“I raise up the Chaldeans”1:6
  3. Habakkuk“Wherefore?”1:13
  4. The LORD“The just shall live by his faith”2:4
  5. Habakkuk“Yet I will rejoice”3:18

What he saw

This is Judah, God's own people, near the end. Most likely good King Josiah had already died in battle (609 BC), and the kings after him let everything slide. Habakkuk looks around Jerusalem and sees:

  1. violencesaid twice, in verses 2 and 3
  2. spoilingmen robbing and ruining others
  3. strifefights and quarrels everywhere
  4. the law slackedgone numb, limp, nobody obeys it
  5. judgment bentthe wicked surround the righteous in court, and justice comes out crooked

He has prayed about it, and nothing has changed. So he says it straight: How long? Why? And God put that prayer in the Bible.

“How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD? for ever? how long wilt thou hide thy face from me?”

Psalm 13:1

Pour it out

Some men were raised to think you can't talk to God like that. Habakkuk didn't pretend. He didn't walk away from God either. He brought his complaint to God, and then he waited for an answer.

  • act like everything's fineGod already knows it isn't
  • walk away angrythat's where the drink and the old life are waiting
  • take it to Him, honestly“O LORD, how long?”

“Trust in him at all times; ye people, pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us. Selah.”

Psalm 62:8

Honest questions aren't unbelief. A man who asks God “why?” is still talking to God.

Part two

I will work a work

Verses 5–11

God answers. He has been working all along. Habakkuk just won't like how.

Not the answer he wanted

God was not silent. He was not asleep. He was already at work, in a way nobody would believe: He would use the Chaldeans of Babylon, a “bitter and hasty” nation, to judge Judah.

Habakkuk asks“Why don't you deal with the wrong in Judah?”
→
God answers“I am. I'm sending Babylon.”

“For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways, saith the LORD. For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts.”

Isaiah 55:8–9

Paul quoted verse 5 in a synagogue in Antioch, about something even harder to believe: God raising Jesus from the dead and offering forgiveness through Him.

“Behold, ye despisers, and wonder, and perish: for I work a work in your days, a work which ye shall in no wise believe, though a man declare it unto you.”

Acts 13:41

Built to hunt

God describes Babylon's army the way a herdsman would describe a predator:

  1. the leopardfaster than one
  2. the evening wolfhungry all day, hunting at dusk
  3. the eaglediving on its food
  4. the east windthe scorching desert wind that withers everything
  5. the sandcaptives gathered up like it

“Their judgment and their dignity shall proceed of themselves” means they answer to nobody but themselves. They laugh at kings. A walled city is a joke to them: they “heap dust,” piling up an earth ramp against the wall and walking right over it.

God knew exactly how cruel they were. Using them did not mean He approved of them, as verse 11 and chapter 2 make clear.

Part three

Purer eyes

Verses 12–17

Now Habakkuk has a bigger question. How can a holy God use men worse than the ones He's judging?

What I know, what I see

What he sees

It doesn't add up

The wicked “devoureth the man that is more righteous than he,” and God seems to hold His tongue.

What he knows

Who God is

From everlasting. My God. My Holy One. Purer eyes than to look on evil. The Rock (the Hebrew of “mighty God”).

Watch what Habakkuk does. Before he asks his hard question, he goes back to what he knows for sure about God. That's where a man stands when he can't see the way forward.

“The eternal God is thy refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms: and he shall thrust out the enemy from before thee; and shall say, Destroy them.”

Deuteronomy 33:27

Correction, not the end

Will Judah be wiped out?No“We shall not die.” God had made promises to David's house, and He would keep them.
Then what is Babylon for?Correction“Ordained them for judgment … established them for correction.” Painful, but meant to bring His people back.

God disciplines those who belong to Him. For a believer in Jesus, that's never God taking salvation back; that's secure. It's a Father dealing with His sons.

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Men caught like fish

Habakkuk pictures Babylon as a fisherman and the nations as fish: helpless, with no ruler to protect them. The “angle” is a fishhook. The “drag” is a dragnet pulled through the water to haul in everything at once.

Then the fisherman bows down to his net. Babylon worshipped the very thing that made it rich: its army, its power. Verse 11 says the same: “imputing this his power unto his god.” His strength was his god.

Bowing to the net

When somethingfills my plate, gets me through, makes me feel strong…
→
it's easy tostart bowing to it

Most men never burn incense to a net. But a man can serve the hustle, the money, his own toughness, or the bottle that made him feel ten feet tall. Whatever he trusts to fill him becomes his god.

“Mortify therefore your members which are upon the earth; fornication, uncleanness, inordinate affection, evil concupiscence, and covetousness, which is idolatry:”

Colossians 3:5

The chapter ends on a question: will they just keep emptying the net forever? Habakkuk doesn't know. In chapter 2 he climbs up and waits for God's answer.

Points to remember

  1. Honest prayer“O LORD, how long shall I cry” 1:2Bring the hard questions to God, not away from Him. He put this prayer in the Bible.
  2. God's silence“Thou wilt not hear!” 1:2God seemed silent, but He was already at work. Keep praying, and keep listening.
  3. God's ways“I will work a work in your days” 1:5God's answers can surprise us. His ways are higher than ours.
  4. Who God is“Art thou not from everlasting … mine Holy One?” 1:12When you can't see what God is doing, stand on who He is.
  5. Correction“Thou hast established them for correction” 1:12God's discipline is meant to bring His people back, not to destroy them.
  6. False gods“They sacrifice unto their net” 1:16Whatever a man trusts to fill him becomes his god, and it can't save him.

Question 1 of 6

Honest prayer

Is it all right to tell God exactly how we feel?

What verses tell us about this?

“Trust in him at all times; ye people, pour out your heart before him: God is a refuge for us. Selah.”

Psalm 62:8

Answer

Yes. Habakkuk asked “how long?” and “why?” and God answered him instead of rebuking him. Honest prayer is still prayer. The danger isn't bringing our questions to God. It's taking them somewhere else, and walking away from Him.

Question 2 of 6

God's silence

What do we do when God seems silent?

What verses tell us about this?

“How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD? for ever? how long wilt thou hide thy face from me?”

Psalm 13:1

Answer

Keep talking to Him, and keep listening. To Habakkuk, God seemed deaf, but He was already working on an answer. Silence from heaven isn't absence. In chapter 2, Habakkuk climbs his watchtower and waits.

Question 3 of 6

God's ways

Does God always answer the way we expect?

What verses tell us about this?

“For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways, saith the LORD. For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts.”

Isaiah 55:8–9

Answer

No. Habakkuk wanted God to fix Judah. God said He would, by sending Babylon. God always answers, but He answers as God, seeing what we can't. That's hard, and it's also why we can trust Him.

Question 4 of 6

Who God is

What do we hold on to when we don't understand?

What verses tell us about this?

“The eternal God is thy refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms: and he shall thrust out the enemy from before thee; and shall say, Destroy them.”

Deuteronomy 33:27

Answer

Who God is. Habakkuk didn't understand what God was doing, so he went back to what he knew: God is everlasting, He is holy, He is “my God.” Our questions don't change who He is. When the ground shakes, stand on that.

Question 5 of 6

Correction

Why would God correct His own people?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Answer

Because He loves them. Judah had walked away, and God sent correction to bring them back, not to wipe them out: “we shall not die.” A believer's salvation is secure in Christ, but a good Father still deals with His sons when they go wrong.

Question 6 of 6

False gods

What happens when a man worships what makes him strong?

What verses tell us about this?

“Mortify therefore your members which are upon the earth; fornication, uncleanness, inordinate affection, evil concupiscence, and covetousness, which is idolatry:”

Colossians 3:5

Answer

It becomes his god, and it turns on him. Babylon bowed to the net that fed it, and chapter 2 shows how that ended. Money, power, toughness, or a bottle can all take God's place. None of them can save a man. Only the Lord can.
