Genesis 2

Dust, breath, and a garden

God rests from his work. Then he forms a man out of the dust, breathes life into him, gives him a garden, a job, one command, and a wife.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. RestWhy did God rest on the seventh day, and what rest does he offer us?
  2. ManWhat is a man made of?
  3. WorkWas work part of God's good plan, or a punishment?
  4. SinWhat did God warn would happen if man disobeyed him?
  5. AloneWhy is it not good for a man to be alone?
  6. MarriageWhat did God make marriage to be?

Part one

The seventh day

Verses 1–3

The work is finished, and God stops.

God rested

שָׁבַת said shavat “rested”: stopped, ceased. Our word sabbath comes from it.
  1. hostthe great army of things that fill heaven and earth
  2. endedfinished, brought to a close
  3. sanctifiedset apart as holy, for God

God wasn't tired. He rested because the work was finished, and nothing needed adding. The seventh day has no “evening and morning” to close it. And it's the first thing in the Bible that God calls holy: not a place or a thing, but a day.

A rest made for man

Later God built this into Israel's law: six days of work, and a seventh to stop. Jesus said who it was for:

“And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:”

Mark 2:27

A man who never stops will break. Many of us ran hard for years, chasing the next high, the next dollar, the next escape, and never once rested. God's rest goes deeper than a day off. It's rest from trying to earn your way with him:

“There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his.”

Hebrews 4:9–10

Part two

Dust and breath

Verses 4–9

Now the camera comes in close, to day six and one man.

“The LORD God”

Chapter 1

God

The mighty Creator. He speaks, and the whole universe appears.

Chapter 2

The LORD God

His own name. He forms a man with his hands and breathes into him.

When the KJV prints LORD in capitals, it stands for God's own personal name, the one he gave Moses at the burning bush (Exodus 3:14–15). Chapter 2 isn't a second, different story. It comes in close on day six, and shows the great Creator is also a personal God who comes near.

  1. generationshere: the account, the story of
  2. tillwork the soil, farm it

Made of dust

אָדָם said adam, “man”; the ground is adamah Man is named after the ground he came from.

“Formed” is a potter's word: the LORD shaped the man with care, like a craftsman with clay. He is the Maker, and we are dust. That keeps a man humble. It also means God is never surprised by how weak we are:

“For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust.”

Psalm 103:14

The breath of life

The animals came when God spoke. The man got God's own breath, face to face. That's what makes him a “living soul”: not just a body, but a man who can know his Maker.

Paul calls Jesus “the last Adam.” The first Adam received life; Jesus gives it:

“And so it is written, The first man Adam was made a living soul; the last Adam was made a quickening spirit.”

1 Corinthians 15:45

After he rose, Jesus did it again. He breathed on his disciples and said, “Receive ye the Holy Ghost” (John 20:22). Every believer has the Spirit of God living in him. A man who was dead inside can live.

Two trees

God planted the garden himself, and filled it with trees “pleasant to the sight, and good for food.” Eden probably means delight.

Verse 9

The tree of life

Life with God, for ever. Lost in chapter 3, and given back in the last chapter of the Bible.

Verses 9, 17

The tree of knowledge of good and evil

The one tree God said no to. Man would learn evil by doing it.

“In the midst of the street of it, and on either side of the river, was there the tree of life, which bare twelve manner of fruits, and yielded her fruit every month: and the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations.”

Revelation 22:2

Part three

To dress it and to keep it

Verses 10–17

A real place, a real job, and one command.

Four rivers

Moses names real rivers. Two we still know: the Euphrates, and the Hiddekel, the Tigris, which runs past Assyria. Daniel stood by it centuries later (Daniel 10:4).

The Pison and the Gihon are lost. So is Havilah. “Ethiopia” here is the Hebrew Cush. The flood in chapter 7 broke up the earth, so no one can point to the garden's spot today.

The point is that Eden is not a fairy tale. It was a real place, in a real world, and the first man had a real address.

Gold, bdellium and onyx

  1. thencefrom there
  2. headsbranches, separate rivers
  3. compassethwinds around
  4. bdelliuma sweet-smelling resin (some think pearls)
  5. onyxa precious stone

Why mention gold and stones? Because Israel would see them again. Gold covered the tabernacle, and the high priest wore onyx stones on his shoulders, engraved with the names of Israel's sons (Exodus 28:9). God's house would be built from Eden's treasures, as if to say: this is where I meet My people again.

Work came first

  1. dresstend it, work it
  2. keepguard it, take care of it

Adam had a job before he ever sinned. Work is not the curse. Sin made it hard and sweaty (chapter 3), but the work itself was God's gift: a man with something worth doing and Someone to answer to.

“And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men;”

Colossians 3:23

If you have a job today, go do it for the Lord. If you're looking for one, that search is work too. Do it heartily.

One “no”

Verse 16

“Freely eat”

Every tree of the garden. A world of yes.

Verse 17

“Thou shalt not eat”

One tree. And God says plainly what it will cost.

God put the yes first, and it was huge. The no was small, and it came with a warning out of love. The day Adam ate, death came in: he was cut off from God and hid from him, and his body began to die (5:5).

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

Part four

Not good to be alone

Verses 18–25

Seven times God said “good.” Now, for the first time, he says “not good.”

An help meet for him

עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ said ezer kenegdo “a help meet for him”: a helper who fits him, who matches him

Meet is the old word for fit or suitable. And help is no small word. The Bible uses it of God himself:

“I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help. My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth.”

Psalm 121:1–2

Adam named every animal, and none of them was like him. God let him feel the lack before he met it.

Two are better than one

Before sin ever came into the world, being alone was already “not good.” A man was not built to make it on his own. That's true in marriage, and it's true for every man in this room, married or not.

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

Isolation is where a lot of us fell. The enemy loves a man alone. When you're struggling, don't wait it out by yourself: call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor. That's not weakness. That's how God built us.

Bone of my bones

This is now bone of my bones,

and flesh of my flesh:

Adam's first recorded words are a poem. After all the animals, here at last is one like him. “This is now!” And her name says it too: in Hebrew, woman is ishshah, taken out of man, ish.

The old preacher Matthew Henry noticed that she wasn't taken from Adam's head, to rule over him, or from his feet, to be walked on, but from his side: to be his equal, under his arm to be protected, near his heart to be loved.

And God himself “brought her unto the man,” like a father walking his daughter down the aisle. The first wedding had God as its giver.

Leave, cleave, one flesh

leavea new home firstcleavehold fast to herone flesh

Cleave means to hold fast, to stick to. Jesus quoted this verse when men asked him about divorce, and added: “What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder” (Matthew 19:6). Paul says it pictures Christ and his church (Ephesians 5:31–32).

Some of us have broken this, badly. The design is still good, and so is God's forgiveness. Start where you are: be faithful today, to her or to the one God may give you.

Not ashamed

Nothing to hide, from God or from each other. That's how God made man. In the very next chapter, sin comes in, and the first thing they feel is shame. They cover up, and they hide.

Shame is not how you were made to live. Jesus carried it for us:

“Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God.”

Hebrews 12:2

“For the scripture saith, Whosoever believeth on him shall not be ashamed.”

Romans 10:11

Points to remember

  1. Rest“He rested on the seventh day from all his work” 2:2God rested because the work was finished. In Christ, we rest from earning our way.
  2. Man“The dust of the ground … the breath of life” 2:7Dust, and God's own breath. Humble, and made to know him.
  3. Work“To dress it and to keep it” 2:15Work was God's gift before sin ever came in.
  4. Sin“Thou shalt surely die” 2:17Sin costs life. God warned us out of love, and in Jesus he gives life back.
  5. Alone“It is not good that the man should be alone” 2:18A man isn't built to fight alone. Call a brother.
  6. Marriage“They shall be one flesh” 2:24One man, one woman, joined by God, held fast for life.

Question 1 of 6

Rest

Why did God rest on the seventh day, and what rest does he offer us?

What verses tell us about this?

“There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his.”

Hebrews 4:9–10

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

Not because he was tired, but because his work was finished. Jesus finished the work of saving us on the cross, so the man who trusts him can stop trying to earn God's favour and rest in what Jesus has done. And a weekly day to stop and remember God is still wise for a man who runs too hard.

Question 2 of 6

Man

What is a man made of?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust.”

Psalm 103:14

“And when he had said this, he breathed on them, and saith unto them, Receive ye the Holy Ghost:”

John 20:22

Answer

Dust, and the breath of God. The dust keeps us humble: we're weak, and God knows it and isn't shocked by it. The breath makes us more than animals: we're made to know him. And Jesus breathes new life into everyone who believes, by his Spirit who lives in us.

Question 3 of 6

Work

Was work part of God's good plan, or a punishment?

What verses tell us about this?

“And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men;”

Colossians 3:23

Answer

Part of the good plan. God gave Adam work in a perfect garden, before there was any sin. Sin made work hard, but it didn't make work bad. Whatever honest work you have today, do it for the Lord, and it becomes worship.

Question 4 of 6

Sin

What did God warn would happen if man disobeyed him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:”

Romans 5:12

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

Answer

Death. God was honest about it from the start, and he was right: sin always costs more than it promises. But the same God who warned of death sent his Son to give life. Death is what sin earns; eternal life is God's free gift, for anyone who believes.

Question 5 of 6

Alone

Why is it not good for a man to be alone?

What verses tell us about this?

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Answer

Because God made us for each other. Even in a perfect world, alone was “not good.” In a fallen world it's dangerous: the man who falls alone has no one to help him up. Stay close to your brothers in Christ. When you're struggling, call or text them, and your pastor, before you fall, not after.

Question 6 of 6

Marriage

What did God make marriage to be?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he answered and said unto them, Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female, And said, For this cause shall a man leave father and mother, and shall cleave to his wife: and they twain shall be one flesh? Wherefore they are no more twain, but one flesh. What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder.”

Matthew 19:4–6

Answer

One man and one woman, brought together by God, leaving their old homes, holding fast to each other, and becoming one for life. Paul says it's also a picture of Christ and his church (Ephesians 5:32). Where we have failed at it, he forgives; from today, we can be faithful.
