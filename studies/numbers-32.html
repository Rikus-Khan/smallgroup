Numbers 32

Be sure your sin will find you out

Reuben and Gad see good grazing east of the Jordan and ask to stay. Moses hears their fathers at Kadesh in it. They promise to cross over armed with their brethren, and Moses holds them to their word.

Part one

“Bring us not over Jordan”

Verses 1–15

Two tribes ask to stay, and Moses hears an old story.

A land for cattle

These towns were in the country Israel had just taken from Sihon king of the Amorites, “from Arnon unto Jabbok” (21:24–26), with Heshbon his chief city. Good grazing country: “a place for cattle” (verse 1). Reuben and Gad had “a very great multitude of cattle”, and they looked, and they wanted it.

Asking wasn't wrong in itself; Moses gave it to them in the end. But listen to the last words: “bring us not over Jordan.” The land God promised was on the other side.

They saw, and they chose

Lot, Genesis 13:10–11

“And Lot lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered every where … Then Lot chose him all the plain of Jordan; and Lot journeyed east”

Reuben and Gad, Numbers 32:1

“when they saw the land of Jazer, and the land of Gilead, that, behold, the place was a place for cattle”

Both looked at the Jordan country with an eye for their herds. Neither one asked first what God wanted. It's the most natural thing in the world to choose by what's good for my stuff, my comfort, my setup. A man can do it without ever thinking he is doing anything wrong.

“Shall ye sit here?”

Shall your brethren go to war, and shall ye sit here?

Moses has heard this before. About thirty-eight years earlier, ten men came back from the land and talked the whole nation out of going in:

“But the men that went up with him said, We be not able to go up against the people; for they are stronger than we.”

Numbers 13:31

Discourage the heart: take the heart out of men, so they lose their nerve. If Reuben and Gad sat down on the east bank, the other tribes would ask, why should we cross? Moses wasn't only worried about Reuben and Gad. He was worried about what their sitting would do to everyone else.

Wholly followed

Every man twenty and up

“not wholly followed me”

Followed out of Egypt, followed to the border, and stopped. They “shall not see the land” (32:11).

Caleb and Joshua

“wholly followed the LORD”

All the way, when everyone around them quit. They were the only two of that generation to go in (26:65).

Wholly means all of it, with nothing held back. Kenezite: of the family of Kenaz. Forty-five years after Kadesh, Caleb was still standing on that word:

“Hebron therefore became the inheritance of Caleb the son of Jephunneh the Kenezite unto this day, because that he wholly followed the LORD God of Israel.”

Joshua 14:14

In your fathers' stead

“Ye are risen up in your fathers’ stead.” Stead is place. Forty years, a whole generation buried in the sand, and here are the sons, standing where their fathers stood and about to say what their fathers said. Augment: add to, make greater.

“But with whom was he grieved forty years? was it not with them that had sinned, whose carcases fell in the wilderness? And to whom sware he that they should not enter into his rest, but to them that believed not? So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief.”

Hebrews 3:17–19

Many of us watched our fathers drink, or run, or rage, and swore we'd never be them. Then one day we heard their words coming out of our own mouths. That pattern is real. But it is not a life sentence:

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Part two

“Be sure your sin will find you out”

Verses 16–27

They make a promise, and Moses tells them what a broken one will cost.

Not leaving the brothers to fight alone

To their credit, they heard Moses. They didn't sulk or walk off. They came near and offered more than he asked: settle the families behind walls, then go over in front, at the head of the army, and not come home until the last brother has his land.

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Before whom?

They offered to go “before the children of Israel” (verse 17). Moses took their words and turned them: go armed “before the LORD.” Then he said it again, and again. Verses 17 to 32, and where “before the LORD” comes:

17 18 19  20×121×122×3 23 24 25 26  27×128 29×1 30 31 32×1

Eight times. And by verse 27 they are saying it themselves. A promise to your brothers is also a promise made in front of God. Guiltless: free of blame, clear.

Your sin will find you

Notice what sin Moses means: not doing what you said you'd do. Sitting here while your brothers go to war. A sin of leaving undone is still sin (James 4:17). And Moses doesn't say “you will be found out”, as if the trouble is getting caught. He says your sin will find you. It follows a man home.

There is one way to have your sin found out and live: bring it out yourself, for “whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy” (Proverbs 28:13).

Then what was hidden is gone, and in its place there is forgiveness.

What you sow

“Therefore to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin.”

James 4:17

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Most of us have learned this one the hard way: the lie that came out at the worst time, the bill that came due, the body that finally gave out, the kids who remember. Sin keeps an address. It knows where you live.

That is a warning. It is also a mercy. God lets sin come looking for us so we will stop hiding and come to him, while there is still mercy to be had:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

First things first

They saidverse 16sheepfolds … for our cattle→cities for our little ones
Moses saidverse 24cities for your little ones→folds for your sheep

They named the cattle first. Moses gave the same two things back the other way round: the children first, then the sheep. Moses doesn't stop to point it out, but it's there in the order of his words.

Then: “do that which hath proceeded out of your mouth.” Two chapters back, the LORD had said the same of any man who makes a vow:

“If a man vow a vow unto the LORD, or swear an oath to bind his soul with a bond; he shall not break his word, he shall do according to all that proceedeth out of his mouth.”

Numbers 30:2

“But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil.”

Matthew 5:37

Part three

“Moses gave unto them”

Verses 28–42

The deal is set before witnesses, and the east side is given and built.

Said in front of witnesses

Moses knew he wouldn't be there when the army crossed (31:2; 27:13). So he put the agreement in front of the men who would be: Eleazar the priest, Joshua the son of Nun, and the heads of the tribes. If Reuben and Gad didn't cross, those men would know, and the east side would not be theirs; they would take their portion “among you in the land of Canaan.”

That isn't distrust. It's wisdom. A promise that only you and God know about is easy to let slide. A promise your brothers know about has help keeping it.

If you've told God you're done with something, tell a brother too, and give him leave to ask you about it.

Two kingdoms, two and a half tribes

Sihon of Heshbon in the south and Og of Bashan in the north had both come out to fight Israel and lost (21:21–35). Their kingdoms now went to Reuben, Gad, and, named here for the first time, half the tribe of Manasseh. The other half of Manasseh would inherit west of the Jordan (the map shows both).

“And the LORD said unto Moses, Fear him not: for I have delivered him into thy hand, and all his people, and his land; and thou shalt do to him as thou didst unto Sihon king of the Amorites, which dwelt at Heshbon.”

Numbers 21:34

Coasts: borders, the country round about.

Most of these are the towns they had asked for in verse 3. Now they build them up: walls for the families, folds for the sheep, as Moses said, children first.

“Their names being changed.” Moses doesn't say why. Two of the names were the names of idols: Baal, whom Israel had just been joined to at Peor (25:3), and Nebo (Isaiah 46:1). A man who belongs to the LORD doesn't have to keep living under the names of his old masters.

The chapter that began with men who wanted to sit down ends with men who got up and went. Same side of the river, different hearts. And Reuben and Gad did keep their word:

They kept it

“And the children of Reuben, and the children of Gad, and half the tribe of Manasseh, passed over armed before the children of Israel, as Moses spake unto them:”

Joshua 4:12

Years of war in Canaan, with wives and children waiting in Gilead. And when it was over, Joshua sent them home with this:

“And said unto them, Ye have kept all that Moses the servant of the LORD commanded you, and have obeyed my voice in all that I commanded you: Ye have not left your brethren these many days unto this day, but have kept the charge of the commandment of the LORD your God.”

Joshua 22:2–3

The sons broke the fathers' pattern. Their sin didn't find them out, because they didn't commit it. “Ye have not left your brethren.” There is no better thing to have said of a man.

Real world: don't sit here

Every man in this room has made promises he didn't keep, and most of us have watched a sin we thought was buried come walking back in the door. This chapter doesn't let us off, and it doesn't leave us there.

  • SinChoosing by what's good for my stuff, and sitting down while my brothers fight. Not doing is still sin.
  • Regret“Risen up in your fathers’ stead”: repeating what we swore we never would.
  • GraceMoses didn't throw them out. He gave them a way, and redemption reaches past what we got from our fathers.
  • HopeThe sons crossed over and kept every word. “Ye have not left your brethren.”
  • TodayKeep one promise you've let slide. And show up for one brother who is in a fight right now.

Question 1 of 6

A place for cattle

What did the children of Reuben and Gad see, and what did they ask for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The land of Jazer and Gilead, good country for their great herds. They asked to have it, and not to be brought over Jordan.

Question 2 of 6

Your fathers

What did Moses fear they were doing all over again?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

What their fathers did at Kadesh-barnea: sitting down and taking the heart out of the rest of Israel, so the whole nation would turn back from the land.

Question 3 of 6

What verses tell us about this?

“Hebron therefore became the inheritance of Caleb the son of Jephunneh the Kenezite unto this day, because that he wholly followed the LORD God of Israel.”

Joshua 14:14

Answer

Caleb and Joshua. Of all the men who came out of Egypt, they alone would see the land, and Caleb received Hebron for it.

Question 4 of 6

Before the LORD

What did they promise their brethren, and before whom would they keep it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To cross over armed at the front and not go home until every tribe had its land. Moses made it a promise before the LORD, and they took up his words.
