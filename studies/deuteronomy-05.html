Deuteronomy 5

The ten commandments, given again to the children

In the fortieth year, Moses tells the sons what God said to their fathers, and says it is theirs: “with us, even us, who are all of us here alive this day.” And God says of a frightened people, “O that there were such an heart in them.”

Part one

“With us, even us”

Verses 1–5

The covenant made at the mountain belongs to the men standing in Moab.

Horeb then, Moab now

  1. Horebthe mountain of God, Mount Sinai, where the LORD spoke the ten words out of the fire (verse 4)
  2. Moab“on this side Jordan, in the land of Moab” (1:5), over against Jericho, “in the fortieth year” (1:3)

The men who heard the voice at the mountain fell in the wilderness, as we saw in Numbers 14. Most of the men listening now were boys then, or not yet born.

Not an old story about dead men

“with us, even us, who are all of us here alive this day”

Moses puts all the weight on the living: most of these men were boys at Horeb, or not yet born, yet he says the covenant was made with them. The covenant is not a piece of family history about men buried in the sand. It is made with whoever is alive and listening today.

Some of you were raised around church, and it all belonged to your mother or your grandmother. Some of you were raised around nothing. Either way, God is not speaking to your family's past. He is speaking to you, today.

“But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

Hebrews 3:13

Face to face, and a man between

Both are true. The LORD spoke to all Israel himself, “face to face… out of the midst of the fire”: they heard his voice, not a rumour of it. And Moses “stood between the LORD and you”, because “ye were afraid by reason of the fire, and went not up into the mount”.

We saw the people back away in Exodus 20. Here Moses tells their sons he was the man in the gap. He will tell them later in this chapter that the people asked for it, and that God said they were right to (verses 23–28).

“For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;”

1 Timothy 2:5

A mediator is a go-between. Moses stood between for a while, and then he died. Christ stands there still.

Part two

“These words the LORD spake”

Verses 6–22

The same ten words, preached again in the fortieth year, with a few things Moses adds for the children.

The sabbath, side by side

Exodus 20:11

For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it.

Deuteronomy 5:15

And remember that thou wast a servant in the land of Egypt, and that the LORD thy God brought thee out thence through a mighty hand and by a stretched out arm: therefore the LORD thy God commanded thee to keep the sabbath day.

We saw the sabbath taught in Exodus 20. There the reason is creation: God made the world and rested. Here Moses gives the children a second reason, and it is their own story: you were a slave, and God brought you out. Both are true. The God who made you is the God who set you free.

A servant here is a bondman, a slave. To sanctify the day is to set it apart as holy. A stretched out arm is the LORD’S power reaching in to save.

“Remember that thou wast a servant”

A slave never gets a day off. That was Egypt. So the day of rest was a weekly reminder: you are not owned any more. And Moses adds who else rests: “that thy manservant and thy maidservant may rest as well as thou.” A man who remembers being a slave must not work his own servant to the bone.

Moses says “remember that thou wast a servant” (or “a bondman”) five times in Deuteronomy: here, and in 15:15, 16:12, 24:18 and 24:22. Every time, it is a reason to treat other people with mercy.

“Jesus answered them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin.”

John 8:34

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

You know what it is to be owned: by a bottle, a pipe, a habit that set your hours and spent your money. Remember it. Not to wallow, but to stay grateful, and to be patient with the next man still in chains.

Moses is not changing the law. He is preaching it to sons, and he points back: “as the LORD thy God hath commanded thee.” He puts the wife first, by herself, with her own word, desire: she is not one more thing on a list of goods. And he names the field, because they are about to be farmers in a land of their own.

“He added no more”

The LORD spoke the ten himself, “with a great voice”, to the whole assembly, and stopped. Then he wrote those same words on two tables of stone and gave them to Moses. The rest of the law came through Moses; these ten God spoke and wrote himself.

“Ye shall not add unto the word which I command you, neither shall ye diminish ought from it, that ye may keep the commandments of the LORD your God which I command you.”

Deuteronomy 4:2

Nobody gets to add an eleventh, and nobody gets to scratch one out. These ten God spoke with his own voice and wrote with his own hand. Ought means anything.

Part three

“O that there were such an heart in them”

Verses 23–33

The people ask for a man between, God agrees, and then God tells Moses what he really wants.

“They have well said”

The heads of the tribes and the eldersGo thou near, and hear all that the LORD our God shall say: and speak thou unto us… and we will hear it, and do it.

The LORDThey have well said all that they have spoken.

They had seen something true: “God doth talk with man, and he liveth.” And something else true: sinful men cannot stand long in that fire. So they asked for a man to go between. God did not scold them. He said they were right.

The Prophet like unto Moses

Moses tells the rest of it in chapter 18. God's answer to that day at Horeb was a promise:

“I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee, and will put my words in his mouth; and he shall speak unto them all that I shall command him.”

Deuteronomy 18:18

“For Moses truly said unto the fathers, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear in all things whatsoever he shall say unto you.”

Acts 3:22

Peter says who that Prophet is: Jesus. The men at Horeb asked for a go-between, and God sent his Son.

What God wanted most

O that there were such an heart in them

“We will hear it, and do it,” they said, and they meant it. God heard the promise, and he knew the heart behind it was not strong enough to keep it. Before Moses came down from the mount with the tables, they had made the golden calf (Exodus 32). This is not God sneering at them. It is God wishing: “that it might be well with them, and with their children for ever!”

“O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets, and stonest them which are sent unto thee, how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not!”

Matthew 23:37

The same longing, in the mouth of Jesus. God does not want your good behaviour more than he wants you. He wants a heart that loves him, and he wants it to go well with you.

The heart God gives

At Horeb

“O that there were such an heart”

Good words, a willing promise, “we will hear it, and do it”, and a heart that could not keep it. The law was written on stone, outside them.

The new covenant

“A new heart also will I give you”

What God wished for, he promised to do himself: “A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you” (Ezekiel 36:26).

“For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, saith the Lord; I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts: and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people:”

Hebrews 8:10

Most of us have made the promise: this time I'll do it. And they have broken it. The answer is not a louder promise. It is a new heart, and the Spirit of God living in it. Every man who has trusted Christ has been given both.

Not to the right hand, nor to the left

The people went home to their tents; Moses stayed by the LORD to hear the rest (verses 30–31). Then he tells them how to walk: straight down the road, not off either side.

Think of two ditches. One is the old life: doing what the law forbids. The other is adding rules and pride, making yourself righteous by your own strictness. A man fresh out of one ditch often runs straight into the other.

The same road for Joshua

God said the same thing to Joshua, after Moses died. The road had a new leader and the same directions.

“Only be thou strong and very courageous, that thou mayest observe to do according to all the law, which Moses my servant commanded thee: turn not from it to the right hand or to the left, that thou mayest prosper whithersoever thou goest.”

Joshua 1:7

Prosper here is not a promise of money. It means to go well, to succeed at what God gave him to do. Joshua would need it: he had a river to cross and a land to take. The way to stay strong was to stay on the road.

Real world: the covenant is yours today

  • SinWe say “we will hear it, and do it”, and mean it, and fall anyway. A promise is not a new heart.
  • RegretOur fathers' failures are real, and so are ours. But God spoke to the sons “alive this day”: the past does not shut you out.
  • GraceGod gave a go-between, and then his own Son, “one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus” (1 Timothy 2:5). He wants it to “go well” with you.
  • HopeWhat God longed for at Horeb, he promised to give: a new heart and his own Spirit within (Ezekiel 36:26–27).
  • TodayStop for one hour today, and spend it remembering what God brought you out of. Then do one kind thing for a man who is still where you were.

Points to remember

  1. With us“but with us, even us, who are all of us here alive this day” 5:3The covenant is not old family history. God speaks to whoever is alive and listening today.
  2. The man between“I stood between the LORD and you at that time” 5:5Moses stood between because the people feared the fire. Christ is the one mediator now.
  3. The sabbath reason“remember that thou wast a servant in the land of Egypt” 5:15Rest because you are not a slave any more, and let the man under you rest too.
  4. The people's fear“they have well said all that they have spoken” 5:28They asked for a go-between, God said they were right, and he promised the Prophet like Moses.
  5. Such an heart“O that there were such an heart in them” 5:29God wants a heart that loves him, and he promised to give a new one.
  6. The way“ye shall not turn aside to the right hand or to the left” 5:32Walk straight, past both ditches, “that ye may live”.

Question 1 of 6

With us

With whom did Moses say the LORD made the covenant in Horeb?

What verses tell us about this?

“But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

Hebrews 3:13

Answer

“With us, even us, who are all of us here alive this day.” Not left behind with the fathers who fell in the wilderness, but made with their sons standing in Moab. God's word is always spoken to the living, today.

Question 2 of 6

The man between

Who stood between the LORD and the people, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;”

1 Timothy 2:5

Answer

Moses, because the people were afraid of the fire and would not go up the mountain. They asked him to go near for them. Now the one mediator between God and men is Christ Jesus.

Question 3 of 6

The sabbath reason

What reason does Moses give here for keeping the sabbath?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That they had been slaves in Egypt and the LORD brought them out. A free people rest, and they let their own servants rest as well as they. Exodus 20 gives creation as the reason; Moses adds their rescue.

Question 4 of 6

The people's fear

What did the people ask, and what did the LORD say about it?

What verses tell us about this?

“I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto thee, and will put my words in his mouth; and he shall speak unto them all that I shall command him.”

Deuteronomy 18:18

Answer

They asked Moses to go near and bring them God's words, for fear they would die in the fire. The LORD said they had spoken well, and later promised a Prophet like Moses, whom Peter names as Jesus (Acts 3:22).

Question 5 of 6

Such an heart

What did the LORD long for in his people?

What verses tell us about this?

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

Answer

A heart that would fear him and keep his commandments always, so that it would go well with them and their children for ever. What he longed for, he promised to give: a new heart and his Spirit within.

Question 6 of 6

The way

How were they to walk, and what would come of it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In all the ways the LORD commanded, turning neither to the right nor to the left, that they might live, and that it might be well with them in the land.
