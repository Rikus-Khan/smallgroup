Deuteronomy 28

The blessing and the curse

Fourteen verses of blessing for the people who hearken to the voice of the LORD, and fifty-four of curse for the people who will not: the longest chapter in the book, and the answer to what was said on the two mounts.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. The conditionWhat did the LORD ask of Israel, for the blessings to come?
  2. The blessingWhere would the blessing meet a man who hearkened?
  3. The cursesWhat was to come on the people who would not hearken?
  4. The reasonWhy did Moses say the curses would come?
  5. The scatteringWhere did Moses say the people would be carried?
  6. The curse borneWho has redeemed us from the curse of the law?

Part one

“All these blessings shall come on thee”

Verses 1–14

The mount of Gerizim, spoken out: what the LORD would do for a people that hearkened.

The word “if”

If“thou shalt hearken diligently unto the voice of the LORD thy God, to observe and to do all his commandments”
→
Then“all these blessings shall come on thee, and overtake thee”

Overtake is the picture of a blessing that runs after a man and catches him up. Hearken is to listen and to obey. Moses has just stood the people between two mounts; this is what the first mount means.

This is the covenant of the LORD with Israel, a people about to enter a land (“in the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee”, verse 8). We will see that none of them kept it, and that the answer to that is not in this chapter, but in Christ.

Every part of a man's day

  • The city and the fieldWhere he lives and where he works: all of his place.
  • Body, ground, cattleHis children, his crops, his herds: kine are cows.
  • Basket and storeWhat he carries home today, and what he has laid by for later.
  • Coming in, going outEvery errand, every door, every day.

Nothing is left out. The same four pairs return, turned around, in the curses (verses 16–19), and we will lay them side by side.

What these blessings are, and are not

Said to Israel, in Moab

Rain, harvest, victory in the land

Blessings of a covenant with a nation, “in the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee” (verses 8, 11). They are not a promise that any man who obeys will be rich or well.

Said to us, in Christ

“all spiritual blessings in heavenly places”

“Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ” (Ephesians 1:3). Kept in him, and not lost with a job.

Anyone who tells you that God will pay you for obeying him, in money or in health, has taken this chapter out of Moab and put it in the wrong place.

Not a promise of an easy life

“And he said unto them, Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man’s life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth.”

Luke 12:15

“These things I have spoken unto you, that in me ye might have peace. In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.”

John 16:33

Jesus did not promise his own an easy road. He promised peace in him, and that he has overcome the world. A believer who is poor, sick or in trouble is not for that reason a man under a curse.

Part two

“All these curses shall come upon thee”

Verses 15–35

Ebal, spoken out: the blessing turned inside out, and then worse.

Fifty-four verses, and we read every one. They are heavy. Moses reads them to a people he loves, standing at the edge of a land that was promised to them, so that none of it needs to come true.

“When heaven is shut up, and there is no rain, because they have sinned against thee; if they pray toward this place, and confess thy name, and turn from their sin, when thou afflictest them: Then hear thou in heaven, and forgive the sin of thy servants, and of thy people Israel, that thou teach them the good way wherein they should walk, and give rain upon thy land, which thou hast given to thy people for an inheritance.”

“And said, If thou wilt diligently hearken to the voice of the LORD thy God, and wilt do that which is right in his sight, and wilt give ear to his commandments, and keep all his statutes, I will put none of these diseases upon thee, which I have brought upon the Egyptians: for I am the LORD that healeth thee.”

Exodus 15:26

Part three

“A nation… from far”

Verses 36–57

Carried off, the harvest eaten, and a siege.

Old words, and the old gods

There is a bitter turn in verse 36: the people the LORD brought out of Egypt to serve him would end up serving “other gods, wood and stone”. Moses has already said what those are: “the work of men’s hands, wood and stone, which neither see, nor hear, nor eat, nor smell” (4:28). A man in recovery knows that road: the thing he served promised everything, and could do nothing.

The reason, in one verse

It is not hunger that is named, or poverty, or even a great crime. It is a heart that had everything and did not serve the LORD gladly. Moses had warned them of exactly this before:

“Lest when thou hast eaten and art full, and hast built goodly houses, and dwelt therein; And when thy herds and thy flocks multiply, and thy silver and thy gold is multiplied, and all that thou hast is multiplied; Then thine heart be lifted up, and thou forget the LORD thy God…”

Deuteronomy 8:12–14

Plenty can do to a man what trouble cannot. Many of us know the day we stopped thanking God, long before the day we fell.

Walls they trusted in

Fenced walls are walls made strong against attack, and besiege is to shut a city in with an army until it is starved or breaks. The weight of verse 52 falls on four words: “wherein thou trustedst”.

The LORD had given them the land and the strong cities (verse 52: “which the LORD thy God hath given thee”). The sin is trusting the walls, and forgetting who gave them. The Bible later says it plainly:

“Thus saith the LORD; Cursed be the man that trusteth in man, and maketh flesh his arm, and whose heart departeth from the LORD.”

Jeremiah 17:5

Whatever a man puts his weight on, other than the LORD, will one day be the thing that comes down.

The hardest verses in the chapter

straitness
a squeeze: no way out, no food
eye shall be evil toward
shall grudge, shall look at with hard, selfish eyes
tender and delicate
soft, used to every comfort

Moses says it once, and plainly, and we will not go over it again. It is what a siege can bring a people to. The Bible says it did come to this (2 Kings 6:24–29; Lamentations 4:10). The point of the verses is how far sin can carry a people, past anything they ever planned.

And note who is named: the tender and the delicate, the people who thought it could never reach them.

Part four

“The LORD shall scatter thee among all people”

Verses 58–68

The last steps down, and what the Bible shows came of them.

“Wonderful” plagues, and a hard verse

Wonderful here means full of wonder: plagues that make men stand and stare. And “as the LORD rejoiced over you to do you good… so the LORD will rejoice over you to destroy you” is the hardest line in the chapter to hold. It says that the God who delighted to bless them is the same God who will judge them, and that he means it.

But the LORD tells us how he feels about it himself:

“Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?”

Ezekiel 33:11

Read the judgment through the mercy. “Turn ye, turn ye… why will ye die?” is the voice of a God who would rather not.

“Would God it were morning!”

Look at what Moses describes in the last verses: a man who cannot rest. Life “hang in doubt”, fear “day and night”, and every evening he wishes it were morning, and every morning wishes it were evening. That is a heart that has left the LORD, and any man who has lain awake after a bad night of his own making knows it.

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

“There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment. He that feareth is not made perfect in love.”

1 John 4:18

The cure is not trying harder to sleep. It is going back to the LORD. And if the nights are long, call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor. Do not lie awake with it alone.

Jesus, in his last days before the cross, warned of a siege of Jerusalem and a captivity into all nations, and said: “these be the days of vengeance, that all things which are written may be fulfilled” (Luke 21:22). What Moses read on the mount was not an empty threat. The people could have been spared it, and they were told so beforehand.

But not utterly

“Nevertheless for thy great mercies’ sake thou didst not utterly consume them, nor forsake them; for thou art a gracious and merciful God.”

Nehemiah 9:31

Moses himself leaves the door open. He says it later in this same book: when “all these things are come upon thee, the blessing and the curse”, and the people “call them to mind”:

“And shalt return unto the LORD thy God, and shalt obey his voice according to all that I command thee this day, thou and thy children, with all thine heart, and with all thy soul; That then the LORD thy God will turn thy captivity, and have compassion upon thee, and will return and gather thee from all the nations, whither the LORD thy God hath scattered thee.”

Deuteronomy 30:2–3

The curses are not the last word. “Return unto the LORD” is always open to a man who has wandered, however far.

Who kept all this? No one

The blessings of verses 1–14 ask for hearkening to all the commandments. The twelfth curse of chapter 27 fell on every man who did not. Nobody in Israel kept it, and we do not either.

So the chapter does what the law was meant to do. It shows us where we stand, and sends us to someone else.

Christ took the curse

We heard it on mount Ebal (27:26): the curse falls on every man who does not do it all, and “Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us” (Galatians 3:13). The sickness, the siege, the scattering and the nights without rest in this chapter are what that curse means, and he carried it.

“That the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ; that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.”

Galatians 3:14

The man who trusts him is no longer under it. The blessing now comes through Christ, by faith.

Is every hard thing a curse?

No. A believer is out from under the curse of the law, and yet hard things still come. Three cases:

  • What we sow“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap” (Galatians 6:7). The marriage, the money and the health of a man's past do not vanish when he is saved.
  • A Father's discipline“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth” (Hebrews 12:6). It is correction, and it is love.
  • Neither of thoseThe disciples asked who sinned, the man or his parents. “Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents: but that the works of God should be made manifest in him” (John 9:2–3).

So do not read every hardship as a curse, and do not read every comfort as a reward.

Real world: serve him gladly

  • SinThe root named in verse 47 is not a crime but a cold heart: not serving the LORD “with joyfulness, and with gladness of heart, for the abundance of all things”. It is easy to slide into when life is good.
  • RegretMany of us have lost things like those in verses 30–41: a house, a family, the fruit of our work. Regret is the right feeling about it. But a man in Christ is not under this curse, and it is not the end of his story.
  • Grace“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law” (Galatians 3:13). The curse of this chapter fell on him.
  • Hope“Return unto the LORD” (Deuteronomy 30:2). The door was open to Israel scattered among all people, and it is open to you.
  • TodayBefore you get busy, thank God out loud for one ordinary thing: a meal, a roof, a brother's text. Then send that brother a text back.

Points to remember

  1. The condition“if thou shalt hearken diligently unto the voice of the LORD thy God” 28:1Hearken and do all his commandments, and do not go aside to other gods.
  2. The blessing“Blessed shalt thou be when thou comest in, and blessed shalt thou be when thou goest out.” 28:6City and field, body and ground, basket and store, in and out: every part of the day, for Israel in the land.
  3. The curses“all these curses shall come upon thee, and overtake thee” 28:15The same places cursed, then sickness, defeat, captivity, siege and scattering.
  4. The reason“Because thou servedst not the LORD thy God with joyfulness, and with gladness of heart, for the abundance of all things” 28:47A heart that had plenty and did not serve him gladly.
  5. The scattering“the LORD shall scatter thee among all people, from the one end of the earth even unto the other” 28:64The Bible shows it come to pass in 2 Kings 17 and 25, and Jesus warned of it in Luke 21.
  6. The curse borne“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us” Galatians 3:13He took the curse, and we are blessed with all spiritual blessings in him.

Question 1 of 6

The condition

What did the LORD ask of Israel, for the blessings to come?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To hearken diligently to the voice of the LORD, to observe and to do all his commandments, and not to go aside to other gods to serve them.

Question 2 of 6

The blessing

Where would the blessing meet a man who hearkened?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Everywhere: in the city and the field, in his body, his ground and his cattle, his basket and his store, when he comes in and when he goes out.

Question 3 of 6

The curses

What was to come on the people who would not hearken?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All the curses: the blessing turned inside out, then sickness, a sky of brass, defeat, captivity, a siege, and scattering among all the nations.

Question 4 of 6

The reason

Why did Moses say the curses would come?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because they forsook the LORD, and did not serve him with joyfulness and gladness of heart, though they had abundance of all things.

Question 5 of 6

The scattering

Where did Moses say the people would be carried?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To a nation they had not known, and scattered among all people. The Bible records Israel carried to Assyria (2 Kings 17:6) and Jerusalem burned, with the people carried away (2 Kings 25).

Question 6 of 6

The curse borne

Who has redeemed us from the curse of the law?

What verses tell us about this?
