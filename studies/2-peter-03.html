2 Peter 3

Why is the Lord taking so long?

Peter ends his last letter with the question every waiting man asks. His answer is the kindest thing in the chapter: the delay is mercy.

2 Peter in one picture

This is the last chapter, and the last chapter of Peter's life that we have. He has told us what we have been given, and to watch for counterfeits. Now he tells us how to wait for Jesus to come back.

Part one

The scoffers

Verses 1–7

Some people laugh at the idea that Jesus is coming back. Peter says to expect it, and tells you what to say.

Where is He?

The scoffersWhere is the promise of his coming? Our fathers have died, and nothing has changed.

PeterNotice what they are walking after: their own lusts.

Peter doesn't say they have found evidence. He says they have found a reason to live as they like. If Jesus is coming back, then how a man lives today matters. It's more comfortable to decide He isn't.

Some of you have heard this from your own friends, or from the voice in your own head: “Nothing is going to happen. Do what you want.” That voice has been saying it since before you were born. It's still wrong.

Things have changed before

  1. of oldMade by the word of Godverse 5
  2. Noah's dayThe world was overflowed with water, and perishedverse 6
  3. nowKept in store by the same wordverse 7
  4. the day of judgmentReserved unto fireverse 7

The scoffers say “all things continue as they were.” Peter says that's what they “willingly are ignorant of.” The world has already ended once, in the flood. God's word made it, God's word flooded it, and the same word is keeping it for what is next.

The delay is not proof that God has forgotten. It's the pause before the next thing He said He would do.

Part two

The Lord's patience

Verses 8–9

The heart of the chapter, and maybe the whole letter. Why hasn't He come yet?

God doesn't watch the clock

This is not a math puzzle. God isn't bound by our calendars. What feels to us like “two thousand years of waiting” doesn't look to Him like being late.

“For a thousand years in thy sight are but as yesterday when it is past, and as a watch in the night.”

Psalm 90:4

He is not slow. He is patient.

Some men “count slackness.” To them it looks like God is late, or not coming. Peter says no: He is “longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.” Every day He waits is another day for someone to turn.

Not some. Any and all. God wants every man, woman and child saved. That includes the man you think is too far gone, and it included you.

Why the wait is good news

“Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord God, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?”

Ezekiel 33:11

“Or despisest thou the riches of his goodness and forbearance and longsuffering; not knowing that the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance?”

Romans 2:4

Look back at your own story. How many days, years, did He wait for you? Peter says in verse 15, “account that the longsuffering of our Lord is salvation.” His patience is the reason you're in this room.

Then ask who you know that He is still waiting on: a brother, a father, the man you used to run with. Pray for him by name. Today may be the day.

Part three

The day will come

Verses 10–14

The patience will end. When it does, it comes like a thief, and what matters is how you are living.

As a thief in the night

  • charts and timelinesJesus said no one knows the day (Matthew 24:36)
  • “it's happening this year”a thief doesn't send a notice
  • picking out the right news storypeople have done this for 2,000 years
  • being ready any daythe only plan that works

“But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only.”

Matthew 24:36

Many Bible teachers see different details in the end times, and good men disagree. But the plain point isn't a puzzle. It's to be the kind of man you'd want to be found as. Don't wait for a sign.

What kind of man, then?

Ifall this will be dissolved,
→
thenwhat manner of persons ought ye to be?

Peter doesn't say “so don't bother with anything.” He says: since the stuff won't last, live for what will. “Holy conversation” is the old way of saying a holy way of life, and godliness was on the ladder in chapter 1.

In plain terms, today: show up for work, keep your word, stay out of the place that pulls you down, and tell one person about the Lord. A man who knows the Lord is coming doesn't live scared. He lives awake.

A home where right lives

a world of sin, regret and goodbyes → new heavens and a new earth “Wherein dwelleth righteousness.” Right isn't a visitor there. It lives there.

“And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.”

Revelation 21:4

This is the hope to hold on a hard day. The craving, the funeral, the thing you did: all of it is “the former things.” None of it follows you in. And verse 14 says you can be “found of him in peace, without spot, and blameless”: that is what He made you when you trusted Him. Remember chapter 1, “purged from his old sins.”

Part four

Keep growing

Verses 15–18

Peter's last words in the Bible: a word about his friend Paul, a warning, and a way to stay on your feet.

“Our beloved brother Paul”

Paul, years beforeI withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed.

Peter, in his last letterOur beloved brother Paul… hath written unto you.

“But when Peter was come to Antioch, I withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed.”

Galatians 2:11

Paul once called Peter out in public for hypocrisy. Peter could have held it against him. Instead, at the end of his life, he calls him “beloved brother” and puts his letters beside “the other scriptures.” That is what forgiveness between men looks like. If there is a man you're sore at, write his name down today.

Hard to understand? Keep reading

  • skip what's hardPeter admits some of Paul is hard, and doesn't tell you to quit
  • wrest it (twist it) to say what I wantthat's the unlearned and the unstable, and it ends badly
  • keep reading, ask a brother, stay in the Wordthe clear parts carry you while the hard parts wait

Even Peter found Paul hard. You are allowed to have questions. “Wrest” means to twist or wrench, as a man might twist a rope, until it says something it never said. The simple test: if your reading lets you off the hook and costs you nothing, check it with someone you trust.

Beware, and be kept

Peter gives the warning, “beware,” then the reason: you could lose your footing, “fall from your own stedfastness,” if you let smooth talk lead you off. That's about losing your balance and your walk, not your place in the family.

“Now unto him that is able to keep you from falling, and to present you faultless before the presence of his glory with exceeding joy,”

Jude 1:24

Both are true. Stay alert, and know that Christ holds you. A man who keeps close to the Word and to other believers is hard to knock over.

Grow

Grow in grace.

These are the last words Peter wrote. Grow in grace: lean harder into God's kindness, not your own record. Grow in the knowledge of Jesus: know Him better, one day at a time. It is the ladder of chapter 1 again, and it never stops.

Today: read one chapter, say one prayer, tell one brother the truth about how you're doing. That is growing. You don't have to be ready for tomorrow. Just be a little further along than you were yesterday.

Looking back over 2 Peter

1Growhis divine power hath given unto us all things1:3
2BewareThe Lord knoweth how to deliver the godly2:9
3Waitgrow in grace3:18

One letter, one old fisherman, one message. God has already given you what you need. Watch out for anyone selling freedom without Christ. And while you wait for Him to come, keep growing, keep clean, and keep telling others, because He is patient so that more can come home.

Peter was a man who failed badly. He ended as a man who finished well. So can you.

Points to remember

  1. The scoffers“Scoffers, walking after their own lusts, and saying, Where is the promise of his coming?” 3:3-4Doubt is often an excuse to live as you please. The delay isn't proof He forgot.
  2. God's clock“One day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day” 3:8God doesn't count time as we do. What looks late to us isn't late to Him.
  3. Longsuffering“Not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance” 3:9The wait is mercy. God wants everyone saved, and His patience is the reason you're here.
  4. Like a thief“The day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night” 3:10No charts, no dates. Just be ready any day.
  5. A new home“We, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness” 3:13The former things pass away. A home is coming where right lives.
  6. Keep growing“Grow in grace, and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ” 3:18A little further along each day: grace, and knowing Jesus better.
