Exodus 32

The golden calf

Weeks after they promised, “All that the LORD hath said will we do,” the people make a calf of gold and dance around it. Moses breaks the tables, and then he goes back up the mountain to stand between a holy God and the people he loves.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. The waitingWhat did the people say when Moses was slow to come down, and what did they ask Aaron to make?
  2. The calfWhat did the people say about the calf, and what did they do the next day?
  3. The prayerHow did Moses plead for the people when the LORD said, “let me alone”?
  4. The excuseWhat did Aaron tell Moses about how the calf was made?

Part one

“Up, make us gods”

Verses 1–6

Moses is late, and the people stop waiting.

Two things happening at once

At the top of the mount, for forty days“And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.”Exodus 25:8. God is giving Moses the plan for a tent, so he can live in the middle of his people.
At the foot of the mount, the same days“Up, make us gods, which shall go before us.”Exodus 32:1. The people decide Moses is not coming back.

Delayed means he was slow, later than they wanted. We wot not is “we don't know.” God was preparing to come and stay with them, and they could not wait to see it. They wanted a god they could see, today.

A calf of gold

  • moltenmelted and poured into a mould
  • a graving toola carving tool, a chisel for shaping the metal
  • fashionedshaped, made

Where did the gold come from? Egypt. When they left, the Egyptians gave them jewels of gold (12:35). The gifts of their rescue became the stuff of their idol.

Why a calf? Egypt worshipped bull gods, and Israel had lived among that for generations. Out of Egypt, their hearts went back to it: they “in their hearts turned back again into Egypt… And they made a calf” (Acts 7:39, 41).

“They made a calf in Horeb, and worshipped the molten image. Thus they changed their glory into the similitude of an ox that eateth grass.”

Psalm 106:19–20

“These be thy gods… which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.” They gave the credit for their rescue to a thing they had just made with their own hands.

“A feast to the LORD”

Notice what Aaron calls it. Not a feast to some other god: “a feast to the LORD.” They kept the name, the altar and the offerings. They just remade God into something they could see and handle, and then they did what they wanted.

“Neither be ye idolaters, as were some of them; as it is written, The people sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play.”

1 Corinthians 10:7

Rose up to play is not a game. The feast turned into a party with no limits: drinking, dancing, and all that comes with it. The Bible does not give details, and we don't need them. Paul quotes this line to warn the church at Corinth.

Religion can be the costume a relapse wears. “I'll still pray, I'll still go. I'll just have this too.”

How far, how fast

  1. Moses is latethe waiting gets long (v. 1)
  2. “we wot not”maybe he's not coming back (v. 1)
  3. “make us gods”something we can see, now (v. 1)
  4. bring the goldthe gifts of the rescue, handed over (vv. 2–3)
  5. rose up to playand called it a feast to the LORD (vv. 5–6)

Only a few months out of Egypt. They had walked through the sea on dry ground, eaten manna every morning, drunk from the rock, and heard God speak from the fire. Just weeks earlier they had said, “All the words which the LORD hath said will we do” (24:3).

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

If you have fallen after a great rescue, you are not the first man it has happened to. This chapter was written for you, and it does not end at the calf.

Part two

“Let me alone”

Verses 7–14

God tells Moses what is happening below, and Moses will not let go.

“Thy people”

the LORD, to Moses…for thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. 32:7

Moses, to the LORD…why doth thy wrath wax hot against thy people, which thou hast brought forth out of the land of Egypt with great power, and with a mighty hand? 32:11

God says, “they're yours, Moses.” Moses hands them straight back: “no, Lord, they're Yours. You brought them out.” A man praying for someone who has fallen reminds God whose he is.

Corrupted themselves: spoiled, ruined. Turned aside quickly: they had barely started on the road and they were already off it.

A stiff neck, and an open door

Stiffnecked is a farmer's word. An ox that will not bend its neck to the yoke, that will not be turned. Stubborn.

Let me alone, that my wrath may wax hot: God's anger at sin is real and right, and it was about to fall. (Wax means grow.)

But who asks to be left alone by someone who isn't holding on? God tells Moses what he is about to do, and leaves room for Moses to stand in the way. The door to prayer is open in the very sentence of judgment.

And he makes Moses an offer: “I will make of thee a great nation.” Start over with Moses' family. Moses doesn't even answer it. He goes straight to praying for the people who had just forgotten him.

How Moses prayed

  1. 1thy people, which thou hast brought forth out of the land of EgyptThey are Yours, and You did the rescuing.v. 11
  2. 2Wherefore should the Egyptians speakYour name is at stake in front of the nations.v. 12
  3. 3Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel… to whom thou swarest by thine own selfYou promised, and You keep Your word.v. 13

Not once does Moses say, “they're not so bad.” Every reason he gives is about God: who owns them, his name, his promise. Besought is begged. Swarest is swore. Mischief here is evil intent.

That is how to pray after a fall, for a brother or for yourself. Not “I'm not that bad,” but “Lord, I'm Yours, Your name is on me, and You promised.”

One man in the gap

“Therefore he said that he would destroy them, had not Moses his chosen stood before him in the breach, to turn away his wrath, lest he should destroy them.”

Psalm 106:23

The LORD repented does not mean God had done wrong. It means he turned from the judgment he had spoken, in answer to a man's prayer. Prayer is not for show. It changes what happens.

Part three

“Who is on the LORD’S side?”

Verses 15–29

Moses comes down the mountain and sees it with his own eyes.

What the noise was

  • the tables of the testimonythe two stones, written by God, that bore witness to the covenant
  • gravencarved, cut into the stone
  • shout for masterythe shout of men who are winning a battle
  • cry for being overcomethe cry of men who are losing

Joshua, a soldier, hears the noise and thinks of battle. Moses, who has just heard from God what is happening, knows better: it isn't winning or losing. It's singing.

Moses walks down with the work of God in his hands, written in God's own writing on both sides. He is carrying the covenant the people had already broken.

The tables broken

Moses' anger waxed hot, grew hot, and he threw down the stones. It is an honest picture: the covenant was already broken in the camp, and now it lay broken on the ground for all to see.

The text does not praise his temper and it does not scold him for it here. Later God will say, “the first tables, which thou brakest” (34:1), and give him new ones. Moses' anger will cost him dearly another day (Numbers 20). Anger at sin can be right, and it still needs watching.

Strawed means scattered. He burned the calf, ground it to powder, scattered it on the water, and made them drink it. Their god could not save itself. It went down their throats and out of their bodies like anything else.

Every idol ends that way: whatever we run to in place of God turns out to be nothing, and we are the ones left swallowing it.

“There came out this calf”

What happened, 32:4

And he received them at their hand, and fashioned it with a graving tool, after he had made it a molten calf…

What Aaron said, 32:24

…So they gave it me: then I cast it into the fire, and there came out this calf.

Aaron carved it with his own tool. In his story the people made him do it, the fire did the rest, and a calf just walked out. “Thou knowest the people, that they are set on mischief.”

It is the oldest excuse there is. Adam said, “The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree.” The woman said, “The serpent beguiled me” (Genesis 3:12–13). It just happened. They made me. You know how they are.

Drop the excuse

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

An excuse is a cover. Take it off: I did it. Nobody made me.

And where the sin was, there is mercy. Aaron was nearly destroyed for this, and Moses prayed for him too (Deuteronomy 9:20). He went on to be Israel's first high priest.

At the gate of the camp

Naked here means let loose, broken loose: the people were out of control. Aaron had let them run wild, “unto their shame among their enemies.” It was a shame to them and to the name of their God.

Moses stands in the gate and makes it a choice: “Who is on the LORD’S side? let him come unto me.” Not a speech. A line on the ground, and a step to take.

The men of Levi came. Remember Levi in Genesis 49: “instruments of cruelty,” a father's curse on his anger. That same tribe now steps forward for the LORD, and it will become the tribe that serves at his tent.

The calf was in the camp. The way back started with a step out of the crowd and toward the man standing for God.

A hard day

This is the hardest part of the chapter, so we will say it plainly. The LORD judged the open rebellion in the camp, and the Levites carried out his word: about three thousand men died that day. Consecrate yourselves means set yourselves apart to the LORD, even above your own family.

It was God's judgment, given once, by his word through Moses, at the birth of the nation. It is no pattern for any man today. Jesus said his servants do not fight for his kingdom:

“Jesus answered, My kingdom is not of this world: if my kingdom were of this world, then would my servants fight, that I should not be delivered to the Jews: but now is my kingdom not from hence.”

John 18:36

Sin is not small. Thousands of years later it still costs lives, families and freedom. That is why what comes next matters so much.

Three thousand, and three thousand

At Sinai, the law given

3,000

“There fell of the people that day about three thousand men” (32:28).

At Pentecost, the Spirit given

3,000

“The same day there were added unto them about three thousand souls” (Acts 2:41).

“Who also hath made us able ministers of the new testament; not of the letter, but of the spirit: for the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life.”

2 Corinthians 3:6

The law shows a man what his sin deserves. The gospel gives him life anyway. Paul calls the law on stone “the ministration of death” (2 Corinthians 3:7). The Bible does not link these two numbers itself, but notice them: the day the calf was judged, about three thousand died; the day the Spirit came, about three thousand were given new life.

Part four

“Blot me… out of thy book”

Verses 30–35

Moses goes back up the mountain with an offer.

A sentence he could not finish

Moses does not soften it: “Ye have sinned a great sin.” Then he goes to make an atonement, a covering for sin that puts a man right with God again. Peradventure means perhaps. He does not know if it will work.

Look at the dash in verse 32: “if thou wilt forgive their sin—.” He breaks off. He cannot even finish the sentence. Then: “and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book.” Take my name out instead.

Paul felt the same about his own people:

“For I could wish that myself were accursed from Christ for my brethren, my kinsmen according to the flesh:”

Romans 9:3

Moses offered. Christ did it.

Moses

“Blot me out”

He offered, and God said no: “Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out.” Moses was a sinner too. He could not carry anyone else's sin.

Jesus Christ

“Made a curse for us”

The only man who never sinned took the place Moses offered to take.

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

“For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;”

1 Timothy 2:5

Mercy, and what sin still costs

God spared the nation and sent them on: “lead the people… mine Angel shall go before thee.” The journey goes on. That is mercy.

But “in the day when I visit I will visit their sin upon them,” and “the LORD plagued the people.” Visit here means come to deal with. Forgiveness does not always erase what sin set in motion. A man forgiven for drinking may still lose his license.

Both are true at once: the consequences may come, and the road goes on with God. And for the man in Christ, his name is not in danger:

“Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.”

Luke 10:20

Real world: after a fall

  • Don't stop waitingWhen God seems slow, that is when the calf gets made. Stay where you were told to wait, near your brothers.
  • No excusesNot “there came out this calf.” Say it plain: I did it.
  • Step out of the crowd“Who is on the LORD’S side?” Take one step toward the men standing with God, today.
  • Someone is in the gap for youJesus prays for you right now. Call or text a brother, and let him pray for you too.

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Points to remember

  1. The waiting“Up, make us gods, which shall go before us… we wot not what is become of him.” 32:1The calf was made while God was planning to live among them. Impatience is where many falls begin.
  2. The calf“These be thy gods, O Israel… the people sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play.” 32:4, 6They gave the credit for their rescue to a thing they made, and dressed the party up as a feast to the LORD.
  3. The prayer“Turn from thy fierce wrath… Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, thy servants.” 32:12–13Moses pleaded from who God is and what he promised, and the LORD turned from the judgment.
  4. The excuse“I cast it into the fire, and there came out this calf.” 32:24An excuse covers sin. Confess it plainly, and find mercy.
  5. The choice“Who is on the LORD’S side? let him come unto me.” 32:26The way back began with one step out of the crowd. Levi, the angry tribe, came first.
  6. The gap“If thou wilt forgive their sin—; and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book.” 32:32Moses offered his own name, and could not pay. Christ, the one mediator, did.

Question 1 of 6

The waiting

What did the people say when Moses was slow to come down, and what did they ask Aaron to make?

What verses tell us about this?

“Saying unto Aaron, Make us gods to go before us: for as for this Moses, which brought us out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him.”

Acts 7:40

Answer

“We wot not what is become of him”: we don't know what happened to him. And they asked for gods they could see. While they gave up waiting, God was giving Moses the plan to live among them. Don't quit waiting on God; that is when the calf gets made.

Question 2 of 6

The calf

What did the people say about the calf, and what did they do the next day?

What verses tell us about this?

“Neither be ye idolaters, as were some of them; as it is written, The people sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play.”

1 Corinthians 10:7

Answer

They said the calf had brought them out of Egypt. The next day they made offerings, ate and drank, and rose up to play: a wild party called “a feast to the LORD.” Watch for the religion that only covers what you want to do anyway.

Question 3 of 6

The prayer

How did Moses plead for the people when the LORD said, “let me alone”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He reminded God that they were his people, that his name was at stake before Egypt, and that he had sworn to Abraham, Isaac and Israel. He never said they were good. And the LORD turned from the evil he had spoken. Pray that way for a brother who has fallen, and for yourself.

Question 4 of 6

The excuse

What did Aaron tell Moses about how the calf was made?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

Answer

He blamed the people and said he threw the gold in the fire “and there came out this calf.” But verse 4 says he shaped it with a tool. An excuse keeps sin covered. Confess it plainly, and there is mercy.

Question 5 of 6

The choice

What did Moses call out at the gate of the camp, and who came?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Who is on the LORD’S side? let him come unto me.” The sons of Levi came, the tribe once cursed for its anger. A man's past does not stop him from taking the LORD's side today.

Question 6 of 6

The gap

What did Moses offer the LORD for the people's sin, and what did the LORD answer?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Answer

He offered to be blotted out of God's book in their place. God said each sinner answers for his own sin: Moses could not pay for others. Only Jesus, who knew no sin, could take our place, and he did.
