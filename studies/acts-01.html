Acts 1

Wait for the promise

Jesus goes back to heaven and leaves His followers a promise and a job. The men He leaves behind are the same ones who ran from Him, and He is not done with them.

The book of Acts

Who: Luke the physician, Paul's traveling companion, who also wrote the Gospel of Luke.

When: about AD 62, from Rome. It covers about thirty years.

To whom: Theophilus, “most excellent” in Luke's Gospel. His name means a friend of God. And through him, every man who wants to know what happened next.

The Gospel is what Jesus began to do. Acts is what He kept on doing, through ordinary people.

The plan of the book

“…ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.”

Acts 1:8
  1. 1
    JerusalemActs 1–7. Right where they crucified Him.
  2. 2
    All JudaeaActs 8–9. Trouble scatters them, and they carry the news.
  3. 3
    SamariaActs 8. The neighbors the Jews wouldn't speak to.
  4. 4
    The uttermost part of the earthActs 10–28. The Gentiles, and finally Rome.

Where the road goes

Most of the book follows one man, Paul, who was once the church's worst enemy. Here is where the “uttermost part” took him.

First journey, about AD 46 to 48: Cyprus and the cities of what is now central Turkey.

Second journey, about AD 49 to 52: across into Europe, to Philippi, Athens and Corinth.

Third journey, about AD 53 to 57: three years in Ephesus, then Greece again.

The last road, about AD 59 to 60: in chains, through a shipwreck, to Rome.

From the hill to Rome

  1. about AD 30 to 33Risen, taken up; the Spirit comesActs 1–2

Today's chapter is the very first dot on the line. About thirty years of church history are packed into this one book.

Notice how the book ends: Paul still preaching in Rome, and no “the end.” Acts never finishes, because the story of what Jesus does through His people is still going. Every man who comes to Him adds a chapter.

Part one

Wait for it

Verses 1–11

Jesus is about to leave. What He says last is what matters most.

Part two of one story

Luke's first book

What Jesus began

The Gospel of Luke: His birth, His teaching, His cross, His empty tomb. All of it written to the same man.

Luke's second book

What He kept on doing

Acts. Jesus did not stop working when He went up. He went on, through the Spirit, in the lives of His people.

“It seemed good to me also, having had perfect understanding of all things from the very first, to write unto thee in order, most excellent Theophilus,”

Luke 1:3

Luke didn't write a legend. He wrote an investigated account, in order, with names and places. The same Jesus who worked then is working in men's lives today, and that includes yours.

Forty days of proof

days He showed Himself alive

“Infallible proofs” means signs that cannot fail: He ate with them, they touched Him, they heard Him, again and again. These weren't men who wanted to believe. They had hidden behind locked doors, sure it was over.

Your faith doesn't rest on a feeling that comes and goes. It rests on something that happened, and was seen, over forty days. On the mornings when you feel nothing, it is still true.

Water, and the Spirit

John the Baptist

Baptized with water

A man in the river, as a sign that you are turning from your sin. Something done to the body, pointing to something else.

Jesus

Baptized with the Holy Ghost

God Himself coming to live in His people. Not a sign of the thing: the thing. And it was only days away.

“And, behold, I send the promise of my Father upon you: but tarry ye in the city of Jerusalem, until ye be endued with power from on high.”

Luke 24:49

Notice what He asks of them: wait. Not try harder, not work it out. For a man who is used to fixing everything by himself, that's hard. Some of God's help only comes to the man who stops long enough to receive it.

A question, and a better answer

The apostlesLord, wilt thou at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel?

JesusIt is not for you to know the times or the seasons… But ye shall receive power.
not what they asked, what they needed

They wanted the schedule. Jesus doesn't scold them. He turns them from the timetable to the task.

Most of us want to know how it turns out, and whether we'll make it. He gives something better: power for today, and a job to do with it.

A witness

μάρτυς said martys a witness: someone who tells what he has seen

Our word martyr comes from this one, because many witnesses paid for it with their lives. But a witness doesn't need to be clever. He doesn't need a sermon. He only needs to say what happened to him.

You can do that today. “I was in a bad place. Jesus found me. Here's what's different.” That is a witness, and nobody can argue with it.

He went up, and He is coming back

He wentin person, the same Jesusin plain sight, while they watchedwith a cloud, up from Olivet
and will comeand will comeand will come
He will returnin person, the same Jesusin plain sight, for every eye“in like manner”

Two men in white ask a fair question: why stand ye gazing? Looking up isn't wrong, but they had a job to do. The One who is coming back left work for His people to do until He does.

That's a real comfort if you've blown it. Your story isn't over, and neither is His. Meanwhile, get on with today.

Part two

The upper room

Verses 12–26

Eleven men who ran away, a few women, and a family that once thought He had lost His mind. This is who God starts with.

Look who is in the room

  1. Peterswore three times he never knew Him
  2. Thomaswouldn't believe until he saw
  3. James and Johnargued over who would be greatest
  4. Matthewa tax collector, the kind everyone hated
  5. Simon Zelotesa Zealot, a man of the revolutionary party
  6. His brethrenHis own brothers, who did not believe in Him

“For neither did his brethren believe in him.”

John 7:5

Every one of them had failed in some way. And every one of them is here, in the same room, praying together. Nobody in that room had a clean record. They had come back anyway.

A hundred and twenty

followers waiting in Jerusalem

That is all that was left after three years of Jesus' ministry. Paul says more than five hundred saw Him alive at one time (1 Corinthians 15:6), but only about a hundred and twenty were still waiting.

Not many. No money, no army, no building, no plan. This is how the greatest movement in history starts: a small group, in one room, praying. Never despise a small start.

The field of blood

חֲקַל דְּמָא said Hakal dema “field of blood,” in the people's own Aramaic

Judas sold Jesus for thirty pieces of silver. When he tried to give the money back, the priests wouldn't take it, and it ended up buying a burial field (Matthew 27:3–8). The people of Jerusalem knew the place by this name.

This is one of the hard texts in the Bible. Judas was one of the twelve. He walked with Jesus for three years, heard everything, and still he betrayed Him. Being close to Jesus is not the same as being His.

Two men who failed the same week

Judas

Went off alone with it

He felt the weight of it but never brought it back to Jesus. Regret with nowhere to go ends in the dark (Matthew 27:5).

Peter

Went back to Jesus

He wept bitterly, and came back. Jesus had already prayed for him. Peter is the one standing up in this room.

“But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Luke 22:32

The difference was where each man took it. If you've fallen, take it straight back to Jesus, and tell a brother today. If dark thoughts ever say it's too late, call or text 988 right then.

“Bishoprick”: an office, a job to do

ἐπισκοπή said episkopē, “overseeing” a bishoprick is the office of an overseer: a post, a place of service. Our word bishop comes from the same root.

Peter is quoting two Psalms. The second one says it in plain words:

“Let his days be few; and let another take his office.”

Psalm 109:8

Judas walked away from his place among the twelve, and God did not leave it empty. The work goes on. A man who has wasted years can still be given a place in it today.

What the replacement needed

  1. ✓He had been with them the whole time“companied with us all the time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us”verse 21
  2. ✓From John's baptism to the ascensionhe saw it all, from the beginning of Jesus' ministry to the endverse 22
  3. ✓A witness of the resurrectionthe one thing an apostle had to be able to say: I saw Him aliveverse 22

An apostle was a first-hand witness, and the test was not talent or a clean past. It was he was there, and he saw Him alive. That is why the apostles hold a special place that no one holds today.

Prayer first, then the lot

Thou, Lord, which knowest the hearts of all men.

They prayed first, and asked the One who sees the heart. Men judge by the outside. He doesn't, and that is good news for a man with a bad record.

“The lot is cast into the lap; but the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD.”

Proverbs 16:33

Casting lots was like drawing straws and trusting God with the result. It is the last time in the Bible. From the next chapter on, the Holy Ghost guides His people from the inside.

Question 1 of 6

The resurrection

How sure can we be that Jesus really rose from the dead?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As sure as men can be of anything they saw. For forty days He showed Himself alive, and the men chosen to tell it had to be eyewitnesses. They were frightened men who had hidden behind locked doors, and something real changed them. You can build your life on it.

Question 2 of 6

The promise

What did Jesus tell His followers to wait for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“The promise of the Father”: the Holy Ghost, with power. John baptized with water as a sign of turning from sin. Jesus would send God Himself to live in His people. He didn't ask them to work for it. He asked them to wait, and receive.

Question 3 of 6

The mission

What did Jesus give His followers to do instead of working out His timetable?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To be His witnesses: to tell what they had seen, starting right where they were. They asked when. He answered with power and a job. You may never know how it all turns out, but you know what He wants today: tell what He has done.

Question 4 of 6

His return

What did the two men in white say about Jesus coming back?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That the same Jesus who went up will come back the same way. And meanwhile: stop gazing. He is coming, and He has left work to do. A man who knows his Lord is coming back lives differently today.

Question 5 of 6

A second chance

Can a man who let Jesus down still be part of what God is doing?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Yes. Look at the room: Peter, who denied Him; Thomas, who doubted; His own brothers, who didn't believe. They all came back, and God began His church with them. Don't wait to be clean before you come back. Come back, and let Him do the cleaning.

Question 6 of 6

Two failures

What happened to Judas, and what does it teach us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Judas was one of the twelve and still betrayed Jesus, and he ended in the dark. Peter failed too, but he came back and was restored. The difference was where each took his sin. Whatever you've done, bring it to Jesus, not away from Him.
