2 Corinthians 10

The weapons of our warfare

Paul answers the men who sneer at him: the real fight is in the mind, the weapons are God's, and the only boast worth making is in the Lord.

Where we are in 2 Corinthians

About AD 56, Paul is writing from Macedonia to the church he started in Corinth, the same church that got 1 Corinthians a year earlier. The first nine chapters are warm: comfort in trouble, a church set right, and a collection for the poor. Now the tone changes.

1–7Paul's heart, a church set right
8–9The offering for the poor
10The weapons
11–12The fool's boast
13Examine yourselves; goodbye

Most of the church has come round to Paul. But some newcomers have been running him down, and some in the church have listened. Chapters 10 to 13 are his answer. Read alone, this chapter still makes sense: a man under attack decides how he will fight back, and then what he will be proud of.

Part one

The weapons we fight with

Verses 1–6

A gentle man, a real war, and strongholds that come down.

Meek is not weak

Paul's criticsHe's tough on paper. In person he's a pushover.

PaulI beg you, by the gentleness of Christ, don't make me be bold when I get there.

“Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.”

Matthew 11:29

Meek does not mean weak. It means strength with a leash on: power held back until it is needed. Jesus called Himself meek, and He turned over the tables in the temple. Paul asks them to let him be gentle, because he can be firm.

If you have a past of rage, this is good news. You don't have to be soft to stop being dangerous. A meek man is the strongest man in the room: he can be insulted and still choose his answer.

We still live in a body, but we don't fight like it

“Walk in the flesh” just means we live in a human body, with hunger and tiredness. “War after the flesh” means fighting with a man's own methods. Paul is in a war, and he won't use these:

  • get evenanswering an insult with a worse one
  • a sharp tonguewinning the argument and losing the man
  • the bottle, the pills, the escape“carnal” weapons, the old way of fighting a pain
  • a clever liea story that makes you look better
  • weapons “mighty through God”the truth, His word, prayer, and the gospel (Ephesians 6:11–17)

Notice who the power belongs to: “mighty through God.” You are not asked to be strong enough. You are asked to use His weapons instead of yours.

A stronghold is a fortress in the mind

“Strong holds” are fortresses. Paul says God's weapons pull them down, and what he means by “imaginations” is reasonings: the arguments a man builds in his head, stone on stone, until they seem like the truth. Not demons in the walls: lies in the mind.

Behind every lie is a plain truth God has already said. You are forgiven (1 John 1:9). You are loved. And you are not finished: He who began a good work will carry it on. These come down one stone at a time, usually by telling yourself the truth out loud.

Catch the thought before it becomes the act

“Every thought” means you start at the first stone, not the last. A thought is not a sin; it flies in. Jesus Himself “was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin” (Hebrews 4:15). What matters is what you do with it in the next ten seconds.

“But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.”

James 1:14–15

What to do with the thought, today

  1. Notice it. “There it is.”Say it plainly, even out loud. A thought you name loses half its grip.
  2. Call it what it is. “A craving, not a command.”It is a voice, not an order. You do not have to obey it.
  3. Hand it to Christ. “Lord, I'm yours. Take this one.”One sentence is enough. That is the “obedience of Christ” in practice: you give Him the thought.
  4. Move your body. Change the room.Walk, call a brother, start a task, eat, pray. “A way to escape” (1 Corinthians 10:13) is usually a door you can walk through.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

And if you lost this fight yesterday? Then it is not too late, and it is not the end. Say it to God (1 John 1:9), and take the very next thought captive. Today is a new fight, not the end of the war.

Patience first, then discipline

Firstthe church that wants to obey is settled,
→
thenPaul deals with those who won't.

“Revenge all disobedience” means to punish it. Paul has the authority, but he waits. He does not swing at the first sign of trouble. He gives the willing time to come round before he deals with the rebels. A good leader (and a good father) fixes the house before he fights the neighbors.

Part two

Looking past appearances

Verses 7–11

They said Paul looked weak. Paul says to look again.

What man sees, and what the LORD sees

Man looks at

the outward appearance

Looks, a smooth speaker, a good résumé, a clean record. And the other way round: a criminal record, a bad history, a man who stumbles over his words. Paul's critics called his speech “contemptible.”

The LORD looks at

the heart

Whose you are, and which way you are facing. You are “Christ's,” and nobody can take that from a man because of how he looks, or what is written on his file.

“But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

If you have been written off because of what you did, hear this: you are not sorted by your appearance or your record. If you are in Christ, you are His, settled.

To build up, and not to tear down.

The same authority that pulls down strongholds in verse 4 is given for edification, which just means building up. God gives a man power to help other people stand, not to push them around.

Test any man with power over you (a boss, a sponsor, a preacher) by this one verse. Is he building you up or wearing you down? And test yourself the same way. When you have a little authority over somebody, which way does it point?

The same man, near and far

What he writeswhen he is far away,
→
is what he doeswhen he is standing in front of you.

His critics made a joke of Paul: brave from a distance, a mouse in the room. Paul's answer is one of the best tests of a man: is he the same man absent as present? That is called integrity: a man all in one piece.

Many of us lived two lives: one for the people at home, one for the street. A new life in Christ is one life. Today, be the same man on the job site that you are in the group. If you promise it here, do it there.

Part three

The Lord's measure

Verses 12–18

Stop comparing. Let the Lord do the measuring.

Measuring yourself by yourselves

Look down, and you feel tall. It is a cheap feeling, and it is the road to pride. A man in recovery knows this trap: “I was never as bad as that guy.”

Look up, and you feel small, and you quit before you start. “I'll never be like that guy.” Either way, you chose your own ruler, so you are not wise.

Only one measure matters, and none of us reaches it. That is why the gospel is good news: Christ measured up for us, and gives us His standing as a gift. Compare yourself with yourself of last year, and thank Him for the gap.

“The regions beyond you”

Paul was the first to bring the gospel to Corinth (about AD 50 to 52). That was his field, “the measure of the rule which God hath distributed to us.” He had no need to boast about other men's work.

His hope is to take the gospel on “in the regions beyond you,” and he does: Romans, written from Corinth a year later, speaks of going to Rome and on to Spain. The dotted line shows where his eyes were turning.

“Another man's line” means work another man has already done. Don't take credit for it. If someone built the road you are walking on, say so. At work, on a team, and in a group, give the credit away.

Let him glory in the Lord.

“Glory” here is boast, and Paul quotes Jeremiah's old rule for it. Not the wise man in his wisdom, not the strong man in his strength, not the rich man in his riches:

“But let him that glorieth glory in this, that he understandeth and knoweth me, that I am the LORD which exercise lovingkindness, judgment, and righteousness, in the earth: for in these things I delight, saith the LORD.”

Jeremiah 9:24

If you have any good news about yourself, such as days clean, a job, a family that took you back, the honest word is “look what the Lord did.” It is no less yours to be glad about. It just stays true on the bad days, because it never depended on you.

Whose “well done” counts?

Self-praise

“Look at me.”

Praise you hand out to yourself, or work for from other people. It never lasts, and it needs feeding every day.

The Lord's approval

“Well done.”

The only verdict that stands. Jesus has already commended every man who is in Him, and no failure yesterday cancels it.

“Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth; a stranger, and not thine own lips.”

Proverbs 27:2

Today: do your work well and don't announce it. If you are going to brag about anything, brag about Jesus. Then thank one person out loud for something they did for you.

Points to remember

  1. Meekness“the meekness and gentleness of Christ” 10:1Meek is strength on a leash. You don't have to be soft to stop being dangerous.
  2. The war“the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God” 10:4The fight is real, and the weapons are His: truth, His word, prayer, and the gospel.
  3. Strongholds“bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ” 10:5Catch the thought at the first stage: notice it, name it, hand it to Christ, and move.
  4. Appearances“Do ye look on things after the outward appearance?” 10:7Men look at the outside. The Lord looks at whose you are, and if you are Christ's, that is settled.
  5. Comparing“comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise” 10:12Look down and you get proud; look up and you quit. Only Christ is the measure, and He measured up for you.
  6. Glorying“he that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord” 10:17Brag about what He did. His “well done” is the only one that stands.

Question 1 of 6

Meekness

How does Paul answer people who say he is weak?

What verses tell us about this?

“Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.”

Matthew 11:29

Answer

With the meekness and gentleness of Christ, which is strength under control, not weakness. And he is the same man in a letter as in person.

Question 2 of 6

The war

What kind of war are we in, and what are our weapons?

What verses tell us about this?

“Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil.”

Ephesians 6:11

Answer

A real war, though not fought with our own methods. The weapons are “mighty through God”: His truth, His word, prayer, and the gospel. We use His, not ours.

Question 3 of 6

Strongholds

What should we do with a thought that pulls us toward sin?

What verses tell us about this?

“But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.”

James 1:14–15

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Answer

Take it captive early, before it becomes a plan or an act. Notice it, name it, hand it to Christ, and move. God always provides a way out.

Question 4 of 6

Appearances

Why must we not judge a man by how he looks?

What verses tell us about this?

“But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

Answer

Because men look at appearances, and the Lord looks at the heart. Paul was called weak and contemptible, and was one of the strongest men alive. A man who is Christ's is not defined by his looks, his speech, or his record.

Question 5 of 6

Comparing

Why is comparing ourselves with other men not wise?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because we pick our own ruler: look down and we get proud, look up and we get ashamed. God hands each of us our own field and measure, and Christ is the only standard. He measured up for us.

Question 6 of 6

Glorying

In whom should a man glory, and whose approval counts?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth; a stranger, and not thine own lips.”

Proverbs 27:2

Answer

In the Lord. He is the one who commends a man, and His approval does not depend on how we did yesterday.
