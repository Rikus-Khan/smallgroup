Titus 2

Grace that saves, grace that trains

Paul tells Titus what a changed life looks like in every age and every job. And then he says what makes it possible: the grace of God, which does more than forgive.

Part one

Sound teaching, every age

Verses 1–10

Last chapter was about leaders. This one is about everyone else. Right teaching is the kind you can see in how a man lives.

What the years should make of a man

  1. Soberclear-headed, not ruled by a drink or a mood
  2. Gravesteady and serious, not a clown or a cynic
  3. Temperateself-controlled, can say no to himself
  4. Sound in faithhealthy in what he believes
  5. In charityin love, which is how his belief shows
  6. In patiencestays steady when things drag on

If you are a new man, this list is a picture of what a few more years with God can build. If you have been in it a while, you are the older man to someone: a younger guy is watching how you handle a bad week. That is a job God has given you, whether you applied or not.

Every age has a job

  1. Older mensteady, so the young can lean on them
  2. Older womenteachers of good things, who teach the young women
  3. Young mensober minded, a pattern of good works
  4. Servantsfaithful in their work

The church Paul pictures is a family, not an audience: older teach younger, and each does his own part. Nobody is left to work it out alone, and nobody is only a student.

Some of the details in verses 4 and 5 are written for homes in Crete 2,000 years ago, and good Bible teachers differ on how to apply every one of them today. The aim is plain, though: homes where the word of God is not made a joke by how the people in them live.

A young man, and a pattern

a young man, with energy and something to prove → sober minded Clear-headed, in charge of himself. It covers drink, but also temper, money, women and pride. A young man's trouble is not too little fire, it is fire with no steering.

Then Paul asks something bigger: “a pattern of good works.” A pattern is the thing others copy: a stamp that leaves its mark. Younger men are watching. A man in his first year of recovery is already a pattern to the one who arrives next, whether he feels like one or not.

Notice the target in verse 8: “sound speech, that cannot be condemned.” Your mouth is part of the pattern.

Doing a job well, for Jesus' sake

  • answering againtalking back, the last word
  • purloiningtaking small things that aren't yours: time, tools, change
  • all good fidelitya man the boss can trust with the keys

A word of honesty: the servants here were slaves, because slavery was part of that world. Paul is not defending it. He is telling men who had no way out how to live with dignity inside it. (Read Philemon to see where the gospel takes that.) Today the same words reach the man with a boss he doesn't like.

“Adorn” means to make something look attractive, like jewelry on a dress. Show up on time, do the job right, keep your hands off what isn't yours, and your life makes the gospel look good. For most of us, that is where the first people hear it.

Who is watching

When believers live a mess

“the word of God is blasphemed”

People hear God spoken of and think of how we act. A man says he's changed and goes back to the same lies at work: they curse the word of God, not just the man (verse 5).

When a life is sound

“having no evil thing to say of you”

The one who opposes the gospel is “ashamed” (verse 8), with nothing to say. And the doctrine is made attractive in everything (verse 10).

This isn't about being perfect to impress people. You have been honest about your past, and nothing in this chapter asks you to hide it. It is about this: the men around you are watching to see if it was real. Today is one more day to show them.

Part two

The grace that teaches

Verses 11–15

The reason for everything above. A man does not do any of this by gritting his teeth. He does it because of what grace has done, and keeps doing.

Grace saves, and grace trains

Verse 11Appeared to all men

Not a few. Everyone is offered it.

A gift, not a wage.

→
Verse 12Teaching usungodlinessworldly lustslive soberlyrighteouslygodly

Most of us were told, or told ourselves, that grace is a free pass. Paul says grace is also a teacher. The same kindness that forgave you will turn around and show you how to live, and it teaches a man who has already been saved, not one trying to earn it.

Three words for how to live

  1. Soberlytoward yourself: clear-headed, in charge of you
  2. Righteouslytoward other people: fair, honest, paying what you owe
  3. Godlytoward God: living as if He is in the room

Notice “in this present world.” Not in a monastery. In the shop, the truck, the apartment. Grace doesn't pull a man out of life. It teaches him to live in the middle of it.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8-9

That is the order: saved by grace, never by works, and then trained for good works by the same grace.

The blessed hope

“The great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ.”

Paul is looking forward to the day Jesus appears again. And he gives Jesus a name that leaves no room: the great God and our Saviour. The One who rescued you is God Himself.

That is the hope for a man who failed yesterday: not that you'll be good enough by then, but that the One coming back is the One who already gave Himself for you. A man with a hope like that does not have to live as if today is all there is.

What He gave, and what He made

He gave HimselfRedeemed

Bought back out of slavery to “all iniquity.” Not some of it. All of it.

He cleansPurified

Washed up and made fit for Him. Not by effort, by His work.

He keepsHis own people

“Peculiar” means treasured, one's very own. And eager to do good, because they are glad.

Whatever it was that owned you, the verse says He bought you out of all of it. Then Paul ends the chapter with a word for Titus, young and leading older men: “Let no man despise thee.” Be faithful, and let your life be your answer.

  4. Work“that they may adorn the doctrine of God our Saviour in all things” 2:10Honest work, done well, makes the gospel look good to the people closest to you.

Question 1 of 6

Everyday

What does “sound doctrine” look like in an ordinary life?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It looks like a life: how men and women of every age behave at home, at work and with their mouths. In Titus 2, teaching that is healthy shows up in sober, steady, honest living. It is not only what a man believes. It is what that belief does to a Tuesday.

Question 2 of 6

Older men

What should the older men be?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Sober, grave, temperate, and sound in faith, love and patience. That is what years with God should make of a man: steady and clear-headed, and healthy in what he believes. Older believers are also teachers, and the younger ones are watching them.

Question 3 of 6

Young men

What is a young man to be, and what is he to show?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Sober minded: clear-headed and in charge of himself. And he is to show a pattern of good works, with speech nobody can fault. Younger men are always copying somebody. Paul asks them to be a pattern worth copying.

Question 4 of 6

Work

Why work well, even for a boss you don't like?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because honest work makes the gospel look good. A man who is on time, doesn't take what isn't his, and doesn't talk back, is a man other people can believe. Paul is not praising bosses. He is saying that how you work is part of what you are saying about God.

Question 5 of 6

Grace

What does the grace of God do besides save?

What verses tell us about this?

“For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.”

Ephesians 2:10

Answer

It teaches. The same grace that offers salvation to all men then trains the saved to say no to ungodliness and worldly lusts, and to live soberly, righteously and godly in this present world. Grace is not a free pass. It is a kind teacher, and we never graduate.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

What is the “blessed hope,” and what did Jesus give to get us there?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The blessed hope is the appearing of Jesus, the great God and our Saviour. To get us there He gave Himself, to buy us back from all iniquity and to make us a people of His own. It is why a failure yesterday is not the end of anyone's story.
