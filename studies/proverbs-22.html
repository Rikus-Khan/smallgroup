Proverbs 22

A good name and the words of the wise

What a good name is worth, how a child is trained, and why the company you keep shapes the man you become.

Part one

A good name

Verses 1–9

Some things money can't buy, and some things it can't fix.

What's your name worth?

Great riches

Can go overnight

Money can be lost, stolen, or spent. And it can't buy people's trust.

A good name

Built a day at a time

A name is what people know they can count on from you. It outlasts you. “A good name is better than precious ointment” (Ecclesiastes 7:1).

Some of us have a name around town we're not proud of. Here's the good news: in Christ you already have a new name before God (2 Corinthians 5:17). And your name with people gets rebuilt the same way it got torn down: one kept promise at a time.

Verse 2 levels the ground: rich and poor sit in the same room, and “the LORD is the maker of them all.” Nobody's worth is set by his bank account.

See it coming

The prudent man isn't braver than anyone else. He's just paying attention. He sees trouble down the road and gets off it early.

The simple man sees the same sign and keeps walking. “It won't happen to me.”

In recovery we'd call this knowing your triggers: the places, people, and moods that lead toward the pit. The time to turn is at the sign, not at the edge.

A principle, not a guarantee

“Train up” is the Hebrew word for dedicating a new house: starting a child off right, on purpose. Like a young tree tied to a stake, he usually grows the way he was set.

But Proverbs tells how life usually works. It isn't a contract. Each person chooses for himself:

“The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son.”

Ezekiel 18:20

God is a perfect Father, and Adam still walked away. A wayward child doesn't prove a parent failed, and a rough home doesn't doom a child.

For the fathers in the room

Some of us weren't there. Years went to the bottle, the pipe, or a cell, and our kids grew up without us. That hurts, and there's no pretending it doesn't.

“And I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten.”

Joel 2:25

God doesn't hand back the calendar, but He is in the business of restoring. Start where you are: show up, keep small promises, say “I was wrong” without excuses, and pray for each child by name. Even with grown children or grandkids, it's not too late to be a faithful example.

You can't force a heart to come back. That's God's work (21:1). But you can be the kind of father Jesus described, one who sees his child “a great way off” and runs to him (Luke 15:20). That's how our heavenly Father treats us.

Debt makes a man somebody's servant.

Every payday, part of your work already belongs to someone else. Payday loans, the dope man's tab, money borrowed from family: they all put a leash on you.

“Owe no man any thing, but to love one another: for he that loveth another hath fulfilled the law.”

Romans 13:8

Verses 8 and 9 finish the thought: sow trouble and you reap emptiness, but the man with “a bountiful eye”, a generous eye that notices people in need, is blessed. Freedom with money comes from paying down what you owe and learning to give.

Part two

Lions and pits

Verses 10–16

Excuses, traps, and the trouble a scorner brings.

One man can poison a room

A scorner mocks everything: God, rules, people who are trying. Where he goes, arguing follows. Show him the door, and the fighting leaves with him.

Sometimes the scorner is a friend we need to step away from. Sometimes, if we're honest, he's the voice in our own head that sneers at change. Either way, he has to go.

Verse 11 shows the opposite man: pure in heart and gracious with his words. Even a king wants a friend like that.

A lion in the street

The lazy man won't go to work because there might be a lion outside. It's ridiculous on purpose. When we don't want to do something, any excuse will do.

“There is a lion without.”ExcuseThe original. Too dangerous to leave the house.
“I'll start Monday.”ExcuseTomorrow is always a great day to change.
“They don't want me at that meeting.”ExcuseFear dressed up as a reason.

What's your lion? Name it out loud, and it usually shrinks.

Smooth words, deep pit

The “strange woman” in Proverbs is the woman who isn't your wife: the affair, the hookup, the screen. Her words sound sweet. What they lead to is a pit that's easy to fall into and hard to climb out of.

The second half is sobering: the man who falls in is one the LORD is already displeased with. The pit is usually the end of a road that started with drifting away from God.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Correction, not rage

Nobody has to teach a child to be foolish. It comes built in. Loving correction is how a parent loosens its grip.

But hear this clearly: this verse is not permission for rage or abuse. If you were beaten in anger as a boy, that wasn't what God meant by correction. God's discipline is steady, fair, and done in love for the child's good.

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

Verse 16 warns the other way: getting rich off the poor, or buttering up the rich, ends in want.

Part three

The words of the wise

Verses 17–29

A new section begins: a teacher calls his student close.

A new section starts here

  1. A father's lessons1–9
  2. Proverbs of Solomon10:1–22:16
  3. Words of the wise22:17–24:22
  4. More from the wise24:23–34
  5. Copied by Hezekiah's men25–29
  6. Agur and Lemuel30–31

Up to here we've had mostly one-line sayings. Now the style changes to short lessons of two or three verses. Some scholars think the Hebrew behind “excellent things” (v. 20) can also be read “thirty,” and they count about thirty sayings in this section.

Why all these sayings?

The teacher tells us why he wrote: not so we'd be clever, but “that thy trust may be in the LORD.” Wisdom isn't a self-help program. It leads you to lean on God.

“This day, even to thee.” He means it personally. These words are for you, today.

“But of him are ye in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption:”

1 Corinthians 1:30

Every road in Proverbs ends up at the same place: trusting God. And God made Jesus our wisdom.

The poor man's lawyer

The city gate was the courthouse. The elders sat there to settle cases. A poor man with no money and no connections was easy to cheat there.

But he isn't alone. “The LORD will plead their cause.” God takes the case of the man nobody else will stand up for. Cheat the poor, and you've picked a fight with Him.

If you've been the man nobody stood up for, God saw it. And if you've been the one who took advantage, there's still time to make it right.

You become who you run with

Anger is catching. Hang around a hothead and you'll start reacting like him without noticing.

“Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.”

1 Corinthians 15:33

Changing your crowd isn't being stuck up. It's guarding your soul. And it works both ways: run with godly men, and you'll learn their ways.

Co-signing

To “strike hands” was to shake on a deal. A surety promised to pay another man's debt if he didn't. Today we call it co-signing.

Youshake on his loanhe misses paymentslender comes to you“thy bed from under thee”

If you can't afford to lose it, don't promise it. You can help a man in need without signing away your own bed. Give what you can freely, and keep your word small enough to keep.

Moving the stones

Land lines were marked with a row of stones. Slide them over a few feet at night, and you've stolen your neighbor's land with nobody the wiser.

God gave each family its land (the stones were set by “thy fathers”). Moving them was theft, and it usually hurt the weakest, like the fatherless (23:10).

Verse 29 ends the chapter on a better note: a man who's good at his work and does it faithfully will be noticed. Do honest work well, and doors open.

  3. God's justice“For the LORD will plead their cause” 22:23God stands up for the man nobody else will.
  4. Excuses“There is a lion without” 22:13Name your lion. Most excuses shrink in the daylight.

Question 1 of 6

Character

What is worth more than money?

What verses tell us about this?

“A good name is better than precious ointment; and the day of death than the day of one’s birth.”

Ecclesiastes 7:1

Answer

A good name, and the favor that comes with it. Riches can disappear, but a reputation for being a man people can count on lasts. Humility and the fear of the LORD lead to the things money can't buy. In Christ we're made new, and our name with people is rebuilt one kept promise at a time.

Question 2 of 6

Fathers and children

Is “Train up a child” a promise or a principle?

What verses tell us about this?

“The soul that sinneth, it shall die. The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son: the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him, and the wickedness of the wicked shall be upon him.”

Ezekiel 18:20

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

Answer

A principle. Children who are trained well usually stay on that road, but every person makes his own choices before God. A godly parent can have a wayward child, and a child from a hard home can come to Christ. For fathers who missed years, God restores. Start today, and be the father who runs to meet his child.

Question 3 of 6

God's justice

Who stands up for the poor?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD Himself. He made rich and poor alike, and He takes up the case of the poor man nobody else will defend. Those who cheat the poor will answer to Him. And He blesses the man with a generous eye who shares his bread.

Question 4 of 6

Excuses

What excuses keep a man from doing what he should?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“There is a lion without.” A man who doesn't want to do something will always find a reason, even a silly one. The prudent man looks honestly at the real dangers and acts. The diligent man just does the work, and he's the one who ends up standing before kings.

Question 5 of 6

Friends

Why does it matter who you run with?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.”

1 Corinthians 15:33

Answer

Because you learn their ways. Run with an angry man and you'll pick up his anger, and it becomes a snare to your soul. Cast out the scorner and the fighting leaves with him. Choose friends who walk with God, and you'll learn their ways instead.

Question 6 of 6

Trust

Why did God give us the words of the wise?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So that our trust would be in the LORD. These sayings aren't just tips for living. They're meant to bring us close to God, to give us certainty in His truth, and to help us answer others well. And all wisdom leads us to Jesus, who is made unto us wisdom.
