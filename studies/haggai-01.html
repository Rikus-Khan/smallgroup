Haggai 1

Consider your ways

God's people came home from exile, roofed their own houses, and left His house in rubble. God tells them to stop and look at where that has got them.

The book of Haggai

Who: Haggai, “the LORD'S messenger” (1:13). His name seems to mean “festive” or “my feast.”

When: about 520 BC, after the exile, “in the second year of Darius,” king of Persia. Four messages in under four months.

To whom: the Jews back home in Judah and Jerusalem, a small remnant living under Persian rule.

About eighteen years earlier, Persia let them go home from Babylon to rebuild the temple. They laid its foundation, then quit.

When Haggai preached

  1. 586 BCBabylon burns the temple
  2. 538Cyrus sends the Jews home
  3. 536foundation laid; work stops
  4. 520Haggai
  5. about 516the temple finished

Sixteen years with a foundation and no house. Then God sent Haggai, and in about four more years the temple stood.

Part one

Is it time?

Verses 1–4

The people say it isn't the right time to build God's house. God asks how it was the right time to build their own.

Who's who

  1. Dariusking of Persia, ruling a huge empire from far away. Judah was one small province in it.
  2. Zerubbabelthe governor of Judah, and a grandson of King Jehoiachin, from David's royal line
  3. Joshuathe high priest, son of Josedech, the priest who was carried off to Babylon
  4. Haggaithe prophet who brings them God's word

The “sixth month” fell in late summer, around our August, just as the grapes, figs and olives were being gathered. Haggai speaks on the first day of the month, when people came together for the new moon.

“Not yet”

The work had stopped for a reason. Their neighbours troubled them, hired men to work against them at the Persian court, and got it shut down.

“Then ceased the work of the house of God which is at Jerusalem. So it ceased unto the second year of the reign of Darius king of Persia.”

Ezra 4:24

But sixteen years later, they weren't saying “we can't.” They were saying “it isn't time.” The fear had turned into a habit, and the habit had a reason ready.

The peopleThe time is not come, the time that the LORD'S house should be built.

The LORDIs it time for you, O ye, to dwell in your cieled houses?
they had time for theirs

Two houses

Their houses

“Cieled”

Panelled. Walls and ceilings covered in wood, maybe even cedar. Not just a roof over their heads: finished, comfortable homes.

God's house

“Lie waste”

A foundation laid years before, and weeds growing through the stones. A ruin in the middle of Jerusalem.

God isn't against a man having a good home. The trouble was the order. Every spare hour and every spare coin went to their own house first, and there was never anything left over for His.

A man in recovery knows this one. “I'll get to church when things settle down. I'll start praying when I've got my life together.” Later has a way of never coming.

Part two

Consider your ways

Verses 5–11

God tells them to take an honest look at their lives. They have been working hard, and getting nowhere.

Set your heart on your roads

שִׂימוּ לְבַבְכֶם said sīmū levavkem set your heart: stop, think it through, take it to heart

“Ways” is the word for a road, a path you've been walking. God is saying: look at the road you're on, and look where it's been taking you. He says it twice, in verses 5 and 7.

This isn't God rubbing their noses in it. It's the first step of every turnaround: an honest look in the mirror.

“Let us search and try our ways, and turn again to the LORD.”

Lamentations 3:40

A bag with holes

  • sown muchbring in little
  • ye eatnot enough
  • ye drinknot filled
  • ye clothe younone warm
  • earneth wagesa bag with holes

They weren't lazy. They were working hard, and it all leaked out the bottom. Some men know that feeling well: a paycheck gone before the week is out, and nothing to show for it.

“Wherefore do ye spend money for that which is not bread? and your labour for that which satisfieth not? hearken diligently unto me, and eat ye that which is good, and let your soul delight itself in fatness.”

Isaiah 55:2

The fix is simple

God doesn't hand them a twelve-part plan. He gives them three plain orders:

  1. Go upto the mountain
  2. Bringwood
  3. Buildthe house
  4. “I will take pleasure in it”saith the LORD

The stone was already there, from the ruins and the old foundation. What they needed was timber from the hills, and a willingness to get started. God didn't ask for Solomon's gold. He asked them to begin.

“But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.”

Matthew 6:33

Why the harvest failed

God tells them plainly that the drought was not bad luck. He called for it. And it touched every part of life:

  1. the heavenno dew
  2. the earthno fruit
  3. the cornthe grain fields
  4. the vines and olivesthe new grapes and the oil
  5. men and cattlehungry and thirsty
  6. the labourof every hand

God had told Israel long before that this is how He would get their attention if they forgot Him. The empty barns were a message: you have been running to your own house, and you have left Me out.

It was discipline, not destruction. God made life hard so they would come back to Him, and as soon as they did, He spoke kindly to them.

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

Part three

I am with you

Verses 12–15

This time, the people listen. And God's answer is five words long.

They listened

This is rare in the prophets. Most of them preached for years and were ignored, mocked, or killed. Haggai preached once, and the leaders and the whole remnant obeyed.

They “did fear before the LORD.” Not panic: a fresh, sober respect for God. They had stopped taking Him lightly.

Theyobeyed and feared the LORD (v. 12)
→
Godsaid, “I am with you” (v. 13)

Notice what God didn't say. No lecture about the sixteen lost years. No probation. The minute they turned, He met them with a promise.

I am with you.

That's the whole message of verse 13. Not “I will be with you once you've finished the job,” but I am, right now, while the place is still a ruin.

For a man starting over, that's everything. God doesn't wait at the finish line for you to get there on your own. He comes and stands with you at the starting line.

“Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.”

Matthew 28:20

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Twenty-three days

day 1Haggai preaches (v. 1)
day 24they are at work on the house (v. 15)
23 daysfrom “not yet” to “let's go”

They chose to obey, and God stirred them up: He put fresh heart in them for the work. The same God who calls a man to get up also gives him the strength to keep going.

Some of those days likely went to clearing the rubble, cutting timber, and getting the harvest in. But the work began, and the house that had lain waste for sixteen years started to rise.

“For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure.”

Philippians 2:13

Points to remember

  1. Priorities“The time is not come” 1:2“Later” has a way of never coming. Put God first now, not when life settles down.
  2. Taking stock“Consider your ways” 1:5An honest look at the road you're on is the first step back to God.
  3. Never enough“A bag with holes” 1:6Work and wages without God leak out the bottom. Only He can fill a man.
  4. God's discipline“I did blow upon it. Why?” 1:9God may let things come up short to get our attention and bring us home.
  5. Obedience“Obeyed the voice of the LORD their God” 1:12When they obeyed, God met them at once, and the work began.
  6. God's presence“I am with you, saith the LORD” 1:13God stands with the man who turns back, right from the first day.

Question 1 of 6

Priorities

What happens when the things of God get put off until “later”?

What verses tell us about this?

“But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.”

Matthew 6:33

Answer

“Later” becomes years. The people had time and money for their own houses, but God's house sat in ruins for sixteen years. Whatever we keep putting off, we are really putting last. God asks to be first, now.

Question 2 of 6

Taking stock

Why does God tell us to stop and consider our ways?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let us search and try our ways, and turn again to the LORD.”

Lamentations 3:40

Answer

Because we can't turn around until we see where we're headed. God tells them twice to take their lives to heart and look at the road they're on. It's not to shame them. It's so they'll turn back to Him.

Question 3 of 6

Never enough

Why does a life that leaves God out never feel like enough?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore do ye spend money for that which is not bread? and your labour for that which satisfieth not? hearken diligently unto me, and eat ye that which is good, and let your soul delight itself in fatness.”

Isaiah 55:2

Answer

Because we were made for God, and nothing else fills that place. They ate and weren't full, drank and weren't satisfied, earned and lost it. More work, more money, or more of anything can't plug the holes. Only coming back to the Lord can.

Question 4 of 6

God's discipline

What is God doing when everything keeps coming up short?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

Answer

Sometimes He is getting our attention. Not every hard time is discipline, but here God says plainly that the drought was His doing, sent to turn His people back. It hurt, and it worked. The moment they turned, He said, “I am with you.”

Question 5 of 6

Obedience

What happens when God's people actually obey?

What verses tell us about this?

“For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure.”

Philippians 2:13

Answer

God meets them. They obeyed, and God promised His presence, stirred up their spirits, and the work that had been stalled for sixteen years started again in twenty-three days. Obedience is often just the first step. God gives the strength for the next one.

Question 6 of 6

God's presence

Is God with a man who turns around and starts over?

What verses tell us about this?

“Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.”

Matthew 28:20

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Answer

Yes. God didn't wait for the temple to be finished. He said “I am with you” while it was still a ruin and they had only just turned around. Jesus promises the same to everyone who follows Him: always, to the end.
