Leviticus 21

Holy priests, and a place at the table

The LORD tells Moses what it costs the men who serve at his altar to stay holy: how they mourn, whom they marry, and who may come near. Then comes the surprise at the end: the man who may not come near the altar still eats the bread of his God.

Part one

“They shall be holy unto their God”

Verses 1–9

The priests of Aaron's house: the dead, the marks of mourning, and the name of their God.

  • Aaronthe high priest
    • Nadab
    • Abihu
    • Eleazar

Whom is he talking to?

“Speak unto the priests the sons of Aaron.” Not to all Israel this time, but to the family the LORD set apart in Exodus 28 and 29, washed, clothed and anointed in Leviticus 8.

Two of Aaron's four sons were already dead when this was spoken. Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD in chapter 10, and their kinsmen carried them out (10:4–5). Aaron knows what burying a son is. Hold that in mind while you read about the dead.

Six, and only six

Defiled means made unclean. A body that has died was unclean, and whoever touched it was shut out of the holy things until he was clean again. A priest who spent his days at the altar could not go from grave to grave. But look at who is allowed:

  • his mother
  • his father
  • his son
  • his daughter
  • his brother
  • his sister“a virgin,” still in his house, living in his house, with no husband of her own

Kin means family, and nigh means near. God does not tell a priest to stop loving his own. He lets him grieve over the six nearest to him, and he gives the grief a place. The rule is about the crowd of other funerals, not about a man's own heart.

The Priest who touched the dead

One day, in the town of Nain, a widow was following the bier of her only son. The law said whoever touched the dead was unclean. Watch what Jesus does:

“And he came and touched the bier: and they that bare him stood still. And he said, Young man, I say unto thee, Arise.”

Luke 7:14

He touched the bier, and the dead man sat up. Jesus did not catch the uncleanness of death; death caught the cleanness of Jesus. And when his friend Lazarus was dead, the shortest verse in the Bible says what he did:

“Jesus wept.”

John 11:35

If you are carrying a grief today, a father, a brother, a son, a friend lost to the bottle or the needle, you do not carry it alone. The priests were allowed to weep for their own. So are you.

Baldness, beards and cuttings

  • baldness upon their headshaving the head bare as a sign of mourning
  • the corner of their beardcutting the beard's edge, another show of grief
  • cuttings in their fleshcutting the skin for the dead
  • a chief mana man of standing, set over others, and watched

These were the ways the nations around mourned their dead. The priests were to mourn like people who know the LORD, not like people who do not. The same marks are forbidden to all Israel in 19:27–28, “for the dead.” The verse is about grief and about whose people you are, not about what is on a man's skin.

If you carry ink or scars from the old life, hear again what chapter 19 said: no man is shut out of Christ by his skin. Ephesians 1:6 says we are “accepted in the beloved.” What God looks at is the blood that covers a man, not what is written on him.

So“therefore they shall be holy”

Profane is the word the chapter keeps coming back to. It means to treat a holy thing as common. It comes nine times in these twenty-four verses (4, 6, 7, 9 twice, 12, 14, 15, 23). A man who handles the LORD's bread every day, and handles it like any other bread, has profaned the name of his God.

The offerings “made by fire” are called “the bread of their God.” The closer a man works to the fire, the more it matters how he lives. And notice the last half of verse 8: “I the LORD, which sanctify you, am holy.” The standard is not a mountain the priests climb on their own legs. It is the holiness of the one who sanctifies them.

A hard verse, plainly

The priest's daughter shared his holy house, and a daughter who profaned herself “by playing the whore” brought it on her father: “she profaneth her father” (verse 9). The sentence, burning, was for Israel's courts at Sinai. It was civil law for that nation, and God himself was the judge of it.

Every man reading this knows what it is to bring shame on a family. The Bible does not play it down: some sins reach past the sinner. But look how the New Testament speaks to people with that kind of past, in the church at Corinth:

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed.” Not burned. Washed. The fire fell on a Substitute, and the sinner is washed.

Part two

“The crown of the anointing oil of his God is upon him”

Verses 10–15

The high priest: more is asked of the man who goes into the holiest place.

More asked, because more given

The other priests

for six near kin

They could go to the dead of their own house (verses 2–3).

The high priest

not even for his father or his mother

“upon whose head the anointing oil was poured”: he did not go out of the sanctuary (verses 10–12).

Rend means tear, and tearing the clothes was the common sign of sorrow. The same order was given to Aaron and his sons on the day Nadab and Abihu died: “Uncover not your heads, neither rend your clothes; lest ye die” (10:6). On the worst day of his life Aaron was not allowed even to show his grief. The glory of the LORD came first.

The high priest who tore his clothes

Centuries later another high priest did the one thing this verse forbids, and he did it for the wrong reason:

“Then the high priest rent his clothes, saying, He hath spoken blasphemy; what further need have we of witnesses? behold, now ye have heard his blasphemy.”

Matthew 26:65

That high priest was Caiaphas (Matthew 26:57). He tore his clothes to show he was shocked at the Son of God. The man in front of him was the true High Priest, and he was about to be taken outside the gate.

The High Priest and his bride

The high priest's wife was to be a virgin of his own people. Not a widow, not divorced, not “profane,” not an harlot. It was one more way to keep the holy line unbroken (“neither shall he profane his seed among his people”).

Paul uses the picture of a pure bride for the church:

“For I am jealous over you with godly jealousy: for I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ.”

2 Corinthians 11:2

He presents her to himself

And for Christ and his people: the Bridegroom takes a bride who is not pure, and he makes her so.

“That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish.”

Ephesians 5:27

Look at who does the work. The bride is not “a glorious church” on her own. He presents her to himself. A man who has fallen again lately is not outside the bride; he is one of the people Christ is cleaning.

Holy, and yet able to feel it

The Old Testament high priest was a man with a rule book that kept him away from the dead. The New Testament High Priest is described like this:

“For such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and made higher than the heavens;”

Hebrews 7:26

Holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners. And yet he is not far off from us:

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:15-16

He is separate from sinners and has not sinned himself. But he did not stay away from people like us. He touched the bier. He wept at the tomb. “Come boldly.” The ones who had to stay away have a High Priest who invites them near.

Part three

“He shall eat the bread of his God”

Verses 16–24

The man with a blemish: what he cannot do, and what he still may.

Old words in the list

  • blemisha defect in the body, a mark that spoils
  • any thing superfluoushaving an extra part, more than the usual
  • brokenfooted, brokenhandedwith a broken foot or hand, a crippled one
  • crookbacktwith a bent, humped back
  • a dwarfa man of very small growth
  • scurvy, scabbeda scaly, itching skin; a skin crusted over

  • come nighcome near

The list is plain, and it is a list of bodies, not of sins. None of these men had done anything wrong. A priest who was blind or lame was still Aaron's son, still a priest by birth. The rule was about the look of the one standing at the altar, because the altar pictured the offering without a spot (22:19–20). It was not about his worth.

What he may not, and what he may

He may not

  • go in unto the vail
  • come nigh unto the altar
  • offer the offerings of the LORD made by fire

He may

  • eat the bread of his God
  • both “the most holy” and “the holy”
  • and he is still “of the seed of Aaron the priest”

Six times in these verses the word blemish comes (17, 18, 20, 21 twice, 23). Not one of them cancels verse 22. The man the altar turned away was fed from the altar. He shared in the most holy things, the portions of the sacrifices only the priests might eat.

There is a door that is closed to him, and there is a table that is open. God never says, “You are no priest at all.”

Here is where the two places were. The altar stands in the court, in the open, and the vail hangs deep inside, before the most holy place. A priest with a blemish stayed back from both. The holy portions were eaten “in the holy place”, which for the priests' food meant “in the court of the tabernacle” (6:16), and he had his share of them.

A lame man at the king's table

The same kindness shows up in David's house. Mephibosheth was Jonathan's son, lame in both feet, and a man who called himself “such a dead dog as I am” (2 Samuel 9:8). David set him at his own table:

“So Mephibosheth dwelt in Jerusalem: for he did eat continually at the king’s table; and was lame on both his feet.”

2 Samuel 9:13

“He did eat continually at the king's table; and was lame on both his feet.” The lameness is named in the same breath as the table. It did not move him to the servants' bench.

A man who has a record, a limp, a history is not turned away from the bread. There may be places you cannot go and things you cannot do any more: a job, a bar, a certain street. But you are fed from the same table as the best of them.

Every priest had a blemish

The law kept a blemished man from the altar, and yet every one of Aaron's sons had a blemish no eye could see:

“Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people’s: for this he did once, when he offered up himself.”

Hebrews 7:27

Aaron's sons had to offer for their own sins first. The altar was never open to a man because he was whole. It was open because of what was laid on it. We are “a royal priesthood” (1 Peter 2:9) with nothing spotless of our own to show, only the one who has no blemish.

Three times, the LORD says it

  • verse 8“I the LORD, which sanctify you, am holy”
  • verse 15“I the LORD do sanctify him”
  • verse 23“I the LORD do sanctify them”

Sanctify and hallow mean to make holy, to set apart for God. In this chapter the LORD says three times that it is he who does it. The priests were told to be holy, and the LORD says he is the one who makes them so.

Real world: you are the priest, and the table is set

The New Testament says it flat out about every believer, and it does not say “every believer without a blemish”:

“But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:”

1 Peter 2:9
  • SinHoliness here was a high standard, and the LORD never lowered it. Nor does the Gospel pretend a man can meet it on his own.
  • RegretA man shut out of the altar for his body was still fed from it. What you did does not cancel your place at the table.
  • GraceThree times: “I the LORD do sanctify.” You are made holy by another.
  • HopeThe High Priest touched the dead, wept at a tomb, and invites you to come boldly.
  • TodayGrieve honestly, with a brother beside you. Call or text one of your brothers in Christ, and tell your pastor what you are carrying. Then eat: open the Word today.

Question 1 of 6

Grief

For whom could an ordinary priest be defiled by the dead?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For six of his nearest kin: his mother, his father, his son, his daughter, his brother, and his sister who was still a virgin in his house. God gives grief a place.

Question 2 of 6

The marks

What were the priests not to do to their heads, their beards and their flesh?

What verses tell us about this?

“Ye shall not make any cuttings in your flesh for the dead, nor print any marks upon you: I am the LORD.”

Leviticus 19:28

Answer

They were not to make baldness upon their head, shave off the corner of their beard, or make any cuttings in their flesh. These were the nations' marks of mourning. The priests were to grieve as the LORD's own people.

Question 3 of 6

Why holy

Why must the priests be holy?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because they offer the offerings of the LORD, “the bread of their God.” And because the LORD who sanctifies them is holy. The closer to the fire, the more it matters.

Question 4 of 6

The high priest

What more was asked of the high priest?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No defiling himself even for a father or mother, no open mourning, no leaving the sanctuary, and a virgin to wife. The crown of the anointing oil was on him.

Question 5 of 6

The blemish

What could the priest with a blemish not do, and what could he still do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He could not offer the offerings, go in to the vail, or come near the altar. But he could eat the bread of his God, both the most holy and the holy. A blemish closed one door; the table stayed open.

Question 6 of 6

Who sanctifies

Who makes the priests holy?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD himself. Three times he says so: verses 8, 15 and 23. What a man could never make of himself, God makes of him.
