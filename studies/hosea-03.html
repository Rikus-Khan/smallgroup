Hosea 3

Bought back

Hosea's wife has sold herself into the gutter. God tells him to go and get her, pay the price, and bring her home. That is how God loves.

Part one

Go yet, love

Verses 1–3

Hosea is preaching to Israel, the northern kingdom, before Assyria carries it away. Now his own story turns.

Go yet, love.

“Yet” means again. Gomer had left Hosea for other men. She's still his wife, “beloved,” and still an adulteress. God doesn't tell Hosea to wait until she's sorry. He tells him to go love her now.

“Flagons of wine” were part of the feasts at the idol shrines. Israel loved the party that came with the false gods. Plenty of men know what it is to love the bottle more than the people who loved them.

Hosea 3:1

“…according to the love of the LORD toward the children of Israel, who look to other gods…”

Romans 5:8

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

The price

Somehow Gomer had ended up for sale, maybe as a slave, maybe in debt to the man who kept her. Hosea paid in silver and barley (an homer was about six bushels), as if he had to scrape it together:

To buy her back (Hosea 3:2)

  • fifteen pieces of silverpaid
  • an homer of barleypaid
  • an half homer of barleypaid
All togetherabout the price of a slave
BOUGHTand brought home

Many Bible teachers figure it came to about thirty pieces of silver, what the law set as the value of a slave:

“If the ox shall push a manservant or a maidservant; he shall give unto their master thirty shekels of silver, and the ox shall be stoned.”

Exodus 21:32

Paid for

Gomer chased freedom and ended up owned. That's what sin does. It promises a good time, then it sends the bill, and one day you find you belong to it.

And Hosea's purchase points past itself. God paid for us too, but not with silver:

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

“Redeemed” means bought back out of slavery. Gomer didn't earn it, and she didn't pay any of it. She was bought because she was loved.

A season set apart

Her part

“Thou shalt abide for me”

Many days at home. No other men, no going back to the old life. A season of being set apart.

His part

“So will I also be for thee”

He waits with her. He doesn't buy her back and then walk off. He keeps himself for her too.

Being bought back happens in a moment. Learning to live as someone who's been bought back takes time. Old habits, old friends and old places have to go, and trust grows slowly.

That's not a test to earn your way in. You're already bought. It's what a new life looks like. And through all of it, the One who paid for you is right there with you.

Part two

Many days

Verses 4–5

What Hosea did for Gomer, God will do for Israel. It will take a long time, but it will happen.

Without

Like Gomer shut away from her lovers, Israel will go a long time without both the good things and the false ones:

  1. a kingno throne of their own
  2. a princeno rulers
  3. a sacrificeno altar for the LORD
  4. an imageno idol pillars either
  5. an ephodthe priest's vest, used to ask God's will
  6. teraphimhousehold idols

It came true. Assyria carried off the north in 722 BC, and Babylon later took Judah. For many centuries since, the Jewish people have had no king on David's throne, and since the temple fell in AD 70, no sacrifice.

David their king

David had been dead about 250 years when Hosea wrote this. So “David their king” means the promised King from David's line: the Messiah.

“He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David: And he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.”

Luke 1:32–33

The angel said that to Mary about Jesus. God will keep this promise to Israel. And it shows us that a long season far from God doesn't have to be the end.

Points to remember

  1. God's love“Go yet, love a woman” 3:1God loves His people while they are still running.
  2. Redemption“So I bought her to me” 3:2We were bought back, with the precious blood of Christ.
  3. Sin's slavery“Who look to other gods” 3:1Sin promises freedom and ends in chains.
  4. New life“So will I also be for thee” 3:3A new life takes time, and He stays with us in it.
  5. Life without God“Many days without a king” 3:4Life without God is empty, but it can end.
  6. The King“David their king” 3:5The Son of David, Jesus, is the King they'll come home to.

Question 1 of 6

God's love

How far will God's love go?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

“The LORD hath appeared of old unto me, saying, Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee.”

Jeremiah 31:3

Answer

All the way to the gutter, and all the way to the cross. Hosea went and found a wife who had betrayed him, and paid to bring her home. God loved Israel the same way while they chased other gods. And Christ died for us while we were still sinners. You can't run past the reach of that love.

Question 2 of 6

Redemption

What does it mean to be bought back?

What verses tell us about this?

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

“What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God’s.”

1 Corinthians 6:19–20

Answer

It means someone paid what you owed to get you out. Gomer couldn't buy her own freedom, and neither can we. Jesus paid the price with His own blood. Now we belong to Him, the Holy Spirit lives in us, and we're not for sale to the old master again.

Question 3 of 6

Sin's slavery

What does sin do to the one who chases it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus answered them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin.”

John 8:34

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

Answer

It makes him a slave. Gomer went looking for a good time and ended up on the auction block. Every man who has been hooked on something knows how that goes. But Jesus doesn't leave the slave where he is. The Son makes him free indeed.

Question 4 of 6

New life

What does a redeemed life look like?

What verses tell us about this?

“What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God’s.”

1 Corinthians 6:19–20

Answer

It looks like coming home and staying home. Gomer would “abide” many days and not go back to other men. A redeemed man stays away from the old life, not to earn his place, but because he's been bought and he belongs to Someone now. And the One who bought him says, “so will I also be for thee.”

Question 5 of 6

Life without God

What happens when people live without God for many days?

What verses tell us about this?

“That at that time ye were without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from the covenants of promise, having no hope, and without God in the world:”

Ephesians 2:12

Answer

They lose the good with the bad. Israel lost its idols, but also its king and its altar. Life without God is “without” all down the line: without hope, without a home. But Hosea's next word is “Afterward.” The empty years don't have to be the end.

Question 6 of 6

The King

Who will Israel come back to in the latter days?

What verses tell us about this?

“But they shall serve the LORD their God, and David their king, whom I will raise up unto them.”

Jeremiah 30:9

“He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David: And he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.”

Luke 1:32–33

Answer

To the LORD their God, and to “David their king,” the promised Son of David. The angel told Mary that Jesus would sit on David's throne for ever. God will keep His word to Israel. And the same King welcomes anyone who comes to Him today.
