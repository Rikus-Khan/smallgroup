Hosea 11

How shall I give thee up?

God remembers Israel as a little boy He taught to walk. The boy ran off. But the Father's heart will not let him go.

Part one

When Israel was a child

Verses 1–4

Hosea still speaks to the northern kingdom before its fall to Assyria. Here a father remembers his boy's first steps.

Out of Egypt

Israel was God's “son” from the beginning. When Moses stood before Pharaoh, God told him to say:

“And thou shalt say unto Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD, Israel is my son, even my firstborn:”

Exodus 4:22

Hosea 11:1

When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt.

Matthew 2:15

And was there until the death of Herod: that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Out of Egypt have I called my son.

Matthew says Hosea's words were fulfilled when Joseph brought the young Jesus back from Egypt. Israel was God's son who kept running off. Jesus is God's Son who walked the same road, out of Egypt and through the wilderness, and never once went astray. He did what Israel couldn't, for Israel and for us.

Taught to walk

Picture a father bent over, holding his little boy up by the arms, one wobbly step at a time. That's how God remembers Israel's early years.

But verse 2: “so they went from them.” The more God called, the further Israel walked, to Baalim, the local Baal gods of rain and crops.

“They knew not that I healed them.” Many of us can see it now: the night we should have died, the arrest that stopped us. God was holding us up before we knew His name.

Cords of love

How a man drives an ox

Yoke, goad, and rope

He pulls it where it doesn't want to go and pokes it when it stops.

How God drew Israel

“Cords of a man, bands of love”

The pull of a father's hand. He eased the yoke off their jaws and fed them by hand.

“The LORD hath appeared of old unto me, saying, Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee.”

Jeremiah 31:3

God still draws men that way: not with a whip, but with love. Jesus said:

“And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me.”

John 12:32

Part two

Bent to backsliding

Verses 5–7

They had every reason to come home, and they refused.

A lean, then a loop

They won't go back to slavery in Egypt. Assyria will be their master instead, “because they refused to return.”

Bent means leaning one way by habit, like a tree that grew crooked in the wind. Israel leaned away from God without even trying. Anyone who has relapsed knows that loop.

But a loop can be broken. The way out is the very thing Israel refused: return. Hosea ends his book on that word (14:1).

Part three

How shall I give thee up?

Verses 8–12

The heart of God turns over inside Him.

Admah and Zeboim

Admah and Zeboim were two of the cities destroyed along with Sodom and Gomorrah. Moses had warned Israel this could be them:

“And that the whole land thereof is brimstone, and salt, and burning, that it is not sown, nor beareth, nor any grass groweth therein, like the overthrow of Sodom, and Gomorrah, Admah, and Zeboim, which the LORD overthrew in his anger, and in his wrath:”

Deuteronomy 29:23

Israel had earned it. And God says: How can I? His heart “is turned within” Him. That's a father who has every right to give up on his son, and can't bring himself to do it.

God, and not man

A man runs out of patience. A man says I'm done with you, and means it.

God says His mercy outweighs His anger because He is God. “My repentings are kindled” means His pity is stirred up and burning.

This isn't God going soft on sin. Assyria will still come (v. 5). But He won't wipe Israel out like Admah. Judgment is real. His mercy is greater.

The Lion calls them home

A lion's roar usually sends everything running away. This Lion roars and His children come running to Him, trembling, from the west, from Egypt, and from Assyria.

Like birds flying home, they come back from the places their sin carried them. “I will place them in their houses”: that is God's promise to Israel, and He keeps His word.

It shows us what He's like, too: He calls people home from wherever sin has taken them.

A fence of lies

Ephraim, the north

“compasseth me about with lies”

To compass is to surround. They fenced God in with smooth words, while the idols stayed in the back room.

Judah, the south

“yet ruleth with God”

For now, Judah still had the temple and David's line of kings. Its own drift would come later.

God isn't fooled by a fence of nice words. But remember verse 8. A man who has lied to God for years can stop and tell Him the truth, and he won't be turned away.

Points to remember

  1. A Father's love“When Israel was a child, then I loved him” 11:1God loved Israel first, held him up, and drew him with cords of love.
  2. Christ in Hosea“And called my son out of Egypt” 11:1Jesus is the true Son who walked Israel's road and never went astray (Matthew 2:15).
  3. Backsliding“My people are bent to backsliding from me” 11:7Sin becomes a lean and then a loop. The way out is to return.
  4. Mercy“How shall I give thee up, Ephraim?” 11:8God's anger is real, but His mercy is greater, because He is God and not man.
  5. Coming home“I will place them in their houses, saith the LORD” 11:11God calls His people home from wherever sin carried them.

Question 1 of 5

A Father's love

How does God describe His love for Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD hath appeared of old unto me, saying, Yea, I have loved thee with an everlasting love: therefore with lovingkindness have I drawn thee.”

Jeremiah 31:3

“Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.”

Psalm 103:13

Answer

Like a father with a little boy. He loved Israel before Israel did anything right, held him up by the arms while he learned to walk, healed him when he didn't know it, and led him with cords of love, not a whip. That's the love God has for every man who comes to Him.

Question 2 of 5

Christ in Hosea

How does Hosea 11:1 point to Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“And thou shalt say unto Pharaoh, Thus saith the LORD, Israel is my son, even my firstborn:”

Exodus 4:22

“And was there until the death of Herod: that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Out of Egypt have I called my son.”

Matthew 2:15

Answer

Matthew tells us it was fulfilled in Jesus. Israel was God's son, brought out of Egypt, who kept running away. Jesus, God's only begotten Son, was also brought out of Egypt as a child. He lived out Israel's story and got it right, every step. Where Israel failed, He stood in for them, and for us.

Question 3 of 5

Backsliding

Why did Israel keep walking away?

What verses tell us about this?

“Return, ye backsliding children, and I will heal your backslidings. Behold, we come unto thee; for thou art the LORD our God.”

Jeremiah 3:22

Answer

Their hearts had a lean. Every time God called, they went the other way, until backsliding was a habit. Habits like that don't break by themselves. But God's offer stood the whole time: return, and I will heal your backslidings. The loop breaks when a man turns around.

Question 4 of 5

Mercy

Why won't God give His people up?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not.”

Lamentations 3:22

Answer

Because of who He is. “I am God, and not man.” A man gives up when he's been hurt enough. God's compassion doesn't run out. Israel still faced real judgment, but God would not destroy them like Admah and Zeboim. If you think you've used up God's patience, read verse 8 again.

Question 5 of 5

Coming home

What will it look like when God's people come home?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

Answer

They'll come at the Lord's call, trembling, from every place their sin took them, and He will settle them in their own homes. God will keep that promise to Israel. And Jesus shows the same Father's heart in the story of the prodigal son: while the son is still far off, the father runs to meet him.
