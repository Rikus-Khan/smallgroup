Deuteronomy 16

Three feasts, the place the LORD shall choose, and judges who take no gift

Passover, weeks and tabernacles: three times a year all the males come before the LORD, and not empty. And the judges at the gates are to take no gift, for “that which is altogether just shalt thou follow”.

Part one

“Observe the month of Abib”

Verses 1–8

The passover, kept in the place the LORD chooses.

Bread of affliction, and a long memory

We read the whole feast in Leviticus 23, and the night itself in Exodus 12. Here Moses adds why. The bread is the bread of affliction, because “thou camest forth out of the land of Egypt in haste”, with no time for the dough to rise. Coast here means the whole country, every border of it. Seven days, and no leaven seen anywhere.

And it is for remembering: “that thou mayest remember the day when thou camest forth out of the land of Egypt all the days of thy life”. It was not a feast for one day or one year. The older man was to keep it until he died, so that the day he was set free was never lost.

“Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.”

1 Corinthians 5:7–8

Paul tells believers to keep the feast: Christ is “our passover”, and the old leaven is the old life. Remember the day you were brought out, and let it shape how you live today.

From the door to the place

In Egypt (Exodus 12)

At home, house by house

Each family killed the lamb in its own house, and put the blood on its own door.

In the land (Deuteronomy 16)

“the place which the LORD thy God shall choose”

Not within any of their gates. “There thou shalt sacrifice the passover at even, at the going down of the sun.” Then, in the morning, back to their tents.

Moses does not name the place. The words “the place which the LORD shall choose” (or “hath chosen”) come six times in this one chapter: verses 2, 6, 7, 11, 15 and 16. What matters is who chooses. God decides where he is met, and a man is not to pick his own spot. Verse 8 ends the week: “a solemn assembly”, a holy gathering, and “thou shalt do no work therein”.

Part two

“Thou shalt rejoice before the LORD”

Verses 9–17

Weeks and tabernacles, with the whole household, and nobody left out.

Weeks: from the sickle to the harvest home

Count seven weeks from the day they first put the sickle to the corn, and keep a feast. A freewill offering is one given willingly, because the heart wants to, with no fixed price on it. And it is measured by the blessing: “according as the LORD thy God hath blessed thee”. Leviticus 23 gave the days of this feast, and Deuteronomy gives its spirit: thanks, given out of what you have been given.

“And when the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all with one accord in one place.”

Acts 2:1

The feast of weeks is the day the New Testament calls Pentecost, when the Holy Ghost came upon the believers (Acts 2:1–4). The firstfruits of the field were brought in on this feast, and on that day the Spirit came to those who believed. Now he lives in every believer from salvation on.

Nine at the LORD's table

His own house: five

  • thou
  • thy son
  • thy daughter
  • thy manservant
  • thy maidservant

Those without a house to bring: four

  • the Levite
  • the stranger
  • the fatherless
  • the widow

The same nine stand in verse 11 and again in verse 14. The Levite had no land of his own (Numbers 18:20); the stranger was from another people, the fatherless had no father and the widow no husband. They all had a place at the feast.

And why? Because “thou shalt remember that thou wast a bondman in Egypt” (verse 12), nearly the words of 15:15. A man who was once outside, with nothing, makes room for the ones who are outside now. If you have ever been the one with no place to go, you are the right man to set a place for someone else.

Joy, by command

Rejoice comes three times in this chapter: verses 11, 14 and 15. “Therefore thou shalt surely rejoice,” says verse 15, after the corn, the wine and all the works of their hands. Joy is not only a feeling that comes when life is good: God tells his people to take it, in the right place and with the right people.

For a man who used to find his joy in a bottle or a pipe or a needle, this matters. The LORD is not against joy. He gave a feast, and he set the table, and he wants a man glad before him, with a clear head and a full table. Our joy is “before the LORD”.

“In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.”

John 7:37–38

The feast of tabernacles was the harvest feast of joy, and on its last day Jesus stood up and offered the thirsty man something better than any harvest: himself. Whoever is thirsty, today, may come.

Three roads to one place

“Three times in a year shall all thy males appear before the LORD thy God in the place which he shall choose.” Three of the feasts of Leviticus 23, now said as one summons. A man's year was to be built around meeting God.

“They shall not appear before the LORD empty”

The measure is plain: “as he is able”, “according to the blessing”. A poor man brought what a poor man had. God sets no sum here, only that no man comes with nothing to give, as he never let a servant go empty (15:13). The word empty stands only twice in Deuteronomy.

“But this I say, He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully. Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:6–7

A man with nothing in his pocket still has something to bring: his thanks, his time, a hand to help a brother. Come to the LORD with what you have.

Part three

“Judges and officers shalt thou make thee”

Verses 18–22

Honest judges, and an altar with nothing of the nations' worship beside it.

A gift blinds the wise

Wrest means to twist: a judge who wrests judgment bends it out of shape. “Respect persons” is to favour a man because of who he is, or what he can do for you. And “a gift doth blind the eyes of the wise”: not the fool, the wise. Even a good man cannot see straight with a gift in his hand.

“And thou shalt take no gift: for the gift blindeth the wise, and perverteth the words of the righteous.”

Exodus 23:8

“That which is altogether just”

That which is altogether just shalt thou follow, that thou mayest live.

Not what is partly just, or just when it costs nothing. Altogether just. And it is tied to life: “that thou mayest live, and inherit the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee”. A land with crooked judges does not stay a land worth living in.

For these men it is not only about judges. It is the boss who is paid to look away, the favour that buys a quiet word, the little “gift” that smooths a hard day. A believer is to be straight at work, in the court, in the dealing nobody sees. And if you have been crooked, it is a sin like another: confess it, and it is forgiven (1 John 1:9); then make it right where you can.

Nothing of the nations beside the altar

A grove here is a planted grove set up for idol worship, the way the nations worshipped. No grove “near unto the altar of the LORD”, and no image. The LORD “hateth” the image; the word is his own. The altar is for the LORD alone, and the chapter that began with a passover ends by guarding it.

“God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth.”

John 4:24

Moses has taught honest worship in the place God chooses, then honest courts at the gates, and then he shuts the door on the idols. A man can mix God with other things: a bit of the old crowd, a bit of the old comfort, set up beside the altar. The LORD wants all of the altar.

Real world: come with what you have

  • SinTaking a gift to bend a ruling, setting something else beside the altar, and the long habit of joy found anywhere but “before the LORD”.
  • RegretThe years that were not kept in remembrance. Moses says to remember the day you came out, “all the days of thy life”: it is not too late to start.
  • Grace“Christ our passover is sacrificed for us” (1 Corinthians 5:7). There is a place at the table for the widow, the stranger and the fatherless, and for you.
  • Hope“Thou shalt surely rejoice.” God wants you glad, with a clear head, in the company of his people.
  • TodayBring what you have, as you are able: thanks, an hour, a hand. Be straight in one dealing at work, and set a place for one man who has none. And if you are low, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today.

Points to remember

  1. The passover“there thou shalt sacrifice the passover at even, at the going down of the sun” 16:6In the place the LORD chose, not within their own gates, and at the season they came out of Egypt.
  2. The bread of affliction“even the bread of affliction; for thou camest forth out of the land of Egypt in haste” 16:3So they would remember the day all the days of their life; Christ is our passover (1 Corinthians 5:7).
  3. Rejoice“thou shalt rejoice before the LORD thy God, thou, and thy son, and thy daughter” 16:11The whole house, and the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless and the widow.
  4. Not empty“they shall not appear before the LORD empty” 16:16Every man gives as he is able, according to the blessing.
  5. The judges“Thou shalt not respect persons, neither take a gift: for a gift doth blind the eyes of the wise” 16:19A gift blinds even a wise man, and twists the words of the righteous.
  6. Altogether just“That which is altogether just shalt thou follow, that thou mayest live” 16:20All of it, and the land is theirs to keep.

Question 1 of 6

The passover

Where and when was the passover to be sacrificed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not within their gates, but at the place the LORD should choose to place his name: “at even, at the going down of the sun”, at the season they came out of Egypt.

Question 2 of 6

The bread of affliction

Why were they to eat unleavened bread for seven days?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It was the bread of affliction, because they came out of Egypt in haste; and so they would remember the day they came out all the days of their life.

Question 3 of 6

Rejoice

Who was to rejoice with Israel at the feasts?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

His own house (son, daughter, manservant and maidservant), and also the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless and the widow within his gates.

Question 4 of 6

Not empty

How were they to appear before the LORD at the three feasts?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not empty: every man was to give as he was able, according to the blessing the LORD had given him.

Question 5 of 6

The judges

What was a judge not to take, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A gift: “for a gift doth blind the eyes of the wise, and pervert the words of the righteous”.

Question 6 of 6

Altogether just

What was Israel to follow, and for what?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That which is altogether just, “that thou mayest live, and inherit the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee”.
