Hosea 13

O death, I will be thy plagues

Israel forgot the God who fed them in the desert. Judgment is coming like a lion. And right in the middle of it, God makes a promise bigger than death.

Part one

Kissing calves

Verses 1–3

Hosea's last warnings to the northern kingdom before Assyria takes it. Ephraim slid from humble to dead.

From respected to dead

  1. spake tremblinghumble, once (v. 1)
  2. exalted himselfgrew proud (v. 1)
  3. offended in Baalturned to idols (v. 1)
  4. sin more and moremelted silver into gods (v. 2)
  5. kiss the calves“and he died” (v. 1)

Ephraim was once a tribe people listened to with respect. Then it got proud, then it turned to Baal, and Hosea says “he died.” The nation was still walking around, but it was dead toward God.

Verse 2 is almost funny, if it weren't so sad: men melted down their own silver, shaped it into a calf, and then kissed it. The thing they made became the thing that owned them. Plenty of us know how that goes.

“They that make them are like unto them; so is every one that trusteth in them.”

Psalm 115:8

Here this morning, gone by noon

  • Morning cloudburns off by mid-morning
  • Early dewgone as soon as the sun is up
  • Chaffblown off the threshing floor by the wind
  • Smokeout the chimney and gone

Four pictures of something that's there one minute and gone the next. That's what a life built on idols adds up to.

Hold on to the dew. In the next chapter, God turns the same picture around: “I will be as the dew unto Israel” (14:5). Israel's goodness dries up. God's keeps coming back every morning.

Part two

No saviour beside me

Verses 4–8

Remembered in the desert, forgotten in the pasture.

Hungry men pray. Full men forget.

  1. Out of Egypt“the LORD thy God” (v. 4)
  2. The wilderness“I did know thee” (v. 5)
  3. The pasture“they were filled” (v. 6)
  4. Pride“their heart was exalted”
  5. Forgotten“they have forgotten me”

In the desert, Israel had nothing, and God fed them every day. In the good land they had plenty, and they forgot Him. Look at the order in verse 6: filled, then proud, then forgot.

A lot of us prayed hardest in a cell or at rock bottom. Then life got better, and the praying stopped. Moses warned about this before Israel ever crossed the Jordan:

“Beware that thou forget not the LORD thy God, in not keeping his commandments, and his judgments, and his statutes, which I command thee this day:”

Deuteronomy 8:11

The lion, and the long wait before it

These are hard words: a lion, a leopard watching from the side of the road, a mother bear robbed of her cubs. God says He will be that to Israel. Why? Verse 6: they forgot Him. And look how long He waited first:

About 200 yearsfrom the first golden calves (about 930 BC) to Samaria's fall (about 722 BC)
19 kingsin the northern kingdom, every one of them leading the people astray
Prophet after prophetElijah, Elisha, Jonah, Amos, Hosea, and more

Judgment didn't come out of nowhere. It came after two centuries of warnings. God is slow to anger, and He is not slow because He doesn't care.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Part three

In me is thine help

Verses 9–13

Israel did this to itself. God is still the answer.

Thou hast destroyed thyself; but in me is thine help.

What Israel did

“destroyed thyself”

Israel didn't fall because God was cruel. It fell because it walked away from the only One who could keep it.

What God still offers

“in me is thine help”

Even with the lion at the door, He says: I am still your help.

Israel had demanded a king, “like all the nations” (1 Samuel 8:5). God gave them kings, and the kings led them to ruin. Now He says, “I will be thy king.”

Every man in recovery knows the first half of verse 9 by heart. The second half is the good news.

Kept on file

Bound up is like papers tied in a bundle and stored away. Hid means put away, not forgotten. Sin never brought to God doesn't fade. It stays on record.

Ephraim's account, bound up

  • Baalon file
  • Lies and broken oathson file
  • Crooked scaleson file
Balancestill owed
PAIDfor whoever comes

Verse 13: a baby at the moment of birth who won't come out. Israel knew it had to change, and wouldn't move. But there is one place a record like that gets settled:

“Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross;”

Colossians 2:14

Part four

O death

Verses 14–16

In the middle of the darkest chapter, a promise that beats the grave.

Death loses

Right in the middle of all this judgment, God says He will ransom and redeem His people from the grave itself. Both words mean buying someone back by paying the price.

Paul quotes this verse when he writes about the resurrection. He throws it at death like a taunt:

“O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory?”

1 Corinthians 15:55

Jesus paid the ransom with His own life, and walked out of the grave. Because He lives, every believer will too. “Repentance shall be hid from mine eyes”: God will never change His mind about this.

The hardest verse

Ephraim means fruitful, and verse 15 plays on it: though he be fruitful, the east wind, Assyria, will dry up his spring.

Verse 16 is what happened when Assyria took Samaria, about 722 BC. It is terrible, and the Bible doesn't hide it. Armies of that time did such things; Israel's own king Menahem had done the same to a town that shut its gates on him (2 Kings 15:16).

God took no pleasure in it. For two hundred years He warned and waited. This is what it looks like when a nation pushes His protection away to the very end.

“Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?”

Ezekiel 33:11

And the very next words in Hosea are “O Israel, return.”

Points to remember

  1. Forgetting God“Their heart was exalted; therefore have they forgotten me” 13:6Hungry men pray; full men forget. Remember Him when life is good.
  2. Idols“Let the men that sacrifice kiss the calves” 13:2Men make idols, then idols own them, and they pass like smoke.
  3. Our help“Thou hast destroyed thyself; but in me is thine help” 13:9We did the damage. God is still the help.
  4. God's patience“Yet I am the LORD thy God” 13:4God warned and waited for two hundred years before judgment came.
  5. Victory over death“O death, I will be thy plagues” 13:14Jesus paid the ransom and beat the grave. In Him, death loses.

Question 1 of 5

Forgetting God

Why do full men forget God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Beware that thou forget not the LORD thy God, in not keeping his commandments, and his judgments, and his statutes, which I command thee this day:”

Deuteronomy 8:11

Answer

Because when we have plenty, we start to think we don't need Him. Israel was close to God in the desert, where it had nothing. In the good land it got full, then proud, then forgot. The answer isn't to stay broke. It's to keep remembering, thanking, and praying when things are going well.

Question 2 of 5

Idols

Why can't an idol save anyone?

What verses tell us about this?

“They that make them are like unto them; so is every one that trusteth in them.”

Psalm 115:8

“Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen.”

1 John 5:21

Answer

Because we made it. A thing made by our hands can't be bigger than we are. It can only take, and it ends up owning the man who bows to it. Idols pass like morning cloud and smoke. Ours may not be silver calves, but anything we run to instead of God will do the same thing to us.

Question 3 of 5

Our help

Who really destroyed Israel, and where was its help?

What verses tell us about this?

“I, even I, am the LORD; and beside me there is no saviour.”

Isaiah 43:11

“Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.”

Acts 4:12

Answer

Israel destroyed itself, by walking away from the only One who could save it. And its help was right where it had always been: in the LORD. That's still true. There's no other Saviour and no other name. The man who admits I did this to myself is one step from the best news there is.

Question 4 of 5

God's patience

How long did God wait before judgment came?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

“Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?”

Ezekiel 33:11

Answer

About two hundred years, from the first golden calves to the fall of Samaria, with prophet after prophet calling Israel back. God doesn't enjoy judgment. He waits because He wants men to turn and live. His patience is real, but it isn't a reason to put off turning to Him today.

Question 5 of 5

Victory over death

How does Hosea 13:14 point to Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory?”

1 Corinthians 15:55

“But thanks be to God, which giveth us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.”

1 Corinthians 15:57

Answer

Paul quotes it to celebrate the resurrection. God promised to ransom His people from death itself, and Jesus paid that ransom on the cross, then rose again. Death couldn't hold Him, and it can't hold anyone who belongs to Him. For a believer, the grave has lost.
