Amos 7

A plumbline

Part one

Three visions

Verses 1–9

Locusts, fire, and a line that doesn't lie.

Amos prays, and God holds back

Quick reminder: Amos was a herdman from Judah, sent north to Israel about 760 BC. In this first vision, God shows him grasshoppers (locusts) coming just as the second crop of grass sprouts. The king took the first cutting as a tax. The “latter growth” was what the people lived on. Locusts then meant a year of hunger.

נִחַם said nicham to relent, to turn from what was coming

“The LORD repented” doesn't mean God had sinned or changed His mind like a man does. It means He held back the judgment He had warned of, in answer to prayer. God had told Amos ahead of time so that Amos would pray.

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.”

James 5:16

“For he is small”

The second vision is fire so fierce it drinks up “the great deep,” the waters under the ground that feed the springs, and starts eating up the land.

Look at how Amos prays. He doesn't say “Israel deserves better.” He says, “he is small.” He's too weak to survive this. He pleads their need, not their goodness. That's how we come to God for ourselves too.

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

A line that doesn't lie

A plumbline is a string with a weight on the end. It hangs perfectly straight, so builders use it to test a wall. You can't argue with it.

Held against God's line, Israel leaned badly. A leaning wall falls, and it hurts whoever stands under it.

So does every one of us: “For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God” (Romans 3:23). We need a Saviour, not just a better effort.

Three visions, one turn

  1. Locustsvv. 1–3 · Amos prays · “It shall not be”
  2. Firevv. 4–6 · Amos prays · “This also shall not be”
  3. The plumblinevv. 7–9 · “I will not again pass by them any more”

Twice God held back. The third time, Amos doesn't pray, and God says He won't “pass by” any more, meaning He won't overlook it again. God's patience is long. It had lasted for generations of prophets. But it isn't endless.

The high places were hilltop shrines. And the word against “the house of Jeroboam” came true: Jeroboam's son Zechariah reigned six months and was murdered (2 Kings 15:8–10).

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Part two

The priest and the prophet

Verses 10–17

“Go home, and preach somewhere else.”

How the report got twisted

Amaziah was the chief priest at Bethel, the king's shrine. He reports Amos to King Jeroboam II for treason. Compare what was said with what was reported:

What Amos said (v. 9)

“I will rise against the house of Jeroboam”

God was the speaker. It was a word about the king's family line. And Amos had just prayed twice for Israel.

What Amaziah reported (v. 11)

“Thus Amos saith”

God's name left out. Made personal: “Jeroboam shall die.” No mention of the prayers. A preacher made to look like a plotter.

“The land is not able to bear all his words.” That part was true. The truth was hard to bear. But the answer isn't to silence the man telling it.

Amaziah, priest of BethelO thou seer, go, flee thee away into the land of Judah, and there eat bread, and prophesy there.

AmosI was no prophet, neither was I a prophet's son … the LORD took me as I followed the flock.

Amaziah's jab: go earn your living preaching back home. He assumed Amos did it for the money, because that's what Amaziah did. And notice what he calls Bethel: “the king's chapel.” Not God's house. The king's. Ever since Jeroboam I set up a golden calf there (1 Kings 12:28–29), it had been the government's church.

Amos answers: I'm not a professional. I didn't come from a school of prophets. I was minding sheep, and God sent me. I'm not here for bread. I'm here because He said “Go.”

God picks ordinary men

a herdman, a fig picker → “Go, prophesy”

Sycomore fruit is a small, cheap wild fig. It had to be nicked with a knife to ripen, and poor people ate it. Amos did seasonal farm work. No title, no pedigree, no school.

Maybe you feel like that: no education, a record, a past. God has always loved to use men like that.

“Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.”

Acts 4:13

You can't silence God

Amaziah says→“Prophesy not”Stop talking. Go away.
God says→“Thus saith the LORD”The word comes anyway, this time aimed at Amaziah himself.

Verse 17 is hard. It describes what happened to a family when an army took a city: his wife forced into prostitution to survive in the ruined city, his children killed, his land measured out to new owners. God isn't approving those evils. He's warning of what conquest would bring on the man who shut out the warning.

And the priest who had made Bethel unclean would die in “a polluted land,” an idol-worshipping country in exile. Amos stands his ground. When men tell you to be quiet about what God has said, the apostles' answer still holds:

“Then Peter and the other apostles answered and said, We ought to obey God rather than men.”

Acts 5:29

  1. Prayer“O Lord GOD, forgive, I beseech thee” 7:2Amos prayed and God held back judgment. Prayer for others matters.
  2. God's standard“I will set a plumbline in the midst of my people” 7:8God measures by His straight line, and we all lean. We need a Saviour.
  3. God's patience“I will not again pass by them any more” 7:8God's patience is long, but it isn't endless. Today is the day to turn.

Question 1 of 5

Prayer

Does it matter when we pray for others?

What verses tell us about this?

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.”

James 5:16

Answer

Yes. Twice Amos prayed for Israel, and twice God held back the judgment. He didn't plead Israel's goodness. He pleaded their weakness: “he is small.” God invites us to pray for each other, and He hears.

Question 2 of 5

God's standard

What does God measure our lives by?

What verses tell us about this?

“For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;”

Romans 3:23

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

By His own straight line, not by comparing us to other men. Against that line, all of us lean. That's bad news if we're trying to stand on our own, and it's why we need Jesus. When we confess, He forgives and cleans us up.

Question 3 of 5

God's patience

Does God's patience ever run out?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God's patience is long. He held back twice in this chapter alone, and He'd sent prophets for generations. But it isn't endless: “I will not again pass by them any more.” That's why the time to turn to Him is today, while He's still calling.

Question 4 of 5

Calling

Can God use an ordinary man?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise; and God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty;”

1 Corinthians 1:27

“Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.”

Acts 4:13

Answer

He always has. Amos was a herdman and a picker of cheap figs, with no title and no school. God took him from behind the flock and said, “Go.” What qualified him wasn't his past. It was that God sent him, the same way people could tell Peter and John had been with Jesus.

Question 5 of 5

Courage

What do we do when people tell us to stop speaking God's word?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then Peter and the other apostles answered and said, We ought to obey God rather than men.”

Acts 5:29

Answer

Keep speaking it, humbly and plainly. Amos didn't argue about his credentials or run home. He said what God sent him to say. A man can silence a preacher, but he can't silence God. We ought to obey God rather than men.
