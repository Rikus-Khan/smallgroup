1 Corinthians 7

Married, single, or somewhere hard

The Corinthians asked Paul about marriage. He answers with honesty and grace: keep yourself pure, love the one you have, and follow Christ right where He found you.

What Paul heard
What they wrote to ask: “Now concerning…”

Chapters 1–6 dealt with bad news Paul had heard: a church split into camps, a man living with his father's wife, believers suing each other, men visiting prostitutes.

Now he turns to a letter they had sent him. “Now concerning the things whereof ye wrote unto me”: their first question was about marriage. From here on, almost every new subject opens with “Now concerning”.

Letters across the sea

Paul was at Ephesus, about AD 55, about 250 miles across the Aegean from Corinth. The church sent him a letter full of questions. Three men from Corinth came to him there (16:17), and many think they carried it.

1 Corinthians is his answer. We only have his side, so sometimes we hear their words quoted back, the way you can guess a question from someone's reply on the phone.

Part one

Every man his own wife

Verses 1–9

God made a good place for a man's desire. He also gives some men the gift of living single.

Two ditches beside one road

One ditch

anything goes

Corinth was a port city with a name for loose living. Some in the church said the body doesn't matter, and went to prostitutes (6:15–16).

The other ditch

touch nothing

Others swung the other way: no sex at all, even for married couples. Verse 1 may be their own slogan quoted back, or Paul agreeing that single life is good.

Paul's road runs between them. “Fornication” is sex outside marriage. God's answer to desire isn't shame and it isn't a free-for-all. It's marriage: one man, one woman, each his own.

It runs both ways

The husband→renders to the wifeher needs, before his own
The wife→renders to the husbandhis needs, before her own

“Due benevolence” is the kindness that's owed: love, care, and the marriage bed. In that world, a husband was thought to own his wife's body. Paul says the husband's body belongs to his wife too. That was new.

Marriage isn't a place to take. It's a place to give.

Defraud ye not

“Defraud” means rob. Holding back from your wife, or using the bedroom as a weapon to punish her, robs her of what's hers.

Only three conditions for time apart: both agree, it's “for a time”, and it's for prayer. Then come together again.

Why? “That Satan tempt you not for your incontinency”: your lack of self-control. Paul is a realist. He knows where a man is weak, and so does the devil. Don't leave the door open.

“Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled: but whoremongers and adulterers God will judge.”

Hebrews 13:4

Listen for who is speaking

  1. v. 6“by permission”advice, not a rule
  2. v. 10“not I, but the Lord”Jesus said it Himself
  3. v. 12“I, not the Lord”Paul, as an apostle
  4. v. 25“my judgment”wise counsel
  5. v. 40“the Spirit of God”all of it Spirit-led

Paul is careful. When Jesus had already spoken to a question on earth, Paul says so. When Jesus hadn't, Paul answers as His apostle. And some things he gives as advice, not as law.

It's all God's Word. But we should be careful the same way: don't turn advice into a rule, and don't turn a command into a suggestion.

Better to marry than to burn

If theycannot contain, can't keep themselves in check,
→
thenlet them marry.

“Burn” here means to burn with desire, not hell. Desire isn't sin. God made it, and He made marriage for it.

Paul was single when he wrote (we aren't told if he'd been married before). He calls it a “gift of God” (v. 7). If you're single today, you're not half a man waiting for life to start. Use these years for the Lord. And if desire is burning, the answer isn't the old way. It's purity now, and marriage God's way, in God's time.

Part two

Not I, but the Lord

Verses 10–16

Marriage is meant to last, even when only one of you believes.

The Lord's own word

Here Jesus had already spoken. When He was asked about divorce, He went back to the beginning:

“What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder.”

Mark 10:9

So: don't leave. And if a believer has already left, the way forward is to stay unmarried or to make peace and go back. “Put away” means divorce.

A light in the house

Jesus never spoke to this one, so Paul does. In Corinth, people were coming to Christ one at a time, and a man might go home to a wife who didn't believe. Should he leave her? No. If she's willing to stay, stay.

“Sanctified” means set apart. It doesn't mean she's saved because he is. It means God has a foothold in that house. The children grow up near a believer, and that's “holy”, set apart, not unclean.

Each one still has to come to Christ by his own faith. But you being there matters.

Called to peace

And if the unbelieving spouse leaves? “Let him depart.” The believer “is not under bondage”: not chained to chasing someone who has walked out.

Good Baptist teachers differ on whether verse 15 frees the one left behind to marry again. What's plain: God has called you to peace, not to a lifelong fight.

Verse 16 is hope: the wife who doesn't believe today may believe one day, because of how you live in front of her.

“Likewise, ye wives, be in subjection to your own husbands; that, if any obey not the word, they also may without the word be won by the conversation of the wives;”

1 Peter 3:1

If your marriage is already broken

Many men in this room are divorced, separated, remarried, or living in a hard marriage right now. This chapter isn't here to sort you into a pile.

Every situation has its own history, and some of it is too tangled for a slide. Take yours to your pastor, sit down, and let him walk through it with you from the Word.

But hear this first. Paul wrote this letter to a church full of men with a past. Just one chapter back he listed the sins, and then he said:

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Whatever you did to wreck a marriage, or whatever was done to you, the blood of Christ is enough for it. Start from where you stand today.

Part three

Abide with God

Verses 17–24

You don't have to fix your life before you follow Christ. Start right where He found you.

What counts

  • circumcisedthe Jewish mark
  • uncircumcisedthe Gentile without it
  • keeping the commandments of Goda life that obeys Him

New believers in Corinth wanted to change everything on the outside: leave the unbelieving wife, get circumcised, undo the mark. Paul says the outside label isn't the point. “So ordain I in all churches”: this is my rule everywhere. The change God wants is inside.

Right where you are

Paul says it three times (vv. 17, 20, 24). A “calling” here is the life you were in when God called you.

You don't have to wait until the case is closed, the debt is paid, or the family is fixed. Follow Christ at that job, in that home, with that record, starting today.

This isn't staying in sin: a thief stops stealing. It's not waiting for a better life before you start walking with God.

Slave and free

a servant → the Lord's freeman

Many in the Corinthian church were slaves, owned by another man. Paul doesn't call slavery good: “if thou mayest be made free, use it rather.” But a slave who knows Christ is already free where it counts most.

And the free man? He's “Christ's servant”. Before God, we all stand on the same ground: set free, and glad to serve.

Bought with a price.

Slaves were bought and sold in the market at Corinth. Paul says you were bought too, and the price was paid in full.

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

“Be not ye the servants of men.” No dealer, no bottle, no old crew, no man's opinion owns you now. Christ does. And “abide with God”: wherever you are today, you're not there alone.

Part four

The time is short

Verses 25–40

Married or single, live with your eyes on the Lord. This world is passing.

Counsel from a man who got mercy

“Virgins” here are the never-married. Jesus left no command about them, so Paul gives his judgment, and look how he describes himself: a man who “obtained mercy”. He once hunted Christians. He never forgot it.

Bound to a wife?

seek not to be loosed

Loosed from one?

seek not a wife

The “present distress” was some hard time they were living through; we don't know exactly what. In it, Paul says, don't rush to change your state. But “if thou marry, thou hast not sinned.” Marriage brings “trouble in the flesh”, real-life pressures, and he'd like to spare them that.

The time is short

Life is short, and the Lord is coming. So hold everything here with an open hand: tears, good times, the things you buy. None of it is the main thing.

“The fashion of this world passeth away”: the whole scene, like a stage set being changed. “As not abusing it” means not using it up as if it were all there is.

Paul isn't telling husbands to ignore their wives. He's saying even the best things here are not forever. Only Christ is.

Without distraction

“Carefulness” means anxious care. A single man can give the Lord his whole attention. A married man rightly has a wife to care for too. That isn't sin. It's his job.

Paul isn't trying to “cast a snare”, to put a leash on anyone. He wants what is “comely”, fitting, for each man.

Single or married, the goal is the same: a heart that attends upon the Lord.

“Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.”

Philippians 4:6

Well, and better

These three verses are hard to read today. “His virgin” is a young woman under his care. Some good teachers think Paul means a father deciding about his daughter's marriage. Others think he means an engaged man and his bride-to-be.

“If she pass the flower of her age”: if she's getting past the usual age to marry, and it's right that they marry, “he sinneth not: let them marry.”

Either way the point is plain. Marrying is good. Staying single to serve the Lord is good too. Nobody in this chapter is a second-class Christian.

Only in the Lord

Marriage is for life: “as long as her husband liveth.” When a husband dies, his widow is free to marry whomever she wants, with one condition: a believer.

“Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness?”

2 Corinthians 6:14

Single men, that's for you too. Pray about who you marry. Marry someone who loves Jesus.

“I think also that I have the Spirit of God”: a gentle way of saying it. Paul's judgment here isn't a guess. The Spirit is in it.

Points to remember

  1. Purity“It is better to marry than to burn” 7:9God made marriage as the good place for desire. Keep yourself pure until then.
  2. Marriage“Render unto the wife due benevolence” 7:3Each belongs to the other. Marriage is a place to give, not to take.
  3. Singleness“Every man hath his proper gift of God” 7:7Single life is a gift, not a waiting room. Use it for the Lord.
  4. Divorce“Not I, but the Lord” 7:10Marriage is meant to last. Take your own situation to your pastor, and know that grace covers the past.
  5. An unbelieving spouse“God hath called us to peace” 7:15If she's willing, stay. Your life may be how she meets Christ.
  6. Your calling“Therein abide with God” 7:24Start following Christ right where you are, today.

Question 1 of 6

Purity

Why does Paul tell some men to marry?

What verses tell us about this?

“Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled: but whoremongers and adulterers God will judge.”

Hebrews 13:4

Answer

To avoid fornication. Desire isn't sin; God made it, and He made marriage as its good and honourable place. A man who can't contain himself shouldn't burn, or go back to the old way. He should marry, in the Lord.

Question 2 of 6

Marriage

What do a husband and wife owe each other?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Love, kindness and the marriage bed, freely given, both ways. Each belongs to the other. Don't hold back or use it as a weapon; apart only by agreement, for a short time, for prayer.

Question 3 of 6

Singleness

Is being single second best?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. Paul calls it a gift of God, and he lived it himself. A single man can give the Lord his undivided attention. Married or single, both are good; each man has his own gift.

Question 4 of 6

Divorce

What does the Lord say about leaving a marriage?

What verses tell us about this?

“What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder.”

Mark 10:9

Answer

Don't. God joins a husband and wife, and Jesus said man must not separate them. One who has left should stay unmarried or be reconciled. Every man's own story is different: take yours to your pastor, and remember that the blood of Christ washes every sin.

Question 5 of 6

An unbelieving spouse

What should a believer do if his wife doesn't believe?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

If she's willing to stay, stay, and love her. A believer in the house sets it apart for God. If she leaves, he isn't chained to chasing her; God has called us to peace. And keep hoping: she may yet come to Christ.

Question 6 of 6

Your calling

Do I have to fix my life before I can follow Christ?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. God called you where you were. Walk with Him there: at that job, in that home, with that record. Leave behind what's sin, but don't wait for a better life to start. You're bought with a price, and you abide with God.
