Part one

Set the trumpet to thy mouth

Verses 1–3

An eagle is coming, and Israel says, “We know You.”

IsraelMy God, we know thee!

The LORDIsrael hath cast off the thing that is good.
words, not a life

Hosea is told to blow the alarm: an eagle, a vulture really, is circling over “the house of the LORD,” meaning the land and people of Israel. That eagle was Assyria. Within a few years, Samaria would fall (about 722 BC).

With the enemy overhead, Israel suddenly cries out, “My God, we know thee!” But they had thrown away “the thing that is good,” God's covenant and His law (v. 1). Their words claimed Him. Their lives didn't.

“They profess that they know God; but in works they deny him, being abominable, and disobedient, and unto every good work reprobate.”

Titus 1:16

Part two

The calf of Samaria

Verses 4–6

A god with a maker's mark on it.

Made by a workman

About two hundred years earlier, when the kingdom split, Jeroboam didn't want his people going south to worship at Jerusalem. So he made his own gods:

“Whereupon the king took counsel, and made two calves of gold, and said unto them, It is too much for you to go up to Jerusalem: behold thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.”

1 Kings 12:28
Ifa workman made it,
→
thenit is not God.

Hosea's logic is simple. A god you can make is a god you can break, and the calf “shall be broken in pieces.” The same went for their kings: “set up ... but not by me.” They chose their own leaders and their own gods, and neither could save them.

You become what you worship

“Their idols are silver and gold, the work of men's hands. They have mouths, but they speak not: eyes have they, but they see not:”

Psalm 115:4–5

“They that make them are like unto them; so is every one that trusteth in them.”

Psalm 115:8

Most of us never bowed to a golden calf. But we've all had something we built our lives around that couldn't see us, hear us, or save us: a bottle, a high, money, a reputation. And slowly we became like it: numb, blind, deaf to God.

God asks the honest question: “how long will it be ere they attain to innocency?” (v. 5). How long until you're clean? He asks because He wants it for you. In Christ a man can be washed clean today (1 Corinthians 6:11).

Part three

The whirlwind

Verses 7–10

What you plant comes back bigger.

A bigger harvest

Farmers know: you reap what you sow, and you reap more than you sow. One seed, a whole stalk of grain.

Israel planted wind: idols, alliances, empty promises, nothing solid. What came back was a whirlwind: Assyria's armies tearing through the land. And what little grain they grew, “the strangers shall swallow it up.”

Every man here has reaped some whirlwind. The good news is that the same law works the other way.

The law works both ways

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap. For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting.”

Galatians 6:7–8

Sowing to the flesh

The whirlwind

One more drink, one more lie, one more night out. Small seeds, a storm of a harvest.

Sowing to the Spirit

Life

One honest phone call, one meeting, one morning in the Word, one day sober. Small seeds too, and they come up.

If you belong to Christ, your sins are forgiven. Some consequences may still come in, and that's hard. But you aren't stuck planting wind. Start sowing something different today. “In due season we shall reap, if we faint not” (Galatians 6:9).

What Ephraim paid for “lovers”

  • 1,000 talents of silver2 Kings 15:19
  • 50 shekels from every rich man15:20
  • tribute “year by year”17:4
  • its land, its people, its freedom17:6

What it boughtnothing that lasted

Paying for love

A wild ass is a stubborn loner that won't be led. Ephraim went off to Assyria alone, and paid. A woman who sells herself is paid by her lovers. Ephraim was so far gone, it paid them.

Israel's kings paid huge sums to Assyria for protection (v. 10, “the burden of the king of princes”). In the end Assyria took everything.

Now remember Hosea's own story. His wife had sold herself, and Hosea went and paid to bring her home: “So I bought her to me for fifteen pieces of silver” (Hosea 3:2). Israel paid strangers for a love that never came. God paid for His people Himself. In Jesus, He paid the whole price for us.

Part four

Many altars

Verses 11–14

More religion, and less of God.

Altars that turned around

Built→altars to deal with sinThat's what an altar was for: a place to bring sin to God and be forgiven.
Became→altars to sinThe more altars Ephraim built, the more sin they produced. Religion became one more way to run from God.

And God's word? “I have written to him the great things of my law, but they were counted as a strange thing.” God had put it in writing, the greatest things ever written, and His own people treated it like a foreign book.

There's one altar that never turns into a trap: the cross, where Jesus dealt with sin once for all. And the Bible isn't a strange book. It's God writing to you. Open it today.

Forgetting the Maker

“They shall return to Egypt” (v. 13): back to slavery, as if the Exodus had never happened. That's where forgetting God leads a man: right back to the chains he was freed from.

  • “temples”the word can also mean palaces. Big buildings, for God or for kings.
  • “fenced cities”walls and forts. Judah trusted its own defenses.
  • remember your Makerthe only real security a man or a nation has

“Beware that thou forget not the LORD thy God, in not keeping his commandments, and his judgments, and his statutes, which I command thee this day:”

Deuteronomy 8:11

Forgetting God rarely happens all at once. Life gets better, we build things up, and slowly we stop needing Him. Then the fire comes (v. 14). Remember Him on the good days too.

Question 1 of 6

Knowing God

Is saying “My God, we know thee” the same as knowing Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“They profess that they know God; but in works they deny him, being abominable, and disobedient, and unto every good work reprobate.”

Titus 1:16

“And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments.”

1 John 2:3

Answer

No. Israel said the right words when the enemy showed up, but they had thrown away what was good. Knowing God shows in how we live. We're saved by faith alone, but a faith that's real starts to change a man's life.

Question 2 of 6

Idols

Why can't a god a man makes save him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Their idols are silver and gold, the work of men's hands. They have mouths, but they speak not: eyes have they, but they see not:”

Psalm 115:4–5

“They that make them are like unto them; so is every one that trusteth in them.”

Psalm 115:8

Answer

Because it's smaller than the man who made it. “The workman made it; therefore it is not God.” It can't see, hear, or help, and it can be broken in pieces. Worse, we become like what we worship. Only the living God, who made us, can save us.

Question 3 of 6

Sowing and reaping

What does it mean to sow the wind and reap the whirlwind?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap. For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting.”

Galatians 6:7–8

“And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not.”

Galatians 6:9

Answer

What we plant comes back, and comes back bigger. Israel planted empty things and reaped a storm. But the same law works for good: sow to the Spirit, one small faithful choice at a time, and in due season you'll reap life.

Question 4 of 6

Bought love

What happens when we pay for the love we should find in God?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Menahem exacted the money of Israel, even of all the mighty men of wealth, of each man fifty shekels of silver, to give to the king of Assyria. So the king of Assyria turned back, and stayed not there in the land.”

2 Kings 15:20

Answer

We pay and pay, and it never lasts. Ephraim hired Assyria to love and protect it, and Assyria took it all. Whatever we pay the world for comfort, it always wants more. God doesn't sell His love. He paid for us Himself, like Hosea buying back his wife.

Question 5 of 6

God's word

How did Israel treat the great things of God's law?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Like a strange, foreign thing that had nothing to do with them. God had written down the greatest things, and they shrugged. It's easy to do the same with a Bible on the shelf. God wrote it for you. Read it like a letter from your Father.

Question 6 of 6

Forgetting God

What happens when a man forgets his Maker?

What verses tell us about this?

“Beware that thou forget not the LORD thy God, in not keeping his commandments, and his judgments, and his statutes, which I command thee this day:”

Deuteronomy 8:11

Answer

He starts trusting what he's built instead, and he drifts back toward the slavery God freed him from. Walls and big buildings can't protect him. Remembering God, on the good days as well as the bad, is a man's real safety.
