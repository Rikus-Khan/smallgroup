Numbers 5

The camp kept clean, and the trial of jealousy

The unclean are put out of the camp, “in the midst whereof I dwell”. A man who has wronged another confesses and pays it back with a fifth part more. And when a husband is jealous and has no witness, the matter is taken out of his hands and set before the LORD.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

“In the midst whereof I dwell”

Verses 1–4

The camp is where the LORD lives, and the unclean are kept from it.

Out of the camp, for the camp's sake

  • a lepera man with the skin disease of Leviticus 13–14
  • an issuea bodily flowing that made a man unclean (Leviticus 15)
  • defiled by the deadunclean for touching a dead body
  • in the midst“that they defile not their camps, in the midst whereof I dwell” (verse 3)

The reason is given in the verse: the LORD dwells in the camp. “Both male and female shall ye put out”: it is not about who a person is. It is about the holiness of the place where God lives. And the Law had a way back in: the unclean were cleansed, and returned.

Jesus went outside, and he touched

“And Jesus put forth his hand, and touched him, saying, I will; be thou clean. And immediately his leprosy was cleansed.”

Matthew 8:3

“Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate. Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach.”

Hebrews 13:12–13

A man in recovery may know what it is to be put out, of a family, a job, a church. Jesus touched the leper, and he suffered outside the gate, so that no man would be left outside. If you are on the outside of everything, you may go to him there: he has been there already.

Part two

“They shall confess their sin”

Verses 5–10

When you have wronged someone, you say so and you pay it back.

Confess, pay it back, and add a fifth

  • a trespassa wrong done, here to a man, and counted as done “against the LORD”
  • confesssay it out loud: “they shall confess their sin which they have done”
  • recompensepay back; “the principal” is the full amount taken
  • the fifth partone part in five added over and above
  • kinsmana relative; if the man wronged is gone and has none, the payment goes to the LORD, to the priest
50what was taken (an example)
+ 10a fifth part added
60what is given back

The “ram of the atonement” (verse 8) is given beside the payment: the man's debt to his neighbour is paid with money, and his sin before the LORD is covered with a sacrifice. Both have to be done.

“A trespass against the LORD”

Read verse 6 again: “any sin that men commit, to do a trespass against the LORD.” The sin was against a man, and still the LORD says it was done against him. David said the same after what he did to Uriah and Bath-sheba:

“Against thee, thee only, have I sinned, and done this evil in thy sight: that thou mightest be justified when thou speakest, and be clear when thou judgest.”

Psalm 51:4

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

There are two debts when we wrong a man: one to him and one to God. Christ has paid the one to God. The one to the man is still ours, and the LORD tells us how to pay it: say it, give it back, and add something over.

Zacchaeus, and the fifth part

Verses 9 and 10 say that what is brought to the priest is the priest's; it is how God provided for the men who served him. Hallowed means set apart as holy. The point for us is back in verse 7: a restoring that costs something.

“And Zacchaeus stood, and said unto the Lord; Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.”

Luke 19:8

Zacchaeus gave back more than the law asked, and he did it because Jesus had come to his house. Restitution is not the price of forgiveness; it is what a forgiven man does. A man with a list of people he has wronged has the start of his work for today.

Part three

“The spirit of jealousy”

Verses 11–31

A husband's suspicion, and the LORD taking the matter into his own hands.

No witness, and a jealous man

This is the hardest passage in the chapter, and the text is plain. It is about unfaithfulness in a marriage, and the case where nobody can prove it. “The spirit of jealousy” comes upon a husband three times in the chapter (verses 14 twice, and 30), and the text gives two cases, side by side. (“Carnally” is in the flesh, as a husband lies with his wife; “kept close” is kept secret; “taken with the manner” is caught in the act.)

It may be so

Verse 13

She is defiled, “and there be no witness against her, neither she be taken with the manner”: the sin is hid, and kept close.

It may not be

Verse 14

“or if the spirit of jealousy come upon him, and he be jealous of his wife, and she be not defiled”: only a feeling, and nothing true under it.

Either wayhe cannot know,
→
sohe brings her to the priest (15).

He does not take it into his own hands

Look at what the LORD does not allow. The law gives the husband no right to beat her, drive her out or punish her on a hunch. He brings her “unto the priest”, and the priest sets her “before the LORD” (verse 16). A jealous man is told to take his suspicion to God, and not to act on it himself.

“For jealousy is the rage of a man: therefore he will not spare in the day of vengeance.”

Proverbs 6:34

“Wrath is cruel, and anger is outrageous; but who is able to stand before envy?”

Proverbs 27:4

Proverbs knows what a jealous man will do when he acts on his own. The law in Numbers 5 puts a stop to it: the husband waits, the priest handles it, and the LORD gives the verdict.

What the priest brought

  1. the offeringa tenth of an ephah of barley meal, with “no oil… nor frankincense”: no sweetness on it (15)
  2. holy water in an earthen vessela clay jar, and dust from the floor of the tabernacle in it (17)
  3. the woman set before the LORDher head uncovered; the offering of memorial in her hands (18)
  4. the bitter water that causeth the cursein the priest's own hand: the curse is the LORD's to give (18)

An ephah is a dry measure; “an omer is the tenth part of an ephah” (Exodus 16:36), a day's manna for one man (Exodus 16:16). The offering is called “an offering of memorial, bringing iniquity to remembrance”: nothing here is hidden from God. Moses doesn't say why her head is uncovered; it comes with setting her before the LORD.

Free, if she is clean

The priest, firstIf no man have lain with thee, and if thou hast not gone aside to uncleanness with another instead of thy husband, be thou free from this bitter water that causeth the curse.

The priest, thenThe LORD make thee a curse and an oath among thy people, when the LORD doth make thy thigh to rot, and thy belly to swell.

The womanAmen, amen.

The innocent woman is told first that she is free. The curse comes only “if thou hast gone aside”. And what happens to the guilty is what “the LORD doth make”: the water does not do it, and the priest does not do it. “Amen, amen” is her own agreement: so be it, if I am guilty.

Written, and blotted out

The priest writes the curses in a book, washes the words off into the bitter water, and she drinks it. The offering is waved before the LORD and a handful of it is burned on the altar, “even the memorial thereof”, and only after that she drinks (verse 26). The words are written, and the words are blotted out. Paul speaks of a written charge that was blotted out for us:

“Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross;”

Colossians 2:14

We are not told how the trial was carried out beyond these verses, and the Bible records no one who was ever tried by it. Nothing here asks us to say more than the text does.

The LORD gives the verdict

If she is defiled

Verse 27

The water “shall enter into her, and become bitter”: “the woman shall be a curse among her people.” She “shall bear her iniquity” (31).

If she is clean

Verse 28

“then she shall be free, and shall conceive seed.” Nothing is taken from her, and nothing is said against her.

“Execute” (verse 30) means to carry out. And the husband: “Then shall the man be guiltless from iniquity” (verse 31). He has done what the LORD said, so he is clear whichever way it goes. The point of the law is that no man was to judge his wife by suspicion. He was to take it before the LORD and leave it there.

Nothing hid from him

“Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do.”

Hebrews 4:13

“Therefore judge nothing before the time, until the Lord come, who both will bring to light the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts: and then shall every man have praise of God.”

1 Corinthians 4:5

This is the whole lesson of the trial. Man cannot see the heart, and God can, so a man is not to condemn on suspicion. And the other side: a sin hid from every eye is not hid from God. “Be sure your sin will find you out” (Numbers 32:23).

The curse, and who took it

10times “curse”, “curses” or “cursing”
10times “jealous”, “jealousy” or “jealousies”
8times “defiled”

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

“And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him:”

Revelation 22:3

The chapter is full of curse, and defiling, and a book of curses blotted out. Christ was made the curse for us, and in the city that comes there shall be no more.

Real world: suspicion, and what is hid

Most of us have stood on both sides of this chapter: the one who was suspected, and the one who suspected.

  • SinJealousy that turns to rage, checking, accusing, control. And the hidden sin: the thing kept close that nobody has seen.
  • RegretA man who has wronged someone has a debt to pay: say it, give back what he owes, and add something over (verse 7).
  • Grace“Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more” (John 8:11). Jesus did not condemn on a hunch, and he did not call sin anything but sin.
  • Hope“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us” (1 John 1:9). What is hid from men is out in the open with God, and still he forgives.
  • TodayIf jealousy is rising in you, do not act on it alone: take it to the LORD, and tell your brothers in Christ and your pastor before it turns to anger at home. And think of one person you owe something to, and go and make it right.

Points to remember

  1. In the midst“that they defile not their camps, in the midst whereof I dwell” 5:3The LORD lives in the camp, and his holiness is why the unclean are kept out. Christ went outside to bring us in.
  2. Making it right“Then they shall confess their sin which they have done: and he shall recompense his trespass with the principal thereof, and add unto it the fifth part thereof” 5:7Confess it, pay it back, and add a fifth.
  3. Against the LORD“any sin that men commit, to do a trespass against the LORD” 5:6Every wrong to a man is also a wrong before God.
  4. The spirit of jealousy“Then shall the man bring his wife unto the priest” 5:15A jealous man was not to act on suspicion. He took it to the priest, before the LORD.
  5. The LORD decides“if the woman be not defiled, but be clean; then she shall be free, and shall conceive seed” 5:28The LORD gave the verdict, and the innocent went free.
  6. The man guiltless“Then shall the man be guiltless from iniquity, and this woman shall bear her iniquity” 5:31The husband who left it with the LORD was clear. Every one answers for his own sin.

Question 1 of 6

In the midst

Why were the unclean put outside the camp?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate. Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach.”

Hebrews 13:12–13

Answer

So that they would not defile the camp, “in the midst whereof I dwell”. The LORD lived there. Jesus suffered outside the gate, and we may go out to him.

Question 2 of 6

Making it right

What was a man to do when he had wronged someone?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Zacchaeus stood, and said unto the Lord; Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.”

Luke 19:8

Answer

Confess his sin, pay back the full amount, add a fifth part, and give it to the one he wronged. If that man is gone and has no kinsman, it goes to the LORD, to the priest.

Question 3 of 6

Against the LORD

Whom had he trespassed against, when he wronged his neighbour?

What verses tell us about this?

“Against thee, thee only, have I sinned, and done this evil in thy sight: that thou mightest be justified when thou speakest, and be clear when thou judgest.”

Psalm 51:4

Answer

The LORD. Every sin men commit against one another is “a trespass against the LORD”, and so there is something to make right with him and with the man.

Question 4 of 6

The spirit of jealousy

What was a jealous husband to do when he had no witness?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Bring his wife to the priest, who sets her before the LORD. He was not to punish her on suspicion. He took it to God.

Question 5 of 6

The LORD decides

Who judged the matter, and what became of the innocent?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore judge nothing before the time, until the Lord come, who both will bring to light the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts: and then shall every man have praise of God.”

1 Corinthians 4:5

Answer

The LORD himself. What came upon the guilty was what “the LORD doth make”. And if the woman was clean, “then she shall be free, and shall conceive seed.”

Question 6 of 6

The man guiltless

Who bore the iniquity at the end?

What verses tell us about this?

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

Answer

The woman, if she was guilty, bore her own iniquity, and the husband who had brought it before the LORD was guiltless. For us, Christ was made a curse in our place.
