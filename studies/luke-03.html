Part one

The word of God came

Verses 1–6

Emperors, governors, and high priests, and God speaks to a man in the desert.

Who ruled the land

When Herod the Great died, Rome split his kingdom. A tetrarch was a ruler over part of one. Herod Antipas took Galilee and Perea. His brother Philip took the lands north and east of the lake. Judaea came under a Roman governor: by now, Pontius Pilate.

Luke names seven powerful men: an emperor, a governor, three rulers, and two high priests. Then: “the word of God came unto John… in the wilderness.” Not to any of them.

When this happened

  1. about 4 BCHerod the Great dies; his kingdom is split
  2. about AD 7Jesus at twelve in the templeLuke 2
  3. AD 14Tiberius becomes emperor
  4. about AD 26Pilate becomes governor
  5. about AD 27–29John preaches; Jesus is baptizedthis chapter
  6. about AD 30–33the cross and the empty tomb

Luke was a careful historian. He pins the date to “the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar,” about AD 27 to 29. Jesus was “about thirty” (v. 23).

“Annas and Caiaphas being the high priests”: Rome had removed Annas years before, but he still pulled the strings. His son-in-law Caiaphas held the office (John 18:13). These two will meet Jesus again the night He is arrested.

Clear the road, the King is coming

Luke quotes Isaiah 40, written some 700 years before. When a king traveled, men went ahead to fill the holes, cut down the hills, and straighten the road.

John's road work was in people's hearts: “the crooked shall be made straight, and the rough ways shall be made smooth.”

And who is it for? “All flesh shall see the salvation of God.” Only Luke carries Isaiah's quote this far. He wants us to know: this is for everybody.

What is repentance?

μετάνοια said metanoia “a change of mind”

Repentance isn't just feeling bad. It's a change of mind about sin and about God that turns a man around. You were walking one way. Now you walk the other. Remission means sins sent away: forgiven.

John's baptism showed on the outside that a man had turned on the inside. The water didn't wash sins away. It never does. It pictures what God does when a man turns to Him.

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Part two

What shall we do?

Verses 7–20

John doesn't soften anything, and the crowds keep coming.

Nothing to lean on

“Generation of vipers”: when a field fire started, snakes would race out ahead of it. John says some came out to the river only to get away from the heat, not to change.

  • “We have Abraham to our father”being born into the right family saves no one
  • I got baptizedgetting wet without turning changes nothing
  • I grew up in churchGod “is able of these stones to raise up children”
  • “fruits worthy of repentance”a turned heart that shows up in a changed life

The fruit doesn't make the tree good. It shows what kind of tree it is. We're saved by faith in Christ, not by fruit. But real faith always bears some.

Three crowds, one question

Three kinds of men asked John the same thing: “What shall we do?” Each got his own answer.

  • The people“He that hath two coats, let him impart to him that hath none; and he that hath meat, let him do likewise.”
  • The publicans“Exact no more than that which is appointed you.”
  • The soldiers“Do violence to no man, neither accuse any falsely; and be content with your wages.”

Publicans collected taxes for Rome and padded the bill for themselves. Exact means to demand payment. Soldiers could squeeze people with threats and false charges. John didn't tell them to quit their jobs. He told them to quit their sins.

Repentance gets practical

Every answer was about the man's own temptation: the greedy man must give, the cheat must be honest, the bully must be gentle, the grumbler must be content.

Recovery works the same way. Turning to God shows up at the point of your old habit: paying back what you took, telling the truth where you used to lie, being satisfied instead of always chasing more.

“And Zacchaeus stood, and said unto the Lord; Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.”

Luke 19:8, a publican who met Jesus

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Not me. Him.

The whole country was wondering if John was the Christ. He could have let them think so. Instead he pointed away from himself. Untying a man's sandals was a slave's job, and John said he wasn't worthy to do even that for Jesus.

John

“I indeed baptize you with water”

An outward sign of a turned heart.

Jesus

“He shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost”

God Himself, coming to live inside every believer.

“For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit.”

1 Corinthians 12:13

Wheat and chaff

At harvest, farmers beat the grain on a hard floor, then tossed it into the wind with a wooden fork, the fan. The heavy wheat fell back and went into the garner, the barn. The light chaff blew away and was burned.

John says Jesus will do that with people one day. It's a sobering word, and he means it. But a man doesn't have to stay chaff. Whoever turns to Christ is gathered in, safe for good.

The truth costs John his freedom

Herodiasleft her husband, Herod's brother PhilipHerod Antipasput away his own wife to marry her
John“It is not lawful for thee to have thy brother’s wife” (Mark 6:18)prison

Herod's brother's wife was off limits under God's law (Leviticus 18:16). John said so to the man who ruled him. “Added yet this above all”: on top of all his other evils, Herod locked up the preacher.

Luke finishes John's story early so the stage is clear for Jesus. John's work was done. He had pointed to the Lamb, and he paid for telling the truth.

Part three

My beloved Son

Verses 21–22

Jesus steps into the Jordan, and heaven opens.

Heaven opened

Only Luke tells us Jesus was praying when heaven opened. Watch how often Luke shows Him praying.

Why would the sinless Son be baptized? Not to be forgiven, for He had no sin. He stepped into the water alongside sinners, the place He came to take for us. “Thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness” (Matthew 3:15).

Baptism doesn't save anyone. It's a believer's first step of obedience, following the Lord who went first.

One God, three Persons, one moment

  • The Fatherspeaks from heaven: “Thou art my beloved Son”
  • The Sonstands in the Jordan, praying
  • The Holy Ghostcomes down “in a bodily shape like a dove”

Jesus hadn't preached a sermon or done a miracle yet. The Father was “well pleased” before any of it. His love for His Son wasn't earned by performance.

Many of us grew up never hearing a father say he was pleased with us. In Christ, God the Father accepts us the same way: not for what we do, but because we're in His Son.

“To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved.”

Ephesians 1:6

Part four

The son of Adam

Verses 23–38

From Jesus, back through David and Abraham, all the way to Adam.

“As was supposed”

About thirty was a man's full age. Priests began serving at thirty (Numbers 4:3), and David was thirty when he became king.

People supposed Jesus was Joseph's son. Luke has already told us He wasn't, not by birth: “The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee… therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God” (Luke 1:35). Joseph raised Him as his own.

Matthew 1 also gives a family line, through Solomon. Luke's runs through David's son Nathan. Many Bible teachers believe Luke gives Mary's family line, with Joseph counted as Heli's son by marriage. Either way, Jesus is truly David's son.

Luke names 75 men between Jesus and God. Most of them we know nothing about. A few we know well: Zorobabel, who led the Jews home from Babylon; David the king; Booz (Boaz), who married Ruth, a foreigner; Abraham; Noe (Noah); Adam.

Some were good men and some weren't. God worked through all of them to bring His Son. Your family history doesn't decide your future. His does.

Back to Adam

Matthew 1

From Abraham down

Starts with the father of the Jews and comes down to Jesus, the King of the Jews.

Luke 3

From Jesus up to Adam

Goes past Abraham to the father of every human being. Jesus came for every son of Adam.

Luke was writing for a Gentile, Theophilus, and for people like us. Whatever your family, you are a son of Adam. And Jesus came for you.

“For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive.”

1 Corinthians 15:22

Next, in Luke 4, Jesus goes into the wilderness to be tempted. Adam fell in a garden. Jesus stood firm in a desert.

Points to remember

  1. Repentance“Bring forth therefore fruits worthy of repentance” 3:8A turned heart shows up right where the old sin used to live.
  2. False security“We have Abraham to our father” 3:8No family, church, or ceremony saves. Only a heart turned to God.
  3. Humility“One mightier than I cometh” 3:16John pointed away from himself to Jesus. So do we.
  4. Who Jesus is“Thou art my beloved Son” 3:22The Father loves His Son and is pleased with Him, and accepts us in Him.
  5. God“The Holy Ghost descended… and a voice came from heaven” 3:22The Father speaks, the Son prays, the Spirit descends: one God.
  6. For everyone“Which was the son of Adam” 3:38Jesus came for every son of Adam, Jew and Gentile alike.

Question 1 of 6

Repentance

What does real repentance look like in everyday life?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It shows up at the exact spot of a man's old sin. The selfish share, the cheat stops cheating, the bully stops pushing people around, and the grumbler learns to be content. Repentance is a turned heart, and a turned heart changes how you live.

Question 2 of 6

False security

Why couldn't the crowd count on having Abraham as their father?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because God doesn't save anyone for his family tree. God could make children of Abraham out of stones. What He looks for is a heart that has turned to Him, and that shows in a changed life. Nobody gets in on his father's faith, his mother's prayers, or his church membership.

Question 3 of 6

Humility

How did John see himself next to Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“He must increase, but I must decrease.”

John 3:30

Answer

As less than a slave beside Him. The crowds wondered if John was the Christ, and he could have taken the credit. Instead he said he wasn't worthy to untie Jesus' sandals. His whole job was to point people away from himself to Jesus.

Question 4 of 6

Who Jesus is

What did the Father say about Jesus at His baptism?

What verses tell us about this?

“To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved.”

Ephesians 1:6

Answer

“Thou art my beloved Son; in thee I am well pleased.” Jesus is God's own Son, loved by the Father, and pleasing to Him in every way. Everyone who believes on Him is accepted by the Father in Him.

Question 5 of 6

God

How do we see the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost at Jesus' baptism?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All three are there at once. The Son is praying in the water, the Holy Ghost comes down on Him like a dove, and the Father speaks from heaven. One God in three Persons. And the Son who stood in the Jordan baptizes every believer with that same Holy Spirit.

Question 6 of 6

For everyone

Why does Luke trace Jesus' family line all the way back to Adam?

What verses tell us about this?

“For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive.”

1 Corinthians 15:22

Answer

To show that Jesus belongs to the whole human race, not just to Israel. Every man alive is a son of Adam, and in Adam we all die. Jesus came as one of us to bring life to all who believe. “All flesh shall see the salvation of God.”
