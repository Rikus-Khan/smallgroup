Part one

Clean hands, far-off hearts

Verses 1–20

The experts from Jerusalem check the disciples' hands. Jesus checks their hearts.

The inspectors arrive

These weren't local men. They came all the way up from Jerusalem, the head office, to look Jesus over. And the first thing they found to complain about was dirty hands.

This wasn't about germs. “The tradition of the elders” was a pile of rules handed down by word of mouth, added around God's law over the centuries. One of them: pour water over your hands a certain way before you eat, to wash off any “uncleanness” from the street (Mark 7:3–4).

God never commanded it. Men did. But to these men, breaking it was as bad as breaking the law of Moses.

The religious loophole

Corban a Hebrew word: “an offering” “It is a gift” to God

“But ye say, If a man shall say to his father or mother, It is Corban, that is to say, a gift, by whatsoever thou mightest be profited by me; he shall be free.”

Mark 7:11

Here's how it worked. Your old parents need help. You say, “Sorry, that money is Corban, promised to God.” Now you're off the hook, and in practice the money often stayed right in your own pocket. God said honour thy father and mother. Their rule let a man walk away from them and still feel holy.

We all know how to make our excuses sound spiritual. Jesus sees through it. Taking care of family, and making things right with them, is part of following God, not a distraction from it.

Isaiah saw them coming

Isaiah 29:13

Wherefore the Lord said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men:

Matthew 15:8–9

This people draweth nigh unto me with their mouth, and honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me. But in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.

Seven hundred years earlier, Isaiah described people who said all the right words to God with nothing behind them. Jesus says, that's you.

A hypocrite (Greek hupokritēs) was a stage actor, a man who played a part behind a mask. Church words, church clothes, church face, and a heart somewhere else. God isn't after the act. He wants the man.

Blind leaders of the blind

The disciples were worried: “You offended the Pharisees!” Jesus wasn't worried. A plant God didn't plant will be pulled up by the roots. “Let them alone.”

A blind guide can sound very sure of himself. He still can't see the ditch. And everyone holding onto his shoulder goes in with him.

Every man follows somebody: the old crowd, a loud voice, his own feelings. Pick a guide who can see. Jesus can.

In one way, out the other

“goeth into the mouth”→the belly, then “the draught”food passes through the body and out to the drain. It never touches the heart
“cometh out of the mouth”←“from the heart”words and deeds come up from inside a man. That's what makes him unclean

Peter calls it a “parable,” a saying with a hidden point. The point is plain once you hear it: dirt on your hands can't make your soul dirty. The trouble starts deeper down.

The problem is on the inside

  • evil thoughtswhere every one starts
  • murdershands

Look where the list starts: evil thoughts. Every one of the others begins as a thought that gets fed. The bottle, the pipe, the lie, the affair: none of them was the deepest problem. The heart was.

That's why new rules alone can't fix a man. He needs a new heart, and that's exactly what God promises:

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

Part two

Crumbs from the table

Verses 21–28

An outsider, a silence, a hard answer, and a mother who wouldn't quit.

Out of the country

Tyre and Sidon were Gentile port cities on the coast, north of Galilee, in what is Lebanon today. For once, Jesus walked right out of the Jewish land.

The woman was “of Canaan,” from the old people Israel had fought for its land. Mark calls her “a Greek, a Syrophenician by nation” (Mark 7:26). Every way a Jew of that day could look at her, she was an outsider.

Yet she called Him “Lord, thou Son of David,” the Messiah's title. She knew who He was before most of Israel did. Afterward, Jesus came back around through the Decapolis to the lake (Mark 7:31).

She didn't take silence for an answer

  1. “Have mercy on me”“not a word”v. 22–23
  2. the disciples“Send her away”v. 23
  3. still crying out“the lost sheep of … Israel”v. 24
  4. “Lord, help me”“It is not meet”v. 25–26
  5. “Truth, Lord”“great is thy faith”v. 27–28

Silence. Then the men around Him want her gone. Then an answer that sounds like a no. Then one that sounds even harder. She kept coming anyway, and every time she came closer: from crying behind them, to kneeling at His feet.

Jesus wasn't being cold. He was drawing her faith out into the open where everyone could see it. When heaven seems quiet, it doesn't mean God has closed the door. Keep praying. Silence isn't the same as no.

Just a crumb

JesusIt is not meet to take the children’s bread, and to cast it to dogs.

The womanTruth, Lord: yet the dogs eat of the crumbs which fall from their masters’ table.
no argument

“Meet” means right or fitting. The “children” are Israel, who had God's promises first. Jews of that day often called Gentiles “dogs.” But the word Jesus used here means the little dogs, the family pets under the table, not the wild dogs in the street.

She didn't argue or get offended. She agreed with Him, “Truth, Lord,” and asked for a crumb. She knew she didn't deserve a seat at the table. She also knew a crumb from Him was enough. That's humble faith, and it's the kind Jesus loves.

Great faith in an unlikely place

A disciple, one chapter ago

“O thou of little faith”

Peter, one of the twelve, sinking in the waves (14:31).

A Gentile mother, today

“O woman, great is thy faith”

An outsider, with no claim on Him but His mercy. Healed from a distance, “from that very hour.”

Jesus came to Israel first. But the door was never locked to anyone who comes to Him on His terms, trusting His mercy.

“For there is no difference between the Jew and the Greek: for the same Lord over all is rich unto all that call upon him. For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:12–13

Mark says this was in the Decapolis, ten Greek towns on the far side of the lake (Mark 7:31). Many in this crowd were likely Gentiles, and they “glorified the God of Israel.” The crumbs were already falling from the table.

Whatever food these people brought had run out. They stayed anyway, three days, out in the open, because they wanted to be near Him.

And Jesus noticed. He cared about their souls, and He cared about their empty stomachs and the long walk home. “Lest they faint in the way.” He knows what you need to get home today, too.

They had watched Him feed five thousand men from one small lunch, not long before. Now they ask the same question as if it had never happened.

We do the same. God gets us through one crisis, and at the next one we panic like He never came through before. Remember what He's already done. Write it down if you have to.

These baskets were the big kind, big enough to lower a grown man over a city wall (Acts 9:25). Then Jesus took a boat across to Magdala on the west shore, the town Mary Magdalene was named for. He gives more than enough, every time.

Points to remember

  1. God's word“Thus have ye made the commandment of God of none effect by your tradition” 15:6When man's rules push God's word aside, God's word loses and people get hurt.
  2. The heart“For out of the heart proceed evil thoughts” 15:19Sin starts inside. A man needs a new heart, not just clean hands.
  3. Guides“If the blind lead the blind, both shall fall into the ditch” 15:14Whoever you follow, you'll end up where he ends up. Follow the One who can see.
  4. Prayer“Lord, help me” 15:25Silence isn't no. Keep coming, closer each time.
  5. Faith“O woman, great is thy faith” 15:28Great faith is humble: it agrees with God and asks for mercy anyway.
  6. Compassion“I have compassion on the multitude” 15:32He cares about the whole man, body and soul, and gives more than enough.

Question 1 of 6

God's word

What happens when man's rules take the place of God's word?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God's word gets pushed aside, and people get hurt. The Corban rule let a man walk away from his own parents and still feel holy. Worship turns into an act: right words, far-off heart. Rules men make can never replace what God has said.

Question 2 of 6

The heart

Where does sin really come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

Answer

From the heart. It starts with evil thoughts and comes out in what we say and do. That's why outward rules can't cure it. Only God can, by giving a new heart to everyone who comes to Him through Jesus.

Question 3 of 6

Guides

Why does it matter who we follow?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because a man ends up where his guide takes him. A blind leader can sound sure of himself and still walk everyone behind him into the ditch. Check who you're listening to. Jesus sees the road and every ditch on it.

Question 4 of 6

Prayer

What should we do when God seems silent?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Keep coming. She got silence, then a hard answer, and she still came closer and said, “Lord, help me.” God's silence isn't the same as no. Sometimes He's drawing our faith out where we can see it.

Question 5 of 6

Faith

What does great faith look like?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

Humble and stubborn at the same time. She agreed she had no claim on Him, and she trusted His mercy anyway: even a crumb from Him is enough. Great faith isn't about how good we are. It's about how good He is.

Question 6 of 6

Compassion

What does the feeding of the four thousand show us about Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He notices real needs, even an empty stomach and a long walk home, and He cares. He provides more than enough. And He's patient with followers who forget what He's already done.
