Deuteronomy 2

Ye have compassed this mountain long enough

Thirty and eight years of going round, and then the word: turn northward. Edom, Moab and Ammon are left alone, the giants who lived there are a lesson, and Sihon is given into their hand.

Part one

Turn you northward

Verses 1–8

Round and round the same mountain, and then a word from the LORD.

Going round

Compassed means went round it, again and again. They were walking out the sentence of Kadesh-barnea, “many days” round mount Seir.

Most of us know that loop from the inside: the same hills, the same place we swore we would never see again.

And the loop has a word at the end of it: “turn you northward.” When he says turn, turn.

Hard words in verses 4 to 6

  1. coastthe border, the whole country along it
  2. meddlestart a quarrel, interfere
  3. foot breadththe width of a single foot
  4. meatfood
  5. take ye good heedwatch yourselves carefully

The children of Esau are Israel’s brethren. “Thus dwelt Esau in mount Seir: Esau is Edom” (Genesis 36:8). Israel was strong, and Edom “shall be afraid of you”. A strong man is most tempted to take.

Not yours to take, but yours to buy

Not theirs to take

“Not so much as a foot breadth”

“I have given mount Seir unto Esau for a possession” (5). The land Israel could easily have taken was a gift God had already given to someone else.

Theirs to buy

“Buy… for money”

“Ye shall buy meat of them for money… and water of them for money” (6). Moses says “for money” four times in this chapter (6 twice, 28 twice). They paid their way.

The men of the wilderness were not to grab. Do not take what God has given another man, his wife, his job, his good name, his peace, and do not live off other people. Pay your way, and work for what you eat. Say a plain “no” to the quick take and a plain “yes” to honest work.

Forty years, and lacked nothing

40years in the wilderness (2:7)
0things lacked: “thou hast lacked nothing”

These were the years of the sentence, and also the years of the LORD’s care: “Thy raiment waxed not old upon thee, neither did thy foot swell, these forty years” (8:4).

If your own wilderness years were of your own making, the sentence does not mean he left. He “knoweth thy walking through this great wilderness.”

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Part two

The children of Lot, and the giants before

Verses 9–23

Lands God gave to other peoples, and a generation that finally passed away.

Hard words in verses 9 to 12

  1. distressto harass and trouble; “distress them not” means leave them in peace
  2. Ara city of Moab, here the name of the whole land the LORD had given to Lot’s children
  3. Emimsa people of old in Moab’s land, “great, and many, and tall, as the Anakims”
  4. Horimsthe people who lived in Seir before Esau’s children
  5. accountedcounted, reckoned; “accounted giants” means counted as giants
  6. giantsthe KJV’s word for those tall peoples
  7. beforetimein the days before; long ago
  8. in their steadin their place

The Emims were accounted giants “as the Anakims”, the same tall people the spies had named at Kadesh. The Moabites had their own name for them, and Moses uses it.

Thirty and eight years

one dot for every year

Wasted out means used up, until none was left. It was the LORD’s doing, and he says so (15). Sin has a price, and the men who would not trust him paid it. Hebrews 3:17 asks why:

“But with whom was he grieved forty years? was it not with them that had sinned, whose carcases fell in the wilderness?”

Hebrews 3:17

Who crossed the brook

Look at who stood on the far side of the brook Zered: the men who had been children at Kadesh-barnea, and Moses to lead them. God’s purposes did not die in the wilderness.

He kept his promise to the next generation, and he keeps it to a man who turns back to him. The years are spent, but the road goes on.

Lot’s two sons, and the lands God gave them

  • LotAbraham’s brother’s son
    • Moabfather of the Moabites: Ar (9)
    • Benammifather of the children of Ammon (19)

“And the firstborn bare a son, and called his name Moab: the same is the father of the Moabites unto this day. And the younger, she also bare a son, and called his name Benammi: the same is the father of the children of Ammon unto this day.”

Genesis 19:37–38

Lot was Abraham’s nephew, and God remembered him. Twice Moses says it: “I have given it unto the children of Lot for a possession” (9, 19). God gives land to peoples who are not Israel, too, and keeps their borders.

Peoples who lived there first

Moses says giants three times here (11, 20 twice). Four peoples, one lesson:

Emims → Moab

1011

“A people great, and many, and tall, as the Anakims”; the Moabites’ land, given to Lot’s son.

Horims → Edom

1222

Esau’s children “destroyed them from before them, and dwelt in their stead”.

Zamzummims → Ammon

2021

“The LORD destroyed them before them”; the Ammonites’ land.

Avims → Caphtorims

23

The Avims dwelt “in Hazerim, even unto Azzah”; the Caphtorims, who “came forth out of Caphtor”, destroyed them and dwelt in their stead.

If God put giants out for Esau’s children and Lot’s children, he could do it for his own people. The giants were never the problem. The refusal to believe was.

Is there a giant in your life that has stood too tall too long: the old habit, the old debt? It can be put out, not by you alone, but by him.

Part three

Sihon king of Heshbon

Verses 24–37

Words of peace first, then the first land across the Arnon.

Now it is time to take

After “not a foot breadth” for Edom and “distress them not” for Moab and Ammon, the word changes: “begin to possess it.” The same God gave both words. A wise man needs to hear which one he is under.

Why Sihon? God had been patient with the Amorites a long time. He told Abram so, before Israel ever went to Egypt:

“But in the fourth generation they shall come hither again: for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.”

Genesis 15:16

Now it was full. The God who waited that long is not hasty with anyone, and he is not slack when the time is come. Verse 25 says the fear of Israel would fall on the nations “that are under the whole heaven”: it was a beginning.

Peace was offered first

Moses, to SihonLet me pass through thy land: I will go along by the high way, I will neither turn unto the right hand nor to the left.

Moses, to SihonThou shalt sell me meat for money, that I may eat; and give me water for money, that I may drink: only I will pass through on my feet.

The same request Israel made of Edom and Moab, and the same promise: stay on the high way and pay for what we eat and drink. They came asking for a road, not a fight.

This is how God deals with men: he sends words of peace first, and gives a man the chance to say yes.

A hard heart

Obstinate means unbending. Sihon heard words of peace and said no, and the LORD hardened his spirit. The Bible speaks the same way of Pharaoh, who hardened his own heart (Exodus 8:15, 32) before he was hardened (9:12).

A man who keeps saying no to God can find that his heart will not bend when he wants it to. That is a reason to say yes today, while it still moves: “To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts” (Hebrews 3:15).

A hard place, plainly

This is hard to read, and we should not hurry past it, or soften it. The Bible gives the reason. God had waited long: he told Abraham “the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full” (Genesis 15:16). Moses will say later that “for the wickedness of these nations the LORD doth drive them out” (9:4). It was God’s judgment on them, carried out when he said the time had come, and not Israel’s own war of conquest.

Israel took only the cattle and the spoil of the cities (35). And the One who judged is the One who is just:

“… Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?”

Genesis 18:25

We do not decide who is judged, and we do not use this chapter for any war of our own. We read it, and we fear the LORD who gave the sentence.

A go, and a no

“From Aroer, which is by the brink of the river of Arnon… even unto Gilead, there was not one city too strong for us” (36). Heshbon is the city of Sihon.

Then comes the line that is easy to miss: “Only unto the land of the children of Ammon thou camest not, nor unto any place of the river Jabbok… nor unto whatsoever the LORD our God forbad us” (37).

They obeyed the “no” as well as the “go”. They took what God gave and left what he forbad, even when they were winning.

Today: turn, leave, buy, take

  1. Sinround and round the same mountain, and grabbing what was never ours to take
  2. Regretthe thirty and eight years are gone. They are spent, and nobody gets them back, but he was with them in every one
  3. Grace“thou hast lacked nothing”; the word “turn northward” came at last
  4. Hopethe giants fell for others, and Sihon fell for Israel. God can do it again
  5. Todaypay for what you use, leave alone what is another man’s, and when he says turn, turn

If you are tired of going round and round, call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor, and tell them where you are circling. Do not wait thirty and eight years.

Points to remember

  1. Turn northward“Ye have compassed this mountain long enough: turn you northward” 2:3When God says turn, turn. The loop has a door.
  2. Not a foot breadth“Ye shall buy meat of them for money, that ye may eat” 2:6Edom’s land was Edom’s. Do not grab what God gave another; pay your way.
  3. Lacked nothing“these forty years the LORD thy God hath been with thee; thou hast lacked nothing” 2:7Even under the sentence, he was with them.
  4. Thirty and eight years“the space in which we came from Kadesh-barnea, until we were come over the brook Zered, was thirty and eight years” 2:14The men of war were wasted out, as the LORD sware; the next generation crossed.
  5. Giants before“a people great, and many, and tall, as the Anakims; but the LORD destroyed them before them” 2:21Giants had fallen before. The trouble was never their size.
  6. Words of peace“I sent messengers… unto Sihon king of Heshbon with words of peace” 2:26Peace first. Sihon would not, and God delivered him up.

Question 1 of 6

Turn northward

What did the LORD say when they had compassed mount Seir many days?

What verses tell us about this?

“While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation.”

Hebrews 3:15

Answer

“Ye have compassed this mountain long enough: turn you northward.” The time of going round was over, and God told them which way to go.

Question 2 of 6

Not a foot breadth

Why was Israel not to take Edom’s land, and how were they to get food?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thus dwelt Esau in mount Seir: Esau is Edom.”

Genesis 36:8

Answer

Because the LORD had given mount Seir to Esau for a possession, and Esau’s children were their brethren. They were to buy their food and water with money, and not to take it.

Question 3 of 6

Lacked nothing

What had the LORD done for them these forty years?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Answer

He blessed the work of their hands, knew their walking through the wilderness, and was with them all forty years, so that they lacked nothing.

Question 4 of 6

Thirty and eight years

How long was it from Kadesh-barnea to the brook Zered, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“But with whom was he grieved forty years? was it not with them that had sinned, whose carcases fell in the wilderness?”

Hebrews 3:17

Answer

Thirty and eight years, until all the generation of the men of war were wasted out from among the host, as the LORD sware unto them. Their unbelief at Kadesh-barnea shut them out.

Question 5 of 6

Giants before

Who had lived in the lands of Moab and Ammon before, and what had become of them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The Emims lived in Moab, and the Zamzummims in Ammon, tall peoples like the Anakims. The LORD destroyed them before Lot’s children, and they dwelt in their stead.

Question 6 of 6

Words of peace

What did Moses send to Sihon, and what came of it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Words of peace: let us pass through your land and we will pay for our food and water. Sihon would not let them pass, came out to fight at Jahaz, and the LORD delivered him before Israel.
