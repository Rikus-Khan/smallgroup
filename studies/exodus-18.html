Exodus 18

Jethro's counsel

Moses' father-in-law comes to the mount of God, rejoices in all the LORD has done, and then tells Moses, kindly and plainly, that the way he is working is not good. The greatest man in Israel listens.

Part one

“Now I know that the LORD is greater”

Moses' family comes

Moses had married Jethro's daughter Zipporah in Midian, where he lived forty years as a shepherd (chapters 2–3). When he went back to Egypt she was “sent back” to her father. The text does not say why, and we do not need to guess.

Now Jethro brings her and the two boys to the mount of God. (An alien is a foreigner living in a land not his own.)

Two names, one story

Gershom

“A stranger there”

“I have been an alien in a strange land.” Moses named his first son for the exile: a man far from home, with a past behind him.

Eliezer

“God is my help”

“The God of my father … was mine help, and delivered me from the sword of Pharaoh.” The second son is named for the rescue.

Moses' two boys carry his two chapters: where he had been, and where God took him. Many a man in this room has a name like each: a strange land, and a help. Tell both parts. The first without the second leaves out God.

At the mount of God

Moses had kept Jethro's flock in Midian and come to Horeb, “the mountain of God” (3:1). Now Jethro comes to him there, in the same country, in the wilderness of Arabia.

“Moses went out to meet his father in law, and did obeisance” (bowed low in respect), “and kissed him.” The leader of a whole nation went out to meet an older man, bowed, and asked about his welfare: how he was. No pride.

They went into the tent, and talked. A man needs somebody who knows his story and asks him how he is.

Telling it, and hearing it

MosesAll that the LORD had done unto Pharaoh and to the Egyptians for Israel's sake… and how the LORD delivered them.

JethroBlessed be the LORD, who hath delivered you… Now I know that the LORD is greater than all gods.

Moses did not play it down or brag. He told it: what the LORD did, the hardship (“travail”, hard labour and trouble) on the way, and how he delivered them. And Jethro, a priest of Midian, rejoiced and blessed the LORD.

Tell your own story the same way: what the LORD has done in you, plainly, and the hard part too. It will do more good than you think, for the man who hears it, and for you.

A meal before God

Jethro took a burnt offering and sacrifices for God, and Aaron and all the elders of Israel came to “eat bread with Moses' father in law before God”.

Notice the company: a Midianite priest, and the elders of Israel, at one table, before the LORD. He is the God of Abraham, whose son Midian was (Genesis 25:2), and Jethro's confession fits that line: the LORD is greater than all gods.

A shared meal after a testimony is what a small group is. We tell what God has done, and then we sit down together. And this is a fine pattern for a man new in the faith: worship, then bread, with brothers.

Part two

“The thing that thou doest is not good”

Enquire of God means to ask what God says. The people came to Moses with disputes, and he judged between one man and another, and “made them know the statutes of God” (God's written rulings, his laws). It was real, good work.

There was one judge, and the whole people in a line, all day. Moses was doing something good, in a way that could not last.

“Not good”

  • wear awayto wear out, like a garment: to collapse from exhaustion
  • too heavymore than one man can carry
  • performto do the whole of it
  • alonehe said it twice: thyself alone, and “thou, and this people”

Jethro said it kindly and plainly: it is not good. He did not say Moses was doing wrong. He said Moses was going to be worn out, and so would the people standing in line. Both would suffer.

It takes a wise friend to say this, and a humble man to hear it. A man in recovery is often a man who carries too much by himself: the secret, the guilt, the work, the family. The answer is not to try harder alone.

Four pieces of counsel

  1. Stand before Godtake their causes to him
  2. Teach themthe laws, the way, the work
  3. Choose able mento rule thousands to tens
  4. Keep the great mattersthe small ones, others judge

God-ward means toward God. Moses' job was to stand between the people and God: to take their causes to him and teach them what he says. That is the work only he could do. The small matters others could judge.

And a safeguard: “and God command thee so.” Jethro is giving counsel, and says Moses must check it with God. Good advice is not a command from heaven, but good advice may still be from him.

Sharing the load

Each ruler handled what he could, and passed up only what he could not. Most disputes never reached Moses at all, and the people “shall also go to their place in peace”.

The same idea shows up later, when the church had a complaint about widows being overlooked. The apostles said they must keep to prayer and the word, and chose seven men to serve (Acts 6:2–4).

What kind of men?

  • ablethey could do the work: capable, with ability
  • such as fear Godthey answered to him, in private too
  • men of truthhonest and reliable, their yes was yes
  • hating covetousnessnot for sale: they would not take a bribe or an easy gain

Note the order. Ability alone is not enough, and a good heart alone is not enough. The men who share the load of a people must be both able and honest, and hate to take what is not theirs.

It is also a list for ourselves. Which one is hardest for you? Covetousness is wanting what is not yours, and it is a close cousin of every addiction. Ask the Lord for a hatred of it.

You were not made to carry it alone

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

“Without counsel purposes are disappointed: but in the multitude of counsellors they are established.”

Proverbs 15:22

There is a burden to carry for yourself, and burdens that are too heavy for one man. Jethro would say so too: thou art not able to perform it thyself alone. The first move for the man who cannot cope is not to try harder. It is to say so, and to let someone bear it with him.

If it is heavy enough that you cannot see a way through, or you are close to the end of yourself, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today. That is what they are for, and God has put them there to share the load.

“Moses hearkened”

“The way of a fool is right in his own eyes: but he that hearkeneth unto counsel is wise.”

Proverbs 12:15

Moses had seen the burning bush, and the sea open. He was a prophet, and the leader of the nation. And he took advice from a Midianite priest, who was no prophet and no leader of Israel. The best sign of a man who is close to God is that he can be told.

Real world: be told, and tell

  • Tell your storyWhat the LORD did, hard part included, to someone today.
  • Put down the loadName the one thing you are carrying alone, and give it to a brother.
  • Take adviceWhen a wise, older man says “this is not good”, listen and do it.
  • Pick good menBe around men who fear God, tell the truth and cannot be bought.

Today: ask yourself what you have been doing alone that is wearing you out. Then tell one brother in Christ, or your pastor, and ask him to help carry it. You have not failed by asking. Moses did.

Moses told all that the LORD had done, hardship included, and Jethro rejoiced and said, “Blessed be the LORD.” Tell your story the same way, and let a brother rejoice with you.

Question 2 of 6

Worship

What did Jethro say about the LORD, and what did he and the elders do together?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That the LORD is greater than all gods. Then Jethro brought an offering and ate bread with Aaron and the elders before God. A testimony leads to worship, and a table with brothers.

Question 3 of 6

The load

What was wrong with the way Moses judged the people?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He was doing it all himself, from morning till evening. It would wear him out, and the people too. A good work, carried alone, is “not good”. Ask a brother to help carry it.

Question 4 of 6

Counsel

What advice did Jethro give Moses?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Stand for the people before God and teach them his laws, choose rulers over thousands, hundreds, fifties and tens, and keep only the great matters for yourself. “They shall bear the burden with thee.”

Question 5 of 6

Character

What kind of men were to be chosen as rulers?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Able men who fear God, men of truth, who hate covetousness. Skill and a good heart both. Ask the Lord to make you that sort of man, and walk with men who are.
