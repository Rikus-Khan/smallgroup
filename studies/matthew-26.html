Matthew 26

Not as I will

A woman pours out her best, a friend sells Jesus for silver, the Lord gives His men the bread and the cup, prays alone in a garden, and is arrested, while Peter warms himself outside and falls.

Part one

Two days before

Verses 1–16

The leaders plot in a palace. A woman pours out a fortune. A disciple asks for a price.

Their plan, His hour

  1. SundayHe rides in21:1–11
  2. Tuesdayteaching in the temple21:23 – 25:46
  3. Two days beforethe plot is laid26:1–5
  4. Thursdaythe Passover; Gethsemane26:17–56
  5. That nightthe trial; Peter's fall26:57–75
  6. Fridaythe crossch. 27

Caiaphas was high priest from about AD 18 to 36, the man Rome let run the temple. The leaders wanted Jesus dead, but quietly, after the crowds of pilgrims went home.

Jesus had already named the day: at the passover. And that's when it happened. Men made their plans; God kept His. Jesus was never a victim of their schedule.

Very precious ointment

At Bethany, two miles out of Jerusalem, Jesus eats with Simon the leper, likely a man He had healed. An alabaster box was a sealed flask of soft white stone. John tells us what was in it:

“Why was not this ointment sold for three hundred pence, and given to the poor?”

John 12:5
1 pennya day's pay (Matthew 20:2)
300 penceabout a year's wages

Waste, or a good work?

the disciples

“this waste”

A year's wages, gone in a minute, poured on a man's head. They did the math and were angry.

Jesus

“a good work”

She saw what the men hadn't: He was going to die. She gave her best while she still could, “for my burial.”

Some men look back and think their whole life has been wasted. Nothing you pour out on Jesus is wasted, not your time, your money, your years, or what's left of them. He remembers it, and here He made sure the whole world would too.

The price of a slave

Right after a woman gives Jesus a fortune, one of the twelve asks what He's worth to His enemies.

Exodus 21:32, the law

If the ox shall push a manservant or a maidservant; he shall give unto their master thirty shekels of silver, and the ox shall be stoned.

Zechariah 11:12, about 500 years before

And I said unto them, If ye think good, give me my price; and if not, forbear. So they weighed for my price thirty pieces of silver.

The law set thirty shekels as payment for a dead slave. That's what the Son of God went for. Matthew comes back to these coins and Zechariah in the next chapter (27:3–10).

Part two

This is my body

Verses 17–35

The last Passover, a traitor at the table, and a meal to remember Him by.

The passover

Every spring Israel remembered the night God brought them out of Egypt. A lamb died for each house, and its blood went on the door. Unleavened bread is bread baked without yeast.

“…and when I see the blood, I will pass over you…”

Exodus 12:13

Every one of those lambs pointed to this one: “For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us” (1 Corinthians 5:7).

“Is it I?”

JesusVerily I say unto you, that one of you shall betray me.

Eleven men, one at a timeLord, is it I?

JudasMaster, is it I? not “Lord”

Nobody pointed across the table. Each man looked at his own heart first, a good habit for us. Judas, already paid, calls Him Master, teacher, never Lord. Jesus knew, and still let him dip in the same dish, as a friend.

The Lord's Supper

the bread

“my body”

Broken and handed to them. His body would be broken on the cross the next day, for them.

the cup

“my blood”

Poured out for the forgiving of sins, the way the lamb's blood marked the door.

Jesus was sitting right there holding them. The bread and the cup are pictures, and the Supper is a memorial. It doesn't save anyone; we keep it to remember the One who does.

“This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me.”

Luke 22:19

A new testament

A testament here is a covenant, a binding promise. God promised a new one through Jeremiah, and Jesus says His blood seals it:

“…for I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more.”

Jeremiah 31:34
ἄφεσις said aphesis, “remission” a sending away; set free

Remission means your sins are sent away, the debt cancelled. “Shed for many”: for anyone who will come. Not for the good men only. For sinners.

Until that day

Jesus calls the cup “this fruit of the vine.” What matters is what it stands for. Most Baptist churches use grape juice, so no man in recovery has to choose between the Lord's table and his sobriety. If you're unsure, ask your pastor; he'll be glad you did.

And the promise: “with you.” On His way to the cross, Jesus is already planning to sit down with these men again in His Father's kingdom. A seat is waiting for every believer.

The hymn was likely from Psalms 113–118, sung at Passover: “The LORD is my strength and song, and is become my salvation” (Psalm 118:14).

The shepherd struck

Zechariah 13:7, about 500 years before

Awake, O sword, against my shepherd, and against the man that is my fellow, saith the LORD of hosts: smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered: and I will turn mine hand upon the little ones.

Matthew 26:32

But after I am risen again, I will go before you into Galilee.

Offended means tripped up. Jesus tells them they'll all run, and in the same breath makes a date to meet them afterward. He knew about their failure before it happened, and planned the reunion anyway.

“I will never”

PeterThough all men shall be offended because of thee, yet will I never be offended.

JesusThis night, before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.

Peter meant every word, and so did the rest (v. 35). That was the problem: he trusted his own strength. Plenty of us have said “never again” and meant it. Being sure of yourself isn't the same as being safe.

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

Part three

Not as I will

Verses 36–56

The Son of God on His face in the dirt, three sleeping friends, and a kiss.

The oil press

Gethsemane means oil press, an olive grove across the Kidron from the city.

From the supper, Jesus leads the eleven out across the valley in the dark.

With Peter, James and John near, the weight comes down: “exceeding sorrowful, even unto death.” Where olives were crushed, He was pressed.

Before morning He'll be marched back to Caiaphas (v. 57).

Watch and pray

the spirit

“willing”

They loved Him. They wanted to stay awake for Him. Peter wanted to die for Him.

the flesh

“weak”

Tired, heavy-eyed, asleep within the hour. Wanting to isn't the same as being able.

Jesus doesn't say try harder. He says watch: stay awake, see it coming. And pray: lean on God before the hour hits. Most battles with temptation are won or lost in the hours before it.

“…but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Three times, the same prayer

  1. “if it be possible”let this cup pass… not as I willv. 39
  2. “except I drink it”thy will be donev. 42
  3. “the same words”settledv. 44

The cup is the suffering ahead, above all God's judgment on sin, which Jesus would drink on the cross in our place. He was a real man, and He didn't want it. He asked honestly, then gave His will to His Father.

That's how to pray when life is crushing you: tell God plainly what you want, then say “thy will be done” and mean it. He knows that place from the inside (Hebrews 4:15).

Betrayed with a kiss

A disciple greeted his teacher with a kiss. Judas used it to point Him out in the dark to a mob with swords and staves, wooden clubs.

“Yea, mine own familiar friend, in whom I trusted, which did eat of my bread, hath lifted up his heel against me.”

Psalm 41:9

And Jesus' answer: “Friend.” Not traitor. Even then He gave Judas one more chance to face what he was doing. And He didn't wait to be dragged out: “Rise, let us be going” (v. 46).

Twelve legions

John tells us the sword was Peter's (John 18:10). Jesus heals the man's ear (Luke 22:51) and tells Peter to put it away. A Roman legion was an army unit of about 6,000 men.

12legions
× 6,000about, in each
72,000+angels, for the asking

He could have walked away from that garden any moment He chose. He stayed so “the scriptures be fulfilled.” Nobody took His life from Him. He gave it.

Part four

Before the cock crow

Verses 57–75

Inside, Jesus tells the truth and is condemned. Outside, Peter lies to save himself.

Afar off

Peter didn't fall all at once. Look at the night one step at a time:

  1. “I will never”sure of himself (v. 33)
  2. asleepinstead of praying (v. 40)
  3. the swordhis own way (v. 51)
  4. fledwith the rest (v. 56)
  5. afar offfollowing at a distance (v. 58)
  6. with the servantsin the enemy's courtyard (v. 58)

Over-confident, under-prayed, acting on his own, then keeping his distance and sitting with the wrong crowd. Most falls look like that from the inside. If you notice yourself on one of those steps, turn around there. Don't wait for the bottom.

Looking for a reason

The council was the Sanhedrin, the Jewish high court of about seventy. The verdict was settled (v. 4); they needed a charge. God's law guarded against exactly this:

“…at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established.”

Deuteronomy 19:15

At last they twisted something He'd said years before: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up” (John 2:19). He meant His body, and in three days He'd do it. Meanwhile “Jesus held his peace” (v. 63), a lamb silent before its shearers (Isaiah 53:7).

Under oath

“I adjure thee” puts Him under oath before God. Silent until now, Jesus answers: “Thou hast said,” meaning yes. Then He quotes two scriptures the court knew well:

Psalm 110:1

The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool.

Daniel 7:13

I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him.

The prisoner tells His judges that one day they will stand before Him. He's the Christ, the Son of God, and He went to the cross for saying so.

“Prophesy!”

Tearing his robe was the high priest's show of horror at blasphemy, words that insult God. It would have been, if Jesus weren't the Son of God. Then they spat on Him and buffeted Him, beat Him with fists. Isaiah saw it:

“I gave my back to the smiters, and my cheeks to them that plucked off the hair: I hid not my face from shame and spitting.”

Isaiah 50:6

inside

“Prophesy!”

Blindfolded (Luke 22:64), He's mocked as a fake prophet.

outside, at that hour

“Thou shalt deny me”

In the courtyard, His word about Peter is coming true.

Louder each time

a damsel, in the palacehe denied“I know not what thou sayest.”
another maid, in the porchwith an oath“I do not know the man.”
they that stood bycursing and swearing“I know not the man.”
“And immediately the cock crew.”

A servant girl, not a soldier. Bewrayeth means gives away: his Galilean accent. To curse and swear wasn't just rough talk; Peter called down curses on himself if he was lying. Each lie needed a bigger one to hold it up.

Wept bitterly, and not the end

Peter's lowest point wasn't the end. On Easter morning the angel said, “tell his disciples and Peter” (Mark 16:7), by name. By the lake in Galilee, Jesus restored him and gave him work to do.

Two kinds of sorrow

Judas

sorry, and alone

He regretted it, took his sorrow to the priests instead of to Jesus, and despaired (27:3–5).

Peter

sorry, and found

He wept, stayed with the disciples, and was there when the risen Lord came looking for him.

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Shame says, hide. Godly sorrow says, go back to Him. If you've fallen, don't carry it alone. Bring it to Jesus, and tell a brother.

Question 1 of 6

Worship

What did the woman do that the disciples called waste?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

She poured a box of very precious ointment, worth about a year's wages, on Jesus' head. The disciples saw waste; Jesus called it a good work, done for His burial, and said it would be told wherever the gospel goes.

Question 2 of 6

The Lord's Supper

What did Jesus say the bread and the cup mean?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The bread is His body, given for us; the cup is His blood of the new testament, shed for many for the remission of sins. We eat and drink in remembrance of Him, showing His death until He comes.

Question 3 of 6

Surrender

How did Jesus pray in Gethsemane?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On His face, in deep sorrow, three times. He honestly asked that the cup might pass, and gave His will to His Father: “not as I will, but as thou wilt.” Then He rose and went to meet the men who came for Him.

Question 4 of 6

Temptation

What did Jesus tell the sleeping disciples to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To watch and pray, so they wouldn't enter into temptation. Their spirit was willing but their flesh was weak. Willpower wasn't enough; they needed to stay alert and lean on God before the hour came.

Question 5 of 6

Who He is

What did Jesus say when the high priest put Him under oath?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Thou hast said”: yes, He is the Christ, the Son of God. And they would see Him at the right hand of power, coming in the clouds of heaven. He was condemned for telling the truth about who He is.

Question 6 of 6

Falling and getting up

What did Peter do when the cock crew?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He remembered Jesus' word, went out, and wept bitterly. It was godly sorrow, and it led him back. Jesus had already promised to meet them in Galilee, and He restored Peter there and gave him work to do.
