James 2

Faith that shows

A man in gold walks into church, and then a man in rags. James asks what we do with each, and then asks the hardest question in the letter: “can faith save him?”

Part one

The man at the door

Verses 1–7

Two men walk into the meeting. One of them gets the good seat.

Two men, one door

A man in a sharp suit and a gold ring is shown to the best seat. A man in “vile raiment,” worn-out, dirty clothes, is told to stand or sit on the floor.

James calls it “respect of persons”: judging a man by his looks and his money before he says a word.

Picture the man who walks in fresh out of prison, still shaking from last night. How does that room treat him? How did it treat you? And if you are in the good chair today, move over.

What God did with the poor man

What the church did

Despised him

Looked him over and gave him the footstool, and bowed to the men with money. Even though, James says, those are the men who “oppress you, and draw you before the judgment seats” and speak against the name of Christ.

What God did

Chose him

“Rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom.” God has never judged a man by his bank account or his record. He picked fishermen, a tax collector, a thief on a cross.

“And the King shall answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.”

Matthew 25:40

If you have ever been the man with the footstool, remember that this verse was written about you. Today: look for the new face in the room, and sit next to him.

Part two

The royal law

Verses 8–13

Favoritism is not a small thing. James holds it up against the law, and then shows how mercy answers the law.

One crack is enough

Verse 10 is hard. The law is not a list where you can score 90 and pass. It is one pane of glass: a single crack, and it is broken. “He that said, Do not commit adultery, said also, Do not kill.” The same God spoke both.

James is not saying every sin is the same size. He is saying nobody keeps the law by being mostly good. That includes the man in the good chair, and the man who sneered at the poor one, and every one of us.

Now the turn. “So speak ye, and so do, as they that shall be judged by the law of liberty.” And then:

Mercy laughs at the judge

The man who shows no mercygets judgment with none
→
butmercy rejoiceth against judgment

“Rejoiceth against” means mercy stands over judgment, and wins. A man who knows how much mercy he has been given will find it hard to withhold it. Look back at the man in vile raiment: every one of us was him when we came to God, with nothing to put on.

The cross is where this is settled. Judgment fell on Jesus, so mercy could stand over every man who trusts Him. Today: where do you owe someone the mercy you were shown?

Part three

Faith that works

Verses 14–26

The most argued-over paragraph in the letter. Read it slowly: James and Paul are on the same side.

Words that fill no stomach

A brother, hungry and coldI have not eaten since yesterday, and I have no coat.

The believerGo in peace. Be warmed and filled.
and gives him nothing

Every word of that was kind, and none of it fed anybody. “What doth it profit?” James asks. That is what faith that never moves looks like: nice words and an empty plate.

Notice what James is not saying. He does not say works add to what Jesus did. He says a faith that never does anything is “dead, being alone.” It is a corpse, a body that has stopped breathing. It looks like a person, but nothing is alive in it.

“My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth.”

1 John 3:18

Even the demons believe

  • Believing God existsThe demons do that, and it makes them shake. They know exactly who He is. They are not saved.
  • Saying “I have faith”James: “shew me.” Words cost nothing. They never fed the brother in the cold.
  • Agreeing with the right doctrineRight answers are good. But a man can ace the test and never trust the Teacher.
  • Trusting Him, and so living like itFaith is not only knowing that He is there. It is resting your whole weight on what He did, and your life starts to lean that way.

If you are worried that you do not have enough works to prove your faith, you are not behind. Every believer starts with nothing to show except trust. James is asking whether that trust is alive, whether it has started to move, even a little, even by one phone call today.

Abraham: believed first, shown later

  1. Genesis 15Abraham believes God“counted it to him for righteousness”
  2. Years passThe promise waitshe and Sarah grow old
  3. Genesis 21Isaac is bornAbraham is a hundred
  4. Genesis 22The altar“now I know that thou fearest God”

“And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness.”

Genesis 15:6

Abraham was counted righteous in Genesis 15, long before he lifted a knife, and before the law, and before Isaac was even born. So when James says Abraham was “justified by works,” he cannot mean that is how he got right with God. He means the altar showed his faith was the real thing. The Scripture “was fulfilled”: Genesis 22 filled up what Genesis 15 said.

“And he said, Lay not thine hand upon the lad, neither do thou any thing unto him: for now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me.”

Genesis 22:12

James and Paul: same side

Paul, in Romans 4

Faith's root

How does a sinner get right with God?

By trusting Him, with no works to bring.

Before God. Abraham in Genesis 15.

Romans 4:5
James, in chapter 2

Faith's proof

How do people know my faith is real?

By what it makes me do.

Before people. Abraham in Genesis 22.

James 2:18

“But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness.”

Romans 4:5

Two writers, two questions. Paul is answering a man who thinks he must earn it. James is answering a man who thinks he can say the words and change nothing. Neither of them has the other's problem. “Justified” in Paul means declared right with God. In James it means shown to be right, proved. A faith that saves is never alone, so James and Paul agree: we are saved by faith alone, but the faith that saves is never alone.

One tree

Nobody asks whether the root or the fruit makes it a tree. A tree is both. Under the ground, faith alone: no works needed to get in. Above the ground, fruit: not to get the tree, but because the tree is alive.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.”

Ephesians 2:8–10

Paul says it in one breath, with both halves: saved by grace through faith, for good works. The works are what He had in mind for you from the start.

Rahab, a woman with a past

Rahab the harlot, of Jericho → in the family line of Jesus She heard what the LORD had done and believed it: “the LORD your God, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath” (Joshua 2:11). Then she hid the spies and sent them out another way. Her trust did something.

“By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not, when she had received the spies with peace.”

Hebrews 11:31

Abraham was the respected father of the Jews. Rahab was a foreigner and a prostitute. Faith worked the same way in both, and she is named in the line of Jesus: “Salmon begat Booz of Rachab” (Matthew 1:5, the same woman). Nobody's past is too far gone.

As the body without the spirit

A body with no breath is a body. It is not a person.

That is verse 26. Faith without works is not a small, weak faith. It is a dead one. And a living faith always breathes: it shows up in something. The size does not matter. A small thing counts. Pick one today:

  1. A seatSit next to the new man.
  2. A mealFeed somebody who is hungry.
  3. A callApologise to the one you hurt.
  4. A promiseKeep the small one you made.

None of these will make God love you more. He already does. These are just what a living faith does: it breathes.

Points to remember

  1. No favourites“Hearken, my beloved brethren, Hath not God chosen the poor of this world rich in faith, and heirs of the kingdom which he hath promised to them that love him?” 2:5God chose the man we were tempted to push to the back. If you were that man, He chose you too.
  2. The royal law“If ye fulfil the royal law according to the scripture, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself, ye do well” 2:8Love your neighbour is the king's own law, and breaking even one point breaks the whole pane. We all need mercy.
  3. Mercy“For he shall have judgment without mercy, that hath shewed no mercy; and mercy rejoiceth against judgment.” 2:13Mercy stands over judgment. A man who has received it will give it away.
  4. Dead faith“Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone.” 2:17Faith alone saves, but the faith that saves is never alone. It is alive, and it moves.
  5. Demons“Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.” 2:19Knowing that God is there is not the same as trusting Him. The demons know, and they are not saved.
  6. Abraham and Rahab“Likewise also was not Rahab the harlot justified by works, when she had received the messengers, and had sent them out another way?” 2:25A respected patriarch and a prostitute: in both, real faith moved. Nobody's past is too far gone.

Question 2 of 6

The royal law

What is the royal law, and what does breaking one point make a man?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Love your neighbour as yourself. Break one point and you are guilty of the whole, like one crack in a pane of glass. None of us keeps it, which is why we need mercy.

Mercy

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Mercy. It stands over judgment and wins, for the man who has received it and gives it away.

Question 4 of 6

Dead faith

What is faith that hath not works?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Dead, being alone, like a body without breath. We are saved by faith alone, but the faith that saves is never alone: it shows up in good works.

Question 6 of 6

Abraham and Rahab

How did faith show itself in Abraham and in Rahab?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Abraham believed God, and his faith showed when he offered Isaac. Rahab believed, and hid the spies and sent them away safe. In each, trust moved.
