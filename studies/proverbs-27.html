Part one

Today, and true friends

Verses 1–10

Don't count on tomorrow, and don't trust a kiss over a wound.

You only have today

Todayin your hands
?to morrow

Making plans isn't wrong. Bragging about them as if tomorrow is guaranteed is. James says the same thing:

“Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow. For what is your life? It is even a vapour, that appeareth for a little time, and then vanisheth away.”

James 4:14

Men in recovery know this one: one day at a time. And if you've been putting off getting right with God, “behold, now is the day of salvation” (2 Corinthians 6:2). Not tomorrow. Today.

Heavier than stone

Try carrying a stone, or a sack of wet sand. A fool's anger is heavier than both. Everyone around him has to carry it: his wife, his kids, his coworkers.

Verse 4 goes further. Anger is cruel and comes out like a flood, but envy is worse. Anger blows up and passes. Envy sits and stews, and nobody can stand against it.

A wound or a kiss

A friend

Wounds you, faithfully

He tells you the truth even when it stings, because he wants you whole. It hurts like a doctor setting a bone.

An enemy

Kisses you, falsely

He tells you what you want to hear. He smiles, and pats your back, and lets you walk off a cliff.

“And forthwith he came to Jesus, and said, Hail, master; and kissed him.”

Matthew 26:49

The worst betrayal in history came with a kiss. Verse 5 says open rebuke beats love that never speaks up. The friend who tells you the truth “in love” (Ephesians 4:15) is worth more than ten who flatter you.

When you're starving, bitter tastes sweet

A full soul→loatheth an honeycombEven the best thing gets pushed away.
A hungry soul→every bitter thing is sweetEven poison looks good.

An empty man will swallow almost anything that promises to fill him: a bottle, a pill, a woman, a high. It tastes sweet because he's so hungry. That's why the answer isn't just to stop. It's to be filled with the right thing.

Verse 8: a man who wanders from his place is like a bird that leaves its nest. Verse 9: a friend's honest counsel is sweet like perfume. Stay close to home, and close to the brothers who tell you the truth.

The friend who's close

near

He can be at your door in five minutes when the night gets bad.

far off

He may love you, but he can't help you from three states away.

Don't drop old friends, especially the ones your father trusted. And in a crisis, the man close by is worth more than family far away. That's part of what this group is for.

Part two

Iron on iron

Verses 11–22

Seeing trouble coming, and the men who keep us sharp.

See it coming

The prudent man looks down the road. When he sees trouble ahead, he gets off the road before he's in it.

For a man in recovery, that's knowing your triggers: the people, places, and moods that lead to a fall. You don't test them. You take another road.

The simple man just keeps walking, sure he'll be fine this time. Verse 13 adds a money warning: a man who cosigns for a stranger has already walked into trouble.

Drip, drip, drip

A leaky roof on a rainy day: drip, drip, drip, all day, and you can't make it stop. Constant quarreling at home wears a man down the same way. And trying to hush it up is like trying to hold the wind, or grab oil in your fist (v. 16).

Verse 14: a loud “blessing” at the crack of dawn sounds nice, but it lands like a curse. Words can be right and the timing all wrong. Whether it's nagging or fake cheer, the question for us is the same: what's it like to live with me?

Iron on iron

You can't sharpen a blade with a pillow. It takes something just as hard, rubbing against it. There will be friction. There may be sparks.

That's what real brothers do. They ask the hard questions. They notice when you've gone quiet. They don't let you get dull.

A man alone gets dull fast. Who is your iron? Who has permission to ask you anything?

Why we need each other

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

“And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.”

Hebrews 10:24–25

Verse 19: look in still water and your face looks back. Men's hearts are a lot alike. The brother next to you has fought the same fight. That's why he can help, and why you can help him.

Never satisfied

The grave never says “that's enough people.” And our eyes never say “that's enough”: one more drink, one more look, one more dollar, one more high.

That's the trap of every addiction. It's a jar with no bottom. You pour your life into it and it's empty again by morning.

“Jesus answered and said unto her, Whosoever drinketh of this water shall thirst again: But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst;”

John 4:13–14

You can't pound it out

Grind a fool in a mortar like grain, and his folly still won't come out. Nobody can beat folly out of a man. It takes a new heart, and only God gives that.

Verse 21: a refiner's pot tests silver, and praise tests a man. Some get humble; some get puffed up. So let somebody else do the praising (v. 2).

“Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.”

Psalm 51:10

Part three

Know thy flocks

Verses 23–27

Steady work beats quick money.

Look after what you've been given

v. 25the hay appears
v. 25grass and herbs
v. 26lambs for clothing
v. 26goats buy a field
v. 27milk for the house

Money comes and goes, and even crowns don't last (v. 24). But a man who faithfully tends his flock, season after season, will have enough for his family. It isn't flashy. It's steady.

What are your “flocks”? Your family, your job, your money, your recovery, your walk with God. Know their state. Check on them every day. Small faithfulness, day after day, is how a life gets rebuilt.

Points to remember

  1. Tomorrow“Thou knowest not what a day may bring forth” 27:1Tomorrow isn't promised. Live today, and get right with God today.
  2. Friendship“Faithful are the wounds of a friend” 27:6A friend who tells you the hard truth loves you more than a flatterer.
  3. Accountability“Iron sharpeneth iron” 27:17Brothers sharpen each other with honest friction. Don't go it alone.
  4. Wanting more“The eyes of man are never satisfied” 27:20Every craving is a jar with no bottom. Only Jesus satisfies.
  5. Praise“So is a man to his praise” 27:21Praise shows what you're made of. Let others give it, and stay humble.
  6. Faithful work“Know the state of thy flocks” 27:23Tend what God gave you, day after day. Steady beats flashy.

Question 1 of 6

Tomorrow

Why shouldn't we boast about tomorrow?

What verses tell us about this?

“For that ye ought to say, If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that.”

James 4:15

“Take therefore no thought for the morrow: for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof.”

Matthew 6:34

Answer

Because we don't know what a day will bring. Our life is a vapor, and tomorrow belongs to God. So we plan humbly, “if the Lord will,” and live faithfully today. And today is the day to get right with Him.

Question 2 of 6

Friendship

How can a wound be faithful?

What verses tell us about this?

“But speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ:”

Ephesians 4:15

Answer

When a true friend tells you a hard truth, it hurts, but he's doing it to help you, like a doctor setting a bone. An enemy flatters you with kisses and lets you walk into ruin. Look for friends who speak the truth in love, and be one.

Question 3 of 6

Accountability

How does one man sharpen another?

What verses tell us about this?

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

“And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works:”

Hebrews 10:24

Answer

By rubbing up against him: honest questions, encouragement, and correction, even when there's friction. A brother lifts you up when you fall. Give a few men permission to ask you anything, and do the same for them.

Question 4 of 6

Wanting more

Why are our eyes never satisfied, and what can satisfy?

What verses tell us about this?

“But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life.”

John 4:14

Answer

We were made for God, and nothing else fills that space, so we keep reaching for more. A hungry soul will even call bitter things sweet. Only Jesus gives water that ends the thirst for good.

Question 5 of 6

Praise

How does praise test a man?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.”

Luke 14:11

Answer

Like fire tests silver, praise shows what a man is made of. If it makes him proud, it was a test he failed. So don't praise yourself. When others praise you, thank God and stay humble.

Faithful work

Answer

Riches don't last, but steady care does. The man who pays attention to what God put in his hands, every day, ends up with enough. Faithfulness in small daily things is how God rebuilds a life.
