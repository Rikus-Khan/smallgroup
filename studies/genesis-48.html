Genesis 48

Crossed hands, and a blessing

Jacob is dying. He blesses Joseph's two sons, crosses his hands to put the younger before the elder, and prays to “the Angel which redeemed me from all evil.”

Part one

Joseph comes to the sickbed

Verses 1–7

A dying father sits up, and tells his son what God promised.

He sat up

Israel strengthened himself, and sat upon the bed.

Jacob has been in Egypt seventeen years, and he is close to a hundred and forty-seven. He is sick, and he knows it is nearly over. So he finds the strength to sit up, because he has something to give his grandsons.

Notice the names. The writer says Jacob for the old, weak man and Israel when he acts in faith, the name God gave him after the night he wrestled. The man who sat up on that bed was Israel.

Some of you are worn out, or sick, or just done. There is a kind of strength that comes from having someone to bless. Find the person you can give something to today.

What a dying man held on to

Jacob had a long list he could have gone over: the stolen blessing, the years on the run, the favourite son, the lost Rachel. Look at what he picks. He tells Joseph what God said to him.

Luz is the old name of Bethel, “house of God”. God Almighty found him there, and again when he came home (35:9–12). Jacob does not say “I did well.” He says, He appeared to me, and he blessed me.

“By faith Jacob, when he was a dying, blessed both the sons of Joseph; and worshipped, leaning upon the top of his staff.”

Hebrews 11:21

The New Testament counts this bedside scene as faith, though Jacob's life had plenty of scheming in it. At the end he is leaning on a staff, and worshipping.

“As Reuben and Simeon, they shall be mine.” Jacob adopts Joseph's two boys as his own sons. They were born in Egypt to an Egyptian mother (41:50–52), and Jacob claims them for God's family anyway.

That is how Joseph's family gets a double share: two tribes, Ephraim and Manasseh. When Levi is later set apart for the LORD and given no land of its own, they keep the number of land-holding tribes at twelve.

The birthright that moved

Reuben was the firstborn, so the double share of the firstborn should have been his. Verse 5 puts Joseph's two sons in the place of the firstborn's portion. Chronicles tells us why:

“Now the sons of Reuben the firstborn of Israel, (for he was the firstborn; but, forasmuch as he defiled his father’s bed, his birthright was given unto the sons of Joseph the son of Israel:”

1 Chronicles 5:1

What a man does can cost his children something. That is a hard line to read, and chapter 49 will give the details. But it also means Joseph, the one who was wronged, is the one given the honour. God does not forget the man who was sinned against.

Old words in this chapter

  • issuechildren, those born to him
  • begettestyou become the father of
  • wittinglyknowingly, on purpose
  • ladsboys
  • embracedput his arms around

Verse 6 means that any more sons Joseph had would not get a tribe of their own. They would be counted under Ephraim and Manasseh, and share in their inheritance.

Rachel, by the road

In the middle of blessing his grandsons, Jacob stops and speaks of Rachel: “Rachel died by me in the land of Canaan in the way… and I buried her there in the way of Ephrath; the same is Bethlehem.”

She died in childbirth, on the road, a little way short of Bethlehem (35:16–20). That was close to fifty years before this bedside, and he still remembers exactly where. Grief does not expire. A man can carry it to the end of his life, and he is allowed to.

Part two

“Who are these?”

Verses 8–20

An old man who cannot see, two boys, and a blessing that goes the unexpected way, on purpose.

More than he asked for

Jacob, long beforeJoseph is without doubt rent in pieces. 37:33

Jacob, nowI had not thought to see thy face: and, lo, God hath shewed me also thy seed. more

For twenty-two years he thought his son was dead. Then he got Joseph back. Now, at the end, he is handed Joseph's boys as well. He had given up even hoping, and God went beyond his hopes.

“In this place”

“And the name of the second called he Ephraim: For God hath caused me to be fruitful in the land of my affliction.”

Genesis 41:52

Joseph says the same of Egypt, the place he was sold into: “whom God hath given me in this place.” The worst place of his life is where the blessings arrived. Some of you may one day say the same of the place you hit bottom.

Hands crossed

Joseph set the boys so the firstborn, Manasseh, would be at Jacob's right hand, the hand of the greater blessing. Jacob crossed his arms and laid his right hand on the younger boy, Ephraim.

The Bible says he did it wittingly: knowing exactly what he was doing. His eyes were dim, but he could see.

Three ways Jacob names God

  • “before whom my fathers did walk”the God of Abraham and Isaac: the family God, who made the promise
  • “the God which fed me”fed as a shepherd feeds his flock: a shepherd who led him for a lifetime (see 49:24)
  • “the Angel which redeemed me”the One who bought him back, out of all evil

This is a man whose life was a mess, much of it of his own making. He gives God the credit for all of it: the walking, the feeding, the redeeming. Every year of it was held by Someone.

Redeemed means bought back, rescued the way a close relative would. As far as I can find, this is the first time the King James uses the word, and it comes from a dying man who had lost a great deal.

“The Angel which redeemed me”

גָּאַל said ga'al “to buy back, to redeem”

Look at the grammar. Jacob says the Angel should “bless the lads”: one verb, singular, for God and the Angel together, as if they were one. This is the same Angel who spoke to him in a dream and said, I am the God of Bethel (31:11–13).

“In all their affliction he was afflicted, and the angel of his presence saved them: in his love and in his pity he redeemed them; and he bare them, and carried them all the days of old.”

Isaiah 63:9

Who redeems us now?

This Angel is the Son of God, before he came as a man. Jacob called on him, and he redeemed him. And the New Testament says how he does it:

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

He did not just rescue us from trouble. He took the curse that was ours, so that every evil that has ever claimed a man, including the worst of his own doing, no longer has the last word.

“Not so, my father”

JosephNot so, my father: for this is the firstborn; put thy right hand upon his head.

JacobI know it, my son, I know it: he also shall become a people, and he also shall be great. I know

Joseph was not a bad man here. He loved what was fair, and he wanted his older boy to get what seemed right. But he was trying to straighten out God's plan, and Jacob would not let him. Manasseh is not cursed either: he is told he will be great too.

We do it too: we decide how God ought to bless, and who should get it, and in what order. His ways cut across ours, and still turn out kind.

The younger, again and again

  • Abelover Cain, the firstborn (4:4–5)
  • Isaacover Ishmael, the older (17:19–21)
  • Jacobover Esau: “the elder shall serve the younger” (25:23)
  • Judah and Josephover Reuben, the firstborn (chapter 49)
  • Ephraimover Manasseh (this chapter)
  • Davidthe youngest of Jesse's sons (1 Samuel 16)

God keeps passing over the one who has the right by birth. Nobody gets in by being first, or best, or strongest.

“So then it is not of him that willeth, nor of him that runneth, but of God that sheweth mercy.”

Romans 9:16

This is good news for men who feel like the youngest, the least, the last in line: the one with the record. The blessing does not depend on your place in the line. It rests on his mercy.

“God make thee as Ephraim and as Manasseh”

Jacob gave his grandsons a blessing, and told Israel to use it: “In thee shall Israel bless, saying, God make thee as Ephraim and as Manasseh.” Many Jewish fathers still bless their sons with those same words.

These two boys grew up in the house of a great official in Egypt, with every temptation of that court around them. Yet an old man near death spoke God's name over them, claiming them for the family of Abraham and Isaac.

Real world: if you have sons, or any young men who look to you, the best thing you can do today is say a blessing over them out loud, by name. Not a lecture. A blessing: God make you strong; God keep you. A few words from a father can reach for a lifetime.

If you cannot do it for your own, because of the damage that was done, pray it for them, and ask your brothers in Christ and your pastor how to start repairing the road.

Part three

“Behold, I die: but God shall be with you”

Verses 21–22

A dying man hands on a promise and a piece of ground.

“I die: but God shall be with you”

Behold, I die: but God shall be with you.

He does not say, “I will always be with you.” He cannot. He says God will, and that God will bring them back to the land of their fathers. Egypt was a refuge, not a home.

Every man will one day hand his family to God, because he has to. A father who is gone does not leave them alone, if they know Whom they belong to.

The portion beyond his brothers

The last thing Jacob gives Joseph is a piece of ground: “one portion above thy brethren, which I took out of the hand of the Amorite with my sword and with my bow.” The Hebrew word for portion sounds like Shechem, and that is the place: John 4:5 speaks of “the parcel of ground that Jacob gave to his son Joseph”, by Sychar.

“And the bones of Joseph, which the children of Israel brought up out of Egypt, buried they in Shechem, in a parcel of ground which Jacob bought of the sons of Hamor the father of Shechem for an hundred pieces of silver: and it became the inheritance of the children of Joseph.”

Joshua 24:32

Genesis does not explain how the “sword and bow” fits with the silver Jacob paid (33:19), and we need not guess. What matters: a small piece of the promised land, held in faith, and Joseph's bones laid in it at last. Chapter 50 tells how that story ends.

Points to remember

  1. What he remembered“God Almighty appeared unto me at Luz in the land of Canaan, and blessed me” 48:3A dying man talks about what God did, not what he did.
  2. Grace“he set Ephraim before Manasseh” 48:20Blessing comes by God's mercy, not by birth or merit.
  3. The Angel“The Angel which redeemed me from all evil” 48:16God bought back this man's whole life, and through Christ he has done the same for us.
  4. Regret and grief“God hath shewed me also thy seed” 48:11God gave back more than he had hoped for. Do not stop hoping.
  5. Death and hope“Behold, I die: but God shall be with you” 48:21A father passes away, but his family is never left alone.
  6. The real world“God make thee as Ephraim and as Manasseh” 48:20Say a blessing over the young men in your life today.

Question 1 of 6

What he remembered

What did dying Jacob choose to talk about, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“By faith Jacob, when he was a dying, blessed both the sons of Joseph; and worshipped, leaning upon the top of his staff.”

Hebrews 11:21

Answer

God's promise. Jacob had plenty to regret, but he went back to the night God Almighty appeared to him and blessed him. The New Testament calls the scene faith. When your own record is long, hold on to what God has said about you, not to what you have done.

Question 2 of 6

Grace

Why did Jacob put the younger boy before the older one?

What verses tell us about this?

“So then it is not of him that willeth, nor of him that runneth, but of God that sheweth mercy.”

Romans 9:16

Answer

On purpose, and by God's choice, the same pattern as Isaac over Ishmael and Jacob over Esau. Nobody gets the blessing by being first or best. It rests on God's mercy, and that is good news for a man who feels last in line.

Question 3 of 6

The Angel

Who is “the Angel which redeemed me from all evil”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

Answer

The One who bought Jacob back from all evil, named in one breath with God: the Son of God, before Bethlehem. Jacob had a Redeemer, and so does every believer.

Question 4 of 6

Regret and grief

What had Jacob never expected to see again?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the name of the second called he Ephraim: For God hath caused me to be fruitful in the land of my affliction.”

Genesis 41:52

Answer

His son Joseph, whom he thought was dead, and Joseph's children as well. God gave him far more than he had dared to hope. If you are in a hard place, do not decide how the story ends.

Question 5 of 6

Death and hope

What did Jacob say to Joseph as he was dying?

What verses tell us about this?

“But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.”

1 Thessalonians 4:13-14

Answer

“I die: but God shall be with you.” He was not afraid, and he did not leave his family alone. They would go home to the land of their fathers. That is how a believer can face death, and how the people left behind can face life.

Question 6 of 6

The real world

What did the old man do for the boys who were left?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He blessed them and named God's name over them, spending his last strength on two boys. If you have sons or younger men looking at you, bless them out loud today. If you cannot reach them, pray for them by name, and ask your brothers in Christ and your pastor how to put things right.
