1 Corinthians 13

The more excellent way

In the middle of a fight about gifts, Paul stops and describes love. Then he holds it up like a mirror.

Where we are in 1 Corinthians

Paul is writing from Ephesus, about AD 55, to a church he started. The letter answers their problems one by one, and from chapter 7 the questions they had asked him.

chapters 1–4DivisionsIs Christ divided?
chapter 5Immoralitysin the church let slide
chapter 6Lawsuits, and the bodybrother against brother
chapter 7Marriageand staying as you are
chapters 8–10Idol meatfreedom, and your brother
chapter 11Worshipand the Lord's Supper
chapters 12–14Giftsand the way of love
chapter 15ResurrectionChrist is risen

The church was proud of its spiritual gifts, especially the showy ones, and used them to rank each other. Chapter 12 said every believer has the Spirit and a gift, and the body needs every member.

The middle of the argument

This chapter is read at weddings, but Paul wrote it to a church in a fight. It sits right in the middle of his answer about gifts, on purpose.

chapter 12Every man has a giftgiven to every man to profit withal
chapter 13 · todayLove is greater than any giftthe greatest of these is charity
chapter 14Use them in orderLet all things be done decently and in order.

chapter 12 ended: “and yet shew I unto you a more excellent way” (12:31)

Gifts were never the point. A gift used without love is like a tool swung without care: it hurts people. Paul is about to show the Corinthians what they were missing, and he does it by describing a Person, more than a feeling.

Part one

Without charity

Verses 1–3

The best gifts in the world, with one thing missing, add up to nothing.

What “charity” means

ἀγάπη said agapē love that gives itself for another man's good

When the King James was made, charity meant this kind of love. Today it means handouts, and verse 3 shows that's not it: a man could give away everything he owns and still not have charity. The same Greek word is “love” in “if ye have love one to another” (John 13:35).

It's not a warm feeling and not being nice. It's the love that went to the cross: “God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us” (Romans 5:8).

Just noise

The Corinthians prized speaking in tongues above every other gift. Paul says: even if I spoke every language on earth and the language of angels too, without love I'm a gong somebody keeps banging.

Corinth was famous across the Roman world for its bronze. Every man there knew the sound of brass in the temples and the street. Tinkling here means clanging, clashing. Loud, and it says nothing.

You can be right, gifted and loud, and still just be noise to the people around you. They hear the clang, not the message.

The math of nothing

verse 1the tongues of men and of angels− charity=“sounding brass”
verse 2prophecy, all mysteries, all knowledge, faith to move mountains− charity=“I am nothing”
verse 3every penny to the poor, my own body to the fire− charity=“it profiteth me nothing”

Paul puts himself in it: “I.” The best preaching, the deepest Bible knowledge, the biggest faith, the greatest sacrifice. Take love out and the answer is zero every time. God doesn't grade on gifts. He looks for love.

Part two

What charity does

Verses 4–7

Fifteen marks of love. Not a feeling: things love does and won't do.

Fifteen marks of charity, plainly

  1. suffereth longpatient a long time; slow to give up on people
  2. is kinddoes good, even to those who don't deserve it
  3. envieth notisn't sore when another man is blessed
  4. vaunteth not itselfdoesn't brag or show off
  5. is not puffed upnot swollen with its own importance
  6. doth not behave itself unseemlyisn't rude or crude
  7. seeketh not her ownisn't out for itself
  8. is not easily provokedno short fuse
  9. thinketh no evilkeeps no tally of wrongs done to it
  10. rejoiceth not in iniquityisn't glad when another man falls
  11. rejoiceth in the truthglad when what's true and right wins
  12. beareth all thingscovers and carries; holds up under the weight
  13. believeth all thingsready to believe the best, not looking for the worst
  14. hopeth all thingsdoesn't write a man off
  15. endureth all thingsstays when it gets hard

Look at the middle ones: envy, bragging, being puffed up. Paul has used that word about the Corinthians again and again (4:18; 5:2; 8:1). This isn't a greeting card. It's a list of exactly what was wrong in that church, turned inside out.

Now read it out loud with your own name in the blank. Be honest: where does it stop being true? “Is not easily provoked”? “Thinketh no evil”? Most of us don't get far.

Now put His name in. Every line holds. Jesus is the only Man who ever lived this list all the way through, and He lived it toward men like us.

Where you fall short

That list will show any man where he falls short: the temper, the envy, the score you keep on the people who hurt you. Some of us carry years of that, and the people we hurt the most were the ones closest to us. Name it. Don't bury it.

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

He didn't wait for you to pass the list. He loved you first, at your worst. And now “the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost” (Romans 5:5). You don't produce this love by gritting your teeth. You let His love work through you, one day and one line at a time.

Today: pick the one line where you fell shortest. Ask God for it by name before you walk into work, and watch for the moment you'll need it.

Part three

Charity never faileth

Verses 8–10

The gifts are for now. Love is forever.

What runs out, and what doesn't

prophecies“shall fail”
tongues“shall cease”
knowledge“shall vanish away”
charity“never faileth”

The three gifts the Corinthians bragged about most are the three Paul says will end. Gifts are scaffolding: needed while the building goes up, taken down when it's done. Love is the building.

Now in part, then complete

  1. nowwe know in part13:9
  2. nowwe see through a glass, darkly13:12
  3. coming“that which is perfect”13:10
  4. thenface to face13:12

Perfect means complete, full-grown. Good Bible teachers differ on what “that which is perfect” is. Many say it's the day Christ comes and we see Him face to face. Others say it's the finished New Testament, when the sign gifts were no longer needed. Godly Baptists hold both.

Either way, what's plain is the same: the gifts are partial and temporary. Love is not. Nobody will need a gift in heaven. Everybody there will be loving.

Part four

Grown up, face to face

Verses 11–13

Childish things put away, a dim mirror, and the three things that last.

Put away childish things

“I spake… understood… thought as a child”

Me first, and right now

A child wants what he wants when he wants it, blames somebody else, and throws a fit when he doesn't get his way. A grown man can still live like that, and many of us did for years.

“when I became a man”

Love that bears, waits, and gives

Grown up, in Paul's sense, isn't about age or toughness. It's love. The Corinthians thought their gifts made them mature. Their fighting showed they were still children.

Put away is something a man does. Nobody grows up by accident. And the good news is that growing up is a process: if you're further along than you were a year ago, God is doing it. Childish things don't all go at once.

Through a glass, darkly

A glass is a mirror. In Paul's day it was polished metal, often bronze: you could make out a face, but dim and wavy. Darkly means as in a riddle, not clearly.

That's how we know God now: truly, but in part. One day, face to face. No more squinting.

“Then shall I know even as also I am known.” God already knows you completely, every bit of your record, and He still sent His Son for you. Being fully known by Him isn't something to fear. It's home.

These three abide

faithtrusting what we can't yet see. One day it gives way to sight.
hopewaiting for what God promised. One day it gives way to having it.
charitynever gives way to anything. It goes on forever, because God is love.

Abideth means stays, remains. When the gifts are gone, these three are left. And of the three, love is the greatest, because love is what God Himself is. Faith and hope get you home. Love is what you'll do there forever.

“Follow after charity, and desire spiritual gifts, but rather that ye may prophesy.”

1 Corinthians 14:1

Follow after charity.

The next chapter picks up right here. Paul goes back to the gifts, but now with love as the rule: chase love first, and then use your gift in a way that builds up the other man.

“Let all your things be done with charity.”

1 Corinthians 16:14

Today: at work, at home, in this group, do one thing that costs you something and gains you nothing, for somebody else's good. Don't announce it. That's charity.

Points to remember

  1. Without love“And have not charity, I am nothing” 13:2The greatest gifts, the biggest sacrifice, minus love, add up to zero.
  2. What love is“Charity suffereth long, and is kind” 13:4Fifteen marks of love. Only Jesus fits every one, and His love works in us.
  3. Never faileth“Charity never faileth” 13:8Gifts are for now and will end. Love lasts forever.
  4. Growing up“I put away childish things” 13:11Grown up means love, not gifts or age. Put away me-first.
  5. Face to face“Then shall I know even as also I am known” 13:12We see dimly now. One day we'll see Him clearly, the One who already knows us fully.
  6. The greatest“The greatest of these is charity” 13:13Faith, hope and love abide. Love is greatest, because God is love.

Question 1 of 6

Without love

What are the greatest gifts worth without charity?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Nothing. Tongues without love are noise. Prophecy, knowledge and great faith without love leave a man “nothing,” and verse 3 says even giving everything away profits nothing. God looks for love, not a show.

Question 2 of 6

What love is

What does charity do, and what does it refuse to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It is patient and kind, glad at the truth, and it bears, believes, hopes and endures all things. It refuses envy, bragging, pride, rudeness, selfishness, a short fuse, keeping score, and gladness at another man's fall. Only Jesus lived it all the way, and His love is poured into us.

Question 3 of 6

Never faileth

What will end one day, and what never fails?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Prophecies, tongues and knowledge will end, because they are partial. When that which is perfect comes, what is in part is done away. Charity never fails. The gifts the Corinthians bragged about are temporary; love is forever.

Question 4 of 6

Growing up

What did Paul put away when he became a man?

What verses tell us about this?

“Brethren, be not children in understanding: howbeit in malice be ye children, but in understanding be men.”

1 Corinthians 14:20

Answer

Childish things: the way a child talks, understands and thinks. Me first, right now, someone else's fault. A grown man in Christ puts those away on purpose, and the mark of growing up is love, not gifts.

Question 5 of 6

Face to face

How well do we see now, and what is coming?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

Dimly, like a face in a polished bronze mirror: we know in part. One day we will see face to face and know as we are known. God already knows us fully, and loved us while we were still sinners.

Question 6 of 6

The greatest

Which three things abide, and which is the greatest?

What verses tell us about this?

“By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another.”

John 13:35

“Follow after charity, and desire spiritual gifts, but rather that ye may prophesy.”

1 Corinthians 14:1

Answer

Faith, hope and charity. The greatest is charity: faith will become sight and hope will be fulfilled, but love goes on forever. It is how the world knows we belong to Jesus, so follow after it first.
