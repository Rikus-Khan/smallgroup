Part one

Peter explains

Verses 1–18

Peter comes home to Jerusalem to a hard welcome, and tells it from the beginning.

Home to hard questions

Peter had stayed with Cornelius, a Roman officer, in Caesarea. He walked back up to Jerusalem, about sixty miles, and the news had beaten him there.

“They that were of the circumcision” were Jewish believers who still kept the old customs. These were not enemies. They were brothers, good men, who loved the Lord. A Jew didn't walk into a Gentile house, and he certainly didn't sit at the table.

Notice what bothered them. Not that the Gentiles believed. It was that Peter ate with them. Sometimes the first thing we argue with is not what God did, but how close it brought us to people we'd rather keep at a distance.

Peter tells it in order

oncetwicethree times

The sheet held every kind of creature the old law called unclean: “fourfooted beasts… creeping things, and fowls of the air.” God had given those food rules to Israel to set them apart (Leviticus 11). Now the same God was telling Peter to eat.

Peter doesn't tell it like a man making excuses. He says “from the beginning,” step by step, and he doesn't leave out the part where he argued with God. When you've got something hard to explain, the honest version, in order, is the strongest one you've got.

“Not so, Lord”

The voiceArise, Peter; slay and eat.

PeterNot so, Lord: for nothing common or unclean hath at any time entered into my mouth.
“No” and “Lord” in one breath

The voiceWhat God hath cleansed, that call not thou common.

“Common” and “unclean” meant set apart as unfit. Peter had kept those rules all his life, and his first answer to God was a flat no.

If a man can say “No, Lord” and still be Peter, we are in good company. God keeps saying it until it sinks in: “What God hath cleansed.” Not what Peter cleaned up. What God cleansed.

Cornelius was already a good man. He prayed and he gave to the poor (Acts 10:2). And God still said he needed “words, whereby thou… shall be saved.” Being decent doesn't save anybody. Hearing the good news and believing it does.

Those six men matter too. Peter didn't ask Jerusalem to take his word for it. He brought witnesses, and he stood in front of the church with six men who had seen it. Peter was the apostle, and he still didn't go it alone.

“Then Peter opened his mouth, and said, Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons: But in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is accepted with him.”

Acts 10:34–35

Each new group, shown to be in

Acts is a book of history, and the church is moving outward just as Jesus said in Acts 1:8. At each new step, God gave a sign so nobody could say, “Those people don't count.”

  1. JewsJerusalem, Acts 2
  2. SamaritansActs 8
  3. GentilesCornelius, Acts 10–11
  4. John's followersEphesus, Acts 19

Peter's proof was simple: “God gave them the like gift as he did unto us, who believed.” Same gift, same faith, no extra steps. He was a witness that these men were fully in.

These scenes are not a pattern to chase. Nobody has to wait for a sign. The Holy Spirit comes to live in every believer the moment he believes (Ephesians 1:13).

Then they held their peace

Peter put it as a question a man can't answer. Verse 2 says they contended. Verse 18 says they held their peace, and glorified God. Same men, same room. What changed was that they heard the evidence and let go of their pride.

“Repentance unto life” means turning around, a new mind and a new direction, and the result is life. God had opened that door to people nobody expected. If you've ever thought grace was for the clean church folk and not for a man with your past, this verse is for you. The door was opened for the outsiders. “Whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved” (Romans 10:13).

Part two

The church at Antioch

Verses 19–26

What scattered the believers turned out to be how the gospel got out.

Scattered, and sowing

When Stephen was killed, the church in Jerusalem was driven out of the city (Acts 8:1). That looked like the end. They travelled as far as Phenice (Phoenicia, the coast north of Judaea), Cyprus, and Antioch.

At first they told the story “to none but unto the Jews only.” Then some men from Cyprus and Cyrene, nobody famous, we're not even given their names, spoke to the Greeks in Antioch.

God took the worst thing that had happened to His people and used it to carry the gospel farther than any plan they'd have made. If your own life got scattered, it is not too late for it to become a place where somebody else finds Jesus.

Barnabas, the encourager

Joses → Barnabas “being interpreted, The son of consolation” (Acts 4:36): a man who builds others up.

Jerusalem sent him to check on a new church of Greeks nobody had planned for. He looked at it and “saw the grace of God, and was glad.” He could have found a hundred things wrong. He saw what God was doing, and he cheered.

Then his advice: “with purpose of heart… cleave unto the Lord.” Cleave means stick like glue. It isn't a single decision on a Sunday. It's a purpose, a choice you make again every morning. Barnabas didn't say it once and leave; he stayed and taught.

Today, send one man a message. Somebody new, somebody struggling. “Thinking of you. Proud of you.” That small thing is what Barnabas did for a whole church.

Saul had been the church's worst enemy, and then the church's biggest worry. When they were afraid of him, it was Barnabas who spoke up for him (Acts 9:27). Now it was Barnabas again who went looking.

Think about those years. A man with a past, back home, with his best days seemingly behind him. And God wasn't done with him. When it looks like you've been benched, and when you've done something you can't undo, you may be in Tarsus. God knows where to find you, and He often sends a friend like Barnabas.

One name for all of them

a Jewa Greekfrom Cyprusfrom Cyrenea Levitethe one who used to persecute us
Hello, my name isChristianChrist's people

The word is Christianos, belonging to Christ. It was probably first used by outsiders, maybe even as a joke. The church kept it. It shows up only twice more in the Bible (Acts 26:28, 1 Peter 4:16).

Whatever other labels you've carried, the ones the world stuck on you and the ones you stuck on yourself, this is the name that counts. You belong to Christ, and it doesn't wear off.

Part three

Relief for Judaea

Verses 27–30

A warning of hard times, and a church that answers it with its wallet.

Sent down the road

“Dearth” is a famine. Agabus, a prophet from Jerusalem, said it was coming, and it came in the days of the emperor Claudius, who ruled about AD 41 to 54. Judaea, about 300 miles to the south, felt it badly.

The gift went “by the hands of Barnabas and Saul”, two men the church trusted with the money. Honest hands matter when money moves.

Every man, according to his ability

no one set the amountevery one gave

Think about who was giving. Many in Antioch's church were Gentiles, people who not long before had no part in all this. They didn't wait for the famine to arrive, and they didn't wait to be asked. They gave to Jewish believers most of them had never met. The wall that nearly split the church in verse 2 was now carrying food across it.

Nobody was told how much. “According to his ability” means nobody was measured against the next man. A small gift and a big one were both welcome, and God loves a cheerful giver (2 Corinthians 9:7). Today, if there's a brother who's short, you can do something about it: a ride, a meal, a few dollars. Don't wait to be rich enough to help.

Points to remember

  1. Grace“Then hath God also to the Gentiles granted repentance unto life.” 11:18Life is God's gift, to anyone who turns to Him. Good people need it too.
  2. Old walls“What was I, that I could withstand God?” 11:17Hear the evidence, let go of pride, and praise God.
  3. Hope“The hand of the Lord was with them.” 11:21God turns scattering into sowing. Your wreck can become somebody else's road to Jesus.
  4. Encouragement“Had seen the grace of God, was glad.” 11:23Be the man who sees what God is doing and cheers. Send that message today.
  5. Second chances“Barnabas to Tarsus, for to seek Saul.” 11:25When it looks like you're benched, God knows where to find you.
  6. Giving“Every man according to his ability.” 11:29Give what you can, before you're asked, and let nobody go hungry.

Question 1 of 6

Grace

What did God give the Gentiles, and how did they get it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Life, and the same Holy Spirit the Jewish believers had received. They got it by hearing the good news and believing on the Lord Jesus Christ, just like anybody else. Nobody earned it, and nobody was too far outside. God opened the door, and they came through.

Question 2 of 6

Old walls

How did the church go from arguing to praising God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Peter told them the whole story, in order, with six witnesses. When they saw that God had done it, they stopped arguing and gave Him the glory. These were good men with an old habit. When the evidence showed them God's hand, they let go.

Question 3 of 6

Hope

What did God do with the scattering after Stephen?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He used it. The believers were driven out by persecution, and wherever they landed they talked about Jesus. Ordinary men from Cyprus and Cyrene started a church in Antioch, and a great number turned to the Lord. What looked like a disaster became the way the gospel travelled.

Question 4 of 6

Encouragement

What kind of man was Barnabas?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A good man, full of the Holy Ghost and of faith. He saw the grace of God, was glad, and encouraged everybody to hold tight to the Lord. He wasn't looking for problems. He was looking for what God was doing, and he helped it grow.

Question 5 of 6

Second chances

Why did Barnabas go looking for Saul?

What verses tell us about this?

“But Barnabas took him, and brought him to the apostles, and declared unto them how he had seen the Lord in the way, and that he had spoken to him, and how he had preached boldly at Damascus in the name of Jesus.”

Acts 9:27

Answer

Barnabas had known Saul since the day nobody else would trust him, and he knew God wasn't finished with him. Saul had been quiet in Tarsus for years. Barnabas went and found him, and Saul ended up teaching a whole year in Antioch. A man with a past can still be the right man for the job.

Question 6 of 6

Giving

What did the believers in Antioch do when they heard a famine was coming?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They decided to help, and every man gave what he was able. They didn't wait until the hunger arrived, and they sent it to people they had never met, through two men they trusted. Real faith has a wallet in it.
