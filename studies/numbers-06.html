Numbers 6

The Nazarite, and “The LORD bless thee, and keep thee”

A man or a woman could separate themselves to the LORD for a time: no grape, no razor, no dead body. And at the end of the chapter the LORD tells Aaron and his sons the words to bless Israel with.

Part one

“To separate themselves unto the LORD”

Verses 1–8

A vow a man or a woman could take, and what it cost.

A Nazarite: one who is separated

  • a Nazariteone separated to the LORD for a time, by his own vow
  • either man or womanthe vow was open to a woman as well as a man
  • separateset apart: the word comes sixteen times in the chapter (counted, with “separation” and “separateth”)
  • a vowa promise made to the LORD: nobody was made a Nazarite by the law; a person chose it
  • consecrationbeing set apart to the LORD: “the consecration of his God is upon his head” (verse 7)

Samson was one from birth (Judges 13:24): “the child shall be a Nazarite to God from the womb to the day of his death” (Judges 13:7). And in Acts, four men with “a vow on them” were still shaving their heads in Jerusalem (Acts 21:23–24). Jesus was called “a Nazarene” (Matthew 2:23), but that is a different word, for a man of Nazareth.

Nothing of the vine

  • wine and strong drinkthe drinks that make a man drunk
  • vinegar of winewine gone sour; he was not to drink it either
  • liquor of grapesthe juice pressed out of the grapes
  • moist grapes, or driedfresh grapes or raisins
  • the kernels, and the huskthe seeds and the skins: nothing of the vine at all

The LORD does not say a Nazarite was holier than other people because he did without. The vine was a good gift of the land, and he gave all of it up for a time to say with his whole life: I belong to the LORD first.

Three marks of a man set apart

No vine

34

Not wine, not strong drink, not vinegar, not grapes fresh or dried, not even the kernels and the husk.

No razor

5

“Let the locks of the hair of his head grow.” The hair was the sign that anyone could see: “he shall be holy.”

No dead body

67

Not even for his father, his mother, his brother or his sister. “The consecration of his God is upon his head.”

The third is the hardest. Every Israelite who touched the dead was unclean for a time. The Nazarite was not even to go near, not for his own family. He was to be the LORD's first, and the LORD sets the rule. And the LORD, in the very next verses, makes provision for a man who fails it without meaning to.

Real world: the vine

Wine and strong drink come first in the list. For some men in this group, the cup is what took everything. So hear what the chapter does and does not say.

  • It was a vow, not a rule for everyoneNobody was made a Nazarite by the law. A person chose it, to be the LORD's in a way that anyone could see.
  • It was a gift to God, not a way to earn himThe Nazarite is holy because he belongs to the LORD, and the vow shows it.
  • For us“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit” (Ephesians 5:18). Not our will alone, but the Spirit, who lives in every believer from the day he believes. And brothers who know the road: call or text them.

Part two

“The days of his separation are fulfilled”

Verses 9–21

When a death spoils the vow, and when the vow is done.

A death beside him, and a new start

  1. The dayone dies “very suddenly by him”he has not chosen it
  2. Day sevenhe shaves his head“the day of his cleansing”
  3. Day eighttwo turtles or two young pigeonsa sin offering and a burnt offering
  4. That same day“shall hallow his head”and a lamb for a trespass offering

A turtle here is a turtledove. The death was sudden, and it was not his fault; still the LORD says “he sinned by the dead”, and sees that it is dealt with. “The days that were before shall be lost”: he starts his count again. The vow is spoiled, but he is not thrown out. There is a priest, an offering, and a new first day.

Some of us have lost a count of days: a sober year, a streak. The count is lost, and the man is not. There is a sin offering, and Christ's one offering has “perfected for ever them that are sanctified” (Hebrews 10:14).

What he brought at the door

a he lamba burnt offering
a ewe lamba sin offering
a rampeace offerings

Each is “without blemish”, with a basket of unleavened bread, cakes of fine flour mingled with oil, wafers anointed with oil, and the meat offering and the drink offerings that go with them. A wafer is a thin cake of bread.

Look at the middle one. This man has kept his whole vow, and at the end he brings a sin offering. Nobody finishes a vow clean enough to go without one. The man who kept every day still stood in need of the blood, and the LORD put it in the law at the very end of his holy days.

The hair in the fire

“The hair of his separation” is not saved or thrown away: it goes into the fire under the peace offerings. What he grew for the LORD goes back to the LORD. The priest then waves the offerings with the “wave breast and heave shoulder”, which are “holy for the priest” (verse 20). Sodden means boiled.

Then: “after that the Nazarite may drink wine.” The vow ended, and an Israelite went back to ordinary life. Verse 21 adds that a man could vow more than the law asked, “beside that that his hand shall get”, and “according to the vow which he vowed, so he must do”. This is not a verse to go back to the cup on. The men of this group know what a first drink does, and the wisdom of staying clear, with brothers beside you, is yours to keep.

Part three

“On this wise ye shall bless”

Verses 22–27

The words the LORD himself gave to say over his people.

Three lines, one blessing

24The LORD bless thee, and keep thee:he gives good, and he guards it
25The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee:his face turned toward you in favour
26The LORD lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace.Countenance is face. The LORD looks at you, and the end of it is peace

Three lines, each beginning “The LORD”, with “thee” six times (counted). It is the LORD who blesses, who keeps, who shines, who gives. The priests say the words, but the blessing is his: “I will bless them” (verse 27). Moses wrote it down, and Aaron and his sons were to say it. “On this wise” (verse 23) is old English for “in this way”.

His face, and his name

The second line asks that the LORD's face shine upon you. Paul tells us where that light is now seen:

“For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ.”

2 Corinthians 4:6

“Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:”

Romans 5:1

The third line ends in peace, and the New Testament says how we have it: peace with God, through Jesus Christ. “And they shall put my name upon the children of Israel”: the LORD's name was set on his people when the blessing was spoken over them. The LORD put his own name on his people, and that is more than any name we made for ourselves.

He lifted up his hands, and blessed them

“And he led them out as far as to Bethany, and he lifted up his hands, and blessed them. And it came to pass, while he blessed them, he was parted from them, and carried up into heaven.”

Luke 24:50–51

“Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid.”

John 14:27

The last thing the Lord Jesus did on earth was to lift up his hands and bless his own. And the peace of the third line is the peace he gives: not as the world gives it.

Real world: the words over you

The chapter began with a man giving something up, and it ends with the LORD giving.

  • SinWe try to be holy by our own vows: no drink, no this, no that. And then the day comes when somebody dies beside us, or we fail, and the count is lost.
  • Regret“The days that were before shall be lost.” The old count is gone. But look at what comes next: a priest, an offering, and the same day a new start.
  • GraceEven the man who finished the vow brought a sin offering. Christ's one offering has “perfected for ever them that are sanctified” (Hebrews 10:14).
  • Hope“The LORD bless thee, and keep thee.” Not a reward for the best days, but words the LORD told them to say over every one of them.
  • TodaySay the three lines to yourself in the morning. Then say them over a brother: send it by text to a man who is having a hard day.

Points to remember

  1. The vow“When either man or woman shall separate themselves to vow a vow of a Nazarite, to separate themselves unto the LORD” 6:2A vow anyone could take: man or woman, for a time, to belong to the LORD.
  2. Separated“All the days of his separation shall he eat nothing that is made of the vine tree” 6:4No vine, no razor, no dead body.
  3. Defiled“if any man die very suddenly by him, and he hath defiled the head of his consecration” 6:9A death he did not choose still spoiled the vow, and there was a way to begin again.
  4. The end of the days“one ewe lamb of the first year without blemish for a sin offering” 6:14Even a man who kept the vow brought a sin offering at the end.
  5. The blessing“The LORD bless thee, and keep thee: The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: The LORD lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace.” 6:24–26The words the LORD gave to say over his people.
  6. His name“And they shall put my name upon the children of Israel; and I will bless them.” 6:27The blessing is his: he puts his name on his people, and he blesses them.

Question 1 of 6

The vow

Who could take the vow of a Nazarite?

What verses tell us about this?

“But he said unto me, Behold, thou shalt conceive, and bear a son; and now drink no wine nor strong drink, neither eat any unclean thing: for the child shall be a Nazarite to God from the womb to the day of his death.”

Judges 13:7

Answer

Either man or woman, by a vow made for the days of their separation to the LORD.

Question 2 of 6

Separated

What three things was a Nazarite to keep from?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The vine (wine, strong drink and everything made of the grape), the razor (he lets his hair grow), and any dead body, even of his own family.

Question 3 of 6

Defiled

What if someone died suddenly beside him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

His vow was defiled. He shaved his head on the seventh day, brought two turtles or pigeons on the eighth, and started his days again: “the days that were before shall be lost”.

Question 4 of 6

The end of the days

What did he bring when the days of his separation were fulfilled?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A he lamb for a burnt offering, a ewe lamb for a sin offering and a ram for peace offerings, with a basket of unleavened bread. He shaved his head at the door and put the hair in the fire.

Question 5 of 6

The blessing

What words were Aaron and his sons to say over the children of Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“The LORD bless thee, and keep thee: The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: The LORD lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace.”

Question 6 of 6

His name

What did the LORD say he would do when they put his name on Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:”

Romans 5:1

Answer

“I will bless them.” The priests speak the words, but the blessing is the LORD's own. And the peace of the third line is ours through Jesus Christ.
