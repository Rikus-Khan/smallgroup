Part one

Two mites

Verses 1–4

Rich men drop in their gifts. A widow drops in two tiny coins. Jesus says she gave the most.

The smallest coin there was

1 mitethe smallest copper coin in the land
2 mitesabout a sixty-fourth of a day's pay
all“all the living that she had”

Jesus is still in the temple, in the last days before the cross. The treasury was a row of offering chests in the temple courts. Rich men came by and their gifts rang into the chests where everyone could see.

A widow in that world had no husband's wage and often no one to speak for her. Penury means poverty, real want. She had two coins. She could have kept one. She gave both.

More than they all

The rich men“of their abundance”

A big gift out of a bigger pile. Plenty left over.

The widow“all the living that she had”

Two coins, and nothing left.

God doesn't count the coins. He counts what it cost, and what's left in your hand. Theirs cost them nothing. Hers cost her everything, and she trusted God with tomorrow.

Some of us have nothing much to give but a few dollars, an hour, a broken life. Given to God, that's never small to Him.

Part two

Not one stone

Verses 5–19

The men admire the temple. Jesus tells them it's coming down, and what His people will face before the end.

The temple will fall

  1. About 20 B.C.Herod the Great begins rebuilding the temple
  2. About A.D. 30Jesus: “not one stone upon another”
  3. A.D. 66the Jews rise against Rome
  4. A.D. 70Rome takes Jerusalem; the temple is burned and torn down

The temple was one of the great buildings of the world: white stone, gold, and gifts, rich offerings hung on its walls. Some of its stones were bigger than a truck. About forty years after Jesus said this, it was gone, just as He said.

The men ask when, and what sign. Watch how Jesus answers. He tells them a lot, but He never gives them a date.

Don't be fooled

  • “I am Christ”Men will come using His name, claiming to be Him or to speak for Him.
  • “The time draweth near”They'll set dates and stir people up. Every one so far has been wrong.
  • “Go ye not therefore after them”Don't follow. Stay with Jesus and His Word.

The first danger Jesus names isn't war or earthquakes. It's being deceived. A man who has been lied to by his own cravings knows how good a lie can sound.

Be not terrified

When ye hear ofwars, earthquakes, famines and plagues,
→
thendon't panic. “The end is not by and by.”

Commotions are riots and uprisings. Divers places means many different places. Pestilences are deadly diseases that sweep through a land. By and by means right away.

Every generation since has seen wars and disasters. Jesus didn't tell us to read the news like a calendar. He told us not to be afraid.

It came true for them

“But before all these”: before the temple fell, Jesus' own men would be dragged into court. Luke wrote the book of Acts too, and there it all is:

  2. ✓into prisonsLuke 21:12Acts 5:18
  3. ✓brought before kings and rulersLuke 21:12Acts 25–26
  4. ✓a mouth and wisdom … not able to gainsay nor resistLuke 21:15Acts 6:10

“And they were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit by which he spake.”

Acts 6:10

Don't rehearse it

Meditate before means to rehearse your defence ahead of time. Gainsay means to answer back, to contradict. Jesus says: don't lie awake practising what you'll say. When the moment comes, I'll give you the words.

That doesn't mean don't study. It means don't worry. Some of us dread the day someone asks, “So what happened to you?” You don't need a speech. Tell the truth about what Jesus did. Nobody can argue with a changed life.

Not an hair

What men can do

Betray, hate, kill

Even family and friends may turn on you. “Some of you shall they cause to be put to death” (21:16).

What they can't touch

Your soul

The man who belongs to Jesus can't be lost. They can take his life; they can't take him out of God's hand.

Patience here means endurance, staying under the load and not quitting. “Possess ye your souls”: by holding on, you keep hold of the life that matters. Some days the only win is that you didn't quit. That counts.

Part three

Jerusalem compassed with armies

Verses 20–24

Jesus tells them the sign to watch for, and what to do when they see it: run.

When you see the armies, run

Compassed means surrounded. When Roman armies circle the city, don't hide behind its walls. Get out, and don't come back in for your things.

In A.D. 66 a Roman army surrounded Jerusalem, then pulled back for a while. An old church history says the believers remembered this warning and fled across the Jordan to a town called Pella.

Rome came back in A.D. 70. Jesus' warning saved the lives of those who believed it.

Trodden down

It happened. Josephus, a Jewish historian who lived through it, describes famine inside the walls, huge numbers killed by the sword, and nearly a hundred thousand carried away as captives. Jesus had wept over the city days before:

“And shall lay thee even with the ground, and thy children within thee; and they shall not leave in thee one stone upon another; because thou knewest not the time of thy visitation.”

Luke 19:44

“Until the times of the Gentiles be fulfilled”: good Bible teachers read this in more than one way, and we don't need to settle it today. What it plainly says is that Jerusalem's trouble has an until. God keeps the end of the story in His hands.

Part four

Watch ye therefore

Verses 25–38

The whole world shaking, and the Son of man coming. Jesus tells His people to look up, and to stay awake.

Two ways to watch the same storm

21:25–26Hearts failing

Signs in the sky, the sea roaring, nations in perplexity, not knowing which way to turn. Men faint with fear.

21:27He comes

“the Son of man coming in a cloud with power and great glory.”

21:28Look up

The same news that terrifies the world tells a believer: your redemption draweth nigh. Your rescue is close.

We don't have to understand every sign to know what to do. Lift up your head. The One who is coming is the One who died for you.

This same Jesus

The Son of man was Jesus' favourite name for Himself, taken from Daniel's vision of one who comes “with the clouds of heaven” to receive a kingdom (Daniel 7:13). He wasn't coming as a teacher this time. He was coming as King.

“Which also said, Ye men of Galilee, why stand ye gazing up into heaven? this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven.”

Acts 1:11

“Looking for that blessed hope, and the glorious appearing of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ;”

Titus 2:13

Leaves don't tell you the date summer starts. They tell you it's coming, so you get ready. That's how Jesus wants us to read the world: not as a puzzle to solve, but as a reminder to be ready.

“This generation”

A hard verse. Good Bible teachers read it more than one way:

The men standing thereA.D. 70They lived to see the city surrounded and the temple fall.
The ones who see the last signsthe endWhatever generation sees these things begin will see them finished.

We don't have to settle it today. What Jesus says next is the part to hold on to: “my words shall not pass away.”

“The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever.”

Isaiah 40:8

A heart weighed down

  • SurfeitingToo much of anything: eating, partying, the morning-after fog.
  • DrunkennessThe bottle, and anything else a man uses to numb himself.
  • Cares of this lifeBills, worry, work. Not sin, just enough to keep his eyes down.

Overcharged means weighed down, made heavy and dull. After all the talk of earthquakes and armies, the danger Jesus ends on is the one we carry inside. Two of these, every man in recovery knows. The third catches the rest of us.

“Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.”

1 Peter 5:7

As a snare

A snare is a bird trap. The bird never sees it. It's busy with the seed, and then it's too late. That day will come on the whole world like that: unawares.

Jesus' answer: “Watch ye therefore, and pray always.” Keep your eyes open, keep talking to God. Accounted worthy isn't earning heaven; that's a gift by faith. It's being found ready, standing unashamed before Him.

His last days of teaching

  1. Into Jerusalemon a colt, the crowd shouting19:28–40
  2. The temple cleansed“a den of thieves”19:45–46
  3. Teaching dailyin the temple by day, on the mount of Olives by night19:47–21:38
  4. The Passoverthe upper room and the arrest22
  5. The crossand the empty tomb23–24

Jesus knew what was days away. He spent His last days teaching, and the people got up early in the morning to hear Him. We still have His words. The best way to be ready for that day is to start this one with Him.

Question 1 of 6

Giving

Why did the poor widow give more than all the rich men?

What verses tell us about this?

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7

Answer

Because they gave out of what they had left over, and she gave all she had. God measures a gift by what it costs the giver and the trust behind it, not by the amount.

Question 2 of 6

Deceived

What is the first thing Jesus warns His followers about?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto them, It is not for you to know the times or the seasons, which the Father hath put in his own power.”

Acts 1:7

Answer

Being deceived. Men will come in His name and claim the time is near. Don't go after them. The dates belong to the Father; our job is to stay with Jesus and His Word.

Question 3 of 6

Hard times

What does Jesus promise His people when they are hated for His name?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That He'll give them the words when they stand before their accusers, that it will become a testimony, and that their souls are safe: not an hair of their head will perish. Our part is to endure.

Question 4 of 6

Jerusalem

What did Jesus say would happen to Jerusalem?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That armies would surround it, the temple would be torn down to the last stone, and the people would be killed or carried away. It happened in A.D. 70, about forty years later. His words came true.

Question 5 of 6

Look up

What should a believer do when the world is shaking?

What verses tell us about this?

“Looking for that blessed hope, and the glorious appearing of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ;”

Titus 2:13

Answer

Not be terrified, but look up. The things that make men's hearts fail are, for a believer, a sign that Jesus is coming and his redemption is near.

Question 6 of 6

Watch

How does Jesus tell us to stay ready for His coming?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

Answer

Watch and pray always. Don't let excess, drink, or the worries of life make your heart heavy and dull. Stay awake, stay close to God, and you won't be caught like a bird in a snare.
