2 Thessalonians 1

Rest for the troubled

A young church is being hammered for its faith, and its faith keeps growing. Paul writes to tell them God has seen every bit of it, and He will set it right.

The second letter to the Thessalonians

“Paul, and Silvanus, and Timotheus, unto the church of the Thessalonians in God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ”

Written from
Corinth, where Paul stayed a year and a half, making tents to pay his way (Acts 18)
When
about AD 51, only a few months after 1 Thessalonians
To whom
the young church at Thessalonica, the busy port and capital of Macedonia. Paul had been run out of town after only a few weeks.
Why
the persecution was getting worse, somebody had them believing “the day of Christ is at hand” (2:2), and some had quit working. Paul writes again to steady them.

Two letters, a few months apart, to the same small church. The first one comforted them about believers who had died. Then a rumor and a scare went through the church, and Paul had to write again, fast.

It's a short letter, three chapters, written to men and women who were scared and suffering. All three chapters lean on one thing: the Lord Jesus is coming back, and that changes how you get through today.

Part one

Faith that grows under pressure

Verses 1–4

The pressure is on, and these people are getting stronger, not weaker.

Three men who had been there

Paul

v. 1

Once the man who hunted Christians. Now he's the one being hunted, and he writes to a church that's going through what he used to dish out.

Silvanus

Acts 17:4

The same man as Silas. He was with Paul in the Philippi jail, singing at midnight, and with him in Thessalonica when the riot started.

Timotheus

1 Thessalonians 3:2

Timothy, the young helper. Paul sent him back alone to check on this church, and he came back with good news.

“And some of them believed, and consorted with Paul and Silas; and of the devout Greeks a great multitude, and of the chief women not a few.”

Acts 17:4

These aren't three professors writing from a quiet office. All three had been chased out of this very town. When they say “Grace unto you, and peace,” they know what it costs to need both.

Pressed down, growing up

“Groweth exceedingly” is one Greek word, hyperauxanei: growing over the top. And it grows in their “persecutions and tribulations,” not after them.

Their love grew too: “every one of you all toward each other.” Pressure pulls a group apart or together. This one pulled together.

Patience means endurance: staying under the load. You don't have to feel strong. Just don't quit today.

What they were enduring

“But the Jews which believed not, moved with envy, took unto them certain lewd fellows of the baser sort, and gathered a company, and set all the city on an uproar, and assaulted the house of Jason, and sought to bring them out to the people.”

Acts 17:5

That's how the church started: a mob hired off the street, a house broken into, a friend dragged to the city rulers. Paul left town; the believers couldn't. They lived next door to the people who hated them, and went to work beside them.

Paul says he brags about them to other churches: “we ourselves glory in you.” Think about that if you're getting grief for changing your life: from old friends, from family who don't trust it yet, from a crew at work that laughs at you. God isn't embarrassed by you. He's proud of a man who keeps standing.

Part two

God will set it right

Verses 5–10

Nobody gets away with anything forever. That's good news for the troubled, and a warning for everyone.

The scales will balance

Right now the scale looks crooked: the bullies are comfortable and the believers are hurting. God will level it. Recompense means pay back.

When? “When the Lord Jesus shall be revealed from heaven.” Some wrongs won't be put right in this life. They will be put right.

“Counted worthy” isn't earning heaven by suffering. Their staying power is a “token,” a sign that God is at work in them.

Payback is not your job

Some men here were badly wronged: beaten as kids, robbed, lied about, locked up with men who hurt them. The rage is real. This chapter says God has seen it, and He will deal with it. That frees you from carrying it.

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

But be honest: some of us were the troublers. So was Paul. He had dragged believers off to prison, and God saved him anyway.

“Who was before a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and injurious: but I obtained mercy, because I did it ignorantly in unbelief.”

1 Timothy 1:13

If you hurt people before you knew Christ, own it, make it right where you can, and stop carrying it as if He hadn't paid for it.

Two kinds of people on that day

Verses 8–9

“Them that know not God”

They “obey not the gospel.” The gospel is obeyed by believing it (Romans 10:16). They are shut out “from the presence of the Lord,” away from the One every good thing comes from, for good.

Verse 10

“All them that believe”

Not the best behaved, but all who believe. Christ is “admired” in them: people will look at what He made out of them and marvel at Him.

Paul says this soberly, and so should we. “Everlasting destruction” isn't a man ceasing to exist. It's a ruin that doesn't end, apart from God. Hell is real, and the Bible never treats it as a joke.

If you belong to Jesus, this isn't your sentence

These verses aren't here to keep a believer awake at night wondering if he's in. Paul says the opposite: that day is the day the troubled get rest.

“Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.”

John 5:24

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

God is patient because He wants everyone in. So the warning is for the people you love who don't know Him yet. Pray for one of them by name today, and when the door opens, tell him what Jesus did for you.

“To be admired in all them that believe.”

When Jesus comes back, He won't only be seen in the sky. He'll be seen in His people. The drunk who got sober, the thief who now gives, the angry man who learned to be gentle: every one of them is a trophy of His grace, and He gets the credit.

Paul adds, “because our testimony among you was believed.” It started when somebody told them, and they believed it. That is all it took for them, and all it takes for you. Your life, as messy as it's been, is going to be part of how Jesus is admired.

Part three

What Paul prays for them

Verses 11–12

With all that ahead, here's what Paul asks God for, every day.

A prayer worth stealing

Worthy of this calling

v. 11

God called them. Paul prays they'll live up to it: not to earn the call, but to fit it, like a man growing into a uniform.

Every good desire filled

v. 11

“All the good pleasure of his goodness”: every good thing God has a mind to do in them, carried all the way through.

Faith that gets to work

v. 11

“The work of faith with power”: faith that shows up in what they do, by His strength, not theirs.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

And it all rests on “the grace of our God.” From the first verse to the last, it's grace. Take this prayer and pray it over one man in this group today, by name.

Points to remember

  1. Growing“Your faith groweth exceedingly” 1:3Pressure doesn't have to break you. It can be where your faith grows most.
  2. Justice“It is a righteous thing with God to recompense tribulation to them that trouble you” 1:6God will set every wrong right. Payback is His job, not yours.
  3. Rest“To you who are troubled rest with us, when the Lord Jesus shall be revealed from heaven” 1:7Some wrongs won't be fixed in this life. They will be fixed when He comes.
  4. Away from Him“Everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord” 1:9Hell is real and sober. For the believer it's not a threat; it's a reason to pray for others.
  5. Admired“To be glorified in his saints, and to be admired in all them that believe” 1:10Your rescued life will be part of how Jesus is admired.
  6. Worthy“That our God would count you worthy of this calling” 1:11Not earning the call, but growing into it, by grace.

Question 1 of 6

Growing

What was happening to the Thessalonians' faith while they were being persecuted?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It was growing exceedingly, and so was their love for each other. They kept enduring in all their persecutions, and Paul bragged about them to the other churches.

Question 2 of 6

Justice

What will God do about the people who trouble His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

Answer

He will recompense them: pay back trouble to those who trouble His people. His judgment is righteous. So we don't take revenge; we leave it with Him.

Question 3 of 6

Rest

When will the troubled get their rest?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

When the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven with His mighty angels. On that day the troubled get rest, along with Paul and everyone who believes.

Question 4 of 6

Away from Him

What happens to those who know not God and obey not the gospel?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Everlasting destruction, shut out from the presence of the Lord. It's real and sober. But God is patient and wants none to perish, and whoever believes the gospel is safe.

Question 5 of 6

Admired

Where will Christ be glorified and admired when He comes?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In His saints, in all them that believe. What He has done in His people will make Him admired, and His name is glorified in them even now.

Question 6 of 6

Worthy

What did Paul pray that God would do for them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That God would count them worthy of His calling, fulfil every good thing He wants for them, and make their faith work with power, so that Jesus would be glorified in them, all by grace.
