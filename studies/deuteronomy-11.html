Deuteronomy 11

Remember what he did, lay up his words, and choose the blessing

Moses reminds the people of what their own eyes have seen, tells them that the land ahead drinks the rain of heaven, and sets before them “a blessing and a curse”, to be spoken from two mountains.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. Eyes that sawWhose eyes had seen the great acts of the LORD?
  2. Not like EgyptHow is the land they are going to different from the land of Egypt?
  3. Laid upWhere were they to lay up the words of the LORD?
  4. TaughtWhen were they to speak of the words to their children?

Part one

“Your eyes have seen all the great acts of the LORD”

Verses 1–9

Love him, Moses says, and remember what you watched him do.

Old words: verses 1–9

  • chargewhat he has put in your keeping to guard
  • alwayalways, every day
  • chastisementthe LORD's discipline: correction that trains, as a father trains his son
  • substancegoods and possessions, everything a man owns (verse 6)
  • prolongmake long, lengthen (verse 9)

Verse 1 starts with “Therefore”. Everything in this chapter stands on what Moses has just said in chapter 10: the LORD asks only that they fear him, walk in his ways and love him (10:12). Love comes first, and the keeping follows from it.

What their own eyes had seen

  1. Verse 3In Egyptmiracles and acts, unto Pharaoh and all his land
  2. Verse 4The Red seathe army, horses and chariots overflowed
  3. Verse 5The wilderness“until ye came into this place”
  4. Verse 6Dathan and Abiramthe earth opened her mouth

Moses speaks to the men who were there. He is clear about the line: “I speak not with your children which have not known, and which have not seen” (verse 2). The ones he is speaking to did not have to take anyone's word for it: “your eyes have seen all the great acts of the LORD” (verse 7).

The way to love him is to remember him. Moses doesn't argue the point: he just lists what happened, and says the LORD “hath destroyed them unto this day” (verse 4).

“And the earth opened her mouth, and swallowed them up, and their houses, and all the men that appertained unto Korah, and all their goods. They, and all that appertained to them, went down alive into the pit, and the earth closed upon them: and they perished from among the congregation.”

Numbers 16:32–33

We read it in Numbers 16. Moses names these two because it happened “in the midst of all Israel” (verse 6): nobody had to be told. They stood against the man the LORD had put over them, and the ground itself ended it. Appertained means belonged to.

One memory, two sides of God

Against those who stood against him

Pharaoh, his army, Dathan and Abiram

The LORD “hath destroyed them unto this day” (verse 4). Pharaoh and Egypt's army in the sea; the two rebels in the earth. The LORD is not mocked, and his people had watched it happen.

For his own people

The sea, and the wilderness

The same water that “overflowed” the army was a wall for Israel. And what he did “unto you in the wilderness” (verse 5) is every day of manna, water and a cloud. Same hand, same God.

Remembering only the grace makes a man careless. Remembering only the judgment makes him afraid. Moses asks them to keep both in front of them, and to love the one who did both.

Why keep it all? So they could go in

Keepall the commandments “which I command you this day”
→
Then“strong”, to “go in and possess the land”, and to “prolong your days in the land”

This is the LORD's covenant with Israel in their own land. It is not a promise that a man who obeys will never suffer, or that a believer is paid in years and money for being good. The land itself was never a wage: the LORD “sware unto your fathers to give” it (verse 9) before anyone in that crowd was born.

Grace came first, and the keeping is the answer to it. “Milk and honey” is the same promise Moses has used since Egypt: a land worth having, given before it was earned.

Part two

“A land which the LORD thy God careth for”

Verses 10–17

Not like Egypt: a land that has to be asked of heaven.

Egypt, and the land ahead

The land you came out of

sowedst thy seed, and wateredst it with thy foot, as a garden of herbs: the man did the watering himself.

The land ahead

drinketh water of the rain of heaven, and the LORD's eyes are on it “from the beginning of the year even unto the end of the year”.

Moses doesn't say Egypt was a bad land, only that it was a land you worked yourself. He says Canaan is “a land of hills and valleys”, and a farmer there cannot make his own rain.

A land you can only ask for

In Egypt, if the field was dry, a man could do something about it. In the land ahead, he could do nothing but look up. That is not a poorer land. It is a land where the LORD “careth” (verse 12) and where his people stay close to him, because every harvest comes by rain they did not make.

“Nevertheless he left not himself without witness, in that he did good, and gave us rain from heaven, and fruitful seasons, filling our hearts with food and gladness.”

Acts 14:17

Many of us came out of an Egypt of our own: a life where we ran it ourselves, “with thy foot”, one more plan, one more trick to manage the thing. It never ended in a harvest. The new life is the other kind of land. You can't water it yourself. You ask, and you wait, and he sends the rain.

And “the eyes of the LORD thy God are always upon it”: this is care, not a camera. He is watching it like a man watches a field he loves.

Old words: verses 10–17

  • due seasonthe right time, when the crop needs it
  • the first rain and the latter rainthe early rain at seedtime and the late rain before harvest
  • corngrain, wheat or barley
  • oilolive oil, for food and light
  • kindledset on fire, as a fire is lit

The rain comes “in his due season”, and its purpose is the harvest: “that thou mayest gather in thy corn, and thy wine, and thine oil” (verse 14).

Wine is named here, plainly, as one of the harvests God gave the land. Just as plainly, it is no excuse for a man God has set free from the cup to go back to it.

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

“It is good neither to eat flesh, nor to drink wine, nor any thing whereby thy brother stumbleth, or is offended, or is made weak.”

Romans 14:21

How a land goes dry

  1. the heart is deceived(verse 16)
  2. they turn aside(verse 16)
  3. they serve other gods(verse 16)
  4. the LORD's wrath is kindled(verse 17)
  5. the heaven is shut upno rain, no fruit (verse 17)

It starts in the heart, before there is a word or an altar: “that your heart be not deceived.” Nobody turns aside in one step. The warning comes first, as mercy: “Take heed to yourselves.” God does not hide the staircase from them.

Every relapse has the same top step. The heart believes a lie, such as “it would be fine just once”, and then the feet follow. Deal with it at the top of the stairs, out loud, with a brother in Christ.

Part three

“Lay up these my words in your heart and in your soul”

Verses 18–25

The words go inside first, then onto the hand, the door, and the children.

Where the words go

  • Heart and soulin the middle of the man, where he decides what he loves (verse 18)
  • The hand“bind them for a sign upon your hand”: what he does (verse 18)
  • Between the eyes“frontlets”: a reminder always in front of him (verse 18)
  • The door postswhat he sees every time he goes in or out of his own house (verse 20)
  • The gateswhere the town sees him come and go (verse 20)

The order matters. Heart and soul first: “in your heart and in your soul”. The hand, the eyes and the door are the outside of what is already inside. A man who only wears the words, and does not hold them, has the signs and not the thing.

“Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee.”

Psalm 119:11

Teach them your children, all day long

Sittingin thine house
Walkingby the way
Lying downat night
Rising upin the morning

Moses doesn't set a lesson hour. He names the ordinary moments of the day: at home, on the road, going to bed and getting up. The words are meant to be spoken of like the weather, not kept for a class.

Some of us are the fathers who weren't there for those moments. It is not too late to start saying them now, with a son, a daughter, or a man younger in the faith. A father who failed can still begin to speak the words of the LORD. “That your days may be multiplied, and the days of your children” (verse 21) is the promise to Israel in its land, but a house where the words are spoken is better for everyone in it.

Moses asks. God promises to do it himself.

“For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, saith the Lord; I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts: and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people:”

Hebrews 8:10

Moses says “lay up these my words in your heart” and the people could not do it by themselves, as the long story of this book shows. In the new covenant the LORD himself says, “I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts.”

So the man in Christ does what Moses asked, hiding the word in his heart, and he does it with a God who is already writing it there. It is grace at both ends: he gives the word, and he gives the heart to keep it.

“To love the LORD your God, to walk in all his ways, and to cleave unto him” (verse 22). Cleave is the word that holds a man to his wife; here it holds a people to the LORD.

The four edges

  • South“the wilderness”
  • North“Lebanon”
  • East“the river, the river Euphrates”
  • West“the uttermost sea”: the great sea (Numbers 34:6)

“Every place that the sole of your foot shall tread upon, that have I given unto you, as I said unto Moses.”

Joshua 1:3

The gift is as big as the walking. It stood on the condition of verse 22, and Joshua took it up in faith.

Why the nations go

Verse 23 says the LORD will “drive out all these nations” before them. A grown man should hear why plainly, and not be hurried past it.

We saw why in chapters 7 and 9: “for the wickedness of these nations” (9:5), after the LORD had waited until “the iniquity of the Amorites” was full (Genesis 15:16). It was not Israel's goodness that won the land, and they could lose it the same way (verses 16–17). The judgment on those nations is a warning to the people who replace them.

Part four

“I set before you this day a blessing and a curse”

Verses 26–32

Two mountains, one choice.

Set before them

The blessing

“if ye obey the commandments”

To hear the LORD and do what he says, “which I command you this day”.

The curse

“if ye will not obey… turn aside”

To go “after other gods, which ye have not known”. They do not even know these gods. They would be walking away from the one they have seen.

Moses says “this day” eight times in this chapter (verses 2, 4, 8, 13, 26, 27, 28 and 32). Everything he asks is for today, and so is the choice: this is the day the blessing and the curse are set in front of them.

And Christ bore the curse

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

“Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ:”

Ephesians 1:3

No man kept the whole of it. The blessing and the curse stand as a mirror: they show what the commandments deserved. Then Christ took the curse on the tree, so a believer is not left to live under it. Any man who has said “I'm cursed, look at my life” can hear: the curse that was yours, he took.

The blessing for us is no field or flock. It is “all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ”, and it does not depend on how well the days go.

Gerizim and Ebal

Moses speaks in the plains of Moab, east of the Jordan. “On the other side Jordan” is the land west of the river, “by the way where the sun goeth down”: the land they are about to enter.

The two mountains stand “over against Gilgal, beside the plains of Moreh”, with Shechem between them (Judges 9:7; Joshua 8:30–33). Champaign is open, level country; the plains of Moreh are by Sichem, that is Shechem, where Abram built his first altar in the land (Genesis 12:6–7). Blessing on Gerizim, curse on Ebal.

The same ground where the promise began

“And Abram passed through the land unto the place of Sichem, unto the plain of Moreh. And the Canaanite was then in the land. And the LORD appeared unto Abram, and said, Unto thy seed will I give this land: and there builded he an altar unto the LORD, who appeared unto him.”

Genesis 12:6–7

“And afterward he read all the words of the law, the blessings and cursings, according to all that is written in the book of the law. There was not a word of all that Moses commanded, which Joshua read not before all the congregation of Israel, with the women, and the little ones, and the strangers that were conversant among them.”

Joshua 8:34–35

Sichem is the same place, and the plain of Moreh the same plain, where the LORD first said to Abram “Unto thy seed will I give this land”. When Israel was over Jordan, Joshua brought them there and read every word, to the women and the little ones too. What Moses commands here, Joshua did there (Joshua 8:30–35).

Real world: remember, lay up, choose

  • SinForgetting. It starts in the heart: “that your heart be not deceived”, and then the feet follow. The old gods are not strangers to us. They are the bottle, the money, the screen, the habit.
  • RegretWe came out of an Egypt where we watered everything “with thy foot” and it never grew. The LORD does not hold the old fields over a man who has left them.
  • GraceThe land was sworn to the fathers before anyone earned it, and “Christ hath redeemed us from the curse” (Galatians 3:13). The curse is not what a man in Christ lives under.
  • HopeThe new land “drinketh water of the rain of heaven”. A man need not produce his own rain, and “the eyes of the LORD thy God are always upon it.”
  • TodayWrite one verse on a card, such as Psalm 119:11, and put it where you will see it going in and out, like the door post. Say it to one brother. And if the old stairs feel steep, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today.

  1. Eyes that saw“But your eyes have seen all the great acts of the LORD which he did” 11:7Moses speaks to men who were there, and love begins with remembering what he did.
  2. Not like Egypt“a land of hills and valleys, and drinketh water of the rain of heaven” 11:11A land a man cannot water himself: he must ask, and the LORD cares for it.
  3. Laid up“lay up these my words in your heart and in your soul” 11:18The words go inside first, and then onto the hand, the eyes and the door.
  4. Taught“speaking of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, when thou liest down, and when thou risest up” 11:19The ordinary moments of the day are the classroom.

Question 1 of 6

Eyes that saw

Whose eyes had seen the great acts of the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The eyes of the men Moses was speaking to: they saw the miracles in Egypt, the Red sea, the wilderness, and what happened to Dathan and Abiram. Their children had not.

Question 2 of 6

Not like Egypt

How is the land they are going to different from the land of Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In Egypt the man watered his seed himself, “with thy foot”. The land ahead is “a land of hills and valleys” that drinks the rain of heaven, and “the eyes of the LORD thy God are always upon it” (verse 12).

Question 3 of 6

Laid up

Where were they to lay up the words of the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In their heart and soul first; then bound as a sign upon the hand and as frontlets between the eyes; and written on the door posts of the house and on the gates.

Question 4 of 6

Taught

When were they to speak of the words to their children?

What verses tell us about this?

“And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.”

Deuteronomy 6:7

Answer

All through the day: when sitting in the house, walking by the way, lying down and rising up.

Question 5 of 6

The choice

What did Moses set before them that day?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A blessing, if they obeyed the commandments of the LORD, and a curse, if they would not obey but turned aside to other gods.

Question 6 of 6

The blessing on mount Gerizim and the curse on mount Ebal, on the other side Jordan, over against Gilgal, beside the plains of Moreh.
