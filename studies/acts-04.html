Part one

By what name?

Verses 1–12

A good deed gets two men arrested, and the questions they're asked turn into a sermon.

Who came upon them

  1. The prieststhe men who ran the temple's services. Peter and John were preaching in their own courtyard.
  2. The captain of the templethe officer in charge of the temple guard, who kept order there.
  3. The Sadduceesthe powerful priestly party. They didn't believe in any resurrection, so this preaching hit them hardest.

Notice what grieved them. Not that a lame man walked: that anyone preached through Jesus the resurrection from the dead. If Jesus rose, then the men who had Him killed were in the wrong.

Doing something good doesn't make everybody glad. A changed man will meet people who preferred him as he was.

Two days, three scenes

About 3 p.m.healed at the gate (3:1)
That afternoonPeter preaches in the porch (3:12)
Eventidearrested, held overnight (4:3)
The morrowbefore the council (4:5)
Thenlet go (4:21)

Peter and John spent the night locked up. Not long before, Peter had denied Jesus to save himself. Now he sits in a cell for Jesus' name, and nobody in this story panics.

Locked up, and the church still grew

At Pentecost

3,000

“added unto them about three thousand souls” (2:41)

Now

5,000

“The number of the men”, and women and children weren't counted.

The priests arrested the preachers, but they couldn't arrest the word. Howbeit, “however”: many who heard it believed. Two men were in a cell, and thousands more were coming to Christ.

Don't let a hard stretch tell you God has stopped working. It may be His busiest night.

The council, in a half circle

Rulers, and elders, and scribes: the Sanhedrin, the high court of the Jews, about seventy men with the high priest. Annas had been high priest, and his son-in-law Caiaphas held the office. They'd handed Jesus over.

Later Jewish writings say the court sat in a half circle. Peter and John were “set in the midst.” Weeks earlier, Peter couldn't face a servant girl's question. Now he stood before all of them.

The question, and the answer

The councilBy what power, or by what name, have ye done this? they expected a trick

Peter, filled with the Holy GhostBe it known unto you all… that by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom ye crucified, whom God raised from the dead, even by him doth this man stand here before you whole.

Peter turns the trial around. “Examined of the good deed.” He was being questioned for helping a cripple, and he gets to say who did it.

Peter was “filled with the Holy Ghost” for this moment. He already had the Spirit living in him from Pentecost. This was a fresh filling for the task, and we'll see the same thing again in verse 31.

The stone they threw out

The head of the corner is the cornerstone, the first stone laid, which sets the line for the whole wall. The council were the builders, and they'd looked at Jesus and thrown Him out. God took the rejected stone and made it the one the whole building rests on.

“The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner.”

Psalm 118:22

One door, open to everyone

“Neither is there salvation in any other.”

Narrow in one way: only one name saves. Jesus said the same:

“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6

And wide open in another way: “among men.” Any man. Not the right family, the clean record, or the right church. Nothing you did last night puts you out of reach of that name.

Part two

We cannot but speak

Verses 13–22

The court can't argue with the man standing there healed, so it tries to order two fishermen to be quiet.

What they saw, and what they saw

What the council noticed

“Unlearned and ignorant”

The Greek words mean no formal schooling in the rabbis' schools, and no standing: ordinary working men. Fishermen from Galilee.

What they worked out

“They had been with Jesus”

These two had sat at His feet. It showed in how they spoke and how they stood: plain, steady, and unafraid.

Nobody's resume got Peter in front of that court. He was a man who'd failed badly, and the court could see he had no polish. What he had was time with Jesus.

You don't need a clean past or a good education to be used by God. People should be able to say about you, too: he's been with Jesus.

They couldn't deny it, so they tried to hush it

The council says“A notable miracle… we cannot deny it.”
→
so they decide“Let us straitly threaten them… speak henceforth to no man in this name.”

They admit the facts in private: a notable miracle, plain to everyone in Jerusalem. The healed man was standing right there, and they could say nothing against it.

And the answer isn't “maybe we're wrong.” It's “let it spread no further.” A man can see the evidence and still decide to shut his eyes. That's what a hard heart does.

“Judge ye”

The council ordered

“Speak no more”

The highest authority they knew told them to be quiet about Jesus.

Peter and John answered

“We cannot but speak”

They'd seen Him alive and heard Him teach. Being quiet about it wasn't a choice they could make.

Notice how they say it. No shouting and no rebellion. They put the question to the court itself: “judge ye.” When a human order and God's command collide, God comes first, and you can say so respectfully.

Today, at work or at home, you may be told to keep your faith to yourself. Be a good worker and a respectful man, and still don't deny what you've seen Jesus do.

Over forty years lame

over forty years, lame from the womb

Luke tells us his age so we feel how long it had been: lame from birth, and carried to that gate day after day. The council couldn't punish Peter and John because all men glorified God.

If you've been stuck in something for ten, twenty, or forty years, God is not out of time. That man stood up in a day.

Part three

Lord, behold their threatenings

Verses 23–31

The first thing they do when they're let go is go to their friends and pray.

How they prayed

  1. 1. They went to their own peopleThey didn't carry it alone. They went straight back to the believers.
  2. 2. They started with who God is“Lord, thou art God, which hast made heaven, and earth.” Nobody on that council made anything.
  3. 3. They remembered the WordThey found their situation in Psalm 2, and God had said it all before.
  4. 4. Then they askedOne short request, in verses 29–30.

When you're threatened or afraid, don't start by telling God how big the problem is. Start with how big He is. Then the problem looks the size it really is.

Psalm 2, found in their own day

David wrote, Psalm 2:1–2

“Why do the heathen rage, and the people imagine a vain thing? The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against the LORD, and against his anointed,”

They prayed, Acts 4:26–27

The kings of the earth stood up, and the rulers were gathered together against the Lord, and against his Christ. For of a truth against thy holy child Jesus… both Herod, and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles, and the people of Israel, were gathered together,

Everybody was in on the cross: a king (Herod), a governor (Pilate), the Gentiles and Israel's own people. They believed God's Word described what had just happened.

Verse 28 says God's “hand and counsel” had already planned it. The men still did it by their own choice, and Peter held them responsible (3:15). God took the worst thing men ever did and turned it into the way of salvation.

What they didn't ask for

  • “make them stop”they didn't ask for the council to be silenced
  • “keep us safe”no request for an easier road
  • “pay them back”not one word of revenge
  • “grant… all boldness”strength to keep speaking, whatever it cost

They said “behold their threatenings,” like putting a letter on God's desk, then asked for courage, not escape.

Praying for strength in the hard place, instead of an exit out of it, is a prayer a man in recovery can use every day.

Indwelt once, filled again

At salvation

The Spirit comes to stay

Every believer is sealed the moment he trusts Jesus. He lives in you for good. You don't need a second blessing to have Him.

Again and again

“Filled” for the task

These people already had the Spirit from Pentecost. This was a fresh filling: His power to speak boldly right then. It's meant for any believer who asks.

“In whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

The shaking of the room was an answer to their prayer, a sign that God had heard them. It isn't something every meeting should expect. What every believer can count on is that the Spirit is already in him, ready to give strength for the day.

Part four

One heart and one soul

Verses 32–37

Believers who'd been strangers a few weeks ago start looking after each other.

Nobody went without

A believer with land or a housesold itthe apostles“laid them down at the apostles' feet”a brother in need

This was a gift, never a rule. Nobody was forced to sell, and nobody said what he had wasn't his. They chose to treat what they owned as there for the family. The poor and the visitors in Jerusalem were looked after.

What held it together was “one heart and one soul.” Men who'd been strangers or enemies became a family. If you've been alone in your addiction for years, that's a gift: a table where you're expected.

And the apostles witnessed to the resurrection “with great power,” while “great grace was upon them all.” The love and the preaching went together.

A man named “Encourager”

Joses → Barnabas “Son of consolation”: someone who comes alongside and builds you up.

The apostles gave him the nickname for the kind of man he was. He was a Levite, from the tribe that served in the temple, and of the country of Cyprus. Later he stands up for Saul when the believers feared him, and gives John Mark a second chance after Mark quit. He backs men others have written off.

Be a Barnabas today. Think of one brother who's struggling, and send him one line of encouragement.

From Cyprus to Jerusalem

Barnabas came from Cyprus, a big island off the coast of Syria. A lot of Jews had settled there. He was part of the Jerusalem church by choice, far from where he grew up.

Later, he'll sail back to that island with Saul on the first missionary trip (Acts 13). The gospel started in Jerusalem, as Jesus said, and then it began to move out along roads like this one.

Points to remember

  1. Opposition“Preached through Jesus the resurrection from the dead” 4:2A changed life and a risen Christ bother people who'd rather things stayed the same.
  2. Only Jesus“None other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved” 4:12One name saves, and it's offered to every man.
  3. Boldness“They had been with Jesus” 4:13No schooling, no polish, just time with Jesus.
  4. Obey God“We cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard” 4:20Be respectful, and still put God first.
  5. Prayer“Grant unto thy servants, that with all boldness they may speak thy word” 4:29Ask for courage in the hard place, not an escape from it.
  6. Together“Of one heart and of one soul” 4:32You don't have to carry it alone. Be a Barnabas for somebody.

Question 1 of 6

Opposition

Why were the priests and Sadducees so upset with Peter and John?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They were grieved that the apostles taught the people and preached the resurrection of the dead through Jesus. The Sadducees didn't believe in a resurrection, and if Jesus rose, the men who killed Him were in the wrong.

Question 2 of 6

Only Jesus

What did Peter say about who can save us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Only Jesus. There's no salvation in anyone else, and no other name under heaven by which we must be saved. That one name is offered to every man.

Question 3 of 6

Boldness

Why did the council marvel at Peter and John?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They saw boldness in two men they could tell were unlearned and ignorant: ordinary working men with no schooling. They worked out what explained it: they had been with Jesus. And the healed man was standing right there.

Question 4 of 6

Obey God

When the council said to stop speaking, what did Peter and John answer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They put it to the court: is it right to listen to men more than to God? And they said they could not keep quiet about what they'd seen and heard.

Question 5 of 6

Prayer

What did the believers ask for when they were threatened?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Boldness. They started with who God is, laid the threats before Him, and asked for courage to keep speaking His word, not for safety or revenge. God answered, and they spoke with boldness.

Question 6 of 6

Together

How did the believers treat one another?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As one family: of one heart and soul, gladly sharing so that none among them lacked. Barnabas, the “son of consolation,” sold his land and gave it.
