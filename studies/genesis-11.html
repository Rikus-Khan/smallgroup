Chapter 10 listed the nations that came from Noah's three sons. Chapter 11 goes back and tells how they came to be spread over the earth, speaking different languages.

Then it narrows down from all mankind to one family line, and from that line to one man. Everything in the rest of the Bible flows through him.

Part one

Let us build

Verses 1–4

One people, one language, one plan, and God left out of it.

The land of Shinar

The ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat (8:4). Over the years, Noah's descendants moved down into the wide, flat plain between the Tigris and the Euphrates. The Bible calls it Shinar. Later it was called Babylonia; today it is southern Iraq.

Rich soil, plenty of water, room to spread out. And everyone still spoke one language.

But God had given them a job when they came off the ark: “Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth” (9:1). Fill the earth. In Shinar they stopped, and dug in.

Brick for stone

  • Go toCome on! Let's go!
  • throughlythoroughly, all the way through
  • slimetar, the black pitch (bitumen) that seeps from the ground there
  • mortermortar, what holds the bricks together

Shinar is river mud, with hardly a stone in it. So they invented their own: bricks baked hard in a kiln, glued with tar. New know-how, and they meant to use it for something big.

What they were really building

  • a citysafety in numbers, walls of their own
  • a tower, whose top may reach unto heavenup to God, on their own terms
  • let us make us a nameglory, for themselves
  • lest we be scatteredfear, and a flat “no” to God's “fill the earth”

Count the words: “let us… build us… make us a name.” God isn't in their plan at all. Building isn't the sin. A city isn't the sin. The sin is a life built around us: our name, our safety, our way up, and God told to stay out.

A man can build that kind of tower out of anything: a reputation, money, a tough-guy image, a bottle he keeps within reach so he'll never feel scattered. It looks strong. It's mud and tar.

Part two

The LORD came down

Verses 5–9

They built up to reach him. He had to come down to see it.

Come down to see it

They meant it to reach heaven. God, in effect, has to go down to see it. Not because he couldn't see: Moses says it this way to show how tiny man's greatest work is next to him.

That is the gospel in a picture: no man can build his way up to God, so God comes down.

“And no man hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven, even the Son of man which is in heaven.”

John 3:13

A brake, not a tantrum

“Restrained” means held back. God isn't worried about bricks. He knows the heart of man: “every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually” (6:5).

Ifall mankind is one crew, of one mind, set against God,

thennothing will hold back the evil they imagine.

So God breaks up the crew. That's mercy. Some of us know it from the inside: the old running buddies, the house where it all went down, the friends who were never going to let us change. Sometimes God scatters that crew on purpose, and it feels like loss. It is rescue.

Their “let us,” and God's

Men↑“Go to, let us build us a city and a tower”going up, to make a name (verse 4)
God↓“Go to, let us go down”coming down, to stop it (verse 7)

Moses uses their own words back at them. They said “let us” three times. God answers in the very same words, and his one “let us” outweighs all of theirs.

Notice God says “us.” He spoke the same way when he made man: “Let us make man in our image” (1:26). One God, and yet he speaks as more than one. The New Testament shows us why: the Father, the Son and the Holy Ghost.

Babel: a gate, or a mess?

בָּבֶל said Bavel; it sounds like balal, “to mix up” The people of that land later said the name meant “gate of god.” Moses says it means confusion.
  • confoundconfuse, mix up, throw into disorder
  • left offstopped, quit
  • buildedbuilt

The tower was never finished. The city became Babylon, and all through the Bible Babylon stands for man's proud world without God, right up to Revelation 18. Every proud tower ends the same way: they left off to build.

Babel undone

Babel is not God's last word on languages. Fifty days after Jesus rose, God came down again: the Holy Ghost, on the day of Pentecost.

Part three

The generations of Shem

Verses 10–26

The nations scatter. God keeps his eye on one family.

Ten names, one road

“Generations” means family history. “Begat” means became the father of. Chapter 5 gave ten generations from Adam to Noah. Now ten more, from Shem to Abram.

Luke 3:34

Eber and Peleg

פֶּלֶג said Peleg “division”

“And unto Eber were born two sons: the name of one was Peleg; for in his days was the earth divided; and his brother’s name was Joktan.”

Genesis 10:25

Many Bible teachers think Peleg's name remembers Babel: in his lifetime mankind was split up by language and spread over the earth. And from Eber, many think, comes the word Hebrew (14:13). These are real men, and the names carry real history.

How long they lived

Before the flood, men lived nine hundred years. After it, the numbers fall: Shem 600, then 430 to 460, then about 240 from Peleg on, and Nahor only 148. By Abraham, 175. Death, the wages of sin, is closing in (Romans 6:23).

Add up the ages and something surprising shows: Shem, who walked off the ark, was still alive when Abram set out for Canaan.

Why a list of names?

Abram is named first most likely because he matters most, not because he was the oldest. Terah was 70 when his sons began to come. Putting 11:32, 12:4 and Acts 7:4 together, Abram was most likely born when Terah was about 130.

Most of these men did nothing the Bible records except live, have children and die. Yet God wrote every one of their names down, and through them he kept his promise moving toward Christ.

God doesn't save “people” in a crowd. He knows men by name. Yours too.

“But now thus saith the LORD that created thee, O Jacob, and he that formed thee, O Israel, Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.”

Isaiah 43:1

Part four

The generations of Terah

Verses 27–32

A grieving father, a fatherless boy, a barren wife, and a trip that stops halfway.

Milcah appears twice: Haran's daughter married her uncle Nahor. Marrying within the family was common then. And Sarai was Abram's half-sister, as he says later (20:12).

A father buries his son

  • nativitybirth; “the land of his nativity” is the land where he was born

Haran died before his father, in his father's lifetime. No cause is given. Only the grief, in a few plain words.

Some of you know that grief, or caused some of it. The Bible doesn't skip over it, and God doesn't either. He sees the father at the graveside.

And look who is left: Lot, a boy without a father. His grandfather takes him in (verse 31), and then his uncle Abram (12:4). Somebody in your life is a Lot. You don't have to be perfect to be the man who takes him along.

But Sarai was barren; she had no child.

“Barren” means unable to have children. Moses says it twice, so nobody misses it. In that world a woman's worth was counted in sons, and a family line with no child was a dead end.

Ten generations of “and begat… and begat…” run straight into a wall. The man God is about to choose has no son, and his wife can't have one.

That is exactly where God likes to begin. He will promise Abram a great nation (12:2), and twenty-five years later he keeps it:

“For Sarah conceived, and bare Abraham a son in his old age, at the set time of which God had spoken to him.”

Genesis 21:2

Halfway

Terah set out from Ur for Canaan. About six hundred miles up the river he came to Haran, settled down, and never went on. He died there.

Stephen tells us God had already called Abram back in Ur. After his father died, Abram finished the trip:

“Then came he out of the land of the Chaldaeans, and dwelt in Charran: and from thence, when his father was dead, he removed him into this land, wherein ye now dwell.”

Acts 7:4

Don't settle in Haran

Haran was a comfortable place to stop: a big trading town, out of Ur, far enough to feel like progress. Many men in recovery know Haran. Out of the worst of it, sober a while, but stopped short of where God is taking them.

Terah's family hadn't been following God at all, back in Ur. Joshua says so plainly:

“And Joshua said unto all the people, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Your fathers dwelt on the other side of the flood in old time, even Terah, the father of Abraham, and the father of Nachor: and they served other gods.”

Joshua 24:2

(“The flood” there means the river Euphrates.) God called Abram out of an idol-worshipping home. Where you came from does not decide where God can take you. So don't stop halfway. Take the next step today.

Two ways to get a name

Babel

“…let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.”

Genesis 11:4

Abram

“And I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing:”

Genesis 12:2

The builders grabbed for a name and lost it: nobody knows who they were. Abram had nothing, no son, no land, a father's house full of idols, and God gave him a name the whole world knows.

You can spend your life making a name for yourself, or you can let God give you one. In Christ he already has: a son of God, forgiven, his.

Question 1 of 6

Pride

What were the builders really after?

What verses tell us about this?

“Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.”

Proverbs 16:18

“But he giveth more grace. Wherefore he saith, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.”

James 4:6

Answer

A name for themselves, safety on their own terms, and a way up to heaven without bowing to God. It was pride, and fear underneath it: “lest we be scattered.” God had told them to fill the earth, and they dug in instead. The same pride lives in us. God resists it, but he gives grace to the man who humbles himself.

Question 2 of 6

Who God is

Can a man build his own way up to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“And no man hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven, even the Son of man which is in heaven.”

John 3:13

“Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it: except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain.”

Psalm 127:1

Answer

No. Babel's best was so small that God “came down to see” it. Religion, good works, cleaning yourself up: none of it reaches heaven. The only way is the One who came down: Jesus, the Son of God, who died for our sins and rose again. We don't climb up to him. We receive him by faith.

Question 3 of 6

Mercy

Why did God scatter them?

What verses tell us about this?

“And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually.”

Genesis 6:5

Answer

To hold back evil, and to get done what he had told them to do. A whole world united against God, with nothing to slow it down, would have run to ruin as fast as the world before the flood. Scattering them was judgment, and it was mercy. Sometimes God breaks up our plans and our crew for the same reason.

Question 4 of 6

Names

Why does God keep a list of names?

What verses tell us about this?

“Which was the son of Jacob, which was the son of Isaac, which was the son of Abraham, which was the son of Thara, which was the son of Nachor,”

Luke 3:34

“But now thus saith the LORD that created thee, O Jacob, and he that formed thee, O Israel, Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.”

Isaiah 43:1

Answer

Because these are real men in real history, and through them God was keeping his promise of a Saviour, all the way from Shem to Abram, and on to Jesus. And because God knows people by name. You are not a face in a crowd to him. He calls you by your name.

Question 5 of 6

Regret

What does it cost to stop halfway?

What verses tell us about this?

“Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before, I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 3:13–14

Answer

Terah set out for Canaan and died in Haran, short of it. A man can leave the worst of his old life and still settle somewhere comfortable, short of what God has for him. The cure for regret isn't staring back at Ur. It's the next step forward, today, with your brothers beside you.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

What does God do with a dead end?

What verses tell us about this?

“For Sarah conceived, and bare Abraham a son in his old age, at the set time of which God had spoken to him.”

Genesis 21:2

“Through faith also Sara herself received strength to conceive seed, and was delivered of a child when she was past age, because she judged him faithful who had promised.”

Hebrews 11:11

Answer

He starts there. The chapter ends with a barren wife and a dead father, and the very next chapter opens with God's promise. Whatever looks finished in your life, a marriage, a family, your own name, is not finished to him. He keeps his word, at his set time.
