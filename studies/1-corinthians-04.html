The Corinthians had picked favorite teachers: “I am of Paul; and I of Apollos.” In chapter 3 Paul said God does the growing. Now he gets personal: how a servant of God should be seen, how proud they've become, and what he wants for them.

Read this chapter alone and it still stands: be faithful, leave the final verdict to the Lord, stay humble, and find a father in the faith.

Part one

Stewards, and the Judge

Verses 1–5

You were handed something that isn't yours. Be faithful with it, and let the Lord have the last word on how you did.

A steward holds another man's keys

A steward ran a rich man's household: the money, the food, the staff. He owned none of it. His whole job was to handle another man's goods the way the owner would want. The mysteries of God are the truths once hidden and now shown in Christ: the gospel.

What is required

  • talentedGod never asked for a gifted steward
  • successful, or well knownnobody gets graded on the size of the crowd
  • never failedthe faithful man gets up again
  • found faithfulthere, doing the job, and doing it straight

That is a standard a man with a rough past can meet by tomorrow morning. Show up. Do what you said. Be the same man when nobody is checking.

“He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much: and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much.”

Luke 16:10

Three courts

Other people“judged of you, or of man's judgment”a very small thing
My own conscience“I know nothing by myself”clear, yet “not hereby justified”
The Lord“he that judgeth me is the Lord”the verdict that counts

Paul isn't saying nobody can ever say a hard word to him. He is saying that other men's opinion of him isn't the final word. For a man trying to live down his record, that is freedom: the people who wrote you off don't get the last say.

He doesn't trust his own verdict either. A clear conscience feels good, but I know nothing by myself; yet am I not hereby justified. A man can be wrong about himself in both directions. Only the Lord sees all of it.

Wait for the One who sees it all

nowHidden

Motives, secrets, what's really in a man's heart. We can't see it in each other.

when the Lord comesBrought to light

The hidden things of darkness and the counsels of the hearts come out. Nothing is lost on Him.

thenPraise of God

Then shall every man have praise of God. Every man who belongs to Him, for whatever was done right.

Judge nothing doesn't mean call wrong right. It means don't pass the final verdict on a man's heart; that isn't your job. And don't carry your own secrets in the dark waiting for judgment day. Bring them out now.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Part two

Puffed up

Verses 6–13

The Corinthians thought they had arrived. The men who brought them the gospel were living on the edge of ruin.

Everything was a gift

“Your mind, your skill, your strength?”receivedYou didn't pick the head you were born with, or the people who taught you a trade.
“Your second chance?”receivedNobody earned it. Someone gave it, and so did God.
“Your days clean?”receivedYou did the work of getting up each day, and God held you up while you did it. Say thank you before you say “look at me.”

Puffed up is like a balloon: full of air, no weight. Pride says I made myself. The truth cuts two ways. You can't boast, because it was all given. And you can't be crushed by what you've lost, because what's been given can be given again.

Paul turns up the sarcasm

the Corinthians, in their own eyesthe apostles, in plain fact full, rich, reigning like kingslast in line, appointed to death wise in Christfools for Christ's sake strongweak honourabledespised

He is being sharp on purpose. They talked like men who had already won. I would to God ye did reign, he says: if you really had, we could share it. But the people who brought you Jesus are still on the road, hungry.

Notice what he does not do. He isn't bitter about it, and he doesn't ask them to pity him. He wants them back down on the ground with the rest of us.

A spectacle

Spectacle is the Greek word for the theatre: a show with the whole crowd watching. Paul seems to mean the parade in the Roman arena, where men appointed to death came out last, to be looked at. That's how he describes serving Jesus: a life exposed, in front of everybody, and not a glamorous one.

What the world called them

the offscouring of all things → a son of God Filth and offscouring are what you scrape off a dirty dish. Paul says the world treated him like that: beaten, hungry, no certain home, and still working with his own hands. If you've ever been treated like garbage, because of your record or your last name, he has been where you are. And God's name for you is not the world's.

“Behold, what manner of love the Father hath bestowed upon us, that we should be called the sons of God: therefore the world knoweth us not, because it knew him not.”

1 John 3:1

And watch how he answers the abuse: being reviled, we bless; being persecuted, we suffer it; being defamed, we intreat. Not payback. That's meat, not milk.

Part three

Fathers

Verses 14–21

A church can have all the teachers in the world and still be short of one thing: a father.

Ten thousand instructers, few fathers

Instructers is the Greek paidagogos: the household slave who walked a boy to school and kept him in line. A needed job, but it was discipline and delivery, not fathering. Teachers can fill your head. A father is the one who stays.

If you never had one

A lot of men in this room never had a dad who stayed, or taught them how to be a man. Paul says a spiritual father can be that, and that he was one: in Christ Jesus I have begotten you through the gospel. He brought them to Jesus, and he didn't leave.

An instructor gives

Information

What to read, what to avoid, what the verse means.

A father gives

Himself

His time, his phone number, his example. Correction without shame: I write not these things to shame you, but as my beloved sons I warn you.

“As ye know how we exhorted and comforted and charged every one of you, as a father doth his children,”

1 Thessalonians 2:11

Jesus said “call no man your father upon the earth” (Matthew 23:9). That's about grabbing a religious title for status. Paul's meaning is the opposite: a man who earns the word by walking with you.

Timothy: a son sent ahead

Paul was writing from Ephesus. He had already sent Timotheus ahead by way of Macedonia (Acts 19:22), and Timothy would remind them of Paul's ways.

“But ye know the proof of him, that, as a son with the father, he hath served with me in the gospel.”

Philippians 2:22

Timothy had a father in the faith, and he became one himself. That's how the chain works. Be ye followers of me means: watch how I live and copy it, as I copy Christ.

Find one. Then be one.

  1. LookWho is a few steps ahead of me?a man who's still faithful
  2. AskWalk with me, and I'll be honestsay the true thing
  3. Turn aroundHelp the man behind youTimothy did

Don't wait until you feel ready. A man a year clean is a father to the man who's a week clean. The church is full of men who needed this and never asked. Ask today.

“Unto Timothy, my own son in the faith: Grace, mercy, and peace, from God our Father and Jesus Christ our Lord.”

1 Timothy 1:2

Talk, or power?

The puffed-up

Word

The speech of them which are puffed up: big claims, big plans, big talk, and a life that hasn't moved.

The kingdom

Power

What God actually does in a man: a temper that cools, a hand that stops, a husband who stays. It shows up on an ordinary Tuesday.

“For our gospel came not unto you in word only, but also in power, and in the Holy Ghost, and in much assurance; as ye know what manner of men we were among you for your sake.”

1 Thessalonians 1:5

Paul will judge them by the power, not the speeches. It's a good rule for a man to use on himself. Talking a good recovery isn't the same as living one. Don't be impressed by words, yours or anyone's. Look at the life.

“What will ye?”

One choice

A rod

A father's discipline. If they stay puffed up and divided, Paul will come and deal with it plainly.

The other

Love, and meekness

In love, and in the spirit of meekness. That is what he wants. The choice is theirs.

Notice who gets to pick: what will ye? A good father doesn't enjoy the rod. He lays it down the moment he can. God works with us the same way, and He does it as a Father, not a judge.

Points to remember

  1. Stewards“A man be found faithful” 4:2We handle what belongs to God. He doesn't ask for talent or a clean record, only faithfulness.
  2. Judging“Judge nothing before the time” 4:5The Lord sees the heart and gives the last verdict. Don't carry others' opinions, or your own secrets, in the dark.
  3. Pride“What hast thou that thou didst not receive?” 4:7Everything we have is a gift. There's no room to boast, and no reason to despair.
  4. The apostles“The filth of the world… the offscouring of all things” 4:13The world treated them like garbage. They answered with a blessing, and God called them His.
  5. Fathers“Ten thousand instructers… not many fathers” 4:15Teachers give information; a father gives himself. Find one, and become one.
  6. Power“The kingdom of God is not in word, but in power” 4:20Look for the changed life, not the big talk.

Question 1 of 6

Stewards

What is required in a steward?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much: and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much.”

Luke 16:10

Answer

That he be found faithful. A steward handles what belongs to another, so the question is whether he does it the way the owner wants. Not how gifted he is, but how faithful.

Question 2 of 6

Judging

Why should we judge nothing before the time?

What verses tell us about this?

“So then every one of us shall give account of himself to God.”

Romans 14:12

Answer

Because the Lord is the judge, and only He sees the hidden things and the counsels of the heart. When He comes, He will bring it all to light, and every man who is His will have praise from God.

Question 3 of 6

Pride

What do you have that you did not receive?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Nothing. Everything we have was given to us, so there's nothing to boast about, and no reason to be puffed up against another man.

Question 4 of 6

The apostles

How did the world treat the apostles?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Like a spectacle, and like garbage: hungry, beaten, with no certain home. And they answered with blessing. In Christ they were still God's beloved sons.

Question 5 of 6

Fathers

What did the Corinthians lack, though they had ten thousand instructers?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Fathers. Plenty of people could teach them. Paul had begotten them in Christ through the gospel, and he stayed. A spiritual father walks with you, warns you out of love, and shows you how to live.

Question 6 of 6

Power

Where is the kingdom of God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not in word, but in power. The proof of God's kingdom in a man is not his talk but his changed life.
