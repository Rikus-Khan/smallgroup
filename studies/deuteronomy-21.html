Deuteronomy 21

Innocent blood, the son of the hated wife, and the man hanged on a tree

A man is found slain and no one knows who did it. A captive woman is not to be sold. The firstborn keeps his right, even if his mother is the wife his father loves less. A stubborn and rebellious son is brought to the gate. And a man hanged on a tree must be buried that day, “for he that is hanged is accursed of God”.

Part one

“Our hands have not shed this blood”

Verses 1–9

A body in a field, and no one to blame.

Nobody's fault is still somebody's business

  1. 1Foundin the field
  2. 2Measureto the nearest city
  3. 3A heifernever yoked
  4. 4A valleynever ploughed
  5. 5Washtheir hands
  6. 6Pray“Be merciful”

No one could shrug and walk on. The judges measured, and the city nearest the body had to answer for it. Eared is an old word for ploughed: the valley was ground no man had worked, and the heifer was one that “hath not been wrought with”. A stroke is a blow, a case of injury, as in 17:8.

A life was taken, and God would not let it be forgotten as if it did not matter. Every man is made in his image (Genesis 9:6).

Clean hands still ask for mercy

The elders were innocent, and they said so. But they did not stop there. They prayed for the whole people: “Be merciful, O LORD… and lay not innocent blood unto thy people”. And God answered: “the blood shall be forgiven them.”

“When Pilate saw that he could prevail nothing, but that rather a tumult was made, he took water, and washed his hands before the multitude, saying, I am innocent of the blood of this just person: see ye to it.”

Matthew 27:24

Pilate washed his hands too, and said much the same words. But Pilate had the power to set Jesus free, and he gave him up. Water on the hands does not make a guilty man clean. Only one thing does:

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Part two

“Thou shalt not sell her at all”

Verses 10–17

God puts a fence around the one with no power.

A wife, not a thing

The chapter before had said the women of a city “very far off” could be taken in war (20:14–15). A captive had no one to speak for her. So God spoke for her. Pare means to trim; bewail means to weep aloud over. Humbled her means he had brought her low by taking her and then putting her away.

  • Time to grieve“a full month” to mourn her father and her mother, in his house, before she became his wife.
  • A wife“she shall be thy wife”, with a wife's place.
  • Free to goIf he had no delight in her, “thou shalt let her go whither she will”.
  • Never sold“thou shalt not sell her at all for money, thou shalt not make merchandise of her.”

A man's desire is strong, and in war it has a sword in its hand. God did not let it run loose. He made the man wait a month, and he forbade him to treat her like goods. The weak are never beneath God's notice.

“Which is indeed the firstborn”

Hated here means loved less. The Bible says it of Leah: Jacob “loved also Rachel more than Leah”, and the next verse calls Leah “hated” (Genesis 29:30–31). A man's feelings were not allowed to rob a son of what was his.

The firstborn“the beginning of his strength”
Each brotherthe other sons

“And when the LORD saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb: but Rachel was barren.”

Genesis 29:31

From the beginning God made one man and one woman (Matthew 19:4–5). This law shows the hurt in a home where that was broken. And it shows God: he sees the one who is loved less, and he takes up her son's cause.

Part three

“He that is hanged is accursed of God”

Verses 18–23

The rebellious son, and the man on the tree.

“A glutton, and a drunkard”

This is not a child who talks back. It is a son who is grown enough to be a drunkard, who has been chastened (disciplined) and still “will not obey”. His father and mother did not punish him themselves: they brought him to the elders at the gate, and the elders judged. It was the law for Israel's courts under God, and it was to make “all Israel… hear, and fear.”

Look at what the charge was: “a glutton, and a drunkard.” God set drink among the things that ruin a son and break a home.

“Be not among winebibbers; among riotous eaters of flesh: For the drunkard and the glutton shall come to poverty: and drowsiness shall clothe a man with rags.”

Proverbs 23:20–21

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

If God has set you free from the bottle, nothing in this chapter, or anywhere in the Book, is a reason to pick it back up.

The son who came home

Most of us know this son. Many of us were him. The law shows how seriously God takes it: rebellion and drink are not small things. But Jesus told of another son, who “wasted his substance with riotous living” (Luke 15:13), and what his father did when he turned for home:

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

“The Son of man came eating and drinking, and they say, Behold a man gluttonous, and a winebibber, a friend of publicans and sinners. But wisdom is justified of her children.”

Matthew 11:19

And notice: Jesus was the Son who did always those things that pleased his Father (John 8:29). Yet his enemies called him almost what this rebellious son was called. It was a lie; but he did not stay away from the publicans and sinners, and on the tree he took the rebel's curse himself (Galatians 3:13).

Down before night

A man put to death for his sin might be hanged on a tree after, for all to see. But he was not to stay there. He was to come down and be buried “that day”. In any wise means by all means.

“The Jews therefore, because it was the preparation, that the bodies should not remain upon the cross on the sabbath day, (for that sabbath day was an high day,) besought Pilate that their legs might be broken, and that they might be taken away.”

John 19:31

Long after Moses wrote it, the same care was kept at Golgotha. Jesus was taken down from the tree and laid in the tomb the same day. The men who asked for it did not know whose body they were keeping the law for.

Made a curse for us

Under the curseof the law

Freeredeemed

Us“a sin worthy of death”
Christwho did no sin

Then they trade places. The law said the man on the tree was accursed of God. Christ went to the tree, and took our curse there.

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

“Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed.”

1 Peter 2:24

Real world: no one left on the tree

  • SinBlood on the ground, a woman used and sold, a son robbed of his right, a son who would not hear and drank: the chapter names it plainly.
  • RegretSome of us were the drunkard son, and our parents wept over us. That is real, and God takes it seriously.
  • Grace“Be merciful, O LORD… And the blood shall be forgiven them.” Christ was “made a curse for us” (Galatians 3:13).
  • HopeThe father runs to the son who turns for home (Luke 15:20). Your story is not ended by what you were.
  • TodayDon't hide from what is yours to answer for, and don't stand by when someone is wronged. If you have parents you hurt, ask God whether today is the day to call them. And if the drink is calling you, call or text your brothers in Christ before you pick it up.

Points to remember

  1. The unknown slain“Our hands have not shed this blood, neither have our eyes seen it. Be merciful, O LORD” 21:7–8The nearest city answered for it, and asked God for mercy.
  2. The captive“thou shalt not sell her at all for money, thou shalt not make merchandise of her” 21:14God speaks for the one with no one to speak for her.
  3. The firstborn“he shall acknowledge the son of the hated for the firstborn, by giving him a double portion” 21:17A father's favour could not take away a son's right.
  4. The rebellious son“This our son is stubborn and rebellious, he will not obey our voice; he is a glutton, and a drunkard.” 21:20God takes rebellion and drink seriously, and the father still runs to the son who comes home.
  5. The tree“His body shall not remain all night upon the tree, but thou shalt in any wise bury him that day” 21:23Jesus was taken down from the tree and buried that same day (John 19:31, 42).
  6. The curse“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us” Galatians 3:13He took the curse of the tree, so we go free.

Question 1 of 6

The unknown slain

What did the elders of the nearest city do when a man was found slain and no one knew who killed him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They brought a heifer that had never been yoked down to a rough valley, washed their hands over it, said their hands had not shed this blood, and prayed the LORD to be merciful to his people.

Question 2 of 6

The captive

What was a man not allowed to do with a captive woman he had taken to wife?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He was not to sell her for money or make merchandise of her. She had a full month to mourn first, and if he had no delight in her he had to let her go free.

Question 3 of 6

The firstborn

Could a father give the firstborn's portion to the son of the wife he loved?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. The son of the hated wife, if he was the firstborn, was to have the double portion. The right of the firstborn was his.

Question 4 of 6

The rebellious son

What did the father and mother say of their stubborn and rebellious son?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

Answer

“This our son is stubborn and rebellious, he will not obey our voice; he is a glutton, and a drunkard.” The law took it that seriously; and Jesus shows the Father running to the son who comes home.

Question 5 of 6

The tree

Why was a hanged man's body not to remain all night upon the tree?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Jews therefore, because it was the preparation, that the bodies should not remain upon the cross on the sabbath day, (for that sabbath day was an high day,) besought Pilate that their legs might be broken, and that they might be taken away.”

John 19:31

Answer

Because “he that is hanged is accursed of God”, and the land was not to be defiled. He was to be buried that day, and at Golgotha the bodies were taken down before the sabbath (John 19:31).

Question 6 of 6

The curse

How does the New Testament use the words “he that is hanged is accursed of God”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

“Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed.”

1 Peter 2:24

Answer

Paul quotes it to show that Christ, hanged on the tree, was made a curse for us, to redeem us from the curse of the law. He bare our sins in his own body on the tree.
