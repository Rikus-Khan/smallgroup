Titus 3

Saved by mercy, sent to do good

The most honest look-back in the letter: this is who we were, and this is what God did. A man who remembers that can be kind to anyone, and doesn't have to win every argument.

Part one

How to live among people

Verses 1–2

Paul says what a changed man looks like on the street: law, work and tongue.

Obey, and be ready

Magistrates are the people with authority: the law, the courts, the boss, the officer you check in with. The rulers Paul meant were Roman, and not friendly to Christians. He still says be subject, and be “ready to every good work.”

“Let every soul be subject unto the higher powers. For there is no power but of God: the powers that be are ordained of God.”

Romans 13:1

For men with a record, this is as practical as it gets: the probation appointment, the court date, the fine, the curfew, the tax form. Show up, and be on time. Believers have always held that if a ruler orders you to deny Jesus, God comes first. Short of that, a man who broke rules his whole life learns to keep them as part of following Him.

Ready to every good work is the other half: not just staying out of trouble, but being the guy who helps carry the couch.

Watch the mouth, and the fists

  • speak evil of no manno cutting people down behind their backs
  • brawlersa man who is always ready for a fight
  • gentle, with all meeknessstrength under control, toward all men

Meekness doesn't mean weak. It is power held in check, like a strong horse that's been trained. And the verse says “unto all men”: the guy who cuts you off, the boss who is unfair, the one who knew you back when. Keep your voice down with every one of them. Paul will give his reason in the next verse.

Part two

Who we were, and what God did

Verses 3–8

The reason for the kindness: we were that man ourselves. And then God did something that none of us earned.

Down the stairs

  1. foolishno sense, no plan
  2. disobedientagainst every rule
  3. deceivedsure it was fine
  4. servinglusts and pleasures, as a slave serves
  5. malice and envybitter and comparing
  6. hatefuland hating one another

Paul writes “we ourselves.” He includes himself. And look at the word “serving”: that is what a slave does. Many of us know what it is to serve a drink, a drug or a craving that never says thank you. “Sometimes” means once, in the past: where we came from, not where we stand.

“But after that”

Then (verse 3)
  • foolish
  • disobedient
  • deceived
  • serving lusts and pleasures
  • malice and envy
  • hateful
But
Now (verses 4 to 7)
  • His kindness and love appeared
  • saved, by His mercy
  • washed, and made new
  • justified by His grace
  • heirs of eternal life

Two little words turn the whole page. “But after that.” Nothing in the first column made God move. He did it because of who He is: kindness (the word means He is good to people who haven't earned it) and love of man. Hold onto that if you are ever tempted to think you are too far gone.

Not by what I've done

  • my good daysthey can't carry the weight
  • my clean timeit's worth keeping, and it is not a payment
  • my promises, my church attendance, my resolutionsnone of them bought it
  • his mercyHe saved us because of who He is

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Mercy is not getting what you deserve. A man on the bad side of the ledger doesn't have to climb out first. He is saved while still on it. That is also why it holds: it never depended on you.

The washing of regeneration

What it is

New birth, from God

Regeneration means born again. Washing is the cleansing that comes with it, and the renewing of the Holy Ghost is the Spirit making a man new inside. God poured the Spirit out “abundantly” on everyone who believes, from the day he believes.

What it is not

Not the water of baptism

Baptism is a believer's step of obedience that pictures this new birth. But the water doesn't do the washing. The Spirit does, when a man trusts Jesus.

“Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you. A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:25-26

Up the stairs

  1. Savedby His mercy
  2. Born againwashed, made new
  3. Given the Spiritpoured out abundantly
  4. Justifieddeclared right with God, by grace
  5. Heirsof the hope of eternal life

Justified is a courtroom word: declared not guilty, with the record cleared. And an heir is someone with a standing claim to what belongs to his Father. You didn't earn the seat, so you can't be thrown out of it. The same hands that saved you are holding it.

First believe, then do good

Having believedthey which have believed in God
→
thencareful to maintain good works.

Look at the order. Paul has just spent five verses saying works can't save you. Now he says “these things I will that thou affirm constantly”: say it again and again, because the same men who believe will want to do good. The works are not the price. They are the fruit.

One thing for today: pay one thing back, fix one thing you broke, or do one thing nobody will thank you for. Not to be saved. Because you are.

Part three

Staying out of the weeds

Verses 9–15

The end of the letter: fights to avoid, a man to watch, and some plain travel plans.

Fights not worth having

  1. Foolish questionsspeculation that goes nowhere
  2. Genealogiesendless family-tree arguments
  3. Contentionsfighting just to win
  4. Strivings about the lawwrangling over rules

The word is “unprofitable and vain.” Paul isn't against honest questions. He's against the ones where the point is the argument. A man who is new in the faith can burn a week on a debate that changes nobody. The question to ask: will this make me, or him, more like Jesus? If not, let it go.

Warned twice, then step back

  1. First warningkindly, to his face
  2. Second warningonce more, with care
  3. Step backstop joining his side

The old word heretick doesn't mean a man with doubts. It means a divider: someone who forms a faction and keeps pulling people his way. Doubters need patience. A man who keeps splitting the group needs two honest warnings, and then the group steps back from him, “condemned of himself” (verse 11) because he chose the division.

Notice the warnings come first, and they are given in the open. Nobody gets dropped on the first offence. Stepping back is not hatred. It is how a group stays safe.

Come to me at Nicopolis

Paul ends with plans, like any letter between friends. Titus is to come to Nicopolis, on the west coast of Greece, where Paul has “determined there to winter.”

He names his team: Artemas or Tychicus to take over, and Zenas the lawyer and Apollos, to be sent on their way so “nothing be wanting unto them.” Paul's work ran on a team, and on friends who supplied travellers. The last order is practical: “let ours also learn to maintain good works for necessary uses.” Find a real need near you and meet it.

Titus on one line

  1. Chapter 1Set the church in ordermen who are growing up; a rough place is not beyond God
  2. Chapter 2Live what you believegrace saves, and grace trains
  3. Chapter 3Remember the mercysaved by mercy, so do good and be kind

A short letter, and one thread through it: God takes rough people, makes them new, and then sends them out to be good for their neighbours. Chapter 1 says it can be done on Crete. Chapter 3 says why it can be done in you.

Points to remember

  1. Citizens“To speak evil of no man, to be no brawlers, but gentle” 3:2Keep the rules, keep your voice down, and be ready to help. Toward everyone.
  2. Before“For we ourselves also were sometimes foolish, disobedient, deceived” 3:3Paul includes himself. We were the man in verse 3, and that is why we can be kind.
  3. Mercy“Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us” 3:5It was His kindness and love. Nothing we did bought it, so nothing we do can lose it.
  4. New birth“the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost” 3:5The new birth, by the Spirit. Baptism pictures it. It doesn't do it.
  5. Heirs“being justified by his grace, we should be made heirs according to the hope of eternal life” 3:7Declared right with God, with a place in the family that can't be taken away.
  6. Careful“A man that is an heretick after the first and second admonition reject” 3:10Skip the pointless fights. A man who keeps splitting the group gets two warnings, then the group steps back.

Question 1 of 6

Citizens

How should a believer treat rulers, bosses and neighbours?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let every soul be subject unto the higher powers. For there is no power but of God: the powers that be are ordained of God.”

Romans 13:1

Answer

Be subject to the authorities, be ready to help, don't run people down, don't be a brawler, and show gentleness to everyone. That covers the courthouse, the job and the street. It is strength under control, not weakness.

Question 2 of 6

Before

What were we before God saved us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Foolish, disobedient, deceived, slaves to our own lusts and pleasures, full of malice and envy, hateful and hating one another. Paul says it plainly and includes himself. It is where we came from, and it is not what God says about us now.

Question 3 of 6

Mercy

Why did God save us, and what did we have to do with it?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

He saved us because of His own kindness, love and mercy. We had nothing to do with earning it: not by works of righteousness which we have done. We receive it by faith. Since He saved us without our good record, nothing in our bad record can take it away.

Question 4 of 6

New birth

What is “the washing of regeneration”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you. A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:25-26

Answer

The new birth. God cleanses a man and makes him new inside by His Holy Spirit, whom He pours out on everyone who believes. It is not the water of baptism: baptism is the believer's step that pictures what the Spirit has done, but it does not do it.

Question 5 of 6

Heirs

What are we now, and what is our hope?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Justified, declared right with God by His grace, and heirs with the hope of eternal life. It is a place in the family that was given and can't be taken away. And because we have believed, we want to be careful to do good, not to earn it but because we've been given it.

Question 6 of 6

Careful

Which fights should we skip, and what do we do with a man who keeps stirring up trouble?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Skip the foolish questions, the genealogy debates and the fights over rules, because they are unprofitable and vain. For a man who keeps dividing the group, warn him a first and second time, and then step back from him. He chose the division.
