Part one

Launch out into the deep

Verses 1–11

A long night, empty nets, and one word from Jesus.

The lake of Gennesaret

It's the Sea of Galilee by another name: a freshwater lake about 13 miles long and 8 wide, sitting about 700 feet below sea level. Fishing towns ringed the shore, Capernaum among them.

The fishermen had worked all night and were washing their nets, finished. Jesus borrowed Simon's boat and sat in it a little off the shore. The water carried His voice to the crowd on the slope.

Simon already knew Jesus. He'd seen his mother-in-law healed (4:38–39). Today Jesus asks for his boat, then for a lot more.

“Nevertheless at thy word”

JesusLaunch out into the deep, and let down your nets for a draught.

SimonMaster, we have toiled all the night, and have taken nothing: nevertheless at thy word I will let down the net.
tired, doubtful, and obedient

Fishermen on that lake fished at night, near the shore. Out in the deep, in broad daylight, every fisherman knew you'd catch nothing. A carpenter's son was telling a professional how to do his job.

Simon didn't feel like it, and he didn't see the point. He did it anyway, because Jesus said so. Faith doesn't wait until the feelings show up.

Some of us have toiled all the night for years, trying to fix our lives in our own strength, and come up with nothing. The turn starts with one word: nevertheless.

all night: nothingone word: more than the nets could hold

All night: nothing. One word from Jesus: so many fish the net tore and two boats nearly sank. Luke doesn't count them. He tells us it was more than they could handle.

Jesus didn't need Simon's skill. He needed Simon's yes.

From “Master” to “Lord”

“Master” → “O Lord” In verse 5 Jesus was the boss on the boat. In verse 8 Simon is on his knees in the fish.

Simon didn't say, “Look what I caught.” He said, “I am a sinful man.” When a man really sees who Jesus is, the first thing he sees is himself. Isaiah said the same when he saw the Lord:

“Then said I, Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips: for mine eyes have seen the King, the LORD of hosts.”

Isaiah 6:5

“Fear not”

Simon said, “Depart from me.” Jesus didn't. He said, “Fear not,” and gave the sinful man a job.

ζωγρέω said zōgreō “to catch alive”

Fish are caught to die. Men are caught to live. And notice when they left it all: on the best day of fishing they ever had. They forsook all at the top, not because the business failed, but because they'd found Someone better.

Part two

I will: be thou clean

Verses 12–26

A man nobody would touch, and a man who couldn't walk.

“Full of leprosy”

In the Bible, leprosy covered a number of serious skin diseases. Luke, a doctor, says this man was full of it: far gone. The Law of Moses said what his life had to look like:

“And the leper in whom the plague is, his clothes shall be rent, and his head bare, and he shall put a covering upon his upper lip, and shall cry, Unclean, unclean.”

Leviticus 13:45

“All the days wherein the plague shall be in him he shall be defiled; he is unclean: he shall dwell alone; without the camp shall his habitation be.”

Leviticus 13:46

Torn clothes, shouting “Unclean!” so people could get out of the way, living alone outside town. No work, no family, no worship, no touch. Maybe for years.

He touched him

He said“If thou wilt, thou canst.”
→
Jesus said“I will: be thou clean.”

The man had no doubt Jesus could. He wasn't sure Jesus would, not for someone like him. Jesus answered with His hand first. Touching a leper made a man unclean, but this time the uncleanness didn't spread to Jesus. The cleanness spread to the man.

If you've felt like the man nobody wants to get close to, hear it: He is willing. “Moved with compassion” (Mark 1:41).

Then: go to the priest. The priest would look him over and declare him clean (Leviticus 14), and he could go home to his family and his town again.

Luke keeps showing Him praying

  1. 3:21at His baptism
  2. 5:16alone, with crowds waitingtoday
  3. 6:12all night, before choosing the twelve
  4. 9:18“Whom say the people that I am?”
  5. 9:29on the mountain
  6. 11:1“Lord, teach us to pray”
  7. 22:41in the garden
  8. 23:46on the cross

The busier He got, the more He pulled away to pray. Big crowds didn't replace time alone with the Father. If the Son of God needed that, we need it more.

Through the roof

Houses had flat roofs, reached by an outside stair, made of beams covered with tiles or packed clay. The friends climbed up, opened a hole, and lowered their friend “into the midst before Jesus.”

He couldn't get himself there. His friends carried him, and they wouldn't quit when the door was blocked. Mark says it took four of them. Jesus saw their faith.

Every man in recovery needs men like that: brothers who'll carry you when you can't walk, and tear up a roof to get you to Jesus.

First things first

Everyone in that room could see what the man needed: his legs. Jesus went to what he needed more. His body was broken, but his sins were the deeper problem, the one that lasts forever.

We usually come to Jesus about the thing that hurts most right now: the habit, the health, the money, the marriage. He cares about all of it. But He starts with the heart.

They were right, and wrong

The scribes (experts in the Law) and the Pharisees (the strictest religious party) got the theology right: only God can forgive sins. A sin is against God, so only He can let it go. Where they went wrong was about who was standing in front of them.

“Thy sins be forgiven thee”

Easy to say

Nobody can see whether it happened.

“Rise up and walk”

Easy to check

Everybody will see whether it happens.

So Jesus did the one they could see, to prove the one they couldn't. “That ye may know.” Only God can forgive sins, and Jesus did. Son of man was His favorite name for Himself, taken from Daniel 7:13–14, the One given an everlasting kingdom.

Carrying the bed that carried him

He came in on a bed, through the roof, carried by others. He walked out the door carrying the bed, on his own two legs, praising God.

The thing that used to hold you down becomes your testimony. You carry it out the door as proof of what God did.

“We have seen strange things to day.” Strange means unheard of, never seen before. That's what forgiveness looks like up close.

Part three

Not the righteous, but sinners

Verses 27–39

A tax man follows Jesus, throws a party, and the religious men don't like the guest list.

Levi the publican

A publican collected taxes and tolls for the rulers who answered to Rome. The receipt of custom was his booth, likely on the busy road through Capernaum. Publicans charged what they could get away with and kept the rest. People counted them traitors and thieves.

Simon the fisherman

Could go back to his nets

And one day he did, for a night (John 21:3).

Levi the publican

Could never get his booth back

Somebody else would have it by morning.

Levi burned the bridge, and threw a party. His first move as a follower was to bring all his crooked friends to meet Jesus. We know him by his other name, Matthew, who wrote the first gospel:

“And as Jesus passed forth from thence, he saw a man, named Matthew, sitting at the receipt of custom: and he saith unto him, Follow me. And he arose, and followed him.”

Matthew 9:9

They that are whole need not a physician; but they that are sick.

A doctor who stays away from sick people isn't much of a doctor. Jesus came for the sick, and He wasn't ashamed to sit at their table. (Luke, who wrote it down, was a physician himself.)

“I came not to call the righteous.” There aren't any truly righteous people (Romans 3:10). But there are people who think they're fine, and they never call the doctor. The first step is admitting you're sick. Every man in recovery knows that step.

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

You don't fast at a wedding

To fast is to go without food for a time to seek God, often in sorrow. The Pharisees fasted twice a week (Luke 18:12). The children of the bridechamber were the groom's friends at the wedding, and a wedding feast could last a week. Nobody went hungry at a wedding.

Jesus is the Bridegroom. Having Him with them was a celebration, not a funeral.

Then a hint of what's coming: “the bridegroom shall be taken away.” This is the first time in Luke that Jesus points toward His death. After that, His people would fast. Christians still do: not to earn anything, but to seek the Lord.

New wine, new bottles

These bottles were wineskins: goat hides sewn into bags. New wine was still working and swelling. A new skin could stretch with it. An old skin had gone dry and stiff.

Pour new wine into an old skin, and it bursts: the wine is lost and the skin is ruined. Same with sewing a patch of new cloth on an old coat: the patch shrinks and tears it.

Jesus isn't giving advice about wine. The life He brings can't be patched onto an old way of living or old religion. He doesn't fix up the old man. He makes a new one.

“The old is better”

People get used to what they know. The Pharisees liked their old system, where they came out looking good, and they didn't want what Jesus was pouring.

The old life says the same thing to a man in recovery: the old was better. The old friends, the old places, the old ways of coping. It's a lie that remembers the taste and forgets the wreckage.

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Points to remember

  1. Obedience“Nevertheless at thy word” 5:5Do what He says, even tired, even doubtful. Feelings can follow.
  2. Seeing yourself“I am a sinful man, O Lord” 5:8Seeing Jesus shows us ourselves, and He answers, “Fear not.”
  3. The touch“He put forth his hand, and touched him” 5:13He is willing. No man is too unclean for His hand.
  4. Forgiveness“That ye may know” 5:24The healing you could see proved the forgiveness you couldn't.
  5. Sinners welcome“Sinners to repentance” 5:32Jesus came for the sick. Admitting it is the first step.
  6. New life“New wine must be put into new bottles” 5:38He doesn't patch up the old life. He makes a new man.

Question 1 of 6

Obedience

What did Simon do when Jesus' word didn't make sense to him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He told Jesus honestly how it looked: a whole night of work and nothing to show for it. Then he obeyed anyway: “Nevertheless at thy word.” He didn't wait to understand or to feel like it, and the nets filled until they broke.

Question 2 of 6

Seeing yourself

What did Simon say when he saw who Jesus really was?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then said I, Woe is me! for I am undone; because I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips: for mine eyes have seen the King, the LORD of hosts.”

Isaiah 6:5

Answer

“Depart from me; for I am a sinful man, O Lord.” Seeing Jesus' power made Simon see his own sin. But Jesus didn't leave. He said, “Fear not,” and called him to catch men. Admitting what we are is where Jesus starts with us.

Question 3 of 6

The touch

What did Jesus do for the leper that no one else would?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, and touched him, and saith unto him, I will; be thou clean.”

Mark 1:41

Answer

He touched him. The Law kept everyone away from a leper, and this man may not have felt a human hand in years. Jesus reached out before He spoke, said, “I will,” and made him clean. He is willing to touch the man everyone else avoids.

Question 4 of 6

Forgiveness

How did Jesus prove He has power to forgive sins?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He forgave the man's sins, something no one could see, and then healed his body, something everyone could see. The man got up, picked up his bed, and walked home praising God. Only God can forgive sins, and Jesus proved He has that power.

Question 5 of 6

Sinners welcome

Who did Jesus say He came to call?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Sinners, to repentance. Like a doctor, He goes to the sick, and He sat at the table with publicans and sinners without shame. The only people He can't help are the ones who think they're well. Whosoever admits he's sick can come.

Question 6 of 6

New life

What do the new wine and the new bottles teach us about the life Jesus brings?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

The life Jesus gives doesn't fit into the old life or old religion. It isn't a patch on an old coat. In Christ, a man is made new, with room to grow, and both the new life and the new man are kept safe.
