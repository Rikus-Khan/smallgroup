John is an old man now, at Ephesus, about AD 85 to 95, writing to believers he calls “little children.”

False teachers had left the church, and the believers were shaken. So John gives three tests: walking in the light, loving the brethren, and believing the truth about Jesus.

Chapter 4 takes two of them: what to believe about Jesus, and what real love looks like.

Part one

Try the spirits

Verses 1–6

Not every voice that talks about God is from God. Here is how to tell, and who is stronger.

One question that sorts them out

Try means test, the way gold is tested. Behind every preacher, book and video is a spirit, and not all of them are from God. Don't ask the easy questions:

  • Is he popular?“the world heareth them” (4:5)
  • Does he sound spiritual?false prophets always do
  • Does he promise me an easy life?that's what a man wants to hear
  • What does he say about Jesus?God come in the flesh, or not?

Antichrist means one who is against Christ and puts something in His place. John says that spirit is “already … in the world.” Any teaching that makes Jesus smaller than God come in a real body, dying for real sins, is that spirit.

Check it in the Book

“These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.”

Acts 17:11

Some of us have followed a voice before because it was loud and sure of itself: a crew, a cause, a man with all the answers. It cost us. A new believer is a target for the same thing in church clothes.

So do what the Bereans did. When you hear something about God, open the Bible and check it, and ask an older believer. A true teacher won't mind. A false one will.

Greater is He

He that is in you is God Himself, by His Spirit. Every believer has Him, from the day he believed (4:13).

He that is in the world is the devil, and every lie and pull that comes with him: the phone call from the old crowd, the voice that says one more won't hurt.

John doesn't say the pull isn't real. He says it isn't bigger. You don't face it alone, and you don't face it with your own strength.

Already overcome

Look at the tense: have overcome. John is writing to ordinary believers who held on to the truth about Jesus when the false teachers walked out. That was a win, and it was God's win in them.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

If you fell yesterday, that doesn't change who lives in you. Get up, confess it (1:9), and remember who is greater. Today, when the pull comes, say it out loud: greater is He that is in me.

Part two

God is love

Verses 7–16

Where love comes from, how God showed it, and where people can see it now.

Not just that God loves. God is love.

Love isn't one of His moods that comes and goes. It is who He is. He doesn't love you on your good days and put up with you on your bad ones.

And it doesn't turn around: love is not God. The world says that and means whatever it feels. John means the love God shows us, and he is about to show us exactly what it looks like.

The word of the chapter

Count it. Love, loved, loveth: in verses 7 to 21 John says it 27 times.

7×3 8×2 9×1 10×3 11×2 12×2 13  14  15  16×3 17×1 18×3 19×2 20×3 21×2

An old man who walked with Jesus for about three years, and by tradition outlived the other apostles, sat down to write what mattered most. This is what came out.

Love came down

Most religion is a ladder: man climbs up to God, and if he climbs well enough, God loves him back.

“Not that we loved God.” John crosses out the ladder. The love started at the top and came down: “he loved us.”

And it came down in a Person. God sent his only begotten Son, so that dead men might live through him.

Propitiation is the sacrifice that turns God's anger away by taking it. God didn't pretend our sin was nothing. He took the punishment Himself, in His Son.

“And he is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world.”

1 John 2:2

Where people see God now

Ifwe love one another,
→
thenGod dwelleth in us, and His love is seen.

Nobody in your life has seen God. But they've seen you. The guys at work, your family, the men who knew you before: they will judge what God is like by how believers treat each other.

“By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another.”

John 13:35

And you don't do it in your own strength. “He hath given us of his Spirit.” The Spirit you received when you believed is the One who loves through you.

Whosoever

“The Saviour of the world.” Not the Saviour of good people, or church people, or people with a clean record. The world.

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

John 3:16

Whosoever shall confess that Jesus is the Son of God. That door is open to every man in this room. And the man who walks through it doesn't just visit God: God dwelleth in him, and he in God.

Notice verse 16: “we have known and believed the love.” Some days you won't feel loved. Faith rests on what God did, not on how this morning feels.

Part three

No fear in love

Verses 17–21

For the man who lives braced for the blow. And for the brother he can't stand.

Perfect love casteth out fear

What if He's done with me? They'll find out. Punishment is coming. Not this sin too. I'll never change. Judgment day. I don't measure up. The knock at the door.

There is no fear in love.he first loved us

Many of us lived years waiting for the blow: the arrest, the phone call, the day it all comes out. Some of us carried that same fear into how we think about God.

Casteth out is a strong word: it means to throw out, the way you'd throw a man out of your house. God's love doesn't just calm fear down. It throws it out.

Why a believer can stop bracing

Fear says

The blow is coming

Torment here means punishment. Fear is always waiting for it to fall, so it hides, keeps God at arm's length, and never rests.

Love says

The blow already fell

It fell on Jesus, the propitiation (4:10). So we can have boldness in the day of judgment. A man in Christ will stand there as he is: accepted.

“For ye have not received the spirit of bondage again to fear; but ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father.”

Romans 8:15

There is still a right fear of God: deep respect for Him. But a believer doesn't have to dread Him. You are a son, not a prisoner waiting on a sentence.

Who moved first

  1. He loved usAt the cross, before we cared4:10, 19
  2. We love HimLove answering love4:19
  3. We love our brotherLove passed on4:11, 21

You didn't find God. He came looking. Your love for Him didn't start the relationship, so your bad day can't end it.

If your love for God feels weak this morning, don't try to squeeze out more. Look at how He first loved you. Love grows by looking at His.

You can't cut one line and keep the other

Strong words from the apostle of love: he is a liar. Not a weak believer. A liar.

The brother is the one you can see. If you can't love him, your love for the God you can't see is only talk.

This isn't about liking everyone. It's about the man you refuse to forgive, the one you'd cross the street to avoid. That's the line to work on.

One brother, today

Hate grows easily in the places many of us have lived: the yard, the street, a house full of yelling. Grudges kept us alive once. Now they're killing us.

“My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth.”

1 John 3:18

So pick one name. Pray for him by name today. Then do one thing: a text, a fair word, letting a debt go. You don't have to feel it first. God loved you before you were lovable, and He'll love that man through you.

Points to remember

  1. Test“Try the spirits whether they are of God” 4:1Ask what a teacher says about Jesus: God come in the flesh, or not? Then check it in the Bible.
  2. Greater“Greater is he that is in you” 4:4The pull of the world is real, but it is not bigger than the God who lives in you.
  3. Love“He loved us, and sent his Son” 4:10Love came down. God sent His Son to take the punishment our sins deserved.
  4. Fear“Perfect love casteth out fear” 4:18The blow fell on Jesus. A believer can stop bracing for it.

Question 1 of 6

Test

How can we tell a spirit that is of God from one that is not?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Test it by what it says about Jesus. A spirit that confesses Jesus Christ come in the flesh is of God; one that denies it is the spirit of antichrist. Popularity proves nothing. Check what you hear against the Scriptures.

Question 2 of 6

Greater

Who is greater: He that is in us, or he that is in the world?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He that is in us. God lives in every believer by His Spirit, and He is greater than the devil and every pull of the world. The fight is real, but we don't fight it alone or in our own strength.

Question 3 of 6

Love

How did God show His love toward us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He sent His only begotten Son into the world to be the propitiation for our sins, taking the punishment we deserved, so that we might live through Him. Love didn't start with us. It came down from Him.

Question 4 of 6

Fear

What does perfect love do to fear?

What verses tell us about this?

“For ye have not received the spirit of bondage again to fear; but ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father.”

Romans 8:15

Answer

It casts fear out. Fear expects punishment, but for a believer the punishment already fell on Jesus. So we can have boldness even in the day of judgment, and come to God as sons, not as prisoners.

Question 5 of 6

First

Why do we love God at all?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because He first loved us. We didn't start it. He loved us before we cared, and our love is an answer to His. When our love runs low, we look again at His.

Question 6 of 6

Brother

What does John call a man who says he loves God but hates his brother?

What verses tell us about this?

“My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth.”

1 John 3:18

Answer

A liar. If he won't love the brother he can see, he can't love the God he can't see. God commands that the man who loves Him love his brother too, in deeds, not just words.
