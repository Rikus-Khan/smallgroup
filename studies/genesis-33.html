Genesis 33

The brother who ran

Jacob limps toward the man who once swore to kill him, bowing seven times. Esau runs to meet him, throws his arms around him, and they weep. Then the brothers go their separate ways, and Jacob settles near Shechem and builds an altar to the God of Israel.

Part one

The meeting

Verses 1–11

Esau comes with four hundred men. Jacob goes out first, limping.

The order of march

  1. FirstJacob himselfhe “passed over before them”
  2. Thenthe handmaidsand their children
  3. ThenLeahand her children
  4. LastRachel and Joseph“hindermost”: the farthest from danger

Old word: hindermost is last, at the very back. Jacob arranged his family by how much he feared for them. The ones he loved most were the farthest from the sword. It tells you how things stood in his house, and it will cost this family later, in Joseph's story.

But look at who goes first. In chapter 32 Jacob sent everyone and everything across the river ahead of him. Now, limping, he goes out in front of them all.

Seven times to the ground.

Bowing to the ground, again and again, was how a servant honoured a king. Jacob, with a hip that had been put out of joint a few hours earlier, bows seven times in the dust.

“Let people serve thee, and nations bow down to thee: be lord over thy brethren, and let thy mother’s sons bow down to thee: cursed be every one that curseth thee, and blessed be he that blesseth thee.”

Genesis 27:29

That was Isaac's blessing, meant for the elder. Jacob was the one who got it, by a trick. Now he is the one doing the bowing. He does not give up the blessing, but he gives up the pride. Humility is not weakness. It is going first, even when it hurts.

Five verbs

  1. ranHe did not wait to be asked.
  2. embracedNo handshake. His arms.
  3. fell on his neckThrew himself on his brother.
  4. kissed himThe kiss of a brother.
  5. weptTwo grown men, both of them crying.

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

Jesus told a story with the same words, about a father and a son who had wasted everything. Esau is not God. But the picture is the same: forgiveness that runs.

Nobody apologised first. The gift had not arrived yet. Esau's forgiveness was already there before Jacob said a word.

What he prepared for, and what he got

What Jacob prepared

A speech, a gift, a plan

A message, five or six waves of animals, two bands in case of attack, a prayer, a night of wrestling.

What he got

A run and an embrace

Esau ran. Twenty years of dread were answered in one hug.

If you are the one who wronged someone, you may be dreading the call as Jacob did. You may be ready for the worst, and find grace. Not always, but sometimes. Make the call.

If you are the one who was wronged, notice what Esau did. He had every reason to hold on. He ran toward the man.

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

The New Testament does call Esau a profane man for selling his birthright (Hebrews 12:16). That does not cancel what he does here. A man can fail badly in one chapter and be gracious in another.

“Find grace in the sight of my lord”

Count the word grace (or graciously) in this chapter:

1  2  3  4  5×1 6  7  8×1 9  10×1 11×1 12  13  14  15×1 16  17  18  19  20 

5 times in twenty verses. Jacob asks for it, thanks God for it, and gets it. Grace means kindness nobody earned. Jacob came planning to buy it. Esau gave it.

Old word: graciously means as a gift, by grace. The children are “graciously given”: gifts from God.

“My blessing”

בִּרְכָתִי said birkhati “my blessing”

This is the same Hebrew word Esau cried out about, twenty years ago, when Jacob stole it:

“And when Esau heard the words of his father, he cried with a great and exceeding bitter cry, and said unto his father, Bless me, even me also, O my father.”

Genesis 27:34

Here Jacob says, “Take, I pray thee, my blessing that is brought to thee.” He is putting a blessing back into his brother's hands. That is what making amends looks like: give back what you can, and do not make the other man beg.

Notice the order. Forgiveness came first (verse 4); the gift came second. It was not the price. It was a sign that Jacob meant it. That is how amends should work: not to buy forgiveness, but because you want to put it right.

Face to face

Genesis 32:30

I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved.

Genesis 33:10

I have seen thy face, as though I had seen the face of God, and thou wast pleased with me.

A man who has seen the face of God and lived can look his brother in the face. The night at the Jabbok came before the morning with Esau. It was the way he could stand there.

You may not be able to face the people you have wronged until you have faced God first. Start there. Then go.

“I have enough”

Esau, verse 9

“I have enough”

In Hebrew, “I have much.” He is already a wealthy man, and he does not need the animals.

Jacob, verse 11

“I have enough”

A different Hebrew word: “I have everything.” God has dealt graciously with him.

Two men who each say they have enough. For years each had made the other's birthright the center of his life. Contentment ended a twenty-year war. A man who says “I have enough” has stopped grabbing.

Part two

Two roads

Verses 12–17

The brothers are at peace. They do not move in together.

“I will lead on softly”

Esau offers“Let us take our journey, and I will go before thee.”
→
But Jacob seeschildren who are tender, and flocks with young
→
So he says“I will lead on softly, according as the cattle … and the children be able to endure”

Old words: tender means delicate. Overdrive means push too hard. Softly means slowly and gently.

The man with the limp sets the pace by the weakest in the group. That is the Good Shepherd's way too:

“He shall feed his flock like a shepherd: he shall gather the lambs with his arm, and carry them in his bosom, and shall gently lead those that are with young.”

Isaiah 40:11

Go at the pace of your kids, a new believer, a brother who has just fallen.

Two roads

Esau goes south to Seir, in Edom. Jacob goes west to Succoth, near the Jordan, then across it toward Shechem. The lines show the general directions; the text gives no route.

Old word: booths are shelters made from branches. Jacob built a house for himself and booths for his animals, and the place is named for them.

Notice Jacob's promise in verse 14: “until I come unto my lord unto Seir.” The Bible never says he went.

At peace, on separate roads

What the text says

Two roads

Esau went home to Seir. Jacob went to Succoth. Jacob said he would come, and as far as we are told, he did not.

What happened later

Side by side

When their father Isaac died, “his sons Esau and Jacob buried him” (35:29).

“And Isaac gave up the ghost, and died, and was gathered unto his people, being old and full of days: and his sons Esau and Jacob buried him.”

Genesis 35:29

Why did Jacob not go? The Bible does not say. Some teachers think he was still hedging, an old Jacob habit: say yes and do something else. Others see sensible caution with a young family and a long road. Either way, the brothers were not enemies any more.

“If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.”

Romans 12:18

Being reconciled does not always mean living in the same house. Forgive freely. Trust has to be rebuilt more slowly. If the relationship cannot go back to what it was, that is not failure. Peace is the point.

Part three

An altar at Shechem

Verses 18–20

Home at last, and Jacob makes the faith his own.

Land, and a family name

  1. Shalema town by Shechem. The word also means “peace”, “whole.”
  2. parcela piece, a plot of land
  3. pieces of moneya hundred pieces of silver: a Hebrew unit of value; its exact weight is not known
  4. Hamorthe head of the family that held the land. His son's name was Shechem.
  5. erectedset up, built

This is the first land Jacob owns in Canaan. He did not take it, or trick it away. He paid. For a man who had grabbed so much, that is a change.

He came “from Padan-aram” and he came safe, as the LORD had told him: “I will be with thee.” Many teachers also note that Jacob stopped at Shechem, not at Bethel, where God first met him. Chapter 34 shows what happened there.

An altar with his own name on it

The God of my father → El-elohe-Israel Hebrew El Elohe Yisrael: “God, the God of Israel.” Israel is Jacob's new name. This God is now his.

“And Jacob vowed a vow, saying, If God will be with me, and will keep me in this way that I go, and will give me bread to eat, and raiment to put on, So that I come again to my father’s house in peace; then shall the LORD be my God:”

Genesis 28:20-21

That was Jacob's vow at Bethel: if God will be with me … then shall the LORD be my God. Look at the end of this chapter. He is back in the land, safe, and the altar says it: the LORD is his God.

In chapter 31 he said “the God of my father” again and again. Now he names the altar for himself and his God together. Many of us start with someone else's faith: a mother's, a pastor's, a group's. The day comes when it has to be your own. Build your own altar: a daily time with God, in your own words.

Question 1 of 6

The meeting

Jacob expected a fight. What did Esau do?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

Answer

He ran to meet him, embraced him, fell on his neck, kissed him, and they both wept. Jacob had dreaded four hundred men; he got a brother. It is a small picture of how God receives a man who comes home, not with a lecture, but running.

Question 2 of 6

Forgiveness

Did Jacob have to earn Esau's forgiveness?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Answer

No. Esau forgave before Jacob said a word and refused the gift at first. That is how God forgave us: not because we earned it, but because of Christ. If you are the one who was wronged, you can forgive the same way. If you are the one who did wrong, you may find more grace than you expect.

Question 3 of 6

Making it right

What was Jacob trying to make right with his gift?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Zacchaeus stood, and said unto the Lord; Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.”

Luke 19:8

“Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.”

Matthew 5:23-24

Answer

The blessing he had taken from Esau. He called the gift “my blessing”, the same word Esau cried over in chapter 27, and he pushed it into his brother's hands. Making amends means giving back what you can. It does not buy forgiveness; it shows you mean it.

Question 4 of 6

Humility

How did Jacob come to his brother?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let people serve thee, and nations bow down to thee: be lord over thy brethren, and let thy mother’s sons bow down to thee: cursed be every one that curseth thee, and blessed be he that blesseth thee.”

Genesis 27:29

Answer

Limping, going first, and bowing seven times to the ground. The man who was told his brother would bow to him bowed instead, and called Esau his lord. Humility is not weakness. It is what a man who has met God at the Jabbok looks like.

Question 5 of 6

Wisdom

Why did Jacob not go on with Esau to Seir?

What verses tell us about this?

“If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.”

Romans 12:18

Answer

The Bible does not say. His reasons were the children, the flocks, and the pace of the weak, and some teachers think old habits were at work as well. Either way, the brothers made peace and did not live as enemies; later they buried their father together. Peace does not always mean moving in together.

Question 6 of 6

Worship

What did Jacob do when he reached Canaan, and what did he name the altar?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Jacob vowed a vow, saying, If God will be with me, and will keep me in this way that I go, and will give me bread to eat, and raiment to put on, So that I come again to my father’s house in peace; then shall the LORD be my God:”

Genesis 28:20-21

Answer

He bought a piece of land, paying for it, and built an altar, calling it El-elohe-Israel: “God, the God of Israel.” At Bethel he vowed that the LORD would be his God. Now the altar says it. Make your faith your own.
