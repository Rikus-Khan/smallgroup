Exodus 22

Make it right

A people just out of slavery gets its first laws for daily life: pay back what you took, answer for what you were trusted with, and never crush the stranger, the widow or the fatherless. “If they cry at all unto me, I will surely hear their cry.”

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. RestitutionWhat did a thief owe when he was caught, and what if he had nothing?

Part one

“He shall make restitution”

Verses 1–15

Stolen goods, a fire, a borrowed donkey: what you owe when you take or lose what is not yours.

Five oxen for an ox

Chapters 21 to 23 are called “the judgments” (21:1): the rulings Israel's judges were to follow. Here is the first one for theft. The thief does not just give the animal back. He pays more.

×5an ox stolen, then killed or sold
×4a sheep stolen, then killed or sold
×2caught with it still alive (v. 4)

Restore and restitution mean to put back, and to put back with interest. An ox ploughed a man's field, so a stolen one cost him his living, and the penalty was five. The law set the price of the crime so that stealing never paid.

Verse 2 is about a thief “breaking up”: digging through the mud wall of a house at night, when the owner cannot see who is there. If he dies in the dark, the owner is not guilty of murder. In daylight (v. 3), when the man could be seen and caught, the owner may not kill him. These were laws for Israel's courts. They limited how far a man could go, and protected even a thief's life once the danger was over.

“If he have nothing”

What if the thief cannot pay five oxen? The law has an answer: “he shall be sold for his theft.” He works it off as a servant until the debt is paid.

  • No prisonIsrael had no jail cells for thieves. The man paid, in goods or in labour.
  • The victim is paidThe point was to give the owner his loss back, not just to punish the thief.
  • A way backWhen the debt was paid, the man was free and the account was closed.

And “if the theft be certainly found in his hand alive” (v. 4), caught red-handed, the price is double. The more plainly guilty and the more has been sold off or eaten, the more it costs.

The man who made it right

Pay it back, and more

Five oxen for an ox. Four sheep for a sheep. Double if the thing is in his hand. The law made a thief count the cost.

Luke 19

Zacchaeus

A tax collector who had cheated his neighbours. Jesus went to his house first. Then Zacchaeus stood up and said what he would do.

“And Zacchaeus stood, and said unto the Lord; Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.”

Luke 19:8

“And Jesus said unto him, This day is salvation come to this house, forsomuch as he also is a son of Abraham.”

Luke 19:9

He paid fourfold, the same measure the law asked for a stolen sheep. The paying did not buy his salvation. It showed it. A man whom Jesus has found wants to put right what he broke.

Real world: making amends

Most of us in this room took something from somebody: money, time, trust, years of a child's life. Restitution is the oldest answer in the Bible, and Jesus kept it.

“Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.”

Matthew 5:23–24
  • Name itSay plainly what you took or broke. No excuses, as in Genesis 50.
  • Count itWrite down what you owe, in money or in time.
  • Pay what you canA little paid on time is worth more than a big promise.
  • Ask firstTalk to your pastor or a brother in Christ before you knock on a door. Some amends need advice, especially where the law, a family or another person's safety is involved. If a court has told you not to contact someone, do not: ask your pastor (and your probation officer, if you have one) about another way.

Your sins are forgiven already, and that does not depend on what you pay. Making it right is what a forgiven man does next. Pick one name today and start.

thenhe pays, from the best of his own.

Nobody here set out to steal. A man's animal strays, or a fire he lit gets away in the dry thorns. It is still his animal and his fire, and the neighbour's loss is real. “Stacks of corn” and “standing corn” are cut or growing grain. Corn in the KJV means grain.

The payment is “of the best of his own field.” Not the leftovers. The man whose crop was eaten gets the quality he lost.

Some of the damage we have done was not planned: a temper, a drunken night, a bad decision that cost somebody else. Saying “I did not mean it” is true, and it still does not pay the bill. Own it.

Goods left in your keeping

  • stuffgoods, belongings (“money or stuff to keep”)
  • challengethclaims (“which another challengeth to be his”)
  • trespasshere, a wrong done over property
  • condemnfind guilty

There was no bank. A man went away and left money or goods with a neighbour to look after. If they were stolen, the thief pays double when found. If no one is found, the keeper goes before the judges to show his own hands are clean. If he was lying, he pays double to his neighbour.

Whoever the judges condemn pays double. The same measure falls on the thief and on the keeper who stole what he was trusted with.

“No man seeing it”

A donkey in a neighbour's care dies or goes missing, and nobody saw what happened. How can anyone know whether the keeper is honest? Verse 11: “an oath of the LORD be between them both.” He swears before God that he did not take it, and the owner accepts his word.

the ownerDid you take my donkey?

the keeper, under oathI did not put my hand on it. The LORD is witness.

The honest keeper is not made to pay (11, 13). If a beast was torn by a wild animal, he brings the remains “for witness.” If the owner was with it (15), or it was hired, the owner bears the loss. The law is fair to the one who was trusted, as well as the one who trusted.

Every man in this room has done things “no man seeing it”: things at night, on the phone, in the truck. The law counts on a man's honesty when no one can check, because someone always can. Walk in the light and tell your brothers. A secret has no power over a man who has said it out loud.

Part two

“I will surely hear their cry”

Verses 16–27

A few hard lines, and then the heart of God for the stranger, the widow, the fatherless and the poor.

A man answers for what he took

  • enticeto talk a girl into it
  • a maid not betrotheda young woman who is not engaged to anyone
  • endowpay the bride-price (the dowry)

In that world, a young woman who had been with a man and was not married to him could be left with no future at all. So the law did not let the man walk away. He had to pay the dowry and marry her, unless her father refused, and then he still had to pay in full.

The weight of the law falls on the man. The women in your life, wives, daughters, girlfriends, are not to be used and left. If that is in your past, bring it honestly to God and to your pastor first, and let him help you decide whether anything more is right or safe to do.

Hard lines, for a nation under God

Three short, severe verses. Hear them for what they were: laws for Israel's courts, while Israel was a nation with the LORD as its king.

  • a witchsomeone who dealt with spirits and sorcery, looking for power or answers apart from God (Deuteronomy 18 describes it)
  • sufferallow (“thou shalt not suffer” = you shall not allow)
  • utterly destroyedcut off from the people, the penalty for sacrificing to another god

Verse 19 names a sin that turns what God made upside down. Verse 20 is about the one thing that was, for a nation under God, like treason: giving to another god what belongs to the LORD alone.

Believers today are not Israel's courts, and Jesus never handed his people that sword. What carries over is that these things are real sins that harm people, and the God who named them is also the One who forgives: Jesus came for people who had done exactly such things.

“Ye were strangers”

A stranger here is a foreigner living in Israel, with no land or family of his own. Vex means trouble, and afflict means press down. God does not say “be nice.” He says: remember. You were the stranger in Egypt, and you know how it felt.

  • the strangerno land, no family
  • the widowno husband to provide or to speak for her
  • the fatherlessno one to stand up for him
  • Godstands up for all three

“A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows, is God in his holy habitation.”

Psalm 68:5

“Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world.”

James 1:27

The cry God hears

“And it came to pass in process of time, that the king of Egypt died: and the children of Israel sighed by reason of the bondage, and they cried, and their cry came up unto God by reason of the bondage.”

Exodus 2:23

Israel knew this cry. Their own cry came up out of Egypt, and “God heard their groaning” (2:24). Now He tells them: the cry of the helpless reaches Me too, and I answer it. Wax is the old word for grow: His anger grows hot.

There are two sides here. If you have been hard on someone weak, in a home, at work, on the street, this is a serious word for you, and God's answer is to turn from it, ask forgiveness and make it right. And if you were the one who was crushed, and nobody listened, hear this: God was listening. He still is.

Do not squeeze a man who is down

  • usurera lender who charges heavy interest, a loan shark
  • usurythe interest charged on a loan
  • pledgesomething handed over as security for a loan
  • raimentclothing: here, the poor man's outer cloak

A poor man borrows for food, not for business. So no interest from “any of my people that is poor by thee” (25). And if you take his cloak as security, hand it back by sundown. It is “his covering only.” “Wherein shall he sleep?” It was his blanket.

“The rich ruleth over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender.”

Proverbs 22:7

Many men in this room have been that borrower: loans, cards, payday lenders, an addiction that ate the rent. God sees the man who lends to squeeze a poor man, and He calls Himself gracious to the one who cannot pay.

“for I am gracious”

The poor man, with nothing but a cloak, cries out in the night. God says: “I will hear; for I am gracious” (v. 27). Gracious means He gives help to people who have no claim on Him.

You will hear this word again in chapter 34, when God says His own name to Moses: “merciful and gracious.” Here it is in the middle of the law about a pledged coat. The God of the law is the God of grace.

Whatever debt you are under, with a bank or a dealer or a family, you can cry to Him today and He hears. Then go to the brothers and your pastor, and make a plan. Prayer and a plan go together.

Part three

“Ye shall be holy men unto me”

Verses 28–31

What God asks of the people who belong to Him: respect, the first of everything, and a life set apart.

Respect, and the first of everything

  • revileto abuse, to insult with words
  • the godshere, the judges and rulers who speak with the law's authority (the word is also used for them in Psalm 82)
  • liquorshere, the juices: the first pressed wine and oil
  • ripe fruitsthe first harvest from the field and vine

Do not curse the men set over you. You may disagree with a ruler, a boss or a judge. You may not tear him down with your mouth.

“Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase:”

Proverbs 3:9

“Thou shalt not delay.” The first of the harvest belongs to God, and the first comes first, not whatever is left at the end of the month. A man who gives God the first has not forgotten who gave him the crop.

“The firstborn of thy sons shalt thou give unto me”

A hard sentence, so read it with the rest of the book. The firstborn belonged to the LORD, because He spared Israel's firstborn in Egypt (12:13). But the sons are not given away. They are bought back:

“And every firstling of an ass thou shalt redeem with a lamb; and if thou wilt not redeem it, then thou shalt break his neck: and all the firstborn of man among thy children shalt thou redeem.”

Exodus 13:13

A lamb dies in place of the son. That is the whole gospel in a household custom: something belongs to God, and a lamb pays for it. The firstborn animals were given on the eighth day, after seven days with the mother (30).

“Ye shall be holy men”

Holy means set apart for God. It does not say perfect. It says: you are His, so live like it.

The last rule: no eating “flesh that is torn of beasts in the field”; throw it to the dogs. A holy people did not eat what a wild animal had killed. Even their meals marked them as different.

The law's list

Pay back. Answer for it. Be fair.

Every rule in this chapter shows what a holy man looks like: honest, responsible, kind to the weak, generous to God.

The gospel

Set apart by Another

None of us kept all of it. The One who did came to bear the penalty for those who did not, and then lives in us by His Spirit so that we can start.

Where in this chapter did you see yourself? Pick one: something you owe, someone you have been hard on, a debt to bring to the brothers. Do one thing about it today.

Points to remember

  1. Restitution“he shall restore five oxen for an ox, and four sheep for a sheep” 22:1A taker pays back, and more. Making amends is what a forgiven man does next, as Zacchaeus did.
  2. Responsibility“he that kindled the fire shall surely make restitution” 22:6“I did not mean it” does not pay the bill. And where no man sees, an oath before God is the check.
  3. Lines“He that sacrificeth unto any god, save unto the LORD only, he shall be utterly destroyed.” 22:20The law's lines protected the weak and kept worship the LORD's alone. They were for Israel's courts, and they show how serious sin is.
  4. Mercy“If thou afflict them in any wise, and they cry at all unto me, I will surely hear their cry” 22:23God hears the cry of the stranger, the widow and the fatherless. Be on their side.
  5. Debt“when he crieth unto me, that I will hear; for I am gracious” 22:27Do not squeeze a man who is down. And if you are down, cry to the gracious God.
  6. Holiness“And ye shall be holy men unto me” 22:31Holy means set apart, and belonging to Him. Start with the first of what you have.

Question 1 of 6

Restitution

What did a thief owe when he was caught, and what if he had nothing?

Answer

Five oxen for an ox, four sheep for a sheep, double if caught with it. If he had nothing, he worked it off. Zacchaeus shows the same thing after Jesus found him: a forgiven man puts right what he broke. Pick a name today, and talk to your pastor about how.

Question 2 of 6

Responsibility

Who paid when a man's beast or fire did harm, or when goods he was keeping were lost?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The man whose beast or fire it was paid, even if he did not mean it. A keeper who lost goods in secret swore an oath before the LORD, and was believed. Own what you did. And walk in the light: bring it to your brothers.

Question 3 of 6

Lines

Where did God draw lines to protect the weak and keep worship His alone?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A man could not use a girl and walk away: he had to answer for her. And worship belonged to the LORD alone. These were laws for Israel's courts under God as king. They show how seriously God takes people being used and God being replaced, and Jesus came for people who had done both.

Question 4 of 6

Mercy

Whom did God say never to afflict, and what would He do if they cried?

What verses tell us about this?

“Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world.”

James 1:27

Answer

The stranger, the widow and the fatherless. God hears their cry and answers it. Israel had been the stranger, so they knew. Be on the side of the one who has nobody, and if you are that man, God hears you.

Question 5 of 6

Debt

How was a man to treat a poor neighbour he lent to?

What verses tell us about this?

“The rich ruleth over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender.”

Proverbs 22:7

Answer

With no interest, and with kindness: the cloak given back by sundown. When the poor man cries, God hears, “for I am gracious.” If you are the one in debt, tell your brothers and make a plan, and bring it to the gracious God.

Question 6 of 6

Holiness

What did God want from a people who belonged to Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase:”

Proverbs 3:9

Answer

That they be His: the first of everything given to Him, respect for those over them, and a life that looks different. Holy means set apart. Nobody keeps it perfectly, and the grace of God is for the man who starts again today.
