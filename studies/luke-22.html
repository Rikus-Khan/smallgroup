Luke 22

The Lord turned, and looked upon Peter

The last supper, a night of prayer on the mount of Olives, a traitor's kiss, and a friend who falls and is not let go.

Part one

This do in remembrance of me

Verses 1–23

A traitor makes his deal. Then Jesus sits down with the twelve for one last Passover, and gives them a meal to remember Him by.

How a man ends up there

  1. One of the twelvechosen, sent out, saw every miracle6:13–16
  2. A thiefhe kept the money bag and stole from itJohn 12:6
  3. Satan enteredthrough a door already open22:3
  4. A price“they … covenanted to give him money”22:5
  5. Waitingfor a time with no crowd around22:6

Judas didn't fall in one night. He had walked with Jesus for about three years, and all that time he was feeding a secret. Satan didn't force his way in. He came through a door Judas had left open for a long time.

The chief priests wanted Jesus dead but feared the people. They needed an insider who could tell them where to find Him alone. Communed means talked it over; covenanted, agreed on a price. Matthew says thirty pieces of silver.

The longest night

Afternoonthe lambs killed
Evening“the hour was come”
At supperthe bread and the cup
Nightthe mount of Olives
Before daythe cock crew

On the Passover day, thousands of lambs were killed at the temple in the afternoon and eaten that night in homes all over the city. A man carrying a water pitcher was easy to spot: carrying water was usually women's work. The goodman of the house is the owner. A guestchamber is a room for guests, here a big room upstairs, furnished with couches and ready.

Jesus keeps the secret from Judas. Nobody knows where they'll eat until Peter and John follow the man. The traitor won't get his chance before the supper.

With desire I have desired

That's a strong way of saying I have wanted this so much. Jesus knew this was His last night. He knew who was at the table and what they would do before morning. And He wanted to spend it with them.

The Passover meal had several cups through the evening. Luke tells of two: this one (v. 17), passed around during the meal, and “the cup after supper” (v. 20). Jesus says He won't eat or drink it again until the kingdom of God comes. He is looking past the cross to a feast with His people.

From Passover to the Lord's Supper

The Passover

Remember Egypt

A lamb's blood on the door, and death passed over. Israel kept it every year “for a memorial” (Exodus 12:14).

The Lord's Supper

Remember Me

His body given, His blood shed, “for you.” He is the Lamb the Passover pointed to.

“Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us:”

1 Corinthians 5:7

A testament is a covenant, a binding promise. God promised a new covenant in Jeremiah 31:31, and Jesus signs it in His own blood.

A memorial

Jesus was sitting right there, holding the bread in His hand. It pictured His body; it didn't become it. The Lord's Supper is a memorial. It doesn't save anyone or add to what the cross did. We keep it to remember the One who saves.

The Passover cup was wine, “the fruit of the vine.” What matters is never what's in the cup, but whose blood it points to. Most Baptist churches use grape juice at the Lord's table, so no man in recovery has to choose between the table and his sobriety. If you're unsure, ask the pastor ahead of time. He'll be glad you did.

“For as often as ye eat this bread, and drink this cup, ye do shew the Lord’s death till he come.”

1 Corinthians 11:26

At the same table

To share a man's bread was to be his friend. Judas took the bread from Jesus' own hand while the deal was already made. David had written it long before:

“Yea, mine own familiar friend, in whom I trusted, which did eat of my bread, hath lifted up his heel against me.”

Psalm 41:9

“As it was determined”: God knew it was coming and worked it into His plan to save the world (Acts 2:23). That didn't make Judas a puppet. He chose it, and Jesus says woe to him. Even then, Jesus kept the door open: He served him at the table and named no names.

Part two

I am among you as he that serveth

Verses 24–38

An argument breaks out at the table. Jesus answers it, then turns to Peter with a warning and a promise.

The way down is the way up

  1. Kings“exercise lordship”: they rule over people
  2. “Benefactors”and give themselves fine titles
  3. “As the younger”the lowest in the house
  4. “As he that serveth”the one carrying the dishes

Hours from the cross, the disciples are arguing about rank. Benefactor, “one who does good,” was a title rulers gave themselves while they bled their people dry. Jesus says: “ye shall not be so.” In His kingdom the greatest is the one who serves, and He is the proof.

“For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.”

Mark 10:45

He still honours them

Look how Jesus treats these men. He has just corrected them for squabbling. Within hours they'll run. And He says: you stayed with Me through My temptations, the trials and hard days. I am giving you a kingdom. You'll eat at My table and sit on thrones.

He sees what they are, and He sees what they will be. Jesus doesn't keep score of a man's worst night. He remembers the times you stayed.

Sifted like wheat

To sift wheat, a farmer shook the grain hard in a sieve until the dirt and chaff fell through. Satan asked for the disciples, to shake them until they came apart.

Satan hath desired to have“you”: all of you. The word is plural.

But I have prayed for“thee”: you, Simon. Just you.

Jesus didn't promise Peter he wouldn't be shaken. He promised something better: “that thy faith fail not.” Peter would fall hard that night, but his faith wouldn't die, because Jesus was holding on to him. He still prays for His own (Hebrews 7:25).

When thou art converted

When thou artconverted, turned back to Me,
→
thenstrengthen thy brethren.

Converted here means turned back. Peter already believed; he didn't need saving again. He needed to come back. And notice it's when, not if. Jesus already saw him home, and gave him work to do once he got there.

Years later, Peter wrote to suffering believers, and used that very word: “stablish, strengthen, settle you” (1 Peter 5:10). Your worst fall, once you're turned back, can be the thing God uses to hold another man up.

“Lord, I am ready”

Peter“Lord, I am ready to go with thee, both into prison, and to death.”

Jesus“the cock shall not crow this day, before that thou shalt thrice deny that thou knowest me.”
before morning

Peter meant every word. That's what makes it hard. A man can mean it with his whole heart and still go down that night, because his heart isn't strong enough on its own.

Here's the grace in it: later, Peter did go to prison for Jesus (Acts 12) and, as Jesus told him, finally to death (John 21:18–19). Not in his own strength that night, but in the Lord's, after he was restored.

Two swords

Then

Sent with nothing

No purse, no scrip (a traveller's bag), no spare shoes, and people took them in. They lacked nothing (9:3, 10:4).

Now

A hostile world

Their Master is about to be counted a criminal. They'll have to carry their own way, and they'll be hated.

This is a hard saying, and the men took it literally: “here are two swords.” Jesus answers, “It is enough”, and many read that as enough of this talk. An hour later, when a sword is used, He stops it and heals the wound (22:51). He wasn't raising an army. He was going to be “numbered with the transgressors” (Isaiah 53:12).

Part three

Not my will, but thine

Verses 39–53

Jesus prays in agony while His men sleep. Then the torches come up the hill.

As he was wont

Wont means used to, His habit. Every night that week He had slept out on the mount of Olives (21:37). Judas knew exactly where to look.

He goes about a stone's cast away, as far as you could throw a stone, and kneels. Matthew and Mark call the olive grove Gethsemane, “the oil press.”

Before the night is over, He will be marched back into the city to the high priest's house (v. 54).

This cup

This isn't the supper cup. In the Old Testament, a cup is often a picture of God's judgment poured out on sin. Jesus is about to drink that cup for us, all of it, and He feels its full weight.

“Not my will, but thine, be done.”

That's the most honest prayer a man can pray. Jesus told the Father what He wanted, plainly, then put it in the Father's hands. It's the prayer every one of us has to learn: not my way any more.

In an agony

Agony is the word for a wrestler's struggle. Luke, the doctor, is the one who tells us the details: an angel sent to strengthen Him, and sweat falling “as it were great drops of blood.” This was no calm, easy prayer. Jesus was fully man, and it cost Him everything.

Notice what He did with the agony: He prayed more earnestly. When the pressure went up, so did the praying. And God answered, not by taking the cup away, but by strengthening Him to drink it.

“Who in the days of his flesh, when he had offered up prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears unto him that was able to save him from death, and was heard in that he feared;”

Hebrews 5:7

Pray, lest ye enter into temptation

Jesus

Prayed

He faced the worst hour in history on His knees, and came up strengthened, ready.

The disciples

Slept

“For sorrow”, worn out with grief. Within the hour they'd run, and Peter would deny Him.

Jesus said it twice, on the way in (v. 40) and on the way out (v. 46): pray, so you don't enter into temptation. Not so you're never tempted. So that when it comes, you don't walk into it. Every man in recovery knows the difference. The time to pray is before the moment hits, not in the middle of it.

“And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil: For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.”

Matthew 6:13

With a kiss

A disciple greeted his teacher with a kiss on the cheek. It was the sign of love and respect, and Judas made it the signal for the arrest, so the soldiers would grab the right man in the dark.

“Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.”

Proverbs 27:6

Even now Jesus calls him by name and asks him a question. It's one last chance to see what he's doing. Sin always comes dressed as something friendly.

He touched his ear

John tells us the swordsman was Peter, and the servant's name was Malchus (John 18:10). Only Luke, the doctor, tells us what Jesus did next. “Suffer ye thus far”: that's enough, stop. Then He reached out and healed the man who had come to arrest Him.

It's the last healing before the cross, and it was for an enemy. In His own worst hour, Jesus was still putting back together what His followers broke. He does that still.

Your hour

Staves are clubs. They came at night with weapons, as if He were a thief, an armed robber, when He had sat teaching in the temple every day in front of them.

“This is your hour, and the power of darkness.” Evil gets its hour. But only an hour, and only because God allowed it. The darkness that night was working its way straight toward an empty tomb.

Part four

The Lord turned, and looked

Verses 54–71

Peter follows at a distance, sits by the wrong fire, and falls. Then he meets the eyes of Jesus.

It starts before the fall

  1. “I am ready”sure of himself (v. 33)
  2. Asleepinstead of praying (v. 45)
  3. “Afar off”following, but at a distance (v. 54)
  4. “Among them”warming at their fire (v. 55)
  5. “I know him not”the fall (v. 57)

Peter's denial didn't come out of nowhere. Give him credit: he followed when the others ran. But every step put a little more distance between him and Jesus, and a little less between him and the crowd.

Every relapse has steps like these: too sure of yourself, the praying stops, a little distance, the old crowd, the old fire. You can see the stairs going down long before the bottom. That's the time to turn around.

Three times

“This man was also with him.”“I know him not”A servant girl stares at his face in the firelight (v. 56).
“Thou art also of them.”“I am not”A little while later, someone else (v. 58).
“He is a Galilaean.”“I know not”About an hour on. His northern accent gave him away (v. 59).

Nobody had a sword at Peter's throat. A servant girl asked a question. The man who swung a sword at an armed crowd caved to a maid by a fire. The big moments don't usually get us. The small, ordinary ones do.

The look

Only Luke tells us this. Jesus was being held across the courtyard. The rooster crowed, and the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. Not a word. Just a look.

It wasn't an I told you so. These were the eyes of the One who had said, hours before, “I have prayed for thee.” The look broke Peter's heart, and that was the start of his healing. He went out, and wept bitterly.

Not the end of the story

Bitter tears weren't the end. On Easter day the word went round: “The Lord is risen indeed, and hath appeared to Simon” (Luke 24:34). He went looking for Peter by name. If you've fallen, don't hide from Him. Go back.

“Prophesy”

The guards turned it into a game: blindfold the prisoner, hit him, and make the “prophet” guess who did it. Blasphemously means they spoke against Him with contempt.

They didn't know that minutes before, out in the courtyard, His prophecy had just come true to the letter: “before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.” And Isaiah had seen this coming hundreds of years earlier:

“I gave my back to the smiters, and my cheeks to them that plucked off the hair: I hid not my face from shame and spitting.”

Isaiah 50:6

On trial at daybreak

The council was the Sanhedrin, the ruling court of about seventy elders, chief priests and scribes. As soon as it was light, they made it official.

“Art thou the Christ?”“ye will not believe”They weren't asking to learn. Their minds were made up.
“Art thou then the Son of God?”“Ye say that I am”A plain yes: you've said it yourselves.

Then Jesus turns the trial around. “Hereafter”, from now on, the prisoner standing in front of them will sit at the right hand of God. One day they will stand before Him.

“The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool.”

Psalm 110:1

Question 1 of 6

Betrayal

How did one of the twelve come to betray Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not all at once. Judas had been stealing from the bag for a long time, and that open door let Satan in. Then came the money and the waiting for a chance. Small, hidden sins grow if we keep feeding them.

Question 2 of 6

The Supper

What did Jesus tell us to remember when we take the bread and the cup?

What verses tell us about this?

“For as often as ye eat this bread, and drink this cup, ye do shew the Lord’s death till he come.”

1 Corinthians 11:26

Answer

Him: His body given for us and His blood shed for us, the new covenant. The Supper is a memorial. It doesn't save us; His death does. We keep it to remember Him until He comes.

Question 3 of 6

Greatness

Who is the greatest in Jesus' kingdom?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The one who serves. The world's great men rule over others and give themselves titles. Jesus, the King, took the servant's place, and calls His men to do the same.

Question 4 of 6

Restored

What did Jesus pray for Peter, and what work did He give him?

What verses tell us about this?

“But the God of all grace, who hath called us unto his eternal glory by Christ Jesus, after that ye have suffered a while, make you perfect, stablish, strengthen, settle you.”

1 Peter 5:10

Answer

That his faith would not fail. Peter fell, but he was not lost. Jesus said when he turned back, not if, and gave him a job: strengthen your brothers. He did, and he's still doing it through his letters.

Question 5 of 6

Temptation

What did Jesus tell His men to do so they would not enter into temptation?

What verses tell us about this?

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Answer

Pray. Get up and pray before the trial comes. Jesus prayed and was strengthened; the disciples slept and fell. God always makes a way of escape, and prayer is how we find it.

Question 6 of 6

The look

What did Peter do when the Lord turned and looked upon him?

What verses tell us about this?

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Answer

He remembered Jesus' words, went out, and wept bitterly. That was godly sorrow, the kind that turns a man back to God. Days later the risen Lord appeared to him and restored him.
