Part one

Plead with your mother

Verses 1–5

Hosea is preaching to Israel, the northern kingdom, about 750 BC. His broken home is God's picture of a broken nation.

The names turn around

Lo-ammi→Ammi“not my people” becomes “my people”
Lo-ruhamah→Ru-hamah“no mercy” becomes “shown mercy”

Then the scene changes to a courtroom. “Plead” means bring a case, argue it out. The children are told to confront their mother: put it away, before it's too late.

“She is not my wife” describes what she has done to the marriage, not God's last word. Verse 3 is the warning: God found Israel as a helpless newborn and clothed her (Ezekiel 16). If she keeps running, He will let her end up as bare and dry as she started.

Who feeds you?

Her “lovers” are the Baals, the gods of the Canaanites. Baal was said to send the rain and make the crops grow. His worship at the hilltop shrines included feasting, drinking, and sex.

Israel (verse 5)I will go after my lovers, that give me my bread and my water, my wool and my flax, mine oil and my drink.

The LORD (verse 8)For she did not know that I gave her corn, and wine, and oil, and multiplied her silver and gold.
she had it backward

Every addiction tells the same lie: I'm what gets you through. I give you calm, sleep, courage, friends. It takes the credit for what God gave, and then it takes everything else.

Part two

A hedge of thorns

Verses 6–13

God loves her too much to let her run without hitting a wall.

Running in circles

God builds the wall so the chase stops working. She runs after her lovers and can't catch them. She looks and can't find them. Then, finally:

“I will go and return to my first husband; for then was it better with me than now.”

“And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father’s have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father…”

Luke 15:17–18

Sometimes the thorns are mercy: a closed door, a lost job, a night in jail, the high that stops working. Not every hard thing is a punishment, but God can use the hard thing to bring a man to himself.

She took God's own gifts and spent them on Baal: His silver and gold went into idols. So God says, “I will return, and take away my corn.” They were always His.

“Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.”

Sometimes the only way a man learns who was feeding him is when the cupboard goes bare.

How she got there

  1. decked herselfearrings and jewels for the idol feasts
  2. burned incenseto the Baalim
  3. went after her loversagain and again
  4. called His gifts her wages“These are my rewards”
  5. forgat methe bottom of it all

Her feasts, new moons, and sabbaths were once God's own holy days. She kept the calendar and lost the God. The party would stop, and the vineyards would go wild.

Look at the last step. The root of all of it wasn't the jewelry or the incense. It was forgetting the LORD. A man rarely decides to walk away from God. He just stops remembering Him, one day at a time.

Part three

I will allure her

Verses 14–23

“Therefore.” After every reason to give up on her, God says He will win her back.

A door of hope

“Allure” means woo, win over. “Speak comfortably unto her” is, in Hebrew, speak to her heart. It's how a man talks to the woman he loves.

Hosea 2:14The wilderness

Away from the lovers and the noise. Alone with God, where He can speak to her heart.

Hosea 2:15The valley of Achor

“Achor” means trouble. The place of shame becomes a door.

Hosea 2:15She shall sing

Like the day she came up out of Egypt, free.

Achor was where Achan was put to death for stealing what God had forbidden, the first sin Israel was judged for in the promised land:

“And they raised over him a great heap of stones unto this day. So the LORD turned from the fierceness of his anger. Wherefore the name of that place was called, The valley of Achor, unto this day.”

Joshua 7:26

God can take the valley you're most ashamed of and hang a door in it.

Not master, but husband

Baali, “my master” → Ishi “my husband”

In Hebrew, baal was an everyday word for master or owner, and a wife could call her husband that. It was also the name of the idol. Israel had blurred the two, until the LORD sounded like one more Baal to them.

He is still Lord, but He isn't after a slave's fear. He wants a bride's love. A master wants your work. A husband wants your heart. Whatever master you used to serve, this is a different kind of love.

The bride-price

To “betroth” was to make a binding promise of marriage. The groom paid a bride-price to the bride's family. Here God pays it Himself, and He pays it in His own character:

  1. righteousnessHe does what is right
  2. judgmentHe is fair
  3. lovingkindnessloyal love that doesn't quit
  4. merciestender pity
  5. faithfulnessHe keeps His word

She was unfaithful. He pays in faithfulness. And it's “for ever.” In verse 18 the whole world is at peace for her: no bow, no sword, lying down safely.

“For I am jealous over you with godly jealousy: for I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ.”

2 Corinthians 11:2

Everything answers again

In verse 3 the land was dry and dying of thirst. Now the sky answers the ground, and the ground answers the grain, all the way down. The rain she thought came from Baal comes back from the LORD.

And “Jezreel” means God sows: “I will sow her unto me.” The last line is the wedding vow said both ways: “Thou art my people.” “Thou art my God.”

“Which in time past were not a people, but are now the people of God: which had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy.”

1 Peter 2:10

Question 1 of 6

Sin's lies

What did Israel think her lovers gave her?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Everything: food, water, clothes, oil, drink. She believed Baal kept her alive and paid her for her loyalty. It was a lie. The idol gave her nothing, and she forgot the One who gave her all of it. Whatever we lean on in place of God tells us the same lie.

Question 2 of 6

God's provision

Who really gives us what we have?

What verses tell us about this?

“Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.”

James 1:17

Answer

God. He calls it “my corn” and “my wine.” Our paycheck, our food, our next breath are His gifts. When we forget that, we start thanking the wrong things, and sometimes He lets the cupboard go bare so we remember who filled it.

Question 3 of 6

Hedged in

Why would God block a man's way?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

Answer

To bring him home. God hedged Israel in so the chase would stop working, and she would say, “I will go and return.” The thorns hurt, but they're aimed at our good. Many a man can look back and see that the wall he hit was where God turned him around.

Question 4 of 6

Hope

Where does God open a door of hope?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In the wilderness, and in the valley of trouble. Not after she cleans herself up, but right where she is, at the bottom. God speaks to her heart there, and the place of shame becomes the way out. Your valley of Achor can be a door too.

Question 5 of 6

God's love

What kind of husband does God promise to be?

What verses tell us about this?

“Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;”

Ephesians 5:25

Answer

Faithful for ever. He's not a master who wants our work, but a husband who wants our heart. He pays the bride-price Himself, in righteousness, fairness, loyal love, mercy and faithfulness. In Christ we see how far He goes: He gave Himself for His bride. And it's a good picture for us husbands too.

Question 6 of 6

Belonging

What does God say to those who were not His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“As he saith also in Osee, I will call them my people, which were not my people; and her beloved, which was not beloved. And it shall come to pass, that in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people; there shall they be called the children of the living God.”

Romans 9:25–26

“Which in time past were not a people, but are now the people of God: which had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy.”

1 Peter 2:10

Answer

“Thou art my people.” He promised it to Israel, and He will keep it. And Paul and Peter show the same door is open to anyone who believes. A man who has never belonged anywhere can hear God say, “You're Mine,” and answer, “Thou art my God.”
