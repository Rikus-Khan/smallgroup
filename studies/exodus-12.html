Exodus 12

The Passover, and the going out

Every house takes a lamb without blemish and puts its blood on the door. At midnight the LORD passes through Egypt, and where he sees the blood, he passes over. Then, after four hundred and thirty years, Israel walks out.

Part one

“When I see the blood”

Verses 1–14

The LORD tells Moses and Aaron what every house must do before the last night.

A new beginning, and four days with a lamb

God resets the calendar. From now on this month, in the spring (it is called Abib in 13:4), is “the first month of the year.” A slave nation is given a new first day.

  1. 10th daytake a lamb12:3
  2. 10th to 14thkeep it up12:6
  3. 14th, at eveningkill it12:6
  4. that nightthe LORD passes over12:12–13
  5. 15thIsrael goes outNumbers 33:3

Hope: if the year starts over for a nation of slaves, it can start over for a man whose last year went badly. God specializes in fresh starts.

A lamb for a house

  1. Genesis 22a ram, in the place of a son“God will provide himself a lamb”

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

On Moriah, Abraham said, “God will provide himself a lamb” (Genesis 22:8). Here God does: a lamb for each house. And a small household was never left out: it joined its neighbour's. Nobody was too poor or too few to have the lamb.

“Without blemish”

A blemish is a flaw or defect. The lamb had to be a male, a year old, and perfect. For four days it lived in the house where the family could look it over, so there was time to be sure.

“But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:19

“Then said Pilate to the chief priests and to the people, I find no fault in this man.”

Luke 23:4

Peter uses these very words about Jesus: a lamb “without blemish and without spot.” He was watched and questioned by his enemies in his last days, and the judge said, “I find no fault in this man.” He did not need a lamb of his own. The one who was perfect took our place, and that is why the rest of us can be forgiven.

What was on the table

  • The lamb, roastthe sacrifice: killed, and eaten by the family that was covered by its blood
  • Unleavened breadbread with no yeast, made fast, “sincerity and truth” (1 Corinthians 5:8)
  • Bitter herbsa sharp taste to remember what slavery was like

“And they made their lives bitter with hard bondage, in morter, and in brick, and in all manner of service in the field: all their service, wherein they made them serve, was with rigour.”

Exodus 1:14

The bitter herbs are the memory: this is what Egypt cost. The lamb is the rescue. They ate both on the same night. You can look back at the bitter years without being stuck in them, because there is a lamb on the table too. Both go on the plate.

Dressed for the road

  • soddenboiled
  • purtenancethe inner parts, the entrails
  • loins girdedthe long robe tied up with a belt, so a man can run
  • shoes, staffsandals on and a walking stick in hand: ready to go

Nothing left over until morning (verse 10): they were not to keep any of it for later. They were to eat it ready to stand up and go, in haste, “it is the LORD'S passover.” This was not a dinner party. They were leaving.

Real world: some of you eat your meals still dressed for Egypt, sitting down in the old place and not planning to leave. God told these people to eat the last meal in the old land with their shoes on. A man who is saved is a man on his way out.

“When I see the blood”

Look carefully at what God says. Not “when I see how good you have been.” Not “when I see how sorry you are.” When I see the blood. The family in the house was under the same sentence as everyone in Egypt, and what saved them was the blood God had told them to use, not anything in them.

“Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him.”

Romans 5:9

A man who fell last night is still under the blood this morning. If you have trusted Jesus, God looks at the door, not at your record. Don't let shame talk you out of it.

Why blood?

The word passover comes from this: the LORD “will pass over you.” The picture is of a judge walking down a street, and passing over every house with the mark. Also, “against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment.” The nine plagues had struck at what Egypt trusted: the river, the sun, the animals, the king. This night would show none of them was a god.

“And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.”

Hebrews 9:22

Remission means being let off, forgiven. From the start, God teaches that sin costs a life: the wages of sin is death, and the only way out is that something innocent takes the place. That is not a ceremony for its own sake. It points at the cross.

“A memorial”

A memorial is something that keeps a thing from being forgotten. An ordinance is a law or standing rule. God gave Israel a feast to keep, every year, so a nation would never forget the night God brought them out.

“And he took bread, and gave thanks, and brake it, and gave unto them, saying, This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me.”

Luke 22:19

“Likewise also the cup after supper, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you.”

Luke 22:20

Jesus took the Passover meal on the night before He died, and gave it a new meaning: His body, and His blood, “shed for you.” We were told to remember too. God knows men forget, so He gives us things to do that keep the rescue in front of us.

Part two

“Put away leaven”

Verses 15–28

Seven days of bread without yeast, a bunch of hyssop, and a door.

Leaven: a little goes a long way

  • cut offremoved from the people: shut out of the community

“Your glorying is not good. Know ye not that a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump?”

1 Corinthians 5:6

“Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.”

1 Corinthians 5:8

Paul reads the leaven as sin that is allowed to stay: “malice and wickedness.” A little left in the house spreads. Real world: you do not need a big sin to start sliding. You need one small thing you keep. Which little thing has been left in your house?

The door

A bunch of hyssop (a small bushy plant) dipped in a bason, which is a basin, to strike the blood on the lintel (the top beam) and the two side posts.

Each mark is the family doing what God said. They did not make the blood work. They obeyed, and stayed in.

“None of you shall go out at the door of his house until the morning.” The safety was inside, under the blood.

Safe inside, by faith

Think about that long night in the house. Nothing was visible. No sound from outside would tell them they were safe. They had a lamb, a mark on the door and a word from the LORD, and that was all. Then they waited.

“Through faith he kept the passover, and the sprinkling of blood, lest he that destroyed the firstborn should touch them.”

Hebrews 11:28

Hebrews calls it faith. The family did not feel safe, and they did not have to. They trusted what God had said about the blood. The LORD himself passed through the land, and where He saw the blood He passed over the door: He “will not suffer the destroyer to come in unto your houses” (12:23).

Assurance works the same way. A man does not have to feel saved at midnight to be saved. He trusts what God said about the blood of Jesus, and stays under it.

“What mean ye by this service?”

a child, years from nowWhat mean ye by this service? 12:26

the fatherIt is the sacrifice of the LORD’S passover, who passed over the houses of the children of Israel in Egypt, when he smote the Egyptians, and delivered our houses. 12:27

Before it had even happened, God tells them how to explain it to their children. The answer is not a lecture on rules. It is the story: the LORD delivered our houses. Then “the people bowed the head and worshipped.”

Real world: you may have a child, or a nephew, or a brother in Christ who will one day ask, “what happened to you?” You have an answer too: the Lord delivered my house. Say it out loud once today, to get used to it.

Part three

“At midnight”

Verses 29–42

The cry goes up, the king gives way, and four hundred and thirty years end in one night.

A sentence for every house, and a lamb

The text is plain and short, and we will keep it that way. It happened as God said, from the palace to the prison.

The sentenceon the firstborn

The lambwithout blemish

The firstbornsentenced, then spared
The lambno fault in it

In the houses with blood on the door, a lamb had died in the place of the firstborn son. That is the whole of it: the same sentence on every house, and for those who trusted, a substitute.

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

“Bless me also”

Pharaoh sends for Moses and Aaron by night, the same two men he swore he would never see again. He says everything he refused nine times: go, serve the LORD, take your flocks and your herds, “as ye have said.” Then he adds a line: “and bless me also.”

It is the sad last line of a proud man. He wants the blessing of the God he refused, now that the price has come. And the Egyptians themselves pressed Israel to go, “for they said, We be all dead men.”

He gave in at last, but too late to save his son. Hear it: do not wait for the night everything falls apart before you say yes to God. He is asking today, while the door is open.

Out with the dough

  • borrowedasked for, as in 11:2: they asked, and were given

The dough was not leavened yet, so they carried it, bowls bound up in cloaks on their shoulders. They were in a hurry. They also took silver, gold and clothing: wages, at last, for centuries of work.

“And also that nation, whom they shall serve, will I judge: and afterward shall they come out with great substance.”

Genesis 15:14

God had said it in the days of Abraham, and it came out exactly as He said. They left with something in their hands, because the LORD gave them favour. You will not leave empty, either.

Out of Rameses

They started at Rameses and came to Succoth, the first stop, on the road toward the Red sea. The route runs across the Sinai, through the sea at Nuweiba, and on to the mountain of God in Arabia.

About six hundred thousand men on foot, “beside children,” and with the women and children, many more. A “mixed multitude” of people who were not Israelites went with them. Victual (verse 39) means food: they had no time to prepare any.

Four hundred and thirty years

  1. about 1876 BCJacob goes downGenesis 46

Sojourning means living as a stranger in a land that is not yours. The 430 years run from Jacob's going down into Egypt to this night. God had told Abraham “four hundred years” (Genesis 15:13), a round number, and here the exact count: “even the selfsame day.”

God keeps time. Every long wait, in a cell, a hospital, or a hard marriage, is on His calendar too. He is never late, and He is never early. “It is a night to be much observed unto the LORD.”

Part four

“Neither shall ye break a bone”

Verses 43–51

The rules for the feast: who may eat, and the one thing that must not be done to the lamb.

“A bone of him shall not be broken”

The rule is short: eat it in one house, don't carry it out, and “neither shall ye break a bone thereof.” Hundreds of years passed. Then, on a hill outside Jerusalem, at the time of the Passover, two men were crucified beside Jesus, and the soldiers broke their legs to hasten their deaths.

“But when they came to Jesus, and saw that he was dead already, they brake not his legs:”

John 19:33

“For these things were done, that the scripture should be fulfilled, A bone of him shall not be broken.”

John 19:36

John says it was no accident. The soldiers did not break His legs, and the Passover rule was kept in the one place nobody planned it. And “in one house”: the lamb was eaten inside, under the blood. Paul says, “Christ our passover is sacrificed for us” (1 Corinthians 5:7).

The door open to a stranger

A foreigner passing through could not eat. But a stranger who made his home with Israel could, if he joined the covenant by being circumcised, the mark God gave Abraham's family. “And he shall be as one that is born in the land.” Homeborn means born there. “One law” for both.

Think what that says. Israel was not a club you had to be born into. The “mixed multitude” of verse 38 went out with them, and God made a way for any outsider who wanted the LORD, not just the family. One God, one law, one table.

It is the same offer now. Salvation is for everyone who believes in Jesus, whoever he is, whatever his record: “whosoever believeth in him” (John 3:16). Nobody is outside the invitation, and nobody is a second-class guest at the table.

The night that made a nation

The chapter ends the way it started: “as the LORD commanded Moses and Aaron, so did they.” Obedience was simple: take the lamb, strike the blood, stay in. Then the LORD “did bring the children of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their armies.”

“I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.”

Exodus 20:2

This is how God introduces himself when He gives the Ten Commandments: not as the God who made the heavens, but as the God who brought them out. The whole Old Testament keeps pointing back to this night. The whole New Testament points to a greater one: “Behold the Lamb of God.”

The lamb

What kind of lamb did each house take, and how long did they keep it?

Answer

A male lamb a year old, without blemish, kept from the tenth day to the fourteenth. Peter calls Christ “a lamb without blemish.”

The LORD smote every firstborn in Egypt, and passed over the houses under the blood. Same sentence on every house, and a lamb died in the firstborn's place.

Question 4 of 6

Haste and leaven

Why were they to eat in haste, and with unleavened bread?

What verses tell us about this?

“Your glorying is not good. Know ye not that a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump?”

1 Corinthians 5:6

Answer

They were leaving that night. Leaven spreads, and Paul reads it as sin allowed to stay. A saved man is on his way out of Egypt: put away even the small thing.

Question 5 of 6

Going out

How did Pharaoh and the Egyptians send Israel away?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In haste, by night, with their gold and clothing. Pharaoh, who refused nine times, begged them to go, and the Egyptians urged them to hurry. God kept His promise to the very day. Say yes to Him before everything falls apart.

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No bone of the lamb was to be broken. Jesus fulfilled it: the soldiers “brake not his legs” (John 19:33). Christ our passover is sacrificed for us.
