1 Timothy 1

The worst sinner, a pattern of mercy

An old pastor writes to a young one he raised in the faith. In the middle of his advice he stops and tells the truth about himself: “of whom I am chief.”

The first letter to Timothy

“Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ… Unto Timothy, my own son in the faith: Grace, mercy, and peace, from God our Father and Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Written from
probably Macedonia, in northern Greece: “when I went into Macedonia” (1:3)
When
about AD 62 to 64, after Paul was let go from his first imprisonment in Rome
To whom
Timothy, left in charge of the church at Ephesus, the big port city of Asia
Why
to steady a young pastor: guard the gospel, keep order in God's house, and don't let anyone look down on you

Where Timothy came from

“Then came he to Derbe and Lystra: and, behold, a certain disciple was there, named Timotheus, the son of a certain woman, which was a Jewess, and believed; but his father was a Greek: Which was well reported of by the brethren that were at Lystra and Iconium.”

Acts 16:1–2

Timothy was a young man from a small town in what is now Turkey. A believing mother, a Greek father, and a good name among the brothers. About AD 49 to 50, Paul asked him to come along.

Where 1 Timothy fits in Paul's life

  1. about AD 49 to 50Timothy joins PaulLystra, Acts 16
  2. about 53 to 56Three years at EphesusActs 19:22
  3. about 60 to 62Paul in chains at RomeTimothy by his side
  4. about 62 to 641 TimothyPaul free again; Timothy at Ephesus
  5. about 64Rome burnsNero turns on Christians
  6. about 66 to 672 TimothyPaul's last letter
  7. about 67 to 68Paul put to deathby tradition

Timothy had been at Paul's side for well over ten years by now, through the road, the riots and the prison. Few men knew Paul better.

Paul wrote this letter at a calm moment: out of prison, with a church to guard. In two years or so the persecution would turn deadly, and Timothy needed to be ready before it did.

An older man and a younger man

The older man

Paul

A former persecutor, beaten, shipwrecked and jailed. He has learned what he can't do alone and doesn't pretend otherwise.

The younger man

Timothy

Young enough that some were despising him (4:12). A believing mother and grandmother behind him. A timid streak: “God hath not given us the spirit of fear” (2 Timothy 1:7).

“But ye know the proof of him, that, as a son with the father, he hath served with me in the gospel.”

Philippians 2:22

Nobody grows up in the faith alone. Today: who is your Paul, an older man you can ask? And who is your Timothy, a newer man you can quietly look after? If you've no one for either, say so in the prayer time.

Part one

Guard the gospel

Verses 1–11

Some in Ephesus were drifting into clever talk. Paul says what the law, and the whole commandment, is really for.

A son who isn't his son

no blood between them, and a Greek father → “my own son in the faith” Paul didn't father Timothy. But he led him, taught him and trusted him, the way a father does. Timothy's own faith began with his mother and grandmother (2 Timothy 1:5); Paul took it further.

Watch the greeting. Paul usually writes “grace and peace.” To Timothy he adds mercy. Mercy is not getting the punishment you've earned. It will come up twice more in this chapter, and the second time it's about Paul himself.

Talk that goes in circles

What some were teaching

“Fables and endless genealogies”

Made-up stories and long family trees, spun into theories. Clever, never finished, and nobody's life changed by them. They “minister questions.”

What God wants built

“Godly edifying which is in faith”

Edifying means building up. Teaching that ends with a man trusting God more and living better.

Every man here knows what it is to win an argument and lose the point. Test any talk, any video, any group chat by one question: is this building me up in faith, or just keeping me busy? Paul tells Timothy to charge them to stop. Not to hate them: to stop.

What it's all for

“The end of the commandment” means its goal: love. And love has three roots. A pure heart: no double life. A good conscience: nothing hidden. Faith unfeigned: no act, the real thing.

A guilty conscience isn't God being done with you. It's a smoke alarm: loud, and doing its job. Don't rip it down. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9). That's how a conscience gets good again.

The list, plainly

The law is good when it's used the way it was meant to be used: to show what sin is. Paul lists the kinds of people it has always spoken against. It's a hard list, so here it is sorted, without dressing it up:

Against God

the lawless and disobedient, the ungodly, sinners, the unholy and profane

Against life

those who strike down or kill a father or a mother, and every manslayer

Against people

the sexually immoral of every kind, and menstealers: kidnappers who sold people as slaves

Against the truth

liars, and perjured persons, who swear to a lie

The law is a mirror. It tells a man what's wrong, and a mirror can't wash anybody. “By the law is the knowledge of sin” (Romans 3:20). If you see your own past on this list, don't look away. Look at the next page.

“And such were some of you”

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11
what the list says I was → washed, set apart, and declared right with God The law names the sin. The gospel (“the glorious gospel of the blessed God”, v. 11) deals with it. Paul puts both in the same paragraph on purpose, because the second half is what he was trusted to carry.

Past tense: such were some of you. Not such are you. Whatever you were called in the worst of it, if you're in Christ, that's the name of a man who has been washed.

Part two

Of whom I am chief

Verses 12–17

The gospel was handed to Paul. Here is the man it was handed to.

Three words for what he was

  1. A blasphemerhe spoke against Jesus and His name
  2. A persecutor“he made havock of the church… haling men and women” (Acts 8:3)
  3. Injuriousa violent, insulting man; a bully who hurt people

That's Paul's own résumé. And then the one word that turns it: “but I obtained mercy.” He never says he was decent underneath. “Because I did it ignorantly in unbelief” isn't an excuse; he still needed all of it. It only means he'd been blind, not defiant, and mercy could still reach him.

If you hurt people, if you hurt your family, Paul knows what that is. You're not too late.

Way more than enough

Where sin abounded…
…grace did much more abound

“Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound. But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound:”

Romans 5:20

“Exceeding abundant.” Paul says the grace he got was more than the sin he had. Whatever your worst looks like, the same grace is in Christ Jesus, with “faith and love” thrown in.

Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners.

“Faithful saying” means you can bet your life on it. “Worthy of all acceptation” means it deserves to be received, by everyone. That includes the man at the back of the room.

“For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.”

Luke 19:10

He didn't come to find the people who already had it together. He came for the lost. If you're one, you're exactly who He came for.

Paul keeps getting more honest

  1. “The least of the apostles”1 Corinthians 15:9, about AD 55
  2. “Less than the least of all saints”Ephesians 3:8, about AD 60
  3. “Of whom I am chief”1 Timothy 1:15, about AD 62 to 64

Read the verb: “I am chief,” not “I was.” The closer Paul walked with Jesus, the better he could see what he'd been, and the less afraid he was of it. A man who's no longer hiding can say the worst thing about himself out loud, and still be at peace.

That's not shame. Shame hides. Paul tells it, because the point of the story was never him.

A pattern, not an exception

A pattern is a model that other copies are made from. God picked the worst one first, on purpose: “that in me first Jesus Christ might shew forth all longsuffering.” If mercy worked on Paul, there is no sinner it can't reach. And that's what He wants every later believer to see.

Then Paul can't hold it in: verse 17 is a burst of worship. Mercy like that ends in thanks.

Your story is somebody's pattern

BeforeWhat I was

Paul: “a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and injurious.” Say yours plainly, in a sentence.

MercyWhat God did

Paul: “but I obtained mercy.” When did you first know He had come for you?

NowWho I'm becoming

Paul: “the grace of our Lord was exceeding abundant with faith and love.” One true thing that's different today.

Three sentences. Write them on a card today. Some man at work, or in your family, or in your own head, is sure he's the one who's too far gone. Your three sentences are the pattern for him.

Part three

War a good warfare

Verses 18–20

Grace isn't a reason to coast. Paul charges Timothy to fight, and names two men who didn't hold on.

Two things to hold onto

A good warfare isn't loud. It's holding: faith, still trusting Him, and a good conscience, keeping nothing hidden from Him. Those are the two things a ship needs to stay off the rocks.

Put away the conscience (stop listening to it, stop confessing, start hiding) and the faith soon follows. “Which some having put away concerning faith have made shipwreck.” It happens slowly, then it happens all at once.

Hymenaeus and Alexander

Two names, and a hard sentence. “Delivered unto Satan” means Paul, with the church, sent them out from its shelter, out of the circle where believers are looked after. The word that matters is “learn.”

  • a way of throwing a man awayThe aim is that they “learn not to blaspheme.” It's meant to teach, not to finish them.
  • a sign they can never returnThe same Paul was once a blasphemer and a persecutor, and mercy found him.
  • a warning, and a way backA man can wreck his testimony and his peace. The door home is still the same door.

Bible teachers explain exactly what happened to these men a little differently. What's plain: Paul names it, because it's real. Believers are kept by Christ's own hand; but a believer who drifts can still wreck his usefulness, his marriage, his peace.

Peter failed too, and was strengthened

“But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Luke 22:32
Jesus says to a man who is about to fall“I have prayed for thee”
→
and when he comes back“strengthen thy brethren”

Peter denied Jesus three times that night. Jesus had already prayed for him and already planned what he'd do next. You can fall, be put back, and then be the man who helps another man up. And if you've drifted lately, don't wait for the rocks: tell God, and one brother, today. That's a ship turning round in time.

Points to remember

  1. Love“The end of the commandment is charity out of a pure heart, and of a good conscience, and of faith unfeigned” 1:5All of God's instruction aims at love, and love grows from a clean heart, a clear conscience and a real faith.
  2. The law“The law is good, if a man use it lawfully” 1:8The law shows us what sin is. It can't wash us, but it points to the gospel that can.
  3. Mercy“Who was before a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and injurious: but I obtained mercy” 1:13Paul was one of the worst, and mercy found him. Whatever you were, you're not too far gone.
  4. A faithful saying“Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief” 1:15You can bet your life on it, and it's for everyone.
  5. A pattern“For a pattern to them which should hereafter believe on him to life everlasting” 1:16The worst sinner was made the pattern so no one would think he was beyond mercy.
  6. Warfare“Holding faith, and a good conscience” 1:19Keep trusting and keep nothing hidden. Put those away and a man drifts toward the rocks; hold them and he stays afloat.

Question 1 of 6

Love

What is the aim of the commandment, and where does it grow from?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Love. It grows from a pure heart, a good conscience and a sincere faith. If your conscience is heavy, confessing to God is how it gets cleansed again.

Question 2 of 6

The law

What is the law good for, if a man uses it lawfully?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight: for by the law is the knowledge of sin.”

Romans 3:20

Answer

It shows what sin is. The law names it, and that's its job. It doesn't save anyone, and it isn't the last word: the glorious gospel is.

Question 3 of 6

Mercy

What was Paul before Christ, and what did he obtain?

What verses tell us about this?

“As for Saul, he made havock of the church, entering into every house, and haling men and women committed them to prison.”

Acts 8:3

Answer

A blasphemer, a persecutor and a bully, and he obtained mercy. The same mercy is there for the man who has hurt people.

Question 4 of 6

A faithful saying

What is the faithful saying worthy of all acceptation?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.”

Luke 19:10

Answer

That Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners. It's worth believing and worth receiving, for everyone, and the one who said it called himself the chief of them.

Question 5 of 6

A pattern

Why did Paul say he obtained mercy?

What verses tell us about this?

“Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound. But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound:”

Romans 5:20

Answer

So that Jesus Christ could show all His patience in the worst sinner first, as a pattern for everyone who would believe after him. If he was reached, no one is out of reach.

Question 6 of 6

Warfare

What must Timothy hold to fight a good warfare and escape shipwreck?

What verses tell us about this?

“But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Luke 22:32

Answer

Faith and a good conscience. Put them away and a man drifts to the rocks like Hymenaeus and Alexander. But a man who has fallen can come back, and then help the next one.
