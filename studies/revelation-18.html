Revelation 18

Fallen in one hour

The city that sold everything, even men's souls, falls in a single hour. Her kings, merchants and sailors stand far off and weep. And a voice from heaven calls to God's people: come out of her.

In chapter 17 an angel showed John the woman with the golden cup, “MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT.” She looked rich. Her cup was poison. Teachers read her as ancient Rome, as a world system still to come, or as the world's way of life in every age.

Chapter 18 is her funeral. Heaven announces it, God's people are called out, and everyone who got rich off her stands far away and cries. Then heaven sings (chapter 19).

Part one

Fallen, fallen

Verses 1–3

Heaven doesn't say “will fall.” It says “is fallen.”

Heaven says it twice, and in the past tense: “is fallen, is fallen.” It hasn't happened yet in the story, but God speaks of it as done. That's how sure it is. Isaiah heard the same cry about old Babylon (Isaiah 21:9), and it came true.

The city that was full of light, music and money becomes a home for devils and birds that eat the dead. What looked unstoppable is a ruin.

Part two

Come out of her

Verses 4–8

“My people.” He calls them His before they take a step.

“Come out of her, my people”

God's people were living inside Babylon: working there, shopping there, used to it. The call isn't “clean yourself up so I'll take you.” He says “my people” first. You're His; now walk out, so her sins don't stick to you and her plagues don't land on you.

“Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you,”

2 Corinthians 6:17

Walking out of the old life

Jeremiah gave the same call to the Jews in old Babylon: “My people, go ye out of the midst of her, and deliver ye every man his soul” (Jeremiah 51:45). For most of us, coming out looks like this:

People

who

The ones who only call when they're using. You can love them and still not ride with them.

Places

where

The bar, the corner, the old house, the website. Take a different road home.

Things

what

The number in your phone, the stash, the app. Delete it today, not someday.

“Remember Lot’s wife.”

Luke 17:32

She got out of Sodom, and then she looked back. Walking out is one step. Not turning around is a thousand small ones. You don't have to take them alone: tell a brother which way you're walking.

“It'll never happen to me”

Babylon, in her heartI sit a queen, and am no widow, and shall see no sorrow.

GodTherefore shall her plagues come in one day, death, and mourning, and famine; and she shall be utterly burned with fire: for strong is the Lord God who judgeth her.
one day

Every man who's hit bottom once said what she said: I've got this. Nothing will happen to me. That's pride, the last thing before the fall. Old Babylon said it too: “I shall not sit as a widow” (Isaiah 47:8). “Double” means she is paid in full. God settles it; we don't have to.

Part three

Standing afar off

Verses 9–19

Everyone who got rich off her cries. Not one of them goes near.

All the ships of the sea

John's first readers knew a city like this. Grain ships came to Rome from Egypt, purple cloth from Tyre, goods from Asia: everything flowed to the capital.

Many teachers see Rome as the first picture, and a last great world market still to come, and the same greed in every age. Old Tyre, too, was mourned by sailors (Ezekiel 27). The pattern is the point: a world that runs on buying and selling, with God left out.

Twenty-six things, and then people. Right after the horses and the chariots, as if a man were one more thing to sell. That's where the world's trade always ends.

“And souls of men”

Some of you have seen this trade up close. The dealer who sells a man his own chains. The trafficker. The boss who works men like animals. The screen that sells a woman to a stranger. The greed that says a person is worth what you can get out of him.

And some of us were the merchandise. Bought and sold, used up. Babylon put a price on your soul. Here's what God paid for it:

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Gold and silver head Babylon's list. They couldn't buy you back. The blood of the Lamb did. You are not merchandise any more.

When it's all gone

Everything she wanted, everything she ran after, is gone, and “thou shalt find them no more at all.” That is where every life lived for things ends up. The prodigal son learned it in a far country:

“And when he had spent all, there arose a mighty famine in that land; and he began to be in want.”

Luke 15:14

“For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?”

Mark 8:36

But the prodigal's story didn't end in the pig pen. When it was all gone, “he came to himself” and went home, and his father ran to him. If you've lost everything, you're closer to home than you've been in years.

Three groups, three times “in one hour,” and every one stands “afar off.” They cry for what they lost: power, profit, business. Not one of them cries for what they did, and not one goes in to help her.

That's the world's friendship. When the money's gone, so are your friends from the old life. They'll miss what you were worth to them. Jesus came close, to men no one else would touch.

Two kinds of tears

The kings, merchants and sailors

The sorrow of the world

Sorry it's gone. Sorry they got caught. Dust on the head, and nothing changes. It ends in death.

A man who turns to God

Godly sorrow

Sorry for what he did, and to the One he did it against. It turns him around, and it leads to life.

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Regret can keep you weeping “afar off” for years. Don't stop there. Bring it to God: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” (1 John 1:9). That's the kind of sorrow you don't have to be sorry for.

Part four

Thrown down

Verses 20–24

A millstone, the sea, and silence.

Like a millstone into the sea

A great millstone, the kind a donkey turned, weighed hundreds of pounds. Throw one into the sea and it doesn't float back up. Nobody fishes it out.

Jeremiah did this with a stone tied to his scroll of judgment on Babylon: “cast it into the midst of Euphrates: And thou shalt say, Thus shall Babylon sink, and shall not rise” (Jeremiah 51:63–64).

Heaven rejoices

The earth weeps for Babylon; heaven sings. Not out of spite, but because God has finally put things right for everyone she crushed: the apostles, the prophets, the saints.

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

Some of you carry what was done to you: a father, a dealer, a cellmate, a system. You don't have to carry revenge too. Put it down. God will settle every account, and He won't miss one.

“Sorceries”

φαρμακεία said pharmakeia drugs, potions, spells

Our word pharmacy comes from it. In John's day it meant drugs and potions mixed with magic, used to get power over people. Galatians 5:20 calls it “witchcraft.”

Babylon didn't win the nations by argument. She drugged them, and they couldn't see straight. We know what that's like. Coming out of her means coming out of that fog: sober, awake, seeing clearly.

Two kinds of “no more”

In Babylon, no more (18:22–23)

  • music: harpers, pipers, trumpeters
  • craftsmen at their work
  • the sound of the millstone
  • the light of a candle
  • the bridegroom and the bride

With God, no more (21:4)

  • no more death
  • neither sorrow
  • nor crying
  • neither any more pain

Babylon loses everything that made life sweet: music, work, bread, light, love. Everything the world offered you, gone. Now look what God takes away from His people:

“And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.”

Revelation 21:4

Points to remember

  1. Fallen“Babylon the great is fallen, is fallen” verse 2God speaks of it as already done. What looks unstoppable is already finished.
  2. Come out“Come out of her, my people” verse 4You're His. Walk out of the old life: people, places, things. And don't look back.
  3. Pride“I sit a queen, and am no widow, and shall see no sorrow” verse 7“It'll never happen to me” is the last thing said before the fall.
  4. Her trade“slaves, and souls of men” verse 13The world's trade ends with selling people. Christ bought you back with His blood.
  5. Afar off“for in one hour so great riches is come to nought” verse 17They weep for what they lost, not for what they did. Bring your regret to God.
  6. No more“shall be heard no more at all in thee” verse 22Babylon loses every joy. God's people lose every tear.

Question 1 of 6

Fallen

How sure is Babylon's fall?

What verses tell us about this?

“And there followed another angel, saying, Babylon is fallen, is fallen, that great city, because she made all nations drink of the wine of the wrath of her fornication.”

Revelation 14:8

Answer

So sure that heaven announces it in the past tense, twice. Like a millstone thrown into the sea, she will be found no more at all.

“I sit a queen … and shall see no sorrow.” It'll never happen to me. Her plagues came in one day. God resists the proud, but He gives grace to the humble.

Question 4 of 6

Her trade

What was Babylon selling, and what was last on the list?

What verses tell us about this?

“For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?”

Mark 8:36

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers;”

1 Peter 1:18

Answer

Everything: gold, cloth, spice, food, horses. And last of all, “slaves, and souls of men.” The world sells people. Christ bought us back, not with gold, but with His own blood.

Question 5 of 6

Afar off

Why do the kings, merchants and sailors weep?

What verses tell us about this?

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Answer

For what they lost: power, riches, business. They stand far off and never repent. That's the sorrow of the world. Godly sorrow turns a man to God.

Question 6 of 6

No more

What will be heard no more, and what does God promise instead?

What verses tell us about this?

“And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.”

Revelation 21:4

Answer

Babylon's music, work, bread, light and weddings. God promises His people the opposite: no more death, sorrow, crying or pain.
