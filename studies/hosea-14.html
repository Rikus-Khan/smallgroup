Hosea 14

I will heal their backsliding

Hosea's last chapter is an invitation: come home, bring honest words instead of offerings, and find a God who loves freely.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. ReturningHow does a fallen man come back to God?
  2. ConfessionWhat words does God give us to bring?
  3. Free loveHow does God receive a backslider?

  5. WisdomWho is wise, according to Hosea?

Part one

Take with you words

Verses 1–3

Hosea warned the northern kingdom for years before Assyria came. He ends his book with an open door.

God writes the prayer

The LORD, through HoseaTake with you words, and turn to the LORD: say unto him …

IsraelTake away all iniquity, and receive us graciously: so will we render the calves of our lips.

Look who's talking. God doesn't just say come back. He hands them the words. You don't have to know how to pray. He has already written the first line.

It starts with “thou hast fallen by thine iniquity”: no excuses, no blaming. And instead of young bulls for sacrifice, they bring “the calves of our lips”: thanks and praise. No more golden calves, either.

“By him therefore let us offer the sacrifice of praise to God continually, that is, the fruit of our lips giving thanks to his name.”

Hebrews 13:15

What we won't lean on

  • “Asshur shall not save us”Asshur is Assyria: no more running to the strong man for rescue
  • “we will not ride upon horses”no more trusting our own strength
  • “Ye are our gods”no more bowing to things we made with our hands
  • “in thee the fatherless findeth mercy”the man with no one finds mercy in God

Every man has his own Asshur: the connection, the money, the bottle, the person he runs to when things fall apart. Coming back to God means naming them and letting them go.

“Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.”

Psalm 20:7

Part two

I will heal

Verses 4–8

God answers the prayer almost before they finish praying it.

Healed, and loved freely

Backsliding is treated here like a sickness, and God is the doctor. He doesn't only forgive the relapse. He heals what's behind it.

Freely means of His own free will, with no price tag. God doesn't love a backslider once he has paid enough. He loves because He chooses to. And His anger is turned away.

As the dew

In that dry land, dew kept plants alive between rains. In 13:3 Israel's goodness was “as the early dew that passeth away.” Now God says: I will be the dew.

And look what grows: a lily; roots “as Lebanon,” deep like its cedars; an olive tree that lasts for centuries.

“They that dwell under his shadow shall return”: a restored man becomes shade for others. (“The wine of Lebanon” is about a famous name and fragrance, not about drinking.)

Done with idols

Ephraim says→“What have I to do any more with idols?”Not “I'll try to cut back.” Done. The man who once kissed calves (13:2) wants nothing more to do with them.
God says→“I am like a green fir tree”An evergreen: no dry season, no bare branches. It's a rare picture: God likening Himself to a tree that gives shelter and fruit.

“From me is thy fruit found.” Every good thing in Ephraim's new life comes from God. Jesus said the same about Himself:

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.”

John 15:5

Part three

Who is wise?

Verse 9

Hosea's last word, and a look back over the whole book.

Same road, two endings

The transgressor

“shall fall therein”

He trips over the very ways of God he refused to walk in.

The just

“shall walk in them”

The road that trips the rebel is solid ground for the man who walks with God.

Hosea ends like a proverb. The wise man isn't the one who knows the most. He's the one who takes this book to heart and comes home.

“If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.”

James 1:5

Hosea, start to finish

  1. Chapters 1–3Hosea marries Gomer, loses her, and buys her back
  2. Chapters 4–7God's charge: no truth, no mercy, no knowledge of God
  3. Chapters 8–10Sowing the wind, reaping the whirlwind
  4. Chapters 11–13A Father's heart, Jacob's story, death defeated
  5. Chapter 14Return, and be healed

The whole book is in one scene. Gomer left Hosea for other men, and it took a price to bring her back. Hosea paid it:

“So I bought her to me for fifteen pieces of silver, and for an homer of barley, and an half homer of barley:”

Hosea 3:2

God did that for Israel. In Jesus, He did it for us, at a far higher price:

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Whatever your story is, it isn't too far gone. The God of Hosea goes after His people, pays what it costs, and brings them home.

Points to remember

  1. Returning“O Israel, return unto the LORD thy God” 14:1Admit the fall, turn around, and come. The door is open.
  2. Confession“Take away all iniquity, and receive us graciously” 14:2God hands us the words. Bring honesty, and bring praise.
  3. Free love“I will heal their backsliding, I will love them freely” 14:4God heals the relapse and loves without a price tag.
  4. New growth“I will be as the dew unto Israel” 14:5With God, a dried-out life puts down roots and grows again.
  5. Wisdom“The ways of the LORD are right” 14:9The wise man walks in God's ways, and comes back when he falls.

Question 1 of 5

Returning

How does a fallen man come back to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts: and let him return unto the LORD, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon.”

Isaiah 55:7

Answer

He admits he fell, by his own sin, and he turns around and comes. He doesn't have to clean himself up first or bring an offering. He brings words: honest ones. And God promises to have mercy and to pardon abundantly. Return is always the next step, no matter how far down you are.

Question 2 of 5

Confession

What words does God give us to bring?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“By him therefore let us offer the sacrifice of praise to God continually, that is, the fruit of our lips giving thanks to his name.”

Hebrews 13:15

Answer

“Take away all iniquity, and receive us graciously.” Confession and a request for grace. Then praise, “the calves of our lips,” in place of sacrifices. God wrote the prayer Himself, which tells you He means to answer it. He is faithful to forgive and cleanse.

Question 3 of 5

Free love

How does God receive a backslider?

What verses tell us about this?

“Return, ye backsliding children, and I will heal your backslidings. Behold, we come unto thee; for thou art the LORD our God.”

Jeremiah 3:22

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

He heals him, and He loves him freely. Not on probation, not after a waiting period. A believer who has slipped hasn't stopped being God's child, and God doesn't wait for him to earn his way back. He loved us at our worst, and Christ died for us while we were still sinners.

Question 4 of 5

New growth

What does life with God grow into?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.”

John 15:5

“And he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth his fruit in his season; his leaf also shall not wither; and whatsoever he doeth shall prosper.”

Psalm 1:3

Answer

Something beautiful and strong: a lily, deep roots, an olive tree, shade for other people. It doesn't come from trying harder. It comes from God, like dew every morning. Stay close to Him, and the fruit is His work in you.

Question 5 of 5

Wisdom

Who is wise, according to Hosea?

What verses tell us about this?

“If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.”

James 1:5

Answer

The man who understands what Hosea has been saying and acts on it: God's ways are right, sin destroys, and God's love brings men home. Wisdom isn't how much you know. It's walking in His ways. And if you lack it, ask Him. He gives it freely.
