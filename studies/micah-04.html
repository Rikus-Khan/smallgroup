Micah 4

Swords into plowshares

Right after the ruins of chapter 3, God shows Micah where history is headed: a mountain the nations climb to learn His ways, and a limping people made strong.

Part one

The mountain of the LORD

Verses 1–5

The hill that was going to be plowed like a field will one day stand above them all.

People flowing uphill

At the end of chapter 3, the temple hill was going to be left “as the high places of the forest,” overgrown and empty. Now Micah sees the same hill “in the last days,” raised higher than every mountain around it.

Rivers flow down. Here, people flow up. Nations who never knew the LORD come on their own, saying, “Come, let us go up.” They don't come to sightsee. They come to be taught: “he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths.”

Isaiah, who preached in Jerusalem in the same years, was given almost the same words (Isaiah 2:2–4). Two witnesses, one promise.

Weapons turned into tools

swords and spears → plowshares and pruninghooks

A plowshare is the iron blade of a plow. A pruninghook is a curved knife for trimming vines. The same metal that used to cut men down will cut furrows and tend grapes. War gets melted down into farming.

Notice who makes it happen: “he shall judge among many people.” This peace doesn't come from treaties or a stronger army. It comes when the LORD Himself rules and settles the disputes.

God does the same thing in a man. Hands that used to do harm can be beaten into hands that build, plant and feed a family. That's not a new tool. It's the same iron, reshaped by a Blacksmith who knows what He's doing.

Your own vine, your own fig tree

In Israel, sitting under your own vine and fig tree was the picture of a good life: your own place, your own harvest, shade in the afternoon, and nobody coming to take it. It happened once, briefly, under Solomon:

“And Judah and Israel dwelt safely, every man under his vine and under his fig tree, from Dan even to Beer-sheba, all the days of Solomon.”

1 Kings 4:25

Compare chapter 2, where rich men stole the poor man's field. Here every man has his own, and “none shall make them afraid.” No more looking over your shoulder.

How can we be sure? “For the mouth of the LORD of hosts hath spoken it.” “LORD of hosts” means the LORD of armies, the God who commands heaven's forces. When He says it, it's settled.

As for us

Verse 5 is the people's answer to the vision. The day isn't here yet. For now, everyone around them follows his own god. Micah's people make a decision anyway: we will walk with the LORD, starting now, and for ever.

“And if it seem evil unto you to serve the LORD, choose you this day whom ye will serve; whether the gods which your fathers served that were on the other side of the flood, or the gods of the Amorites, in whose land ye dwell: but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.”

Joshua 24:15

You don't have to wait until the world gets better to live like it will. The men around you may walk in the name of the bottle, the money, the next high. You can still say, “As for me.”

Part two

Her that halteth

Verses 6–8

God builds His strong nation out of the people who limp.

From limping to strong

“Halteth” means limps, like a lame sheep that falls behind the flock. God pictures His people as a flock: the lame, the strays that were driven off, and the ones He Himself had to correct (“her that I have afflicted”). He gathers all three.

verse 6Her that halteth

Lame, driven out, afflicted. Falling behind the flock.

verse 7A remnant

What's left after the storm, kept and gathered by God.

verse 7A strong nation

“…the LORD shall reign over them in mount Zion…”

God doesn't pick the strong ones and leave the rest. He starts with the ones who limp. That promise belongs to Israel, and He'll keep it. It also tells us exactly what kind of God we're dealing with.

“He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.”

Psalm 147:3

The tower of the flock

מִגְדַּל־עֵדֶר said Migdal Eder “tower of the flock”

Shepherds built stone towers to watch over their sheep. A place by this name stood near Bethlehem. Jacob camped there right after Rachel died on the road to Bethlehem:

“And Israel journeyed, and spread his tent beyond the tower of Edar.”

Genesis 35:21

Here it stands for Jerusalem, the watchtower over God's flock. The “first dominion” is the kingdom as it was under David, coming back. In the very next chapter Micah names the town where that Ruler would be born: Bethlehem (5:2). Matthew quotes it about Jesus.

Part three

Labour pains

Verses 9–13

Before the promise comes, the pain. But it's the pain of a birth, not a death.

Babylon, named early

When Micah said this, about 700 BC, Assyria was the giant. Babylon was a city Assyria kept under its thumb. Yet God names Babylon as the place Judah would be carried off to, and then rescued from.

  1. about 700 BCMicah names BabylonAssyria still rules
  2. 612Nineveh fallsAssyria is finished
  3. 586Judah is taken to Babylon“thou shalt go”
  4. 539Babylon falls; the Jews may go home“there… delivered”

“Behold, the days come, that all that is in thine house, and that which thy fathers have laid up in store until this day, shall be carried to Babylon: nothing shall be left, saith the LORD.”

Isaiah 39:6

Isaiah said the same thing to King Hezekiah. God told the ending more than a hundred years before it happened, and it went just as He said.

Pain that leads somewhere

“Travail” is labor in childbirth. It hurts, but it's going somewhere. And notice “there”: God would rescue them in Babylon itself. He meets us at the bottom, not after we've climbed out.

“A woman when she is in travail hath sorrow, because her hour is come: but as soon as she is delivered of the child, she remembereth no more the anguish, for joy that a man is born into the world.”

John 16:21

Who's gathering whom?

The nations→are gathered→against Zion“Let her be defiled, and let our eye look upon Zion.” They come to gloat (v. 11).
The LORD→shall gather→them as sheavesHe's the One doing the gathering, onto His threshing floor (v. 12).

A threshing floor was hard, flat ground where oxen trampled the sheaves to loosen the grain. God will make His people like an ox with a horn of iron and hoofs of brass. The enemies thought they were in charge. When the crowd gathers against you, remember who has the last word.

“For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways, saith the LORD. For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts.”

Isaiah 55:8–9

  2. God's word“He will teach us of his ways” 4:2Learning God's ways leads to walking in His paths.
  3. Peace“Swords into plowshares” 4:3Real peace comes when the LORD rules, in the world and in a man.

Question 1 of 5

God's plan

Where is history going?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.”

Revelation 11:15

Answer

Toward the LORD's reign. Empires come and go: Assyria, Babylon, and every one since. But Micah saw the end of the story: the LORD reigning, the nations learning His ways, and peace on the earth. The world isn't spinning out of control. It's headed for its King.

Question 2 of 5

God's word

What happens when people learn God's ways?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They start walking in them. God's word isn't just for knowing. It's for walking. And when people walk His way, they stop learning war. The same thing happens in a man: the more of God's Word gets into him, the less of the old fight is left in him.

Question 3 of 5

Peace

Where does real peace come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee.”

Isaiah 26:3

Answer

From the LORD ruling. The nations get peace when He judges among them, not from their own deals. A man gets peace the same way: when the LORD is in charge of his life and his mind is fixed on Him. Peace isn't the absence of trouble. It's the presence of the King.

Question 4 of 5

The broken

What does God do with the lame and the outcast?

What verses tell us about this?

“He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.”

Psalm 147:3

Answer

He gathers them first, and He makes them strong. God's strong nation is made out of limping, scattered people. If you feel like the one falling behind the flock, you're exactly who He came looking for. He heals the broken in heart and binds up their wounds.

Question 5 of 5

Suffering

Can God bring good out of pain?

What verses tell us about this?

“A woman when she is in travail hath sorrow, because her hour is come: but as soon as she is delivered of the child, she remembereth no more the anguish, for joy that a man is born into the world.”

John 16:21

Answer

Yes. God called Judah's coming pain “travail,” the pain of giving birth. It was real, and it led somewhere. He rescued them in Babylon itself. Whatever you're going through, in God's hands it can be labor, not loss. He redeems us right where we are.
