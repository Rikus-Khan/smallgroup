Numbers 2

Every man by his own standard

The LORD tells Israel where to pitch their tents: three tribes on each of four sides, and the tabernacle in the middle. Not a crowd, but a camp, with God in the midst.

Part one

Pitch by your standard

Verses 1–2

Every man knows his tribe, and every tribe knows its place.

Hard words in verse 2

  1. pitchto set up tents, to camp
  2. standardthe banner a tribe camped under
  3. ensigna sign or flag: “the ensign of their father’s house” marked each family
  4. far off aboutat a distance, all the way round on every side
  5. host, armiesthe tribe as an army, and its companies
  6. captainthe leader of a tribe's host

Moses does not say what was drawn on a standard, so we will not guess. What he does say is that every man had one, and that no man was to pitch anywhere but under his own.

The camp, seen from above

This is the picture the next verses draw, side by side. The numbers are the ones from chapter 1.

Part two

East and south

Verses 3–16

Judah leads the way, and Reuben follows.

Judah leads

Judah pitched on the east, “toward the rising of the sun”, where Moses and Aaron themselves were camped (3:38). With Issachar and Zebulun it was the biggest of the four camps, 186,400 men, and when the camp moved, Judah went first. Our Lord came out of this tribe:

“For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood.”

Hebrews 7:14

“And one of the elders saith unto me, Weep not: behold, the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailed to open the book, and to loose the seven seals thereof.”

Revelation 5:5

The tribe that went first is the tribe the King came from. Whatever a man's past, the one who walks ahead of him is Christ.

Hard words in verses 14 to 16

  1. ranka division in the line of march: first, second, third
  2. set forthstart on the march
  3. an hundred thousand and fifty and one thousand and four hundred and fifty100,000 + 50,000 + 1,000 + 450 = 151,450
  4. an hundred thousand and fourscore thousand and six thousand and four hundred100,000 + 80,000 + 6,000 + 400 = 186,400 (verse 9)

Fourscore is eighty. These old totals read as plainly as the new ones once you take them one piece at a time.

A man who lost the first place

Reuben is “Israel’s eldest son” (1:20). By birth he should have led. In the camp he is on the south and goes second, behind Judah. Moses does not say why here, and we do not need to know: look at what he still has.

  1. A standardhis own banner, like every other tribe
  2. A captainElizur the son of Shedeur
  3. A placeon the south side of the tabernacle, with Simeon and Gad beside him
  4. A part in the march“the second rank”

If you have lost a place you once had, a job, a marriage, a name for being steady, the camp is a picture of grace. The LORD does not throw a man out of the camp for losing the lead. He gives him a standard, and a place, and a part.

Part three

The midst, the west and the north

Verses 17–34

The tabernacle moves in the middle, and the rest follow in order.

God in the midst

Everything in the camp is built round this tent. Twelve tribes, four standards, and in the middle, God dwelling with his people. The LORD had said why they made it: “And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them” (Exodus 25:8). When the camp moved, the tabernacle moved, in the middle of the line.

He is still in the midst

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.”

Revelation 21:3

“For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.”

Matthew 18:20

A man in the small group, with two or three brothers in Christ, has the same promise as the camp in the wilderness. The LORD does not wait for the camp to get itself in order before he comes to dwell in the middle of it.

The line of march

Read left to right, this is how the camp moved out. Each block is as wide as the camp is large.

  1. FirstJudah186,400
  2. SecondReuben151,450
  3. In the midstTabernacleand the Levites
  4. ThirdEphraim108,100
  5. HindmostDan157,600

Add the four camps: 186,400 + 151,450 + 108,100 + 157,600 = 603,550. That is the total of chapter 1 and verse 32 here. Judah's camp goes first (verse 9), then Reuben's (verse 16), then the tabernacle (verse 17), then Ephraim's (verse 24), and Dan's last (verse 31). Hindmost means last, at the rear, and later the book calls Dan's camp “the rereward of all the camps” (10:25): the rear guard.

Someone has to walk last, guarding the back of the whole line. Nobody was left to straggle behind and nobody was left out in front alone.

Not a mob but a camp

More than 600,000 fighting men, with their wives, their children and their flocks, and no one in doubt where his tent goes or where he walks. The chapter ends the way chapter 1 did: “the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses.”

“For God is not the author of confusion, but of peace, as in all churches of the saints.”

1 Corinthians 14:33

“Let all things be done decently and in order.”

1 Corinthians 14:40

Paul wrote those lines about a church meeting, but they say who God is. A man coming out of a life of chaos does not have to invent order alone. God gives it: a place, a time, a standard to gather under, and men on every side.

Make your camp, today

You do not have a tabernacle in the middle of your tent, but you can still arrange the day round the LORD.

  1. Sinchaos is how sin lives. Name the habit that rules your mornings.
  2. Regretdo not wait for the lost years to be given back before you start to order today
  3. Gracehe dwells in the midst of a camp that has failed before. He is with men.
  4. Hopethere is a place for you, under a standard, with men on every side
  5. Todayput one fixed time with the LORD in the middle of your day, and put your brothers in Christ on either side of it: call or text one before noon

Points to remember

  1. By his own standard“Every man of the children of Israel shall pitch by his own standard” 2:2Every man had his place, and knew it.
  2. Judah on the east“on the east side toward the rising of the sun shall they of the standard of the camp of Judah pitch” 2:3Judah first, and from Judah came our Lord (Hebrews 7:14).
  3. Four camps“On the south side shall be the standard of the camp of Reuben according to their armies” 2:10Four sides, three tribes to a side, twelve tribes in all.
  4. In the midst“the tabernacle of the congregation shall set forward with the camp of the Levites in the midst of the camp” 2:17God in the middle. “There am I in the midst of them” (Matthew 18:20).
  5. The line of march“These shall first set forth.” 2:9 “They shall go hindmost with their standards.” 2:31Judah first, Dan last. Every camp had its place in the line.
  6. As commanded“And the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses” 2:34Order is God's gift, and obeying it is how a camp lives.

Question 1 of 6

By his own standard

How were the children of Israel to pitch their tents?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let all things be done decently and in order.”

1 Corinthians 14:40

Answer

Every man by his own standard, under the ensign of his father's house, round about the tabernacle at a distance. Nobody camped where he liked.

Question 2 of 6

Judah on the east

Which tribe pitched on the east, toward the rising of the sun, and who was its captain?

What verses tell us about this?

“For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood.”

Hebrews 7:14

Answer

Judah, with Issachar and Zebulun beside it. Their captain was Nahshon the son of Amminadab. Judah's camp went first, and our Lord came out of Judah.

Question 3 of 6

Four camps

How many camps were there, and how many tribes in each?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Four camps, one on each side of the tabernacle: Judah on the east (verse 3), Reuben on the south, Ephraim on the west, Dan on the north. Each had three tribes, so twelve tribes in all, and the Levites in the middle.

Question 4 of 6

In the midst

Where was the tabernacle to be when the camp set forward?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.”

Revelation 21:3

“For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.”

Matthew 18:20

Answer

In the midst of the camp, with the Levites round it. God did not stay at the back or lead from far off. He moved with his people, in the middle of them.

Question 5 of 6

The line of march

Which camp set out first, and which went hindmost?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The camp of Judah set out first, then Reuben's in the second rank, then the tabernacle, then Ephraim's in the third. The camp of Dan went hindmost. Every camp had its place in the line.

Question 6 of 6

As commanded

What did the children of Israel do?

What verses tell us about this?

“For God is not the author of confusion, but of peace, as in all churches of the saints.”

1 Corinthians 14:33

Answer

They did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses: they pitched by their standards and set forward in their families, in the order he gave. God gave the order, and they kept it.
