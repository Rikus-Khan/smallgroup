Leviticus 26

The blessing, the warning, and the remembering

Walk in his statutes and the LORD will walk among you. Walk contrary to him and the warnings come, four rounds of them, to the scattering. But even then, if they confess, he will remember his covenant, and will not cast them away.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. The first rulesWhat did the LORD forbid first, and what did he tell them to keep?
  2. The blessingsWhat would the LORD give them if they walked in his statutes?
  3. The presenceWhat did the LORD promise to do among them, and who would they be?
  4. Seven timesHow many times does the LORD say he will punish them “seven times”?
  5. The landWhat would the land do while the people were in their enemies' land?
  6. The rememberingWhat would make the LORD remember his covenant, and with whom?

Part one

“If ye walk in my statutes”

Verses 1–13

Two rules to begin, and then rain, bread, peace, and the LORD himself walking among them.

Two rules to begin

  • no idolsnothing made by hand to worship
  • graven imagea carved image
  • standing imagea pillar set up to worship at
  • image of stonea stone to bow down to
  • keep my sabbathsthe seventh day, and the seventh year of chapter 25
  • reverence my sanctuaryhonour the tabernacle with awe

Hold on to these two. The whole chapter comes down to them. Verse 30 ends with the idols, and verses 34 and 35 are about the sabbaths the land never got. The LORD begins with what comes between a man and him: other gods, and no rest.

“Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen.”

1 John 5:21

John ends his first letter with it. An idol is whatever a man worships in the place of God: it need not be carved. The men in this room have bowed to a few of them.

Five, a hundred, ten thousand

5of you chase
100of them, and of you, a hundred
10,000put to flight

The numbers are not fair, and they are not meant to be. When the LORD fights for a people, five do the work of a hundred. “The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace” (Exodus 14:14).

Notice how much of the blessing is ordinary: rain, a harvest, bread “to the full”, sleep with no one to make you afraid. And a warning for us: these are promises to Israel in their land, under the covenant at Sinai. They are not a promise that a Christian who obeys will be rich or safe.

“Yea, and all that will live godly in Christ Jesus shall suffer persecution.”

2 Timothy 3:12

Our blessing is better, and not of the same kind: the LORD himself with us, whatever the harvest.

“I will set my tabernacle among you”

The tent they stood beside is the promise. “I will set my tabernacle among you: and my soul shall not abhor you. And I will walk among you.” To abhor is to hate, to turn away from. That is the best thing in the chapter.

“I will walk among you”, now

“And what agreement hath the temple of God with idols? for ye are the temple of the living God; as God hath said, I will dwell in them, and walk in them; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.”

2 Corinthians 6:16

Paul quotes verse 12 to Christians: the LORD's “I will walk among you” is now true of the church, and of the man whose body is the temple of the Holy Ghost. He moved in for good.

The yoke broken

“bondmen”, under a yoke → “go upright” A yoke is the wooden bar that bound an ox to the plough. “The bands of your yoke” are its straps. In Egypt they bent under it. The LORD broke the straps, and they could stand up straight.

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.”

Matthew 11:28–30

Jesus takes one yoke off, and offers another: his, which is easy. A man who walks upright after years bent under the yoke of drink or debt knows exactly what verse 13 means.

Part two

“If ye will not hearken unto me”

Verses 14–39

Four rounds of warning, each one “seven times” more, ending in a scattering.

Two roads

“If ye walk in my statutes”

verses 3–13

Eleven verses of blessing: rain, bread, peace, and the LORD in the camp.

“If ye will not hearken unto me”

verses 14–39

Twenty-six verses of warning, in four rounds, each one beginning “if ye will not”.

The old words: terror is sudden fear. Consumption is a wasting sickness, and the burning ague a burning fever. “I will set my face against you” is the opposite of the priest's blessing (Numbers 6:25–26), where the LORD makes his face to shine on them. The enemy would reign over them, and they would run “when none pursueth”.

Why is the warning so long? Not because he is eager to use it. It is long because he means it, and because every round is another chance to turn, as the next verses show.

“Seven times”, four times

  1. “if ye will not yet for all this hearken”seven times more for your sins (18)
  2. “if ye walk contrary unto me”seven times more plagues (21)
  3. “if ye will not be reformed by me”yet seven times (24)
  4. “if ye will not for all this hearken”seven times for your sins (28)

Count it: “seven times” comes 4 times in this chapter. And “walk contrary”, or “walked contrary”, comes 7 times: four times of the people (verses 21, 23, 27 and 40), and three times of the LORD answering them (verses 24, 28 and 41). Each round starts with an if, with the LORD waiting to see.

Old words, and a father's patience

  • reformedset right, corrected
  • heaven as iron, earth as brassa sky that will not rain, ground like metal
  • staff of breadthe bread that holds a man up, the food supply
  • pestilencea deadly sickness
  • the quarrel of my covenantthe breaking of it (verse 25)

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

A father who warns his boy four times is not a father who hates him. The chastening is love's long patience, with the door left open.

The worst of it

“my soul shall not abhor you” (11) → “my soul shall abhor you” (30) Same words, one little “not” between the blessing and the curse.

Verse 29 is the hardest verse in the chapter, and the LORD does not soften it: a hunger so great that fathers would eat their children. The Bible says it did come to pass, in a siege of Samaria (2 Kings 6:28–29), and it is written into Jeremiah's lament (Lamentations 4:10). We read it plainly.

Verse 30 comes back to the idols and the “high places”. Carcases are dead bodies: they had given themselves to dead things, and that is where it ends. Where sin ends is never where it promised.

“I will scatter you among the heathen”

Heathen means the nations. “Waste” means ruined, empty.

The Bible tells us it came to pass, in two blows. “Israel” was carried away to Assyria when Samaria was taken, in the ninth year of Hoshea (2 Kings 17:6). Judah and Jerusalem were burned in the eleventh year of Zedekiah, and carried away to Babylon (2 Kings 25:2, 8).

The LORD said it at Sinai, long before he did it. A warning kept is a mercy: nobody could say he was not told.

The sabbaths they never kept

“And them that had escaped from the sword carried he away to Babylon; where they were servants to him and his sons until the reign of the kingdom of Persia: To fulfil the word of the LORD by the mouth of Jeremiah, until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths: for as long as she lay desolate she kept sabbath, to fulfil threescore and ten years.”

2 Chronicles 36:20–21

Chapter 25 said: the seventh year, the land rests. Here the LORD says what will happen if they never let it: he would give it the rest himself. “Sabbaths” comes 5 times in this chapter: once at the start, for the people to keep (verse 2), and four times at the end, about the land's rest (verses 34, 35 and 43). Second Chronicles says the land kept sabbath “to fulfil threescore and ten years”, the seventy years of the captivity.

Look at what the LORD is doing even here. The people who would not rest lose their land, and the land rests. The ground is not forgotten. It will be there when they come back.

The sound of a shaken leaf

A people so frightened that a rustling leaf sends them running. Faintness is weakness of heart, no courage left. To pine away is to waste away in grief, and a man can do that in his iniquity, his sin, alone.

“The wicked flee when no man pursueth: but the righteous are bold as a lion.”

Proverbs 28:1

You know this one. The man who has done wrong hears everything: a car slowing down, a phone ringing, a knock. His own guilt runs him. He flees when no man pursueth, and he is tired.

That is not where Leviticus leaves a man. And if the weight gets so heavy you cannot carry it, call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor. Do not sit alone with it.

Part three

“Then will I remember my covenant”

Verses 40–46

Even in their enemies' land, a way back.

The way back

  1. confesstheir iniquity, and their fathers'verse 40
  2. be humbleduncircumcised heartsverse 41
  3. acceptthe punishment of their iniquityverse 41
  4. then“will I remember my covenant”verse 42

“Uncircumcised hearts” means hard, shut hearts, not opened to God. Confess, be humbled, take it: three things a man can do in his enemies' land. Not make up for it, not undo it. And the LORD's answer is one sentence: then will I remember.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

The father got there first

“I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee, And am no more worthy to be called thy son: make me as one of thy hired servants. And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:18–20

The son in the far country did what verse 40 describes: he confessed, and he was humbled. And look who got there first: the father, running.

He remembers three names

  • Abrahammy covenant with Abraham will I remember
    • Isaacmy covenant with Isaac
      • Jacobmy covenant with Jacob

He names them in this order: Jacob, Isaac, Abraham (verse 42), and “I will remember the land”. His promises to those three were not made on the people's goodness, so they cannot be taken back for the people's badness. The covenant was his, and it held.

“And yet for all that”

After all that scattering, verse 44 starts with the greatest little phrase in the chapter: and yet for all that. “I will not cast them away, neither will I abhor them… for I am the LORD their God.” And Nehemiah remembered it when he prayed:

“Remember, I beseech thee, the word that thou commandedst thy servant Moses, saying, If ye transgress, I will scatter you abroad among the nations: But if ye turn unto me, and keep my commandments, and do them; though there were of you cast out unto the uttermost part of the heaven, yet will I gather them from thence, and will bring them unto the place that I have chosen to set my name there.”

Nehemiah 1:8–9

Nehemiah held the LORD to the word Moses wrote: “Remember, I beseech thee, the word that thou commandedst thy servant Moses.” The statutes and judgments and laws “which the LORD made between him and the children of Israel in mount Sinai by the hand of Moses” (verse 46) were still being prayed back to the LORD a long time afterwards, and he held to them.

Real world: the chapter for a man who has failed

  • Sin“Walk contrary” is the chapter's word for it. Not one slip: walking the other way, and the LORD answering in kind.
  • RegretYou may be living in some “enemies' land” of your own making, and you know the sound of a shaken leaf.
  • Grace“I will not cast them away, neither will I abhor them.” His covenant with Jacob, Isaac and Abraham never depended on how well they kept it.
  • Hope“If they shall confess… then will I remember.” The way back is three things: confess it, be humbled, take what comes.
  • TodaySay it out loud to the LORD, and to a brother. Then take the next small step, in the direction of his word.

He is the same LORD who said “I will walk among you” in verse 12, and who in Christ never changes his mind about the man who comes back.

Points to remember

  1. The first rules“Ye shall make you no idols nor graven image… Ye shall keep my sabbaths, and reverence my sanctuary” 26:1–2No other gods, and no ignoring his rest. The whole chapter comes down to these.
  2. The blessings“I will give you rain in due season, and the land shall yield her increase” 26:4Rain, bread, peace, no fear: covenant promises to Israel in their land.
  3. The presence“I will walk among you, and will be your God, and ye shall be my people” 26:12The best of the blessings, and Paul says it is true of the church (2 Corinthians 6:16).
  4. Seven times“I, even I, will chastise you seven times for your sins” 26:28Four times, four rounds of warning, each with a way to turn back.
  5. The land“Then shall the land enjoy her sabbaths, as long as it lieth desolate” 26:34The land got the rest the people would not give it.
  6. The remembering“If they shall confess their iniquity… Then will I remember my covenant with Jacob” 26:40, 42Confess, be humbled, and he remembers. “I will not cast them away.”

Question 1 of 6

The first rules

What did the LORD forbid first, and what did he tell them to keep?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He forbade idols, graven images, standing images, and stones to bow down to. He told them to keep his sabbaths and reverence his sanctuary. Nothing in the place of God, and his rest kept.

Question 2 of 6

The blessings

What would the LORD give them if they walked in his statutes?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Rain in its season, a harvest that runs into the next, bread to the full, peace, sleep without fear, and the sword kept out of their land. These were covenant promises to Israel in their land.

Question 3 of 6

The presence

What did the LORD promise to do among them, and who would they be?

What verses tell us about this?

“And what agreement hath the temple of God with idols? for ye are the temple of the living God; as God hath said, I will dwell in them, and walk in them; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.”

2 Corinthians 6:16

Answer

He would set his tabernacle among them and walk among them. He would be their God, and they would be his people. Paul says this is true of the church now.

Question 4 of 6

Seven times

How many times does the LORD say he will punish them “seven times”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Four times: verses 18, 21, 24 and 28. Each answers a refusal to hearken, and the way back stays open.

Question 5 of 6

The land

What would the land do while the people were in their enemies' land?

What verses tell us about this?

“And them that had escaped from the sword carried he away to Babylon; where they were servants to him and his sons until the reign of the kingdom of Persia: To fulfil the word of the LORD by the mouth of Jeremiah, until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths: for as long as she lay desolate she kept sabbath, to fulfil threescore and ten years.”

2 Chronicles 36:20–21

Answer

The land would rest and enjoy her sabbaths while it lay desolate, the rest they had never given it. The Chronicles say it lay desolate threescore and ten years.

Question 6 of 6

The remembering

What would make the LORD remember his covenant, and with whom?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Confessing their iniquity, humbling their hearts, accepting the punishment. Then he would remember his covenant with Jacob, Isaac and Abraham: “I will not cast them away” (verse 44).
