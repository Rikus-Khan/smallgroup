1 Thessalonians 4

Clean, quiet, and comforted

How to live in your own body, how to live at work, and what happens to the believers we have buried. It ends with one instruction: comfort one another.

Where we are in 1 Thessalonians

Paul planted this church in a few weeks, and a mob drove him out (Acts 17). From Athens he sent Timothy back to check on them.

Timothy brought good news to Corinth (3:6), and Paul wrote from there, about AD 50 to 51. But some believers had died. Would they miss the Lord's coming?

Part one

The will of God for your body

Verses 1–8

Paul talks to new believers in a loose city about sex, plainly. A man can learn self-control.

“What's God's will for my life?”

Men ask it about jobs and moves and who to marry. Here is one answer God gives in so many words: “this is the will of God, even your sanctification.” To be sanctified is to be set apart for God and made holy, a little more each year. Paul starts with the body.

“The Gentiles which know not God”

The lust of concupiscence

Desire that runs the man. Whatever he wants, when he wants it. Thessalonica was a port city, and that was simply how people lived.

Called of God (verse 7)

Sanctification and honour

Desire the man runs. “Abstain from fornication”: sex belongs inside marriage, and only there. That isn't a cage. It's honour.

Notice “more and more” in verse 1. These were believers Paul was proud of. Holiness isn't a line you cross once. It's a direction you keep walking.

“Know how to possess his vessel”

A vessel is a clay jar. Most Bible teachers read it here as a man's own body (we are “earthen vessels,” 2 Corinthians 4:7); some read it as taking a wife. Either way: be master of your desires, in a way that honours God and other people.

Look at the words “know how.” That's a skill, and skills are learned. Nobody is born with self-control. A man who has lived by his appetites can learn it, with God's help and honest guard rails: what's on the phone, where you go, who you're with after dark.

Nobody sins alone

“Defraud” means to cheat, to take what isn't yours. Sexual sin always takes from someone, and it ripples out.

And it isn't a rule made up by Paul: “He therefore that despiseth, despiseth not man, but God.”

But look how verse 8 ends. The same God “hath also given unto us his holy Spirit.” Every believer has the Spirit in him from the day he's saved. You don't fight this alone.

If you've already failed here

Some men read verses 3 to 8 and feel only the weight of the past. Paul wrote to the Corinthians, a city with the same reputation, about men who'd done all of it:

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Such were some of you. Past tense. What you did is real, and it hurt people. Regret is the right response, and so is making it right where you can. But it isn't who you are any more.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

If you fell yesterday, say so to God in plain words this morning, and tell one brother who will ask you about it again. Then put up the guard rail you were missing.

Part two

A quiet, honest life

Verses 9–12

Love your brothers, mind your own business, and go to work.

An ambition to be quiet

First, love: they already loved each other, and Paul says, “more and more.” Then he says “study.” His word, philotimeomai, means make it your ambition. Most men's ambition is to be noticed. Paul's is the opposite:

ONE“Be quiet”Not silent: settled. No drama, no hustle, no schemes, no one waiting for you to blow up.
TWO“Your own business”Tend what's yours. Stay out of other people's mess, and out of the gossip.
THREE“Your own hands”Honest work for an honest wage. No job is too small to do for the Lord.

Some in Thessalonica had quit working, maybe thinking the Lord would come any day (Paul has to say it again in his second letter, 2 Thessalonians 3:10–12). Paul says: He's coming, so go to work.

Your work is a testimony

Two reasons, in verse 12. “That ye may walk honestly toward them that are without”: the men on your crew who don't know Jesus are watching how you work. And “that ye may have lack of nothing”: you pay your own way, and you have something left to give.

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28

If your past was the hustle, the quick money and the deal on the side, an ordinary paycheck can feel small. It isn't. Showing up on time, doing the job right, and keeping your word is one of the clearest sermons you'll ever preach. Go to work today as if Jesus signs your check.

Part three

Them which are asleep

Verses 13–18

The funerals you've stood at, and the morning that answers them.

Grief with hope in it

Paul doesn't say don't grieve. He says don't grieve “even as others which have no hope.” Both lines go down. Only one comes back up. At the grave of His friend Lazarus, knowing He was about to raise him, “Jesus wept” (John 11:35). Tears are not a lack of faith.

Why “asleep”? Sleep ends in waking. It's the body that sleeps; the believer himself is “present with the Lord” (2 Corinthians 5:8). And because Jesus died and rose again, those who sleep in Him will rise too.

For the friends you've buried

Many of us have stood at too many funerals, for men too young: an overdose, a shooting, a man who didn't come home. Some of those deaths leave questions nobody can answer. We can't see into another man's heart, and we won't try.

“That be far from thee to do after this manner, to slay the righteous with the wicked: and that the righteous should be as the wicked, that be far from thee: Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?”

Genesis 18:25

Leave every one of them with a God who is just and full of mercy. Hold on to what is sure: for everyone who sleeps in Jesus, this is not goodbye. It's “see you in the morning.”

And don't carry grief alone. Grief that's never said out loud goes looking for something to numb it. Say the name to God, and to a brother. That's what verse 18 is for.

“Prevent” means “go before”

  • “we won't stop them”what “prevent” means now
  • “we won't go ahead of them”what it meant in 1611

That was the Thessalonians' fear: that the believers who had died would miss out, and only the living would see the Lord come. Paul says “by the word of the Lord”: the living get no head start. “The dead in Christ shall rise first.”

And notice who comes: “the Lord himself.” Not an angel sent with a message. The same Jesus they knew, the One the angels promised would “so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven” (Acts 1:11). Three sounds announce Him: a shout, the voice of the archangel, and the trump of God. No one will miss it.

Together, and for ever

First, the believers who have died are raised. The graves give them back.

Then the believers still alive are “caught up together with them.” Look at the word together. The reunion and the meeting with the Lord happen at once, and it never ends.

What is plain, and what good men debate

Where Bible teachers differ

The timing

The words “caught up” give us the word rapture (from the Latin for it). Many Baptists teach it comes before the great tribulation; other good Baptist teachers place it after. Paul gives no date and no chart here.

What the text says plainly

He is coming

The Lord Himself will come. The dead in Christ are not lost; they rise first. We'll be together with them, and with Him, for ever.

“But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only.”

Matthew 24:36

Anyone selling you a date is selling something. Hold the plain part with both hands.

Comfort one another with these words.

Paul didn't write verses 13 to 17 to win an argument about the end times. He wrote them so grieving people could hold each other up. The last word of the passage is comfort.

“And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.”

Revelation 21:4

Who do you know who is grieving? Call him today, or sit with him. You don't need a speech. Read him these six verses. Paul says they're enough: “these words.”

Points to remember

  1. Holiness“For this is the will of God, even your sanctification, that ye should abstain from fornication” 4:3God's will for you is a holy life, and He starts with the body. Keep walking that way, more and more.
  2. Self-control“That every one of you should know how to possess his vessel in sanctification and honour” 4:4Self-control is learned. Ask God for help, and put up the guard rails.
  3. Work“study to be quiet, and to do your own business, and to work with your own hands” 4:11A quiet, honest working life is a sermon the men around you can read.
  4. Grief“that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope” 4:13Grieve, but grieve with hope. Those who sleep in Jesus will wake.
  5. His coming“For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout … and the dead in Christ shall rise first” 4:16He is coming. The dead in Christ rise first, and we'll be together with Him for ever.
  6. Comfort“Wherefore comfort one another with these words” 4:18The point of it all is comfort. Find someone grieving and share these verses.

Question 1 of 6

Holiness

What is the will of God for you, according to verse 3?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

Our sanctification: to be set apart and made holy, starting with sexual purity. God has called us to holiness, not uncleanness, and in Christ we are already washed. Now we keep walking that way, more and more.

Question 2 of 6

Self-control

What does it mean to possess your vessel in sanctification and honour?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men, Teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly, in this present world;”

Titus 2:11–12

Answer

To be master of your body and its desires, honouring God and other people, not ruled by lust. "Know how" means it's learned, with the Spirit's help and honest guard rails.

Question 3 of 6

Work

What does Paul tell us to study, and why does it matter to those outside the church?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28

Answer

To make it our ambition to live quietly, mind our own business, and work with our own hands. People outside the church are watching, and an honest working life lets us walk honestly before them, pay our way, and have something to give.
