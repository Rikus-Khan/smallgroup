“In those days” skips about thirty quiet years. Jesus grew up in Nazareth and worked as a carpenter (Mark 6:3). Luke says He was “about thirty years of age” when He began (Luke 3:23).

By now Judaea was ruled directly by a Roman governor, and the people were waiting for God to do something. Then a prophet showed up in the desert.

Part one

The voice

Verses 1–6

A strange man in the desert, and the whole country walking out to hear him.

Out in the wilderness

The wilderness of Judaea is the dry, empty hill country falling from Jerusalem down to the Dead Sea and the Jordan. John 1:28 names the place: Bethabara, beyond Jordan.

Not the temple, not the city. People had to leave their comfort and walk out to hear him.

And one day Jesus Himself would walk down from Galilee to the same river (v. 13).

What “repent” means

μετάνοια said metanoia a change of mind that turns the whole man around
Turn←away fromsinNot just feeling bad: turning your back on it.
Turn→towardGod“ye turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God” (1 Thessalonians 1:9).

Why now? “The kingdom of heaven is at hand”: the King is about to appear.

Fixing the road for the King

Isaiah wrote it about 700 years before (Isaiah 40:3). Before a king traveled, men went ahead to fix the road.

“The crooked shall be made straight, and the rough places plain” (Isaiah 40:4).

John did that road work in hearts. Repenting is letting God level the pride and straighten what's crooked, so nothing stands between you and Jesus.

Dressed like Elijah

2 Kings 1:8

Matthew 3:4

“His raiment of camel’s hair, and a leathern girdle about his loins.”

The last Old Testament prophet ended with a promise: “I will send you Elijah the prophet” (Malachi 4:5). Then four hundred quiet years. Now a man in Elijah's clothes is preaching in the desert. People got the message.

Locusts were food the law allowed (Leviticus 11:22). John lived on what the desert gave. Nothing about him was for show.

The water didn't wash their sins away. It was a public sign that they had owned up and turned around. Baptism still works that way: it's a believer's step of obedience after he trusts Christ, not what saves him.

And confession is still where it starts:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Part two

Fruit and fire

Verses 7–12

The religious leaders show up, and John doesn't go easy on them.

Two parties, one warning

Strict about the law

Pharisees

Kept the law of Moses and piles of rules added to it. Popular with ordinary people. Believed in angels and the resurrection.

The priestly upper class

Sadducees

Wealthy, ran the temple, got along with Rome. “The Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, neither angel, nor spirit” (Acts 23:8).

They agreed on almost nothing, but both came to look. John calls them vipers: snakes running ahead of a brush fire. They wanted to escape the wrath, without turning from their sins.

What doesn't count, and what does

  • “We have Abraham to our father”The right family can't save you.
  • a seat on the councilA religious title can't either.
  • showing up for the baptismGoing through the motions isn't turning.
  • fruits meet for repentancea changed life that fits a changed heart

Fruit doesn't make a tree alive. It shows that it is. God could make children of Abraham out of the rocks on the riverbank (v. 9). What He's looking for is a heart that has really turned.

The fan and the floor

Grain was beaten loose on a hard, flat floor, then tossed into the wind with a fan, a wooden fork. The wheat fell back. The chaff blew away and was burned.

Jesus will do that with people. The “unquenchable fire” is real.

But nobody turns himself from chaff into wheat. Jesus came to do that: “he shall save his people from their sins” (1:21). The door is open today.

One mightier than John

John

Water

On the outside. A sign of a turned heart. “Not worthy to bear” His shoes: a slave's job, carrying his master's sandals.

Jesus

The Holy Ghost

On the inside. God's own Spirit coming to live in every believer the moment he is saved.

“For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit.”

1 Corinthians 12:13

“And with fire”: the very next verse burns the chaff, so fire here speaks of judgment, and of God's cleansing. Jesus will do what no preacher can.

Part three

My beloved Son

Verses 13–17

The sinless One steps into the line of sinners, and heaven opens.

“Comest thou to me?”

John (v. 14)“I have need to be baptized of thee, and comest thou to me?”

Jesus (v. 15)“Suffer it to be so now: for thus it becometh us to fulfil all righteousness.”

John was right: Jesus had no sins to confess. “Suffer it” means let it happen. “It becometh us” means it's fitting, it's the right thing.

Jesus came to do everything the Father asked, all of it. And the Father's plan was for His Son to stand with sinners.

At the Jordan, He stepped into our place in line. At the cross, He would step into our place all the way.

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Father, Son, and Holy Spirit

The Son comes up out of the water. The Spirit comes down like a dove and lights on Him. The Father speaks from heaven.

One God, and all three are here in one scene. The word “Trinity” isn't in the Bible, but this is what it means.

Pleased before a single miracle

Psalm 2:7, the King

… the LORD hath said unto me, Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee.

Isaiah 42:1, the Servant

Behold my servant, whom I uphold; mine elect, in whom my soul delighteth; I have put my spirit upon him.

The Father's words echo both: Jesus is the royal Son and the Servant who would suffer. And He said it before Jesus had preached a sermon or done one miracle.

Everyone who trusts Christ is brought into that love: “he hath made us accepted in the beloved” (Ephesians 1:6). You don't earn your way into the Father's favor. In Christ, you already have it.

Points to remember

  1. Repentance“Repent ye: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand” 3:2Turn from sin and toward God. Not just sorry: turned around.
  2. Fruit“Fruits meet for repentance” 3:8A changed life shows a changed heart. Fruit shows the root is alive.
  3. Religious pride“We have Abraham to our father” 3:9No family, title, or ritual can save you. Only a heart turned to God.
  4. Who Jesus is“He that cometh after me is mightier than I” 3:11John gave water. Jesus gives the Holy Ghost.
  5. Baptism“To fulfil all righteousness” 3:15Jesus stood in line with sinners, to take our place.
  6. The Father's love“My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” 3:17In Christ, believers are accepted in the Beloved.

Question 1 of 6

Repentance

What does it mean to repent?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To change your mind in a way that turns your whole life around: away from sin and toward God. It starts with honestly confessing your sins, and it clears the road for Jesus to come in.

Question 2 of 6

Fruit

How can you tell a man has really repented?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By the fruit: a life that starts to match the turned heart. Fruit doesn't make you saved, any more than apples make a tree alive. But a living tree bears fruit, and a heart that has really turned to God starts to show it.

Question 3 of 6

Religious pride

Can your family or your religion save you?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. The Pharisees and Sadducees had the best family line and the top religious jobs, and John called them vipers. God isn't impressed by who your daddy was or what church you grew up in. He looks for a heart that has turned to Him.

Question 4 of 6

Who Jesus is

Who is mightier than John, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Jesus. John was the greatest preacher of his day, and he said he wasn't fit to carry Jesus' shoes. John could put men in water. Jesus puts God's own Spirit inside every believer, and He is the Judge of all.

Question 5 of 6

Baptism

Why was Jesus baptized if He had no sin?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Answer

Not for His own sin; He had none. He did it “to fulfil all righteousness”: to do all the Father asked, and to stand in the sinners' place, the first step toward the cross.

Question 6 of 6

The Father's love

What did the Father say about His Son?

What verses tell us about this?

“To the praise of the glory of his grace, wherein he hath made us accepted in the beloved.”

Ephesians 1:6

Answer

“This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.” The Father delights in His Son. And everyone who trusts Christ is accepted in Him: loved, not for what we've done, but for whose we are.
