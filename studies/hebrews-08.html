Hebrews 8

A better covenant

The tent in the desert was only a copy. Jesus serves in the real one, under a new covenant where God writes His law on the heart and remembers our sins no more.

Chapter 8 is short, and half of it is a quote: one of the longest Old Testament passages quoted anywhere in the New Testament, from Jeremiah 31. God promised this new covenant about 600 years before Jesus came.

Part one

The real tabernacle

Verses 1–5

Our High Priest is seated in heaven, serving in the place the tent in the desert was copied from.

A priest who sits down

“This is the sum”: the main point so far. Our High Priest is “set on the right hand of the throne.”

“And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins:”

Hebrews 10:11

There was no chair in the tabernacle: the work was never done. Jesus sat down, because His work for your sins is finished.

The copy and the real

On Mount Sinai, God showed Moses a pattern, and Moses built the tabernacle exactly to it.

“And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.”

Exodus 25:8

But the tent was a model of something real: God's own presence in heaven. The priests served in “the example and shadow.” Jesus serves in the original. Once the real thing is here, nobody goes back to hugging the shadow.

Part two

A better covenant

Verses 6–9

The old agreement broke down. God promised a new one, and this time He would carry it.

Better promises: count the “I will”s

A covenant is a binding agreement. A mediator is the go-between who brings two sides together and makes it stick. Now look at who does the promising in God's new covenant, verses 8 to 12:

8×1 9  10×3 11  12×2

I will make… I will put… I will be… I will be merciful… will I remember no more. Six times, and every one of them is God. You won't find a single “ye shall” holding it up.

That's what makes the promises better. They rest on God's word, not on yours.

The fault was with us

God doesn't blame His law. He says “finding fault with them.” At Sinai, Israel answered with a confident promise:

“And all the people answered together, and said, All that the LORD hath spoken we will do. And Moses returned the words of the people unto the LORD.”

Exodus 19:8

Before long they were dancing round a golden calf. “They continued not in my covenant.” And that was after God had led them out of Egypt “by the hand”, like a father with his boy.

You know this story. “I'll never do it again.” “This time I mean it.” The old covenant ran on our promises, and our promises break. The new one runs on God's.

Part three

Written on the heart

Verses 10–13

What God promised to do inside a man, and the sins He promised to stop bringing up.

From stone to heart

The old law was cut into stone tablets: outside a man, telling him what to do, with no power to make him want to.

The new covenant: “I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts.” God changes what a man wants. That's the Holy Spirit, living in every believer from the day he's saved.

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

From the least to the greatest

the leastthe greatest

Under the old covenant, a man depended on the priest to stand between him and God. Under the new one, every believer knows the Lord himself. Not just about Him: knows Him.

The man fresh out of prison and the preacher with forty years behind him stand on the same ground. Nobody in this room is too far down the line to know God personally.

  • the time I fell after I was saved

“remember no more”

God chooses not to remember

This is the last promise of the new covenant, and the one that holds the rest up: “For I will be merciful.”

God doesn't lose His memory. He knows everything. He chooses not to bring your sins up again: not to hold them against you, not to keep them on file, not to throw them back in your face.

“I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins.”

Isaiah 43:25

For the man who can't forget

Some of you replay it every night. You may remember for the rest of your life, and some things you did still have consequences. But your memory isn't God's record.

Your memory says

“You'll never get past this.”

It plays the tape again. It says you're still that man, and that God must be keeping score.

God's word says

“Will I remember no more.”

If God has chosen not to bring it up, you don't have to keep bringing it to Him. Confess it once, and believe Him.

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

“…forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before,”

Philippians 3:13

Ready to vanish away

  1. Mosesthe tabernacle at Sinaiabout 1440 BC
  2. Jeremiahpromises a new covenantabout 600 BC
  3. Jesus“the new testament in my blood”about AD 30–33
  4. Hebrews“ready to vanish away”about AD 64–68
  5. The temple fallsRome destroys JerusalemAD 70

The moment God said “new” through Jeremiah, the old was on its way out. When Hebrews was written, priests were still offering sacrifices in the temple. A few years later, Rome burned it, and the sacrifices stopped. They have never started again.

“Likewise also the cup after supper, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you.”

Luke 22:20

What to do with this today

  1. Sin“They continued not.” Our promises break. Be honest that yours have too.
  2. RegretGod has chosen to remember it no more. Stop replaying what He has stopped bringing up.
  3. GraceSix times, “I will.” The new covenant rests on God's promises, not yours.
  4. HopeHe writes His law on your heart. You're being changed from the inside.

For the real world: the next time the old memory plays today, don't argue with it on your own. Say Hebrews 8:12 out loud: “Their sins and their iniquities will I remember no more.” Then get on with the work in front of you.

Question 1 of 6

Seated

Where is our High Priest now, and why is He sitting down?

What verses tell us about this?

“But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;”

Hebrews 10:12

Answer

At the right hand of God's throne in heaven. He is sitting because His sacrifice is finished. The old priests stood every day because their work was never done. His was done once.

Question 2 of 6

Shadow

Why does the writer call the earthly tabernacle a shadow?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because it was built from a pattern God showed Moses: a copy of the real place where God is. A shadow points to the real thing. Jesus serves in the true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, not man.

Question 3 of 6

Better

What makes the new covenant better than the old one?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It has a better Mediator, Jesus, and better promises. The old covenant broke down because the people didn't keep it. In the new one, God says “I will” again and again: He does the work and He keeps the promises.

Question 4 of 6

Hearts

Where does God write His laws under the new covenant?

What verses tell us about this?

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

Answer

In the mind and the heart. Not on stone outside a man, but inside him. God, by His Spirit, changes what a believer wants, so he begins to love what God loves.

Question 5 of 6

Know me

Who gets to know the Lord?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All of God's people, from the least to the greatest. Every believer knows God personally, not through a priest standing in between. The newest believer in the room knows Him as truly as the oldest.

Question 6 of 6

No more

What does God do with the sins He forgives?

What verses tell us about this?

“I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins.”

Isaiah 43:25

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

He chooses to remember them no more. He blots them out and removes them as far as the east is from the west. He will never hold them against a believer again.
