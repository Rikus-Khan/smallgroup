Hebrews 13

Faith with its work boots on

The last chapter. After twelve chapters on how much better Jesus is, the writer turns to Monday morning: love, prison visits, marriage, money, leaders, and a promise for every man who has ever been left.

Chapter 11 was faith, and chapter 12 was running the race without quitting. Chapter 13 is what faith looks like on an ordinary day: how you treat a brother, a stranger, a prisoner, your wife and your money.

The writer of Hebrews never gives his name. Many think Paul; others think Apollos or Barnabas. Whoever he was, he ends the way a pastor ends: with plain duties, a great blessing, and news of friends.

Part one

Love that keeps going

Verses 1–6

Brothers, strangers, prisoners, a wife, and a wallet.

Keep it going

He doesn't say start loving each other. They already did. He says “continue”: don't let it cool off. Love in a group of men usually doesn't end in a fight. It ends when men stop calling and stop showing up.

“Entertain strangers” means open your door to people you don't know. A travelling believer often had nowhere safe to sleep but a Christian's house. “Some have entertained angels unawares” is Abraham, who ran out to three travellers in the heat of the day:

“And he lift up his eyes and looked, and, lo, three men stood by him: and when he saw them, he ran to meet them from the tent door, and bowed himself toward the ground,”

Genesis 18:2

The new man in the group is a stranger. Be the one who walks over to him first.

“As bound with them”

Some of you have done time. Some of you have a brother, a son or a friend inside right now. The writer says: don't forget them. Think of them as if you were in there with them. You know that cell: the count, the noise, mail call when nothing comes.

These readers had done it before: “ye had compassion of me in my bonds” (10:34). And Jesus counts it as done to Him: “I was in prison, and ye came unto me” (Matthew 25:36).

Today: write the letter, put a little on his books, or take the call. “Them which suffer adversity” counts too: the man in the hospital, or out of work.

Marriage, said plainly

God made marriage, and He calls it “honourable”: worth honoring, for every man. “The bed undefiled” means sex is good and clean inside a marriage, between a husband and his wife. The world laughs at that. God doesn't.

The warning

“God will judge”

A whoremonger is a man who lives in sexual sin; an adulterer breaks his marriage vow. Nobody else may know. God does, and He takes it seriously.

The way back

“But ye are washed”

Paul wrote to men who had lived exactly that way, and then he wrote the word “but”.

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

If this is your past, it can be washed. If it's your present, it has to stop. Today, cut off one thing that feeds it: the app, the number, the late-night drive.

For the man who has been left

Some of you were left: by a father who never came back, a wife who had enough, friends who vanished when you got locked up.

And some of you did the leaving. Kids who grew up without you. A mother you didn't call. That one hurts worse, because it was you.

To that man, both of them, God says: I will never leave you. Not because you never left anyone. Because of who He is.

Five times “no”

In the Greek, this one short promise stacks up five words for no. English can't hold it all, so the old hymn tried: “I'll never, no never, no never forsake.”

I will not no leave thee, nor not no forsake thee

It's an old promise. God said it to Joshua, the day Moses died and Joshua had to lead alone:

“There shall not any man be able to stand before thee all the days of thy life: as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee: I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.”

Joshua 1:5

Here it is handed to every believer. That is why a saved man is safe: not because he holds on so well, but because He will not let go (John 10:28). On your worst day, He is still there. Go home with that.

Content, and not afraid

“Conversation” is the old word for your whole way of life. Covetousness is always wanting more: more money, more stuff, what the other guy has. It keeps a man restless, and restless men use.

Look at the reason he gives for being content. It isn't you have enough money. It's you have Him. A man who has God beside him has the one thing he can't lose. Then he can say, with a psalm:

“The LORD is on my side; I will not fear: what can man do unto me?”

Psalm 118:6

The parole officer, the boss, the man you owe: they matter, but they don't own you. Today, thank God for three things you already have before you ask for one more.

Part two

The same, and outside the camp

Verses 7–14

Leaders come and go. One doesn't. And He's waiting outside.

Look how they finished

“Them which have the rule over you” are the church's leaders, the men who first “spoken unto you the word of God.” Some of them had likely died by now. “Considering the end of their conversation” means: look at how their lives turned out, and how they finished.

Think of the man who first told you about Jesus, or sat with you at your lowest. Don't copy his haircut. Copy his faith. And then the writer turns, right away, to the one Leader who never dies.

Yesterday, to day, and for ever

Everything on the lower line moves. Prophets, priests and leaders all die; the temple these readers loved would be rubble within a few years; and your own life goes up and down. He doesn't.

The Jesus who forgave the thief on the cross yesterday is the same Jesus to day, this morning, in your kitchen. What He did for them He'll do for you, for ever.

Anchored by grace, not by food rules

“Carried about” is a boat with no anchor, pushed wherever the wind goes. “Divers and strange doctrines” are all kinds of odd teachings. Here it was “meats”: food rules and holy meals, as if eating the right things made a man right with God. The writer says they never “profited” the people who were busy with them.

What steadies a heart is “grace”, what God gives free in Jesus. A man who keeps adding rules to feel clean is always restless. A man who rests in grace can finally stand still.

“We have an altar”: the cross, where Jesus was offered once. The men who still served the old tent had “no right to eat” from it: you can't keep the old system and have Christ too. The Lord's Supper is not this altar; it's a memorial that points back to it.

Without the camp

On the Day of Atonement the high priest carried the blood into the holiest place. The bodies were taken outside the camp and burned, where the unclean were sent.

So Jesus, the real sin offering, died outside Jerusalem's gate: “the place where Jesus was crucified was nigh to the city” (John 19:20). Thrown out.

And the call is: go out to Him there. Leave the safe, approved place, and stand with the outcast Christ, even if it costs you some respect.

He went outside. That's where we were.

Many of you have lived on the outside: outside the family, outside the job market, outside the respectable crowd, outside the law. Jesus didn't die in the temple court. He died out there, with criminals, under shame, “that he might sanctify the people with his own blood”: to make them clean and set them apart as God's own.

“Bearing his reproach” means taking a little of His shame: being called a Jesus freak at work, losing the old crowd. That's a fair trade for a Saviour who took all of yours.

“Here have we no continuing city.” If you've lost an apartment, a house, a hometown: nothing here was going to last anyway. “We seek one to come,” the city God is building (11:10, 16). You have a home waiting.

Part three

Sacrifices that please God

Verses 15–19

No more blood on the altar. So what do we bring?

What we bring now

The old altar

Bulls, goats, blood

Brought again and again, year after year, and never finished (10:11). Jesus ended all of that with “one sacrifice for sins for ever” (10:12).

Our sacrifices

Praise, good, sharing

Not to pay for sin, which is paid. These are a thank-you: “with such sacrifices God is well pleased.”

“The fruit of our lips” is praise said out loud. The prophet Hosea told a guilty people what to bring back to God: “Take with you words… so will we render the calves of our lips” (Hosea 14:2). Words are the offering.

“To communicate” means to share what you have. A sacrifice costs something: a ride, a meal, an hour, twenty dollars. “Continually” means not only Sunday. Today, say thank you to God out loud, and do one good thing for somebody who can't pay you back.

Men who lose sleep over you

“Watch” is a word for staying awake, like a shepherd up at night with the flock. A good pastor loses sleep over his men. And he will “give account” to God for how he cared for you, so he answers to Someone too. This is no permission for a leader to bully anyone.

Many of you have spent years resisting every man in charge, and some had good reason. A godly leader is different. Let him help you. Don't make him chase you. A man who is easy to lead makes his pastor's job a joy, and the writer says that is better for you.

“Pray for us”

The man who wrote the greatest letter about Jesus' priesthood asks ordinary believers to pray for him. Leaders need it. Something was keeping him away from them for now, and he asks them to pray that he might be “restored to you the sooner.”

Look at his goal: “a good conscience… willing to live honestly” in all things. Not a perfect record. A clean conscience and a will to be honest, at work, on the time sheet, with your PO, with your wife.

Before you leave today, pray by name for the man who leads this group and for your pastor. It takes one minute, and he'll feel it.

Part four

The God of peace

Verses 20–25

A blessing big enough for a torn-up life, and a few last words.

“Make you perfect”

This word does not mean flawless. It's katartizō: to mend, to fit back together. It's the word for James and John in their boat, “mending their nets” (Matthew 4:21).

That's what the God of peace does with a torn-up life. He doesn't throw the net away. He mends it, knot by knot, until it can hold fish again.

And see whose work it is: “working in you that which is wellpleasing in his sight.” He does the mending from the inside. Your part is to stay in His hands.

One Shepherd, three titles

  1. The good shepherdat the cross“giveth his life for the sheep” John 10:11
  2. That great shepherdrisen, alive now“brought again from the dead” Hebrews 13:20
  3. The chief Shepherdcoming again“shall appear” 1 Peter 5:4

This is the only place in Hebrews that names Jesus' resurrection outright. The God of peace raised the Shepherd “through the blood of the everlasting covenant”: the new covenant, sealed in Jesus' own blood, which never runs out and never has to be signed again.

He died for the sheep, He lives for the sheep, and He's coming for the sheep. The God who did all that is called the God of peace, peace with Him, bought and paid for. A man who has that can face anything today.

Last words

“Suffer the word of exhortation”: bear with what I've said, even the hard parts. He calls thirteen chapters “few words”: few, next to the subject.

“Our brother Timothy is set at liberty.” Most take this to mean that Paul's young helper had been locked up, and now he was out. Some of you will be that good news for somebody.

“They of Italy salute you”: believers from Italy send their love. We aren't told where the writer was, but God's family stretched across the sea.

These readers were tempted to go back to what felt safe: the temple, the priests, the sacrifices. The whole letter says don't. Everything you'd go back to was only a shadow. Jesus is the real thing, and He is better than all of it.

You have your own old system to go back to: the old crowd, the old habit, the old way of fixing pain. Don't go back. You'd be trading the Shepherd for a shadow. Hold fast.

He will never leave. So hold on.

The letter that warned so hard about drifting ends with the safest promise in the Bible. The warnings shake loose the man who only sat near the faith. To the man who really belongs to Jesus they say: hold on, because He is holding you.

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

One thing for today, from this chapter: remember one man who's locked up, or one man who is new. Write him, call him, or sit by him. That is brotherly love that keeps going.

And the last word of the letter is the first thing you need every morning: “Grace be with you all.”

Points to remember

  1. Brothers in chains“Remember them that are in bonds, as bound with them” 13:3Don't forget the man inside. Write, call, visit, as if you were in there with him.
  2. Marriage“Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled” 13:4God made sex for marriage and will judge what breaks it. But any man can be washed.
  3. Never forsaken“I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee” 13:5You have Him, so you have enough. He never leaves, even the man who left.
  4. Never changing“Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever” 13:8Everything else moves. He is the same this morning as at the cross.
  5. Outside the camp“Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp” 13:13He died outside, where the outcasts are. Go out to Him, and carry a little of His shame.
  6. Made whole“Make you perfect in every good work to do his will” 13:21The God of peace mends torn men, working in you from the inside.

Question 1 of 6

Brothers in chains

Whom are we told to remember, and how?

What verses tell us about this?

“Naked, and ye clothed me: I was sick, and ye visited me: I was in prison, and ye came unto me.”

Matthew 25:36

Answer

Them that are in bonds, and those who suffer hard times. We remember them as if we were bound with them, and as if their hurt were in our own body, because it could be.

Question 2 of 6

Marriage

What does God say about marriage, and about the men who break it?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

Marriage is honourable and the marriage bed is clean. God will judge sexual sin. But a man who turns to Jesus can be washed, and start living clean today.

Question 3 of 6

Never forsaken

Why can a man be content with what he has?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because God has said, “I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.” A man who has the Lord as his helper has what he can't lose, and he doesn't have to fear what any man can do.

Question 4 of 6

Never changing

Who is the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Jesus Christ. Leaders die and the temple fell, and our lives go up and down, but He never changes. What He did for sinners then, He does now.

Question 5 of 6

Outside the camp

Where did Jesus suffer, and where are we called to go?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Outside the gate of Jerusalem, like the sin offering burned outside the camp. We are called to go out to Him there, bearing His reproach, because our lasting city is still to come.

Question 6 of 6

Made whole

What does the God of peace do in us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He makes us “perfect,” mending us like a torn net, so we can do His will. He works in us what pleases Him, through Jesus Christ, the great Shepherd He raised from the dead.

Prayer requests

What can we pray about for you today? Someone you've lost or left, a brother who's locked up, or a place where you need mending?

Amen.”
