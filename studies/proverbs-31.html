Proverbs 31

A mother's counsel and a woman of strength

A king's mother tells him how a ruler should live, then paints the picture of a woman worth more than rubies.

Part one

What his mother taught him

Verses 1–9

Stay away from what destroys kings, keep a clear head, and speak up for people who can't.

A king and his mother

Nobody knows who King Lemuel was. He isn't on any list of Israel's or Judah's kings. His name means something like “belonging to God.” Old Jewish tradition said it was a pet name for Solomon, but nobody can prove it.

What stands out is who did the teaching: his mother. She calls him “the son of my vows,” which sounds like a mother who prayed for him and promised him to God, the way Hannah did with Samuel (1 Samuel 1:11).

Lemuel's mothertaught her sonKing Lemuel
LoisgrandmotherEunicemotherTimothy

Paul said Timothy's faith “dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois, and thy mother Eunice” (2 Timothy 1:5). Some of us are here today because a mother or grandmother never stopped praying.

What destroys kings

She names two things that have taken down more strong men than any army: women (chasing one after another) and wine (the next verse).

Verse 3

Your strength

Don't pour your strength, your money, and your years into chasing women. Solomon had hundreds of wives, and they turned his heart away from God (1 Kings 11).

Verses 4–5

Your mind

Don't hand your clear head over to the bottle. A king who can't think straight can't rule.

A mother who loves her son tells him the truth about what will ruin him. That's love, not nagging.

A king needs a clear head

A king was the judge of last resort. The poor and the hurting came to him for justice. A drunk king forgets the law and lets the powerful get away with it.

One cup can tip the scales. The people who pay for it are the ones with no one else to help them.

And Revelation 1:6 says Christ “hath made us kings and priests unto God.” People depend on you staying clear. “Be sober, be vigilant” (1 Peter 5:8).

Is this telling a hurting man to drink?

These two verses get pulled out of the Bible as an excuse: “See? Even the Bible says drink when you're down.” Read them in their place, and here's what they do not say:

  • Drink to deal with a bad daynot what it says
  • Wine is God's answer for a heavy heartnot what it says
  • Permission for a man in recoverynot even close
  • Strong drink is not for the man who leadsthe king must stay clear (vv. 4–5)

The mother is drawing a contrast. If strong drink belongs anywhere, it's with the man who is “ready to perish”, a man dying and out of time. It does not belong with the man who still has a life to lead and people counting on him. That's you.

Mercy for the dying, and a cup Jesus refused

In that world there were no hospitals and no pain medicine. For a man dying in agony, or about to be executed, wine was the only thing that could dull the pain. An old Jewish writing (the Talmud) quotes this very verse to explain why condemned men were given a cup of drugged wine on the way to their death.

Now watch what happened at the cross:

“And they gave him to drink wine mingled with myrrh: but he received it not.”

Mark 15:23

Jesus was the man “ready to perish.” He was offered the cup that dulls the pain, and He said no. He went through the cross with a clear mind, feeling all of it, for us. “Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows” (Isaiah 53:4).

Forgetting isn't healing

Look at what the wine does in verse 7: it helps a man forget. That's all it can do. The poverty and the misery are still there in the morning, and usually worse. Proverbs already told us how that story ends:

“They have stricken me, shalt thou say, and I was not sick; they have beaten me, and I felt it not: when shall I awake? I will seek it yet again.”

Proverbs 23:35

If your heart is heavy today, there's a better place to take it than a bottle:

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Don't wait for the craving to win the argument. Pray, then pick up the phone and call one of the men in this room. That's not weakness. That's wisdom.

Open thy mouth for the dumb.

“The dumb” means people who can't speak for themselves: no money for a lawyer, no voice in the room. “Appointed to destruction” are people left to go under, with nobody fighting for them.

A good king's mouth isn't for drinking. It's for speaking up. This is a calling for every man here: the new guy who doesn't know how the system works, the kid with no father around, the man everybody has written off. A lot of us know what it's like to have nobody speak up for us. Now we can be that voice.

A woman of strength

In Hebrew, verses 10 to 31 are an acrostic: each verse starts with the next letter of the 22-letter alphabet, from aleph to tav. (Psalm 119 is built the same way.) It's the whole of a godly woman's character, “A to Z,” and it was easy to memorize.

This is a portrait to admire, not a checklist to beat a woman over the head with. No real woman does all of it at once, every day.

“Virtuous” is a warrior's word

חַיִל said chayil (KHAH-yil) strength, valor, ability, even “an army”

It's the same word the angel used for Gideon: “The LORD is with thee, thou mighty man of valour” (Judges 6:12). And Boaz said of Ruth, “thou art a virtuous woman” (Ruth 3:11). This isn't a soft, quiet word. It's strength of character.

Her worth is “far above rubies,” the same thing Proverbs says about wisdom itself (3:15, 8:11). And her husband can “safely trust in her.” Trust is worth more than anything she could buy.

A day in her life

Before dawnfeeds her household (v. 15)
Morningwool and flax (v. 13)
Marketfood from afar (v. 14)
Afternoonbuys a field (v. 16)
Nighther lamp still burns (v. 18)

She “worketh willingly,” or as the Hebrew puts it, with delighted hands. She isn't dragging herself through it. And “like the merchants' ships,” she goes out and brings back the best for her family.

She works hard, and she works with joy. That's a picture for anybody, man or woman.

She thinks it through

“considereth”thinks it over“buyeth it”then acts“planteth a vineyard”

She doesn't buy on impulse. She looks the field over first, then pays for it out of money she earned (“the fruit of her hands”), and then she makes it produce.

“Her candle goeth not out by night” doesn't mean she never sleeps. A lamp burning through the night was the sign of a home that was alive and well supplied, with oil to spare.

For men rebuilding their finances: think first, pay as you go, and invest in what grows.

The same hands

The distaff was a stick that held the raw wool. The spindle was a weighted stick she spun to twist the wool into thread. Hours of patient work with her hands.

Then verse 20: those same hands reach out to the poor. She works so that she has something to give.

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28

Part three

Let her works praise her

Verses 21–31

Clothed in strength, known by her kindness, and praised by the people closest to her.

Known in the gates

The city gate was where the town's leaders sat to settle disputes and do business. When Boaz needed a legal matter settled, “Then went Boaz up to the gate, and sat him down there” (Ruth 4:1).

Her husband has a good name among the elders, and part of the reason is her. A home with a trustworthy woman in it makes a man's whole life steadier.

She's also ready before trouble comes. When the snow hits, her family already has warm clothes (v. 21). She thinks ahead.

What she really wears

Proverbs 31:22

her clothing is silk and purple.

Proverbs 31:25

Strength and honour are her clothing.

She dresses well, but what people really notice is her strength and dignity. And “she shall rejoice in time to come”: she can laugh at the future instead of dreading it, because she's ready for it and she trusts God with it.

When she talks, it's wise, and “in her tongue is the law of kindness”: kindness is the rule her mouth lives by. And she doesn't eat “the bread of idleness”. She isn't living off someone else's work.

The book ends where it began

Proverbs opens with “The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge” (1:7) and closes with “a woman that feareth the LORD.” Charm fools and looks fade. A heart that honors God lasts.

And the wisdom this book points to has a name: Christ Jesus, who “of God is made unto us wisdom” (1 Corinthians 1:30).

Honoring the women in your life

Her husband says it out loud. He doesn't just think it. Maybe you're married, maybe not. But every man here has a mother, a grandmother, a sister, a daughter, a wife, or a woman at church who has stood by him.

“Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;”

Ephesians 5:25

“The elder women as mothers; the younger as sisters, with all purity.”

1 Timothy 5:2

Some of us hurt the women who loved us most. You can't undo yesterday, but you can start honoring them today: tell her thank you, keep your word, be someone she can “safely trust,” and treat every woman as a mother or a sister. Some trust takes years to rebuild. Keep showing up.

Godly character for us, too

This poem is about a woman, but almost everything in it is what God wants to grow in any man of valor:

  1. Trustworthypeople can count on him (v. 11)
  2. Works willinglyand with joy (v. 13)
  3. Gets up earlyto provide for his own (v. 15)
  4. Thinks before he spends(v. 16)
  5. Generousto the poor and needy (v. 20)
  6. Readybefore trouble comes (v. 21)
  7. Kind in his speech(v. 26)
  8. Fears the LORDthe root of it all (v. 30)

Don't read this list and feel crushed. None of this comes by willpower. It's what the Holy Spirit grows in a man who walks with Christ, one day at a time.

  1. A mother's teaching“Give not thy strength unto women” 31:3She warned him about women and wine, the two things that have ruined more strong men than any army.
  2. Strong drink“It is not for kings to drink wine” 31:4Verses 6–7 are mercy for the dying, not permission. Jesus refused the cup and carried our sorrows.

Question 1 of 6

A mother's teaching

What did Lemuel's mother warn her son to stay away from?

What verses tell us about this?

“When I call to remembrance the unfeigned faith that is in thee, which dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois, and thy mother Eunice; and I am persuaded that in thee also.”

2 Timothy 1:5

Answer

Two things that destroy kings: giving his strength to women, and drinking. Sexual sin and alcohol both steal a man's strength and cloud his judgment, and the people under him pay for it. She told him the truth because she loved him.

Question 2 of 6

Strong drink

Do verses 6 and 7 tell a hurting man to drink?

What verses tell us about this?

“And they gave him to drink wine mingled with myrrh: but he received it not.”

Mark 15:23

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

No. They contrast the king, who must stay clear-headed, with a man who is dying, for whom wine was the only painkiller there was. Wine can only help a man forget, and the trouble is still there in the morning. Jesus refused that cup and carried our sorrows. For a heavy heart, He offers real rest.

Question 3 of 6

Speaking up

Who are we supposed to open our mouths for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For people who can't speak up for themselves: the poor, the needy, and anyone left to go under with nobody fighting for them. A wise man uses his mouth to judge fairly and plead their cause, and his hands to help.

Question 4 of 6

True strength

What does “virtuous” really mean?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the angel of the LORD appeared unto him, and said unto him, The LORD is with thee, thou mighty man of valour.”

Judges 6:12

Answer

The Hebrew word means strength, valor, and ability. It's the same word used for a “mighty man of valour.” A virtuous woman, or a virtuous man, is strong in character: trustworthy, hardworking, generous, and wise. That kind of strength is worth more than rubies.

Question 5 of 6

Honor

How can we honor the women God has put in our lives?

What verses tell us about this?

“Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered.”

1 Peter 3:7

“The elder women as mothers; the younger as sisters, with all purity.”

1 Timothy 5:2

Answer

Praise them out loud. Give them credit for what they do. Be a man they can safely trust. Husbands, love your wives the way Christ loves the church. And treat every woman as a mother or a sister, with purity. Start today, even where trust has to be rebuilt.

Question 6 of 6

The fear of the LORD

What is worth more than charm and good looks?

What verses tell us about this?

“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.”

Proverbs 1:7

“But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

Answer

A heart that fears the LORD. Charm can lie and beauty fades, but God looks at the heart. Proverbs begins and ends with the fear of the LORD, because that's where all real wisdom starts, for women and for men.
