Matthew 20

The last shall be first

Workers hired at the last hour get a full day's pay. Jesus tells the twelve what waits in Jerusalem, two brothers ask for the best seats, and two blind men won't be quiet.

Part one

Labourers in the vineyard

Verses 1–16

A boss who keeps hiring all day long, and pays the latecomers first.

Peter's question

This story answers something Peter had just asked. The rich young ruler walked away from Jesus, and Peter said:

“Then answered Peter and said unto him, Behold, we have forsaken all, and followed thee; what shall we have therefore?”

Matthew 19:27

Matthew 19:30, just before

But many that are first shall be last; and the last shall be first.

Matthew 20:16, just after

So the last shall be first, and the first last: for many be called, but few chosen.

The same saying sits on both sides of the story like bookends. A householder is the owner of a house and land. A penny was a Roman silver coin, the denarius: a fair day's wage for a farm hand.

All day long, he keeps coming back

The Jews counted the day's hours from sunrise. Men who needed work stood in the marketplace and waited to be picked. By five o'clock, the men still standing there were the ones everybody had passed over.

“Because no man hath hired us.” Some of us know that feeling: the record, the gaps in the work history, the years we lost. But this owner kept going back out. He went looking for the men nobody wanted, and he had a place for them.

Every man a penny

The steward, the manager, pays from the last up to the first. The one-hour men get a full day's wage. The first men watch it happen and start counting.

They murmured, grumbled, against the goodman of the house, the master. Nobody had cheated them. They got exactly what they agreed to. What hurt was somebody else getting what he didn't earn.

Fair, and then some

to the first men

“I do thee no wrong”

He's fair. They agreed to a penny, and they got a penny. “Take that thine is.”

to the last men

“I will give unto this last”

He's generous. They got what they hadn't earned, because the owner is good.

An evil eye is an envious eye, one that goes sour when someone else is blessed. The owner's question cuts deep: Are you angry because I'm good?

Salvation isn't a paycheck. Nobody works long enough to earn it. It's a gift, the same gift, to the man who's served God fifty years and the man who came to Him last month.

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

Never too late

The plainest eleventh-hour man in the Bible hung on a cross beside Jesus. He had nothing left to give, not even one hour of work. All he had was a prayer:

“And he said unto Jesus, Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom.”

Luke 23:42

“And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.”

Luke 23:43

“Many be called, but few chosen.” The call, the invitation, goes out to many: every man in that marketplace was asked. The chosen are the ones who come when He calls, and come on His terms, grace and not wages. If you're hearing the call today, it's not too late to come.

Part two

Going up to Jerusalem

Verses 17–28

Jesus tells them plainly He is going to die. Two of them want to talk about thrones.

Up to Jerusalem

Jesus has left Galilee for the last time (19:1) and come down the far side of the Jordan. You always went up to Jerusalem: the city sits high in the hills.

This is the third time He has told them (16:21, 17:22–23), and the plainest. He takes the twelve apart and spells it out.

He wasn't walking into a trap. He was walking to the cross on purpose, eyes open.

Every word came true

  5. ✓to scourge27:26

  1. ✓the third day he shall rise again“He is not here: for he is risen, as he said.”28:6

The Gentiles here are the Romans. The Jewish council could condemn Him, but only Pilate could put Him to death. To scourge is to whip with a lash. Jesus knew every step before it happened, and He kept walking. And the last line is the one that matters most: “as he said.”

The cup

The sons are James and John, fishermen Jesus called early on (4:21). Mark says the brothers asked it themselves (Mark 10:35); here their mother asks for them.

Jesus had just said He would die, and they heard kingdom. A cup is what God hands a man to go through. His cup was the cross:

“O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt.”

Matthew 26:39

“We are able,” they said. Not yet, they weren't. But they would drink it. James was the first of the twelve to die for Him:

“And he killed James the brother of John with the sword.”

Acts 12:2

On His right and left

They pictured a throne, and themselves in the two best seats. Now look who was really there, on His right hand and His left:

“Then were there two thieves crucified with him, one on the right hand, and another on the left.”

Matthew 27:38

The way to His glory went through the cross. The honours are the Father's to give. Ours is to follow.

Greatness turned upside down

The world's wayThe princes of the Gentiles sit on top. The great ones exercise authority over everybody under them.

Jesus' wayThe great one gets underneath and holds the others up. A minister is one who serves; a servant here is a slave.

The ten were angry at James and John, likely because they wanted the same seats. Jesus doesn't scold their wish to be great. He shows them where greatness is.

In recovery we learn this the hard way: the man who helps the next man up is the strongest man in the room.

A ransom

A ransom was the price paid to set a captive or a slave free. Sin had us in chains, and we couldn't pay our own way out.

So He paid it with His own life, and took our place. “For many” doesn't mean some are left out. Paul says it plainly:

“Who gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time.”

1 Timothy 2:6

Part three

Have mercy on us

Verses 29–34

Two blind beggars by the road out of Jericho, and a crowd that wants them to shut up.

The last climb

Jericho was an oasis town of palms near the Jordan, the last stop before the climb. Passover was near, and pilgrims crowded the road. Mark names one of the two men: blind Bartimaeus (Mark 10:46).

They sat by the way side to beg. They couldn't see Jesus, but they saw who He was. “Son of David” is a King's name: the promised Messiah from David's line.

They cried the more

Two blind menHave mercy on us, O Lord, thou Son of David.

The multitudeHold your peace. rebuked

Two blind men, louderHave mercy on us, O Lord, thou Son of David!

To the crowd, they were two beggars bothering an important Man. There are always voices telling a man he's not worth Jesus' time: other people, his past, his own head. These two didn't argue. They just got louder. Cry out to Him anyway.

The same question, twice

to the mother of James and John (v. 21)

“What wilt thou?”

Seats of power, one on each side of the King.

to two blind beggars (v. 32)

“What will ye?”

“Lord, that our eyes may be opened.” Just mercy.

He was walking to His death, and still He stood still for two beggars the crowd wanted gone. He had compassion, touched their eyes, and they could see.

And then “they followed him,” straight up the road to Jerusalem. The disciples who asked for thrones were still learning. The men who asked for mercy got it, and followed.

Points to remember

  1. Grace“They received every man a penny” 20:9Salvation is a gift, not wages. It's never too late to come.
  2. Envy“Is thine eye evil, because I am good?” 20:15Don't let God's kindness to someone else make you sour. He's been more than fair with you.
  3. The cross“And the third day he shall rise again” 20:19Jesus knew every step of the cross ahead, and walked toward it on purpose.
  4. Ambition“Ye know not what ye ask” 20:22They wanted the best seats. The way to glory goes through the cross.
  5. Greatness“Whosoever will be great among you, let him be your minister” 20:26In His kingdom the great man serves, like the Son of man who gave His life a ransom.
  6. Mercy“They cried the more” 20:31Don't let anyone hush you. Jesus stands still for the man who cries for mercy.

Question 1 of 6

Grace

Why did the men hired last get a full day's pay?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the owner was good, not because they earned it. He chose to give the last the same as the first. That's grace. Salvation is God's gift, offered to whosoever will come, early or late.

Question 2 of 6

Envy

What made the first workers grumble?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They compared. They got what they agreed to, but they couldn't stand the latecomers getting the same. Their eye was evil, envious, because the owner was good. Comparing turns thanks into grumbling.

Question 3 of 6

The cross

What did Jesus tell the twelve would happen in Jerusalem?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He would be betrayed, condemned, handed to the Romans, mocked, whipped and crucified, and the third day He would rise again. Every word came true. He went to the cross knowing all of it, for us.

Question 4 of 6

Ambition

What did the mother of Zebedee's children ask for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The two best seats in His kingdom for her sons, James and John. They didn't know what they were asking. The way to His glory went through His cup, the cross, and they would share His sufferings too. The honours are the Father's to give.

Question 5 of 6

Greatness

Who is great in Jesus' kingdom?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The servant. The world's great men rule over others. In His kingdom the great man gets underneath and serves, like Jesus, who came not to be served but to serve, and to give His life a ransom.

Question 6 of 6

Mercy

What did the blind men do when the crowd told them to be quiet?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They cried out even louder. Jesus stood still, called them, had compassion, and opened their eyes. Then they followed Him. Don't let anyone hush you from crying to Jesus for mercy.
