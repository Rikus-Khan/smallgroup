Matthew 12

A greater is here

The Pharisees fight Jesus over the sabbath and say His power comes from the devil. He answers as the gentle Servant, the strong man's Master, and the head of a new family.

Part one

Lord of the sabbath

Verses 1–14

A handful of grain, a withered hand, and a plot.

Picking grain on the sabbath

“Corn” here means grain, wheat or barley. “An hungred” means hungry. The sabbath was Saturday, the seventh day, the day of rest God gave Israel (Exodus 20:8–11).

What God's law said

Help yourself

“When thou comest into the standing corn of thy neighbour, then thou mayest pluck the ears with thine hand; but thou shalt not move a sickle unto thy neighbour’s standing corn.” (Deuteronomy 23:25)

What the Pharisees added

That's harvesting

Over the years their teachers had built a fence of rules around the sabbath. To them, picking grain was reaping, and rubbing it in your hands was threshing: work.

The disciples didn't break God's law. They broke the Pharisees' rules about it.

David and the holy bread

loaves on the golden table, one for each tribe, fresh every sabbath

The shewbread sat before the LORD in the tabernacle all week. Then the old loaves were for the priests alone (Leviticus 24:5–9). When David was running from Saul, starving, the priest gave it to him and his men:

“So the priest gave him hallowed bread: for there was no bread there but the shewbread, that was taken from before the LORD, to put hot bread in the day when it was taken away.”

1 Samuel 21:6

The Pharisees honored David. God didn't condemn him for feeding hungry men with holy bread. Meeting a real need was what the law was for.

One greater than the temple

On the sabbath the priests worked harder than any day of the week: double offerings to kill, cut, and burn (Numbers 28:9–10). Nobody called that breaking the sabbath. Temple work came first.

Then Jesus says something no rabbi would dare. The temple was the house where God met His people. “In this place is one greater than the temple.” If the temple's service came before the sabbath rules, how much more the service of the One the temple pointed to?

Mercy, and not sacrifice

Hosea 6:6

For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings.

Matthew 12:7

But if ye had known what this meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice, ye would not have condemned the guiltless.

This is the second time Jesus has quoted Hosea to the Pharisees (9:13). They were careful with every rule and hard on every person. God never wanted religion without a heart.

“Lord even of the sabbath”: the One who made the day of rest gets to say what it's for. Some of us came out of churches that were all rules and no mercy. Others never had a rule in our lives. Jesus is neither. He is Lord, and He is merciful.

Better than a sheep

They weren't asking to learn. They asked “that they might accuse him.” The man with the withered hand was bait.

if your one sheep“fall into a pit on the sabbath day”
→
you“lay hold on it, and lift it out”
if a man“is … better than a sheep”
→
then“it is lawful to do well on the sabbath days”

Every farmer in the room would pull his sheep out of a hole on the sabbath. A man is worth more than a sheep. They had it backwards: they cared more about their rules than about the man standing in front of them.

Two kinds of sabbath work

Jesus, on the sabbath

restored a hand

“it was restored whole, like as the other”

The Pharisees, on the sabbath

plotted a death

“how they might destroy him”

Look at what Jesus asked: “Stretch forth thine hand.” That's the one thing the man couldn't do. He tried anyway, because Jesus said so, and the power came as he obeyed.

Maybe something in your life is withered: a marriage, a trust, your work, your hope. He still says, “Stretch it out to Me.”

Part two

Behold my servant

Verses 15–21

No shouting, no showing off, and no one too broken.

Isaiah saw Him coming

  1. about 700 B.C.Isaiah: “Behold my servant”Isaiah 42:1–4
  2. His baptism“in whom I am well pleased”3:17
  3. hereHe heals them all, quietly12:15–16
  4. after He rose“teach all nations”28:19
  5. todayGentiles trust His name12:21

“Behold my servant, whom I uphold; mine elect, in whom my soul delighteth; I have put my spirit upon him: he shall bring forth judgment to the Gentiles.”

Isaiah 42:1

The Pharisees were plotting murder. Jesus didn't fight back or stage a show of power. He slipped away and kept healing. “He shall not strive, nor cry”: the promised King came as a quiet servant. And “judgment” here means justice, setting things right, even for Gentiles like most of us.

He won't finish you off

A reed grew by the water. Once it was bruised and bent, it was no good to anybody, and a man would snap it and throw it away. Flax was the wick in a clay oil lamp. When it only smoked, you pinched it out.

Jesus does the opposite. He doesn't snap the man who's bent over, or pinch out the faith that's down to a little smoke. He straightens and binds and breathes on the wick until it burns again.

Part three

By what power?

Verses 22–37

A divided house, a strong man bound, and words that come from the heart.

Two verdicts on the same miracle

A man who was blind, unable to speak, and held by a devil was set free in a moment. Everybody saw it. Nobody could deny it. So the only question left was where the power came from.

The peopleIs not this the son of David?
the promised King?

The PhariseesThis fellow doth not cast out devils, but by Beelzebub the prince of the devils.
the devil's man

“Son of David” was a name for the Messiah: God had promised David a son whose throne would last forever (2 Samuel 7:12–13). Beelzebub was a name for Satan (10:25). The Pharisees couldn't argue with the miracle, so they attacked the Man.

Their charge doesn't add up

if“I by Beelzebub cast out devils”
→

No army fights itself and wins. Jesus was tearing down Satan's work, not building it. And that left only one answer: God's kingdom had come, and its King was standing in front of them.

The strong man bound

the strong manis Satan, guardinghis housefull ofmen he holds captive
Jesuswalks in,binds himand carries offhis goods: us

“Spoil” means to plunder, to carry off the goods. Every devil Jesus cast out was proof the strong man had met someone stronger.

“He that committeth sin is of the devil; for the devil sinneth from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil.”

1 John 3:8

Whatever held you, the bottle, the pills, the rage, the shame, it's strong. He is stronger. And there's no neutral ground: “He that is not with me is against me.”

The sin that won't be forgiven

Can God forgive what I've done?Yes“All manner of sin and blasphemy.” Even words against Jesus Himself (v. 32). Paul once called himself “a blasphemer” (1 Timothy 1:13), and he was forgiven.
Then what is the one that isn't?ThisLook at verse 24. The Holy Spirit showed them plainly that Jesus is from God, and they called the Spirit's work the devil's. It's a heart that sees the light and settles for good against it, so it never comes to Him to be forgiven.
Could I have done it, if I'm worried that I have?NoThe man who did it doesn't care. If you're troubled about it, the Spirit is still dealing with you. Come to Jesus, and He won't turn you away (John 6:37).

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

The mouth shows the heart

v. 35A good manout of the good treasure of the heartbringeth forth good things:
the manwhat's stored insidewhat comes out
andan evil manout of the evil treasurebringeth forth evil things.

A “generation of vipers” is a nest of snakes. A “treasure” is a storehouse. Whatever is stored up comes out when you're squeezed: angry, drunk, tired, cornered. An “idle word” is a careless one, said without thinking. God hears those too.

You can't fix a bad tree by stapling good fruit on it. Cleaner language isn't the cure. A new heart is, and only God gives that.

Part four

A sign, a house, a family

Verses 38–50

Jonas, an empty house, and the people Jesus calls His own.

The sign of Jonas

They had just watched a blind, mute man made whole, and they asked for a sign. Jesus gives them one more, the biggest of all: He would be buried, and He would come back.

“Now the LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.”

Jonah 1:17

Jonas is the Greek form of Jonah. In Jewish counting, any part of a day was counted as a day. Jesus was buried on Friday before sundown and rose early Sunday: “the third day” (16:21).

Something greater is here

  • greater than the templewhere the priests served (12:6)
  • greater than Jonasthe prophet who preached (12:41)
  • greater than Solomonthe wisest king (12:42)

Priest, prophet, king: Jesus is greater than every one. Nineveh, Assyria's capital, was the enemy of Israel, and it repented at one sermon from a reluctant prophet (Jonah 3:5). The queen of the south, the queen of Sheba, traveled well over a thousand miles to hear Solomon (1 Kings 10:1).

Pagans crossed deserts and repented at a stranger's preaching. These men had the Son of God in their own synagogue and wanted more proof. On judgment day the pagans will testify against them.

The house was cleaned up but left empty. The old tenant came back, and he brought seven worse. Jesus said this about His nation: they'd tidied up under John's preaching but never let the King move in.

Most of us know this from the inside. Getting the bottle out of the house isn't the whole job. An empty, swept life gets refilled with something, often worse. The answer is a house that's occupied: Christ living in you by His Spirit.

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

A new family

  1. herestanding outside12:46
  2. worried“He is beside himself”Mark 3:21
  3. not yet“neither did his brethren believe”John 7:5
  4. risen“he was seen of James”1 Corinthians 15:7
  5. insidepraying with the disciplesActs 1:14

His brethren were His younger brothers, sons of Mary and Joseph: James, Joses, Simon, and Judas (13:55). Mark tells us His own people thought He was “beside himself” (Mark 3:21). Jesus wasn't disowning His mother. He was opening the door wider.

“These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brethren.”

Acts 1:14

Some of us have lost family, or burned those bridges. In Christ there's a family for “whosoever.” Look around this room.

Points to remember

  1. Mercy“I will have mercy, and not sacrifice” 12:7God wants a heart of mercy more than a rulebook kept to the letter.
  2. Gentle“A bruised reed shall he not break” 12:20He doesn't finish off the broken. He binds them up and fans the flame.
  3. Power“Except he first bind the strong man” 12:29Satan is strong. Jesus is stronger, and He sets the captives free.
  4. Forgiven“All manner of sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven” 12:31Any sin can be forgiven to the man who comes. Only the heart that refuses Him for good stays unforgiven.
  5. Filled“He findeth it empty, swept, and garnished” 12:44Cleaning up isn't enough. Let Christ move in and fill the house.
  6. Family“The same is my brother, and sister, and mother” 12:50Whoever does the Father's will belongs to Jesus' family.

Question 1 of 6

Mercy

What does Jesus care about more than rule-keeping?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Mercy: real people with real needs. Hungry men, a crippled hand, a man worth more than a sheep. The Pharisees kept every rule and had no mercy. Jesus is Lord of the sabbath, and He used it to do good.

Question 2 of 6

Gentle

How does Jesus treat the bruised and the weak?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Gently. He healed them all, without a show. He won't snap a man who's bent and cracked, or pinch out faith that's down to smoke. He binds up and breathes on it until it burns again. Anybody, Gentiles included, can trust in His name.

Question 3 of 6

Power

Who is stronger, Jesus or Satan?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that committeth sin is of the devil; for the devil sinneth from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil.”

1 John 3:8

Answer

Jesus. Satan is the strong man, but Jesus walks into his house, binds him, and carries off the men he held. Every devil cast out proved it, and the cross and the empty tomb finished it. Whatever has a grip on you, He is stronger.

Question 4 of 6

Forgiven

What is the sin that shall not be forgiven?

What verses tell us about this?

“All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.”

John 6:37

Answer

Blasphemy against the Holy Ghost: seeing the Spirit's work in Jesus and calling it the devil's, a heart settled against Him for good. Every other sin can be forgiven. If you're worried you've done it, you haven't. The Spirit is still drawing you, and Jesus turns no one away who comes.

Question 5 of 6

Filled

Why isn't a life swept clean enough?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

Answer

Because an empty house doesn't stay empty. Quitting the old thing is good, but if nothing moves in, the old thing comes back with friends. The answer is to be filled: Christ living in us by His Spirit, and our days filled with Him and His people.

Question 6 of 6

Family

Who belongs to Jesus' family?

What verses tell us about this?

“These all continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brethren.”

Acts 1:14

Answer

Whosoever does the will of the Father: all who come to Jesus and follow Him. His own brothers didn't believe at first, and later they did and joined the family of faith. Nobody is too far outside to be brought in.
