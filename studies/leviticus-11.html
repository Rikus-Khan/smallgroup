Leviticus 11

Clean and unclean

The LORD tells Israel what they may eat and what they may not, on land, in the water and in the air, and what makes a man unclean until the evening. Then he gives the reason: “ye shall be holy; for I am holy.”

Why these and not those?

People have offered all kinds of reasons: health, hygiene, the habits of the animals. Moses gives none of them. He gives one reason, and he saves it for the end of the chapter:

God drew a line through the animal world and said, “this side is yours.” Israel did not have to understand every line to obey it. At every meal they were reminded whose they were. That is the point we will keep coming back to.

Everything else in the waters was “an abomination unto you.” An abomination is a thing to be detested, turned from. The word comes ten times in this chapter. Here it is not about cruelty or crime; it means “not for you, keep away.” Carcases are dead bodies.

Notice who made the line. Israel didn't vote on it, and the fishermen didn't either. A people who belong to God take his word on what is clean.

For the birds there is no rule, only names: twenty of them, and the LORD's word on each. Many are hunters and eaters of the dead. But the reason given is still the one at the end: whose people they were.

Legs to leap with

Creeping things with wings, “going upon all four,” were unclean. But some had “legs above their feet, to leap withal”: the locust, the bald locust, the beetle and the grasshopper. Those they could eat. (Withal here means with.)

“And the same John had his raiment of camel’s hair, and a leathern girdle about his loins; and his meat was locusts and wild honey.”

Matthew 3:4

John the Baptist lived on a clean food from Leviticus 11. A man of the wilderness, living by the law of Moses, getting the way ready for the Lamb of God.

Part two

“Unclean until the even”

Verses 24–40

Touching the dead, and what it does to the things around you.

Until the evening, and then clean

Even is evening. Ought (verse 25) means anything at all. Nine times this chapter says “until the even.” Uncleanness shut a man out of the holy things for a while, but it had an end. He washed, the sun went down, and he was clean.

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

An earthen pot soaks it in. It was broken, and so were the oven and the ranges for pots, a cooking hearth with places for pots.

“A fountain or pit, wherein there is plenty of water, shall be clean.” A dead thing could fall in, and the water stayed clean.

A picture worth keeping. Some things can be washed. Some soak it in so deep they have to be broken. But living water is not made unclean by what falls into it. There is a fountain like that:

“In that day there shall be a fountain opened to the house of David and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem for sin and for uncleanness.”

Zechariah 13:1

Part three

“Ye shall be holy; for I am holy”

Verses 41–47

At last, the reason.

Holy, because he is

Here, at 11:44, is the first time in Leviticus that God says “I am holy.” He says it again in the very next verse, and three more times later in the book (19:2, 20:26, 21:8). Defiled means made unclean. To sanctify yourselves is to set yourselves apart for God.

Look at the order in verse 45. First: “I am the LORD that bringeth you up out of the land of Egypt, to be your God.” Then: “ye shall therefore be holy.” He rescued them first. Holiness was not the price of getting out. It was the life of a people already brought out.

“But as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation; Because it is written, Be ye holy; for I am holy.”

1 Peter 1:15–16

A table that taught

“To make a difference.” That was the job of this law. Every meal in Israel taught the same lesson the priests were told to teach in 10:10: there is holy and unholy, clean and unclean, and God's people are to know the difference. Eating as the LORD said marked Israel off from every nation around them.

Then the Son of God came, and said this about food:

“And he saith unto them, Are ye so without understanding also? Do ye not perceive, that whatsoever thing from without entereth into the man, it cannot defile him; Because it entereth not into his heart, but into the belly, and goeth out into the draught, purging all meats?”

Mark 7:18–19

“Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days: Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.”

Colossians 2:16–17

The food laws were “a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.” A shadow shows you something is coming. When the one casting it arrives, you look at him.

What God hath cleansed

Peter, a lifelong keeper of Leviticus 11, saw a sheet let down from heaven full of unclean beasts, and heard a voice:

“And there came a voice to him, Rise, Peter; kill, and eat. But Peter said, Not so, Lord; for I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean. And the voice spake unto him again the second time, What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common.”

Acts 10:13–15

Soon after, standing in a Gentile's house in Caesarea, he understood what it meant:

“And he said unto them, Ye know how that it is an unlawful thing for a man that is a Jew to keep company, or come unto one of another nation; but God hath shewed me that I should not call any man common or unclean.”

Acts 10:28

Some of you have been called unclean by other people, or have called yourself that. If God has washed you, do not call yourself common.

Points to remember

  1. The beasts“Whatsoever parteth the hoof, and is clovenfooted, and cheweth the cud… that shall ye eat” 11:3Both marks were needed: one alone left the camel and the swine unclean.
  2. The waters“whatsoever hath fins and scales in the waters… them shall ye eat” 11:9God drew the line, and his people took his word on it.
  3. The touch“whosoever toucheth the carcase of them shall be unclean until the even” 11:24Uncleanness shut a man out for a while, and it had an end.
  4. The reason“ye shall be holy; for I am holy” 11:44Not health or habit: God is holy, and he rescued them to be his own.
  5. The difference“To make a difference between the unclean and the clean” 11:47Every meal taught Israel that God's people know the difference.
  6. The change“What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common” Acts 10:15The food laws were a shadow; the body is of Christ, and he washes men clean.

Question 1 of 6

The beasts

What two marks made a beast clean to eat?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It had to part the hoof, cloven all the way, and chew the cud. One mark was not enough: the camel chews the cud, the swine parts the hoof, and both were unclean.

Question 2 of 6

The waters

Which creatures in the waters could Israel eat?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Whatever had fins and scales, in the seas or the rivers. All the rest was an abomination to them, not for them. God drew the line.

Question 3 of 6

The touch

What happened to a man who touched a dead unclean thing, and how long did it last?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Answer

He was unclean until the evening, and if he carried it he washed his clothes. Then the day ended and he was clean. Uncleanness had an end.

Question 4 of 6

The reason

What reason does the LORD himself give for these laws?

What verses tell us about this?

“But as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation; Because it is written, Be ye holy; for I am holy.”

1 Peter 1:15–16

Answer

“Ye shall be holy; for I am holy.” He is the LORD who brought them out of Egypt to be their God. He rescued them first, then called them to be like him.

Question 5 of 6

The difference

What was this law for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To make a difference between the unclean and the clean. Every meal reminded Israel that they belonged to the LORD and were set apart from the nations.

Question 6 of 6

The change

What does the New Testament say about clean and unclean meats?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he saith unto them, Are ye so without understanding also? Do ye not perceive, that whatsoever thing from without entereth into the man, it cannot defile him; Because it entereth not into his heart, but into the belly, and goeth out into the draught, purging all meats?”

Mark 7:18–19

“Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days: Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.”

Colossians 2:16–17

Answer

Jesus said what goes into a man from without “cannot defile him,” because it enters the belly, not the heart: “purging all meats.” The food laws were a shadow; the body is of Christ. And what God has cleansed, we are not to call common.
