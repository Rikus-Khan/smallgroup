Matthew 18

Seventy times seven

The disciples argue about who is greatest. Jesus sets a little child in the middle of them, goes after one lost sheep, and teaches a forgiven man to forgive.

Part one

Who is the greatest?

Verses 1–9

Grown men arguing about rank, and a child standing in the middle of the room.

An argument on the road

They had just come back to Capernaum (17:24). Mark tells us what happened on the way there:

“But they held their peace: for by the way they had disputed among themselves, who should be the greatest.”

Mark 9:34

Jesus had just told them, for the second time, that He would be killed and rise again (17:22–23). They spent the walk arguing about who would be top man.

Turn around, and start small

“Converted” means turned around. They asked who is greatest in the kingdom. Jesus says: unless you turn, you won't even get in.

  • not because children are sinlessevery parent knows better
  • not because children are cutethat's not the point
  • because a child has no rank and knows itnothing to bring, and he trusts the one who does

In that world a child had no standing at all. He couldn't earn his keep or argue his case. That's how every man comes into God's kingdom: empty-handed, trusting his Father.

Greatness goes down, not up

the world→climb over people→to be the greatestStatus, respect, being the man nobody crosses.
the kingdom→humble yourself→and be the greatestLow, honest, teachable, like the child in the middle of the room.

Notice who does the humbling: “shall humble himself.” Life can humble a man the hard way, and many of us know it. Jesus asks us to go low on purpose.

And how you treat the small, unimportant person is how you treat Jesus: “whoso shall receive one such little child in my name receiveth me.”

A millstone around the neck

μύλος ὀνικός said mylos onikos a donkey's millstone

Not a small hand mill. The big upper stone that a donkey walked in circles to turn, heavy enough to take a man straight to the bottom.

To “offend” here means to trip someone up, to cause him to fall into sin. “These little ones which believe in me” are young believers. Jesus guards them fiercely. Leading a new believer back into the old life is no small thing to Him.

Stumbling blocks will come

Jesus is honest: in a fallen world, temptations and traps will come. That doesn't excuse the man who sets them.

someonehanded usthe first drink, the first pill
and some of ushanded itto someone else

Both of those are on this slide. If the second one is you, the same Jesus who said “woe” also came “to save that which was lost” (v. 11). Confess it, and become the man who now leads others the other way.

Cut it off

Jesus is not telling anyone to hurt his body. A blind man can still lust, and a man with no hands can still steal in his heart. He's using the strongest picture He can find: take radical action against whatever makes you fall.

For us that might be the dealer's number, the route past the bar, the phone, the friends we used with. It can feel like losing a limb. Life is worth more.

Part two

The one that went astray

Verses 10–14

A hundred sheep, one missing, and a Shepherd who won't write it off.

Nobody small to God

To “despise” is to look down on, to treat as not worth your time. The disciples wanted to be great. Jesus warns them not to look down on the least believer.

Why? Heaven is watching over them. Their angels “do always behold the face” of the Father, like servants standing ready before a king. The Bible doesn't tell us much more than that, and we shouldn't build more on it. The point is plain: God's eye is on the least of His people.

“Are they not all ministering spirits, sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation?”

Hebrews 1:14

“For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.”

Luke 19:10

He goes after the one

Ninety-nine are safe. One is missing. Any shepherd might say, “I've still got ninety-nine.” This one climbs the mountains after it, and rejoices more over that one than over all the rest.

“All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.”

Isaiah 53:6

If you've wandered, or relapsed, He isn't waiting for you to find your own way back. He comes looking. It is not His will that even one should perish (v. 14).

Part three

If thy brother trespass

Verses 15–20

What to do when a brother wrongs you: not gossip, not a grudge, but a road back.

Four steps, one goal

  1. alonejust you and himv. 15
  2. one or two morewitnessesv. 16
  3. the churchthe gathered believersv. 17
  4. an outsider“as an heathen man”v. 17

Step one is the hardest: go to him, not to everybody else about him. Most of the time it ends right there, and you've “gained thy brother.” Winning him back is the whole point, not winning the argument.

The “two or three witnesses” come from the law (Deuteronomy 19:15). They keep it fair: one man's word against another's doesn't settle anything.

As a heathen and a publican

A “heathen” was a Gentile, outside God's people. A “publican” was a tax collector. A man who won't hear his brother, the witnesses, or the church has stepped outside, and the church treats him as an outsider.

Matthew the publican → Matthew the apostle Remember who wrote this book (9:9, 10:3). How did Jesus treat publicans? He ate with them, called them, and kept going after them.

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Bound on earth, bound in heaven

“Bind” and “loose” were words Jewish teachers used for forbid and allow. Here they follow right after a church dealing with a sin. When the church acts the way Jesus just laid out, by His word, its decision lines up with what heaven has already decided.

It isn't a blank check for any group to get whatever it wants. It's a promise that a church handling hard things His way is not acting alone.

“There am I in the midst of them.”

Two or three is about the smallest gathering there is. A group this size, meeting in His name today, has Jesus Himself in the room.

Part four

Seventy times seven

Verses 21–35

Peter wants a number. Jesus tells a story about a debt nobody could pay.

Stop counting

Some Jewish teachers said you forgive the same man three times, and after that you're done. Peter doubles it and adds one. Seven sounded generous.

Peter: seven times

Jesus: seventy times seven

Jesus doesn't mean keep a tally until 491. Nobody keeps count that long. He means forgiveness that has stopped keeping score.

A debt nobody could pay

A talent was the biggest weight of money there was, about 6,000 days' wages for a working man. Ten thousand was the biggest number in Greek counting. Jesus is saying more money than you can imagine.

≈ 6,000days' wages in one talent
× 10,000talents
≈ 60,000,000days' wages

For a sense of size: Herod Antipas collected about 200 talents a year in taxes from all of Galilee and Perea. This one servant owed fifty times that.

Selling a debtor and his family as slaves was a real practice then. Even that wouldn't have paid a sliver of it.

Forgiven, all of it

The servant's account with the king

  • Sell the mannot enough
  • Sell his wife and childrennot enough
  • Sell all that he hadnot enough
Still owing10,000 talents
FORGIVENthe whole debt

“I will pay thee all” was never going to happen. The king knew it. He didn't set up a payment plan. He “loosed him, and forgave him the debt.”

That's us before God. We couldn't pay what we owed, and Jesus paid it on the cross. Everything after this in the story grows out of this one moment.

The same words, a different answer

Matthew 18:26

The servant therefore fell down, and worshipped him, saying, Lord, have patience with me, and I will pay thee all.

Matthew 18:29

And his fellowservant fell down at his feet, and besought him, saying, Have patience with me, and I will pay thee all.

Word for word, the same plea he had made himself, maybe an hour before. The king heard it and forgave. He heard it and grabbed the man by the throat.

A “penny” was a day's wage (20:2), so a hundred pence was about three or four months' pay. Not nothing. But see how it compares.

The two debts, to scale

What he owed the king10,000 talents
about 60,000,000 days' wages
What his fellowservant owed him100 pence
about 100 days' wages. True to scale it would be thinner than a hair; even this line is far too big.

The king forgave a debt about×as big

What people owe us is real. Some of it hurt us badly. But set beside what God forgave us, it's a sliver. A man who knows how much he was forgiven has room to forgive.

Shouldest not thou also?

The “tormentors” were jailers. This is a hard ending, and Jesus means it to be. He is not saying a saved man loses his salvation the next time he holds a grudge. He is showing what an unforgiving heart is: a man living as if he had never been forgiven at all.

Grace that has truly reached a man doesn't stop with him. It flows on through him. And a man who refuses to forgive builds his own prison. Resentment keeps the debt, and the debt keeps him.

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

What forgiving is, and isn't

  • saying it didn't hurtit did, and Jesus calls it a real debt
  • pretending it never happenedstep one in verse 15 is telling him his fault
  • trusting him again overnighttrust is rebuilt over time
  • cancelling the debt, from the heartyou stop collecting, and leave him with God

Some days you'll have to do it again for the same old wound. That's what seventy times seven looks like. Ask God for the strength. He has plenty of it, and He's the one who forgave you first.

Points to remember

  1. Humility“Whosoever therefore shall humble himself as this little child, the same is greatest” 18:4In His kingdom, greatness goes down, not up.
  2. Sin“If thy hand or thy foot offend thee, cut them off” 18:8Whatever makes you fall, cut it out of your life. Life is worth more.
  3. The lost“It is not the will of your Father… that one of these little ones should perish” 18:14The Shepherd goes after the one that wandered, and rejoices to bring it home.
  4. Restoring“Go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone” 18:15Go to him, not around him. The goal is to gain your brother.
  5. Forgiveness“Until seventy times seven” 18:22Forgiveness stops keeping score.
  6. Grace“Shouldest not thou also have had compassion on thy fellowservant, even as I had pity on thee?” 18:33We were forgiven a debt we could never pay. Forgiven men forgive.

Question 1 of 6

Humility

Who is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The man who humbles himself like a little child. We come into the kingdom with no rank and nothing to bring, trusting our Father. The world climbs over people to be great. In God's kingdom, the greatest goes low on purpose.

Question 2 of 6

Sin

How seriously does Jesus want us to deal with what makes us fall?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As seriously as cutting off a hand. He doesn't mean hurting our bodies. He means taking radical action against whatever leads us into sin: people, places, habits, a phone. It may feel like losing a limb, but life is worth more than anything we give up.

Question 3 of 6

The lost

How does the Father feel about one who goes astray?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He doesn't write the one off. The Shepherd leaves the ninety and nine and goes looking, and He rejoices when He finds it. It isn't His will that even one should perish. If you've wandered, He is coming after you.

Question 4 of 6

Restoring

What should you do when a brother sins against you?

What verses tell us about this?

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Answer

Go to him first, alone, and tell him. Not everybody else. If he won't hear, take one or two more, and then the church. The aim at every step is to win your brother back, gently, remembering you could fall too.

Question 5 of 6

Forgiveness

How many times should I forgive my brother?

What verses tell us about this?

“And if he trespass against thee seven times in a day, and seven times in a day turn again to thee, saying, I repent; thou shalt forgive him.”

Luke 17:4

Answer

Peter offered seven, which sounded generous. Jesus said seventy times seven. He doesn't mean keep count until 490. He means stop keeping score. Forgiving can mean letting go of the same hurt again and again.

Question 6 of 6

Grace

Why should a forgiven man forgive?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Answer

Because of how much he has been forgiven. We owed God a debt we could never pay, and He cancelled all of it through Jesus. What anyone owes us is small beside that. Grace that has really reached us flows on to others.
