Exodus 25

“That I may dwell among them”

God asks his people for a gift, given willingly, to build him a sanctuary. Then he gives Moses the pattern: the ark with its mercy seat, the table of bread, and the lamp of gold.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. The offeringWho was to give to the building of the sanctuary, and how?
  2. The reasonWhy did the LORD ask for a sanctuary?
  3. The arkWhat went into the ark, and what sat on top of it?
  4. The meetingWhere would the LORD meet with Moses and speak with him?
  5. Bread and lightWhat was set on the table, and what stood across from it?
  6. The patternBy what pattern was everything to be made?

Part one

“Let them make me a sanctuary”

Verses 1–9

The God who brought them out of Egypt asks for a house, so that he can live in the middle of them.

“Willingly with his heart”

God could have taxed them. He does not. He asks for a gift, and only from the man whose heart moves him to give it: “of every man that giveth it willingly with his heart ye shall take my offering.”

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7

Notice who is asked: a people who, a few months ago, owned nothing. They had been slaves. Their gold and silver came from the Egyptians who sent them away (12:35–36). God is asking them to give back out of what he has just given them.

Later in this book you will see what happens when a freed people is asked to give like this. They have to be told to stop (36:5–7).

The list, and the old words in it

  • brassin the Bible's time this was bronze or copper, used for the tough, outside pieces
  • blue, purple, scarletthree dyed threads, costly and bright, woven into the cloth
  • fine linenwhite cloth spun from flax. Goats' hair is the rough cloth of the outer tent
  • badgers' skinsa tough, weatherproof leather. Nobody is sure which animal; the old word is just “badger”
  • shittim woodacacia, the desert tree: hard, straight, and slow to rot
  • onyx, ephod, breastplatestones for the priest's clothes. The ephod is his vest and the breastplate a square piece over his chest (chapter 28)

Gold, silver, bronze; cloth, skins, wood; oil, spices, stones. A whole house out of what a camp in the desert could give. Every one of those things came from a man who had it and let go of it.

“That I may dwell among them”

This is the heart of the next sixteen chapters. Not “that they may have a temple.” Not “that they may find me.” That I may dwell among them. Their God wanted to live in the middle of his people, close enough to hear.

Exodus 25:8

And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.

John 1:14

And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

σκηνόω said skēnoō “to live in a tent”: John wrote that Jesus pitched his tent among us

The house in the middle of the camp

A tent inside a fenced court, set down in the middle of the camp. We will see it piece by piece over three chapters. For now, see how small and plain a house it is for the God of the whole earth.

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.”

Revelation 21:3

A copy of something real

Twice in this chapter God says the same thing: make it “after the pattern,” the one “shewed thee in the mount” (verses 9 and 40). Moses did not design it, and no craftsman improved on it.

“Who serve unto the example and shadow of heavenly things, as Moses was admonished of God when he was about to make the tabernacle: for, See, saith he, that thou make all things according to the pattern shewed to thee in the mount.”

Hebrews 8:5

“For Christ is not entered into the holy places made with hands, which are the figures of the true; but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us:”

Hebrews 9:24

The New Testament says it plainly: the tent was a shadow, a picture drawn from the real thing in heaven. That is why every measure matters, and why we will keep asking one question as we read: what is this showing me about Jesus?

Part two

The ark and the mercy seat

Verses 10–22

A gold-covered chest with the law inside, and a lid on top where God says he will meet with Moses.

How big is “two cubits and a half”?

A cubit is the length from a man's elbow to his fingertip: about 18 inches. So the ark is a chest you could carry between two men:

2½ × 1½ × 1½cubits, long, wide, high
≈ 45 × 27 × 27inches
≈ 3¾ × 2¼ × 2¼feet: a chest a bit shorter than a sofa
  • overlaya skin of gold over the wood, inside and out
  • crowna gold rim running round the top edge
  • stavestwo poles, slid through four gold rings, for carrying

Verse 15 says the poles stay in the rings. They are never taken out. The ark is always ready to be lifted, because where the people go, God goes with them.

What the ark held

Verse 16, and again in verse 21: “thou shalt put into the ark the testimony which I shall give thee.” The testimony is the law, the ten commandments on two tables of stone. Hebrews tells us what else came to be kept with it:

“Which had the golden censer, and the ark of the covenant overlaid round about with gold, wherein was the golden pot that had manna, and Aaron’s rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant;”

Hebrews 9:4
  • the tablesGod's law, written by him
  • a pot of mannathe bread he gave, kept as a reminder (chapter 16)
  • Aaron's rodthe staff that budded, to show whom God had chosen (Numbers 17)

Commune in verse 22 means talk together. The law lay inside, covered. And above it, the LORD said, “there I will meet with thee.”

Law below, blood on the lid, God above

The law, in the arkevery commandment, and every one of us has broken them
The mercy seat a lid of pure gold: a covering. The blood is sprinkled here.
The two cherubimsgold angels, wings spread over the lid, looking down at it
The LORD“there I will meet with thee”

The law lies underneath, and what it says is true: guilty. But God does not meet his people on the law. He meets them on the lid, the covering, where blood has been put between his holy law and the sinner.

The mercy seat has a name in the New Testament

The Hebrew word means a covering. In Greek, the same idea is the word the New Testament uses in Romans 3:25, where the KJV translates it propitiation: the sacrifice that turns away God's anger and covers the sin. Hebrews 9:5 uses the very same word, and the KJV there prints it as “mercyseat.”

“For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God; Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus: Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God;”

Romans 3:23–25

Jesus is the mercy seat. The place where God meets a guilty man and does not strike him is not a lid of gold. It is the blood of Christ, “set forth” in the open for anyone who believes.

The cherubims: once guards, now watchers

At Eden

Guarding the way

“And he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubim, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life.” Genesis 3:24

On the mercy seat

Looking at mercy

Gold, with wings over the lid, “their faces shall look one to another; toward the mercy seat shall the faces of the cherubims be.”

The same kind of beings. At Eden, they stood between sinful man and God. Here their eyes are fixed on the place where the blood goes. Peter says the angels are still looking:

“Unto whom it was revealed, that not unto themselves, but unto us they did minister the things, which are now reported unto you by them that have preached the gospel unto you with the Holy Ghost sent down from heaven; which things the angels desire to look into.”

1 Peter 1:12

Part three

The table and the candlestick

Verses 23–40

Bread always on the table, and a lamp that gives light in the room where there is no window.

The table of shewbread

2 × 1 × 1½cubits, long, wide, high
≈ 36 × 18 × 27inches: a small desk
≈ 3 inchesthe “hand breadth” border, a lip to hold things on
  • shewbreadshew is show: bread “shown” before God, always on the table
  • dishes, spoons, bowlsplates, and cups for the incense; bowls and jars for pouring out the drink offering (“to cover withal”)
  • twelve loavesone for each tribe, replaced every sabbath (Leviticus 24:5–8)

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

Fresh bread, before God, “alway”. Not eaten by the people, but set out in his presence for all twelve tribes at once. And Jesus says: I am that bread, and I will feed every man who comes.

The candlestick: a lamp of gold

Not a candle: it held oil lamps. One stem, three branches out each side, seven lamps in a row, all hammered out of a single piece of gold (“beaten work”).

  • knopsornamental knobs on the stem
  • bowls like almondscups shaped like almond blossoms
  • snuffdishes, tongstrays and tweezers to trim the wicks
  • a talentabout 75 pounds of gold

Light in a room with no window

The holy place was a closed tent: no window, no sun. The only light inside came from this lamp. Without it the priests would have been in the dark, with the bread.

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

“Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid.”

Matthew 5:14

Jesus said it of himself first, and then he said it of his people. The light is his. A man who follows him does not walk in the dark, and the lamp in the tent was lit by oil the people brought and the priests tended every night (chapter 27 will show how).

All three, in the room

The table and the lampstand stand in the first room, the holy place. Then the vail. Then, in the inner room, the ark and the mercy seat, where God said he would meet with Moses. Bread, light and mercy: all a man could need, in one small tent.

Real world: the house God lives in now

God asked for a house so he could live among his people. Paul tells men like us where he lives now.

“What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own?”

1 Corinthians 6:19
  • You failed yesterdayThe law says guilty. The covering is already there: the blood of Christ, your mercy seat. Go to him now, not after you clean up.
  • Give willinglyTime, a ride, a meal, a few dollars. Not from guilt: from a heart he moved.
  • Take the lightRead a few verses before work. You are walking into a place with no window.

He did not wait for a perfect people to build him a perfect place. He asked for gold, wood and a willing heart, and came to live in the middle of a camp of ex-slaves.

Points to remember

  1. The offering“of every man that giveth it willingly with his heart ye shall take my offering” 25:2God asks for gifts, and only from willing hearts. Give from what he gave you.
  2. The reason“And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.” 25:8God wanted to live among his people. In Jesus he did, and now he lives in you.
  3. The ark“thou shalt put into the ark the testimony which I shall give thee” 25:16The law was inside, and the mercy seat above it. The law says guilty; the blood covers.
  4. The meeting“there I will meet with thee, and I will commune with thee from above the mercy seat” 25:22God meets a sinner at the place of the blood, which is Christ, our propitiation.
  5. Bread and light“thou shalt set upon the table shewbread before me alway” 25:30Bread and lamp, set before God always. Jesus is the bread of life and the light of the world.
  6. The pattern“look that thou make them after their pattern, which was shewed thee in the mount” 25:40The tent was a shadow of heavenly things. Ask of each piece what it shows about Jesus.

Question 1 of 6

The offering

Who was to give to the building of the sanctuary, and how?

What verses tell us about this?

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7

Answer

Every man whose heart moved him to give, and willingly. God asked for a gift, not a tax. He loves the giver who gives cheerfully, from what God first gave him.

Question 2 of 6

The reason

Why did the LORD ask for a sanctuary?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

John 1:14

Answer

“That I may dwell among them.” God wanted to live in the middle of his people. In Jesus, the Word became flesh and pitched his tent among us, and now the Spirit lives in every believer.

Question 3 of 6

The ark

What went into the ark, and what sat on top of it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The testimony, God's law on the tables of stone, went inside. On top went the mercy seat, a lid of pure gold, with the two cherubims. The law says guilty; the covering, sprinkled with blood, is where God meets sinners.

Question 4 of 6

The meeting

Where would the LORD meet with Moses and speak with him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God;”

Romans 3:25

Answer

Above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubims. God meets a sinner at the place of blood. For us that place is Jesus, “a propitiation through faith in his blood.”

Question 5 of 6

Bread and light

What was set on the table, and what stood across from it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Answer

Shewbread, always, before the LORD, and across from it a candlestick of pure gold with seven lamps. Jesus is the bread of life and the light of the world, and he feeds and lights every man who follows him.

Question 6 of 6

The pattern

By what pattern was everything to be made?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The pattern God showed Moses on the mount, not Moses' own idea. Hebrews 8:5 calls it “the example and shadow of heavenly things.” Every piece points to Jesus.
