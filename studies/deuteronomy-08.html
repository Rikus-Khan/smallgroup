Deuteronomy 8

Remember all the way

Forty years of manna, clothes that never wore out, and a Father's hand. Now a good land lies ahead, and Moses warns them that a full stomach can make a man forget the God who fed him when he was empty.

Part one

“Thou shalt remember all the way”

Verses 1–6

Moses looks back over forty hard years and tells them what God was doing in them.

All the way

  1. Egyptthe house of bondage8:14
  2. Mannabread every morningExodus 16
  3. Horebthe law given5:2
  4. Kadesh-barnea“ye would not go up”1:26
  5. 38 yearstill the brook Zered2:14
  6. Moabat the edge of the land1:5

“All the way”: not only the sea and the manna, but Kadesh too, and the long years that came after it. God led them through every mile, including the miles they walked because of their own unbelief.

Moses gives three reasons: to humble thee, to bring them low; to prove thee, to test them; and to know what was in thine heart. The wilderness showed Israel what was in them. Hard years do that to a man.

A book that says “remember”

The word remember comes fourteen times in Deuteronomy, and forget (with forgotten) twelve times. In this chapter it is “remember” twice (verses 2 and 18) and “forget” three times (verses 11, 14 and 19).

Moses is an hundred and twenty years old (31:2), near the end of his life, and he keeps saying the same thing: don't forget. He knows a people can lose their memory of God faster than they lose anything else.

Recovery works the same way. The man who forgets what the old life cost him, and what God brought him out of, is the man who goes back. Remembering isn't living in the past. It's keeping your story where you can see it.

Hungry on purpose

First“he humbled thee, and suffered thee to hunger”
→
Then“fed thee with manna, which thou knewest not”
→
So that“he might make thee know that man doth not live by bread only”

Suffered means allowed. God let them get hungry, then fed them with bread nobody had ever seen, so they would learn that their lives hung on his word, not on the food in their hands.

We saw in Matthew 4 that Jesus, hungry after forty days, answered the devil with this very verse: “It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God” (Matthew 4:4). Where Israel grumbled, he trusted.

Fed by every word

“Neither have I gone back from the commandment of his lips; I have esteemed the words of his mouth more than my necessary food.”

Job 23:12

A man can have a full refrigerator and a starving soul. Most of us tried to live by bread only: money, a high, a woman, a feeling. None of it kept us alive inside.

God sometimes lets a man get hungry so he will find out what he really lives on. If you are in a hungry season right now, that may be what it is for. Open the Book before you open anything else today.

Forty years in the same clothes

one dot for every year

A miracle, every single day, that nobody had to pray for. Moses says it again at the end: “your clothes are not waxen old upon you, and thy shoe is not waxen old upon thy foot” (29:5). Waxed not old means it didn't wear out. The feet that walked the desert for forty years did not swell.

“Yea, forty years didst thou sustain them in the wilderness, so that they lacked nothing; their clothes waxed not old, and their feet swelled not.”

Nehemiah 9:21

These were the grumblers, the people who wanted Egypt back. And God still kept their sandals together. “Your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things” (Matthew 6:32).

A father's hand

What a man fears it is

An enemy's blow

Meant to hurt him, to pay him back, to be rid of him.

What Moses says it is

“As a man chasteneth his son”

Meant to train him, from a father who means to keep him.

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth. If ye endure chastening, God dealeth with you as with sons; for what son is he whom the father chasteneth not?”

Hebrews 12:6–7

Some of you had a father whose hand meant only anger. This Father is different: “Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby” (Hebrews 12:11). A hard season after a fall isn't God throwing you out. It's God keeping you as his son.

Part two

“A good land”

Verses 7–10

After forty years of sand, Moses describes what is waiting across the Jordan.

What was across the river

  • Waterbrooks, fountains, deep springs
  • Seven cropswheat, barley, vines, figs, pomegranates, olive oil, honey
  • Under the groundiron in the stones, brass in the hills

Moses says “a land” five times in three verses (7–9), and names seven things that grow in it. Without scarceness means without running short. Brass is metal they could dig out of the hills. They were camped across the Jordan from Jericho, looking at it.

From the dry land to the good land

Behind them

“that great and terrible wilderness”

  • fiery serpents and scorpions
  • drought, “where there was no water”
  • bread from heaven, a day at a time
Ahead of them

“a good land”

  • brooks, fountains and depths
  • wheat, barley, vines, figs, pomegranates, olives, honey
  • “bread without scarceness”

Both came from the same hand. The wilderness was not God forgetting them, and the good land would not be them earning it. Verse 10 tells them what to do once they are in.

Bless him when you're full

Most men pray when the plate is empty. Moses says: when you have eaten and you are full, then bless the LORD. Thank him for what he has given, at the full table.

“In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.”

1 Thessalonians 5:18

Giving thanks is how a man remembers. You can't thank God for a paycheck and at the same time believe you earned it all yourself. That is exactly the danger the next verses warn about.

Part three

“Beware that thou forget not the LORD thy God”

Verses 11–20

The danger isn't the wilderness any more. It's the good life.

How a man forgets God

  1. eaten and full(verse 12)
  2. goodly housesbuilt and dwelt in (verse 12)
  3. all that thou hast is multiplied(verse 13)
  4. thine heart be lifted upproud (verse 14)
  5. thou forget the LORD thy God(verse 14)

Not one step down is a sin by itself. Food, a house, money in the bank: all gifts. The fall is in the heart, “lifted up”, which is the old way of saying proud. And it happened, just as Moses said: “they were filled, and their heart was exalted; therefore have they forgotten me” (Hosea 13:6).

For a man in recovery, the danger often comes after the good news: ninety days clean, the job back, the family talking again. That is when a man starts thinking he can handle it. “Let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall” (1 Corinthians 10:12).

Water out of the rock of flint

Moses tells them what to remember: the fiery serpents (we saw them in Numbers 21), the scorpions, the drought, and water out of flint, hard dry rock. At Horeb God said, “thou shalt smite the rock, and there shall come water out of it” (Exodus 17:6).

“And did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ.”

1 Corinthians 10:4

Paul says it plainly: “that Rock was Christ.” The rock struck so that dying people could drink is a picture of the cross. And see why God led them the hard way: “to do thee good at thy latter end.” Latter end means the end of the story. The wilderness was for their good.

“And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.”

Romans 8:28

“Hath gotten me this wealth”

  • “My power”what the proud heart says
  • “the might of mine hand”“I did this myself”
  • “it is he that giveth thee power”the strength to work, the land to work in (verse 18)

This isn't a promise that God makes believers rich. Moses is warning men who will have wealth not to take the credit for it. The strength in your hands, your health, the job: all of it is given.

“For who maketh thee to differ from another? and what hast thou that thou didst not receive? now if thou didst receive it, why dost thou glory, as if thou hadst not received it?”

1 Corinthians 4:7

The same goes for sobriety. A man who says “my willpower got me this” has already started down the stairs. Every clean day is a gift: “Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above” (James 1:17).

Rich, and not highminded

“Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy;”

1 Timothy 6:17

Paul and Moses say the same thing. Money isn't the enemy, and it isn't the proof of God's favour either. The danger is trusting it and forgetting who gave it. Verse 18 says why God gave Israel the power to get wealth: “that he may establish his covenant which he sware unto thy fathers.” It was for his promise, not for their pride.

When the money comes in, the first thing to do with it is thank him. The second is to remember the man you were when you had none.

The same measure

Moses ends with a warning, and it is the same measure that God used on the nations of Canaan (7:2): “As the nations which the LORD destroyeth before your face, so shall ye perish.” God had no favourites when it came to idols. Years later it came true for the northern tribes: “For so it was, that the children of Israel had sinned against the LORD their God… and had feared other gods” (2 Kings 17:7).

But a man who has forgotten God can remember him again. The prodigal had forgotten his father, and then:

“And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father’s have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee,”

Luke 15:17–18

“Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works” (Revelation 2:5). The way back starts with remembering.

Real world: remember on a full stomach

  • SinPride on a full stomach: “my power… hath gotten me this.” It starts quietly, when life gets good.
  • RegretSome of your wilderness was your own fault, like Israel's. God led them through those years too, “to do thee good at thy latter end.”
  • GraceManna every morning, clothes that never wore out, a Father's hand: all given to grumblers.
  • HopeThe hard years were not wasted. The wilderness was for good, and the good land was ahead.
  • TodayWrite down three things God has given you since you came to him, and thank him for them by name. Then tell a brother in Christ one of them.

Points to remember

  1. The way“to humble thee, and to prove thee, to know what was in thine heart” 8:2The wilderness humbled them, tested them, and showed what was in their hearts.
  2. Bread“man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD doth man live” 8:3God let them hunger and fed them, to teach them to live by his word. Jesus answered the devil with it.
  3. Kept“Thy raiment waxed not old upon thee, neither did thy foot swell” 8:4Their clothes did not wear out and their feet did not swell, for forty years.
  4. A father's hand“as a man chasteneth his son, so the LORD thy God chasteneth thee” 8:5God's chastening is a father training a son he means to keep.
  5. The full table“Lest when thou hast eaten and art full… thou forget the LORD thy God” 8:12, 14The danger comes when life is good and the heart is lifted up.
  6. Wealth“for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth” 8:18Not my power or my hand: God gives the strength, and gets the thanks.

Question 1 of 6

The way

Why did the LORD lead Israel forty years in the wilderness?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To humble them, to prove them, and to know what was in their heart, whether they would keep his commandments; and in the end to do them good.

Answer

Not by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD. Jesus answered the devil with this verse.

Nehemiah 9:21

Answer

Their clothes did not wear out, and their feet did not swell, for all forty years.

What verses tell us about this?

Hebrews 12:6–7

Answer

As a man chastens his son: out of love, to train a son he means to keep.

Question 5 of 6

The full table

When is a man in most danger of forgetting God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

When he has eaten and is full, and all that he has is multiplied. Then the heart is lifted up, and he forgets the LORD.

Question 6 of 6

Wealth

Who gives a man power to get wealth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD. Not a man's own power or the might of his hand. So he remembers the LORD and thanks him.
