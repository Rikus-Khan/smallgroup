Numbers 22

Balaam, and the ass that spoke

Israel camps in the plains of Moab, and a frightened king sends money to a prophet to curse them. God says no, and says why. Balaam keeps the men overnight, asks again, and rides out, until the angel of the LORD stands in the road, and his own ass sees what he cannot.

Part one

“Curse me this people”

Verses 1–14

A king who is afraid, a prophet for hire, and a God who has already made up his mind.

The last camp

After forty years, Israel pitches “in the plains of Moab on this side Jordan by Jericho”: the flat ground on the east bank of the Jordan, across the river from Jericho, just north of the Salt Sea. On this side Jordan means the side they were on, the east.

They never move camp again in this book. Everything from here to the last verse happens on these plains:

“These are the commandments and the judgments, which the LORD commanded by the hand of Moses unto the children of Israel in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho.”

Numbers 36:13

Afraid of a fight that was never coming

Balak had watched what Israel did to the Amorites (Numbers 21). Now he pictures an ox in a field, licking up the grass to the roots, and his own people as the grass. So Moab goes to the elders of Midian, its neighbours, and they plan together.

But read what the LORD had already told Moses about Moab, on the way there:

“And the LORD said unto me, Distress not the Moabites, neither contend with them in battle: for I will not give thee of their land for a possession; because I have given Ar unto the children of Lot for a possession.”

Deuteronomy 2:9

Israel was under orders not to touch Moab. Balak was fighting a war God never meant to bring to him. A lot of a man's worst choices start right here: not with greed, but with fear of something that was never going to happen.

A prophet from far away

Balaam lived at Pethor, “by the river”: the great river, the Euphrates. Moses says it plainly later: Balaam was “of Pethor of Mesopotamia” (Deuteronomy 23:4). Balaam himself says Balak brought him “from Aram, out of the mountains of the east” (23:7). Balak sent a long way, and paid well, for the man he wanted.

The Bible does not mark Pethor any closer than that, so neither does the map.

Who really holds the blessing?

Balak, to Balaam (22:6)

“for I wot that he whom thou blessest is blessed, and he whom thou cursest is cursed.”

Isaac, to Jacob (Genesis 27:29)

“cursed be every one that curseth thee, and blessed be he that blesseth thee.”

Balak thinks the power is in the prophet's mouth: hire the right man, say the right words, and the blessing can be bought or broken. But it had been settled long before, over Jacob himself, and before that to Abram. The blessing on this people is not Balaam's to give or take. Wot means know; peradventure, perhaps.

The rewards of divination

They came with the money in their hands, and Balaam did not send them home. He said, stay the night, and I'll see what the LORD says. The New Testament tells us what was going on inside him:

“Which have forsaken the right way, and are gone astray, following the way of Balaam the son of Bosor, who loved the wages of unrighteousness; But was rebuked for his iniquity: the dumb ass speaking with man’s voice forbad the madness of the prophet.”

2 Peter 2:15–16

He “loved the wages of unrighteousness.” Every man here knows the feeling: you know what God thinks, but you keep the offer on the table overnight, just in case.

A clear no, and the reason

“thou shalt not curse the people: for they are blessed.”

God asks, “What men are these with thee?” He knew. It is the question he asked Adam, “Where art thou?”, a door opened for a man to tell the truth.

And the answer is not vague. Don't go. Don't curse them. They are blessed. Three plain sentences. Balaam never had to wonder what God thought about this job. Most of the time, neither do we.

How a no gets smaller

God saidverse 12“Thou shalt not go with them; thou shalt not curse the people: for they are blessed.”
Balaam told the princesverse 13“the LORD refuseth to give me leave to go with you.”gone: “for they are blessed”
The princes told Balakverse 14“Balaam refuseth to come with us.”gone: the LORD

Balaam passed on the part about going, and left out the part about the people being blessed. By the time it reached Balak, God had dropped out of it altogether, and it sounded like a man holding out for a better price. So Balak raised the price.

Part two

“More, and more honourable”

Verses 15–21

The second offer is bigger, and so is the temptation to ask God again.

Fine words, and an open door

What Balaam said

verse 18

“If Balak would give me his house full of silver and gold, I cannot go beyond the word of the LORD my God, to do less or more.”

What Balaam did

verse 19

“Now therefore, I pray you, tarry ye also here this night, that I may know what the LORD will say unto me more.”

God had already answered. There was nothing more to know. But a bigger offer made Balaam want to ask again, hoping the answer might have changed. Tarry means stay.

We do it too. God has said no to the bottle, the pills, the old crowd, the woman who isn't your wife. Then the offer comes back bigger and shinier, and we start praying about it again. That isn't seeking God's will. It is hoping he'll change it.

Let go, but not let off

God lets Balaam go, with a tight rein: “but yet the word which I shall say unto thee, that shalt thou do.” And in the next verse, “God’s anger was kindled because he went.” Why would God be angry at a man for going when he had said go?

Moses gives us what we need. God saw the heart: verse 32 calls it “thy way is perverse before me,” and Peter says Balaam “loved the wages.” Balaam went with the money in his eyes. A man can get permission and still be going for the wrong reason. Israel did the same in the wilderness:

“And he gave them their request; but sent leanness into their soul.”

Psalm 106:15

The ass is a donkey, the common riding animal of the day. Balaam saddles his own and rides out early in the morning.

Part three

“The angel of the LORD stood in the way”

Verses 22–35

The road gets narrower, three times, and the one who sees the danger is the donkey.

The road narrows

Into the field“the ass turned aside out of the way” (23)room to turn aside
Against the wall“a wall being on this side, and a wall on that side” (24–25)a wall each side
No way to turn“she fell down under Balaam” (26–27)no way to turn

First a field to swerve into. Then a path between two vineyard walls. Then a place so narrow there was no way to turn at all. Each time the angel stood further in, and each time Balaam beat the animal that was keeping him alive.

When God narrows the road

The angel of the LORD stood “for an adversary against him”: in Balaam's way, on purpose. God was not trying to trap Balaam. He was trying to stop him. He does the same to men he loves:

“Therefore, behold, I will hedge up thy way with thorns, and make a wall, that she shall not find her paths.”

Hosea 2:6

Some of us know this road. First a warning you could swerve around. Then the walls closed in: the job, the marriage, the health, a crushed foot of your own. Then a narrow place with no way to turn, and you went down. Like Balaam, we tend to beat whatever is in front of us. The question is who is standing in the road.

“There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.”

Proverbs 14:12

A miracle, and a blind man

“The LORD opened the mouth of the ass.” It happened, and Peter writes it down as plain history: “the dumb ass speaking with man’s voice forbad the madness of the prophet” (2 Peter 2:16). Dumb means unable to speak; wont, used to. The God who made the mouth can open it.

And look at Balaam: so angry he argues with a donkey and never stops to wonder why it can talk. He wishes for a sword, while an angel with a sword drawn stands a few feet away. That is what rage does to a man's eyes.

The one you beat was saving your life

Then the LORD opened Balaam's eyes, and he saw what the donkey had seen all along. The angel says it plainly: “unless she had turned from me, surely now also I had slain thee, and saved her alive.” Every blow Balaam gave her, she took for him. Perverse means crooked, twisted; to withstand is to stand against.

“Be ye not as the horse, or as the mule, which have no understanding: whose mouth must be held in with bit and bridle, lest they come near unto thee.”

Psalm 32:9

Think about who has been “in your way”: the wife who wouldn't let it go, the brother who kept calling, the judge, the closed door. We kicked at them. Some of them were the reason we are still alive. Thank God for them, and if you can, thank them.

“I have sinned … if it displease thee”

Balaam falls on his face and says the right words: “I have sinned.” Then he adds, “if it displease thee, I will get me back again.” If? An angel with a drawn sword had just told him. It is the sorry of a man who is sorry he got caught, and still hopes to keep going.

Balaam's way

Sorry, if …

Names the sin, keeps the plan, and leaves the door open to go on.

God's way

Confess, and be cleansed

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.” (1 John 1:9)

The angel sends him on, with the same rein as before: “only the word that I shall speak unto thee, that thou shalt speak.” Balaam's mouth now belongs to God for this errand, whatever his heart wants.

Part four

“The word that God putteth in my mouth”

Verses 36–41

The king meets his prophet, and takes him up a hill to look.

A king, a fee, and a hilltop

  • the border of Arnonthe river Arnon, Moab's northern border (on the first map)
  • the utmost coastthe far edge of his land: coast here means border
  • Kirjath-huzotha city of Moab; the name is all the Bible gives us
  • the high places of Baalhilltop shrines to Baal, an idol worshipped in Moab

Balak still thinks it's about money: “am I not able indeed to promote thee to honour?” Balaam's answer is the truest thing he says in the chapter: “have I now any power at all to say any thing? the word that God putteth in my mouth, that shall I speak.”

Then Balak feasts the men and takes Balaam up to Baal's high places, so that he might “see the utmost part of the people”, as if a curse needed a clear view and a holy hill to work. Next chapter: seven altars, and the first time Balaam opens his mouth.

Real world: the offer that keeps coming back

Balaam is not a cartoon villain. He is a religious man who talks about God, knows God's answer, and loves the money anyway. That is a mirror for a lot of us.

  • SinKeeping the offer overnight. Asking God again about what he has already said no to.
  • RegretThe walls that crushed our foot, and the people we beat who were standing between us and death.
  • GraceGod stood in the road. He stopped a greedy man with a sword he did not use, and a donkey that would not move.
  • HopeThe same LORD opens eyes. Balaam saw the angel; you can see where your road was going, and turn.
  • TodayName the offer that keeps coming back. Tell a brother what God already said about it, and ask him to hold you to it.

“But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition. For the love of money is the root of all evil: which while some coveted after, they have erred from the faith, and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.”

1 Timothy 6:9–10

Points to remember

  1. The plains of Moab“Moab was sore afraid of the people, because they were many” 22:3Balak feared a war God had told Israel not to fight. Fear starts a lot of bad choices.
  2. The hired prophet“with the rewards of divination in their hand” 22:7Balak sent money to buy a curse. Balaam “loved the wages of unrighteousness.”
  3. God's no“thou shalt not curse the people: for they are blessed” 22:12God's answer was plain, with its reason. Don't let it shrink when you pass it on.
  4. Asking again“that I may know what the LORD will say unto me more” 22:19Asking again about a settled no is hoping God will change his mind.
  5. The ass“the LORD opened the mouth of the ass” 22:28The donkey saw the angel and saved her master's life; he beat her for it.
  6. Open eyes“Then the LORD opened the eyes of Balaam” 22:31God stood in the road to stop a man going the wrong way. Thank him for the walls.

The ass

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

She saw the angel of the LORD with his sword drawn. When the LORD opened her mouth she asked what she had done to be beaten three times. She had turned aside to save Balaam's life.

Question 6 of 6

Open eyes

When the LORD opened Balaam's eyes, what did he see, and what did he say?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

He saw the angel of the LORD standing in the way with his sword drawn, and he fell on his face. He said, “I have sinned,” but added “if it displease thee.” Real confession doesn't bargain; it trusts God to forgive and cleanse.
