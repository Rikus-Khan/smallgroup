Acts 2

The day the Spirit came

Fifty days after the cross, the Holy Ghost comes, and Peter, who swore he never knew Jesus, stands up and says His name to thousands.

Part one

A sound from heaven

Verses 1–13

The promise from chapter 1 arrives, and a city full of visitors hears the gospel in its own language.

Pentecost means “fiftieth”

Day 1: Jesus rises. Day 40: He goes up. Day 50: the Spirit comes.

Pentecost was an old harvest feast. God told Israel: “shall ye number fifty days.” (Leviticus 23:16). So Jerusalem was packed with visitors, and that is why people from so many nations were there to hear.

Wind and fire

πνεῦμα said pneuma wind, breath, spirit. One word for all three.

Wind you can't see but can't miss. Fire has always meant God is present. In Exodus, He met Moses in a bush that burned and was not burned up. Here a little flame “sat upon each of them.” Not on a few leaders. On every one.

The Spirit God promised is not for a special class. He came to ordinary people sitting in a room, waiting.

What the tongues were

  • not a mumble nobody could followthe crowd understood every word, each in his own language (verses 6, 8)
  • not a test of whether you're savedsalvation comes by faith in Jesus, not by a feeling or a sound
  • not something you must wait forit was a sign for one day, to one crowd, for one reason
  • real languages, heard and understooda sign that God's news is for every nation, spoken so each man could hear it

A door opened, group by group

  1. Acts 2Jews, at Jerusalem
  2. Acts 8Samaritans
  3. Acts 10Gentiles, in Cornelius's house
  4. Acts 19John's followers, at Ephesus

Each time a new kind of people came in, God showed it in a way they couldn't miss, so nobody could say those people don't belong. Peter later pointed back to exactly this when he defended letting Gentiles in.

These were doors being opened, not steps every believer must repeat. Today the Spirit comes to live in a man the moment he trusts Christ, whether or not anything dramatic happens.

“But ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his.”

Romans 8:9

Where the crowd came from

Fifteen places are named. Every one had Jews living there, who came back for the feast.

The east: Parthians, Medes, Elamites, Mesopotamia, and the Arabians.

The north: Cappadocia, Pontus, Asia, Phrygia, Pamphylia.

The south: Egypt, and Libya around Cyrene.

The west: Crete, and the capital itself, Rome. “Proselytes” were Gentiles who had joined the Jewish faith.

Full of new wine?

Some people explained away what they couldn't understand: they're drunk. Peter answers plainly in verse 15: it's nine in the morning (“the third hour”).

Wine

Takes you over

It promises to make you bigger and braver. Then it leaves you wrecked, and wanting more.

The Spirit

Fills you for good

He also takes over, but He leaves you whole: clearer, steadier, and more yourself.

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

Many of us reached for the bottle to feel something bigger than ourselves. That longing wasn't wrong. It was pointed at the wrong thing. When the craving hits today, ask Him to fill what the drink never could.

Part two

Peter stands up

Verses 14–36

Fifty days ago he denied he knew Jesus. Now he can't stop saying His name.

The same Peter

“Woman, I know him not.” → “Ye men of Judaea… hearken to my words” Luke 22:57, the night he denied Jesus, and Acts 2:14, fifty days later.

Between those two moments, Jesus found Peter, forgave him, and handed him his job back. He had prayed for him before it ever happened: “when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Your worst night does not get the last word. The man who failed the loudest became the man who spoke the loudest, after he was forgiven.

Upon all flesh

sonsdaughtersNot only the men. “Your sons and your daughters shall prophesy.”
young menold menVisions for the young, dreams for the old. No age is too late or too early.
mastersservants“On my servants and on my handmaidens.” The Spirit doesn't ask your rank, or your record.

Joel's words, written hundreds of years before, said the Spirit would one day be poured out on everyone who belongs to God, not just a few prophets.

Whosoever

Ifwhosoever calls on the name of the Lord,
→
thenhe shall be saved.

Not whoever deserves it. Not whoever has been clean for a year. Whosoever. That includes the man who relapsed yesterday, and the man who feels too far gone to bother God.

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

All it takes is calling. Today, if you need to, that can be as short as: “Lord Jesus, save me.”

You did this. God undid it.

What men did

Took, crucified, slain

Peter doesn't soften it. These were the crowds who had shouted for it, and he says so to their faces.

What God did

Raised Him up

Death couldn't hold Him. “It was not possible that he should be holden of it.”

Notice both are true: it was “the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God,” and it was also their wicked hands. God knew ahead what men would choose, and He turned even that into the way of salvation. Yet the guilt was still theirs. And ours: it was our sin that needed it.

Not left in the grave

ᾅδης said hadēs the place of the dead, the grave. Not the lake of fire.

Peter quotes David (Psalm 16). “Hell” here is the realm of the dead, and “corruption” is the body decaying. David said God would not leave his Holy One there. David was speaking, as Peter explains, about Jesus.

And one line to hold onto today: “my flesh shall rest in hope.” Because Jesus came out of the grave, those who belong to Him have a future past anything they've lost.

Two tombs

David

Occupied

The great king died and was buried. His tomb was well known in Jerusalem, and everyone listening could walk there.

Jesus

Empty

His tomb is empty, and Peter and the others said they had seen Him alive. That was Peter's whole argument.

Every other religion's founder has a grave. Only one man's grave is empty. That's why you can lean your whole weight on Him.

Lord and Christ

“Jesus of Nazareth”
the man they crucified → “both Lord and Christ” Lord means master, the one in charge. Christ is Greek for the Anointed One, God's promised King.

That is the sermon in one line: the man you killed is now the Master of everything. And Peter tells them to their faces, because he knows what comes next, the offer of forgiveness.

Part three

What shall we do?

Verses 37–47

Men who had just been told they killed the Messiah ask the only question that matters.

Pricked in the heart

κατενύγησαν said katenygēsan to be stabbed through, pierced to the heart

That is what conviction feels like: sudden, sharp, and not made up. They didn't make excuses. They didn't say it wasn't that bad. They asked what to do.

Regret is painful, but it can be the doorway. Hiding it or drowning it keeps you stuck. Letting it point you to Jesus, as these men did, is the way out. And notice what Peter says next: not a sentence, but a way forward.

What “for” means here

In English, for works two ways. “He went to jail for stealing” means because of it. “He went to the store for bread” means to get it. The Greek word can mean either, and the rest of the Bible tells us which.

“to get”

Baptized to be forgiven

Then water would be what saves you, and the Bible says otherwise (Ephesians 2:8–9).

“because of”

Baptized because you're forgiven

The same sense as the men of Nineveh, who repented at the preaching of Jonah.

“…because they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here.”

Matthew 12:41

The order matters

  1. Hearthe word
  2. Prickedin the heart
  3. Repent and believesins forgiven, the Spirit given
  4. Baptizeda step of obedience
  5. Addedto the church

Look at verse 41: “they that gladly received his word were baptized.” Believing came first, and the baptism followed. Baptism is like a wedding ring: it shows the marriage, it doesn't make it.

“To him give all the prophets witness, that through his name whosoever believeth in him shall receive remission of sins.”

Acts 10:43

Forgiveness comes through believing. And if you have trusted Christ and never been baptized, take that step. Tell your pastor today.

“All that are afar off”

The promise is unto you, and your children, and all that are afar off.

To the Jews in that crowd, “afar off” meant the nations. It reaches us too, and anyone who feels far away: from God, from home, from who they used to be.

“But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.”

Ephesians 2:13

Get out of the crooked crowd

σκολιᾶς said skolias “untoward”: crooked, bent, twisted

“Save yourselves” doesn't mean earn it. It means be rescued from this crowd. Step out of the crooked company that crucified Him, and into the right one.

For a man in recovery, that's concrete: some people and places are part of the crooked generation. Today, who is one you need to step away from?

Three thousand

each dot is fifty people

From about a hundred and twenty to over three thousand in one afternoon. Not by force, and not by a show. By people believing a plain message about Jesus, and then being baptized to say so out loud.

Four things they stuck to

  1. The teachinglistening to the apostles, learning what Jesus said
  2. Fellowshipsharing life together, not just sitting in rows
  3. Breaking of breadmeals together, and the Lord's Supper in remembrance of Him
  4. The prayerstalking to God, out loud and together

None of these is complicated, and none of them can be done alone. You don't recover, or follow Jesus, by yourself. That's why this group meets.

Every day

They kept it up daily, and the Lord added to them daily.

Nothing here is a one-time rush. Their strength was showing up every day, in the temple and in each other's homes, eating together “with gladness and singleness of heart.”

They also looked after each other. Some freely sold what they owned to meet the needs they saw (verses 44–45). That is what brothers do.

Who is one brother you can check on today?

Points to remember

  1. The Holy Spirit“every man heard them speak in his own language” 2:6Real languages, a sign that the gospel is for every nation. The Spirit now lives in every believer.
  2. Wine and the Spirit“be filled with the Spirit” Eph. 5:18Reach for God, not the bottle, when you want to feel something bigger than yourself.
  3. Salvation“whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved” 2:21Whosoever means you. Call on Him today.
  4. Jesus“both Lord and Christ” 2:36The man they crucified is now Master of everything, and He raised Himself from the grave.
  5. Repentance and baptism“Repent, and be baptized every one of you” 2:38Turn and trust, then be baptized. It pictures forgiveness; it doesn't earn it.
  6. The church“continuing daily with one accord” 2:46You can't do this alone. Show up daily, and look out for your brothers.

Question 1 of 6

The Holy Spirit

What was the miracle of the tongues at Pentecost, and who heard it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Real languages. Visitors from many nations each heard the believers speaking about God's wonderful works in their own mother tongue. It was a sign that the gospel is for every nation, given once to this new group. It isn't something every believer must repeat: the Spirit lives in every man who trusts Christ.

Question 2 of 6

Wine and the Spirit

What did some in the crowd say was going on, and what did Peter answer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Some mocked: they're drunk. Peter said no, it's only nine in the morning, and this is what the prophet Joel said would happen. For a man who has fought the bottle, the real answer to wanting to be filled is the Spirit, not wine.

Question 3 of 6

Salvation

Who does God offer salvation to?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Everyone: “whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord.” Sons and daughters, young and old, Jews and the nations, those near and “all that are afar off.” Nobody is too far gone. You receive it by calling on Him, not by cleaning yourself up first.

Question 4 of 6

Jesus

Who did Peter say Jesus is?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A real man, approved by God with miracles. The one they crucified, whom God raised from the dead. And now both Lord and Christ: Master and promised King. Peter has seen Him alive, and so has everyone with him.

Question 5 of 6

Repentance and baptism

What did Peter tell the crowd to do, and what does baptism do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Repent, and be baptized. Turn to God, trust Jesus, then be baptized. Baptism doesn't wash sin away: forgiveness comes by faith, and baptism is the believer's step of obedience that pictures it. Notice it was the ones who “gladly received his word” who were baptized.

Question 6 of 6

The church

What did the first believers do together every day?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They kept learning the apostles' teaching, shared life and meals, remembered Jesus together, and prayed, and they did it daily. They looked after each other, and the Lord kept adding to them. Strength for the day comes from showing up, together, again tomorrow.
