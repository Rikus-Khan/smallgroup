Zephaniah 3

He will joy over thee with singing

The city that wouldn't listen hears one more hard word. Then comes one of the most tender promises in the Bible: God Himself, singing over His people.

Part one

The city that wouldn't listen

Verses 1–7

Zephaniah, about 630 BC, to Judah under King Josiah. The nations have heard their sentence. Now the finger turns back to Jerusalem.

Four “nots”

  1. Obeyed notwouldn't listen
  2. Received not correctionwouldn't be told
  3. Trusted notleaned on herself
  4. Drew not nearkept God at arm's length

Verse 1 doesn't name the city. Right after Nineveh in chapter 2, it could almost be Nineveh. Then verse 2 lands: “her God.” It's Jerusalem.

Every man in recovery knows this staircase. First you quit listening. Then you won't let anybody correct you. Then you trust yourself. Then you stop coming near. The way back up starts at the bottom step: draw near again.

“Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.”

James 4:8

Lions and wolves in charge

  1. Princes“roaring lions”: they ruled by fear
  2. Judges“evening wolves”: hunting at dusk, leaving nothing by morning
  3. Prophets“light and treacherous”: reckless men you couldn't trust
  4. Priestspolluted the holy place and twisted the law

Every man who should have protected the people was eating them. Wolves that hunt at evening “gnaw not the bones till the morrow”: they're so hungry they leave nothing for the morning.

Some of us were raised or used by men like that, men who were supposed to protect us. God saw it, and He calls it what it is. And the next verse says who was standing in the middle of that city the whole time.

Every morning

verses 3–4Wolves at evening

The leaders do their worst in the dark, and the unjust know no shame.

verse 5Every morning

The just LORD is still in the city. He brings His judgment to light, and He never misses a day.

Lamentations 3:23New every morning

“They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

The judges were wolves who ate all night. God shows up every morning, and “he faileth not.” The same God who faithfully exposes sin is faithful with mercy too:

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not.”

Lamentations 3:22

Up early, for the wrong thing

What God hoped

“Surely thou wilt fear me”

He cut off nations right next door (v. 6). Israel, Judah's own sister, was gone. Surely Judah would learn from that.

What they did

“They rose early”

Not to pray. To get an early start on sin. They were eager for it.

Watching other men crash should have been warning enough. Some of us watched friends die from this and still went back the next morning. It isn't enough to see the wreck. A man has to take the correction.

“Whoso loveth instruction loveth knowledge: but he that hateth reproof is brutish.”

Proverbs 12:1

Part two

A pure language

Verses 8–13

The fire falls on the nations. And out of it comes a people who call on His name.

Babel undone

Verse 8 sounds like the end of the world: all the nations gathered, all the earth devoured by fire. Then verse 9 says “for then.” The fire isn't the end. It's what clears the way.

Genesis 11:9

…because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the LORD scatter them abroad…

Zephaniah 3:9

For then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of the LORD, to serve him with one consent.

At Babel, men joined together against God, and He scattered them. Here, people from every nation join together to call on Him, from as far as “beyond the rivers of Ethiopia,” the far edge of the world they knew.

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

No more shame

ashamed → not ashamed Not because the doings never happened. God names them: “wherein thou hast transgressed against me.” But He takes away the shame, and the pride that caused it.

Shame tells a man, you are what you did. God says something else. He deals with the sin, and then He doesn't let you carry the shame of it for the rest of your life.

“For the scripture saith, Whosoever believeth on him shall not be ashamed.”

Romans 10:11

Who's left standing

The proud are taken out (v. 11). What's left is a remnant: not the impressive ones, but “an afflicted and poor people” who trust in the LORD's name. Humble men who know they need Him.

And look how they live: no lies, no deceit, and “they shall feed and lie down, and none shall make them afraid,” like sheep with a good shepherd. That's the peace the proud never find.

“Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.”

Matthew 5:3

Part three

Sing

Verses 14–20

The book that opened with “I will utterly consume” ends with God rejoicing.

Taken away

TAKEN AWAYverse 15

The same city, the same sins. And God says, “The LORD hath taken away thy judgments.” That promise was made to Jerusalem. But it shows us how God deals with sinners who come to Him, and Paul says how He does it:

“Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross;”

Colossians 2:14

“In the midst”

1 2 3 4  5midst 6 7 8 9 10  11midst 12midst 13 14  15midst 16  17midst 18 19 20 

In verse 5 God was in the midst of a rotten city, as its Judge. By verse 15 He's in the midst of it as its King, and by verse 17 as its Saviour. He never left. What changed was the people.

So: “Fear thou not … Let not thine hands be slack.” Slack hands hang limp, the way a man's do when he's given up. God says, pick them up.

“Wherefore lift up the hands which hang down, and the feeble knees;”

Hebrews 12:12

Six things in one verse

  1. In the midst of theeHe is close
  2. Is mightyHe is strong
  3. He will saveHe rescues
  4. Rejoice over thee with joyHe is glad over you
  5. Rest in his loveHe is at peace with you
  6. Joy over thee with singingHe sings over you

The first three we might expect: God is near, strong, and able to save. The last three are a surprise. They're not about what God does for His people. They're about how He feels about them.

In verse 14 the people were told to sing. In verse 17, God sings.

Quiet in His love

יַחֲרִישׁ said yacharish he will be quiet, he will be still

“Rest in his love” is, word for word, “be silent in his love.” Think of a father standing over his sleeping child. He doesn't say anything. He doesn't need to. He's just glad.

In chapter 1, men had to hush before God as their Judge (1:7). By chapter 3, God Himself goes quiet, not in anger, but in love. He isn't keeping a list. He's content with His people.

God sings

Most of us figure God puts up with us, at best. Tolerates us. Waits for us to slip again. Zephaniah says the opposite: He sings.

This promise was spoken to Israel, and it's still theirs. But the heart behind it is the same heart Jesus showed when He told about a son who wasted everything and came home:

“And bring hither the fatted calf, and kill it; and let us eat, and be merry: For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found. And they began to be merry.”

Luke 15:23–24

“I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.”

Luke 15:7

That's how God feels about a man who comes home. Not disappointed. Glad.

The limping, brought home

Then“her that halteth”“driven out”“put to shame”
savedgatheredturned to
Nowwalking homebrought back in“praise and fame”

Halteth means limps. God doesn't wait for His people to get healthy before He gathers them. He gathers the lame, the outcasts, and the ones everybody laughed at. Micah heard the same promise:

“In that day, saith the LORD, will I assemble her that halteth, and I will gather her that is driven out, and her that I have afflicted;”

Micah 4:6

God brought Judah home from Babylon, and He keeps His word to Israel still. And He hasn't changed: He still takes in men who come limping.

The whole book

Zephaniah's judgment is real: the day of the LORD came on Jerusalem, and it's coming on the world. But it isn't God's last word. His last word is a song.

Points to remember

  1. Correction“She received not correction” 3:2A man who won't be corrected slides step by step. The way back is to draw near.
  2. Faithfulness“Every morning … he faileth not” 3:5God shows up every single morning. His mercies are new every day.
  3. Shame“Thou shalt not be ashamed” 3:11God deals with the sin, then takes away the shame. You are not what you did.
  4. Humility“An afflicted and poor people” 3:12God leaves the humble standing, men who trust His name and not themselves.
  5. God's joy“He will joy over thee with singing” 3:17God doesn't just put up with His people. He rejoices over them.
  6. Restoration“I will save her that halteth” 3:19God gathers the limping and the outcast, and turns shame into praise.

Question 1 of 6

Correction

What happens when a man won't receive correction?

What verses tell us about this?

“Whoso loveth instruction loveth knowledge: but he that hateth reproof is brutish.”

Proverbs 12:1

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

Answer

He keeps sliding. Jerusalem wouldn't listen, wouldn't be told, wouldn't trust, and wouldn't come near, and each step led to the next. Correction hurts, but it's how God grows a man. The man who takes it ends up with peace. The man who refuses it keeps falling until he hits bottom.

Question 2 of 6

Faithfulness

What does God do “every morning”?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Answer

He shows up. Every morning He brings His judgment to light, and He never misses a day. While Jerusalem's leaders did their worst at night, God was faithful every morning. That same faithfulness means His mercy is new every morning too. Whatever yesterday was, today starts fresh with Him.

Question 3 of 6

Shame

How does God deal with a man's shame?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the scripture saith, Whosoever believeth on him shall not be ashamed.”

Romans 10:11

Answer

He takes it away. He doesn't pretend the sin didn't happen; He names it. But He deals with it, removes the pride behind it, and won't let His people carry the shame forever. He even turns shame into praise. Whoever believes on Christ shall not be ashamed.

Question 4 of 6

Humility

What kind of people does God leave standing?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Psalm 34:18

“But he giveth more grace. Wherefore he saith, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.”

James 4:6

Answer

The humble. God takes out the proud and leaves “an afflicted and poor people” who trust in His name. Not the strong or the impressive, but men who know they need Him. If life has broken you down, you're exactly the kind of man God draws near to.

Question 5 of 6

God's joy

How does God feel about His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“I say unto you, that likewise joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repenteth, more than over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.”

Luke 15:7

“And bring hither the fatted calf, and kill it; and let us eat, and be merry: For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found. And they began to be merry.”

Luke 15:23–24

Answer

He rejoices over them. He's quiet in His love, and He sings over them. That promise was given to Israel, but Jesus showed the same heart: heaven throws a party over one sinner who comes home. God isn't just putting up with you. If you're His, He's glad over you.

Question 6 of 6

Restoration

What does God promise to the lame and the driven out?

What verses tell us about this?

“In that day, saith the LORD, will I assemble her that halteth, and I will gather her that is driven out, and her that I have afflicted;”

Micah 4:6

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

He'll save them and gather them home. God promised Israel He'd bring back the limping and the outcast and give them a good name. He kept that word, and He still keeps it. And the same God, in Jesus, calls every worn-out man to come to Him. You don't have to walk right to come. You just have to come.
