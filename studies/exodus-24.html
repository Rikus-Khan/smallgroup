Exodus 24

The blood of the covenant

The people say, “All that the LORD hath said will we do.” Moses seals it with blood at the foot of the mountain, seventy elders see the God of Israel and eat and drink, and then Moses goes up into the cloud for forty days and forty nights.

Part one

“All the words which the LORD hath said will we do”

Verses 1–8

The people say yes, and a covenant is sealed in blood.

Who may come how near

The peopleat the foot of the mount: they hear, and may not go up
Aaron, Nadab, Abihu and seventy elders“worship ye afar off”
Moses alone“shall come near the LORD”

Afar off is far away, and nigh is near. God is holy, and the way to Him has steps. Nobody walks up on his own.

Remember the names Nadab and Abihu, Aaron's two oldest sons. They stand on the mount in this chapter. In Leviticus 10 they will be at the altar again, and they will not come back from it.

“Will we do”

the people, at the mount, in the third monthAll that the LORD hath spoken we will do. 19:8

the people, after the judgments and the ten commandmentsAll the words which the LORD hath said will we do. 24:3

the people, after the book is read in their hearingAll that the LORD hath said will we do, and be obedient. 24:7

Three times, and they meant it. “With one voice.” Moses wrote it all down, and he builded an altar with twelve pillars, one for each tribe: a standing stone for every family in the covenant.

Every man in this room has said it too: I will never do that again. I promise. And meant it. Hold on to that while we read, because forty days from now this same people will have forgotten. They were honest, and they were weak. The covenant that follows does not rest on their promise.

An altar, and two kinds of offering

Burnt offering

All of it to God

The whole animal goes up in the fire. It says: the sin needs covering, and I give it all to You.

Peace offering

A meal with God

Part is burned, and part is eaten by the worshippers. It says: we are at peace, so we sit down together.

“Young men” of Israel offered them: the priests had not yet been appointed. Basons in verse 6 are bowls, basins: Moses puts half the blood in bowls, and half he sprinkles on the altar. The blood is kept for the next act.

“Behold the blood of the covenant”

  • covenanta binding agreement between God and a people
  • in the audience ofin the hearing of: read out loud so everyone could hear
  • sprinkled on the peoplehalf the blood on the altar, which is God's side, and half on the people, who are the other side

Both parties to the covenant are touched by the same blood. God is joined to His people, and the people are joined to Him, and the thing that joins them is a death. A covenant in the Bible is not sealed with a handshake. It is sealed with blood.

The people had just said, “we will do.” Before they have done anything, Moses sprinkles the blood on them. The blood comes first.

The same words, nearly fifteen hundred years later

Exodus 24:8

Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD hath made with you concerning all these words.

Matthew 26:28

For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins.

Testament and covenant are the same word in the Greek of the New Testament. At the Last Supper Jesus took the cup and used Moses' words. He was saying: this is the blood that seals the covenant, and it is Mine.

“For when Moses had spoken every precept to all the people according to the law, he took the blood of calves and of goats, with water, and scarlet wool, and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book, and all the people, Saying, This is the blood of the testament which God hath enjoined unto you.”

Hebrews 9:19–20

“And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.”

Hebrews 9:22

Why not the promise alone?

What the people offered

“We will do”

A sincere promise, three times. And in forty days a calf of gold.

What sealed the covenant

Blood

Not their promise. A death, applied to them. “Behold the blood of the covenant.”

“Therefore by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight: for by the law is the knowledge of sin.”

Romans 3:20

“Wherefore the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ, that we might be justified by faith.”

Galatians 3:24

The law, said yes to, shows a man he cannot do it. That is its work: it brings him to Christ. A man who says “I promise” and fails has not lost everything. He has found the place the blood is for.

Real world: your promises, and the blood

You have promised yourself, your wife, your mother, your judge. Some promises were true and you broke them anyway. That does not make you a liar who never meant it. It makes you one of the people at the foot of Sinai.

  • Mean itSay it to God and to your brothers. A promise made out loud is stronger than one made alone.
  • Do not rest on itYour standing before God is not your promise. It is the blood of the covenant.
  • Fall and get upIf you fall, you are still a man the blood was shed for. Tell a brother the same day, and keep going.

Your hope is not in how well you keep your word. It is in a covenant God made and sealed for you. “The blood of the new testament… shed for many for the remission of sins.”

Part two

“They saw the God of Israel”

Verses 9–11

After the blood, seventy-four men go up the mountain, and sit down to eat with God.

What they saw

  • a paved worka floor, like tiles laid down
  • sapphire stonea deep blue, clear and bright
  • the body of heaven in his clearnesslike the sky itself, clear and bright
  • noblesthe chief men of Israel

Notice what Moses does not describe. He tells us what lay under his feet, a sky-blue pavement, and nothing about a face. The men saw something real, and it was more than words could hold.

“No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.”

John 1:18

John says no one has seen God as He is in Himself, and the Son has made Him known. What the elders saw was the God of Israel making Himself seen, to the degree men could stand. In Jesus we see all that can be seen of God.

“He laid not his hand… and they did eat and drink”

Unholy men in front of a holy God. The elders could have been struck down, and “upon the nobles of the children of Israel he laid not his hand.” They were not destroyed.

Why not? Because of what had just happened at the foot of the mountain. The blood had been sprinkled. They came up under the covenant.

And they “did eat and drink.” A covenant meal. In the Bible, to sit and eat with someone is to be at peace with him. The men went up to meet God, and God had them stay and eat.

There is a day coming when Jesus will drink with His people again (Matthew 26:29). A man covered by the blood does not have to fear the table. He is invited to it.

Part three

“Moses was in the mount forty days”

Verses 12–18

Moses goes up into the cloud, and the people are left waiting at the foot.

Up the mountain

Mount Sinai, “the mount of God,” in Arabia (Galatians 4:25). The LORD calls Moses higher: “Come up to me into the mount, and be there.”

  • tablesflat slabs, tablets of stone
  • ministera helper, an assistant (Joshua)
  • tarrywait

“A law, and commandments which I have written; that thou mayest teach them.” God writes, and Moses teaches.

“Aaron and Hur are with you”

Moses leaves the elders with Aaron and Hur: “if any man have any matters to do, let him come unto them.” They are the men who held up his arms at Rephidim:

“But Moses’ hands were heavy; and they took a stone, and put it under him, and he sat thereon; and Aaron and Hur stayed up his hands, the one on the one side, and the other on the other side; and his hands were steady until the going down of the sun.”

Exodus 17:12

Good men, left in charge, with no word of when Moses would come back. “Tarry ye here for us.” The waiting will be harder than the climbing. In chapter 32 the people will say, “as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him.”

Your own waiting is like that. A man can look strong in the meeting and be tempted when nothing is happening. Do not drift when the leader is out of sight. Stay near the brothers who hold your arms up.

Six days in the cloud, then forty

  1. six daysthe cloud covers itthe glory abides
  2. the seventh dayHe calls Moses“out of the midst of the cloud”
  3. forty daysMoses in the mountand forty nights

Abode means stayed, rested. Gat him up is “got himself up.” The glory of the LORD looked “like devouring fire” on the top, in plain sight of everyone below, and Moses walked into the midst of it.

“And he was there with the LORD forty days and forty nights; he did neither eat bread, nor drink water. And he wrote upon the tables the words of the covenant, the ten commandments.”

Exodus 34:28

Forty days, a day at a time

one dot for every day and night

Forty days at the bottom of the mountain is a long time. The people could see the fire on the top, and they could not see Moses. Down below, they will decide he is not coming back.

But up on the mountain, God was giving Moses the plan for a tent where He could live among them: the pattern for the tabernacle, in the next chapters. While the people grew restless, God was already preparing to come and stay.

When it feels like God is silent, and nothing is happening, He may be arranging the thing you cannot yet see. Keep your place, and keep to the brothers, while He does.

Points to remember

  1. The promise“All the words which the LORD hath said will we do.” 24:3The people meant it, and could not keep it. We have all said the same.
  2. The altar“builded an altar under the hill, and twelve pillars, according to the twelve tribes of Israel” 24:4Every tribe was in the covenant, and the way to God went by an altar and a sacrifice.
  3. The blood“Behold the blood of the covenant, which the LORD hath made with you” 24:8The covenant is sealed with blood, not promises. Jesus used the same words about His own blood.
  4. Seeing God“they saw the God of Israel… also they saw God, and did eat and drink” 24:10–11Under the blood, men met a holy God and lived, and sat at His table.
  5. The mount“Come up to me into the mount, and be there: and I will give thee tables of stone” 24:12Moses went up, and left the others to wait. Do not drift when the leader is out of sight.
  6. Forty days“Moses was in the mount forty days and forty nights” 24:18When God is silent, He may be preparing something. Stay in your place.

Question 1 of 6

The promise

What did the people say, twice, when Moses read them the book?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ, that we might be justified by faith.”

Galatians 3:24

Answer

“All that the LORD hath said will we do.” They meant it, and in forty days they would break it. We say the same things. The law shows us we cannot, and it brings us to Christ. Say it out loud to your brothers, and then lean on the blood.

Question 2 of 6

The altar

What did Moses build and offer at the foot of the mount?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

An altar with twelve pillars, one for each tribe, and burnt offerings and peace offerings of oxen. All of it given to God, and a meal with Him. A covenant starts with a sacrifice.

Question 3 of 6

The blood

What did Moses say as he sprinkled the blood on the people?

What verses tell us about this?

“For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins.”

Matthew 26:28

“And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.”

Hebrews 9:22

Answer

“Behold the blood of the covenant.” Jesus said nearly the same at the Last Supper, about His own blood, shed for the forgiveness of sins. Your standing with God is sealed with His blood, not your promise.

Question 4 of 6

Seeing God

What did the elders see, and what did they do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They saw the God of Israel, with a sapphire-blue pavement under His feet, and God did not strike them. They ate and drank in His presence. Under the blood, a man can come near and sit at His table.

Question 5 of 6

The mount

Why did the LORD call Moses up, and who stayed behind?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To give him the tables of stone, the law and commandments God had written. The elders stayed with Aaron and Hur. Waiting was the hard part. Stay near the brothers who hold up your arms, and do not drift.
