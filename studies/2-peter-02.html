2 Peter 2

Promised freedom, delivered chains

Peter warns about men who talk smooth, sell religion, and lead people back into the very things they escaped. It is a hard chapter. It is also a chapter about how God gets His own people out.

2 Peter in one picture

1Growadd to your faith1:5
2Bewarethere shall be false teachers among you2:1

Chapter 1 told us what God has given and how to grow. Today is the warning: not everyone who talks about God is on God's side. Peter is not trying to scare you. He wants you to know a counterfeit when it walks in the door.

Part one

They slip in

Verses 1–3

Every age has teachers who bring in something that sounds like God but sells a different thing.

“The Lord that bought them”

The word “bought” is the slave-market word: pay a price, set a slave free. Peter says the Lord paid it even for these men, who now deny Him. Christ didn't die for a few. He died for everyone.

The price was paid for the whole world (1 John 2:2), but a gift has to be received. These teachers turned their backs on the One who bought them.

Three marks of the counterfeit

  1. privilythey slip in quietly, in the dark, like a thief
  2. feigned wordsthe words sound right, but they're made up
  3. merchandiseyou are what they're selling

“Make merchandise of you” means they treat people as stock to be sold. Their real aim is money, or power, or a following. If a teacher's message always ends at your wallet, with “send in a seed and God will multiply it,” that is merchandise. God's good news is free.

And verse 2 tells you what it costs: “the way of truth shall be evil spoken of.” A fake makes the real thing look bad. If a church or a preacher burned you, don't throw out Jesus with the counterfeit. He was never in on it.

Part two

God has seen this before

Verses 4–9

Peter gives three examples from the Old Testament. In every one, God judged the wicked and carried His own people through.

Peter is answering a worry: “These teachers get away with it. Where's God?” His answer: God has never been asleep. He judges, and He also saves. The only thing He's slow on is judgment (that's chapter 3). The hell in verse 4 is a Greek word that appears only here, for the holding place of the angels who sinned.

How Lot ended up in Sodom

  1. lookedtoward the well-watered plain (Genesis 13:10)
  2. pitched his tenttoward Sodom (13:12)
  3. lived inSodom (14:12)
  4. sat in the gateof the city (19:1)

Lot was no hero, and look how far he slid. Yet Peter calls him “just Lot” and “that righteous man.” A man sick of a foul place “from day to day” is not the same as the men who love it. “Vexed” means worn out by what he saw and heard.

“And while he lingered, the men laid hold upon his hand, and upon the hand of his wife, and upon the hand of his two daughters; the Lord being merciful unto him: and they brought him forth, and set him without the city.”

Genesis 19:16

Lot lingered, and God brought him out anyway. If the old crowd and the old streets are pulling at you, know this: your rescue never depended on how fast you ran.

He knows how

The Lord knoweth how to deliver.

Notice who knows: the Lord. You don't have to work out the exit alone. He did it for Noah and for Lot, and He does it for men like you.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Look for the way out, and take it when it shows: leave the room, make the call, turn the car around.

Part three

Wells without water

Verses 10–17

Peter describes the teachers plainly, so that no one is fooled by a good speaker.

A prophet who loved the pay

Balak, a kingCome and curse God's people for me, and I will pay you well.

Balaam, the prophetHe knew better. He went as far as he dared, because he “loved the wages of unrighteousness.”

Balaam's story is in Numbers 22 to 24. He knew God's will and still ached for the money. It took a talking donkey to stop him: “the dumb ass speaking with man’s voice forbad the madness of the prophet.” The animal had more sense than the man.

Money is a quiet master. Before you listen to anyone's advice about God, ask what they gain if you say yes.

Dry wells

In a dry country a well without water is a cruel joke: a traveler walks miles to it and finds nothing. These teachers have the look and the loud voice of a good source. Come thirsty, and you leave thirstier. Jesus said, “If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink.”

Part four

Promised liberty, bought chains

Verses 18–22

The chapter ends with the sentence every addict can recite, and one verse that is hard to read.

Anyone who has been through it knows the shape of this. The first drink, the first hit, the first bet: it was sold as freedom. And it was, for an hour. “Of whom a man is overcome, of the same is he brought in bondage.” What you thought you were using ends up using you.

The teachers in this chapter sell “liberty”, a religion with no rules and no cost, and they are slaves themselves. A slave can't lead another man out.

The way out is a different Master

Whoever sells you "freedom"

Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin

That is Jesus' own description (John 8:34). Every road ends in a master.

Jesus

If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed

He doesn't just say it. He paid for it. John 8:36.

The honest news is that freedom isn't having no master. It is having the right One. Today, tell Him what you're being pulled toward. And tell one man as well.

The dog and the sow

This is a hard verse, so read it carefully. Peter is describing the false teachers of verse 1. They cleaned up on the outside by knowing about Christ, but never got a new nature. Washing a sow doesn't make her a lamb.

A sheep that falls in the mud is still a sheep. Believers are “partakers of the divine nature” (1:4). A believer can wander, but these teachers never became sheep.

“They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us: but they went out, that they might be made manifest that they were not all of us.”

1 John 2:19

If you fell last night

Peter's warning is about the dangers of going back, and he means it. The old life isn't a harmless place to visit. “The latter end is worse.” But hear what a son does, as opposed to a dog:

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

“All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.”

John 6:37

A dog goes back to its vomit and doesn't mind. A son who has slipped is sick over it and gets up and heads home. If the sight of the mud makes you sick, that is not the sign of a lost man. That's the Spirit in a son. Get up. Call your sponsor. Tell God, and tell a brother, today.

Points to remember

  1. Slipped in“False teachers among you, who privily shall bring in damnable heresies, even denying the Lord that bought them” 2:1Counterfeits come in quietly. Christ paid for everyone, even the ones who turn away.
  2. The motive“Through covetousness shall they with feigned words make merchandise of you” 2:3Ask what a teacher gains. If it always ends at your wallet, walk away.
  3. God's examples“Saved Noah the eighth person… delivered just Lot” 2:5, 7God judges sin, and He carries His own through it.
  4. Delivered“The Lord knoweth how to deliver the godly out of temptations” 2:9You don't plan the escape alone. He knows how. Take the way out when He shows it.
  5. Promised freedom“While they promise them liberty, they themselves are the servants of corruption” 2:19What promised freedom is often a chain. The real freedom is the Son's.
  6. Back to the mire“The dog is turned to his own vomit again; and the sow that was washed to her wallowing in the mire” 2:22Never treat the old life as home. A son who slips gets up and goes back to the Father.

Slipped in

How do false teachers come in, and what do they deny?

What verses tell us about this?

They come in quietly, in secret, bringing in destructive teaching. They deny the Lord who bought them. Christ paid for the whole world, and they turn away from the One who paid.

Question 2 of 6

The motive

What drives them, and what do they do with the people?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Covetousness: they want what you have. They make merchandise of the people, selling them smooth words. Ask what a teacher gains if you say yes.

Question 3 of 6

God's examples

What examples of judgment does Peter give, and whom did God save?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The angels who sinned, the world of Noah's day, and Sodom and Gomorrha. God saved Noah, the eighth person, and just Lot. He judges sin, and He carries His own through it.

Promised freedom

What do these teachers promise, and what are they themselves?

What verses tell us about this?

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

Answer

They promise liberty, and they are themselves the servants of corruption. Whatever overcomes a man becomes his master. The real way out is a different Master: the Son.
