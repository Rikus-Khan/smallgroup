Deuteronomy 23

The congregation, the curse turned to a blessing, and the LORD in the midst of the camp

Who may enter the congregation of the LORD, and why Moab may not. The curse Balaam was hired to speak, turned into a blessing. A clean camp, because the LORD walks in it. A runaway servant given a home. And a word, once spoken to God, kept.

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

“The congregation of the LORD”

Verses 1–8

Who may come in, who may not, and the door God opened later.

Kept out, and let in

The congregation of the LORD is Israel gathered before him as his people. Verse 1 speaks of a man so wounded in his body that he could not father children (privy means private). Verse 2 speaks of a child born of a forbidden union. The law kept them out. Then, through Isaiah, the LORD spoke of a day to come:

“Neither let the son of the stranger, that hath joined himself to the LORD, speak, saying, The LORD hath utterly separated me from his people: neither let the eunuch say, Behold, I am a dry tree. For thus saith the LORD unto the eunuchs that keep my sabbaths, and choose the things that please me, and take hold of my covenant; Even unto them will I give in mine house and within my walls a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters: I will give them an everlasting name, that shall not be cut off.”

Isaiah 56:3–5

The eunuch on the road

“And he arose and went: and, behold, a man of Ethiopia, an eunuch of great authority under Candace queen of the Ethiopians, who had the charge of all her treasure, and had come to Jerusalem for to worship,”

Acts 8:27

“And Philip said, If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest. And he answered and said, I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God. And he commanded the chariot to stand still: and they went down both into the water, both Philip and the eunuch; and he baptized him.”

Acts 8:37–38

Here is a man the law of verse 1 kept out of the congregation, a stranger as well, coming home from Jerusalem with a scroll of Isaiah. Philip preached Jesus to him, he believed, and he was baptized on the spot. He “went on his way rejoicing” (Acts 8:39).

Whatever has been done to you, or whatever you have done, the door into Christ is not shut. “If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest.”

“Because the LORD thy God loved thee”

We saw it happen in Numbers 22–24. Balak of Moab hired Balaam to curse Israel, and three times the curse came out a blessing: “I called thee to curse mine enemies, and, behold, thou hast altogether blessed them these three times” (Numbers 24:10).

Now Moses tells the new generation why: not because Israel was good, but “because the LORD thy God loved thee.” It is the same reason he gave in 7:8. Ammon and Moab would not even give a hungry people bread and water, and then paid a prophet to curse them. The LORD would not hearken.

“Because they met not the children of Israel with bread and with water, but hired Balaam against them, that he should curse them: howbeit our God turned the curse into a blessing.”

Nehemiah 13:2

Long after, in Nehemiah's day, the people heard this chapter read “in the audience of the people” (Nehemiah 13:1) and still said it: our God turned the curse into a blessing. Whatever has been spoken over your life, the LORD has the last word.

A woman of Moab

The law shut the door of the congregation on Moab, for what Moab did. It never shut the door on faith. In the days of the judges, a widow of Moab said this to her mother-in-law:

“And Ruth said, Intreat me not to leave thee, or to return from following after thee: for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God:”

Ruth 1:16

“And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth; and Obed begat Jesse;”

Matthew 1:5

Ruth the Moabitess became the great-grandmother of King David, and Matthew names her in the line of Jesus Christ. Next to her stands Rachab, that is Rahab of Jericho, who “hid the messengers” (Joshua 6:25). God puts his grace where men would never think to look.

Two kinds of neighbour

Not to the tenth generation

Ammon and Moab

“they met you not with bread and with water”, and hired Balaam to curse you (verses 3–4).

In the third generation

Edom and Egypt

The Edomite, “for he is thy brother”: Edom is Esau, Jacob's brother. The Egyptian, “because thou wast a stranger in his land”.

Egypt had made them slaves, and still God said: don't abhor him. Egypt had also taken Jacob's family in when the famine came, and given them a home. Abhor means to hate as loathsome. God would not let Israel live on a grudge.

Most of us carry one: against a father, an ex, a man who did us wrong. God asks us to remember the bread we were given too, and not to let the wrong turn into hatred.

Part two

“Therefore shall thy camp be holy”

Verses 9–14

The LORD walks through the camp, so even the ordinary things matter.

The LORD in the midst

Out on campaign, far from home, men get careless: “keep thee from every wicked thing.” A man unclean by something that “chanceth him by night” (happens to him) went out of the camp, washed, and came back at sundown. And the plain business of the body was done in “a place… without the camp”, with a paddle (a small spade) on his gear to cover it.

Why such ordinary rules? Because “the LORD thy God walketh in the midst of thy camp.” Nothing in a man's life is too ordinary for God to care about it, because he lives right there with him.

He walks in us now

“And what agreement hath the temple of God with idols? for ye are the temple of the living God; as God hath said, I will dwell in them, and walk in them; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people.”

2 Corinthians 6:16

Israel kept the camp clean because God walked in it. The believer has God closer than that: the Holy Spirit lives in him from the day he is saved. He does not leave when we fail; he is the one who convicts us and cleans us up.

So the question for a man away from home, on a job site, in a motel, on the road, is the same as Israel's in the field: is my camp clean? What's on my phone, in my room, in my truck? “That he see no unclean thing in thee.”

Part three

“That which is gone out of thy lips”

Verses 15–25

A refuge for the runaway, clean money, a kept word, and a neighbour's field.

A runaway given a home

A servant who fled his master and came to Israel was not to be handed back. He could live in any town he chose, “where it liketh him best” (where it pleases him), and no one was to oppress him. Israel had been slaves in Egypt, and the LORD had brought them out. Now Israel was to be a refuge.

“The LORD also will be a refuge for the oppressed, a refuge in times of trouble.”

Psalm 9:9

Many of us ran from a hard master: the bottle, the needle, the street, the gang. The LORD does not hand a man back to it. He gives him a place to live, and he says to the rest of us: don't oppress him; make room.

Not that money

No daughter of Israel was to sell herself, and no son of Israel was to give himself to that sin. And God would not take the money from it: not “the hire of a whore”, and not “the price of a dog”, which the verse sets beside it as money from the same kind of trade. God is not paid off with the wages of sin.

“What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God’s.”

1 Corinthians 6:19–20

Some of us have bought sin, or sold ourselves to get by. We were bought back at a price no one could pay but Christ. A man who is bought doesn't have to sell himself any more.

Don't make money off a brother's trouble

Usury is interest on a loan; victuals are food. A man who lent his brother money, or food, was not to take back more than he gave. With a stranger, a foreigner, he might. A brother in need was to be helped, not milked.

“In whose eyes a vile person is contemned; but he honoureth them that fear the LORD. He that sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not. He that putteth not out his money to usury, nor taketh reward against the innocent. He that doeth these things shall never be moved.”

Psalm 15:4–5

Fronting a brother and charging him double on payday, or lending to a man you know can't pay so he owes you: that is the old life. Help him, and take nothing for it.

A word spoken to God

We read the law of vows in Numbers 30: “he shall not break his word.” Here Moses adds three things.

  • Don't be slow“thou shalt not slack to pay it”: slack means to be late, to put it off. God “will surely require it”.
  • You don't have to“if thou shalt forbear to vow, it shall be no sin in thee.” Forbear means to hold back. No man is made to vow.
  • But once it's said“That which is gone out of thy lips thou shalt keep and perform”, even a freewill offering that no one asked of you.

The promise made in a crisis (“God, get me out of this and I'll…”) is still a promise. If you made one, keep it. If you can't keep it yet, tell God so, and start. And be slow to make the next one.

Eat, but don't carry it off

A hungry man walking past a vineyard or a field of grain could eat his fill. He could not fill a basket, or bring a sickle. The owner was to be generous; the passer-by was to be honest. Corn is grain, and the ears are its heads.

“At that time Jesus went on the sabbath day through the corn; and his disciples were an hungred, and began to pluck the ears of corn, and to eat. But when the Pharisees saw it, they said unto him, Behold, thy disciples do that which is not lawful to do upon the sabbath day.”

Matthew 12:1–2

The disciples were doing just what this verse allows. The Pharisees did not say it was stealing; they found fault only with the day. And Jesus answered that “the Son of man is Lord even of the sabbath day” (Matthew 12:8).

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28

Real world: a clean camp and a kept word

  • SinRefusing bread to the hungry, a grudge that turns to hatred, an unclean camp, a broken promise, money made from sin or from a brother's need.
  • RegretSome of us have been the eunuch shut out, the runaway, or the man who sold himself. Some of us have been the man who took more than his hand could hold.
  • Grace“The LORD thy God turned the curse into a blessing unto thee, because the LORD thy God loved thee.”
  • HopeThe eunuch was baptized, Ruth came in, and the runaway found a home. The door into Christ is open to you.
  • TodayClean one thing out of your camp today: your phone, your room, your truck. Keep one promise you've been putting off. And if you owe a brother, or he owes you, settle it without taking more.

Points to remember

  1. The congregation“Because they met you not with bread and with water in the way… and because they hired against thee Balaam” 23:4The law shut the door on Moab; it never shut the door on faith.
  2. The blessing“the LORD thy God turned the curse into a blessing unto thee, because the LORD thy God loved thee” 23:5The LORD has the last word over what is spoken against his people.
  3. The brother“Thou shalt not abhor an Edomite; for he is thy brother… because thou wast a stranger in his land” 23:7Don't live on a grudge; remember the bread you were given.
  4. The camp“For the LORD thy God walketh in the midst of thy camp… therefore shall thy camp be holy” 23:14God lives with his people, so nothing is too ordinary to keep clean.
  5. The runaway“Thou shalt not deliver unto his master the servant which is escaped from his master unto thee” 23:15The LORD does not hand a man back to his old master.
  6. The vow“That which is gone out of thy lips thou shalt keep and perform” 23:23You don't have to vow; once you have, pay it, and don't be slow.

Question 1 of 6

The congregation

Why were the Ammonite and the Moabite kept out of the congregation of the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because they did not meet Israel with bread and water on the way out of Egypt, and because they hired Balaam to curse them.

Question 2 of 6

The blessing

What did the LORD do with Balaam's curse, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He would not hearken to Balaam, and he turned the curse into a blessing, because he loved Israel.

Question 3 of 6

The brother

Why was Israel not to abhor the Edomite or the Egyptian?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The Edomite was Israel's brother, and Israel had been a stranger in the Egyptian's land. Their children could enter the congregation in the third generation.

Question 4 of 6

The camp

Why was Israel's camp to be kept holy?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the LORD walked in the midst of the camp to deliver them, and he was to see no unclean thing in it.

Question 5 of 6

The runaway

What was Israel to do with a servant who had escaped from his master?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not hand him back, but let him live among them in the town he chose, and not oppress him.

Question 6 of 6

The vow

What does Moses say about a vow made to the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Don't be slow to pay it, for the LORD will require it. Not vowing is no sin. But what has gone out of your lips, you must keep and perform.
