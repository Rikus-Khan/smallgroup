Numbers 11

“We remember the fish”

The people complain, and a mixt multitude falls a lusting for the food of Egypt until the whole camp is weeping for it. Moses breaks under the load. The LORD gives him seventy helpers, pours out his Spirit, and gives the people what they craved.

Part one

Fire at the edge of the camp

Verses 1–3

The LORD hears it when his people complain, and he hears it when one man prays.

Two cries, two answers

  • kindledlit, set burning: his anger was kindled
  • consumedburned up
  • uttermostfarthest, outermost: the edge of the camp
  • quenchedput out

The people complained

“The LORD heard it”

Moses doesn't say what they complained about. It was enough that it “displeased the LORD”, and fire burned “in the uttermost parts of the camp”.

The people cried to Moses

“Moses prayed”

The same people turned to a man who stood between them and God. He prayed, and the fire was put out. The place got a name that says what happened there: Taberah, “because the fire of the LORD burnt among them”.

A man prayed, and the fire went out

Paul says these things “happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition” (1 Corinthians 10:11), and about Israel's grumbling in the wilderness he writes, “Neither murmur ye, as some of them also murmured, and were destroyed of the destroyer” (10:10). God is holy, and a camp that only grumbles is a camp in danger.

But look at the end of the story: a man prayed, and the fire went out. Moses stood between the people and the fire. And there is one who does it for ever:

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Part two

“We remember the fish”

Verses 4–9

Fed every morning by a miracle, a whole camp weeps for the menu of the place that enslaved it.

The mixt multitude

Mixt is the KJV's old spelling of mixed. When Israel left Egypt, “a mixed multitude went up also with them” (Exodus 12:38). The text says only that they were “among them”, and then names “the children of Israel” separately: they “also wept again”. The craving started in the crowd on the edge, and it spread to the camp.

  • not “the whole camp began it”it began with the crowd among them
  • not “only the others were to blame”“the children of Israel also wept again”
  • a craving that spreadfrom the edge to the whole camp

To lust, in the KJV, is to crave strongly: here, for meat. Being tempted is not itself the sin: Jesus “was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin” (Hebrews 4:15). The trouble is what the craving does when it is let to talk.

Know who is at your tent door

“Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.”

1 Corinthians 15:33

Some of the loudest voices in this camp had left Egypt with everyone else, and they had not left Egypt's appetite behind. The people from your old life still come along into the new one, and their craving is catching.

That doesn't mean hating them. It means knowing whose voice you are listening to at night, and who you stand next to when it starts. Keep the company of men who are walking away from Egypt, not back toward it.

The fish, and the whip

What they remembered

“Freely”

“We remember the fish, which we did eat in Egypt freely; the cucumbers, and the melons, and the leeks, and the onions, and the garlick.” Six foods, said with a sigh. Freely means for nothing, without paying.

What Egypt was

“Hard bondage”

“And they made their lives bitter with hard bondage, in morter, and in brick, and in all manner of service in the field: all their service, wherein they made them serve, was with rigour.”

Exodus 1:14

Nothing in Egypt was free. They paid for the fish with their backs. A craving remembers the fish and forgets the whip: it keeps the pleasant part of the old life and leaves out the bill. That is not honest memory. It is the craving talking.

“What fruit had ye then in those things whereof ye are now ashamed? for the end of those things is death.”

Romans 6:21

When Egypt starts to look good

Every man here knows this chapter from the inside. The old life, in a bad week, grows a good smell: the bar's warm light, the old crowd laughing, the first minute after, and none of the morning. The picture in your head has the fish and no brick kilns.

So do what Israel did not: tell the whole story. Say it out loud to a brother in Christ: what it cost you, who got hurt, where it ended up, and what the morning after was like. Say the whole of it, the fish and the whip. A craving cannot stand being told in full.

And Moses chose the other way: he refused “to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season” (Hebrews 11:25). The word is season. It was short. What followed was not. Being tempted is not what undoes a man. Weeping about it alone, and going back to take a second look, is.

“Nothing at all, beside this manna”

  • coriander seedsmall, round and pale (Exodus 16:31)
  • bdelliuma sweet-smelling resin, a pale gum, which is what the manna looked like
  • mills, mortar, panshand mills to grind it, a bowl to pound it in, pans to bake it. A man could make cakes of it
  • fresh oilits taste was like fresh olive oil

The bread from heaven fell on the camp with the dew every night. They ground it, pounded it, baked it, and made cakes. And what did the people say? “There is nothing at all, beside this manna, before our eyes.” A miracle is “nothing at all” once it is the same miracle every day. Later they will call it “this light bread” and loathe it (21:5).

Ingratitude makes everything the LORD gave look like nothing. The test for a craving is simple: what am I not seeing on my table today? A job, a clean day, a brother who answers the phone, a Saviour: write them down. “Our soul is dried away” was what they said while they were being fed by God.

How the camp went down

  1. a crowd lustsand Israel weeps with them (verse 4)
  2. the fish rememberedthe whip forgotten (verse 5)
  3. the manna is nothing“nothing at all” (verse 6)
  4. weeping, each alone“every man in the door of his tent” (verse 10)
  5. “Why came we forth out of Egypt?”the whole road regretted (verse 20)

Five steps. It did not start with a plate of fish. It started with a thought that was fed, and it ended with a whole people asking whether leaving was a mistake. The word wept or weep comes five times in this chapter (verses 4, 10, 13, 18, 20).

Notice the tent doors: “Moses heard the people weep throughout their families, every man in the door of his tent.” A whole camp was crying, and each man was crying in his own doorway. Sorrow carried alone gets heavier. Take it to the other man before it gets to the fifth step.

Part three

“I am not able to bear all this people alone”

Verses 10–23

Moses has had enough, and says so to the LORD. The LORD does not scold him. He sends help.

A good man at the end of himself

  • afflictedtroubled, burdened with trouble
  • a nursing fathera man who carries a baby in his arms
  • swarestswore: you swore (to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob)
  • out of handat once, right now
  • wretchednessmisery, my own ruin

This is the “very meek” man (12:3), and the load has crushed him. He says to the LORD what he cannot say to the people: Why me? Did I give birth to them? I am not able. If this is how it is, kill me. He was at the end of himself, and he said it plainly.

Take it to the LORD, and to your brothers

Moses took it to the LORD, and God did not rebuke him. He sent seventy men. Elijah prayed the same way under a tree, “It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life” (1 Kings 19:4), and God's first answer was food and sleep: “Arise and eat” (19:5). If you ever sit where Moses sat in verse 15, call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor, today. Don't carry it alone to the tent door.

“Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.”

1 Peter 5:7

One load, seventy men

Moses alone

all this people
“too heavy for me”

Seventy with him

“that thou bear it not thyself alone”

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

The LORD did not make the load lighter. He made it shared. The same Spirit that was on Moses was put on seventy more. The Lord who says “Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest” (Matthew 11:28) gives that rest through his people.

“Why came we forth out of Egypt?”

Sanctify yourselves against to morrow means set yourselves apart, ready for tomorrow. They shall have meat, not for a day, nor five, nor twenty, “but even a whole month, until it come out at your nostrils, and it be loathsome unto you.”

Why? “Because that ye have despised the LORD which is among you.” The craving for fish was not about fish. It was a way of saying that the LORD in the middle of the camp was not enough. And the LORD answers, You want it? Have it, until you are sick of it.

He gave them their request

“And he gave them their request; but sent leanness into their soul.”

Psalm 106:15

Sometimes the hardest thing God lets a man do is get what he craves. It does not satisfy: a man who goes back to his old drink finds a leaner soul. And when the question comes, “Why came we forth out of Egypt?”, the answer is in Exodus 1:14: bondage, brick and rigour.

Moses counts, and the LORD asks

Moses

“Six hundred thousand footmen”

Footmen are men on foot: the fighting men, the 603,550 counted in chapter 1, besides their families. “Shall the flocks and the herds be slain for them… shall all the fish of the sea be gathered together for them?” He is doing the arithmetic.

The LORD

“Is the LORD’S hand waxed short?”

Waxed short means grown too short to reach. It is a gentle question, not a rebuke: Do you think my hand has shrunk? “Thou shalt see now whether my word shall come to pass unto thee or not.”

“Behold, the LORD’S hand is not shortened, that it cannot save; neither his ear heavy, that it cannot hear:”

Isaiah 59:1

Even the best man in the camp did the arithmetic and forgot the Giver. We do too: the bills, the days sober, the years that remain. The LORD does not want the sum worked out first. His hand is not short.

Part four

“Would God that all the LORD’S people were prophets”

Verses 24–30

The Spirit comes on seventy, and on two men who weren't even at the tent.

Two men who stayed in the camp

  1. the cloudthe LORD comes downverse 25
  2. seventythe Spirit rests on themat the tabernacle
  3. two moreEldad and Medadin the camp
  4. Moses“would God…”verse 29

Prophesied here means they spoke by the Spirit of God, and they “did not cease”: it was a sign, in front of everyone, that the Spirit of the LORD was on them. Eldad and Medad were written in the list of the seventy but “went not out unto the tabernacle”. They stayed in the camp, and the Spirit came to them there. He is not tied to a place or a meeting.

A young man ran to tell Moses. And Joshua, Moses' own servant, said, “My lord Moses, forbid them.” Joshua was guarding the leader's place. It is a good loyalty, aimed the wrong way. Moses answers, “Enviest thou for my sake?” (Are you jealous for my sake?) and then says his whole heart.

“Forbid him not”

Joshua, in the camp

“Forbid them”

Two men are prophesying, and they aren't the ones the leader chose. Joshua wants them stopped.

John, with Jesus

“We forbad him”

“And John answered him, saying, Master, we saw one casting out devils in thy name, and he followeth not us: and we forbad him, because he followeth not us.”

Mark 9:38

“But Jesus said, Forbid him not: for there is no man which shall do a miracle in my name, that can lightly speak evil of me. For he that is not against us is on our part.”

Mark 9:39–40

Two good men, centuries apart, made the same mistake: they thought God's work belonged to their circle. Both were answered by a man greater than themselves who wanted more people to have more of God. A new believer needs this too: don't envy another man's gift, and don't fence off the Lord's people.

Moses' wish came true

“And it shall come to pass in the last days, saith God, I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh: and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, and your young men shall see visions, and your old men shall dream dreams: And on my servants and on my handmaidens I will pour out in those days of my Spirit; and they shall prophesy:”

Acts 2:17–18

“But ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his.”

Romans 8:9

“Would God that all the LORD’S people were prophets, and that the LORD would put his spirit upon them!” Moses wished it, and on the day of Pentecost Peter said it was now being done. The Holy Spirit lives in every believer from the day he trusts Christ. You are not a second-class man in the camp: you did not need to be one of the seventy. If you are in Christ, he is in you.

Part five

“While the flesh was yet between their teeth”

Verses 31–35

The LORD gives them what they asked for, and it costs them dearly.

Quails from the sea

  1. they lust“Who shall give us flesh?”verse 4
  2. the windquails from the seaverse 31
  3. two days and a nightthey gather and spreadverse 32
  4. the plagueand the gravesverses 33–34

The LORD said he would do it, and he did. A wind from him brought quails from the sea, a day's journey around the camp on every side, as it were two cubits high (about three feet) on the ground. The man who gathered least gathered ten homers (a homer is a large dry measure of grain). They spread them out for themselves, all round the camp.

“And while the flesh was yet between their teeth, ere it was chewed” (ere means before), the wrath of the LORD was kindled and a very great plague struck. The place was named Kibroth-hattaavah, “because there they buried the people that lusted”. The psalm puts it this way: “while their meat was yet in their mouths, the wrath of God came upon them” (Psalm 78:30–31).

What the craving cost

Two places, two names. At Taberah the fire burned among them, and one man's prayer put it out. At Kibroth-hattaavah they buried “the people that lusted”: not the whole camp.

“Now these things were our examples, to the intent we should not lust after evil things, as they also lusted.”

1 Corinthians 10:6

“But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.”

James 1:14–15

That is the whole road: drawn away, enticed, conceived, finished. The chapter shows each step. It is not meant to frighten a man who has already fallen. It is meant to show him where the staircase starts, so he can step off at the top.

Fish or bread

The fish of Egypt

Craved, and not enough

Remembered fondly. Paid for with bondage. Gets a man what he asks for and leaves his soul lean. “The end of those things is death.”

The bread of heaven

Given, and enough

“I am that bread of life. Your fathers did eat manna in the wilderness, and are dead. This is the bread which cometh down from heaven, that a man may eat thereof, and not die.” John 6:48–50

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

The people who ate the manna died, some in the wilderness for their lusting, and all in the end. Jesus says the true bread from heaven is himself, given “for the life of the world” (John 6:51). The fish of Egypt promised a full stomach and delivered graves. Come to him, and the hunger that sends you back to Egypt is met.

  • Tell all of itThe fish and the whip. What it cost, who it hurt, how the morning felt.

  • Share the loadMoses wasn't meant to carry it alone, and you aren't either. Find your seventy: your brothers and your pastor.
  • Mind the crowdWho is in your “mixt multitude”? Their craving is catching.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

And if you ate the fish last week: the chapter does not end with Israel thrown out. It ends with “the people journeyed from Kibroth-hattaavah unto Hazeroth; and abode at Hazeroth.” The camp moved on, and so can you. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9). Confess it, get up, and take today's manna.

Question 1 of 6

Complaining

What happened when the people complained, and what quenched the fire?

What verses tell us about this?

“Neither murmur ye, as some of them also murmured, and were destroyed of the destroyer.”

1 Corinthians 10:10

Answer

“The LORD heard it”, his anger was kindled, and fire burned among them at the edge of the camp. When the people cried to Moses and Moses prayed, the fire was put out. Take the complaint to the LORD in prayer, and have a brother pray with you.

Question 2 of 6

Craving

Who started the craving for the food of Egypt, and what did they remember?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The mixt multitude among them, and then Israel too. They remembered the fish, the cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions and garlick, and forgot the “hard bondage” of Exodus 1:14 that went with them. When Egypt looks good, tell a brother the whole story, the fish and the whip.

Question 3 of 6

The burden

What did Moses say when the burden was too heavy, and who did the LORD give him to help?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“I am not able to bear all this people alone, because it is too heavy for me.” The LORD gave him seventy elders and put his Spirit on them to share the load. If it is too heavy for you, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today. He gave you them for this.

Question 4 of 6

The Spirit

What did Moses say when Eldad and Medad prophesied in the camp?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his.”

Romans 8:9

Answer

The spirit rested on Eldad and Medad in the camp, and Moses said, “Enviest thou for my sake? would God that all the LORD’S people were prophets.” Moses had no jealousy, and he wished the Spirit on everyone. In Christ it came true: the Holy Spirit lives in every believer.

Question 5 of 6

The cost

What happened when the LORD gave the people the flesh they craved?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A very great plague, and graves at Kibroth-hattaavah. “He gave them their request; but sent leanness into their soul” (Psalm 106:15). Getting what you crave does not satisfy. Step off the staircase at the top.

Question 6 of 6

Christ

What did Jesus say about the bread that comes down from heaven?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

“This is the bread which cometh down from heaven, that a man may eat thereof, and not die.”

John 6:50

Answer

That he himself is the true bread: those who ate the manna died, but whoever eats of this bread “shall live for ever”. Egypt's fish left a man lean. Come to him, hungry, today.
