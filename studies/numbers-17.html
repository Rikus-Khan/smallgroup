Numbers 17

The rod that budded

Twelve dead sticks are laid up before the LORD overnight, one for each tribe. In the morning one of them has budded, blossomed and borne almonds. God settles who he has chosen with life out of a dry rod, and keeps it as a token, “that they die not”.

After the plague

Chapter 16 asked one question over and over: whom has the LORD chosen to come near? Korah said every man. The earth opened, the fire fell, and the next morning the whole camp still murmured, and a plague took fourteen thousand and seven hundred.

The LORD's answer now is not another plague. It is a quiet test, kept overnight in his own tent, that anyone can come and look at in the morning. Moses had said it in 16:5: “the LORD will shew who are his … even him whom he hath chosen will he cause to come near unto him.” Chapter 17 is God showing it.

Part one

“Twelve rods”

Verses 1–7

A prince's name on every rod, and all of them laid up before the testimony.

Before the testimony

  • a roda staff: a prince's walking stick, the sign of his rule over his tribe
  • the testimonythe two tables of the law, kept in the ark
  • the tabernacle of witnessthe tabernacle, which held the testimony

Twelve princes, twelve rods, twelve names, and on Levi's rod, Aaron's name. Moses laid them before the ark, in the place where the LORD said, “I will meet with you”. Nobody could slip in during the night to fix the result: only the LORD could do anything there.

Life out of a dead stick

A rod is cut wood. It has no root and no sap. It does not bud in a year, let alone a night. Buds, blossoms and ripe almonds all at once on a dry stick is not nature doing something unusual. It is the LORD doing what only he can do, and Moses brought the rods out before all the children of Israel, “and they looked” (verse 9).

The almond was no accident either. The golden candlestick in the holy place had its bowls “made like unto almonds” (Exodus 25:33). Aaron's rod bore the same fruit as the lamp he tended.

God showed whom he had chosen as priest by bringing life out of death. That is how he showed us his Son, the High Priest who “glorified not himself” (Hebrews 5:5):

“And declared to be the Son of God with power, according to the spirit of holiness, by the resurrection from the dead:”

Romans 1:4

Kept before the testimony

The other princes took their rods home (verse 9). Aaron's went back in before the testimony, to stay. A token is a sign, a proof; to quite take away is to take away wholly. The rod was a standing answer: the next time a man wanted to argue about who could come near, there it was, the rod that budded.

“Which had the golden censer, and the ark of the covenant overlaid round about with gold, wherein was the golden pot that had manna, and Aaron’s rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant;”

Hebrews 9:4

And notice why it was kept: “that they die not”. The token was against the rebels, but it was for their life. God would rather settle the question with a flowering stick than with another plague. Even his warnings are mercy.

From pride to despair

16:3

“Ye take too much upon you”

Every man can come near. We don't need Aaron.

17:12–13

“We all perish”

No man can come near. “Whosoever cometh any thing near … shall die.”

Both are wrong, and many of us swing between them: I can handle this myself, and then, after the fall, there's no hope for a man like me. God's answer comes in the very next chapter: the priests he chose would bear the iniquity and keep the charge of the sanctuary,

“And ye shall keep the charge of the sanctuary, and the charge of the altar: that there be no wrath any more upon the children of Israel.”

Numbers 18:5

Come near through him

“Shall we be consumed with dying?” No. Not if you come the way God made. We have a High Priest whom God chose and raised from the dead, and through him the way near is open:

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:15–16

If you are at the place Israel was at the end of this chapter, saying “I'm done, I perish”, don't sit alone with it. Call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor, today. Then come boldly, through Christ, to the throne of grace. It is a throne of grace.

Real world: the rod in blossom

  • SinMurmuring against the ones God put in place, and then deciding God is only out to destroy us.
  • RegretIsrael had a plague's worth of regret. Regret that ends in “we all perish” has forgotten the priest.
  • GraceGod answered the murmuring with a flowering stick, “that they die not”.
  • HopeThe God who made dead wood blossom overnight raised his Son, and he can bring life into a dry life.
  • TodayStop arguing about your place, and stop writing yourself off. Come near to God through Christ, and tell one brother where you are.

Points to remember

  1. The rods“write thou every man’s name upon his rod … lay them up … before the testimony” 17:2–4A prince's name on each of twelve rods, Aaron's on Levi's, laid up where only the LORD could act.
  2. The choice“the man’s rod, whom I shall choose, shall blossom” 17:5God would show whom he had chosen, and end the murmuring.
  3. The morning“was budded, and brought forth buds, and bloomed blossoms, and yielded almonds” 17:8Life out of a dead stick in one night: the priest God chose, shown by resurrection.
  4. The token“to be kept for a token against the rebels … that they die not” 17:10A standing answer, kept in the ark, and kept for their life. Even his warnings are mercy.
  5. The cry“Behold, we die, we perish, we all perish” 17:12From pride to despair. The answer to both is the priest God gave: come boldly through Christ.

It would blossom, and so the LORD would make the murmurings against Moses and Aaron cease. No man takes the priesthood to himself; God calls him.

Question 4 of 5

The token

Why was Aaron's rod kept before the testimony?

What verses tell us about this?

“Which had the golden censer, and the ark of the covenant overlaid round about with gold, wherein was the golden pot that had manna, and Aaron’s rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant;”

Hebrews 9:4

Answer

As a token against the rebels, to take away their murmurings wholly, “that they die not”. It was kept with the ark of the covenant, a standing answer and a mercy.
