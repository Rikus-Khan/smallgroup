Romans 8

No condemnation

It opens with “no condemnation” and closes with nothing that can separate us. In between, the Spirit of God moves in, calls you son, prays for you, and holds you to the end.

Chapter 7 ended with a cry and a thank-you. Chapter 8 is the answer to that cry. It begins with “no condemnation” and ends with nothing that “shall be able to separate us from the love of God.” Many believers count it the greatest chapter in the Bible.

“Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.”

John 5:24

The man who fell yesterday and confessed it this morning is in Christ Jesus. The end of the verse describes men like him: they “walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.” That's the direction of a life, not a perfect record. The verdict rests on being in Christ.

A stronger law

Chapter 7's “law of sin” pulls every time, like gravity. Here Paul names a stronger law.

Gravity never switches off; a stone still falls. But a living bird rises above it every morning, by the law of life. That's the Spirit in you. The pull is still there. It's no longer the strongest thing in you.

“Hath made me free”: already done.

What the law could not do, God did. He sent His Son “in the likeness of sinful flesh,” a real man without sin, “and for sin,” as an offering for it. On the cross God “condemned sin in the flesh,” in Jesus' own body.

The sentence was carried out. Just not on you. And God never collects the same debt twice.

Part two

Sons, not slaves

Verses 12–17

The Spirit of adoption, and a Father who never walks out.

We owe the flesh nothing

“Mortify” means put to death. Not manage it: kill it. And “through the Spirit,” not on willpower alone. Starve the flesh: the old numbers, the old places, the apps. Feed the Spirit: the Word, prayer, church, an honest friend.

Paul isn't saying a believer can lose his salvation (vv. 1, 39). He's saying the flesh's road always ends in death: dead marriages, dead dreams, early graves.

Abba, Father

אַבָּא said ab-bah “Father,” the word a child says at home

A slave lives in “fear” of the master. A son lives at home. Jesus Himself prayed “Abba, Father” the night before the cross (Mark 14:36). Now the Spirit puts that same word in your mouth.

Some of us never had a father worth the name. Some had one who left, or one we were afraid of. God isn't that father. He's the Father you never had, and He never walks out.

“When my father and my mother forsake me, then the LORD will take me up.”

Under Roman law, an adopted son took his new father's name and became his full heir. It was done before witnesses.

Your witness is the Spirit Himself. That quiet knowing, deep down, that God is your Father? That's Him.

Whatever belongs to Jesus, you share. And sons share His hard road too, on the way to the glory.

Part three

Groaning and glory

Verses 18–27

Hard days now, a glory coming, and help in between.

Not worth comparing

“I reckon”: I've done the math. Paul had been beaten, jailed and shipwrecked, and he weighed it all.

Put it on the scale: the lost years, the prison time, the cravings, the people who won't forgive you. Then put what God has coming for His children on the other side. It isn't close.

“For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory;”

2 Corinthians 4:17

Birth pains, not death pains

Since Adam's sin, all creation has been “subject to vanity,” never quite what it was made to be, and in “the bondage of corruption”: rust, sickness, death.

But “travail” is labor pain. It comes closer and harder, and it ends in a birth. We groan too, waiting for “the redemption of our body”: a new body with no craving in it.

Hope you can't see yet

The world's hope→“I hope it works out”a wish: maybe yes, maybe no
Bible hope→sure, just not here yet“hope that is seen is not hope” (v. 24)

Think of a man who's been told his release date. He can't see it yet, but it's coming, so he does his time with his head up. “Patience” here means endurance: staying under the load without quitting.

Help on the other end

“Infirmities” are weaknesses. “Helpeth” is a word for taking hold of a load together with someone. Martha used it: “bid her therefore that she help me” (Luke 10:40).

Some days you don't even know what to pray. You've got nothing but a groan. The Spirit carries that groan to the Father, “according to the will of God.”

And Jesus prays for you too (v. 34). One prays in you; One prays for you.

Where God is taking you

“Foreknow” means to know beforehand. Before the world began, God knew who would believe on His Son (1 Peter 1:2).

“Predestinate” is about the destination: every believer will be made like Jesus. The offer is to all; God is “not willing that any should perish” (2 Peter 3:9).

Paul even writes “glorified” as if it's already done. That's how sure your finish is.

He already gave the biggest gift

Hespared not his own Son,
→
sohow shall he not with him also freely give us all things?

If God already gave His own Son for you, the costliest gift heaven had, He won't hold back anything you truly need. And He gave Him “for us all,” not for a chosen few.

“If God be for us” doesn't mean “maybe He is.” It means since He is. The odds against you don't matter when God is on your side.

Court is in session

“Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God's elect?”God justifiesThe devil is “the accuser of our brethren” (Revelation 12:10). He can bring charges. But the Judge has already declared you righteous. “God's elect” are His chosen people: all who are in Christ.
“Who is he that condemneth?”Christ diedThe only One with the right to condemn you died for you, rose again, and sits at God's right hand, praying for you.
“Who shall separate us from the love of Christ?”NothingVerses 35–39 answer this one.

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Seven things that couldn't

Paul lived this whole list. “Tribulation” is pressure; “distress,” being hemmed in; “nakedness,” having nothing.

Verse 36 quotes Psalm 44:22. God's people have always suffered. Hard times don't mean He stopped loving you.

Trouble can take your job, your health, your freedom, even your life. It can't get between you and the love of Christ.

“the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord”

“More than conquerors” is one word in Greek, something like super-conquerors. Not just surviving: winning, “through him that loved us.” And “any other creature” includes you. You can't even separate yourself. That's eternal security: you're kept by His love, not by your grip.

From the cry to the song

Same man, same struggle, one chapter apart. The fight of chapter 7 doesn't vanish. But now he fights it as a son: the Spirit in him, no condemnation over him, and nothing able to pull him out of God's love.

Today, when the old pull comes, say out loud what's true: “There is therefore now no condemnation.” Then take the next right step, through the Spirit.

Points to remember

  1. Condemnation“There is therefore now no condemnation” 8:1For the man in Christ, the verdict is in: not guilty, today, for good.
  2. The Spirit“The Spirit also helpeth our infirmities” 8:26He lives in every believer, leads him, assures him, and prays for him.
  3. Sons“Whereby we cry, Abba, Father” 8:15You're not a slave in fear. You're an adopted son and heir, with a Father who stays.
  4. Suffering“Not worthy to be compared with the glory” 8:18Weigh today's pain against the glory coming. It isn't close.
  5. All things“All things work together for good” 8:28Not everything is good, but God works all of it together for good for His children.
  6. Security“Nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us” 8:39Nothing, not even you, can pull you out of God's love in Christ.

Question 1 of 6

Condemnation

What does God say to the man who is in Christ Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.”

John 5:24

Answer

“No condemnation,” not later but now. God condemned our sin in Jesus' body, so no sentence is left for us. God declares us righteous, and Christ, who died for us, prays for us.

Question 2 of 6

The Spirit

What does the Holy Spirit do for a believer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He lives in every believer from the moment he's saved. He frees us from sin's pull, leads us, tells our hearts we're God's children, helps us in our weakness, and prays for us when we have no words.

Question 3 of 6

Sons

What does it mean to cry “Abba, Father”?

What verses tell us about this?

“When my father and my mother forsake me, then the LORD will take me up.”

Psalm 27:10

Answer

It means we're adopted sons, not frightened slaves. “Abba” is the word a child calls his father at home, the word Jesus used. God gave us His name, made us His heirs with Christ, and He will never walk out on us.

Question 4 of 6

Suffering

How should a believer weigh the hard things he goes through?

What verses tell us about this?

“For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory;”

2 Corinthians 4:17

Answer

Put them on the scale against the glory to come. The pain is real, and we groan. But it's labor pain, not death pain, and it ends in new bodies and a new creation. Next to that glory, today's troubles are light and short.

Question 5 of 6

All things

What does Romans 8:28 promise, and what doesn't it?

What verses tell us about this?

“But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive.”

Genesis 50:20

Answer

It doesn't promise that everything is good, or that it will all feel good. It promises that God works all of it together for good for those who love Him. And the “good” is this: making us like Jesus.

Question 6 of 6

Security

What can separate a believer from the love of God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Nothing. Not trouble, not death, not any power, not anything today or tomorrow, not even ourselves. We're held by His love, not by our grip. That's eternal security, and it's the last word of the chapter.
