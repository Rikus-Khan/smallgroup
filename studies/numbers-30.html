Numbers 30

“He shall not break his word”

What a man says to the LORD, he does. Moses gives the heads of the tribes the law of vows: a man's word stands, and a household has its covering, and the LORD forgives where a vow is rightly made void. Jesus takes it one step further: let your yea be yea.

Part one

“He shall not break his word”

The chapter opens by saying who it was for: “Moses spake unto the heads of the tribes.” These were the men who would carry it into every household in Israel.

His word

He shall not break his word.

The rule for a man comes first, and it has no exceptions in it: “If a man vow a vow unto the LORD, or swear an oath to bind his soul with a bond; he shall not break his word, he shall do according to all that proceedeth out of his mouth” (verse 2). What he said is what he does.

Many of you know what a broken word costs, on both sides: the promises you were given that were never kept, and the promises you gave that you did not keep. “I'll be there.” “I'll pay it back.” “Never again.” A wife stopped believing it. A boy stopped asking. A boss stopped trusting it. This chapter does not wave that away, and it does not pile shame on it either. It starts by saying what a man's word is: something that binds him.

What a broken word leaves behind

“Confidence in an unfaithful man in time of trouble is like a broken tooth, and a foot out of joint.”

Proverbs 25:19

A broken tooth and a foot out of joint: both are things you lean on, and they give way when you put your weight on them. That is what a promise is for. People put their weight on it. Some of you have stood on a promise that gave way, and some of you were the broken tooth. Both are real, and neither is the end of the story.

“He that sweareth to his own hurt, and changeth not.”

Psalm 15:4

Psalm 15 asks who may “abide in thy tabernacle,” and this is one of the answers: the man who keeps his word when it costs him. When it does not cost anything, anyone can.

Better not to vow, than to vow and not pay

“When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it; for he hath no pleasure in fools: pay that which thou hast vowed. Better is it that thou shouldest not vow, than that thou shouldest vow and not pay.”

Ecclesiastes 5:4–5

Notice what Solomon does not say. He does not say “never promise anything.” He says: if you vow, pay, and do not put it off. And if you are not going to, it was better not to promise.

A man in recovery hears this twice. Do not make a big promise in a weak moment, in front of a crowd, to feel better. And when you have made one, honestly, do not let it drift.

“It is a snare to the man who devoureth that which is holy, and after vows to make enquiry.”

Proverbs 20:25

That is a snare: to make the vow first and then ask, “What did I just promise?” Count it before you say it.

Jesus: let your yea be yea

“Again, ye have heard that it hath been said by them of old time, Thou shalt not forswear thyself, but shalt perform unto the Lord thine oaths: But I say unto you, Swear not at all; neither by heaven; for it is God’s throne: Nor by the earth; for it is his footstool: neither by Jerusalem; for it is the city of the great King. Neither shalt thou swear by thy head, because thou canst not make one hair white or black. But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil.”

Matthew 5:33–37

“Ye have heard… perform unto the Lord thine oaths.” That is this chapter. Jesus does not throw it out. He goes to the root: a man whose yes is yes does not need an oath. A man who has to swear, “I swear on my mother, I swear on my life,” is a man whose plain yes has not been believed. “Communication” in the KJV means talk, what you say. Let it be yes or no.

“But above all things, my brethren, swear not, neither by heaven, neither by the earth, neither by any other oath: but let your yea be yea; and your nay, nay; lest ye fall into condemnation.”

James 5:12

Part two

“Her father… held his peace”

Verses 3–5

A young woman in her father's house, and the man who hears her vow.

Under a covering, and the day he hears

A young woman living in her father's house made a vow to the LORD. The law gives her father a part in it. If he hears it and says nothing, it stands. If he forbids it “in the day that he heareth,” it does not stand. And the LORD says what the vow is then: “the LORD shall forgive her, because her father disallowed her.”

Two things to see. First, the LORD does not hold a vow against someone whose own father stopped it. She is not made guilty. Second, the father cannot sit on it. His no has to come “in the day that he heareth.” Saying nothing is itself an answer: it confirms the vow. A man in charge who stays quiet has said yes.

This is the law for Israel's households, where a father stood over a daughter's youth. It does not say a woman's word is worth less to the LORD. It puts the weight of her promise on the man who stands over her, so that a girl could not be trapped by a rash promise she could not keep.

Part three

“Her husband hath utterly made them void”

One clock runs through the whole chapter: “in the day that he heard it.” A vow that is not stopped that day is established: “he confirmeth them, because he held his peace at her in the day that he heard them” (14). Silence counts as a yes.

“The LORD shall forgive her”

Three times in this chapter the LORD says it about a woman whose vow was stopped (5, 8, 12): “the LORD shall forgive her.” He does not say she was careless, and he does not hold her to a promise the one over her has rightly stopped. She is not left carrying the guilt.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

That is the same God and the same word. Whatever broken vow, broken oath or broken promise you carry, his answer to a man who comes clean is the same one: “he is faithful and just to forgive.” Not “try harder and we will see.”

“He shall bear her iniquity”

The last word on the husband is a hard one: if he hears the vow, says nothing, and then makes it void later, the vow is not simply cancelled. “Then he shall bear her iniquity” (15). The weight does not vanish. It moves onto him. A man who stands over others carries what he lets stand and what he stops too late. If he lets it go by, he owns it.

If you have a wife, a child, a younger brother in Christ, a man you are working with: what you hear and say nothing about is not neutral. Say it the day you hear it. Do not wait, and do not hold it for later.

And there is one who bore it all, not for his own failure:

“Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed.”

1 Peter 2:24

A rash oath, and the way back

What if a man swore something thoughtlessly, did not keep it, and only saw it later? Leviticus has an answer, and it is not “too bad”:

“Or if a soul swear, pronouncing with his lips to do evil, or to do good, whatsoever it be that a man shall pronounce with an oath, and it be hid from him; when he knoweth of it, then he shall be guilty in one of these. And it shall be, when he shall be guilty in one of these things, that he shall confess that he hath sinned in that thing: And he shall bring his trespass offering unto the LORD for his sin which he hath sinned, a female from the flock, a lamb or a kid of the goats, for a sin offering; and the priest shall make an atonement for him concerning his sin.”

Leviticus 5:4–6

Three steps: “when he knoweth of it,” “he shall confess,” and “the priest shall make an atonement for him.” That is the way back for a man who broke his word: know it, say it out loud, and bring what God provides. We bring Christ (Hebrews 10:12), not a lamb.

A word that cannot be broken

“That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation, who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us:”

Hebrews 6:18

Every man in this room has a word that has failed somebody. Hebrews says there are two things that cannot fail: what God promised and what he swore. “Impossible for God to lie.” His word does not depend on his mood, his strength, or what he can afford that week.

“But I will sacrifice unto thee with the voice of thanksgiving; I will pay that that I have vowed. Salvation is of the LORD.”

Jonah 2:9

Jonah says it from the belly of the fish: he will pay what he vowed, and then, “Salvation is of the LORD.” Keeping a vow is a good thing, and it does not save a man. The LORD does. A man who keeps his word is not earning anything. He is walking the way he was put on.

Real world: a yes that can be trusted

  • SinA broken word is a sin against the man who leaned on it, and the LORD heard you say it. Name it plainly. No excuses, and no drama.
  • RegretYou cannot unsay what you said. You can say it to the LORD, to the person, and to your brothers: “I said I would, and I did not.”
  • Grace“The LORD shall forgive.” “He is faithful and just to forgive.” You do not get a yea that is yea by trying alone.
  • HopeNext time the answer is a small, plain yes that you can keep. Trust grows back the way it was lost: one kept word at a time.
  • TodayMake one small promise and keep it before dark: be where you said, pay what you owe, make the call. Do not swear to anything. Say yes or no. And before you make a big promise to anyone, say it to a brother in Christ first, and to your pastor.

Points to remember

  1. His word“he shall not break his word, he shall do according to all that proceedeth out of his mouth” 30:2A man's vow stands. What he said, he does. Jesus: let your yea be yea.
  2. The daughter“But if her father disallow her in the day that he heareth; not any of her vows… shall stand” 30:5If her father holds his peace, it stands. If he disallows it the day he hears, it does not.
  3. The wife“But if her husband disallowed her on the day that he heard it; then he shall make her vow… of none effect” 30:8The husband may make it void the day he hears. After that day it stands.
  4. The widow“But every vow of a widow, and of her that is divorced, wherewith they have bound their souls, shall stand against her.” 30:9A widow's or a divorced woman's vow stands: no one stands over her to stop it.
  5. Forgiven“her husband hath made them void; and the LORD shall forgive her” 30:12The LORD forgives the woman whose vow is made void: said three times (5, 8, 12).
  6. The husband“But if he shall any ways make them void after that he hath heard them; then he shall bear her iniquity.” 30:15A husband who waits, then makes it void, bears her iniquity. Speak the day you hear.

Question 1 of 6

His word

What must a man do who vows a vow to the LORD, or swears an oath?

What verses tell us about this?

“But let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay: for whatsoever is more than these cometh of evil.”

Matthew 5:37

Answer

He shall not break his word. He shall do according to all that proceedeth out of his mouth. Jesus says to let our talk be plain: yea, yea, and nay, nay.

Question 2 of 6

The daughter

What happens to a young woman's vow in her father's house if he holds his peace, and if he disallows it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

If he hears it and holds his peace, all her vows stand. If he disallows her in the day that he heareth, none of them stand, and the LORD shall forgive her.

Question 3 of 6

The wife

If her husband hears her vow and disallows it, what happens to it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It is made of none effect: it does not stand. But if he holds his peace from day to day, he establishes all her vows.

Question 4 of 6

The widow

Whose vows stand against her?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The widow's, and the vows of her that is divorced. Every vow wherewith they have bound their souls shall stand against her.

Question 5 of 6

Forgiven

What does the LORD do for the woman whose vow is made void?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD shall forgive her: said of the daughter (5) and of the wife (8, 12).

Question 6 of 6

The husband

What happens to a husband who makes her vow void after he has heard it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed.”

1 Peter 2:24

Answer

He shall bear her iniquity. If he holds his peace in the day that he hears, he confirms the vow; if he waits and then makes it void, the weight is on him.
