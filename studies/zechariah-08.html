Part one

Jealous for Zion

Verses 1–8

Chapter 7 told the sad story of the past. Now God tells what He's going to do.

Ten times: “Thus saith the LORD”

Quick reminder: Zechariah preached in Jerusalem about 520–518 BC, after the exile, while Persia ruled and the returned Jews rebuilt the temple. In chapter 7, men asked whether to keep fasting. God's full answer is this chapter.

Ten times God signs His name to a promise. The men in Jerusalem were few, poor, and tired. They needed to know this wasn't Zechariah's wishful thinking. It was the word of “the LORD of hosts,” the Lord of heaven's armies.

A jealous God

God's jealousy isn't the petty, insecure kind. It's the fierce, protective love of a husband for his wife: he won't share her, and he'll fight for her. That love is why He was angry when she ran after idols, and it's why He's coming back for her now.

ruined Jerusalem → “a city of truth” “I am returned unto Zion, and will dwell in the midst of Jerusalem.” Zion is the hill of the temple, a name for the whole city. Where God lives, the place gets a new name.

“For thou shalt worship no other god: for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God:”

Exodus 34:14

A street worth living on

God's picture of a restored city isn't gold and armies. It's old folks sitting out with their walking sticks, and kids running loose in the street. Men live long, and children are safe. That's peace.

What they could see

Empty streets

Rubble, a half-built temple, and not many people. Even about seventy years later, Nehemiah found the city nearly empty.

What God promised

Full streets

Grandparents growing old in peace. Boys and girls playing. Families, home again.

“Now the city was large and great: but the people were few therein, and the houses were not builded.”

Nehemiah 7:4

Too hard for you. Not for Me.

“Marvellous” here means too wonderful, too hard to believe or do. The remnant looked at their ruins and thought: no way.

A clean life, a family put back together, peace in the morning: some days that looks as far off as children playing in Jerusalem's rubble. What's impossible for us is not hard for God.

“Ah Lord GOD! behold, thou hast made the heaven and the earth by thy great power and stretched out arm, and there is nothing too hard for thee:”

Jeremiah 32:17

My people. Their God.

Most Jews were still scattered: east in Babylon and Persia, west in Egypt and beyond. God promises to gather them from both directions, where the sun rises and where it sets, and bring them home to Jerusalem.

“They shall be my people, and I will be their God” is the heart of God's covenant with Israel, said again and again from Moses on. That promise is Israel's, and God will keep it. And it shows us what God is like: what He's always wanted is to live with His people. The Bible ends with the same words, for everyone who belongs to Him:

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.”

Revelation 21:3

Part two

Let your hands be strong

Verses 9–17

Hard years behind them. A new day ahead. Keep building.

“But now”

The prophets in verse 9 are Haggai and Zechariah, who preached when the temple's foundation was laid again, about 520 BC. Before that, the people had stopped building for about sixteen years, and everything went wrong.

Before these days

Nothing to show

No wages for men or animals. No peace going out or coming in. Every man against his neighbor.

But now

Growth

The seed grows, the vine bears, the ground gives its crop, and the sky gives its dew.

Some of us know those “before” years: work with nothing to show, no peace, everybody against everybody. God doesn't pretend they didn't happen. He says “but now.”

“Is the seed yet in the barn? yea, as yet the vine, and the fig tree, and the pomegranate, and the olive tree, hath not brought forth: from this day will I bless you.”

Haggai 2:19

From a curse to a blessing

When the nations wanted a picture of disaster, they pointed at Judah and Israel: may you end up like them. God says He'll turn that around.

“a curse among the heathen” → “a blessing” It's what God told Abraham at the very start: “thou shalt be a blessing” (Genesis 12:2). He hasn't forgotten.

Maybe your name was a warning in your family or your town. God can make the man who used to be the bad example into the man others come to for help. Notice the two commands that come with it: “fear not,” and “let your hands be strong.”

Just as sure

“As I thought to punish you”→“So again have I thought … to do well”He kept His word when He judged; He'll keep it now

“I repented not” means I didn't change My mind. When their fathers kept sinning, God said judgment would come, and it came. That's sobering. But it also means that when God says “I will do well unto you,” you can count on it just as much.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Built on truth

God's grace comes first (verses 1–15). Then He tells them how to live in it. It's the same list as chapter 7, and it's all about honesty:

  • lies to a neighbor“Speak ye every man the truth”
  • crooked courts“judgment of truth and peace in your gates,” where the elders held court
  • scheming in secret“let none of you imagine evil in your hearts”
  • swearing to a lie“love no false oath”

A city of truth needs men of truth. So does a life in recovery. Lies were the glue that held the old life together. The new life is built on telling the truth, even when it costs.

“Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another.”

Ephesians 4:25

Part three

Fasts into feasts

Verses 18–23

At last, God answers the question about fasting. And then the whole world starts coming.

Four sad days

By now the Jews kept four fasts, each one remembering a step in Jerusalem's fall to Babylon:

  1. tenth monththe siege begins2 Kings 25:1
  2. fourth monththe wall brokenJeremiah 39:2
  3. fifth monththe temple burned2 Kings 25:8–9
  4. seventh monthGedaliah killed2 Kings 25:25
  5. one dayall four: feastsjoy and gladness

The men in chapter 7 asked, “Should I weep?” God's answer: a day is coming when every one of your days of mourning will be a party. That's how God answers. Not only “you can stop crying,” but “I'll give you something to celebrate.”

“Thou hast turned for me my mourning into dancing: thou hast put off my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness;”

Psalm 30:11

“I will go also”

The “skirt” is the hem of a man's robe. Picture ten foreigners grabbing one Jewish man's robe and refusing to let go: “We will go with you: for we have heard that God is with you.” Once the nations laughed at Judah. Now they want what Judah has.

God made this promise to Israel, and He will keep it. But it already points to something we've seen: the Saviour came from Israel, and men from every nation have taken hold of Him. Jesus said it Himself:

“Ye worship ye know not what: we know what we worship: for salvation is of the Jews.”

John 4:22

“God is with you.”

Look at what draws people in verse 23. Not Jerusalem's walls or its money. People could see that God was with them.

That's still how it works. Men don't usually come to Jesus because we won an argument. They come because they knew us before, and they can see something's different now. Somebody watching you today may say, “I will go also.”

Points to remember

  1. God's love“I was jealous for Zion with great jealousy” 8:2God's love is fierce and protective. He fights for His people and comes back for them.
  2. Too hard?“Should it also be marvellous in mine eyes?” 8:6What looks impossible to us isn't hard for God.
  3. Courage“Fear not, but let your hands be strong” 8:13Keep building. God says “but now,” and He finishes what He starts.
  4. A fresh start“As ye were a curse … ye shall be a blessing” 8:13God can turn the bad example into a blessing to others.
  5. Truth“Speak ye every man the truth to his neighbour” 8:16God's city is a city of truth. A new life is built on honesty.
  6. Joy“Joy and gladness, and cheerful feasts” 8:19God doesn't just stop the mourning. He gives something to celebrate.

Question 1 of 6

God's love

How does God feel about His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“For thou shalt worship no other god: for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God:”

Exodus 34:14

Answer

He loves them with a jealous love: fierce, protective, and not willing to share them with idols. That's why He judged their sin, and it's why He came back to live among them. God's love isn't soft or careless. He fights for His own.

Question 2 of 6

Too hard?

Is anything too hard for God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Is any thing too hard for the LORD? At the time appointed I will return unto thee, according to the time of life, and Sarah shall have a son.”

Genesis 18:14

“For with God nothing shall be impossible.”

Luke 1:37

Answer

No. A full, peaceful Jerusalem looked impossible to a handful of tired men in the rubble. God asked: does it look hard to Me? He gave an old couple a son, and He can rebuild a wrecked life.

Question 3 of 6

Courage

Where do we find the courage to keep building?

What verses tell us about this?

“Strengthen ye the weak hands, and confirm the feeble knees.”

Isaiah 35:3

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Answer

From God's word and God's promises. The builders were told twice, “let your hands be strong,” because God had said “but now” and promised to do them good. We keep going because the One who started the work in us will finish it.

Question 4 of 6

A fresh start

Can God turn a curse into a blessing?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

Yes. Judah's name was used as a curse, and God said they'd be a blessing instead. Our past doesn't decide our future when God takes hold of us. “Such were some of you,” Paul says, but now washed. A man known for trouble can become a help to the next man.

Question 5 of 6

Truth

Why does God care so much about the truth?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another.”

Ephesians 4:25

Answer

Because He is true, and His city is “a city of truth.” Lies, schemes, and false oaths are “things that I hate,” because they tear people apart. We belong to each other, so we tell each other the truth. Honesty is where a new life starts.

Question 6 of 6

Joy

Can sorrow really be turned into joy?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou hast turned for me my mourning into dancing: thou hast put off my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness;”

Psalm 30:11

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.”

Revelation 21:3

Answer

Yes. Four days of mourning were promised to become four feasts. God doesn't erase what happened, but He can make the worst days of a man's life the place where his joy starts. And in the end, when God lives with His people, the crying will be over.
