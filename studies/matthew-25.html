Matthew 25

Well done

Three stories from the same evening: ten virgins waiting for a bridegroom, three servants trusted with their lord's money, and the King dividing the sheep from the goats.

It's the same evening as chapter 24, about AD 30. Jesus has just said, “Watch therefore… be ye also ready” (24:42, 44). Now He tells three stories to show what being ready looks like.

  1. Ten virginsBe ready. It can't be borrowed at the last minute.
  2. The talentsBe faithful with what He has put in your hands.
  3. Sheep and goatsReal faith shows in how we treat the least.

Part one

The door was shut

Verses 1–13

Ten young women with lamps, a late bridegroom, and a cry at midnight.

A wedding in Jesus' day

  1. promisedbetrothed, often a year ahead
  2. he comesto fetch his bride, often at night
  3. the processionlamps lit, through the streets
  4. the feastat his home, sometimes for days
  5. the door shutthe guests are in

Customs varied, but this was the usual shape. The virgins here are young unmarried women, the bride's friends, waiting to meet the bridegroom and light his way to the feast. Nobody knew exactly when he'd come.

Jesus is the Bridegroom (9:15). His people wait for Him to come and take them home to the feast.

They all slept

Read it again: all ten fell asleep, the wise ones too. The wise weren't the ones who stayed awake. They were the ones who had oil when they woke up.

That's good news for tired men. Being ready doesn't mean never nodding off, never failing, never having a bad day. It means the thing that matters was settled beforehand.

“Tarried” means delayed. “Trimmed their lamps” means they cut the burnt wick and topped up the oil so the flame burned bright. At midnight the cry came, and the only question left was: is there oil in your lamp?

Oil you can't borrow

From the outside all ten looked the same. The difference was hidden until midnight.

The wise weren't being selfish. Some things can't be handed over. A man can't live on his mother's prayers, his wife's faith, or his sponsor's walk with God. Each one has to come to Jesus himself.

Many teachers see the oil as that real, inner life: a living faith, the work of the Spirit in a man. Jesus doesn't spell it out. He just makes plain that it has to be yours.

The door was shut

The saddest words in the story. They knocked, they called him “Lord, Lord,” and he said, “I know you not.” Jesus said the same about some in the Sermon on the Mount:

“Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven.”

Matthew 7:21

But read the story the other way round too: right now the door is open. The invitation still stands, and it is for whosoever will come.

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

Part two

Faithful over a few things

Verses 14–30

A master goes on a long journey and leaves his money with three servants.

What's a talent?

1 talentabout 6,000 days' wages
about 20 yearsof a working man's pay
5 talentsmore than a lifetime of work

A talent wasn't a skill. It was a huge weight of silver. Even the servant with one was trusted with a fortune. Our word “talent,” meaning a gift or ability, comes from this very story.

“Several ability” means each man's own ability. The master knew his men and gave each what he could handle. Then he left, the way Jesus would soon go away to heaven and leave His work in His servants' hands.

Put to work, or put in the ground

What each was given

fivetwoone

What each brought back

tenfourone

The first two “traded”: they did business with it and doubled it. The third dug a hole. In those days burying money was a common way to keep it safe. Nothing was lost, and nothing was gained.

The same “well done”

To the man with five, verse 21

Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things: enter thou into the joy of thy lord.

To the man with two, verse 23

Well done, good and faithful servant; thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things: enter thou into the joy of thy lord.

Word for word the same. The Lord doesn't grade on how much a man started with, only on whether he was faithful with it. Start with the few things in front of you today.

“I was afraid”

The third servant“I knew thee that thou art an hard man… And I was afraid, and went and hid thy talent.” a wrong picture

His lord“Thou wicked and slothful servant…”

He blamed his master, and fear made him bury what he'd been given. Fear does that to a man: he hides, plays it safe, does nothing, and calls it wisdom.

Many of us grew up with a hard father, and we picture God the same way. But the God who gave His Son is not a hard man.

“There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment. He that feareth is not made perfect in love.”

1 John 4:18

Use it, or lose it

“Exchangers” were money changers who kept money for people, a kind of bank. “Usury” here is interest. The master says: if you were so afraid of me, you could at least have put it in the bank. “Slothful” means lazy.

he that hath→more is givenUse what God gives you, and He trusts you with more.
he that hath not→even that is takenBury it, and you lose even what you had.

He called him lord, but didn't know or trust him, and his life showed it. “Outer darkness” is outside the lit feast, away from the Lord. Jesus says hard words so no one has to hear them about himself.

Part three

Inasmuch

Verses 31–46

The King on His throne, all nations before Him, and the people nobody else noticed.

Sheep and goats

Shepherds in Israel often grazed sheep and goats together all day, then parted them at evening. To an outsider the flock looked like one. The shepherd knew every animal.

Now the Son of man sits on His throne as King, and all nations stand before Him. The right hand is the place of honour and welcome.

Churches, like flocks, are mixed. Only the King sorts them, and He never gets one wrong.

An inheritance, not a paycheck

Listen to the King's words. “Blessed of my Father.” “Inherit.” “Prepared for you.” Nobody earns an inheritance. It comes because you're in the family. God planned this kingdom for His people long before any of them did a single good deed.

So their kindness wasn't the price of getting in. It was the proof of who they already were. The Bible puts both halves together:

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.”

Ephesians 2:8–10

Six people nobody notices

  1. an hungred“ye gave me meat”
  2. thirsty“ye gave me drink”
  3. a stranger“ye took me in”
  4. naked“ye clothed me”
  5. sick“ye visited me”
  6. in prison“ye came unto me”

No great sermons, no miracles. A plate of food, a coat, a visit. Some of us have been the man in the cell or the hospital bed, and know what one visitor meant. Jesus says that visitor came to Him.

“Remember them that are in bonds, as bound with them; and them which suffer adversity, as being yourselves also in the body.”

Hebrews 13:3

“When saw we thee?”

The righteous are surprised. They weren't keeping score or doing it to be seen. They just loved people, and never knew they were serving the King.

“Inasmuch” means “in so far as,” “to the extent that.” Who are “the least of these my brethren”? Some teachers say anyone in need. Others say Jesus' own followers, His brothers, especially those who suffer for His name. Jesus Himself said:

“For whosoever shall do the will of my Father which is in heaven, the same is my brother, and sister, and mother.”

Matthew 12:50

Either way the lesson lands in the same place: Jesus takes how we treat the weakest as how we treat Him.

Prepared for whom?

The kingdom

“prepared for you”

Made ready for people, from the foundation of the world (v. 34).

The everlasting fire

“prepared for the devil”

Made for the devil and his angels. Not for men.

Hell was never God's plan for any man. Those who go there go against His wishes, having turned down His mercy:

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Two roads that never end

The same Greek word stands behind “everlasting” and “eternal” here. However long the life lasts, the punishment lasts as long. Jesus spoke about this more than anyone, because He didn't want anyone to go there. Daniel saw the same two roads:

“And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt.”

Daniel 12:2

Which road a man is on is settled by Jesus, not by a tally of good deeds. Anyone who comes to Him by faith is safe in Him:

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Points to remember

  1. Ready“They that were ready went in with him to the marriage” 25:10The wise had oil before the cry came. Get settled with Jesus now.
  2. Borrowed oil“Go ye rather to them that sell, and buy for yourselves” 25:9No one can be ready for you. Each man must come to Jesus himself.
  3. Faithful“Well done, thou good and faithful servant” 25:21God rewards faithfulness, not size. Be faithful with the few things in front of you.
  4. Fear“I was afraid, and went and hid thy talent” 25:25Fear and a wrong picture of God make a man bury what he's been given.
  5. The least“Ye have done it unto me” 25:40What we do for the hungry, the sick and the prisoner, we do for Jesus.
  6. Forever“Everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal” 25:46There are two roads that never end. Jesus came so that no one has to take the wrong one.

Question 1 of 6

Ready

What made the five wise virgins ready when the bridegroom came?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They took oil with their lamps beforehand. They slept like the others, but what mattered was settled before the midnight cry. When the bridegroom came, they were ready and went in with him.

Question 2 of 6

Borrowed oil

Why couldn't the wise share their oil with the foolish?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because some things can't be handed over. There wasn't enough for both, and each had to have her own. No one can believe for us or be ready for us. Every man has to come to Jesus for himself.

Question 3 of 6

Faithful

What does the Lord say to servants who are faithful with what He gave them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Well done, good and faithful servant.” He gives the same praise to the man with two as to the man with five, makes them rulers over many things, and brings them into His joy. He looks for faithfulness, not size.

Question 4 of 6

Fear

What kept the third servant from using his talent?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Fear, and a wrong picture of his lord. He thought his master was hard, so he was afraid and buried the talent. He didn't know or trust his lord, and he ended up doing nothing with what he was given.

Question 5 of 6

The least

How does the King count what we do for the least of His brethren?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As done to Him. Feeding the hungry, taking in the stranger, visiting the sick and the prisoner: He says, “ye have done it unto me.” And neglecting them is neglecting Him.

Question 6 of 6

Forever

Where do the two groups go at the end?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The righteous inherit the kingdom prepared for them and go into life eternal. The others go into everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels, not for men. Both are forever. God is not willing that any should perish, and the door is open today to anyone who comes to Jesus.
