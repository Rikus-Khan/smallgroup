Where we are in Philippians

About AD 60 to 62, Paul writes from Rome, chained to a guard in a rented house, to the friends in Philippi, the church he started on his second journey (Acts 16). It's a thank-you letter, and it is full of joy.

1Joy in chains
2The mind of Christ
3Gain and loss, and the race
4Peace, and enough

Read alone, this chapter still holds together: a man with every reason to boast says he would trade it all, and then he shows us how he runs.

Part one

The best résumé

Verses 1–6

Paul lists everything a man could be proud of. Then he turns the page.

Who Paul is warning against

The pressure

“Jesus, and the mark”

Some teachers told Gentile believers that faith in Jesus wasn't enough. They had to be circumcised and keep the Jewish rules to be really saved. Paul has no soft words for them: “dogs,” “evil workers,” and “the concision,” a sneer that means “the cutters” (Galatians 5:2–4 is the same fight).

Paul's answer

“We are the circumcision”

The ones who belong to God are the ones who “worship God in the spirit, and rejoice in Christ Jesus, and have no confidence in the flesh.” “Flesh” here means what I have and what I've done on my own: my record, my family, my effort.

Those hard words are for men leading others away from Jesus. They aren't for you, wrestling with a habit. And the line to carry through the whole chapter is “no confidence in the flesh.” What are you leaning on?

Paul's résumé

If anyone could trust his own record, he says, “I more.” Here is the page, in his own order:

SAUL OF TARSUS

  1. Circumcised the eighth dayborn into the covenant
  2. Of the stock of Israelpure family line
  3. Tribe of Benjaminthe tribe of Israel's first king
  4. Hebrew of the HebrewsHebrew parents, Hebrew ways
  5. A Phariseethe strictest school
  6. Zeal: persecuting the churchhe was proud of it
  7. Blameless in the lawnobody could fault him

The first four he was handed at birth. The next three he worked for. And look at line six. He lists the worst thing he ever did, hunting down Christians, as a credential. That's how wrong a man can be about his own record.

“Blameless” means by the law's outward measure: nobody could name a rule he broke. And still he knew he needed Christ.

Part two

The ledger

Verses 7–11

Paul does the books on his life, and moves everything to the loss column.

Everything moves to the other column

“Count” is a bookkeeper's word. Paul sat down and did the sums, and the answer was plain: all of it, next to knowing Christ, is “dung,” a rough word for rubbish, the scraps you throw away.

He isn't saying his family or his upbringing was bad. He's saying none of it can be what he stands on. The same goes the other way. The good things you have going now, the days clean, the job, the family name, are real gifts. They are not what saves you. And the worst page of your record isn't what defines you either. Both columns go across, and Christ is what's left.

Two kinds of righteousness

Mine own

“Which is of the law”

Earned. Measured against a rule, and only as good as my last good day. Paul's was as good as any man's, and he still counted it as loss.

God's

“Through the faith of Christ”

“The righteousness which is of God by faith.” Given, not earned. You receive it by trusting Jesus, and it stays put no matter what yesterday looked like.

“But we are all as an unclean thing, and all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags; and we all do fade as a leaf; and our iniquities, like the wind, have taken us away.”

Isaiah 64:6

That's good news for a man who fell yesterday. You were never going to out-good your past, and neither was Paul. Being “found in him” means that when God looks at you, He sees Christ's record in your account.

Not just about Him. Him.

Verse 10 is what the trading was for: “That I may know him.” Not know about Him, but know Him, the way you know a friend. Three things come with that:

  1. His power“the power of his resurrection”the strength that raised Him, for Monday morning
  2. His company“the fellowship of his sufferings”when it costs you to do right, He has been there
  3. His likeness“conformable unto his death”the old self dying, day by day

Verse 11, “If by any means I might attain unto the resurrection of the dead,” doesn't mean Paul doubts he'll be saved. He has just said he's found in Christ. It's the cry of a man running hard for the finish line, sure of the One who waits there (Romans 8:1).

Part three

The race

Verses 12–16

A man who has already failed, and keeps going.

Forgetting what is behind

Paul says it two ways: “forgetting” what is behind, “reaching forth” to what's ahead. The word he uses for reaching is a runner stretching for the tape. You can't run a straight line while you stare over your shoulder.

And notice why he can run: “I am apprehended of Christ Jesus.” Jesus grabbed hold of Paul first, on the Damascus road. Paul isn't running to earn Christ's grip. He's running because he has it.

What “forgetting” does not mean

  • pretending it never happenedyou can't, and God never asked you to
  • acting like it cost nobody anythingpeople were hurt. Make right what you can
  • keeping it secret from every brotherhidden things grow heavy
  • letting it stop steering youit was dealt with at the cross, so you stop running by it

Paul never forgot who he'd been. He wrote it down himself:

“For I am the least of the apostles, that am not meet to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the church of God. But by the grace of God I am what I am”

1 Corinthians 15:9–10

He remembers it with a humble heart. But it doesn't drive. “By the grace of God I am what I am.” If you fell yesterday, do the next right thing today: tell a brother, make the call, go to the meeting. Then run.

Grown up means still running.

“As many as be perfect” (verse 15) means full-grown, not flawless. Paul has just said he hasn't arrived. A mature man is one who knows he hasn't, and keeps going.

And verse 15 is kind: if you see something differently from your brothers, “God shall reveal even this unto you.” You don't have to have it all worked out yet. Keep walking “by the same rule” in what you already know, and let God teach you the rest.

“I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith: Henceforth there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, shall give me at that day: and not to me only, but unto all them also that love his appearing.”

2 Timothy 4:7–8

Years later, Paul wrote this from another prison. The race had an end, and he finished it.

Part four

Citizens of heaven

Verses 17–21

Two roads, and the home we are walking toward.

The road that ends in destruction

  1. an appetitethat gets to rule: “their belly”
  2. proud of the wrong thing“glory in their shame”
  3. eyes only on this world“mind earthly things”
  4. the end“destruction”

Look at how Paul says it: “now tell you even weeping.” He isn't sneering. He's crying over them. And many of us know this road from the inside: the appetite that became the boss, and even the bragging about things we ought to be ashamed of. Paul puts it plainly, and so does the road itself:

“What fruit had ye then in those things whereof ye are now ashamed? for the end of those things is death.”

Romans 6:21

“Enemies of the cross” are people living as if the cross changed nothing. Paul's point is the road, not a label on a man. You can step off it today, and the next slides show where you're headed instead.

A little Rome, far from Rome

The Philippians knew exactly what Paul meant. Their city was a Roman colony: soldiers retired there, and they were citizens of Rome. They dressed Roman, kept Roman law, and spoke the emperor's language, hundreds of miles from the capital (Acts 16:12).

Where Paul writes “conversation”, he means citizenship. For the believer, home is not here. “Our conversation is in heaven.” We live in a rough town, run by other rules, but we belong to a better country, and we keep its ways.

That fits a man starting over. You haven't got to settle for what this town says about you. Your papers say heaven. Today, do one thing the way your country does it.

The end of the story

Nowa citizen of heaven, still in this body and this town
→
when He comesthe body is changed to be like His

We “look for the Saviour.” He is coming, and it's something to look forward to. In the meantime, the body can feel like a battlefield: cravings, tiredness, old damage. “Vile” here just means lowly, the body of our humbling. The Lord Jesus will change it, “according to the working whereby he is able even to subdue all things unto himself.”

He can subdue all things. That includes whatever is bigger than you. Today is not the end of your story. The end of the story is a changed body and a Saviour's face.

Points to remember

  1. Confidence“what things were gain to me, those I counted loss for Christ” 3:7Paul put his whole résumé in the loss column. Don't stand on your record, good or bad.
  2. Righteousness“not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ” 3:9A righteousness you can't earn is given to you in Christ, and it doesn't wobble.
  3. Behind“forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before” 3:13Deal with it honestly, then stop steering by it. Run forward.
  4. Press on“I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus” 3:14Still running, still short of perfect, and held by Christ.
  5. Appetites“whose God is their belly, and whose glory is in their shame” 3:19An appetite that rules is a god, and that road ends in ruin. Step off it today.
  6. Home“our conversation is in heaven; from whence also we look for the Saviour” 3:20Our papers say heaven. He is coming, and He'll change the body.

Question 1 of 6

Confidence

What did Paul once trust in, and what did he count it in the end?

What verses tell us about this?

“For ye have heard of my conversation in time past in the Jews’ religion, how that beyond measure I persecuted the church of God, and wasted it: And profited in the Jews’ religion above many my equals in mine own nation, being more exceedingly zealous of the traditions of my fathers.”

Galatians 1:13–14

Answer

His birth, his strictness and his zeal: the best résumé in Israel, even the persecuting. He counted all of it as loss, and as dung, next to knowing Christ. Don't lean on your record, good or bad.

Question 2 of 6

Righteousness

Whose righteousness does Paul want to be found wearing?

What verses tell us about this?

“But we are all as an unclean thing, and all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags; and we all do fade as a leaf; and our iniquities, like the wind, have taken us away.”

Isaiah 64:6

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

God's: the righteousness that comes through faith in Christ. His own, even at its best, couldn't do it. This is a gift, so a fall yesterday doesn't take it away.

Question 3 of 6

Behind

What does Paul do with the things that are behind him?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I am the least of the apostles, that am not meet to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the church of God. But by the grace of God I am what I am”

1 Corinthians 15:9–10

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Answer

He forgets them, which means he stops steering by them. He doesn't pretend they weren't real: he remembers it humbly. But there is now no condemnation, so he reaches forward and runs.

Question 4 of 6

Press on

What is Paul still running toward?

What verses tell us about this?

“I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith: Henceforth there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, shall give me at that day: and not to me only, but unto all them also that love his appearing.”

2 Timothy 4:7–8

Answer

The mark and the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus. He says he hasn't arrived. He runs because Christ took hold of him first.

Question 5 of 6

Appetites

What happens to people whose god is their belly?

What verses tell us about this?

“What fruit had ye then in those things whereof ye are now ashamed? for the end of those things is death.”

Romans 6:21

Answer

Their end is destruction. An appetite that gets to rule becomes a god, and the road ends in ruin. Paul says it weeping. He cares, and so does Jesus, and there is a way off that road.

Question 6 of 6

Home

Where is our citizenship, and who are we waiting for?

What verses tell us about this?

“If ye then be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ sitteth on the right hand of God. Set your affection on things above, not on things on the earth.”

Colossians 3:1–2

Answer

In heaven, and we're waiting for the Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ, who will change our lowly body to be like His glorious body. Your papers say heaven. Live like it today.
