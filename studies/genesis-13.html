Part one

Back to the altar

Verses 1–4

A man who has failed goes back to where he started with God.

Back where he began

Abram came up out of Egypt rich: cattle, silver and gold. But he didn't go looking for a new place to settle. He walked back “from the south even to Bethel,” to the very spot of his tent and his first altar.

Genesis records no speech of sorrow. It records a road back, and then: “there Abram called on the name of the LORD.” (Hai is the town the book of Joshua calls Ai.) In the verse below, covereth means hides and forsaketh means gives up.

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

The way back is the road you started on.

When a man has blown it, the temptation is to hide, or to wait until he feels better, or to try a new plan. Abram went back to the first altar and called on the LORD again. No new religion, no big speech.

If you fell yesterday, last night or this morning, the road is the same. Get back to the place and the habit where you met God: your Bible, your knees, your church, your brothers. Call on the name of the LORD. He is where you left Him.

Part two

Strife

Verses 5–9

The more they had, the less room they had.

Too much stuff, too little room

  1. substanceeverything they owned: flocks, herds, tents
  2. strifefighting, quarrelling
  3. herdmenthe hired men who looked after the cattle

The quarrel started with the hired hands, not the bosses. Two men had more blessing than the land could carry, and wealth, even blessed wealth, brings its own trouble.

And watch the last line of verse 7: “the Canaanite and the Perizzite dwelled then in the land.” Unbelievers were looking on while the two men who carried God's name fought over grass.

“Letteth out water” is breaking a dam. Leave off contention, before it be meddled with means drop the fight before it gets going.

“The beginning of strife is as when one letteth out water: therefore leave off contention, before it be meddled with.”

Proverbs 17:14

Abram gives up his pick

Ifthou wilt take the left hand,
→
thenI will go to the right.

Hebrew speakers named directions facing east, so “left” was north and “right” was south. Brethren is “brothers.” Separate thyself means “let's part company.” Lot was Abram's nephew, but Abram calls him brother.

Abram was the older man. God had promised him the land. He had every right to choose first, and he gave it up. He picked peace over his own rights.

“If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.”

Romans 12:18

Lieth in you means depends on you.

Part three

Lot's choice

Verses 10–13

He picked by what he could see, and he couldn't see Sodom.

The plain of Jordan

From the high ground near Bethel you look down and east to the Jordan valley. In Lot's day it was green and well watered, “as the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt.” A plain is a wide, flat valley.

Where exactly Sodom stood is not certain. The usual guess is the south end of the Dead Sea; some place it at the north.

Abram, on the other hand, went to the hills at Hebron. We'll come to that at the end.

What he saw, and what he didn't

What Lot could see (10)

Green

Water everywhere. Like the garden of the LORD. Like Egypt, the land that fed the whole region. It looked like the best of everything.

What the text tells us (13)

Sodom

“The men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly.” Lot's eyes found the grass. They missed the neighbours.

“Before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah” (verse 10) is the writer telling us how it ended. Lot picked by sight, and he didn't ask what kind of place it was to live in, or to raise a family in.

“For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world.”

1 John 2:16

A slow slide, one step at a time

  1. lifted up his eyes(13:10)
  2. chosethe plain (13:11)
  3. pitched his tenttoward Sodom (13:12)
  4. dweltin Sodom (14:12)
  5. sat in the gateof Sodom (19:1)

Lot didn't jump. He turned his tent toward it first. The New Testament calls him a righteous man, and says Sodom tormented his soul day after day. He was saved, and he still paid for the road he walked. (Vexed means tormented, and conversation means way of life.)

“And delivered just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked: (For that righteous man dwelling among them, in seeing and hearing, vexed his righteous soul from day to day with their unlawful deeds;)”

2 Peter 2:7–8

Which way is your tent facing?

Lot started by pitching his tent toward Sodom. For a man in recovery that might be a street, a bar, a phone number, a website, a friend who still uses. It looks small, and it feels like a decision you haven't made yet. It's the first step.

“A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the simple pass on, and are punished.”

Proverbs 22:3

Foreseeth means sees coming, and the simple are the naive.

Today, turn one thing around: delete the number, change the route home, tell a brother in Christ where the pull is strongest. And if you are already living in Sodom, don't lose heart: God didn't drop Lot, and chapter 14 shows who came after him.

Part four

All the land

Verses 14–18

After Lot left, God spoke.

Two men lift up their eyes

What Lot did

“And Lot lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan…”

Genesis 13:10

What the LORD told Abram

“Lift up now thine eyes, and look from the place where thou art northward, and southward, and eastward, and westward:”

Genesis 13:14

Lot picked the best of it by sight. Abram let him go, and then God gave him all of it by promise, “to thee will I give it, and to thy seed for ever.” God waited until “Lot was separated from him.” Having to let someone go is not always a loss.

“I will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help. My help cometh from the LORD, which made heaven and earth.”

Psalm 121:1–2

As the dust of the earth

“if a man can number the dust of the earth”

At this moment Abram and Sarai had no child at all (11:30). God was promising more descendants than a man can count. That's a promise only God can keep, and it's the kind He makes: much bigger than the man hearing it.

He told him to walk the length and breadth of it, to see it with his own feet. Seed here means offspring: children and their children.

Another tent, another altar

  1. Shecheman altar, after the LORD appeared (12:7)
  2. Bethelan altar, and he called on the LORD (12:8; 13:4)
  3. Mamre, at Hebronan altar again (13:18)

Mamre was a grove of oaks at Hebron, up in the hills. Abraham and Sarah will be buried near here (Genesis 23). The land God promised was lived in first, by one man with a tent, before it was ever owned.

The pattern holds: a tent for himself, an altar for the LORD. The man who nearly lost his altar in Egypt built another, and God's hand was on all of it.

He could open his hand, because God held the deed.

That's why Abram could let Lot choose first. God had already said, “Unto thy seed will I give this land” (12:7). A man who is sure of what God has promised doesn't have to grab.

For us: someone is going to get the bigger piece today, the better shift, the last word, the credit. Before you grab it or fight for it, ask whether God has promised it to you. If not, you can open your hand, and keep the peace.

“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.”

Proverbs 3:5–6

Points to remember

  1. Coming back“Unto the place where his tent had been at the beginning” 13:3When you fall, the road back is the one you started on: back to the altar, calling on the LORD.
  2. Trouble“The land was not able to bear them” 13:6Even blessing brings trouble. Stuff starts fights, and the world is watching.
  3. Peace“Let there be no strife, I pray thee… for we be brethren” 13:8A peacemaker gives up his rights for the sake of the brother.
  4. Choices“Lot lifted up his eyes” 13:10Picking by sight alone led him step by step to Sodom. Watch which way your tent faces.
  5. God's promise“Lift up now thine eyes… to thee will I give it” 13:14–15What God promises, He gives, and a man who lets go is never left empty-handed.
  6. Trust“Built there an altar unto the LORD” 13:18Abram could be generous because he trusted God to keep His word.

Question 1 of 6

Coming back

Where did Abram go when he came up out of Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

Answer

Back to Bethel, to the altar he had built at the first, and there he called on the name of the LORD. He did not go and look for a new start somewhere else. When a man falls, the way back is to the altar, calling on the LORD, with nothing hidden.

Question 2 of 6

Trouble

What started the fight between Abram's men and Lot's men?

What verses tell us about this?

“From whence come wars and fightings among you? come they not hence, even of your lusts that war in your members?”

James 4:1

Answer

They had too much for the land to hold. Both men were rich in flocks and herds, and the hired men began to fight over grass and water, with unbelievers looking on. Prosperity is not the problem, but wanting more of it, or wanting it first, is where fights come from. (“Your members” in James means the parts of your body.)

Question 3 of 6

Peace

How did Abram stop the quarrel?

What verses tell us about this?

“Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called the children of God.”

Matthew 5:9

Answer

He spoke first, he called Lot his brother, and he gave Lot first choice of the whole land, though he was older and had been the one God promised it to. He gave up his rights for the sake of peace. That is what a peacemaker does.

Question 4 of 6

Choices

How did Lot choose, and where did it lead?

What verses tell us about this?

“And delivered just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked:”

2 Peter 2:7

Answer

He chose by sight: green and well watered. He pitched his tent toward Sodom, and later lived inside it. Peter calls him a righteous man whose soul was tormented there. A believer's choice, with a believer's cost.

Question 5 of 6

God's promise

What did God say to Abram after Lot left?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Lift up now thine eyes.” All the land he could see, forever, and descendants like the dust of the earth. Abram gave up the best of it, and God gave him all of it.

Question 6 of 6

Trust

Why could Abram let Lot choose first?

What verses tell us about this?

“…Unto thy seed will I give this land…”

Genesis 12:7

“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.”

Proverbs 3:5–6

Answer

Because God had already promised him the land. He did not have to grab it. A man who trusts that God keeps His word can afford to be generous, to let go of the better piece and keep the peace.
