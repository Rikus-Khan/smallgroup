2 Timothy 1

A father's last words

Paul sits in a Roman prison, sure he will die. He writes to the young man he loves like a son: don't be afraid, don't be ashamed, don't quit.

The second letter to Timothy

“Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God… To Timothy, my dearly beloved son”

Written from
a prison in Rome. This time it is no house arrest. Paul suffers “as an evil doer, even unto bonds” (2:9), in a chain (1:16).
When
about AD 66 to 67, under the emperor Nero. This is Paul's last letter.
To whom
Timothy, his young helper, most likely at Ephesus (1:18)
Why
Paul knows he is dying: “the time of my departure is at hand” (4:6). He wants his son in the faith to stand firm, and to hand the work on.

Where 2 Timothy fits in Paul's life

  1. about AD 49 to 50Timothy joins Paul at LystraActs 16
  2. about 62 to 64Paul freed; writes 1 TimothyTimothy at Ephesus
  3. about AD 64Rome burns; Nero blames the Christiansa dangerous time to be one
  4. soon afterPaul is arrested againand chained
  5. about 66 to 672 Timothy, from a Roman prisonPaul's last letter
  6. about 67 to 68Paul put to death in Romeby tradition

Seventeen years sit between the first meeting and this letter. Paul met Timothy as a teenager or young man at Lystra (Acts 16:1–3). “Timotheus” is the same name as Timothy. Now Timothy is the man Paul trusts most, and Paul is the one in chains.

Read the whole letter as a dying father's last words to his son. A man who knows he has little time says only what matters.

2 Timothy in one picture

Four chapters, four last words: don't be afraid, pass it on, hold to the Book, finish well. Today we are in chapter 1: Paul tells a nervous young man to stir up what God has already put in him.

Part one

A father remembers his son

Verses 1–7

Paul begins with love, tears and a family's faith. Then comes the word a frightened young man needs.

He remembered his tears

Paul remembers thy tears.

Paul doesn't say “pull yourself together.” Near the end of his life, in a cold cell, one of the things he holds onto is a young man crying, probably at their last goodbye. A man who prays for you “night and day” isn't embarrassed by your tears.

Some of you were told, as boys, that men don't cry. Paul didn't think so, and he wasn't weak. Real fathers, and the Father in heaven, see tears and remember them: “Thou tellest my wanderings: put thou my tears into thy bottle” (Psalm 56:8).

Faith, handed down

Lois, his grandmother“dwelt first”Eunice, his motherand inTimothy

Unfeigned means not put on, not an act. Paul isn't saying Timothy's family was perfect. His father was a Greek (Acts 16:1), and no faith of his is mentioned. Two women kept the faith alive, and it reached a boy.

“Then came he to Derbe and Lystra: and, behold, a certain disciple was there, named Timotheus, the son of a certain woman, which was a Jewess, and believed; but his father was a Greek:”

Acts 16:1

Now notice the word “first.” Somebody always has to be first. Some of you had a Lois. Some of you had nobody, or worse than nobody. If that's you, you are the first link: your children and your grandchildren may someday say, “it started with him.”

Stir up the gift

The Greek word (anazōpyreō) means to fan a fire back into flame. Timothy's fire hadn't gone out. It was low, like coals under ash. Paul hadn't told him to light a new one.

The “gift of God, which is in thee” is whatever God has put in a believer to serve Him with. It was in Timothy when the elders laid hands on him. It's already in you. Fear and shame bank the coals. Reading this chapter, praying, serving, sitting with these men: that's the fanning.

Not fear, but three gifts

fear what I didwhat they'll sayI'll fail againthey'll find outI'm not enough
powerstrength to do the right thing today
loveGod's love for you, and yours for others
a sound minda steady, self-controlled head

“Fear” here is timidity: the shrinking back, the hiding, the cowardice. God did not give you that. If it's in you, it came from somewhere else. What He gave: power (the Holy Spirit, who lives in every believer), love, and a sound mind, which means self-control and clear thinking. Think of the man who stays steady when the craving or the memory hits.

For men in recovery this is a whole plan: the shame of the past, the fear of falling again, and the head that spins. Say verse 7 out loud when it hits. And if worry or panic is heavy, tell a brother or a doctor. That is not a lack of faith.

Part two

Not ashamed

Verses 8–14

A man in a chain says “I am not ashamed.” Then he tells Timothy where his safety comes from.

What shame says

Shame saysEveryone knows what you did. Keep your head down, and stay away from people like Paul, who might get you noticed.

Paul, from a chainBe not thou therefore ashamed of the testimony of our Lord, nor of me his prisoner.

Timothy was tempted to hide. Being tied to a prisoner Nero hated was dangerous, and it was embarrassing too. Paul asks him to stand beside him anyway, and to “be partaker of the afflictions of the gospel.”

Paul knew shame better than most. Before he met Jesus he was “a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and injurious” (1 Timothy 1:13), and he stood by while Stephen was killed (Acts 8:1). Yet he says “I am not ashamed” (v. 12). Not because his past was small, but because Christ's grace was bigger. That is where you stand too.

What saved us, and what didn't

  • my good works“not according to our works”
  • my record: clean days, clean yearsgood, but not the reason God saved you
  • my past: how bad it wasit can't un-save you either
  • his own purpose and gracegiven us in Christ Jesus “before the world began”

God had your name in mind before you had any record at all, good or bad. “Hath saved us, and called us” is already done. And verse 10 says why that's safe: Jesus “hath abolished death, and hath brought life and immortality to light.” The last enemy is already beaten.

A deposit that's kept

Paul says “I know whom I have believed,” not “what.” His peace rests on a Person. “Committed” is a banking word (Greek parathēkē): a deposit left in a trusted keeper's hands. Paul handed his whole life to Jesus, and he is “persuaded” He is able to keep it safe “against that day,” the day Jesus returns.

Most Bible teachers read “that which I have committed” as Paul's own soul and life. That's eternal security: you aren't held by your grip, which slips, but by His. “Neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand” (John 10:28). Your past can't pry it open, and neither can your next bad day.

He keeps you. You keep the truth.

Verse 12

Christ keeps you

He is able to keep what you gave Him. You are not holding yourself up.

Verses 13 and 14

You keep the good thing

You guard what Paul taught you: “the form of sound words,” the gospel. And you don't do it alone. The Holy Spirit “dwelleth in us.”

That's the whole Christian life in two sentences. He holds you, and you hold on to His word with His help. The Holy Spirit moved in when you believed. You didn't have to wait for a second blessing to be strong enough.

Part three

The friend who came

Verses 15–18

When Paul was at his lowest, most of his friends walked away. One man walked toward him.

Everyone left

“All they which are in Asia” had turned away from Paul, including two named men, Phygellus and Hermogenes. Probably they weren't giving up Jesus. They were abandoning Paul, a prisoner Nero wanted, because standing with him was dangerous.

Paul doesn't hide it. The great apostle writes it down: people left me. If you know what it is to be dropped by friends, by family, or by the people you used with, you're in good company.

But there is one more name in this story.

He came looking

Onesiphorus came from Ephesus, across the Aegean and around Greece to Italy. In Rome he “sought me out very diligently, and found me.” Paul was a prisoner the city's rulers wanted forgotten, so finding him took effort.

He “was not ashamed of my chain.” That is the opposite of shame: showing up in person for a man in chains. He did it “oft,” often, not just once.

For today: who is the Onesiphorus in your life? Thank him. And who in this group is in a chain right now? Text him: “I'm not ashamed of you.”

Points to remember

  1. Passed down“The unfeigned faith… which dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois” 1:5Faith can be handed down, but somebody is always first, and that can be you.
  2. Stir it up“Stir up the gift of God, which is in thee” 1:6God has already put something in you. Fan it back into flame.
  3. Not fear“Not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind” 1:7Fear and shame aren't from God. He gave power, love and a steady mind.
  4. Not ashamed“I am not ashamed: for I know whom I have believed” 1:12Paul's peace rested on a Person, not on a spotless past.
  5. Kept safe“Able to keep that which I have committed unto him against that day” 1:12What you've handed to Jesus, He keeps. You are held, not holding.
  6. A real friend“He oft refreshed me, and was not ashamed of my chain” 1:16Be the man who shows up. And let a brother show up for you.

Question 1 of 6

Passed down

Whose faith does Paul remember in Timothy, and who had it first?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then came he to Derbe and Lystra: and, behold, a certain disciple was there, named Timotheus, the son of a certain woman, which was a Jewess, and believed; but his father was a Greek:”

Acts 16:1

Answer

Timothy's grandmother Lois had it first, then his mother Eunice, then Timothy. His father was a Greek. If nobody in your family had faith before you, you are the first link, and the ones who come after you can say it started with you.

Question 2 of 6

Stir it up

What did Paul tell Timothy to do with the gift of God that was in him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Stir it up: fan it back into a flame, like coals. The gift was already in him. And he doesn't do it alone: the Holy Spirit lives in every believer.

Question 3 of 6

Not fear

What has God not given us, and what has He given us instead?

What verses tell us about this?

“For ye have not received the spirit of bondage again to fear; but ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father.”

Romans 8:15

Answer

Not a spirit of fear. He gave power, love and a sound mind. When shame, fear of falling, or a spinning head grabs you, say verse 7 out loud, and call a brother.

Question 4 of 6

Not ashamed

Why was Paul not ashamed, even in a prison chain?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek.”

Romans 1:16

Answer

Because he knew whom he had believed. His peace didn't come from a clean record: he had once been a persecutor. It came from Christ, whose grace was bigger than his past. It's the same for you.

Question 5 of 6

Kept safe

What is Jesus able to keep for us, and until when?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Answer

Everything we have committed to Him, our whole life and soul, until that day when He returns. A believer is kept by Christ, not by his own grip. A bad day can't undo it.

Question 6 of 6

A real friend

What did Onesiphorus do when everyone else turned away?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He showed up. He refreshed Paul often, wasn't ashamed of the chain, and in Rome searched until he found him. Be that man for someone today. When you're the one in chains, let someone find you.
