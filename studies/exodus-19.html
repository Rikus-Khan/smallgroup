Exodus 19

Israel at Mount Sinai

Six weeks out of Egypt, Israel comes to the mountain in Arabia. God reminds them how he carried them, calls them his own, and comes down in fire. The people cannot come up, and they need someone to go between.

Part one

“On eagles’ wings”

Verses 1–8

Before God asks anything, he tells them what he has already done.

Sinai, in Arabia

Israel has crossed the sea at Nuweiba and come on through the wilderness to “the mount”: Mount Sinai, in Midian, in Arabia. Moses had come to this same mountain to keep Jethro's flock, and God spoke to him from a bush (3:1–2).

The New Testament says it plainly:

“For this Agar is mount Sinai in Arabia, and answereth to Jerusalem which now is, and is in bondage with her children.”

Galatians 4:25

Agar is Hagar. Paul uses the old story to speak about the law. We'll get to that.

About six weeks out of Egypt

  1. first monthPassover, then the seachapters 12–14
  2. on the wayMarah, Elim, the mannachapters 15–16
  3. Rephidimwater from the rockchapter 17
  4. third monthMount Sinai19:1
  5. second yearthe tabernacle raised40:17

“In the third month, when the children of Israel were gone forth out of the land of Egypt, the same day came they into the wilderness of Sinai.” They left on the night of the Passover, in the first month. Now it is about six weeks later.

They will stay at this mountain for the rest of Exodus and all of Leviticus: almost a year. Here God gives them his law, and tells them how he will live among them.

Carried first

Read the order. Before one command is given, God says: Ye have seen what I did unto the Egyptians, and how I bare you on eagles' wings, and brought you unto myself.

“As an eagle stirreth up her nest, fluttereth over her young, spreadeth abroad her wings, taketh them, beareth them on her wings:”

Deuteronomy 32:11

Moses pictures an eagle that stirs up her nest, spreads abroad her wings, and bears her young on them (Deuteronomy 32:11). That is the picture God chooses for what he did at the sea: they could not save themselves, and he bore them.

Notice where he brings them: unto myself. Not just out of Egypt. To him. God did not free them to leave them on their own.

Grace comes first. God does not say, “Keep my rules and I will think about rescuing you.” He rescued them, and then he spoke. Hold on to that order. Every man in this room was carried before he did anything.

If… then

Ifye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant,
→
thenye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people.

Peculiar here means my own: a treasure that belongs to me, the way a king has a private store of jewels that are his alone. A covenant is a solemn agreement. God is offering Israel a relationship, and telling them what it will take on their side.

“For all the earth is mine.” He could have chosen anyone. He chose a nation of freed slaves, and said: you are mine.

Look at that little word if. It matters, because of what happens in the next verse.

A kingdom of priests

Slaves in Egypt → A kingdom of priests, an holy nation Holy means set apart: kept for God, and not for the old master.

A priest is a man who may come near to God. These were people who had been brickmakers a few weeks before. Now they are to serve him before the whole earth.

“But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:”

1 Peter 2:9

Peter says it of everyone who believes in Jesus. You, whatever you were last year.

“We will do”

the LORDIf ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant… 19:5

all the people, togetherAll that the LORD hath spoken we will do. 19:8

They said it all together, and they said it before they had heard one command. They did not know yet what he would ask. And they meant it. Nobody there was lying.

A few weeks from now, at the foot of this same mountain, they will be dancing around a golden calf (chapter 32). The promise was sincere, and it was not strong enough.

You know about promises like that: “I will never do it again,” said with all your heart, and meant. The point of this book is not that Israel was worse than we are. It is that a good intention is not the same as a changed heart. Their answer here shows us how much we need what God does next.

Part two

“Sanctify them”

Verses 9–15

God says he is coming down, and the people must be made ready.

Getting ready to meet God

  • sanctifyset apart for God; made ready to meet him
  • a thick clouddense cloud around the mountain, so they would hear and not see
  • believe thee for evertrust Moses' word from now on
  • the third daythe day God comes down

God has two reasons. He comes in a thick cloud so that “the people may hear when I speak with thee, and believe thee for ever.” They will not have to take Moses' word for it; they will hear the voice themselves.

And they must get ready first. Two days of washing their clothes and setting themselves apart. A holy God is not met carelessly.

The bounds

A line was set “round about” the mountain, with one rule: do not go up, and do not touch the border of it. A man who broke it “shall be surely put to death”, and no hand was to touch him: he was to be stoned, or shot through, from where the others stood. The same for a beast.

This is how holy God is. A mountain where he has come down is not a place to wander on. It was a mercy to say so first, with a fence and a warning, so that nobody would walk into it by accident.

“When the trumpet soundeth long, they shall come up.” Only when he called, and no sooner.

Three days of getting ready

Moses went down and did what God said: the people washed their clothes, and for three days they set aside even the ordinary, lawful things of life (come not at your wives) so that their whole attention would be on God.

Notice what the washing could and could not do. Clean clothes did not change a heart. But it taught them one thing: you do not rush into the presence of God as if he were anyone.

Real world: some of us rush into every day with a phone in the hand and nothing in the head. Today, before the noise starts, take one minute. Say a prayer. Ask God to go with you. You are not washing your clothes. You are just remembering who he is.

Part three

“The whole mount quaked greatly”

Verses 16–25

God comes down, and a whole nation learns why a go-between is needed.

The morning God came

  • netherlower: they stood at the bottom, the foot of the mountain
  • thunders and lightningsthe storm, with a thick cloud
  • the voice of the trumpeta long trumpet blast, exceeding loud
  • trembledshook with fear: all of them

The third day, in the morning. Nobody had to be told that something had happened. A whole camp, hundreds of thousands of people, shaking.

Moses brought them out “to meet with God”, and they stood at the foot of the mount. It was not a place to be comfortable. Do not let anyone tell you that the fear of the LORD is a small thing.

Smoke like a furnace

The mountain was smoking and shaking. Waxed means grew: the trumpet grew louder and louder. Over all of it, Moses spoke, and “God answered him by a voice.”

“For ye are not come unto the mount that might be touched, and that burned with fire, nor unto blackness, and darkness, and tempest, And the sound of a trumpet, and the voice of words; which voice they that heard intreated that the word should not be spoken to them any more:”

Hebrews 12:18–19

“For our God is a consuming fire.”

Hebrews 12:29

Hebrews looks back at this day, and says: you are not come to this. The people begged that the voice would not speak to them any more. They could not bear it.

Here is the truth about us. We are not able to stand in the presence of a holy God by ourselves. A man who has sinned, and knows it, ought to feel this. And the answer is not to run away; the answer is in the next chapters, and in Jesus.

Moses goes up, and goes down

  1. Up: God calls Moses to the top
  2. Down: “charge the people”
  3. Down: “let not the priests and the people break through”
  4. Up again: “thou, and Aaron with thee”

Nobody can walk up this mountain. “Lest they break through unto the LORD to gaze, and many of them perish.” Moses says what is plain: The people cannot come up. God does not dispute it. He says: get thee down, and then come up again with Aaron.

The people need someone to go between. The whole chapter is built to make that plain.

“One mediator”

Mount Sinai

A mountain you cannot touch

Fire, smoke, a fence, a trumpet, and a nation standing at a distance with one man between them and God.

The new covenant

A mediator of your own

“But ye are come unto mount Sion, and unto the city of the living God… And to Jesus the mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling.” Hebrews 12:22, 24

“For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;”

1 Timothy 2:5

Mediator means a go-between. Moses stood between a holy God and a frightened people. But Moses was a sinner too. Jesus is the man who can stand there in his own right, and he did it for you.

What this chapter gives you

  • Sin, honestlyWe cannot stand before this God as we are.
  • RegretNot even a sincere “we will do” was enough. You are not the first.
  • GraceFirst he carried them: “I bare you on eagles' wings.”
  • HopeA mediator stands between you and the fire.
  • TodayCome to him through Jesus, and not by being good enough.

“Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession. For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:14–16

The fire is real, and so is the throne of grace. If you fell yesterday, you do not have to stay at the foot of the mountain. Come “boldly”, because of the one who has gone before you. And tell your brothers in Christ and your pastor where you are, so you do not carry it alone.

Points to remember

  1. Grace first“Ye have seen what I did unto the Egyptians, and how I bare you on eagles’ wings, and brought you unto myself” 19:4God carries first, and then he speaks. You were carried before you did anything.
  2. Calling“ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation” 19:6God took a nation of slaves and called them his own treasure. Peter says it of every believer.
  3. The promise“All that the LORD hath spoken we will do” 19:8A sincere promise is not a changed heart. Look for what God does, not only what we promise.
  4. Holiness“Take heed to yourselves, that ye go not up into the mount, or touch the border of it” 19:12God is holy, and we cannot walk up to him as we are.
  5. Fear“there were thunders and lightnings, and a thick cloud upon the mount… so that all the people that was in the camp trembled” 19:16Thunder, fire, a trumpet and a shaking mountain: a whole camp trembled.
  6. The go-between“The people cannot come up to mount Sinai” 19:23The people could not come up. Moses went between, and Jesus is the one mediator.

Question 1 of 6

Grace first

What had God already done for Israel before he asked anything of them?

What verses tell us about this?

“As an eagle stirreth up her nest, fluttereth over her young, spreadeth abroad her wings, taketh them, beareth them on her wings:”

Deuteronomy 32:11

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

He brought them out of Egypt and carried them as an eagle carries her young, and brought them to himself. All of that came before a single command. A man is saved by grace, and then he lives in it.

Question 3 of 6

The promise

What did the people promise, and was it enough?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ, that we might be justified by faith.”

Galatians 3:24

Answer

They said, “All that the LORD hath spoken we will do,” and they meant it. It was not enough: they were worshipping a golden calf within weeks. Our promises fail too. The law was given to show us that, and to bring us to Christ.

Question 4 of 6

Holiness

Why did God set bounds round the mountain?

What verses tell us about this?

“For our God is a consuming fire.”

Hebrews 12:29

Answer

Because he is holy, and a sinful people would perish in his presence. The fence was a mercy: it warned them first.

Question 5 of 6

Fear

What was it like when God came down on the mountain?

What verses tell us about this?

“For our God is a consuming fire.”

Hebrews 12:29

Answer

Thunder, lightning, a loud trumpet, fire, smoke like a furnace, and a mountain that shook. All the people trembled. Our God is a consuming fire, and he is also the God who carried them.

Question 6 of 6

The go-between

Who stands between a holy God and a people who cannot come up?

What verses tell us about this?

“For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;”

1 Timothy 2:5

Answer

At Sinai it was Moses, going up and down. Behind him stands the real one: Jesus, who has gone before you. You may come boldly to the throne of grace.
