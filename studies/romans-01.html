Romans 1

Not ashamed

Paul writes to the believers in Rome: the gospel is God's power to save anyone who believes. Then he shows why every man needs it.

The letter to the Romans

Written from

When

To whom

When Paul wrote this letter, he had been a believer for over twenty years and had planted churches across what is now Turkey and Greece. Rome was the capital of the world, and he had never been there.

He did get to Rome, about three years later. Not the way he planned: in chains, after a shipwreck. We'll see that in verse 10.

Part one

To all that be in Rome

Verses 1–15

A former persecutor writes to a church he's never met, in the capital of the world.

Who is writing

Saul of Tarsus, persecutor → Paul, a servant “Servant” is doulos: a slave, a man who belongs to his master.

“As for Saul, he made havock of the church, entering into every house, and haling men and women committed them to prison.”

Acts 8:3

He once dragged believers to prison. Now the first thing he says about himself is that he belongs to Jesus. An apostle is one sent with a message; separated means set apart for one job. If God could turn Saul around, no man is too far gone.

What is “the gospel”?

εὐαγγέλιον said euangelion “good news”

“the seed of David according to the flesh”

Truly a man

Born in King David's family line, the King the prophets promised (v. 2).

“the Son of God with power… by the resurrection”

Truly God

The empty tomb settled it. Death couldn't hold Him.

The gospel isn't advice about what you should do. It's news about what Jesus has done. And the whole thing is about a Person: “Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Called, three times

  1. Paul“called to be an apostle” · v. 1
  2. You“the called of Jesus Christ” · v. 6
  3. Every believer in Rome“called to be saints” · v. 7

Saints doesn't mean statues or super-Christians. It means holy ones, people set apart for God. Paul calls every believer in Rome a saint, slaves and soldiers and shopkeepers alike. If you belong to Jesus, God calls you one too.

“Grace to you and peace.” Grace is God's favor you didn't earn. Peace is what follows: being at peace with God, and then inside yourself. It's always in that order. Grace first, then peace.

A prayer answered the hard way

“Let hitherto” is old English for hindered until now. Paul had tried again and again to get to Rome.

He prayed for “a prosperous journey,” and God said yes: about three years later, as a prisoner, after a shipwreck, with the empire paying his fare (Acts 27–28). A setback isn't always a no.

“Be of good cheer, Paul: for as thou hast testified of me in Jerusalem, so must thou bear witness also at Rome.”

Acts 23:11

A debt Paul owed everybody

What Paul owes, and to whom

  • the Greeksthe gospel
  • the Barbariansthe gospel
  • the wisethe gospel
  • the unwisethe gospel
  • you that are at Romethe gospel

Balance dueevery man he meets

Barbarians meant anyone who didn't speak Greek; to Greek ears other languages sounded like “bar-bar-bar.” Insiders and outsiders, schooled and unschooled: Paul owed them all.

If you're handed a fortune to deliver, you owe it to the man it's meant for. Somebody delivered the gospel to you. Who is it meant for next?

Part two

Not ashamed

Verses 16–17

The two verses the whole letter hangs on.

Why would anyone be ashamed?

“But we preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews a stumblingblock, and unto the Greeks foolishness;”

1 Corinthians 1:23

In Rome, the cross was how you killed slaves and rebels. Telling the capital of the world that the Savior died on one sounded crazy. Paul says it anyway, because it works:

δύναμις dunamis, “power” the root of our word dynamite

Not power for those who help themselves: “to every one that believeth.” “The Jew first” is the order history went, Israel first and then the nations (Acts 13:46). It isn't a ranking.

Righteousness

Righteousness means being right: right with God, measuring up to what He is. Nobody does. Not the decent man, not the church man, not you on your best day. “All have sinned, and come short” (3:23).

Here's the news: God doesn't just demand righteousness. He gives it. “The righteousness of God” is a right standing with Him that He hands to the man who believes. It's received, not achieved.

The just shall live by faith

Habakkuk 2:4, about 600 years earlier

“Behold, his soul which is lifted up is not upright in him: but the just shall live by his faith.”

Romans 1:17

“as it is written, The just shall live by faith.”

The just is the man God counts right. It's a courtroom word: the Judge looks at the case and declares, this man is in the right. Paul will call that being justified (3:24, 5:1).

“From faith to faith”: it starts with trusting Christ, and it goes on that way. You're not saved by faith and then kept by your own effort. The just man lives by faith, every day, including today.

Part three

God gave them up

Verses 18–32

Why men need the gospel: a slide that starts small and ends in the dark.

Wrath

Wrath is God's settled, holy anger at evil. It isn't a temper. A God who shrugged at murder, abuse and lies wouldn't be good.

“Hold the truth in unrighteousness” means hold it down, like a ball under water. It takes constant effort, and it keeps pushing back up. Every man who's lived a lie knows how tiring that is.

Wrath comes before the cure, because nobody wants a rescue until he sees the danger. The cure: “we shall be saved from wrath through him” (5:9).

Clearly seen

Nobody needs a Bible to know there is a God. The night sky, the ocean, a newborn baby: “the things that are made” all say Someone powerful made this.

Godhead means God's divine nature. Creation can't tell you how to be saved, but it tells every man enough to look for Him.

“Without excuse” means no one can say, nobody ever told me. The stars over the prison yard preached the same sermon as the ones over the church.

The road down

  1. “knew God”where it starts
  2. “glorified him not”didn't give Him His due
  3. “neither were thankful”took it all for granted
  4. “vain in their imaginations”empty thinking
  5. “heart was darkened”can't see straight
  6. “became fools”bowing to things they made

Look where the slide starts. Not with drugs or crime. It starts with a man who knows God and won't thank Him. Ingratitude is the first step down.

Relapse works the same way. It starts days before the drink: quit praying, nurse a resentment, decide you know better. Then the thinking goes dark, and a man ends up bowing to a bottle, a pill or a screen, things far smaller than himself.

Bad trades

“the glory of the uncorruptible God”→an image of man or beastverse 23
“the truth of God”→a lieverse 25
“the natural use”→“that which is against nature”verse 26

To worship something is to serve it: give it your time, your money, your loyalty. Nobody bows to statues anymore, but plenty of men serve a bottle, a needle, a paycheck or a woman “more than the Creator.”

“God gave them up”

181920212223 24gave up2526gave up27 28gave over29303132

To give up is to hand over. God stopped holding them back and let them have what they insisted on. Getting what you want, and then finding it owns you: every addict knows that judgment.

But it isn't the last word. Paul uses the very same word for what God did with His own Son:

“He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?”

Romans 8:32

A hard passage, read plainly

Vile affections means shameful passions. Paul's example is sex between women and between men. He calls it “against nature”, against the way God made man and woman for each other, and he names it as sin, plainly.

It's one more bad trade: God's design swapped for desires that go against it. “That recompence of their error which was meet” is the harm that comes built in.

But the next verses list envy, gossip, pride and disobeying parents. Nobody here stands above this list. And Paul wrote this to a church of men who had lived every kind of life, this one included:

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Reprobate

ἀδόκιμος said adokimos “failed the test,” like a fake coin thrown out
They decided God wasn't worth keeping“they did not like to retain God”
→
So God handed them a mind that can't judge right“a reprobate mind”

They tested God and threw Him out, so their minds stopped passing the test. “Not convenient” means not fitting. If you're worried you've gone too far for God, that worry is a sign He's still speaking to you. A reprobate mind doesn't care.

The list

Murder is on the list. So are gossip, bragging and lying, the sins we do before lunch. Paul's case is about to take in every reader, the decent man most of all (chapter 2).

Verse 32 is the bottom: not just doing it, but cheering others on. Every one of us has stood in that crowd.

Where Paul is going

Romans 1–3All have sinned

Chapter 1 the wild man, chapter 2 the decent man, chapter 3 everybody. No one is singled out.

Romans 3:21–5But now

“Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus” (3:24).

Romans 8:1No condemnation

For every man who is in Christ Jesus. Nothing can separate him from God's love (8:39).

If chapter 1 read like your own story, good: that means you see the need. You haven't read the end of the story yet.

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Points to remember

  1. The good news“I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ” 1:16The gospel is news about Jesus, the risen Son of God, and it has the power to save.
  2. For everyone“To every one that believeth” 1:16Jew or Greek, wise or unwise: no one is too far gone, and no one is first in line.
  3. Right with God“The just shall live by faith” 1:17Righteousness is God's gift to the man who believes, and he lives by faith every day.
  4. What every man knows“They are without excuse” 1:20Creation tells every man there is a powerful God he must answer to.
  5. The road down“Neither were thankful” 1:21The slide away from God starts small: not giving Him thanks.
  6. Given up“God gave them up” 1:24Keep insisting on sin and God lets you have it. But He gave up His own Son to get you back.

Question 1 of 6

The good news

What is the gospel, and why was Paul not ashamed of it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The gospel is the good news about Jesus: truly a man from David's line, and declared the Son of God by rising from the dead. A crucified Savior sounded foolish to the world, but Paul wasn't ashamed, because it works. It is God's own power to save.

Question 2 of 6

For everyone

Who is the gospel for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Everyone: Greeks and Barbarians, the wise and the unwise, Jews and Gentiles. It saves “every one that believeth.” There's no one it was not meant for, including you.

Question 3 of 6

Right with God

How does a man become right with God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:”

Romans 5:1

“And be found in him, not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith:”

Philippians 3:9

Answer

By faith, not by measuring up. No man reaches God's standard on his own. God gives His righteousness to the man who trusts Christ, and declares him right. Then that man goes on living by faith, from the first day to the last.

Question 4 of 6

What every man knows

What can every man know about God, even without a Bible?

What verses tell us about this?

“The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork.”

Psalm 19:1

Answer

That He is there, that He is powerful, and that He is God and we are not. Creation shows it to everyone, so no one can say he was never told. It can't tell us how to be saved. That's what the gospel is for.

Question 5 of 6

The road down

Where does the road away from God begin?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

With men who knew God but wouldn't honor Him or thank Him. From there the thinking goes empty, the heart goes dark, and they trade the truth for a lie. So guard the small things: thank God today, and keep your thinking in the light.

Question 6 of 6

Given up

What does it mean that God “gave them up”?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?”

Romans 8:32

Answer

God stopped holding them back and handed them over to what they kept choosing, and it ruled them. But God also handed over His own Son for us, so no man has to stay given up.
