Genesis 46

“Fear not to go down into Egypt”

An old man leaves the land God gave him, and God meets him at the border with a promise. Then the family is counted, name by name, and Jacob sees Joseph's face again.

Part one

God at Beer-sheba

Verses 1–7

The last town in the Promised Land, and a night of visions.

The edge of the land

Jacob had been living around Hebron. Beer-sheba is the last town in the south of Canaan, the place where the road leaves the Promised Land and heads west into the desert toward Egypt.

It was also a place that belonged to his family's faith. His grandfather Abraham had called on the name of the LORD there (21:33), and his father Isaac had built an altar there (26:25). Jacob stops at that place, and offers sacrifices “unto the God of his father Isaac”.

Notice: “his father Isaac's God.” Jacob is going to Egypt, and he is going as a man who knows the God of his fathers.

“Go not down”, and “fear not to go down”

Genesis 26:2, to Isaac

And the LORD appeared unto him, and said, Go not down into Egypt; dwell in the land which I shall tell thee of:

Genesis 46:3, to Jacob

I am God, the God of thy father: fear not to go down into Egypt; for I will there make of thee a great nation:

Same God, same country, opposite instructions. Isaac was told to stay. Jacob is told to go. And Abraham went down once on his own, without a word from God, and it ended with his wife in Pharaoh's house (12:10–20).

So the lesson is not “never go” or “always go”. It is: don't move without His word. We don't wait for visions in the night. We have the written Word, and we have pastors and brothers in Christ to ask. Before a big move (a new job, a new city, ending a relationship), take it to them first.

“I will”, four times

  1. 1I will there make of thee a great nationThe reason for going down: God's promise to Abraham is still on.46:3
  2. 2I will go down with thee into EgyptNot a promise that nothing will go wrong. A promise of company.46:4
  3. 3I will also surely bring thee up againEgypt is not the end. There is a way back out.46:4
  4. 4Joseph shall put his hand upon thine eyesJoseph will be there when Jacob dies, and close his eyes. He will not die alone.46:4

“Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.”

Isaiah 41:10

Notice the first words God says: “Jacob, Jacob.” He calls him by name, twice, and Jacob answers: “Here am I.” The God who is about to send an old man into a foreign country starts by saying his name.

Four hundred and thirty years

“Now the sojourning of the children of Israel, who dwelt in Egypt, was four hundred and thirty years.”

Exodus 12:40

God had told Abram this long before: “thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not theirs” (15:13). This is the moment the words begin to come true. A family walks down into Egypt, and the clock God set on the promise starts ticking.

What looks to Jacob like a risk is God's plan, and He told it in advance.

Twelve sons by four mothers, and Leah also had a daughter, Dinah. These are the men whose names will become the twelve tribes of Israel. In the middle of the list you will see names that matter later: Levi, whose family will serve as priests, and Judah, from whom the kings, and the Saviour, will come.

Counting the seventy

Leah33verse 15
Zilpah16verse 18
Rachel14verse 22
Bilhah7verse 25

70 souls of the house of Jacob (verse 27)

33 + 16 + 14 + 7 = 70. The usual way to add it: Leah's 33 counts Jacob himself, and leaves out Er and Onan, who “died in the land of Canaan”. Rachel's 14 counts Joseph and his two sons, who were already in Egypt, which is why verse 26 gives 66 for those who came with Jacob.

Stephen says “threescore and fifteen” in Acts 7:14. He was quoting from the old Greek translation of the Old Testament, which counts five more of Joseph's grandsons. Same family, counted two ways.

“And Judas begat Phares and Zara of Thamar; and Phares begat Esrom; and Esrom begat Aram;”

Matthew 1:3

“Phares” is the Greek spelling of Pharez, and “Esrom” of Hezron. Matthew's list of the ancestors of Jesus passes straight through these two names. Er and Onan died in Canaan, and the one who carried the line forward was Pharez, a son of the very Judah who had once sold his brother, and Tamar (chapter 38). God's line does not run through the best men. It runs through the ones He chooses to rescue.

Why a list of names matters

“Thy fathers went down into Egypt with threescore and ten persons; and now the LORD thy God hath made thee as the stars of heaven for multitude.”

Deuteronomy 10:22

Seventy people went down. Hundreds of years later, Moses says they have become as many as the stars. Every nation starts with a list of names.

“Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.”

Luke 10:20

And the Lord counts names. Jesus told His followers to rejoice because their names are written in heaven. He knows every man who trusts Him, by name: not by his record, his past, or the nickname from his worst years. If you ever feel like nobody, remember that God sat down and wrote down who went down to Egypt, even down to Serah, Hushim and Muppim.

Part three

Goshen

Verses 28–34

A father and a son meet again, and a plan to keep the family apart.

“He sent Judah before him.”

Out of all the sons, Jacob sends Judah ahead to show the way to Goshen. This is the man who once suggested selling Joseph (37:26–27), and who then stood up and offered to be a slave in Benjamin's place (44:33). Now he is the one the family trusts to lead.

That is what change looks like. It does not mean the past did not happen. It means the man you were is not the man who gets sent ahead of the family. Judah will later be the one whose line the sceptre belongs to (49:10).

“Now let me die”

Jacob, twenty-two years beforeFor I will go down into the grave unto my son mourning.

Jacob, nowNow let me die, since I have seen thy face, because thou art yet alive. peace

Compare the two. In chapter 37 he said he would go down to the grave grieving. In this chapter he says he can go in peace.

This is not a man who wants to die. It is a man whose long grief is finished: he says, in effect, I have seen what I was waiting for, and I can rest. Joseph “wept on his neck a good while.” Both men cried for a good long time, and nobody hurried them.

If you have a long sorrow, and you've said the same thing Jacob said in chapter 37, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor. Don't carry it by yourself. Jacob's story shows what God can still do with a heart that thought it was finished.

“Every shepherd is an abomination”

What Egypt thought

Keep away from them

The Egyptians found shepherds offensive. A family of herdsmen would be looked down on, kept at a distance, and not wanted in the cities.

What God was doing

A people kept apart

Goshen, the best grazing, to themselves. God was growing a nation, and the nation needed to stay a nation: not scattered, not swallowed up by Egypt.

Notice that Joseph does not tell them to lie. They are shepherds: “thy servants' trade hath been about cattle from our youth.” He just tells them to say it plainly.

There is a lesson in that. The world may sneer at your trade, your past, or the kind of man you are. Don't dress it up and don't be ashamed. God can use exactly what makes you unwelcome to keep you where He wants you.

Points to remember

  1. Before the big step“came to Beer-sheba, and offered sacrifices unto the God of his father Isaac” 46:1He had Joseph's invitation and Pharaoh's wagons, and he still stopped to worship and ask God.
  2. Fear“fear not to go down into Egypt; for I will there make of thee a great nation” 46:3God did not only say “don't be afraid”. He said “I will go down with thee.”
  3. Names“all the souls of the house of Jacob, which came into Egypt, were threescore and ten” 46:27God counts people one by one. Seventy names went down, and a nation came back out.
  4. Reunion“Now let me die, since I have seen thy face, because thou art yet alive” 46:30The old man who said “I will go down into the grave mourning” now says he can die in peace.

Question 1 of 5

Before the big step

Why did Jacob stop at Beer-sheba and offer sacrifices before going down to Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD appeared unto him, and said, Go not down into Egypt; dwell in the land which I shall tell thee of:”

Genesis 26:2

Answer

Leaving the Promised Land was serious. Abraham went down to Egypt on his own and it went badly, and God told Isaac, “Go not down.” Jacob had every reason to go, and still stopped to worship and hear from God. Before a big move, stop and ask: take it to your pastor and your brothers in Christ.

Question 2 of 5

Fear

What did God tell Jacob not to fear, and what did He promise?

What verses tell us about this?

“Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.”

Isaiah 41:10

Answer

He told him not to fear going down, and gave reasons: “a great nation”, “I will go down with thee”, and “I will also surely bring thee up again.” God's answer to fear is not “be brave”. It is “I am with thee.”

Question 3 of 5

Names

Why does Genesis list every name that went down to Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

“Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.”

Luke 10:20

Answer

Because to God every man is a name, not a number. These seventy are the seed of a nation, and the Lord who counted them tells His people today, “your names are written in heaven.” You matter to Him by name, whatever your name was on your worst day.

Question 4 of 5

Reunion

What did Jacob say when he saw Joseph's face again?

What verses tell us about this?

“And all his sons and all his daughters rose up to comfort him; but he refused to be comforted; and he said, For I will go down into the grave unto my son mourning. Thus his father wept for him.”

Genesis 37:35

Answer

“Now let me die, since I have seen thy face.” Twenty-two years earlier he said he would go down to the grave mourning. His sorrow ended in joy, and he wept on his son's neck “a good while.” Whatever grief you carry has not been given the last word either.

Question 5 of 5

Goshen

Why did Joseph tell his brothers to say they were shepherds?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto Abram, Know of a surety that thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not theirs, and shall serve them; and they shall afflict them four hundred years; And also that nation, whom they shall serve, will I judge: and afterward shall they come out with great substance.”

Genesis 15:13-14

Answer

Because Egypt looked down on shepherds, which was just what Joseph wanted: Pharaoh would put them in Goshen by themselves. The family would not be swallowed up by Egypt. A people promised a great future had to stay a people apart.
