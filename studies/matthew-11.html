Part one

A question from prison

Verses 1–6

The strongest prophet in Israel has a dark day.

John in prison

Herod Antipas, who ruled Galilee and Perea for Rome, had locked John up for telling him he had no right to his brother's wife (14:3–4). The historian Josephus, who wrote later that century, says the prison was Machaerus, a fortress on the hills east of the Dead Sea.

Meanwhile Jesus kept working up north in Galilee. John could only hear about it secondhand. So he sent two of his own disciples on the long walk to ask.

The bold man has a dark day

John, at the Jordan, earlierBehold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world. (John 1:29)

John, in a cellArt thou he that should come, or do we look for another?
doubt

This is the same man who baptized Jesus and saw the Spirit come down on Him (3:16). But a dungeon has a way of shrinking a man's faith. Maybe John expected the Messiah to clean house by now, and here he sat in Herod's prison.

Notice what John did with his doubt. He didn't quit and he didn't hide it. He took the question straight to Jesus. That's the right place to bring yours.

Isaiah 35:5–6

Jesus didn't scold John. He answered with Isaiah's own list, and John knew his Isaiah. The answer was yes. “Offended” means tripped up: blessed is the man who doesn't stumble over the kind of Messiah Jesus is.

Part two

More than a prophet

Verses 7–15

As John's men walk away, Jesus stands up for him.

What did you go out to see?

  • “a reed shaken with the wind”John didn't bend to every breeze, not even for a king
  • “a man clothed in soft raiment”he wore camel's hair and a leather belt (3:4)
  • “A prophet? … and more than a prophet”a man who spoke for God, and the one God promised

Jesus said this “as they departed”: right after John's question, while John's men were walking off. One honest doubt didn't change what Jesus thought of him. Soft clothes are in kings' houses. John was in a king's house too, but in the dungeon, for telling the truth.

The messenger Malachi promised

Malachi 3:1, about 430 B.C.

Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me: and the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly come to his temple…

Matthew 11:10

For this is he, of whom it is written, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face, which shall prepare thy way before thee.

Malachi was the last prophet of the Old Testament. Four hundred years later, John stepped out of the wilderness to get the road ready. Every other prophet looked ahead to the Messiah. John got to point at Him.

Look at the words: in Malachi the LORD says “before me.” Here it reads “before thy face”: the Father speaking to the Son. The One John got the road ready for was the LORD Himself, come in person.

The least is greater?

John

At the door

He pointed to the Lamb, but he was killed before the cross. He never saw the empty tomb, or the Spirit poured out at Pentecost.

The least believer today

Inside the house

He knows the whole story: Christ died for his sins and rose again, and the Holy Spirit lives in him from the day he believed.

It isn't that the newest believer is a better man than John. Nobody was greater. It's that he stands on this side of the cross. The weakest man in this room who trusts Christ has more than the greatest prophet ever saw.

“Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD:”

Malachi 4:5

John wasn't Elijah come back from heaven (John 1:21). He came “in the spirit and power of Elias” (Luke 1:17). “Suffereth violence” is a hard phrase, and good teachers read it two ways: the kingdom was under attack (John was in chains), and men were pushing hard to get in (Luke 16:16, “every man presseth into it”). Both were true.

Part three

This generation

Verses 16–24

Too strict, too loose, and three towns that saw it all.

Kids in the market square

Children in the town square played grown-up: weddings and funerals. A “piped” tune on a little flute meant a wedding. Wailing meant a funeral.

Let's play wedding

“we have piped”

“and ye have not danced”

Let's play funeral

“we have mourned”

“and ye have not lamented”

Some kids sulk in the corner no matter what game you pick. Jesus says that's this generation: it's not the tune they object to. They just won't play.

Too strict, too loose

John fasted in the wildernessHe hath a devil. too strict

Jesus ate with tax menBehold a man gluttonous, and a winebibber, a friend of publicans and sinners. too loose

A “winebibber” is a heavy drinker. That was a smear. Jesus sat at the table with sinners (9:10), but He was never a drunkard. People who don't want God will always find a reason.

But one part was true: He is the friend of publicans and sinners. That's the best news men like us can hear. And “wisdom is justified of her children”: wisdom is proved right by what it produces. Changed lives answer the critics.

Three towns that saw it all

Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum sat within a few miles of each other at the top of the lake. Capernaum was Jesus' home base (4:13). They saw more miracles than any towns on earth, and shrugged.

Tyre and Sidon were pagan port cities. Sodom was burned for its wickedness (Genesis 19). Jesus says they would have repented in sackcloth and ashes, the rough clothes and dust of deep grief, if they had seen what these towns saw.

Today all three of those towns are ruins.

More light, more to answer for

“Woe” is a cry of grief more than a curse. Jesus wasn't glad about it. And notice their sin: not some wild wickedness, just “they repented not.” They watched, they were impressed, and they stayed the same.

We've heard more gospel than most people in history. Hearing isn't the same as turning. The door is still open today.

Part four

Come unto me

Verses 25–30

Hidden from the proud, open to babes, and an invitation to every tired man.

Wise men and babes

The wise and prudent

Too smart to need it

The experts in the law, sure of their own learning. They studied the Scriptures and missed the One they were about.

Babes

Know they need help

Fishermen, a tax man, beggars, sinners. Men with nothing to prove, who came to Jesus with empty hands.

God isn't against brains. He's against pride. The door into the kingdom is low, and a man has to bend down to come in. A lot of us had to hit bottom before we were small enough to fit. Jesus thanks the Father for exactly that.

The only way to know the Father

the Fatherhands all things tothe Sonwho shows the Father towhomsoever He will

Only the Son truly knows the Father, and only the Son can introduce us. So who does He want to show the Father to? Read the very next verse:

Not a select few. “All ye.” Whoever comes.

heavy laden trying harder the wreckage the habit shame guilt

rest

All ye that labour

“Labour” is working till you drop. “Heavy laden” is a load piled on your back. The Pharisees piled rule on rule on the people. Sin piles up its own load: what we did, what was done to us, and the exhausting work of trying to fix it ourselves.

Jesus doesn't say “try harder.” He says “Come.” The rest is His to give, and He gives it.

“All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.”

John 6:37

An easy yoke

A yoke is the wooden beam that joins two oxen so they pull together. A young ox could be yoked beside an older, steady one and learn by walking with him.

“Easy” means well-fitted, kind. It doesn't rub you raw. Jesus is “meek and lowly in heart”: gentle, not harsh. He's not another boss to fail. He walks in the yoke beside you and takes the weight.

Following Him is still a yoke. There's work. But it's work with Him, and it's light next to what we carried before.

“Thus saith the LORD, Stand ye in the ways, and see, and ask for the old paths, where is the good way, and walk therein, and ye shall find rest for your souls. But they said, We will not walk therein.”

Jeremiah 6:16

Points to remember

  1. Doubt“Go and shew John again those things which ye do hear and see” 11:4Bring your doubts to Jesus. He answers with the evidence, not a scolding.
  2. Critics“A friend of publicans and sinners” 11:19People who don't want God will always find a reason. Changed lives answer them.
  3. Light“Because they repented not” 11:20Seeing and hearing aren't the same as turning. More light, more to answer for.
  4. Humble“Hast revealed them unto babes” 11:25God shows Himself to men humble enough to admit they need Him.
  5. Invited“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden” 11:28The invitation is for all. Whoever comes, He won't turn away.
  6. Rest“My yoke is easy, and my burden is light” 11:30He takes the crushing load, walks beside us, and gives rest to the soul.

Question 1 of 6

Doubt

What did Jesus do with John's doubts?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He didn't scold him or write him off. He sent back the evidence: the very works Isaiah said the Messiah would do. Then, as John's men walked away, He praised John in front of everybody. Honest doubt brought to Jesus doesn't make Him love you less.

Question 2 of 6

Critics

Why can some people never be pleased?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the problem isn't the messenger, it's the heart. John was too strict for them and Jesus too loose. When a man doesn't want God, he'll always find a reason. Don't let the critics run your life. Wisdom is proved right by its fruit, and Jesus really is the friend of sinners.

Question 3 of 6

Light

Why is it dangerous to see much and not repent?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because more light means more to answer for. Those towns saw more miracles than anywhere on earth, and it would go easier for Sodom than for them. Being impressed by Jesus isn't the same as turning to Him. While it's still today, the door is open.

Question 4 of 6

Humble

Who does God show these things to?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To “babes”: people humble enough to know they need help. Pride shuts the eyes; humility opens them. Only the Son can show us the Father, and He shows Him to everyone who comes to Him to learn.

Question 5 of 6

Invited

Who is invited to come to Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.”

John 6:37

Answer

All who are tired and loaded down. Nobody is too far gone or too dirty. Jesus is the friend of sinners, and whoever comes to Him, He will never throw out. The only requirement is to come.

Question 6 of 6

Rest

What kind of rest does Jesus give?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thus saith the LORD, Stand ye in the ways, and see, and ask for the old paths, where is the good way, and walk therein, and ye shall find rest for your souls. But they said, We will not walk therein.”

Jeremiah 6:16

Answer

Rest for the soul. Not a life with no work, but the end of the crushing load: guilt forgiven, and no more trying to save ourselves. We take His yoke, walk beside Him, and learn from a Master who is gentle and humble. His load is light.
