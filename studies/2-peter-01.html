2 Peter 1

A fisherman's last letter

Peter knows his time is short. He has failed badly, and been forgiven completely. Now he tells the next generation what God has already given them, and what to do with it.

The second letter of Peter

“Simon Peter, a servant and an apostle of Jesus Christ, to them that have obtained like precious faith with us”

Written by
Simon Peter, the fisherman Jesus called, who once denied Him three times
Written from
most likely Rome, where Peter was near the end of his life
When
about AD 64 to 68, in the last years of the emperor Nero. This is Peter's last letter, and he knows it: “shortly I must put off this my tabernacle, even as our Lord Jesus Christ hath shewed me” (1:14).
To whom
the same scattered believers as his first letter, across what is now Turkey (3:1)
Why
Teachers who twist the faith are on their way. Peter wants his people grounded in Christ, living clean, and watching for His return.

  3. about AD 64Rome burns; Nero blames the Christiansa dangerous time to be one

Thirty-odd years sit between the night Peter lied and cursed in a courtyard and this letter. Jesus told him he would one day be old, and carried where he would not choose to go (John 21:18). Peter is that old man now, and he has not stopped following.

Read the whole letter as a dying man's last advice. When time is short, a man only says what matters.

Three chapters, three words: grow, beware, wait. Today we are in chapter 1. Before Peter warns about anything, he tells us what we already have, and how we grow.

Part one

Everything you need

Verses 1–4

Before Peter asks for one thing, he lists what God has already handed over.

The man who wrote it

He calls himself “a servant,” a slave, before he calls himself an apostle. He knows how badly he blew it, and he was handed his job back anyway.

A promise Peter kept

Before he ever denied Jesus, Jesus told him what would come after:

“But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Luke 22:32

“When,” not “if.” Jesus knew Peter would fall and He planned the comeback before the fall. This whole letter is Peter strengthening his brothers.

If you're carrying a failure, notice who is writing this letter. God uses men who have fallen and been picked up. Yours isn't the first.

Given, not earned

What God has done

“Hath given”

Everything you need for life and for living godly is already in your hand: forgiveness, His Spirit, His Word, His promises. Not one thing is still on back-order.

What we do with it

“Giving all diligence”

That's verse 5. Because it was given, we now put effort in: not to get God's favor, but to use what we've been handed.

Many of you were told, all your life, that you were short on whatever it takes: willpower, brains, chances. God says the supply is already delivered. “Through the knowledge of him that hath called us.” It comes through knowing Him, not through being impressive.

Partakers of the divine nature

That phrase is easy to misread. Here is what it does not mean, and then what it does:

  • that you become a godGod is God, and we are not
  • that you will never sin againa believer still stumbles (1 John 1:9)
  • God's own life in youthe Holy Spirit moves in the day you trust Christ

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

“The corruption that is in the world through lust” is desire out of control: the craving that rots a life from the inside. Peter says you have escaped it. The chain is cut. Old habits can still pull, but they no longer own you.

Part two

Add to your faith

Verses 5–11

Saved by faith, then growing. Peter gives the men a ladder of seven steps, and the top step is love.

The ladder

faithwhere you start
virtuegoodness, moral courage
knowledgeknowing God and His Word
temperanceself-control
patiencestaying power
godlinessliving with God in mind
brotherly kindnesslove for fellow believers
charitylove that gives itself away

The old word temperance means self-control, the one many of these men have fought for years. And charity is the old word for love, the Greek agapē. Notice where it sits: at the top.

Faith isn't a step you climb. It's the ground floor, and you are already standing on it. Each new step is built on the last one. Nobody jumps to the top.

Growing, not earning

Ifthese things are in you and abound,
→
thenyou won't be barren or unfruitful.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8-9

This isn't a climb to get saved. The ladder is what a saved man's life grows into: a tree puts out fruit because it is alive, not to become alive.

Some days you'll be two steps down from last week. Put your foot back on the step you're on and keep going.

Don't forget where you came from

The man who forgotI'm just an addict. That's all I'll ever be.

Peter…is blind, and cannot see afar off, and hath forgotten that he was purged from his old sins.

“Purged” means washed clean, and it's in the past tense. A man can forget that: he stares at yesterday's failure so hard he can't see the forgiveness he already has. Peter calls that blind.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

When the regret comes back at 2 a.m., don't rehearse the details. Say what is true: I was purged.

Make it sure

God's side is settled: He called, and He saves everyone who comes to Him by faith. Peter isn't telling you to make God sure. He's telling you to make yourself sure, by seeing the evidence growing.

The evidence is the fruit of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness (Galatians 5:22-23). Look back a year. Is there more of that in you? Then something real is happening.

“Ye shall never fall”

What it does not mean

You will never stumble

Peter stumbled more than any man in the Gospels. He isn't promising a perfect record, and he isn't threatening a lost salvation.

What it does mean

You won't be tripped up and ruined

A man who is growing and who knows where he stands is steady on his feet. He doesn't wreck his life and his witness. And he ends with “an entrance… abundantly into the everlasting kingdom” (v. 11).

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Your place in heaven is held by Jesus (John 10:28), not by your grip. What you're growing for is a steady life now, and a sure entrance into the kingdom when He comes.

Part three

A sure word

Verses 12–21

Peter is leaving, so he points to two things that will not leave: what he saw with his own eyes, and the written Word.

A tent coming down

My tent is coming down.

“Tabernacle” is a tent. Peter pictures his body as a tent that he is about to put off, like taking off a coat. Jesus had shown him, years before, that he would be old when he died (John 21:18).

And look at what he does with that knowledge. He isn't panicking and he isn't hiding. He spends his last strength on reminding people of what matters. “I will endeavour that ye may be able after my decease to have these things always in remembrance.”

The question for today: if you knew you had a short time left, who would you write to, and what would you say? You may not have to write it down. You may just need to pick up the phone.

Not a story we made up

  • cunningly devised fablesclever stories, polished to sell
  • a feeling someone hadthat would be one man's word
  • eyewitnesses of his majesty“we heard,” “we were with him”

Peter doesn't ask anyone to take it on feeling. He says: I was there. A few years earlier, on a mountain, he and two others saw Jesus shine, and heard the Father speak. We'll see what that was on the next slide.

That matters for a man who wonders whether it's all true. The Christian faith is built on things that happened, and the people who saw them stayed with it for the rest of their lives, many of them to the death.

On the holy mount

This is the Transfiguration. Jesus took Peter, James and John up a mountain, and His face shone like the sun. Then the Father spoke from a bright cloud.

Peter heard it himself, and never forgot it: “This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.” The same Peter who had once said, “I know not the man.”

“While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him.”

Matthew 17:5

A light in a dark place

NowA lamp in the dark

The written Word, a light that shineth in a dark place. You may not see the whole road, but you can see the next step.

UntilThe day dawns

Until the day dawn. The dark place isn't forever. Christ is coming back.

ThenThe morning star

The day star arises in your hearts. Many Bible teachers take this to be Christ Himself, fully known at last.

“Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.”

Psalm 119:105

The world can feel like a dark place. The Bible is how you keep from stumbling in it. A man in recovery who reads a few verses every morning is lighting the lamp before he steps out the door.

Carried along

The word “moved” is a sailing word: the Greek means carried along, like a ship filled by wind. The men who wrote the Bible weren't making things up. They set the sail, and God's Spirit carried them.

That's why “no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation” (v. 20): it didn't start with one man's idea. When this Book says you are forgiven, God said it.

“All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness:”

2 Timothy 3:16

Points to remember

  1. Already given“His divine power hath given unto us all things that pertain unto life and godliness” 1:3Everything you need has already been handed to you. It comes by knowing Him.
  2. A new nature“Partakers of the divine nature, having escaped the corruption that is in the world through lust” 1:4The chain is cut and a new life has begun. You are not who you were.
  3. The ladder“Add to your faith virtue… and to brotherly kindness charity” 1:5-7Faith is the ground floor. Build on it one step at a time, and the top step is love.
  4. Don't forget“Hath forgotten that he was purged from his old sins” 1:9Don't stare at yesterday's failure. Remember what God already did.
  5. Make it sure“Give diligence to make your calling and election sure” 1:10Look for the fruit. It is the evidence that God's work in you is real.
  6. A sure word“Holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost” 1:21The Bible is God's Word, a light in a dark place. Read a little every morning.

Question 1 of 6

Already given

What has God's divine power already given to us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All things that pertain to life and godliness, and exceeding great and precious promises. It's already in your hand. You don't earn it; you use it, by knowing Him.

Question 2 of 6

A new nature

What have we escaped, and what have we become partakers of?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

We have escaped the corruption of runaway desire, and we share in God's own life through the Holy Spirit. Old habits still pull, but you're a new creature and they no longer own you.

Question 3 of 6

The ladder

What are we to add to our faith, and where does the ladder end?

What verses tell us about this?

“But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law.”

Galatians 5:22-23

Answer

Virtue, knowledge, temperance (self-control), patience, godliness, brotherly kindness, and charity, which is love. The ladder ends in love. Faith is the ground floor, and you grow one step at a time.

Question 4 of 6

Don't forget

What has the man who lacks these things forgotten?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

That he was purged from his old sins. They have been washed away. When the regret comes back, say what is true: I was purged. God wants you to remember that He did it.

Question 5 of 6

Make it sure

What does Peter tell us to make sure?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God:”

Romans 8:16

Answer

Our calling and election: that God really did call us. We make it sure by watching the fruit grow. It's not a way to earn salvation; it's evidence that God's work in you is real, so you can stand on it.

Question 6 of 6

A sure word

Why can we trust what the prophets wrote?

What verses tell us about this?

“All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness:”

2 Timothy 3:16

Answer

Because it didn't start with man's will. Holy men were carried along by the Holy Ghost, like a ship by the wind. Read a few verses before you step out the door today: it's a light in a dark place.
