Deuteronomy 15

The year of release, the open hand, and the servant set free

Every seven years debts are let go. A poor brother is not turned away. A servant goes out free, and not empty-handed, because Israel was once a bondman in Egypt. “Thou shalt open thine hand wide unto thy brother, to thy poor, and to thy needy.”

Part one

“At the end of every seven years”

Verses 1–11

Debts let go, and a hand that opens wide.

The seventh year

  1. 1Sow, reap, lend
  2. 2Sow, reap, lend
  3. 3Sow, reap, lend
  4. 4Sow, reap, lend
  5. 5Sow, reap, lend
  6. 6Sow, reap, lend
  7. 7The release“the LORD’S release”

A creditor is the man who lent, ought means anything at all, and exact means to demand payment. In the seventh year the lender was not to demand it of a brother in Israel. The word release comes six times in verses 1–9.

Moses does not say whether it was for the year or for good, so we should not guess. A foreigner could still be asked to pay (verse 3): this was a kindness inside the family.

If Israel obeyed, no one would be poor

If“thou carefully hearken unto the voice of the LORD thy God, to observe to do all these commandments”
→
Then“no poor among you”, and a people who “lend unto many nations” and borrow of none

This is the LORD's covenant with Israel in the land. It is not a rule that a man who does right will get rich: read it that way and you will lose heart the first time an honest man goes broke.

“For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich.”

2 Corinthians 8:9

A shut hand and an open hand

The hard heart

“harden thine heart, nor shut thine hand”

Find the reason it is not your business. The heart goes first, and then the hand follows it.

The LORD's way

“open thine hand wide unto him”

“sufficient for his need, in that which he wanteth.” Not a fortune, and not nothing: what the man needs.

“But whoso hath this world’s good, and seeth his brother have need, and shutteth up his bowels of compassion from him, how dwelleth the love of God in him?”

1 John 3:17

The poor man here is “thy brother”. In the church the men around you are brothers in Christ, and some of them are as short of money as you have ever been.

A man who used to take

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28

Some of us were the man with the shut hand and the open pocket, taking from family and friends to feed a habit. Paul does not stop at “steal no more”. He says why a man works now: that he may have to give. The old life took. The new one has something to give.

Regret over what you took is real, and the answer is not to carry it for ever. Stop, work honestly, make right what you can, and start to give.

“A thought in thy wicked heart”

the thoughtThe seventh year, the year of release, is at hand. Why lend him anything now? I will never see it again.

the LORD“and thine eye be evil against thy poor brother, and thou givest him nought; and he cry unto the LORD against thee, and it be sin unto thee.”

Nought is nothing. Notice what the LORD calls wicked: not the refusing yet, but the thought that comes first, as the year of release comes near. He deals with the heart before the hand.

And the poor man's cry does not go unheard: “and he cry unto the LORD against thee”. The man who has been poor, and cried, knows that this is true.

Lending to the LORD

“He that hath pity upon the poor lendeth unto the LORD; and that which he hath given will he pay him again.”

Proverbs 19:17

“I have shewed you all things, how that so labouring ye ought to support the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he said, It is more blessed to give than to receive.”

Acts 20:35

“Thine heart shall not be grieved when thou givest” (verse 10): God wants it given without a sour face, from a heart that has let it go. And the LORD notices every gift: he is the one who pays back, in his own way.

“The poor shall never cease”

“For ye have the poor always with you; but me ye have not always.”

Matthew 26:11

“For ye have the poor with you always, and whensoever ye will ye may do them good: but me ye have not always.”

Mark 14:7

Moses said it first, and Jesus took his words. Look at where Moses lands: not “so why bother”, but “therefore I command thee”. Because the poor will always be there, the open hand is a standing order. Mark adds: “whensoever ye will ye may do them good”. The words “open thine hand wide” stand twice, in verses 8 and 11.

The LORD's release for us

“Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross;”

Colossians 2:14

Every man here owes a debt he cannot pay. Here is an illustration, not a type the New Testament names: as the seventh-year release let a brother's debt go, the LORD has let go the debt of sin: the bill that stood against us is taken out of the way and nailed to the cross. The creditor is the One who let it go, and the debtor never got to the end of the bill.

If you are in debt to a man on earth, that is a different bill, and God still expects an honest man to pay what he owes. But the debt of your sin is paid, and no one can collect it from you again.

Part two

“Thou shalt not let him go away empty”

Verses 12–18

The servant is set free, and sent out full-handed.

Free, and not empty

  • Out of thy flockSheep and goats to start again with.
  • Out of thy floorThe threshing floor, where the grain is beaten out: bread for the road.
  • Out of thy winepressThe fruit of the vineyard, from the master's own harvest.

Furnish means to supply, and liberally means freely, with an open hand. A Hebrew servant served six years and went out with a start in life, and the master was told to give it “of that wherewith the LORD thy God hath blessed thee”.

The word empty comes only twice in Deuteronomy: here in 15:13, “thou shalt not let him go away empty”, and in 16:16, “they shall not appear before the LORD empty”. It is the way God sent Israel out of Egypt: “when ye go, ye shall not go empty” (Exodus 3:21). He is that kind of God, and he asks his people to be that kind of master.

“Remember that thou wast a bondman”

Remember where you were, and who brought you out.

Four times in Deuteronomy Moses says “thou shalt remember that thou wast a bondman”: 15:15, 16:12, 24:18 and 24:22. Each time it comes right after a law about being kind: to the servant going free (15:15), to the stranger, the fatherless and the widow at the feast (16:12), and twice more to the stranger, the fatherless and the widow (24:18, 22).

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

The point of remembering is not to be shamed. A man who remembers the pit he was in, and that he was brought out of it, is the last man to look down on another, and the first to give. God does not hold your old chains over you: “the LORD thy God redeemed thee”. He holds them up to teach you mercy.

The servant who would not go

the servantI will not go away from thee.

why“because he loveth thee and thine house, because he is well with thee”

“And if the servant shall plainly say, I love my master, my wife, and my children; I will not go out free: Then his master shall bring him unto the judges; he shall also bring him to the door, or unto the door post; and his master shall bore his ear through with an aul; and he shall serve him for ever.”

Exodus 21:5–6

An aul is a sharp tool for piercing, an awl. The mark in his ear showed that he stayed because he chose to: nobody kept him against his will. And for the servant who does go, the master is told, “It shall not seem hard unto thee, when thou sendest him away free” (verse 18): he has been “worth a double hired servant” for six years.

A servant of his own free will

“Sacrifice and offering thou didst not desire; mine ears hast thou opened: burnt offering and sin offering hast thou not required. Then said I, Lo, I come: in the volume of the book it is written of me, I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart.”

Psalm 40:6–8

“Wherefore when he cometh into the world, he saith, Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not, but a body hast thou prepared me: In burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin thou hast had no pleasure. Then said I, Lo, I come (in the volume of the book it is written of me,) to do thy will, O God.”

Hebrews 10:5–7

Psalm 40 says “mine ears hast thou opened” and “I delight to do thy will”, and Hebrews 10 gives those words to Christ, who came to do the Father's will. The ear opened for the servant who loves his master has long been read as a picture of him.

Free, and he stayed

“But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men: And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.”

Philippians 2:7–8

He was free, and he took the form of a servant for us, and he did not walk away from the door. The man he has set free can say the same: I love my master. Not because he has to, but because he is well with him.

Part three

“All the firstling males”

Verses 19–23

The first and the best belongs to the LORD, and is eaten before him with joy.

The firstling, and the blemished one

Without blemish

Before the LORD, with the household

“Sanctify” means set apart. The firstling was not worked or shorn, and was eaten “year by year” in the place the LORD chose, “thou and thy household”: a meal of thanks.

Lame, blind, or ill-blemished

Not for the altar

It was not to be sacrificed to the LORD. It could be eaten at home, “within thy gates”, by the clean and the unclean alike, like a roebuck or a hart: a small deer and a stag, wild meat of no ceremony.

Whichever it was, “thou shalt not eat the blood thereof; thou shalt pour it upon the ground as water”. We saw why in Leviticus 17:11: “the life of the flesh is in the blood… it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.” The blood belongs to God.

The best was to go to God, and he gave his own best for us. “Lame” and “blind” are not words God uses to shut men out. A man who feels himself blemished is not turned away from the Saviour; it is the Lamb who is without blemish (1 Peter 1:19), and he takes the blemished man who comes to him.

Real world: open the hand

  • SinThe “thought in thy wicked heart” that finds a reason not to help, and the old habit of taking instead of giving. Named plainly, and shared by every man here.
  • RegretWhat we took, what we owe, and the years we were someone's bondman. Remember them in order to give, not to be crushed.
  • Grace“The LORD thy God redeemed thee.” The bill against you was nailed to the cross (Colossians 2:14), and the Son has made you free (John 8:36).
  • HopeA servant was sent out with flock, floor and winepress. God does not set a man free and leave him empty: he sends him out with something to start again with.
  • TodayGive one thing to one brother in need today, however small: a meal, a ride, an hour. And if the thought comes that it is no use, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor.

Points to remember

  1. The release“At the end of every seven years thou shalt make a release.” 15:1Debts to a brother were let go: “the LORD’S release”.
  2. The open hand“Thou shalt open thine hand wide unto him” 15:8Lend or give what he needs, and do not harden your heart.
  3. The wicked thought“Beware that there be not a thought in thy wicked heart” 15:9The LORD deals with the thought before the act.
  4. The poor“For the poor shall never cease out of the land” 15:11So the open hand is a standing order, and Jesus took the words (Matthew 26:11).
  5. Remember“thou shalt remember that thou wast a bondman in the land of Egypt, and the LORD thy God redeemed thee” 15:15Remember the bondage in order to be kind, and to thank the One who redeemed you.
  6. The servant who stays“thrust it through his ear unto the door, and he shall be thy servant for ever” 15:17The servant who loved his master chose to stay, and the pierced ear marked it.

Question 1 of 6

The release

What was to be done at the end of every seven years?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A release. The creditor was not to exact from his neighbour or brother what he had lent him, “because it is called the LORD’S release”.

Question 2 of 6

The open hand

How was a man to treat his poor brother?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

With an open hand and a soft heart: lend him “sufficient for his need, in that which he wanteth”.

Question 3 of 6

The wicked thought

What thought did the LORD warn a man to beware of?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“The seventh year, the year of release, is at hand”: the thought that makes a man refuse to lend, and give his poor brother nothing. The LORD calls it a wicked heart, and sin.

Question 4 of 6

The poor

What does the LORD say about the poor in the land?

What verses tell us about this?

“For ye have the poor always with you; but me ye have not always.”

Matthew 26:11

Answer

That they will never cease out of the land, and therefore: “Thou shalt open thine hand wide unto thy brother, to thy poor, and to thy needy”.

Question 5 of 6

Remember

What was Israel to remember when a servant went free?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That they were once bondmen in Egypt, and that the LORD their God redeemed them. So they were to send the servant out free, and not empty.

Question 6 of 6

The servant who stays

What was done to the servant who would not go away?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

His master took an aul and thrust it through his ear unto the door, and he was his servant for ever: a servant who stayed because he loved the house.
