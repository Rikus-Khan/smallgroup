Part one

Cows of Bashan

Verses 1–3

A herdman uses herdman's language.

Fat and comfortable

A quick reminder: Amos, a herdman from Judah, is preaching in Israel about 760 BC, in boom times. Kine means cows. Bashan was the rich grassland east of the Sea of Galilee, famous for fat, strong cattle (Psalm 22:12). Amos looks at the well-off women of Samaria and calls them prize cows.

What they said

“Bring, and let us drink.”

To their husbands, at the party in the big house on the hill.

What God saw

The poor oppressed, the needy crushed

Every round was paid for by somebody at the bottom.

The drinking wasn't the root. The root was a life built on taking, numbed with comfort so they never had to look at who was paying for it.

God swore “by his holiness”: they'd be led away like cattle, with hooks, out through the holes in the broken city wall, one after another. Assyrian carvings show it: captives led away in long lines, roped together.

Part two

Come to Bethel

Verses 4–5

Amos borrows the priest's words and turns them inside out.

Church as usual

Bethel and Gilgal were Israel's big worship centers. A priest would call out, “Come to Bethel, and worship.” Amos says it with one word changed:

“Come to Bethel”→and worshipwhat the priests said
“Come to Bethel”→and transgresswhat God said it really was

They were busy: sacrifices every morning, tithes paid on schedule, thank-offerings, and gifts announced out loud so everybody knew. “For this liketh you”: you enjoy it. It was for them, not for God. Nothing in their lives had changed.

“And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.”

1 Samuel 15:22

Part three

Yet ye returned not

Verses 6–11

Five wake-up calls, and the same sad line after each.

Not just bad weather

“Cleanness of teeth” is a blunt way of saying nothing to chew: famine. Then God held back the rain three months before harvest, when the grain needed it most. And He did it in a way nobody could miss:

One city: rain

rainharvest

The next city over: none

dustwithered

Rain on one field and not on the one next to it. People walked from town to town looking for water. God was writing the message across the land. “Yet have ye not returned unto me.”

Five wake-up calls

  1. Famineempty mouths · v. 6
  2. Droughtrain held back · vv. 7–8
  3. Blight and locustsgardens, vines, figs, olives · v. 9
  4. Plague and waryoung men killed · v. 10
  5. Overthrownlike Sodom and Gomorrah · v. 11

Blasting and mildew are crop diseases: grain scorched by the hot east wind, or rotting. The palmerworm is a kind of locust. Each blow was harder than the last.

Many of us know this staircase: the lost job, the wreck, the arrest, the hospital, the people who stopped calling. Each one was a chance to turn around. God wasn't trying to destroy Israel. He was trying to get them to come home.

“Returned”

Notice what God says He wanted: not “yet ye didn't suffer enough,” but “unto me.” He wasn't after punishment. He was after them. Returning isn't cleaning yourself up first. It's turning around and coming back to Him, the way the lost son did:

“And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father’s have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee,”

Luke 15:17–18

A stick pulled out of the fire.

A firebrand is a burning stick. Somebody reaches into the fire and yanks it out just before it's gone: blackened, smoking, but not burned up.

That was Israel. Some of their cities had been wiped out like Sodom, and they had been snatched out at the last moment. Even that didn't turn them.

A lot of us are firebrands. The overdose we lived through, the crash we walked away from, the night that should have been our last. Scorched, but here. God didn't pull you out of the fire for nothing. He pulled you out so you could come home.

“And the LORD said unto Satan, The LORD rebuke thee, O Satan; even the LORD that hath chosen Jerusalem rebuke thee: is not this a brand plucked out of the fire?”

Zechariah 3:2

Part four

Prepare to meet thy God

Verses 12–13

The warnings are over. Now God Himself is coming.

Who you will meet

Israel had been dealing with God's messengers and God's warnings. Now they would meet God Himself. Amos tells them who He is:

  • He forms the mountainsthe biggest things you can see
  • He creates the windwhat you can't see at all
  • He declares unto man his thoughtnothing in a man's mind is hidden from Him
  • He turns morning to darknessday and night obey Him
  • He walks on the high placesthe whole earth is His floor

“The LORD, The God of hosts, is his name.” “Hosts” means armies: the armies of heaven. This is not a God you hide from, bargain with, or outrun. And He knows the thoughts you've never said out loud.

“Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do.”

Hebrews 4:13

Ready for that meeting

For Israel, this was a warning: the next time they met God, it would be as their Judge. About forty years later, Assyria came.

Every man will meet God. “It is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment” (Hebrews 9:27). The question is how you'll meet Him.

Unprepared

Meet Him as Judge

On your own record, with every warning ignored. That's where Israel was headed.

Prepared

Meet Him in peace

“Being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ” (Romans 5:1). Not on your record, but on His.

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

Points to remember

  1. Comfort“Which oppress the poor, which crush the needy” 4:1God sees who pays for our comfort.
  2. Empty religion“Come to Bethel, and transgress” 4:4Busy religion with an unchanged life is still sin.
  3. Hard times“Yet have ye not returned unto me” 4:6God uses hard things to call us home.
  4. Repentance“Returned unto me” 4:8Turn around and come back to Him, just as you are.
  5. Rescue“A firebrand plucked out of the burning” 4:11Scorched but saved. God pulled you out for a reason.
  6. Meeting God“Prepare to meet thy God” 4:12Everyone meets God. Meet Him in peace, through Christ, today.

Question 1 of 6

Comfort

What does God see when comfortable people crush the poor?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that oppresseth the poor reproacheth his Maker: but he that honoureth him hath mercy on the poor.”

Proverbs 14:31

Answer

He sees who's paying. The women of Samaria saw only the party; God saw the poor being crushed to pay for it, and He swore by His own holiness to act. To crush a poor man is to insult the God who made him. God notices, and He stands with the man at the bottom.

Question 2 of 6

Empty religion

Can religion cover a life that hasn't changed?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.”

1 Samuel 15:22

“Therefore when thou doest thine alms, do not sound a trumpet before thee, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, that they may have glory of men. Verily I say unto you, They have their reward.”

Matthew 6:2

Answer

No. Israel never missed a sacrifice or a tithe, and God called it “transgression,” because it was for show and nothing in their lives changed. Church, giving, and meetings are good things, but they can't stand in for a heart that has turned to God and obeys Him.

Question 3 of 6

Hard times

Why does God let hard things come?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come, and let us return unto the LORD: for he hath torn, and he will heal us; he hath smitten, and he will bind us up.”

Hosea 6:1

Answer

Not every hard thing is a punishment for a particular sin. But in Amos 4, God says plainly why He sent famine, drought, and the rest: so they would come back to Him. Hard times can be God's hand on the shoulder. The God who tears is the God who heals, and He wants us home.

Question 4 of 6

Repentance

What does it mean to return to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near: Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts: and let him return unto the LORD, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon.”

Isaiah 55:6–7

“And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father’s have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee,”

Luke 15:17–18

Answer

It means turning around: leaving your own way and coming back to the Lord Himself, not just to a better routine. You don't fix yourself first. Like the lost son, you come to yourself, get up, and go home, admitting where you've been. And God promises mercy, and abundant pardon.

Question 5 of 6

Rescue

What is a firebrand plucked out of the burning?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD said unto Satan, The LORD rebuke thee, O Satan; even the LORD that hath chosen Jerusalem rebuke thee: is not this a brand plucked out of the fire?”

Zechariah 3:2

“He hath not dealt with us after our sins; nor rewarded us according to our iniquities.”

Psalm 103:10

Answer

A burning stick snatched out of the fire at the last second: scorched, but not consumed. That was Israel, and it's many of us. God hasn't dealt with us as our sins deserved. If He pulled you out, it wasn't so you could go back in. It was so you would turn to Him.

Question 6 of 6

Meeting God

What does “prepare to meet thy God” mean for us?

What verses tell us about this?

“And as it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment:”

Hebrews 9:27

“Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life.”

John 5:24

Answer

Every man will stand before the God who made the mountains and knows his thoughts. For Israel, the warning meant judgment was coming. For us, it's an invitation while there's time: believe on Jesus, and you will not come into condemnation. The way to be ready to meet God is to trust His Son today.
