Proverbs 4

Two roads and a guarded heart

A father passes down what his own father taught him: get wisdom, stay off the wicked road, and guard your heart.

Part one

A father's words

Verses 1–9

What my father told me, I'm telling you.

Passed down the line

David“Let thine heart retain my words”Solomon, his son
Solomon“Hear, ye children”his sons
God's word, through this book“Get wisdom”you

Solomon had brothers, so “only beloved” means treasured, one of a kind in his mother's eyes. He grew up loved, and taught.

Some of us never got that. What got passed down to us was anger, bottles, or nothing at all. The good news: with God, a new line can start with you. Your kids, or the younger men watching you, can get from you what you never got.

“The principal thing”

רֵאשִׁית said rē'shîth first, beginning, the main thing

It's the same word as “the beginning of knowledge” back in 1:7, and close kin to the Bible's first words, “In the beginning.” Wisdom isn't one item on the list. It goes first.

“With all thy getting” means whatever it costs you. A man will spend everything on what he thinks matters most. Spend it here.

Part two

Two roads

Verses 10–19

One gets brighter the farther you walk. The other gets darker.

Take fast hold of instruction; let her not go.

Straitened means cramped, boxed in. Sin promises freedom and ends up hemming a man in: the habit, the debt, the warrant, the people you can't get away from. Wisdom gives you room to walk, even to run.

Verse 13 says to grab hold like a man who won't let go of a rope: keep her; for she is thy life.

Give it a wide berth

Four commands in one verse, all saying the same thing: don't get close. Not “drive by and see if you're strong enough.” Go the long way around.

For a man in recovery, that's concrete: the old block, the old bar, the old contact in your phone. You don't have to win a fight you never walk into.

“Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart” (2 Timothy 2:22).

Hooked on it

These men can't sleep until they've done some damage. Evil isn't a slip for them anymore. It's a craving, like a habit that won't let you rest until it's fed.

“Bread of wickedness” and “wine of violence” is a word-picture: they live off what they take. What a man feeds on is what he becomes.

Every addiction works this way. It starts as something you do and ends as something you need. Only Jesus breaks that kind of hold, and He does: “If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed” (John 8:36).

Brighter as you go

The just man's road is like sunrise. It starts dim, just a little light. But it keeps getting brighter, “more and more,” until full noon.

You may only see a little light right now. That's how dawn starts. Keep walking.

The wicked man's road goes the other way: “they know not at what they stumble” (v. 19). He keeps getting hurt and can't even tell you what hit him.

Jesus and the two roads

“Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the way, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it.”

Matthew 7:13–14

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Proverbs shows two roads. Jesus shows us He is the road and the light on it: “I am the way, the truth, and the life” (John 14:6). The path of the just is walking with Him.

Part three

Guard your heart

Verses 20–27

Heart, mouth, eyes, feet: a guard for every gate.

Where life flows from

“With all diligence” means above everything else you guard. Guard your heart more than your money, your phone, or your reputation.

“Issues” means springs, the places where water flows out. Your words, choices, and actions all flow out of what's in your heart. Poison the spring, and every stream downhill gets it.

In the Bible, the heart is more than feelings. It's the real you: what you think, want, and decide.

A heart needs more than a guard

“The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?”

Jeremiah 17:9

We can't guard our way to a clean heart. Ours needs replacing. And that's exactly what God promises:

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

“And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 4:7

You keep watch, and God keeps watch with you. In Christ, you don't guard your heart alone.

Guard every gate

What you say, what you look at, and where you go either feed the heart or poison it.

Ponder means weigh it before you step. Most men don't fall off a cliff. They drift, one small step off the road at a time.

Keep your eyes fixed ahead: “Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith” (Hebrews 12:2).

Points to remember

  1. Passing it on“Let thine heart retain my words” 4:4Wisdom gets passed down. With God, a new line can start with you.
  2. First things first“Wisdom is the principal thing” 4:7Wisdom goes first, whatever it costs.
  3. Temptation“Avoid it, pass not by it, turn from it” 4:15Don't test yourself near it. Go the long way around.
  4. Two roads“As the shining light” 4:18The just man's road gets brighter. The wicked man's gets darker.
  5. The heart“Keep thy heart with all diligence” 4:23Everything in your life flows out of your heart. God gives a new one.
  6. Staying on track“Turn not to the right hand nor to the left” 4:27Watch your mouth, eyes, and feet. Men drift one small step at a time.

Question 1 of 6

Passing it on

What did this father pass down to his son?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The same thing his own father gave him: God's words, kept in the heart, and the charge to get wisdom. If no one passed that to you, you can still receive it from God, and pass it on to someone else.

Question 2 of 6

First things first

What does Solomon call the principal thing?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Wisdom. It comes first, before everything else, and it's worth whatever it costs. Hold her close, and she lifts you up.

Question 3 of 6

Temptation

What's the best way to handle the path of the wicked?

What verses tell us about this?

“Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart.”

2 Timothy 2:22

Answer

Stay away from it. Don't step onto it, don't walk past it, turn away, and keep going. Run from it, and run toward good people who call on the Lord.

Question 4 of 6

Two roads

How is the path of the just different from the way of the wicked?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Answer

The just man's road gets brighter the longer he walks it, like the sunrise. The wicked man's road is dark, and he can't even see what trips him. Following Jesus is walking in the light.

Question 5 of 6

The heart

Why must a man guard his heart above all else?

What verses tell us about this?

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

Answer

Because everything in life flows out of it: words, choices, actions. Keep God's words in the middle of it. And remember, God doesn't just ask you to guard your heart. He gives you a new one, and His peace guards it with you.

Question 6 of 6

Staying on track

How do you keep from drifting off the path?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Watch your mouth, keep your eyes looking straight ahead, and think before you step. Don't drift right or left. And keep your eyes on Jesus, who started your faith and will finish it.
