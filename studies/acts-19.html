Acts 19

Ephesus: the fire and the uproar

Paul settles in Ephesus for two years. Men who dabbled in the dark arts burn their books, and a whole city nearly riots over lost business.

Part one

Twelve men who hadn't heard

Verses 1–7

Paul reaches Ephesus and finds a small group with part of the story.

The road to Ephesus

This is the start of Paul's third journey, about AD 53. He came down from the inland highlands, “the upper coasts,” the long way round, and arrived at Ephesus, the biggest city of Roman Asia.

Ephesus was a busy harbor and a city of trade. It was also home to the temple of Diana, one of the wonders of the ancient world. A good place to plant a church, and a hard one.

Apollos had just been working in Corinth (18:24–28), a good man who, like these twelve, knew only the baptism of John until he was taught more.

Looking ahead, then looking back

John's baptism

“Someone is coming”

A turn from sin, and a promise that the Messiah was on His way. Good, and true, but it was waiting for Someone.

Baptism in Jesus' name

“He has come”

A believer's step that pictures what has happened: he trusts the Christ who died and rose. Baptism doesn't save. Faith in Jesus does.

These twelve were honest men with half the story. They had repented, but nobody had told them that the One John pointed to had come, died for them and risen. Once they heard it, they believed, and were baptized to show it.

Honest about what you don't know? That's a fine place to start. Nobody gets lectured here. Paul simply asked, listened, and told them the rest.

One more new group, one more sign

Acts is a history of the church spreading outward. Each time a new kind of people came in, God made it plain that they truly belonged:

  1. JerusalemJewish believers. The Spirit comes, and real languages are heard (Acts 2)
  2. SamariaHalf-Jewish neighbors. Peter and John come, and the Spirit is given (Acts 8)
  3. CaesareaCornelius, a Gentile. The Spirit falls as Peter speaks (Acts 10)
  4. EphesusJohn's followers, about AD 53. The last group, and the same Spirit (Acts 19)

These are scenes in a history, not a pattern for you to copy. The Holy Spirit lives in every believer from the moment he trusts Jesus: “after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit” (Ephesians 1:13).

You don't need hands laid on you or a second experience. If you've put your trust in Jesus, He's already in you. On your worst day, He's still there.

Part two

Two years in a lecture hall

Verses 8–12

When the synagogue closes its doors, Paul rents a classroom and teaches every day.

Three years in one city

  1. About AD 53Paul arrives. Three months in the synagogue (v. 8)
  2. Two yearsDaily teaching in Tyrannus' hall (v. 10)
  3. About AD 551 Corinthians is written from Ephesus
  4. About AD 56“By the space of three years” in all (Acts 20:31)

Some in the synagogue were “hardened” and “spake evil of that way,” the early name for following Jesus. So Paul didn't argue forever. He took the believers and moved down the street, to a hired hall, a schoolroom.

There was no building, no budget, and no big event. Just a man who showed up every day and talked about Jesus. Slow, daily faithfulness is how a whole region heard.

All Asia heard

“Asia” here is the Roman province, the west end of what is now Turkey. Ephesus sat at the crossroads of its roads and sea lanes, so people from all over passed through that hall and carried the news home.

The cities on this map are the same ones named in Revelation 2 and 3, the seven churches, plus nearby Colosse. Years later, Jesus would write letters to churches in this very region.

One man's two years of daily work reached a whole province. You may not see what your small, steady days are reaching either.

God wrought them.

Look at who the verb belongs to. Luke doesn't say Paul had the power. He says “God wrought special miracles”, and special means out of the ordinary. It was God's doing, at a time when He was putting His stamp on a new work.

The handkerchiefs weren't a magic cloth, and nobody sold them. This isn't a method anyone can run, and no one's health or paycheck depends on mailing in a cloth. God heals when He chooses, and He is always worth asking.

Hold this thought. In the next verses, somebody tries to treat the name of Jesus like a magic word, and it goes badly.

Part three

The books that burned

Verses 13–20

A bad imitation, a city's fear, and a bonfire of fifty thousand pieces of silver.

Borrowed words

Seven brothers, traveling exorcistsWe adjure you by Jesus whom Paul preacheth.

The evil spiritJesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye?
no relationship

“Vagabond” means wandering, and an exorcist drove out spirits for pay. These men watched Paul work and thought they'd found a phrase that worked. They used Jesus' name like a password.

The name of Jesus is not a spell. It has power for the people who belong to Him, and only as He wills. These men knew about Him. They had never come to know Him, and it ended with them running down the street, beaten and half-dressed.

You can't borrow somebody else's faith. Not your mother's, not your sponsor's, not the guy who got you through last month. Paul's relationship with Jesus didn't cover those seven. Yours has to be your own.

Fear, then the truth

They came. They confessed. They shewed.

Notice who these were: “many that believed.” They were already believers, but some still had a foot in the old world: the spells, the charms, the secrets. When the whole city saw how the sons of Sceva were exposed, fear fell and the name of Jesus was lifted high. And these believers stopped hiding.

They didn't confess in private to themselves. They came, they said it out loud, and they shewed their deeds: they brought it into the light. Nobody in the text says they were shamed for it. Luke says the name of the Lord was “magnified.”

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

If you fell yesterday, this is the way back in. Not hiding, not promising to do better. Come, say it, show it, and let Him cleanse it. And tell one trusted man, so you're not carrying it alone.

“Before all men”

Ephesus was known for its charms and spells, written on scrolls and sold for a good price. “Curious arts” means magic: reading the stars, casting spells, calling up spirits.

They “counted the price” first. That's a sober thing to do. They knew exactly what it cost, and they burned it anyway, in public, where nobody could dig it back out later.

Fifty thousand pieces of silver is more than most of these men would earn in a lifetime. Nobody made them. They looked at what was in the pile and decided it had to go.

What fifty thousand means

A piece of silver here was probably worth about a day's pay for a working man. Work it out:

That was the value of the old life, and they put a match to it. Why? Because a man cannot hold that and Jesus with the same hands. Whatever it costs to cut something off, it costs less than keeping it.

“Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto treasure hid in a field; the which when a man hath found, he hideth, and for joy thereof goeth and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field.”

Matthew 13:44

Look at the verse right after the fire: “So mightily grew the word of God and prevailed” (v. 20). The old life burned, and the new one grew.

What's in your pile?

Your books won't be scrolls. Maybe they're these. Pick one today and burn it, openly, where you can't get it back:

You won't do it perfectly, and you may need to do it again next month. Those people did it in front of a whole city. God doesn't ask you to be strong enough. He asks you to put it where you can't reach it, and to ask Him for the rest.

Part four

The riot at the theatre

Verses 21–41

The silversmiths see their business shrinking, and the whole city goes loud.

A plan, in three steps

Paul is thinking ahead: Macedonia and Achaia (northern and southern Greece), then Jerusalem, “and after that I must also see Rome.” He is looking at the whole map.

He'll get there, but not the way he pictures it: in chains, after a shipwreck (Acts 27–28). Our plans seldom run in a straight line. Paul kept going anyway, because the destination was God's, not his.

Timotheus and Erastus went ahead into Macedonia (v. 22). Paul stayed on in Ephesus and sent his people ahead.

What the riot was really about

Demetrius made little silver shrines of the goddess, and sold them to visitors and pilgrims. As more people turned to Jesus, fewer bought shrines. Listen to what he says, and what he means:

  • “Our goddess is being insulted”the rallying cry
  • “The honor of Ephesus is at stake”the shouting point
  • “By this craft we have our wealth”the real reason, and he says it out loud (v. 25)

Paul never attacked the goddess or her temple (v. 37). The truth just emptied the shrine business. When the gospel costs somebody money, watch how fast the religion shows up in their argument.

It's worth remembering for yourself too. When you leave a way of life, you may cost people who made a living off it: your supplier, your old crew. They will have plenty of reasons, and money won't be the one they name. Don't be surprised, and don't go back to settle it.

The great theatre

The theatre of Ephesus was an open-air bowl that held about 25,000 people. They grabbed Paul's traveling companions, Gaius and Aristarchus, and dragged them in.

Paul, brave as ever, wanted to walk in and speak. “The disciples suffered him not,” and some friends among the officials of Asia sent word begging him to stay out (v. 31).

Sometimes the bravest thing is to be held back. God used his friends to stop him. If somebody who loves you says “don't go in there,” listen.

Two hours. One slogan. Nobody knew why.

Some shouted one thing, some another. Most of the crowd had no idea why they were there. When a Jew, Alexander, tried to speak, the shouting only got louder: “Great is Diana of the Ephesians”, for about two hours straight.

A mob doesn't think. It just gets louder, and every man in it feels braver than he is. Most of us know the feeling of going along with the crowd and finding out later we never knew why. Pause and ask yourself: why am I here?

One calm voice

The townclerk was the city's top official. He knew Rome would punish a riot with no cause. So he calmed them down: nobody doubts the goddess, he said, and these men are neither temple-robbers nor blasphemers.

God used a pagan official, who didn't believe a word of it, to protect His people. Paul's team had been careful: they didn't mock the temple, they just told the truth about Jesus. Telling the truth, and not insulting people for believing something else, takes the air out of a mob.

Nobody was hurt

The clerk reminded them there were proper courts: “the law is open.” If Demetrius had a complaint, he could bring it there. Then he sent everyone home.

Look at what the chapter began and ended with. It started with twelve men who had never heard, and ended with a whole city that couldn't ignore Jesus. The fire in the street and the roar in the theatre were the sound of something real taking hold.

And through all of it, Paul didn't lose a hair. He wasn't spared the trouble, but he was kept through it by friends, by officials, by the Lord. If you've got trouble today, the same hands are around you.

Points to remember

  1. The Holy Spirit“after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit” Ephesians 1:13The Spirit lives in every believer from the moment he trusts Jesus. You don't need a second step.
  2. The name of Jesus“Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye?” 19:15Jesus' name isn't a magic word. You have to know Him yourself.
  3. Starting over“came, and confessed, and shewed their deeds” 19:18Come out of hiding. Say it, show it, and let Him cleanse it.
  4. The cost“they counted the price of them” 19:19They counted the price and burned it anyway. Then the word of God grew.
  5. Money“by this craft we have our wealth” 19:25When the truth costs people money, watch the real reasons. Don't go back to settle it.
  6. God's care“the disciples suffered him not” 19:30God keeps His people through friends, officials, and even unlikely strangers.

Question 1 of 6

The Holy Spirit

When does the Holy Spirit come to live in a believer?

What verses tell us about this?

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

“But ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his.”

Romans 8:9

Answer

At the moment he believes. The Spirit lives in every believer from salvation on: nobody who belongs to Christ is without Him. The twelve at Ephesus were a special case, new believers who had never heard of Jesus, and God marked them as part of His church. It's not a pattern we need to repeat.

Question 2 of 6

The name of Jesus

Why did the sons of Sceva fail when they used the name of Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.”

Acts 4:12

Answer

They treated His name like a magic password. They knew about Jesus because of Paul, but they didn't know Jesus. The name has power for the people who belong to Him, and there is salvation in no other. Know Him for yourself.

Question 3 of 6

Starting over

What did the new believers do about their old life?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

They stopped hiding. They came, confessed, brought their deeds into the light, and burned the tools of their old life where everyone could see. God forgives and cleanses everyone who confesses.

Question 4 of 6

The cost

Is it worth the cost to leave the old life behind?

What verses tell us about this?

“Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto treasure hid in a field; the which when a man hath found, he hideth, and for joy thereof goeth and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field.”

Matthew 13:44

Answer

Yes. They counted fifty thousand pieces of silver and burned it. Like the man who found the treasure, they joyfully gave up everything for something worth more. After the fire, the word of God grew and prevailed. What you give up is real, but what you get is more.

Question 5 of 6

Money

What happens when the gospel gets in the way of someone's money?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the love of money is the root of all evil: which while some coveted after, they have erred from the faith, and pierced themselves through with many sorrows.”

1 Timothy 6:10

Answer

People who lose money get angry, and they dress it up in religion. Demetrius said the goddess was in danger, but he said himself that it was about the wealth. The love of money still stirs up trouble. Don't be surprised when leaving an old life costs somebody else.

Question 6 of 6

God's care

Who looked after Paul when the whole city was in an uproar?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

His fellow believers held him back. His friends among the officials sent a warning. And even a pagan townclerk calmed the mob. God used all of them. Nobody in this chapter is left alone, and neither are you.
