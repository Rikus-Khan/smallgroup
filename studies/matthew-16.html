Matthew 16

Thou art the Christ

Far up north, Jesus asks the one question every man has to answer for himself. Peter gets it right, then minutes later gets it badly wrong, and Jesus shows the road ahead: a cross, and a crown.

Part one

Signs and leaven

Verses 1–12

Men who could read the sky but not the Son of God, and a warning about what spreads.

Enemies on the same side

The Pharisees

the strict rule-keepers

Popular with ordinary people. Believed in angels and the resurrection, and buried God's law under their traditions (15:3).

The Sadducees

the temple's rich families

Ran the priesthood and got along with Rome. Said there was “no resurrection, neither angel, nor spirit” (Acts 23:8).

These two groups couldn't stand each other. But they came together against Jesus. “Tempting” means testing Him, trying to trap Him. He had already done miracle after miracle. They didn't want proof. They wanted a show they could pick apart.

They could read the sky

“Lowring” means dark and threatening, heavy with cloud. Any farmer or fisherman could read that sky. These men could forecast the weather, yet they couldn't see that the promised Messiah was standing right in front of them, healing the sick and feeding thousands.

The one sign they would get

Jonah 1:17

Now the LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.

Matthew 12:40

For as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale’s belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth.

Jonas is the Greek form of Jonah. Jonah went down into the deep and came back up. Jesus would go into the grave and come back out on the third day. The empty tomb is the sign that settles it.

“And he left them, and departed.” When a man's mind is made up against Jesus, more proof won't change it. Jesus didn't put on a show. He walked away and let them think about it.

Worried about bread, with Him in the boat

The 5,000The 4,000

One loaf in the boat (Mark 8:14), twelve men, and they panicked about lunch. Sitting next to them was the Man who had fed thousands twice. Jesus didn't scold them for forgetting the bread. He scolded them for forgetting Him.

Fear has a short memory. When the next worry hits, look back at what He's already done for you. He hasn't changed.

Leaven

Leaven is yeast, a bit of old sour dough kept back and worked into the new batch. You can't see it work, but give it time and it spreads through all of it.

The Pharisees' leaven was hypocrisy (Luke 12:1): looking good outside, rotten inside. The Sadducees' was unbelief: no resurrection, no angels, no spirit, a religion with nothing supernatural left in it.

Wrong thinking works the same way. “Just one won't hurt.” “I've got this on my own.” It starts small and quiet, and it spreads. Watch what you let in.

Part two

Whom say ye that I am?

Verses 13–20

The big question, asked in a town named for Caesar.

Caesarea Philippi

A city at the far north edge of the land, where one of the springs of the Jordan gushes out of a cliff at the foot of Mount Hermon.

Herod's son Philip rebuilt it and named it for Caesar, the Roman emperor. In the cliff was a cave with a shrine to Pan, a Greek god. Temples, idols, and the emperor's name on everything.

Of all places, it was there that Jesus asked, “Whom do men say that I … am?”

What people said, and what Peter said

  • John the Baptistback from the dead, Herod feared (14:2)
  • Eliaspromised to come back (Malachi 4:5)
  • Jeremias, or one of the prophetsa great man, a messenger from God
  • “the Christ, the Son of the living God”God's promised King, and God's own Son

People said kind things about Jesus, then as now: a prophet, a good teacher. It still falls short. Christ means the Anointed One, the Messiah. And “the living God”, said in a town full of dead idols.

Then the question gets personal: “But whom say ye?” Not your mother, not your church. You. And Peter didn't figure it out on his own. The Father showed him. God still opens a man's eyes to who Jesus is, and whosoever will may come.

Peter, and the rock

Simon Barjona → Peter Barjona means “son of Jona.” Peter (Greek Petros) means “a stone.” Then Jesus says “this rock” (petra), a different word: solid bedrock.

Christians have read this verse different ways. Baptists have long understood the rock to be the truth Peter just confessed: Jesus, the Christ, the Son of the living God. Christ Himself is the foundation. Peter wrote it himself, years later:

“Wherefore also it is contained in the scripture, Behold, I lay in Sion a chief corner stone, elect, precious: and he that believeth on him shall not be confounded.”

1 Peter 2:6

“For other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ.”

1 Corinthians 3:11

My church, and the keys

This is the first time the word church appears in the Bible. It means an assembly of people called out: not a building, but the people who belong to Jesus. “My” church: He builds it, and it's His. “The gates of hell” are the gates of death and the grave, and they can't hold it in or keep it down.

  1. PentecostJews hear, about 3,000 savedActs 2
  2. SamariaSamaritans receive the SpiritActs 8
  3. CaesareaGentiles believeActs 10

Keys open doors. In the book of Acts, Peter is the one who preaches as the door swings open, first to Jews, then to Samaritans, then to Gentiles. The key was the gospel itself.

“Bind” and “loose” were teachers' words for forbid and allow. The apostles would declare on earth what heaven had already settled, and Jesus gives the same words to all of them in 18:18. No man forgives sins on his own say-so. Only God does that, through Jesus.

Part three

The road to the cross

Verses 21–28

The Christ must suffer, and the men who follow Him will carry a cross too.

From that time forth

Matthew uses these words twice. “From that time Jesus began to preach” (4:17). And now, “from that time forth” He began to show them the cross.

From here on, every road leads to Jerusalem. The elders, chief priests and scribes made up the Jewish ruling council. The men who should have welcomed Him would kill Him.

He told them the end of the story too: “be raised again the third day.” They only heard the first half.

Same man, same afternoon

Verse 17

“Blessed art thou”

Listening to the Father, Peter says the truest thing a man can say about Jesus.

Verse 23

“Get thee behind me, Satan”

Listening to his own fears, Peter tries to talk Jesus out of the cross.

Peter even “took” Jesus aside to correct Him. But a Christ without a cross is exactly what Satan had offered in the wilderness: the kingdoms of the world, no suffering required (4:8–10). “Savourest” means set your mind on. Peter was thinking like a man, not like God.

One good day doesn't make any of us bulletproof. But one bad moment didn't finish Peter either. Jesus corrected him and kept him. He does the same with us.

Three things He asks

deny himselfsay no to self running the show
take up his crossaccept whatever it costs to belong to Jesus
follow mego where He goes, one step at a time

To deny yourself isn't just giving up a few things. It's giving up the throne. And this is not how a man gets saved. Salvation is a free gift. This is how a saved man follows.

“And he said to them all, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me.”

Luke 9:23

Luke adds one word: daily. Not once for all, but every morning. One day at a time.

Do the math

Put everything this world has on one side: money, pleasure, fame, every high a man ever chased. Put one soul on the other.

It isn't close. The world doesn't last, and you can't take any of it with you. Your soul lasts forever.

Plenty of us traded a lot for very little: a family for a bottle, years for a few good nights. But the trade isn't final while you're still breathing. Jesus already paid the price for your soul.

He's coming back

Do our works save us?No“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast” (Ephesians 2:8–9).
Do our works matter?Yes“If any man’s work abide which he hath built thereupon, he shall receive a reward” (1 Corinthians 3:14). A saved man's life counts for something.

Verse 28 is a hard one. Many Bible teachers see it come true six days later, in the very next verses: three of these men saw Jesus in His kingdom glory on the mountain. Peter wrote that they were “eyewitnesses of his majesty” (2 Peter 1:16). Read on, and see what they saw.

Points to remember

  1. Signs“There shall no sign be given unto it, but the sign of the prophet Jonas” 16:4The empty tomb is God's sign. More proof won't move a heart that's made up.
  2. Leaven“Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees” 16:6Hypocrisy and unbelief spread quietly. Watch what you let in.
  3. The Christ“Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God” 16:16Every man has to answer for himself who Jesus is.
  4. The church“The gates of hell shall not prevail against it” 16:18Jesus builds His church on Himself, and nothing can stop it.
  5. The cross“Let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me” 16:24Give up the throne, every day, and follow Him.
  6. The soul“What is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?” 16:26Your soul outweighs the whole world.

Question 1 of 6

Signs

Why wouldn't Jesus give the Pharisees the sign they asked for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They weren't looking for truth. They were testing Him, trying to trap Him. He had already done miracle after miracle, and they refused to see it. So He pointed them to one sign: like Jonah, He would come back from the grave on the third day.

Question 2 of 6

Leaven

What is the leaven we need to beware of?

What verses tell us about this?

“In the mean time, when there were gathered together an innumerable multitude of people, insomuch that they trode one upon another, he began to say unto his disciples first of all, Beware ye of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy.”

Luke 12:1

Answer

False teaching, like the hypocrisy of the Pharisees and the unbelief of the Sadducees. It works like yeast: small, quiet, and it spreads until it's through everything. Watch what you take in and who you listen to.

Question 3 of 6

The Christ

Who do you say Jesus is?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That's the question every man answers for himself. Not just a prophet or a good teacher, but the Christ, the Son of the living God. God opens our eyes to see it, and whoever believes on Him is saved.

Question 4 of 6

The church

What did Jesus promise about His church?

What verses tell us about this?

“For other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ.”

1 Corinthians 3:11

Answer

That He would build it Himself, on the rock of who He is, and that the gates of hell would never overpower it. It's His church, made of people who confess Him as the Christ. Death itself can't stop it.

Question 5 of 6

The cross

What does it mean to deny yourself and take up your cross?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said to them all, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me.”

Luke 9:23

Answer

To step off the throne of your own life, accept whatever it costs to belong to Jesus, and follow Him, every day. It isn't how we're saved; it's how saved men live. The life we grab for ourselves, we lose. The life we hand to Him, we find.

Question 6 of 6

The soul

What is a man's soul worth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

More than the whole world. Everything a man could gain here ends, and his soul goes on forever. Nothing he owns could buy it back. That's why Jesus gave Himself to pay for it.
