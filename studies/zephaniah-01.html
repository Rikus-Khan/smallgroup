Zephaniah 1

The great day of the LORD

A prophet with a king's blood in his veins tells Jerusalem the truth: the day is near, and nobody can buy his way out of it.

The book of Zephaniah

Who: Zephaniah, great-great-grandson of Hizkiah, most likely good King Hezekiah. A prophet with royal blood.

When: under King Josiah, about 640–609 BC, most likely before his clean-up of the idols or just as it began. Before the exile, with Assyria fading and Babylon rising.

To whom: Judah and Jerusalem, the southern kingdom. Israel in the north had been carried off a hundred years before.

Part one

Sweeping the land

Verses 1–6

God names what's been hiding in Jerusalem, on the rooftops and in the temple.

Four generations back

Hizkiahfather ofAmariahfather ofGedaliahfather ofCushifather ofZephaniah

Prophets usually name a father, if that. Zephaniah goes back four generations, most likely because the man at the top is King Hezekiah (Hizkiah is the same name). Zephaniah was a distant cousin of the king he preached under.

And look at that king's family. Josiah's grandfather Manasseh filled Judah with idols for fifty-five years. His father Amon did the same and was murdered after two. Then an eight-year-old boy took the throne, and this happened:

“For in the eighth year of his reign, while he was yet young, he began to seek after the God of David his father: and in the twelfth year he began to purge Judah and Jerusalem from the high places, and the groves, and the carved images, and the molten images.”

2 Chronicles 34:3

At sixteen, from the worst family line in Judah, Josiah went looking for God. Your father's road doesn't have to be yours.

Creation running backward

  1. Manmade on the sixth day
  2. Beastmade on the sixth day
  3. Fowlsmade on the fifth day
  4. Fishesmade on the fifth day

Notice the order. In Genesis 1, God made the fish and birds, then the beasts, then man. Zephaniah lists them in reverse, as if the creation were being rolled back up. It sounds like the flood:

“And the LORD said, I will destroy man whom I have created from the face of the earth; both man, and beast, and the creeping thing, and the fowls of the air; for it repenteth me that I have made them.”

Genesis 6:7

The stumblingblocks are the idols, the things that trip men up. God will clear them away along with the men who keep them. He starts wide, with the whole earth, and then in verse 4 He narrows it down to one city.

Some of these are open idolaters. Some look religious. Some just drifted. God's list covers all of them.

Josiah cleans house

Zephaniah named them. A few years later, after the book of the law was found in the temple, King Josiah went after every one:

  1. ✓the remnant of BaalZephaniah 1:42 Kings 23:4–5
  2. ✓the name of the ChemarimsZephaniah 1:4“the idolatrous priests,” 2 Kings 23:5
  3. ✓the host of heaven upon the housetopsZephaniah 1:52 Kings 23:5, 12

“And he put down the idolatrous priests, whom the kings of Judah had ordained to burn incense in the high places in the cities of Judah, and in the places round about Jerusalem; them also that burned incense unto Baal, to the sun, and to the moon, and to the planets, and to all the host of heaven.”

2 Kings 23:5

“Idolatrous priests” is the same Hebrew word as Chemarims. The idols came down, but most hearts didn't change. After Josiah died, Judah went right back.

One foot in each camp

On the one hand

“By the LORD”

They came to the temple. They used God's name when they made a promise. They looked like believers.

On the other

“By Malcham”

The name means “their king.” Most likely Milcom (Molech), the god of the Ammonites, whose worship could include burning children.

“And Elijah came unto all the people, and said, How long halt ye between two opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him. And the people answered him not a word.”

1 Kings 18:21

Most of us tried this. Church on Sunday, the old life on Saturday. A little God, and a little of whatever we still wanted. It doesn't work. God isn't one option on the shelf.

How a man drifts

  1. Both waysthe LORD and Malcham · v. 5
  2. Turned backquit following · v. 6
  3. Not even lookingnever seek, never ask · v. 6

Nobody wakes up and decides to walk away from God. It goes step by step: one compromise, then skipping the meeting, then not praying, then not caring. That's how a relapse works too.

But the road back is just as plain. You turn around and start seeking Him again:

“And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart.”

Jeremiah 29:13

Part two

Hush

Verses 7–13

The guests are invited, the city is the sacrifice, and God goes searching with a lamp.

Hush.

That's what “hold thy peace” means: be quiet. Stop arguing, stop explaining. The Judge has walked in.

Then a shock. God has “prepared a sacrifice” and invited guests to the feast. In Judah, a sacrifice meant a temple meal. But this time Judah isn't the guest. Judah is the sacrifice. The guests are the armies God would call in to judge her.

“But the LORD is in his holy temple: let all the earth keep silence before him.”

Habakkuk 2:20

There's a time to pour out your heart to God, and there's a time just to shut up and listen. When God has said a hard thing, the first right answer is silence.

Starting at the top

  1. The princes and the king's sonsthe men who ran the country
  2. “Strange apparel”foreign fashion, and the foreign gods that came with it
  3. “Leap on the threshold”probably a pagan habit of jumping over a doorstep
  4. Violence and deceitservants who robbed others to fill the boss's house

Judgment begins with the leaders, and Zephaniah was related to them. Dressing like the nations meant more than clothes: they wanted to look like Assyria and live like Assyria. The doorstep custom may be the one the priests of the Philistine god Dagon kept (1 Samuel 5:5).

Then the men who worked for them: they grabbed whatever they could by force or by fraud, and called it doing their job. God doesn't accept “I was just following orders.”

A cry across the city

Steep valleys guarded three sides of Jerusalem, so invaders came from the north. The fish gate was in the north wall, where fish came in to market.

Then the cry spreads to “the second”: the newer quarter on the western hill (where Huldah the prophetess lived, 2 Kings 22:14).

Then down to Maktesh, “the mortar,” probably the market hollow in the middle of the city. The merchants are wiped out.

Babylon broke in from the north about forty years later.

Settled on the dregs

Lees are the thick mud that sinks to the bottom of a wine jar. A winemaker poured new wine from jar to jar to keep it clean. Left alone, it thickened, went syrupy, and kept its bad taste. Jeremiah used the same picture:

“Moab hath been at ease from his youth, and he hath settled on his lees, and hath not been emptied from vessel to vessel, neither hath he gone into captivity: therefore his taste remained in him, and his scent is not changed.”

Jeremiah 48:11

It's a winemaker's picture, not a drinking one. It means a man who's gotten comfortable, never stirred, never changed. Nothing bad has happened yet, so he figures nothing will.

“He won't do anything”

The men at ease, in their heartThe LORD will not do good, neither will he do evil.

The LORDI will search Jerusalem with candles.

They didn't deny God. They just figured He wasn't going to do anything, good or bad. God answers by lighting a lamp and going through the dark corners, house by house.

Comfort is where a man in recovery gets careless. The good stretch, when the meetings feel optional and the prayers get short. That's when to stay awake.

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

God keeps His word

Deuteronomy 28:30, about 800 years earlier

…thou shalt build an house, and thou shalt not dwell therein: thou shalt plant a vineyard, and shalt not gather the grapes thereof.

Zephaniah 1:13

…they shall also build houses, but not inhabit them; and they shall plant vineyards, but not drink the wine thereof.

The men of verse 12 said God wouldn't do anything. But Moses had warned Israel what would happen if they turned to other gods, and God does what He said. That cuts both ways: His warnings are sure, and so are His promises.

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Part three

That day

Verses 14–18

Near, and coming fast. And nothing in a man's wallet can stop it.

Day, day, day

1 2 3 4 5 6  7day 8day 9day 10day 11 12 13  14×2 15×6 16day 17  18day

Fourteen times in one chapter, and six of them in a single verse. Zephaniah hammers it like a bell: wrath, trouble, distress, wasteness, desolation, darkness, gloominess, clouds, thick darkness.

“The day of the LORD” is the time when God steps in and settles accounts. The trumpet was the ram's horn that warned a city of attack, and no wall or tower would hold.

Near, and still coming

  1. about 630 BC“it is near”
  2. 605Babylon's first strike
  3. 597the king carried off
  4. 586Jerusalem burned
  5. still ahead“the day of the Lord”2 Peter 3:10

Zephaniah meant it: within about forty years, Babylon came. Men who heard him preach lived to see it.

But the New Testament says there is a day of the Lord still to come for the whole world, and calls it by the same name. It will come suddenly, and it will surely come.

“For yourselves know perfectly that the day of the Lord so cometh as a thief in the night.”

1 Thessalonians 5:2

God's jealousy is not pettiness. It's the fierce love of a husband who won't share his wife. The fire burns because He cares.

Is there anywhere to hide?

Chapter 1 ends in fire, with no way out. But Zephaniah isn't finished. In the very next breath he says, “Seek ye the LORD … it may be ye shall be hid in the day of the LORD’S anger” (2:3).

Points to remember

  1. The day of the LORD“The great day of the LORD is near” 1:14God will step in and settle accounts. It came on Jerusalem, and it's still coming.
  2. A divided heart“Swear by the LORD, and … by Malcham” 1:5God isn't one option among many. He won't share a man's heart with an idol.
  3. Drifting“Them that are turned back from the LORD” 1:6Drifting happens one step at a time. So does coming back.
  4. Complacency“Settled on their lees” 1:12Comfortable isn't safe. God searches the corners with a lamp.

Question 1 of 6

The day of the LORD

What is “the day of the LORD”?

What verses tell us about this?

“For yourselves know perfectly that the day of the Lord so cometh as a thief in the night.”

1 Thessalonians 5:2

Answer

It's the time when God stops waiting and steps in to judge. For Judah it came about forty years later, when Babylon burned Jerusalem. The New Testament says a final day of the Lord is still coming on the whole world, as suddenly as a thief. It's real, and it's not a reason to panic. It's a reason to be ready.

Question 2 of 6

A divided heart

What's wrong with swearing by the LORD and by Malcham too?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Elijah came unto all the people, and said, How long halt ye between two opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him. And the people answered him not a word.”

1 Kings 18:21

“No man can serve two masters: for either he will hate the one, and love the other; or else he will hold to the one, and despise the other. Ye cannot serve God and mammon.”

Matthew 6:24

Answer

It treats God as one god among several. These men kept the LORD's name in their mouths and kept an idol on the side, just in case. God doesn't take a share. Jesus said no man can serve two masters. Whatever we're still holding on the side, a habit, a drug, a relationship, God wants us to let go of it and follow Him all the way.

Question 3 of 6

Drifting

What does it look like to turn back from the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

“Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near:”

Isaiah 55:6

“And ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart.”

Jeremiah 29:13

Answer

It looks like quitting the seeking. First a man splits his loyalty, then he stops following, then he stops even asking. It happens slowly, the way a relapse does. The way back is the same road in the other direction: start seeking Him again, with your whole heart. God promises to be found.

Question 4 of 6

Complacency

What does it mean to be “settled on their lees”?

What verses tell us about this?

“But without faith it is impossible to please him: for he that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him.”

Hebrews 11:6

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

Answer

Like wine left sitting on its dregs, they'd gone thick and stale. Life was comfortable, nothing had shaken them, and they decided God wouldn't do anything, good or bad. That's the danger of a good stretch: we stop praying, stop reaching out, and think we've got it. God does act. He rewards the men who seek Him and searches out the ones who've gone to sleep.

Question 5 of 6

Money

Why can't silver and gold save a man in that day?

What verses tell us about this?

“Riches profit not in the day of wrath: but righteousness delivereth from death.”

Proverbs 11:4

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Answer

Because the problem isn't money. It's sin, and God can't be bribed. The rich men of Jerusalem found their silver was worth nothing when Babylon came. The price of a man's soul was paid with something silver can't match: the blood of Christ. That's free to us, and it's the only thing that redeems.

Question 6 of 6

Refuge

Where can a man hide from the day of wrath?

What verses tell us about this?

“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”

Psalm 46:1

“Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him.”

Romans 5:9

“For God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ,”

1 Thessalonians 5:9

Answer

Not behind walls, and not behind money. Only in God Himself. Zephaniah will say it in the next chapter: “Seek ye the LORD.” Jesus took God's wrath against sin on the cross, so whoever trusts in Him is saved from wrath through Him. A believer doesn't face that day alone or afraid. He's hidden in Christ.
