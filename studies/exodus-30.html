Exodus 30

The same ransom for every man

The altar of incense, where prayer goes up like sweet smoke. Half a shekel from every man, rich and poor alike, as a ransom for his soul. A basin to wash in, so that the priests “die not.” And the holy oil and incense that no one is to copy.

Part one

“A perpetual incense before the LORD”

Verses 1–10

A small golden altar in front of the vail, and a smoke that goes up morning and evening.

A little golden altar

  • incensesweet-smelling gum and spice, burned so the smoke rises
  • foursquaresquare on every side: a cubit each way
  • cubitabout 18 inches, so a table 18 inches wide and 3 feet high
  • hornsfour corner points, made of the same piece
  • stavespoles, slid through rings so it is carried, not touched

Shittim wood covered in pure gold, with a golden crown round the top. It is small, and it is made of the best.

Right in front of the vail

The vail is the heavy curtain that hides the ark and the mercy seat. The altar of incense stands as close to it as a man can get. Verse 6 says why: it is “where I will meet with thee.” Perpetual here means regular and never skipped: every morning at the lamps, and again “at even,” day after day.

  1. every morninghe dresseth the lampsand burns incense
  2. every daymorning and eveningbefore the LORD
  3. at evenhe lighteth the lampsand burns incense

Dresseth means trims and tends: he sees to the lamps. The priest comes at the start of the day and at the close of it, every day, and the group you are sitting in meets every day too. A day opened and closed before God is an old and good habit.

The smoke is prayer

“Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense; and the lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice.”

Psalm 141:2

“And it came to pass, that while he executed the priest’s office before God in the order of his course, According to the custom of the priest’s office, his lot was to burn incense when he went into the temple of the Lord. And the whole multitude of the people were praying without at the time of incense.”

Luke 1:8–10

“And another angel came and stood at the altar, having a golden censer; and there was given unto him much incense, that he should offer it with the prayers of all saints upon the golden altar which was before the throne. And the smoke of the incense, which came with the prayers of the saints, ascended up before God out of the angel’s hand.”

Revelation 8:3–4

David asked for his prayer to be like incense. Centuries later the people stood outside praying “at the time of incense,” and in heaven the prayers of the saints go up with the smoke before the throne. A prayer from a man like you is not noise to God. It is a sweet smell.

What does not go on this altar

  • no strange incensenot a mix of a man's own making
  • no burnt sacrificethat belongs on the other altar, in the court
  • no meat offering“meat” here means a grain offering (food), not flesh
  • no drink offeringno wine poured out on it
  • but blood, once a yearon its horns, “atonements”: the cover for sin

Atonement is a covering over sin so that a man can stand before God. Once a year the high priest put the blood of the sin offering on these four horns (the day is told in Leviticus 16). Even the altar where prayer rises needs sin covered.

“Strange” means not what he said

Remember Nadab and Abihu from chapter 24, who went up the mount and ate in God's presence. Here is what happened to them later:

“And Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, took either of them his censer, and put fire therein, and put incense thereon, and offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not. And there went out fire from the LORD, and devoured them, and they died before the LORD.”

Leviticus 10:1–2

Plain and sobering. Two men who had seen God and sat at his table brought their own fire to the altar, which he “commanded them not.” The lesson is not that God is touchy. It is that he had told them the way, and they chose their own.

A man in recovery knows what it is to decide, “I'll do it my way, just this once.” The way in to God is the one he gave. Prayer needs no performance and no ritual of your own: it needs the blood that covers, and a man who comes as he is.

Part two

“The rich shall not give more”

Verses 11–16

Every man is counted, and every man pays the same: a ransom for his soul.

Counted, and ransomed

  • the sumthe total, the head-count
  • ransomthe price paid to buy a life back
  • passeth among thempasses by, in line, to be counted
  • shekel of the sanctuarythe weight kept at the tabernacle as the standard, so nobody could cheat
  • gerahsthe smallest weight: twenty of them make a shekel

A half shekel is about a fifth of an ounce of silver, a small coin. Count the men, and every man pays a ransom “for his soul.” The reason is given: “that there be no plague among them.” A number of men standing before a holy God is serious, and no man is there on his own merit.

“The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less.” Not a tithe, not a sliding scale: the same, from every man twenty years old and up. The rich man could not buy a better standing with God, and the poor man was not given a worse one. Memorial means a reminder: the silver stood before the LORD as a reminder that this people had been bought back.

Every man in this room has a ledger he cannot pay. No man here owes more than another, and none owes less than the price. The price is the same for the man who has lost the most and the man who thinks he has lost nothing.

The same price, paid in blood

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

“Even as the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.”

Matthew 20:28

“What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own? For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God’s.”

1 Corinthians 6:19–20

Peter says the price was never silver or gold. It was “the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot.” One price, for the banker and for the man who slept in his car. You cannot pay more to be safer, and your past cannot make the price higher. It was paid for you in full, and you are bought.

Where the silver went

“And of the hundred talents of silver were cast the sockets of the sanctuary, and the sockets of the vail; an hundred sockets of the hundred talents, a talent for a socket.”

Exodus 38:27
603,550men counted
½ shekelfrom each

We will read it in chapter 38: the ransom silver was cast into the sockets, the silver feet the tabernacle's frames stood in, and the pillars of the vail. A talent is a large weight of silver, and each socket was cast from one. Every man's half shekel became part of the foundation of the place where God lived among them. Your life, bought back, is built into something bigger than you.

Real world: you are counted

Some of you feel like the one who owes more than the rest. Some of you feel like you've done well compared with the others in the room. Exodus 30 puts both of you at the same line.

  • You are not too muchYour worst year did not raise the price. It was already paid in full.
  • You are not enough on your ownYour best year did not lower it either. Nobody here is settled by comparing himself with the next man.
  • Say it to a brother“It's paid.” When the old guilt comes, say it out loud to a brother, and say it again tomorrow.

If you fall tomorrow, the ransom does not go back to unpaid. Call a brother in Christ, tell him, and stand up.

Part three

“That they die not”

Verses 17–21

A bronze basin between the altar and the tent, for hands and feet.

Altar, then basin, then tent

  • lavera basin on a stand, to wash in
  • of brassbronze
  • statutea law that stands
  • the congregationthe whole assembly of Israel

The order is the lesson. First the altar, where blood is shed. Then the basin. Then the tent. The priests wash hands and feet, the hands that did the work and the feet that walked the court, “that they die not.” A man does not walk straight into God's presence with dirt on him.

Bathed once, and the feet washed daily

“Peter saith unto him, Thou shalt never wash my feet. Jesus answered him, If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me. Simon Peter saith unto him, Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and my head. Jesus saith to him, He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit: and ye are clean, but not all.”

John 13:8–10

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.”

Hebrews 10:22

At the table Jesus washed his disciples' feet and said the man who has been bathed needs only his feet washed. A believer is clean in Christ, and still his hands and his feet pick up the road. The answer is to confess it and be cleansed, not to be afraid of being thrown out.

Real world: wash up every day

Hands

What I did

The lie, the drink, the temper, the thing I took. Name it. Don't hide it.

Feet

Where I walked

The place I should not have gone, and the people who pulled me there. Bring it in the same day.

The priests washed at the basin every time they went in. There was no day when they had been clean enough already. Make it a habit, not a crisis: tell a brother the same day, confess it to God, and go on. “He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us.”

Part four

The holy oil, and the holy perfume

Verses 22–38

Two recipes, set apart for God, and a warning not to make copies.

The recipe

500shekels of myrrh
250of sweet cinnamon
250of sweet calamus
500of cassia

About 1,500 shekels of spice, a good thirty-odd pounds of it, in a hin of olive oil. A hin is about a gallon.

  • myrrha fragrant tree gum
  • calamusa sweet reed, aromatic
  • cassiaa spice from bark, like cinnamon
  • apothecarya maker of perfumes and medicines: a skilled craftsman
  • ointment compounda blend, mixed by a craftsman

Set apart by the oil

The oil went on the tent, the ark, the table, the candlestick, the two altars, the basin: everything. To anoint something was to say, this belongs to God, and it is for his use only.

משיח said mashiach anointed one, in Greek Christos: Messiah, Christ

The names “Messiah” and “Christ” both mean the Anointed One. Priests were anointed here, and later kings and prophets. Jesus is the One all of them point to.

Not poured on man's flesh

Aaron and his sons are anointed to “minister unto me in the priest's office,” which means to serve. But the oil was never to be poured “upon man's flesh,” meaning on any ordinary man, nor copied.

  • consecrateset apart for God's work
  • compositionthe recipe: the exact mix
  • cut off from his peopleremoved from the community of Israel: a serious penalty, stated plainly, for abusing what was holy

A man cannot make himself holy and cannot manufacture holiness. It is given, and it is set apart by God. The Hebrew idea is simple: this one thing is for his use, and it is not a product.

The anointing we have been given

“But ye have an unction from the Holy One, and ye know all things.”

1 John 2:20

“But the anointing which ye have received of him abideth in you, and ye need not that any man teach you: but as the same anointing teacheth you of all things, and is truth, and is no lie, and even as it hath taught you, ye shall abide in him.”

1 John 2:27

“Now he which stablisheth us with you in Christ, and hath anointed us, is God; Who hath also sealed us, and given the earnest of the Spirit in our hearts.”

2 Corinthians 1:21–22

Unction is another word for anointing. John says every believer has it from the Holy One, and Paul ties it to God sealing us and giving the Spirit in our hearts. In Exodus the oil came from an apothecary and went on a few men. Now God himself anoints, and the Spirit lives in every man who trusts Jesus. Nobody can buy it or make it, and nobody who believes is left out.

The incense

  • stactea drop-like resin gum from a tree
  • onychaa spice burned for its sweet smell
  • galbanuma strong-smelling resin
  • frankincensea white resin, sweet when burned
  • perfumea sweet-smelling mixture
  • tempered togetherblended evenly
  • confectiona mixture, mixed by a skilled hand

Four spices, “of each shall there be a like weight,” blended evenly. A little was beaten “very small,” ground fine, and put before the testimony, “where I will meet with thee.” The smoke of it fills the room where God meets his people, and verse 36 says: “it shall be unto you most holy.”

Not for your own use

The perfume of the tabernacle was God's. Nobody was to make a copy “to smell thereto,” to enjoy for himself. The chapter that began with a prayer-smoke altar ends the same way: what belongs to God is set apart for him.

A copy

“Like it, for me”

The same sweetness without the same God: a pleasant smell, with nobody at the door.

The real thing

Set apart for him

Made to his pattern, used where he meets us, and given to him first.

Men in recovery know all about the copy: the thing that promises the comfort God gives, and that has no one behind it. Take the comfort God gives, in his way, in his room.

Real world: the day is for him

  • Open with prayerThe morning incense: before the phone, before the job, a minute with him.
  • Close with prayerThe evening incense: tell him how it went, the good and the bad.
  • Wash dailyConfess what your hands and feet did. Don't carry it to bed.
  • You are countedThe ransom is paid, the same for every man. You belong to him.

One thing today: when you wake, and before you sleep, pray in your own words for a minute. It is a sweet smell to him.

Question 1 of 6

The incense altar

Where did the altar of incense stand, and when was incense burned on it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Right in front of the vail, as near the ark as a man could come, “where I will meet with thee.” Incense was burned every morning when the lamps were trimmed, and again at evening, a perpetual incense. Prayer, morning and evening, goes up to him like sweet smoke.

Question 2 of 6

Strange incense

What was never to be offered on that altar, and what was done on its horns once a year?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No strange incense, no burnt sacrifice, no meat offering and no drink offering. Once a year Aaron put the blood of the sin offering on its horns and made atonement. Come to God the way he gave, and know that even prayer rests on the blood.

Question 3 of 6

The ransom

What did every man give when he was numbered, and how much more did the rich give?

What verses tell us about this?

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Answer

A ransom for his soul, half a shekel, and the rich gave no more and the poor no less. It is the same price for every man, and the price that bought you was the blood of Christ, paid in full.

Question 4 of 6

The basin

Why did Aaron and his sons wash their hands and their feet?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

So that they would not die when they went into the tent or came near the altar to serve. A man does not walk into God's presence with dirt on him, and a believer washes daily by confessing, and being cleansed.

Question 5 of 6

The oil

What was the holy anointing oil for, and what was it never to be put on?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To set apart the tent, its furniture, and Aaron and his sons as holy to the LORD. It was never to be poured on man's flesh, and never copied. Now the anointing is the Spirit of God, given to every believer.

Question 6 of 6

The perfume

What was to be burned before the testimony, and what was not to be copied?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A perfume of four spices, with pure frankincense, burned before the testimony where God meets his people. It was not to be copied for a man's own use: what is God's stays set apart for him.
