For four chapters Paul has argued one thing: a man is made right with God by faith in Christ, not by keeping the law. Chapter 4 ended, “So then, brethren, we are not children of the bondwoman, but of the free.”

Now comes the “therefore.” If you're free, how does a free man live? Not back in chains, and not running wild. Chapter 5 shows a third way: walking with the Spirit.

Part one

Stand fast

Verses 1–12

Don't put the chains back on.

Don't put it back on

A yoke was the wooden bar laid across an ox's neck so it could be made to pull. These teachers wanted to lay the law of Moses across Gentile necks. Peter had called it “a yoke … which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear” (Acts 15:10).

“Entangled” means caught again, like an animal in a snare. Christ broke the yoke. Don't walk back and put your neck under it.

“Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.”

Matthew 11:29–30

Fallen from what?

  • “He lost his salvation”no man can pluck you from His hand
  • “He fell into a big sin”that's not Paul's subject here
  • He stepped off grace onto law-keepingas his way to be right with God

Add one law to Christ to be saved, and you owe God the whole law (v. 3), and Christ “shall profit you nothing” as your way to be right with God. You've left the high ground for a treadmill. Every believer is kept by God, but you can live like a debtor.

What counts

  • Circumcised“availeth” nothing: it gets you nowhere with God
  • Uncircumcisedthat gets you nowhere either
  • “faith which worketh by love”the only thing that counts

Paul isn't against working. He's against working to get saved. Real faith in Christ goes to work, and the engine it runs on is love. Faith that never does a thing for anybody isn't the faith Paul means.

For us: going to church, staying clean, a year chip. All good things. None of them is what saves you. Trust Christ, then let love put that trust to work today.

A little leaven

“Ye did run well.” They started strong. “Hinder” is a runner's word: someone cut in on them and knocked them off their stride.

Leaven is yeast. A pinch works through the whole batch of dough. One small idea, “Jesus, plus this,” works its way through a whole church. One small compromise works its way through a whole life.

Paul is sure of them anyway: “I have confidence in you through the Lord.” His trust isn't in them. It's in the Lord who is working in them.

The cross offends because it says you bring nothing.

“Offence” means a stumbling stone. Preach “Jesus, plus circumcision,” and nobody is bothered: man still gets some credit. Preach the cross alone, and proud men trip over it. That's why Paul was still being persecuted.

Verse 12 is Paul at his bluntest. These men were eager to take a knife to other men's flesh. Many Bible teachers hear a hard, sarcastic wish: if they love the knife so much, let them go the whole way and cut themselves off.

Strong words, because these men were putting freed men back in chains. Paul loves the Galatians too much to be polite about it.

Part two

Walk in the Spirit

Verses 13–18

Free from the law, but not free to feed the flesh.

Free for what?

The wrong way · v. 13

“an occasion to the flesh”

An “occasion” was a base camp, a place to launch an attack from. “I'm under grace, so it doesn't matter.” That turns freedom into a launch pad for the old life.

The right way · vv. 13–14

“by love serve one another”

“Serve” is the word for a slave's work. Christ set you free from the law, and now you choose to serve people out of love. That keeps the whole law (v. 14).

Verse 15 is what happens in a church, or a house, or a recovery group, that forgets this: “bite and devour one another,” like dogs in a pit, until nobody is left standing.

If youwalk in the Spirit,
→
thenye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh.

Read it slowly. It doesn't say “fight the flesh harder and you'll walk in the Spirit.” It's the other way round. You don't beat the old cravings by staring them down on willpower. You walk with Someone, step by step, all day, and the turn-offs fall behind you. The pull may still be there; you just don't “fulfil” it.

The war inside

If you've felt torn in two since you got saved, that's not a sign you aren't saved. It's a sign you are. Before Christ there was no fight; the flesh just won. Now the Spirit lives in you, and He pulls the other way.

Which side wins? The one you feed. Paul says, “make not provision for the flesh” (Romans 13:14), so starve it of the old numbers, the apps, the places. Feed the Spirit: the Word, prayer, church, an honest friend.

Part three

Works and fruit

Verses 19–26

What the flesh makes, and what the Spirit grows.

“Manifest” means plain to see. Nobody needs a Bible scholar to spot these. Most of us have lived some of this list. Notice that quarreling and jealousy sit right beside murder and drunkenness: the flesh isn't only the dramatic sins.

“They which do such things”

What Paul describes

A life ruled by them

“Do” here means to practice, to make a way of life of it. A man who lives this way, untroubled, and calls himself saved, should ask himself honestly whether he has ever been born again.

What Paul does not describe

A believer who stumbled

Paul calls that man “overtaken in a fault” (6:1), and tells the church to restore him. A child of God who falls is still a child of God, and he hates it when he falls.

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Read the list as a picture of where you came from, not where you must stay. Paul says “such were some of you.” Were.

Fruit grows

The flesh has “works,” plural: things a man makes. The Spirit has “fruit,” singular: one crop with nine flavors, and it grows.

Nobody grits his teeth and squeezes out a grape. A branch just stays joined to the vine, and fruit comes in its season. You can't manufacture love, joy and peace. Stay close to Christ, and He grows them in you.

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.”

John 15:5

Nine flavors of one fruit

  1. loveseeking another's good, at your own cost
  2. joygladness in God that hard days can't take
  3. peaceright with God, and quiet inside
  4. longsufferinga long fuse with people
  5. gentlenesskindness
  6. goodnessdoing right by people, on purpose
  7. faithfaithfulness: a man whose word is good
  8. meeknessstrength kept under control
  9. temperanceself-control: the appetites ruled, not ruling

The last one matters to every man here. “Temperance” isn't a white-knuckle program. It's fruit: the Spirit giving you the mastery that whiskey, pills and porn took from you. “Against such there is no law”: no law ever had to be written against too much love or too much self-control.

The old man was nailed down

“Have crucified”: it already happened. When you trusted Christ, the old you, with his “affections and lusts,” was counted as dying with Him on the cross. He still makes noise. But he's not your master anymore, and you don't owe him anything.

“I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me.”

Galatians 2:20

“Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin.”

Romans 6:6

So when the old man shouts, answer him: “You died. I'm not taking orders from you today.”

Keep in step

This “walk” is a different word from verse 16. It means to walk in line, to keep in step, like a soldier marching behind the man in front. The Spirit already gave you life. Now match His pace: one step, then the next one.

Verse 26 is the warning for a group like ours: no showing off who's been clean longer, no needling each other, no envying the man who seems to have it easier. Fruit doesn't compete.

Points to remember

  1. Liberty“Be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage” 5:1Christ set you free from the law as a way to earn God, and from sin as your master. Don't go back.
  2. Fallen from grace“Ye are fallen from grace” 5:4Not lost salvation: stepping off grace and back onto law-keeping as the way to be right with God.
  3. Love“By love serve one another” 5:13Freedom isn't a launch pad for the flesh. It's the freedom to serve people out of love.
  4. The Spirit“Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh” 5:16Not by willpower. Walk with the Spirit, step by step, and feed the side you want to win.
  5. The works of the flesh“The works of the flesh are manifest” 5:19The list is plain and honest. But “such were some of you”: washed, justified, and not left there.
  6. The fruit“But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace” 5:22Fruit grows. Stay joined to the Vine, and the Spirit grows it in you.

Question 1 of 6

Liberty

What has Christ set a believer free from?

What verses tell us about this?

“For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.”

Matthew 11:30

Answer

From the yoke of the law as a way to earn God's favor, and from the flesh as our master. Christ did what we never could. We stand in that freedom, and we don't walk back into the chains.

Question 2 of 6

Fallen from grace

What does Paul mean by “ye are fallen from grace”?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Answer

The man who tries to be justified by the law has stepped off grace as his way to be right with God and put himself back on the treadmill of law-keeping. It doesn't mean a believer loses his salvation; Jesus holds His own. It means living like a debtor when you've been set free.

Question 3 of 6

Love

What should a free man do with his freedom?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not use it as an excuse for the flesh. He uses it to serve others in love. Real faith goes to work, and love is how it works. Loving your neighbor as yourself keeps the whole law.

Question 4 of 6

The Spirit

How does a believer win against the pull of the flesh?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not by willpower alone. The war is real, but the Spirit lives in every believer. We walk with Him step by step: in the Word, in prayer, with other believers. We starve the flesh and feed the Spirit, and we don't carry out what the flesh wants.

Question 5 of 6

The works of the flesh

Is there hope for a man who has lived the works of the flesh?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

Yes. A life still ruled by these sins is not a saved life. But the gospel is for exactly these men: in Christ the old man was crucified, and “such were some of you” becomes your story. Washed, justified, and not left there.

Question 6 of 6

The fruit

Where does the fruit of the Spirit come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.”

John 15:5

Answer

From the Spirit, not from us. It's fruit, not a work: it grows in a man who stays close to Christ. Love, joy, peace, and all the rest, right down to temperance, the self-control that addiction stole.
