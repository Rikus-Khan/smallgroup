Romans 6

Dead to sin, alive unto God

If grace covers everything, why not keep sinning? Paul's answer is the heart of the free life: you died with Christ, you rose with Him, and you have a new Master.

Part one

God forbid

Verses 1–10

Grace isn't a license. A man who has died with Christ doesn't go back to live in the grave.

“Shall we continue in sin?”

SomeoneIf grace is bigger than all my sin, and sinning makes more room for grace, why stop?

PaulGod forbid. How shall we, that are dead to sin, live any longer therein? not a chance

God forbid is the strongest “No!” Paul has. A man who wants grace so he can keep sinning hasn't understood grace at all.

But notice who this verse is not about: the man who fell yesterday and hates it. That hatred is a sign grace has hold of him. Dead to sin doesn't mean sin stops calling. It means sin doesn't own you any more.

Baptism tells the story

The New Testament believers were baptized by going down into the water and coming back up (Acts 8:38). It acts out the gospel: Christ died, was buried, and rose again. And it says out loud that the believer was joined to Him in all three.

Planted together: like a seed in the ground. If you're joined to Him in His death, you're joined to Him in His rising.

A picture, not the means

  • the water washes sin awayonly the blood of Christ does that
  • baptism makes you a Christianfaith in Jesus does, before you ever get wet
  • a believer's step of obediencea public picture of what Jesus has already done in him

“And Philip said, If thou believest with all thine heart, thou mayest. And he answered and said, I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God.”

Acts 8:37

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Believe first, then be baptized. A wedding ring doesn't make a man married, but a married man wears it gladly. If you've trusted Christ and haven't been baptized yet, say so today, and we'll talk about it.

The old man

the old man → crucified The old man is who you were in Adam: the man who belonged to sin. He was nailed to the cross with Christ. That's a fact God has done, not a goal you're working toward.

“He that is dead is freed from sin.” A slave owner can shout all he wants at a grave. The dead man doesn't have to get up and obey. Sin still shouts. You don't have to answer.

“I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me.”

Galatians 2:20

Once, for good

Christ died unto sin once. Not over and over. And now death hath no more dominion over him. He will never be in that grave again.

“By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.”

Hebrews 10:10

You're joined to Him. If you shall also live with him, and He can never die again, the life He gave you can't die either. You can't lose what He holds.

Part two

Reckon, and yield

Verses 11–14

The first command in the chapter isn't “try harder.” It's “count on what God has done.”

Do the math

Reckon is a bookkeeper's word: count it, add it up, put it on the books as true.

He died unto sin once6:10
+ you are in Him6:3–5
= dead indeed unto sinand alive unto God

You don't reckon it because you feel dead to sin. Some mornings you won't. You reckon it because God says it's so. A man who is told his debt is paid stops answering the collector's calls, even before he feels rich.

Try it on the hard morning: “That's not who I am any more. I'm dead to that, and alive to God, in Christ.” Say it out loud if you have to.

Part three

His servants ye are

Verses 15–23

Every man serves somebody. Two masters, two roads, and what each one pays at the end.

Two masters, two roads

Nobody plans to become a slave. The first drink, the first hit, the first look didn't own anybody. But every yield is a step down a road, and that road goes iniquity unto iniquity: it always wants more.

The other road works the same way. Every time you hand yourself to God, the path gets more worn in, and it ends somewhere good. You can't walk both. Which one are you on this morning?

“Ye were”

Then

Servants of sin

Owned. It told you where to go, and you went.

Now

Servants of righteousness

Bought free, and serving a Master who's good to His men.

That form of doctrine: the gospel, poured into you like metal into a mold. They obeyed from the heart: they believed it.

Paul uses slavery “after the manner of men”: everyone in Rome knew it. Then: as ye have yielded… even so now yield. Put the energy you once gave the old life into the new one. You knew how to chase a high. Chase this.

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

What did it ever pay?

6:20–21Then

Free from righteousness, and what did you have to show for it? Things you're ashamed of now.

6:22But now

Made free from sin. Servants to God. Fruit that's good for something.

6:22The end

Everlasting life. That's where this road comes out.

Paul asks the question honestly: what fruit had ye then? Look back once, squarely, and name it. That shame tells the truth about the old life. Then let Paul turn the page for you: but now. You don't live in “then” any more.

Points to remember

  1. Grace“God forbid” 6:2Grace is never a reason to keep sinning. We died to sin. Why live in the grave?
  2. Baptism“Buried with him by baptism” 6:4A picture of Christ's death, burial and rising, and ours with Him. It shows salvation; it doesn't save.
  3. The old man“Our old man is crucified with him” 6:6Who you were in Adam died with Christ. Sin still shouts, but it doesn't own you.
  4. Reckon“Reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin” 6:11Count on what God has done, even on mornings you don't feel it.
  5. Yield“His servants ye are to whom ye obey” 6:16Every yield is a step down one road or the other. Hand yourself to God today.
  6. Wages and gift“The gift of God is eternal life” 6:23Sin pays in full: death. God gives what we could never earn, and never takes it back.

Question 1 of 6

Grace

Since grace is bigger than sin, should we keep on sinning?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God forbid. We are dead to sin, so how could we keep living in it? Grace doesn't give a man permission to sin. It gives him a new life and a new Master. A believer who falls still belongs to God; he gets up and turns back.

Question 2 of 6

Baptism

What does baptism picture?

What verses tell us about this?

“Buried with him in baptism, wherein also ye are risen with him through the faith of the operation of God, who hath raised him from the dead.”

Colossians 2:12

Answer

Christ's death, burial and rising, and the believer joined to Him in all three. It's a step of obedience after faith. The water doesn't save.

Question 3 of 6

The old man

What happened to our old man?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

He was crucified with Christ, so that we would no longer serve sin. A dead man is freed from sin's claim. Sin can still tempt us, but it no longer owns us.

Question 4 of 6

Reckon

What are we told to reckon about ourselves?

What verses tell us about this?

“This I say then, Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh.”

Galatians 5:16

Answer

That we are dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God through Jesus Christ our Lord. We count it true because God says so, not because we feel it, and we live from it, in the Spirit's strength.

Question 5 of 6

Yield

How do you know whose servant you are?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus answered them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin.”

John 8:34

Answer

Look at whom you obey. Whatever you keep yielding yourself to is your master. So yield yourself to God, your whole self, every part, as a man who's been brought back from the dead.

Question 6 of 6

Wages and gift

What does sin pay, and what does God give?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Sin pays wages, and the wage is death. God gives a gift, eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord. Nobody earns it. Whoever receives it by faith has it, and it never runs out.
