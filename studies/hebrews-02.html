Hebrews 2

So Great Salvation

A warning not to drift, and then the reason to hold on: the Son of God came down lower than the angels, tasted death for every man, broke the devil's grip, and now calls us brothers. He knows temptation from the inside.

Chapter 1 set the Son high above the angels: on the throne, called God. Chapter 2 asks: then why did He come down lower than the angels? The answer is the gospel.

But first comes the letter's first warning. There are five of them, and this is the shortest.

Part one

Don't drift

Verses 1–4

Most men don't walk away from God in one big decision. They drift.

Slipping the mooring

Let them slip is a sailor's word: to drift past, to float away. A boat that isn't tied up doesn't have to do anything to be lost. The current does it for it.

Skip the Bible a few days. Skip the group. Stop calling the brother who checks on you. Nobody decides to go back. They just stop holding on, and one day they wake up a long way from the dock.

If that, then how much more this

Ifthe law, spoken by angels at Sinai, held firm, and every sin got its just pay,
→
thenhow shall we escape if we neglect the salvation spoken by the Lord Himself?

Neglect is the same word Jesus used for the men invited to the king's feast: “they made light of it, and went their ways, one to his farm, another to his merchandise” (Matthew 22:5). They didn't fight the invitation. They were just busy.

Who is this warning for?

The man who only sat near

Heard it, never took it

He's been around church, liked the people, saw lives change, but never trusted Jesus for himself. For him there is no other way out. “How shall we escape?” We won't. Come to Jesus today.

The man who belongs to Jesus

Held, but told to hold on

He can't be lost. But he can drift in how he lives: cold, careless, miserable. For him the warning says: give the more earnest heed. Tie up again.

The warnings in Hebrews shake loose the man who has only been sitting near the faith, and they drive the true believer to hold on tighter. They are never meant to make a struggling believer fear he has lost his salvation.

Held, so hold on

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Your hold on Jesus may slip; His hold on you won't. That's exactly why you can tie up again today without fear: open the Word, show up, call your brother.

Part two

But we see Jesus

Verses 5–9

Man was made to rule the world. Look around: he doesn't. But look again.

Made to rule, ruled instead

What God made man for: Psalm 8:4–6

“What is man, that thou art mindful of him? … thou hast made him a little lower than the angels, and hast crowned him with glory and honour. Thou madest him to have dominion over the works of thy hands; thou hast put all things under his feet”

What we see now: Hebrews 2:8

“But now we see not yet all things put under him.”

David sang that God made man to rule His world. But sin turned it upside down. A man meant to rule ends up ruled: by a bottle, a pill, a temper, a screen. If that's you, the writer doesn't pretend otherwise. “We see not yet.”

But we see Jesus

We don't see man ruling yet. But we see Jesus: the Son, who stepped down below the angels, became a real man, and went all the way down into death.

Then God raised Him and crowned Him. The man Psalm 8 sang about finally showed up, and He's the first of many.

Where He went, He's bringing us. Your story has a low point too. It doesn't have to be the end of the line.

For every man

not some of them: every one“for every man”

Taste death means He went through death fully, every bitter bit of it. And He did it by the grace of God, a gift, for every man. Not for a few already on the list. For every man, which means for you, whatever you've done.

“And he is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world.”

1 John 2:2

Part three

Not ashamed

Verses 10–13

The Captain goes first, and He calls the men behind Him brothers.

The Captain goes first

Captain means the leader who goes first and cuts the trail for everyone behind him. The same word comes back in 12:2: “the author and finisher of our faith.”

Perfect through sufferings doesn't mean He was ever flawed. It means He was made fully fit for the job by going through it. He doesn't shout directions from the top. He walked every hard mile Himself, and the trail He cut ends in glory.

Not ashamed of you

what you call yourself → brother what Jesus calls you

Some of you carry a lot of shame: what you did, where you've been, the names people called you. Jesus knows all of it. And He is not ashamed to stand up in front of heaven and say, “This is My brother.”

He that sanctifieth (Jesus, who makes us holy) and they who are sanctified (us, set apart for God) are all of one: one Father, one family. You didn't earn a seat at that table. You were given it.

From the cross to “my brethren”

Psalm 22:1

“My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me? why art thou so far from helping me, and from the words of my roaring?”

Psalm 22:22, quoted in Hebrews 2:12

“I will declare thy name unto my brethren: in the midst of the congregation will I praise thee.”

Jesus cried the first line of Psalm 22 from the cross. The same psalm, after the suffering, turns to praise among my brethren. That's the order: He went through the cross first, and came out the other side calling us brothers.

Verse 13 quotes Isaiah 8:17–18: “I will put my trust in him … Behold I and the children.” Jesus trusted His Father as a man, and He stands with the family God gave Him. You're in that line.

Part four

Made like his brethren

Verses 14–18

Why the Son became a man: to break the devil's grip, to be our priest, and to help us when we're tempted.

The chain is broken

Jesus took on flesh and blood so He could die, and by dying He broke the power of the one who held death over us. The devil's best weapon was death and the guilt behind it. At the cross, Jesus paid the guilt and took the weapon away.

Fear of death is a lifelong chain. A lot of what drove us, the using, the running, the anger, was fear underneath. A man who knows death can't hold him anymore can face today.

A priest who is one of us

Verse 16: He didn't come to rescue angels. He came for people, the seed of Abraham. So He became one of us in all things.

Toward us

Merciful

He knows what it is to be tired, hungry, betrayed and grieving. He doesn't look down on weak men.

Toward God

Faithful

He did everything God required, all the way to the cross, to make reconciliation for the sins of the people.

This is the first time Hebrews calls Jesus our high priest, the man who stands between God and the people. Chapters 4 to 10 will open this up. For now: there's Someone standing up for you before God right now.

He knows it from the inside

βοηθῆσαι said boēthēsai to succour: to run to someone who cries out for help

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.”

Hebrews 4:15

Jesus felt the pull of temptation. He never gave in, so He felt its full weight longer than any man who folds. He isn't shocked by your craving. When it hits today, cry out to Him right then, in the moment. He runs toward that cry.

He is not ashamed to call them brethren.

Here's the chapter in one breath: don't drift from a salvation this great. The Son came down lower than the angels, tasted death for every man, broke the chain, and stands by you as a brother and a priest who knows temptation from the inside.

Something to do today: tie up. Pick one rope (ten minutes in the Word, the group, a call to a brother) and hold it. And the next time temptation hits, pray one sentence on the spot: “Jesus, help me now.”

Points to remember

  1. Drifting“give the more earnest heed … lest at any time we should let them slip” 2:1Men don't usually walk away; they drift. Hold on to what you've heard.
  2. So great salvation“which at the first began to be spoken by the Lord” 2:3The Lord spoke it, witnesses passed it on, and God confirmed it.
  3. For every man“should taste death for every man” 2:9By God's grace Jesus died for everyone, which means for you.
  4. Brothers“he is not ashamed to call them brethren” 2:11Jesus isn't ashamed of you. He calls you brother.
  5. Set free“that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death” 2:14Jesus broke the devil's grip and freed us from the fear of death.
  6. Help when tempted“he is able to succour them that are tempted” 2:18He was tempted too, so He runs to help when you call.

Question 1 of 6

Drifting

What does verse 1 tell us to do, and what happens if we don't?

What verses tell us about this?

“But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

Hebrews 3:13

Answer

Give the more earnest heed to what we have heard. If we don't, we slowly slip away, like a boat drifting off its mooring. The answer is daily: the Word, and brothers who encourage us.

Question 2 of 6

So great salvation

Who first spoke this salvation, and how was it confirmed?

What verses tell us about this?

“And they went forth, and preached every where, the Lord working with them, and confirming the word with signs following. Amen.”

Mark 16:20

Answer

The Lord Jesus spoke it first. The men who heard Him passed it on, and God backed it up with signs, wonders, miracles and gifts of the Holy Ghost.

Question 3 of 6

For every man

For whom did Jesus taste death?

What verses tell us about this?

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

John 3:16

Answer

For every man. By the grace of God, Jesus died for the whole world, and whosoever believes in Him has everlasting life. No one is outside His reach.

Question 4 of 6

Brothers

What is Jesus not ashamed to call us?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

Brethren: His brothers. He loved us while we were still sinners, and now He makes us one family with Himself and is not ashamed of us.

Question 5 of 6

Set free

What did Jesus destroy through His death, and whom did He deliver?

What verses tell us about this?

“O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory? The sting of death is sin; and the strength of sin is the law. But thanks be to God, which giveth us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.”

1 Corinthians 15:55–57

Answer

He destroyed the power of the devil, who held death over us, and He delivered everyone who had lived their whole life chained by the fear of death.

Question 6 of 6

Help when tempted

Why is Jesus able to help us when we're tempted?

What verses tell us about this?

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.”

Hebrews 4:15

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Answer

Because He Himself suffered being tempted. He was tempted in every way we are, yet without sin, so He understands, and He runs to help everyone who calls on Him.
