Deuteronomy 6

Hear, O Israel: love the LORD, and do not forget him

The words Jesus called “the first of all the commandments”. Keep them in your heart, talk of them to your children, and beware the day you are full and comfortable. Two of Jesus' answers to the devil come from this chapter.

Things to watch for

Part one

“Hear, O Israel”

Verses 1–9

One LORD, all of you, and his words in your heart, your home and your gates.

“The first of all the commandments”

Deuteronomy 6:4–5

Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD: And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.

Mark 12:29–30

And Jesus answered him, The first of all the commandments is, Hear, O Israel; The Lord our God is one Lord: And thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind, and with all thy strength: this is the first commandment.

When a scribe asked Jesus which commandment came first, he went straight to this chapter. Two things, in order. Who God is: one LORD, with no rival beside him. What he wants: all of you. The word all comes three times. There is no corner kept back for the old life.

Knowing it is not loving him

“Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.”

James 2:19

A man can say “The LORD our God is one LORD” every morning and not love him at all. The devils know it's true. What God asks for is love.

And notice the order of the book: Moses has just reminded them, “I am the LORD thy God, which brought thee out of the land of Egypt” (5:6). He asks for love from people he has already rescued. Love can't be forced out of a man. It answers love.

“We love him, because he first loved us.”

1 John 4:19

No man in this room has loved God with all his heart for one whole day. One man did, every day of his life: Jesus. Start where John starts: with how much he loved you first.

From the heart outward

The words go in first: “shall be in thine heart.” Then they go out: to your children, to what your hands do and your eyes look at, to the door of your house, and to the gate where the town did its business. A man's faith starts inside him, and it doesn't stay there.

On the hand, or in the heart?

Frontlets are bands worn on the forehead, “between thine eyes”. Posts are the doorposts of the house. In Jesus' day the scribes and Pharisees wore phylacteries, and some made them broad to be seen:

“But all their works they do for to be seen of men: they make broad their phylacteries, and enlarge the borders of their garments,”

Matthew 23:5

They had the words on their heads and not in their hearts. Verse 6 comes before verse 8 for a reason. A Bible on the dashboard or a verse tattooed on your arm is no help if the words never got inside.

But hand and eyes are honest places too. What does your hand reach for? What do your eyes rest on? Put his words there, where the temptation actually comes.

Sitting, walking, lying down, rising up

  • When thou sittest in thine houseat the table, on the couch
  • When thou walkest by the wayin the truck, on the way to work
  • When thou liest downthe last words of the day
  • When thou risest upthe first words of the day

Not a class once a week. A father who talks about God as easily as he talks about the weather. Diligently means carefully, and keep at it.

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

Some of us lost those years with our children, and it hurts to read this. It is not too late to start. Grown children still listen to a changed father. Grandchildren do. So does the young man in the group who never had a father. Start today, with one plain sentence about what God has done for you.

Part two

“Beware lest thou forget the LORD”

Verses 10–19

The danger isn't the desert. It's the day you are full.

“Which thou buildedst not”

Great and goodly citieswhich thou buildedst not
Houses full of all good thingswhich thou filledst not
Wells diggedwhich thou diggedst not
Vineyards and olive treeswhich thou plantedst not

Buildedst, filledst, diggedst, plantedst: old forms of you built, filled, dug, planted. Four gifts, and a not in every one. They would move into houses with the food still on the shelves.

“For who maketh thee to differ from another? and what hast thou that thou didst not receive? now if thou didst receive it, why dost thou glory, as if thou hadst not received it?”

1 Corinthians 4:7

Every man here could make that list: a bed he didn't build, a meal he didn't buy, a second chance he didn't earn, a brother who stayed up with him. Grace is living in houses you didn't fill.

Full, and forgetful

“when thou shalt have eaten and be full; Then beware”

Moses doesn't warn them about the wilderness. They remembered God there, because they had to. He warns them about the day the cupboard is full. He will say it again in chapter 8.

“According to their pasture, so were they filled; they were filled, and their heart was exalted; therefore have they forgotten me.”

Hosea 13:6

Every man in recovery knows this. In the first weeks you pray because you are desperate. Then you have a job, a car, ninety days, your family talking to you again, and prayer slips. That is the moment of danger. Not the bad day: the good one.

The cure is in the verse: remember “the LORD, which brought thee forth out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage”. Remember where you were.

Jesus' answers, from this page

Deuteronomy 6:16

Ye shall not tempt the LORD your God, as ye tempted him in Massah.

Matthew 4:7

Jesus said unto him, It is written again, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God.

Deuteronomy 6:13

Thou shalt fear the LORD thy God, and serve him, and shalt swear by his name.

Matthew 4:10

Then saith Jesus unto him, Get thee hence, Satan: for it is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve.

We saw the temptation in Matthew 4. Here is the page Jesus was quoting. And “him only” is verse 14 in two words: “Ye shall not go after other gods.” Swear by his name: Israel was to take its oaths in the LORD's name, never another god's.

“As ye tempted him in Massah”

Israel at Massah

“Is the LORD among us, or not?”

No water, so they demanded that God prove he was with them (Exodus 17:7).

Jesus in the wilderness

“If thou be the Son of God”

Jump, and make God prove it. Jesus would not. He already knew the Father was with him.

To tempt God is to put him on trial: “If you're really there, do this.” Israel did it at Massah when they were thirsty. The devil asked Jesus to do it on the temple. Jesus answered with the very verse that named Massah.

“Neither let us tempt Christ, as some of them also tempted, and were destroyed of serpents.”

1 Corinthians 10:9

We do it too: “If God loves me, he'll get me this job.” “If he's real, he'll take the craving away by Friday.” Trust doesn't set God a test. It holds on when the water hasn't come yet.

Part three

“When thy son asketh thee”

Verses 20–25

The son asks what the rules mean, and the father answers with a story.

The answer is a story

The sonWhat mean the testimonies, and the statutes, and the judgments, which the LORD our God hath commanded you?

The fatherWe were Pharaoh’s bondmen in Egypt; and the LORD brought us out of Egypt with a mighty hand.

The boy asks about rules. The father does not start with rules. He starts with “We were slaves, and the LORD brought us out.” The commands only make sense after the rescue. (Testimonies, statutes, judgments: the LORD’S words of witness, his standing orders, and his rulings. Sore in verse 22 means severe.)

“Howbeit Jesus suffered him not, but saith unto him, Go home to thy friends, and tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee, and hath had compassion on thee.”

Mark 5:19

You don't need to be a Bible teacher to answer your son. You need to be able to say what the Lord did for you.

Out, so he could bring us in

He brought us outfrom thence
→
that he mightbring us in

God didn't rescue Israel from Egypt to leave them wandering. He brought them out so that he could bring them in. And he gave the commands “for our good always”: not to spoil their lives, but to keep them alive in the land.

“Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son:”

Colossians 1:13

Out of something, and into someone: Christ. Sobriety is the “out”; he is the “in”. A man who is only out, and never in, is standing in the desert.

“It shall be our righteousness”

Verse 25 says it plainly: “if we observe to do all these commandments”. That is the righteousness of the law, and Paul quotes Moses on it:

Of the law

do it all, and live

“For Moses describeth the righteousness which is of the law, That the man which doeth those things shall live by them.” (Romans 10:5)

Of God, by faith

found in him

“And be found in him, not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith:” (Philippians 3:9)

“For Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth.”

Romans 10:4

Nobody kept all of it. Not Israel, not us. Christ did, and he gives his righteousness to every man who believes. So we keep his words now as sons, not to earn our place: the place is already given.

Real world: don't forget him on a good day

  • SinForgetting God once the cupboard is full, and saying “one LORD” with a heart that is split.
  • RegretYears we never talked to our children about God, or talked and didn't live it.
  • GraceHouses we didn't fill, wells we didn't dig. “We love him, because he first loved us.”
  • HopeHe brought us out to bring us in. He isn't finished with you, or with your children.
  • TodayPut one verse where your hand and eyes go: your phone's lock screen, your truck. And tell one person, plainly, what the Lord brought you out of.

Points to remember

  1. One LORD“The LORD our God is one LORD: And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart” 6:4–5One God, no rivals, and all of you. Jesus called it the first of all the commandments.
  2. In thine heart“these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart” 6:6Inside first, then out to the hand, the eyes, the house and the gate.
  3. Thy children“when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way” 6:7All day, in ordinary talk. It is never too late to start.
  4. Full and forgetful“when thou shalt have eaten and be full; Then beware lest thou forget the LORD” 6:11–12The good day is more dangerous than the bad one. Remember where you were.
  5. It is written“Ye shall not tempt the LORD your God, as ye tempted him in Massah” 6:16Jesus answered the devil from verses 13 and 16. Don't put God on trial; serve him only.
  6. The son's question“We were Pharaoh’s bondmen in Egypt; and the LORD brought us out” 6:21Answer with what God did. He brought us out to bring us in.

Question 1 of 6

One LORD

What does Israel hear about the LORD, and how are they to love him?

What verses tell us about this?

“We love him, because he first loved us.”

1 John 4:19

Answer

That the LORD our God is one LORD, with no rival. They are to love him with all their heart, soul and might. Jesus called it the first of all the commandments (Mark 12:29–30), and our love answers his love first.

Question 2 of 6

In thine heart

Where must these words be first?

What verses tell us about this?

“But all their works they do for to be seen of men: they make broad their phylacteries, and enlarge the borders of their garments,”

Matthew 23:5

Answer

In the heart. Then on the hand and between the eyes, on the doorposts and the gates. Words worn on the outside and missing on the inside are only for show.

Question 3 of 6

Thy children

When were fathers to talk of these words with their children?

What verses tell us about this?

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

Answer

All the time: sitting at home, walking on the road, lying down at night, getting up in the morning. Teaching them diligently, in everyday talk, not just at set times.

Question 4 of 6

Full and forgetful

When is a man most in danger of forgetting the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

“According to their pasture, so were they filled; they were filled, and their heart was exalted; therefore have they forgotten me.”

Hosea 13:6

Answer

When he is full: when life is good and the gifts are in hand. Remembering where the LORD brought him from is the guard against it.

Question 5 of 6

It is written

Which words of this chapter did Jesus use against the devil?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then saith Jesus unto him, Get thee hence, Satan: for it is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve.”

Matthew 4:10

Answer

Verse 16, “Ye shall not tempt the LORD your God” (Matthew 4:7), and verse 13, to fear the LORD and serve him, with no other gods beside him (verse 14; Matthew 4:10). The first was spoken of Massah, where Israel demanded that God prove himself.

Question 6 of 6

The son's question

What was a father to tell his son when he asked what the commandments meant?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The story of the rescue: we were slaves, and the LORD brought us out with a mighty hand, to bring us into the land, and he commanded us these things for our good always.
