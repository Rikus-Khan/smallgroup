Matthew 6

Thy Father which seeth in secret

Jesus teaches us to give, pray, and fast for God's eyes alone, to store our treasure in heaven, and to stop worrying about tomorrow.

Still on the mountain

Where: a hillside in Galilee, near Capernaum on the north shore of the lake. Jesus “went up into a mountain: and when he was set, his disciples came unto him” (Matthew 5:1). Teachers sat down to teach.

When: early in His ministry, about AD 28. Rome rules the land, and Herod Antipas rules Galilee for Rome.

Who's listening: His disciples up close, and behind them “great multitudes of people from Galilee, and from Decapolis, and from Jerusalem, and from Judaea, and from beyond Jordan” (4:25). Jews and Gentiles, the religious and the broken, all on one hill.

The middle of the Sermon on the Mount

Matthew 5, 6, and 7 are one sermon, the longest stretch of Jesus' teaching anywhere in the Bible. Today we're in the middle of it.

  1. 5:1–12“Blessed are…” the poor in spirit, the mourners, the meek
  2. 5:13–48Salt and light. “Ye have heard… But I say unto you”
  3. 6:1–18In secret: giving, praying, fastingtoday
  4. 6:19–34Treasure, two masters, and worrytoday
  5. 7:1–29Judging, asking, two gates, two houses

In chapter 5 Jesus said our righteousness has to go deeper than the scribes' and Pharisees' (5:20). Now He shows what that looks like in three things every religious man did: giving to the poor, praying, and fasting. The question in all three is the same: who are you doing it for?

Part one

In secret

Verses 1–18

Giving, praying, and fasting for an audience of One.

A man behind a mask

ὑποκριτής said hupokritēs “a stage actor”

In Greek theaters an actor wore a mask and played a part. A hypocrite is a man playing a part. He gives, but the giving is a performance, and the crowd is his real audience.

“Alms” are gifts to the poor. Nobody knows if men literally blew trumpets. It may be a picture, like our “blowing your own horn.” And “let not thy left hand know” means: don't even keep score yourself. Give, and forget it.

Paid in full

The Greek word behind “they have” was written on business receipts. It meant paid in full, nothing more owed.

Received from men

  • a prayer on the corner (v. 5)admiration

  • from the Fathernothing more
PAID IN FULL“they have their reward”

The “closet” was the inner storeroom of a house, often the only room with a door. Go in, shut the door, and talk to your Father. He sees you there. Nobody else has to.

Not more words, a better Father

The heathen

“Much speaking”

They piled up words and names of gods, hoping that enough of them would finally get through. Prayer was a machine, and you had to work it hard.

Your Father

“Before ye ask”

He already knows what you need. You don't have to talk Him into caring. You come to Him like a son to his dad.

Asking more than once isn't the problem. Jesus prayed the same words three times in the garden (Matthew 26:44). The problem is empty words said to impress God or anybody else. Short and honest beats long and hollow.

The shape of the prayer

“After this manner” means like this. It's a pattern to pray by, not only words to recite. It has six requests, and God comes first.

for GodHallowed be thy nameThy kingdom comeThy will be done

“Hallowed” means treated as holy. And it starts with “Our Father.” Everyone who has received Jesus can say that: “as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God” (John 1:12). Before you bring God your list, remember who you're talking to.

One day's bread

Israel learned this in the wilderness. Manna couldn't be stored up. You gathered today's, and tomorrow there would be more.

“Then said the LORD unto Moses, Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no.”

Exodus 16:4

Notice the three requests for us. Bread is today's need. Debts are yesterday's sins. Temptation is tomorrow's danger. Jesus taught us to bring all three to the Father.

God gives grace the same way He gave manna: enough for today. Don't try to carry next month's sobriety today. Ask for today's bread, and come back tomorrow.

Lead us not into temptation

God never tempts anyone to sin (James 1:13). This is a man who knows his weakness, asking his Father to steer him:

“Lead us not”→keep me away from it
“Deliver us”→get me out of it

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Then live like you meant it. A man who prays “lead me not” and then drives past the old bar is praying one way and walking another.

It ends where it began

Thine is the kingdom, the power, and the glory.

The prayer starts with God's name and ends with God's glory. Our needs sit in the middle, held on both sides by who He is.

That's why we can ask with confidence. The kingdom is His, so He can give it. The power is His, so He can deliver. The glory is His, so we don't have to make ourselves look good. “Amen” means so be it, or it's true.

This isn't earning heaven; salvation is a gift received by faith. He's talking to sons about walking with their Father. You can't hold a grudge and close fellowship with God at once.

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Resentment is poison for a man in recovery. Forgiving doesn't say it was fine. It hands the debt to God.

Fasting with a clean face

To fast is to go without food for a time to seek God. Some Pharisees fasted twice a week, and made sure people noticed: “I fast twice in the week, I give tithes of all that I possess” (Luke 18:12). “Disfigure their faces” means they looked rough on purpose.

Jesus says, “when ye fast,” not “if.” But fix your hair and wash your face. Olive oil on the head was everyday grooming. Look normal, and let it stay between you and God.

Part two

Treasure in heaven

Verses 19–24

Where your treasure is, your heart follows. And it can only follow one master.

Everything down here wears out

In Jesus' day, wealth was kept in fine clothes, in coins, and in what you could store at home. Each one had an enemy:

Motheats the fine robes
Rusteats away the metal
Thieves“break through” a mud-brick wall

Houses were often mud brick. A thief didn't pick the lock. He dug through the wall. Jesus isn't saying it's wrong to own things. He's saying they won't last, and they'll own your heart if you let them: “where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.”

Only one master

“Mammon” is an Aramaic word for money and possessions. Jesus talks about it like a person, a master that wants to own you.

A slave can't belong to two owners. Sooner or later one wins. You can't keep it level: “he will hold to the one, and despise the other.”

The “single” eye is a clear, undivided one, fixed on one thing. In Proverbs, an “evil eye” is a stingy, grasping one (Proverbs 23:6, 28:22). What a man stares at fills him, with light or with darkness.

Part three

Take no thought

Verses 25–34

Birds, wildflowers, and a Father who knows what you need.

What worry adds

In 1611, “take thought” meant to be anxious, to worry. Jesus isn't against planning. He's against the gnawing fear that eats a man alive. “Meat” is food, and “raiment” is clothing.

one long nightlying awake, worrying
+ 0 cubitsa cubit is about 18 inches
+ 0 daysthe word can also mean length of life

Birds don't plant, harvest, or build barns, and they eat every day. They work for it, but they don't worry about it. Your Father feeds them. “Are ye not much better than they?” You matter more to God than every bird in the sky.

Dressed by God

The “lilies of the field” were the wildflowers that cover Galilee's hills in spring. Nobody planted them. Nobody sewed them anything to wear.

Solomon was the richest king Israel ever had. Jesus says one wildflower was better dressed than he was.

And that flower is gone in a few days. Dry grass was burned to heat the clay “oven.” If God dresses something that short-lived that well, “shall he not much more clothe you”?

First things first

Ifyou seek first the kingdom of God, and His righteousness,
→
then“all these things shall be added”

“All these things” means what He just named: food, drink, and clothes. It's a promise to supply what we need, not a promise to make us rich. Jesus Himself had “not where to lay his head” (Matthew 8:20).

“Gentiles” here means people who don't know God. They chase these things because they have no Father to trust. You do. Put Him first, and let Him handle the rest.

One day at a time

  1. Yesterdayforgiven: “forgive us our debts”
  2. Today“Give us this day our daily bread”live here
  3. Tomorrowin His hands: “lead us not into temptation”

“Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof” means each day has enough trouble of its own. Don't borrow tomorrow's. Most of us have wrecked a good today by living in a bad tomorrow that never came.

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Points to remember

  1. Motives“Thy Father which seeth in secret” 6:4Do it for God's eyes. Applause from men is the whole payment; the Father's reward lasts.
  2. Prayer“After this manner therefore pray ye” 6:9Talk to your Father. Put His name, kingdom, and will first, then bring Him your needs.
  3. Temptation“Lead us not into temptation” 6:13Ask Him to keep you away from it and get you out of it. Then take the way of escape.
  4. Forgiveness“Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors” 6:12A man who knows how much he's been forgiven learns to let others off the hook.
  5. Treasure“Ye cannot serve God and mammon” 6:24Your heart follows your treasure, and it can only have one master.
  6. Worry“Seek ye first the kingdom of God” 6:33Your Father knows what you need. Put Him first and live one day at a time.

Question 1 of 6

Motives

Who are we really doing it for when we give, pray, and fast?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For our Father, who sees in secret. If we do it to be seen by men, their applause is the whole payment. If we do it for Him alone, He sees every hidden gift, prayer, and fast, and He rewards it.

Question 2 of 6

Prayer

How did Jesus teach us to pray?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Simply and honestly, as sons talking to a Father who already knows what we need. Start with Him: His name, His kingdom, His will. Then ask for today's bread, forgiveness for yesterday, and protection for tomorrow, and end with His glory.

Question 3 of 6

Temptation

What are we asking when we pray, “lead us not into temptation”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God: for God cannot be tempted with evil, neither tempteth he any man:”

James 1:13

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Answer

God never tempts us to sin. We're admitting we're weak and asking our Father to steer us away from temptation, and to rescue us from evil when we're in it. He is faithful, and He always provides a way of escape. Our part is to take it.

Question 4 of 6

Forgiveness

Why does Jesus tie being forgiven to forgiving others?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Answer

We don't earn salvation by forgiving; it's a gift received by faith. But a son who refuses to forgive can't walk close with his Father. God forgave us a debt we could never pay, so we forgive as we've been forgiven. Holding a grudge only keeps us chained to it.

Question 5 of 6

Treasure

Why can't a man serve God and mammon?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because a master wants all of you. Money and things can't last, and they'll still take over your heart if you let them. A man ends up loving one and despising the other. Treasure laid up in heaven is safe, and it keeps your heart pointed at God.

Question 6 of 6

Worry

What does Jesus tell us to do with worry?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Stop carrying it. Worry adds nothing to our lives. Our Father feeds the birds and dresses the flowers, and He knows what we need. Seek His kingdom first, trust Him for the rest, and live today, not tomorrow.
