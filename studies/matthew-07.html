The end of the sermon

We're still on the hillside near Capernaum, about AD 28. Matthew 5, 6, and 7 are one sermon, and today Jesus finishes it.

  1. 5:1–12“Blessed are…”
  2. 5:13–48Salt, light, and “But I say unto you”
  3. 6:1–34In secret; treasure; worry
  4. 7:1–12Judging, asking, the golden ruletoday
  5. 7:13–29Two ways, and the people's amazementtoday
  6. 8:1“When he was come down from the mountain”

Jesus doesn't end with a summary. He ends with a fork in the road. Watch for the pairs:

  • Two gateswide and strait (13–14)
  • Two treesgood and corrupt (15–20)
  • Two claimssaying and doing (21–23)
  • Two housesrock and sand (24–27)

Part one

Judge not

Verses 1–6

A speck, a plank, and the man who can only see one of them.

What “judge not” means

  • never call anything wrongverses 6 and 15 require telling good from bad
  • never help a brother with his sinverse 5 says help him, after dealing with your own
  • don't condemn others while excusing yourselfthe harsh, fault-finding, “I'm better than him” spirit

“Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment.”

John 7:24

“With what measure ye mete” means the measuring cup you use. The standard you hold over others is the one held over you.

The speck and the plank

A “mote” is a speck of sawdust. A “beam” is a roof beam, a log. Jesus grew up a carpenter: “Is not this the carpenter” (Mark 6:3). He'd seen both.

It's meant to be funny: a man with a log in his eye, hunting for a speck in someone else's.

We're quickest to spot in others the sin we hide in ourselves. Recovery starts where this verse does: “thine own eye.” Then you'll see clearly enough to help your brother.

Dogs and pearls

These weren't pets. Dogs in that day were wild scavengers that ran the streets, and swine were unclean animals to a Jew. Give a pig a pearl, and he'll stomp it into the mud because he can't eat it, then turn on you.

Right after “judge not,” Jesus tells us to use judgment. Some people will mock and attack the holy things of God no matter how you offer them. You don't have to keep throwing your pearls at a man who keeps trampling them.

Pray for him. Love him. Live it in front of him. Be ready when he finally asks. But wisdom knows when to stop arguing.

Part two

Ask, seek, knock

Verses 7–12

A Father who gives good gifts, and a rule for treating everyone else.

Keep on knocking

  1. Ask keep on asking
  2. Seek keep on seeking
  3. Knock keep on knocking

In Greek, these verbs keep going: keep asking, keep seeking, keep knocking. Prayer isn't one try and then give up. And look who gets in: “every one that asketh.” Not the best men. Every one.

Some of us knocked on a lot of wrong doors looking for something. This is the door that opens.

How much more

Bread was baked in round, flat loaves that could look like the stones on a Galilee hillside. Some fish of the lake look a bit like a snake. Would any father play a cruel trick like that on his hungry boy?

An earthly father

“being evil”

Even a sinful man, even one who's failed his kids, still knows how to hand his son bread and not a rock.

Your Father in heaven

“how much more”

He has no evil in Him at all. He never tricks His children, never gives a snake for a fish.

“If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?”

Luke 11:13

Maybe your own father gave you stones. God is not him. He is the Father you never had.

The golden rule

Others had said something like it, the other way around. A well-known rabbi named Hillel, a generation earlier, taught: what you hate, don't do to your neighbor. Jesus turned it from a fence into a road:

The old saying→don't do harmStay out of trouble. A man can keep that by staying home and doing nothing.
Jesus→“do ye even so to them”Go and do good. Think about what you'd want, then do that for him first.

“This is the law and the prophets”: the whole Old Testament's teaching about how to treat people, in one sentence. Think of the men you've wronged, and the amends you'd want if it had been done to you.

Part three

Two ways

Verses 13–23

A strait gate, wolves in wool, and a word no one wants to hear.

Strait, not straight

“Strait” means tight, narrow, like the Strait of Gibraltar. It's a gate you squeeze through, one at a time.

The broad way takes no effort; you just follow the crowd. The narrow way runs through one Person: “I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me” (John 14:6).

Narrow doesn't mean closed: “I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved” (John 10:9). Few find it because few will leave the crowd and come.

Wolves in wool

a sheep → a wolf “Ravening” means ravenous, tearing for food.

A false prophet is someone who claims to speak for God but doesn't. He looks like one of the flock and talks like one. Underneath, he's feeding on the flock: their money, their trust, their bodies, their faith.

How do you tell? Not by the costume. “Ye shall know them by their fruits.” Thorn bushes don't grow grapes. Sooner or later, what a man really is shows up in what he produces.

You can't fake fruit for long

Fruit grows out of what a tree is. A good tree doesn't strain to make good fruit. It just does, because of what's inside it.

A corrupt tree can hang fake fruit for a while. But the season always comes when the real crop shows.

Good fruit: “the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance” (Galatians 5:22–23). The Spirit grows it in every believer.

“Lord, Lord”

Many, in that dayLord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name? and in thy name have cast out devils? and in thy name done many wonderful works?
all about what we did

JesusI never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.

We prophesied, we cast out, we did. Not one word about trusting Him. And “never”: not “I knew you once, and you lost it.” Whoever truly belongs to Christ is known by Him and kept by Him.

“The will of my Father” is “that every one which seeth the Son, and believeth on him, may have everlasting life” (John 6:40). It starts with trusting Him.

Part four

Rock or sand

Verses 24–29

Two builders, one storm, and a teacher like no other.

The same storm

Both men heard. Both built. Both got the same rain, flood, and wind. The difference was underneath, out of sight until the storm.

In Galilee, a dry stream bed looks like a fine flat lot in summer. Winter rains turn it into a river overnight.

The difference wasn't hearing. It was doing. Every man in recovery knows the storm is coming. A life built on Christ, doing what He says, “fell not.”

As one having authority

The scribes were the experts in the law of Moses. They taught by quoting other teachers: Rabbi So-and-so says this, Rabbi Such-and-such says that. Jesus quoted no one but Scripture. He spoke as the one who wrote it.

13 times“I say unto you” in chapters 5 and 6
6 times“Ye have heard” or “It hath been said,” then “But I say unto you”
7:21–23He's the Judge “in that day”

“Doctrine” simply means teaching. The crowd was “astonished”, stunned. No ordinary teacher talks like this. Either Jesus is out of His mind, or He is who He says He is. And if He is, His sayings are the rock to build on.

Next, He comes down the mountain and shows the same authority over sickness, storms, and demons (Matthew 8).

Points to remember

  1. Judging“First cast out the beam out of thine own eye” 7:5Don't condemn others while excusing yourself. Deal with your own sin first, then help your brother.
  2. Prayer“Every one that asketh receiveth” 7:8Keep asking, seeking, and knocking. Your Father gives good gifts, never stones.
  3. Others“Do ye even so to them” 7:12Don't just avoid harm. Do for others the good you'd want done for you.
  4. The way“Enter ye in at the strait gate” 7:13The way to life runs through Jesus alone. It's narrow, but open to anyone who comes.
  5. Real or fake“By their fruits ye shall know them” 7:20Look past the costume and the talk. What's inside shows up in what grows.
  6. Foundation“It fell not: for it was founded upon a rock” 7:25Hear Jesus' words and do them. The storm will come, and the house on the rock stands.

Question 1 of 6

Judging

What does Jesus mean when He says, “Judge not”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment.”

John 7:24

Answer

Not that we can never call anything wrong. He means don't be the harsh, self-righteous critic who condemns others and excuses himself. The measure we use on others is used on us. Deal honestly with your own sin first; then you'll see clearly enough to help your brother.

Question 2 of 6

Prayer

What does Jesus promise to those who ask, seek, and knock?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That every one who keeps asking receives, every one who keeps seeking finds, and the door opens to every one who keeps knocking. Our Father is far better than any earthly father. He gives good things to His children who ask Him.

Question 3 of 6

Others

How should we treat other people?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The way we'd want to be treated, and we go first. Not just avoiding harm, but actively doing good. We measure others with the grace we hope to receive, and we stop hunting for specks while ignoring our own beam.

Question 4 of 6

The way

Why is the gate strait and the way narrow?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6

Answer

Because there's only one way to life, and it's a Person: Jesus. The broad way is easy; you just follow the crowd. The narrow gate means leaving the crowd and coming to Christ yourself. It's narrow, but it's open to whosoever will come.

Question 5 of 6

Real or fake

How can you tell what's real from what's fake?

What verses tell us about this?

“But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law.”

Galatians 5:22–23

Answer

By the fruit, not the costume. False prophets look like sheep, and some people say “Lord, Lord” and list their works. But what a man really is shows up over time in what he produces. Real faith in Jesus grows the Spirit's fruit.

Question 6 of 6

Foundation

What does it take to build a life that stands?

What verses tell us about this?

“For other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ.”

1 Corinthians 3:11

Answer

A foundation on the rock: Jesus Christ and His words, heard and done. Hearing alone isn't enough; the foolish man heard too. Storms come to every house. The life built on Christ, doing what He says, stands.
