Part one

First of all, pray

Verses 1–7

Prayer goes first, and it goes wide. Then Paul says why: God's heart is wide too.

Pray past your own door

Four kinds of praying: supplications (asking), prayers, intercessions (standing in for someone else) and thanks. First on the list of people: “kings, and all that are in authority.” In AD 62 to 64 that was Nero.

Why? “That we may lead a quiet and peaceable life.” It's hard to hate a man you're praying for: your boss, your judge, your parole officer. Name one today.

Even exiles in pagan Babylon were told to “pray unto the LORD for it” (Jeremiah 29:7).

He wants all of them

“All men” means all men: Nero, the guy in the next cell, the man who hurt you, and you. Paul calls God “our Saviour” and says what He wills: that every one “be saved, and … come unto the knowledge of the truth.”

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Offered to everyone, received by faith: “whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved” (Romans 10:13). You never have to wonder if God wants you in.

One bridge, for all

A mediator stands between two sides and brings them together. Between God and us is a gap we can't cross by being good or trying harder. Paul says there's “one mediator.” Only one.

“The man Christ Jesus”: truly one of us, so He could stand with us. And a ransom is the price that buys a slave free: He “gave himself a ransom for all.”

“And he is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world.”

1 John 2:2

Part two

In God's house

Verses 8–15

How the men pray, how the women are known, and one passage where good teachers don't all agree.

Hands up, fists down

clenched fists, a grudge, “will He even hear me?” → open, lifted hands Lifting hands was a common way to pray: open, empty, asking (“Lift up your hands in the sanctuary, and bless the LORD,” Psalm 134:2). Holy means set apart for Him, not spotless. Two things spoil it: wrath at someone, and doubting that God will listen.

“Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.”

Matthew 5:23–24

Paul speaks to the men here: anger is a common trouble for men at prayer. Before you pray today: who are you angry with? Name it to God. If you can fix it, go and fix it first.

What makes a woman beautiful in God's house

Not

Showing off

“Broided hair, or gold, or pearls, or costly array.” In Ephesus a rich woman could wear a fortune on her head. In a church where slaves and the wealthy sat side by side, that sent the wrong message.

But

“Good works”

Modest dress, with “shamefacedness” (a gentle sense of shame, not showing off) and “sobriety” (a steady mind). Her adorning is what she does.

“But let it be the hidden man of the heart, in that which is not corruptible, even the ornament of a meek and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God of great price.”

1 Peter 3:4

It isn't a ban on a wedding ring. Christians apply it in different ways, but the point is plain: don't make your clothes the first thing about you. That holds for men too. What are you trying to prove with how you look?

A hard text, taken slowly

Good, godly, Bible-believing teachers read these verses a little differently. Here is what we can say honestly.

“Let the woman learn”

The first word is permission to be taught. Women were often shut out of religious study. Paul says: come and learn.

“In silence”

The word means quietness, a calm, learning spirit. It's the same word as “a quiet and peaceable life” in verse 2.

“To teach… authority over the man”

Paul is speaking of the church's teaching and leading. Baptists hold that the office of pastor is for men, and see these verses, with chapter 3, as the reason.

What's plain: men and women are equal in Christ (“there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus,” Galatians 3:28). Women served God in the early church, and a woman like Priscilla helped teach even Apollos (Acts 18:26).

What's plain, and where teachers differ

Plain

Agreed by nearly everyone

Women are saved the same way men are. Women are to learn, and are valued. Women have always served: “Phebe our sister, which is a servant of the church” (Romans 16:1). Paul's reasons come from creation (vv. 13–14), not only from Ephesus.

Differ

How far it reaches

Good Baptist teachers differ over how far “teach” and “authority” go: a class of men and women, teaching women or children, missions, writing. The office of pastor is held to be for men. The rest, a church works out with its leaders, prayerfully, together.

It would be easy to skip a passage like this. But you can say two things at once: we take the whole Bible seriously, and we're not looking for ways to put anyone down. Both are true here.

“Saved in childbearing”

Here the teachers really do differ, so we'll go slowly. First, what it can't mean:

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

The Saviour was born

God promised a Son born of a woman who would crush the serpent (Genesis 3:15). The woman who fell brought the Saviour into the world.

Kept safe through it

Brought safely through the danger of childbirth, as a mother who trusts God.

Saved in her calling

She is kept and fulfilled, not shut out, in the ordinary life God gave her, if she continues in faith.

No one is saved by having children, or by anything done. We are saved by grace, through faith, in Jesus. Everyone agrees on that, and what Paul adds, “faith and charity and holiness,” is what a saved life looks like in a man or a woman.

Points to remember

  1. Prayer“Supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks, be made for all men; for kings, and for all that are in authority” 2:1–2Pray for everyone, including the people over you, and pray with thanks.
  2. God's heart“Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth” 2:4God wants every man saved. That includes you, and the man you've given up on.
  3. The mediator“One mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus; who gave himself a ransom for all” 2:5–6One bridge between God and us, and Jesus paid the price for all.
  4. Men at prayer“Lifting up holy hands, without wrath and doubting” 2:8Pray with open hands: no grudge, and no doubt that He hears.
  5. Beauty“Which becometh women professing godliness, with good works” 2:10A godly woman is known by what she does, not by what she wears to be noticed.
  6. A hard text“For Adam was first formed, then Eve” 2:13Paul gives creation as his reason. Baptists hold that the pastor's office is for men, and teachers differ on how far the rest goes.

Question 1 of 6

Prayer

Who should we pray for, and what do we ask for them?

What verses tell us about this?

“And seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives, and pray unto the LORD for it: for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace.”

Jeremiah 29:7

Answer

All men, and especially kings and everyone in authority. We ask, we plead for them, and we give thanks, so that we can lead a quiet, peaceable life in godliness. It's hard to hate a man you're praying for.

Question 2 of 6

God's heart

What does God our Saviour want for all men?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

He wants every man saved and brought to know the truth. Nobody is left off the list, and whoever calls on Him will be saved.

Question 3 of 6

The mediator

Who stands between God and men, and what did He give?

What verses tell us about this?

“For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.”

Mark 10:45

“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6

Answer

The man Christ Jesus, the only mediator. He gave Himself as a ransom: the price that buys a slave free. And it's offered for all.

Question 4 of 6

Men at prayer

How should men pray?

What verses tell us about this?

“Lift up your hands in the sanctuary, and bless the LORD.”

Psalm 134:2

“Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.”

Matthew 5:23–24

Answer

With open, lifted hands, without anger and without doubt. If there's a grudge, deal with it first. Then pray, sure that He hears.

Question 5 of 6

Beauty

What does Paul say should adorn a woman who professes godliness?

What verses tell us about this?

“Whose adorning let it not be that outward adorning of plaiting the hair, and of wearing of gold, or of putting on of apparel; But let it be the hidden man of the heart, in that which is not corruptible, even the ornament of a meek and quiet spirit, which is in the sight of God of great price.”

1 Peter 3:3–4

Answer

Good works, with modest dress and a steady mind, not showing off. In God's eyes the best ornament is a gentle, quiet spirit.

Question 6 of 6

A hard text

What reasons does Paul give for how men and women serve in the church?

What verses tell us about this?

“There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female: for ye are all one in Christ Jesus.”

Galatians 3:28

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Creation: Adam was formed first, and the woman was deceived. Baptists hold that the pastor's office is for men; good teachers differ on how far the rest reaches. Everyone agrees men and women are saved the same way, by grace, and are equal in Christ.
