Numbers 9

The passover in the wilderness, the men kept back, and the cloud

A year after the night in Egypt the LORD tells Israel to keep the passover again. Some men are unclean and cannot, and ask why they should be kept back: the LORD gives them a second month. And over it all the cloud: when it rose they journeyed, and when it rested they rested, at the commandment of the LORD.

Part one

“Keep the passover”

Verses 1–5

The LORD's word, a year after Egypt, and Israel's obedience.

In the wilderness of Sinai

Israel was still camped where the law had been given: “the wilderness of Sinai” (verses 1 and 5), at the mountain the map marks, Jabal al-Lawz in Arabia.

“The first month of the second year after they were come out of the land of Egypt”: a year to the month since the night of the first passover, and the tabernacle two weeks old. They were to keep it where they were, tents round the tent of the LORD.

Two months at Sinai

  1. 1st day, 1st monthThe tabernacle reared upExodus 40:17
  2. 14th day, 1st monthThe passover, at evenNumbers 9:5
  3. 1st day, 2nd monthThe people numberedNumbers 1:1
  4. 14th day, 2nd monthThe second passoverNumbers 9:11
  5. 20th day, 2nd monthThe cloud lifts: they leave SinaiNumbers 10:11

Chapter 9 steps back a month, to the first month, to tell of the passover before it tells of the cloud that ends their stay at Sinai.

The passover, as the LORD appointed it

  • “his appointed season”the time he set: not when it suited them, but when he said
  • “at even”in the evening, as the fourteenth day ended
  • “the rites… the ceremonies”the way it was to be done, step by step, as already given in Exodus 12
  • “the wilderness of Sinai”they kept it in a dry land, with no field or house of their own

They had once kept it in Egypt, in a night of fear, with blood on the door. A year later it is kept in peace, by a free people with the tabernacle in the midst. The first was kept for a rescue about to happen, and this one remembers a rescue that had.

“Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us:”

1 Corinthians 5:7

Paul says it plainly: Christ our passover. The lamb of Egypt and the lamb of Sinai pointed ahead to the one who was sacrificed for us.

Part two

“Wherefore are we kept back?”

Verses 6–14

Men who could not keep it, and the second month the LORD gave them.

“Wherefore are we kept back?”

The menWe are defiled by the dead body of a man: wherefore are we kept back, that we may not offer an offering of the LORD in his appointed season among the children of Israel?

MosesStand still, and I will hear what the LORD will command concerning you.

Wherefore means why. Defiled means made unclean: anyone who touched a dead body was unclean by the law, and could not take part in the passover until he was clean again. These men were not careless. Moses does not say whose body it was or how it happened, only that the day had come and they could not.

They asked, and Moses asked

Look at what the men did. They did not shrug and stay away, and they did not sneak in unclean. They came to Moses and Aaron “on that day”, said what was true, and asked.

And look at Moses. He did not make up an answer. “Stand still, and I will hear.” The best leader in Israel did not know, and said so, and went to ask the LORD.

A man in recovery learns this early: the strong thing is to say what is true and ask, whether it is the matter of the day or something he has done. Nobody has to have the answer ready.

A month later

Ifa man is unclean by a dead body, or in a journey afar off,
→
thenhe keeps it a month later: the fourteenth day of the second month, at even.
  • “your posterity”those who come after you: this was for their children as well
  • “afar off”far away, on a journey that kept a man from the camp
  • “unleavened bread and bitter herbs”the same meal as the first month: bread without leaven, and bitter plants
  • “nor break any bone of it”the same rule as in Egypt, and every ordinance of the passover

Not a different passover and not a lesser one. The same lamb, the same bread, the same rules, a month late.

“Nor break any bone of it”

The lamb's bones were to be left whole, in the second month as in Egypt (Exodus 12:46).

“For these things were done, that the scripture should be fulfilled, A bone of him shall not be broken.”

John 19:36

John says it was fulfilled when Jesus died and the soldiers did not break his legs. The bone no one was to break was a sign of the one whose bone was not broken.

Kept back, or cut off

The man who could not

verses 6–11

Unclean by a dead body, or far away on a journey. He wanted to keep it and could not. The LORD gave him a second month.

The man who would not

verse 13

Clean, at home, and he “forbeareth to keep the passover”. Forbeareth means holds back, refuses. He “shall be cut off from among his people… that man shall bear his sin.”

The second month was for the man who was kept back and wanted to be there. It was not for the man who just did not bother. The first man was in trouble through no fault of his own, and the LORD made room for him. The second had the door open and walked past it.

And the stranger who lived among Israel and wanted to keep the passover could: “one ordinance, both for the stranger, and for him that was born in the land” (verse 14).

Far off, and made nigh

Some of us were “in a journey afar off”: a long way from home, a long way from clean. Others have been so far into the wrong life that they feel unfit to come near at all. The LORD does not say to that man, “too late.” He says, “yet he shall keep the passover unto the LORD.”

“But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.”

Ephesians 2:13

Nigh means near. The men who were unclean and far off had a second month. We have more than a second month: we have the blood of Christ, which makes the far-off man near. Come now.

Part three

“At the commandment of the LORD”

Verses 15–23

The cloud that led them, and how they followed it.

By day a cloud, by night fire

“On the day that the tabernacle was reared up the cloud covered the tabernacle” (verse 15): the very day it was set up, the LORD came to it. Reared up means raised, set up. The tent of the testimony is the tabernacle, which held the tables of the law, the testimony.

“So it was alway”: always, day and night. There was no hour when they were walking without him (Exodus 13:21).

As long as the cloud stayed

  1. One night“from even unto the morning”verse 21
  2. A few days“when the cloud was a few days”verse 20
  3. Two days“or whether it were two days”verse 22
  4. A month“or a month”verse 22
  5. A year“or a year”: they stayedverse 22

The people never knew how long. They could pitch in the evening and be gone before dawn, or unpack and sit for a year. “whether it was by day or by night that the cloud was taken up, they journeyed.” Pitched means set up their tents; tarried means stayed on; charge means the duty the LORD gave them.

Seven times in this one chapter the LORD's commandment is named as the reason: “at the commandment of the LORD”, or “according to the commandment of the LORD”.

Under the cloud

“Moreover, brethren, I would not that ye should be ignorant, how that all our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea; And were all baptized unto Moses in the cloud and in the sea;”

1 Corinthians 10:1–2

Paul looks back at this cloud and tells the Corinthians: they were all under it, and all were led by it. And yet he goes on to say that “with many of them God was not well pleased” (1 Corinthians 10:5). Being under the cloud did not make a man follow it. The chapters ahead show what the following looked like when it got hard.

The cloud is God's way of saying: I am here, and I will say when. He did not hand Israel a map. He gave them himself, and asked them to watch him.

Waiting, and going

“Wait on the LORD: be of good courage, and he shall strengthen thine heart: wait, I say, on the LORD.”

Psalm 27:14

“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.”

Proverbs 3:5–6

When the cloud stayed, staying was obedience. When it rose, going was. A man early in recovery often wants to move, to fix, to decide everything today. Some seasons are tent seasons, and the right thing is to stay in the same job, the same church, the same fellowship, until he is told to go.

And when you do not know, do what Moses did: stand still, and ask. Call a brother, and your pastor.

Real world: a second month, and a cloud

  • SinNeglect: the clean man, at home, who forbore to keep the passover. The LORD counted it his sin (verse 13): a door left open and never used.
  • Regret“Wherefore are we kept back?” Missing what you wanted to be at, through your own past or another's. The LORD heard those men, and he hears this question still.
  • GraceA second month, the same lamb, the same bread. Nothing was thinner on the fourteenth day of the second month.
  • Hope“Ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.” Far off is not too far.
  • TodayAsk before you move. Tell one brother what you are deciding, and wait for the cloud: do not run ahead of it, and do not sit when it has risen.

Points to remember

  1. The month“in the first month of the second year after they were come out of the land of Egypt” 9:1A year after Egypt, the LORD tells Israel to keep the passover in the wilderness of Sinai.
  2. The day“In the fourteenth day of this month, at even, ye shall keep it in his appointed season” 9:3His appointed season, in the evening: they kept it exactly as he said.
  3. The men kept back“We are defiled by the dead body of a man: wherefore are we kept back” 9:7They did not hide, and they did not stay away: they asked.
  4. The second month“The fourteenth day of the second month at even they shall keep it” 9:11The LORD makes a way for the man who could not: the same lamb, a month late.
  5. The cut off“the same soul shall be cut off from among his people” 9:13The man who could and would not has no excuse: he shall bear his sin.
  6. The cloud“At the commandment of the LORD the children of Israel journeyed, and at the commandment of the LORD they pitched” 9:18They moved and rested at his word, whether a night or a year.

Question 1 of 6

The month

In which month of which year did the LORD tell Moses to keep the passover?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In the first month of the second year after they were come out of Egypt, in the wilderness of Sinai: Let the children of Israel keep the passover at his appointed season.

Question 2 of 6

The day

On what day of the month, and at what hour, was the passover to be kept?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On the fourteenth day of the first month, at even, according to all the rites and ceremonies. And they kept it so.

Question 3 of 6

The men kept back

Why could certain men not keep the passover on that day?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They were defiled by the dead body of a man. They came to Moses and Aaron and asked why they should be kept back, and Moses went to hear what the LORD would command.

Question 4 of 6

The second month

Who could keep the passover a month later?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Any man unclean by a dead body, or in a journey afar off: he keeps it on the fourteenth day of the second month at even, with unleavened bread and bitter herbs.

Question 5 of 6

The cut off

What was to happen to a clean man at home who forbore to keep it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He was to be cut off from among his people, because he brought not the offering of the LORD in his appointed season: that man shall bear his sin.

Question 6 of 6

The cloud

How did Israel know when to journey and when to stay?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By the cloud. When it was taken up they journeyed; where it abode they pitched; and as long as it stayed they rested: at the commandment of the LORD.
