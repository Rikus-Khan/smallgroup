Luke 15

Lost, and found

A lost sheep, a lost coin, and two lost sons, told to men who grumbled that Jesus welcomed sinners.

Part one

Rejoice with me

Verses 1–10

The wrong crowd comes close to Jesus, the religious crowd complains, and Jesus answers with stories.

The complaint

“This man receiveth sinners”

Publicans were tax collectors for Rome, known as traitors and cheats. Sinners were people the whole town knew for their sin. They were coming close to Jesus “for to hear him.”

The Pharisees and scribes, the strict keepers and teachers of the law, murmured: they grumbled. To eat with a man was to accept him. A holy teacher, they thought, should keep his distance.

They meant it as an accusation. It's the best news in the Bible. He still receives sinners. That's why men like us are in this room.

One parable, three lost things

Jesus answers the complaint with one parable in three parts. Each time something is lost, someone searches, it's found, and there's a party.

1 of 100

a sheep (15:4)

1 of 10

a piece of silver (15:8)

1 of 2

a son (15:11)

Watch the numbers shrink: one in a hundred, one in ten, one in two. The loss gets more personal each time, until it's a father's own son. And every story ends the same way: found.

Until he find it

“What man of you”: Jesus puts the Pharisees in the shepherd's sandals. Any of you would go. The ninety-nine were left in open pasture while he went out into the rough country, and he kept looking until he found it.

A lost sheep often just lies down, scared and worn out, and won't move. So he picks it up and carries it home across his shoulders. No beating, no lecture: “rejoicing.”

Who are the ninety and nine?

The sheep didn't find its way home. It got found. The work was all the shepherd's. All the sheep did was get carried. That's how salvation works: Jesus came looking for us before we ever went looking for Him.

“All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all.”

Isaiah 53:6

And the “ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance”? There aren't any. “For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God” (Romans 3:23). Jesus is talking to men who thought they were in that group. The only people heaven can't celebrate are the ones who think they don't need finding.

A coin in the dust

A piece of silver was a Greek drachma, about a day's pay. To a poor woman, one in ten was a lot of money.

A village house had few windows and a dirt or stone floor, often with straw on it. So she lights her little clay lamp, her candle, in broad day, and sweeps every corner until it glints. A coin can't know it's lost, and it can't come back on its own. Somebody has to want it.

A party in heaven

The Pharisees grumbled when sinners came near. Heaven throws a party. That's the contrast Jesus is drawing: the people who should have been glad were angry, and the angels of God were rejoicing.

Notice “one sinner.” Not a crowd. One man turning around is enough for heaven to rejoice. When you turned to Christ, there was joy in heaven over you, by name.

Repenteth means to turn: a change of mind that changes direction. The sheep and the coin can't do that, so Jesus tells a third story about someone who can.

Part two

He came to himself

Verses 11–24

A son takes the money and runs, hits bottom in a pigpen, and heads home with a speech.

“Give me”

A son normally got his share when his father died. Asking for it early was close to saying, I wish you were dead. I want your money, not you. By the law, the elder son got a double share, so this younger son walked off with about a third of the estate (Deuteronomy 21:17).

2 sharesthe elder son
1 sharethe younger son
0left after “riotous living”

The father let him go. God doesn't chain men to the house. Then the son sold what he got, “gathered all together,” and went as far from home as he could: a far country, a land of Gentiles. Riotous living is wild, wasteful living. His substance, everything he had, went through his fingers.

All the way down

  1. “give me”(15:12)
  2. a far country(15:13)
  3. wasted his substance(15:13)
  4. spent all(15:14)
  5. in want(15:14)
  6. feed swine(15:15)
  7. no man gave unto him(15:16)

The money ran out right when the famine came. The friends ran out with the money. He joined himself, hired himself out, to a foreigner, who sent him to feed swine. For a Jew, pigs were unclean. There was no lower job.

He'd fain, gladly, have eaten the husks, the pods of the carob tree that farmers fed to pigs. Some of us know that place: the money gone, the friends gone, and wanting what nobody should want.

When he came to himself

15:17“I perish”

Starving in a pigpen, still telling himself it would turn around.

15:17He came to himself

He woke up. He saw the truth about where he was, and remembered his father's house.

15:18“I will arise”

Seeing it wasn't enough. He made a choice, and he got up and went.

A lot of men in recovery know that moment of clarity. You stop lying to yourself. That's what repenting looks like: “I have sinned”, no excuses, no blaming the famine, then turning around and going to the Father.

The father ran

He saw him a great way off, so he must have been watching the road. A man of standing in that culture didn't run. He'd have to hitch up his robe and show his legs. This father didn't care. He ran, threw his arms around his son's neck, and kissed him, pig smell and all.

The son never had to walk the whole way back alone. That's God the Father. He's not waiting with folded arms to see if you've suffered enough. He runs.

The speech he never finished

What he practised, 15:18–19

Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee, And am no more worthy to be called thy son: make me as one of thy hired servants.

What he got to say, 15:21

Father, I have sinned against heaven, and in thy sight, and am no more worthy to be called thy son.

He planned to earn his way back as a hired servant, a day labourer who worked for wages and went home at night. He got the confession out. He never got to the bargain. The father cut him off with a party.

Confession matters. Bargaining doesn't. You can't work your way back into the family. You come home as a son or not at all.

Dressed as a son

  1. The best robethe finest one in the house, maybe the father's own. Honour, covering his rags.
  2. A ringlikely a signet ring, used to seal family business. He belongs and he's trusted.
  3. Shoesslaves and hired men often went barefoot. Sons wore shoes. Not a servant.
  4. The fatted calfkept for a great feast. The whole village is invited.

Every gift answers the son's shame. He came home in rags and went in to the feast in a robe. That's what God does with a sinner who comes home: forgiven, clothed, and called His son.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Down, and back up

The son said “no more worthy to be called thy son.” The father said “this my son.” The father's word was the one that counted. Lost and dead is how God sees a man without Him. Found and alive is what He makes of the man who comes home.

Part three

He would not go in

Verses 25–32

The party is going strong. One man stands outside in the dark with his arms folded.

The father went out twice

The elder son was out working, like always. He came in from the field, heard musick and dancing, and had to ask a servant what was going on. Nobody had run to tell him. Maybe they knew how he'd take it.

Verse 20

He ran out

to the son who had wasted everything, while he was still a great way off.

Verse 28

He came out

to the son who was angry, and intreated him: pleaded with him to come in.

The father left his own party for both of them. He wants both sons at the table.

Listen to how he talks

The elder sonLo, these many years do I serve thee, neither transgressed I at any time thy commandment: and yet thou never gavest me a kid, that I might make merry with my friends: But as soon as this thy son was come… not “my brother”

The fatherSon, thou art ever with me, and all that I have is thine. It was meet that we should make merry, and be glad: for this thy brother was dead, and is alive again…

He says serve, like a slave counting hours, not a son. He won't call him brother. And he brings up the harlots, something nobody had told him. A kid is a young goat, a cheap meal. He'd lived next to all his father had, and never once enjoyed it.

Two lost sons

The younger son

Lost far from home

Broke every rule. Knew he was lost. Came home with nothing, and was welcomed in.

The elder son

Lost at home

Kept every rule. Never knew he was lost. Stood outside, angry, while the party went on.

Remember who Jesus was talking to (15:2). The younger son is the publicans and sinners. The elder son is the Pharisees and scribes, murmuring outside while sinners go in.

The story never says whether the elder son went in. Jesus left it open, for the Pharisees and for us. Both kinds of men are invited. Some of us have been the younger son. Some days we act like the elder one, when another man's comeback makes us bitter. The door is open to both.

Dead, and alive again

“He was lost, and is found.”

The three stories end in one place: a lost thing found, and a party. God is not a reluctant Father. He looks, He sweeps, He runs, and He rejoices.

“For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.”

Luke 19:10

If you've come home, you're His son, not a hired hand. If you're still in the far country, the road home is open today, and the Father is watching it.

Points to remember

  1. Sinners“This man receiveth sinners” 15:2They meant it as a complaint. It's the best news there is.
  2. Seeking“Until he find it” 15:4God goes looking for lost people, and carries them home.
  3. Joy“Joy … over one sinner that repenteth” 15:10One man turning around makes heaven glad.
  4. Rock bottom“When he came to himself” 15:17He saw the truth, owned his sin, and got up and went home.
  5. Welcome“His father … ran” 15:20The Father runs to meet us, and restores us as sons.
  6. The elder son“He was angry, and would not go in” 15:28A man can be lost at home. Grace is for rule-keepers too.

Question 1 of 6

Sinners

Why did the Pharisees murmur about Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

Because publicans and sinners came close to Him, and He welcomed them and ate with them. They thought a holy man should keep his distance. Jesus came for sinners, and He still receives them.

Question 2 of 6

Seeking

Who does the searching in the first two stories?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The owner, not the lost thing. The shepherd goes after the sheep until he finds it and carries it home. The woman lights a lamp and sweeps until she finds the coin. God goes looking for lost people first.

Question 3 of 6

Joy

What makes heaven rejoice?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

One sinner who repents: who turns around and comes to God. Not a crowd, just one. While the Pharisees grumbled, heaven was throwing a party.

Question 4 of 6

Rock bottom

What changed when the younger son came to himself?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He stopped lying to himself. He saw where he was, remembered his father's house, owned his sin with no excuses, and got up and went home. Seeing the truth turned into a choice, and the choice turned into walking.

Question 5 of 6

Welcome

How did the father receive his son?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He saw him far off, ran to him, hugged him and kissed him before the son said a word. Then he cut the bargain short, dressed him as a son with a robe, a ring and shoes, and threw a feast. That's how God receives a sinner who comes home.

Question 6 of 6

The elder son

Why wouldn't the elder son go in?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He was angry. He thought his years of service earned him more, and his brother deserved less. He served his father like a hired man, not a son, and wouldn't even call his brother “brother.” The father came out and pleaded with him too.
