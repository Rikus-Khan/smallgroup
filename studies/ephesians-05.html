Paul wrote this from a Roman prison, about AD 60 to 62, to the believers at Ephesus. The first half told them who they are in Christ. The second half tells them how to walk.

Chapter 4 said: put off the old man, put on the new. Chapter 5 shows what the new man looks like with the lights on: in his mouth, in his habits, with his time, and in his own house.

Part one

Walk in love

Verses 1–7

Children copy their father. So copy yours, and leave the old life where it belongs.

Like father, like son

“Followers” is mimētai, where we get mimic. A little boy walks like his dad, talks like his dad, even pretends to shave.

Some of us had a father we didn't want to copy. Here is one you can copy safely. You are His dear child, loved, not just tolerated.

What does He look like? Verse 2: Christ loved us, and gave himself for us. Love that costs you something. That's the walk.

A sweetsmelling savour

In the Old Testament, when an offering burned on the altar, the smoke went up as “a sweet savour unto the LORD” (Leviticus 1:9): a gift God gladly accepted.

Paul says the cross was that kind of gift. When Christ gave Himself for you, the Father accepted it fully. Nothing is left to pay. Your record does not cancel what He did.

And a life of love done out of thanks for that, Paul says elsewhere, smells good to God too (Philippians 4:18). Paying back what you owe, keeping your word, helping the next man: offerings God is glad to receive.

“Not convenient” means not fitting. It doesn't suit who you are now, like a man in a clean suit rolling in the mud. And “not be once named among you”: don't let it even be your reputation. You can name it to confess it. Just don't let it be the name people know you by.

What the mouth does instead

The old mouth

Dirty jokes and big talk

On the job site, at the bar, in the cell. You learned to talk that way to fit in.

The new mouth

Giving of thanks

Paul doesn't just say stop. He gives the mouth a new job. A thankful man has less room for filth.

You don't have to preach at the guys at work. Just don't join in, and let them hear you thank God for something. That alone sets you apart.

What Paul is saying, and what he isn't

A “whoremonger” lives in sexual sin, the way a fishmonger lives by fish. Paul describes a life ruled by these things, not a believer who fell yesterday and hates it. And greed sits right beside them: whatever you can't stop wanting more of has become a god.

Vain words (v. 6)

“It's no big deal”

Everybody does it. It's not hurting anybody. You only live once. Empty lines that make sin sound small.

God's word

“Such were some of you”

“…but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus” (1 Corinthians 6:11).

Part two

Children of light

Verses 8–17

You weren't just in the dark. You were the dark. Now you're light. Wake up and walk in it.

A new name

darkness → light in the Lord “Sometimes” is old English for once, at one time. That was then.

Paul doesn't say you were in the dark. He says you were darkness. And now you don't just have a little light. You are light, “in the Lord”: His light, shining through you.

So walk like it. Not to become light. You already are.

What light grows

  1. goodnesskind and generous, the opposite of using people
  2. righteousnessdoing what's right, at home and at work
  3. truthno more lies, no stories to keep straight

“Proving” means testing, finding out by trying. A new believer learns what pleases God the way a new hire learns the job: step by step, asking, watching, getting it wrong and getting it right.

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Bring it into the light

Sin grows best in the dark: the second phone, the hidden bottle, the money nobody knows about. “Reprove” means expose, bring it out where it can be seen.

Start with your own. Tell God the truth about it, and tell one brother you trust. Said out loud, a secret loses half its grip.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9). Where the secret was, there is light.

Wake up

asleepAwake thou that sleepest

Going through the motions, numb, half alive.

deadArise from the dead

God raises men who were “dead in trespasses and sins” (2:1).

morningChrist shall give thee light

He brings the light. You just get up.

“He saith” points back to Scripture. Many Bible teachers hear Isaiah 60:1 in it: “Arise, shine; for thy light is come, and the glory of the LORD is risen upon thee.” Some think the early Christians sang it as a hymn.

Buy the hours back

“Circumspectly” means looking all around, carefully, like a man crossing ice. “Redeeming” is buying back, like getting something out of the pawn shop.

Part three

Filled with the Spirit

Verses 18–21

What used to fill the empty place, and what fills it now.

What filled the hole

“Excess” is the same word family as the prodigal son's “riotous living” (Luke 15:13): a life poured out and wasted.

For some of us it was the bottle. For others, pills, porn, a woman, money, rage. Whatever it was, it filled the empty place for a night, and left it bigger by morning.

Paul doesn't only say stop. He says be filled with something else. The empty place was made for God. Nothing else ever fit.

About the wine

Drunkenness is plainly sin. For a man who has fought the bottle, this isn't a fine line to walk. Southern Baptists have long urged believers to leave alcohol alone, and for a man in recovery, that is the wise road, and the safe one.

“Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging: and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise.”

Proverbs 20:1

Notice the two go side by side: drunk with wine, filled with the Spirit. A drunk man is under the influence: something else is steering his mouth, his feet and his choices. Paul says: let the Spirit be the One steering.

What “filled” means

  • a second blessing for a fewevery believer received the Spirit the day he believed
  • a feeling, or a showit's seen in a changed life, not goose bumps
  • getting more of Himyou have all of Him; the question is whether He has all of you
  • keep on being filledyield to Him, every room, every day

“In whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

In Greek, “be filled” is a command that keeps going: keep on being filled. Yesterday's yielding doesn't cover today. Each morning: Lord, You lead today.

What overflows

Drink makes a man loud, sloppy, ungrateful and ready to fight. The Spirit makes him sing, thank, and give way. You can tell what a man is full of by what spills out of him.

Today: before you sleep, write down three things you're thankful for. “For all things”: even the hard ones God is using.

Part four

As Christ loved the church

Verses 22–33

A short word to wives, and a long, hard, hopeful word to husbands.

Who gets the longer talk

to wives

about 66 words

to husbands

about 164 words

Verse 21 sets the tone: every believer gives way to every other. Then Paul asks wives to follow their own husband's lead, willingly, “as unto the Lord.” Not every man's; her husband's. And never into sin: no one has to follow a husband into wrong, or stay under his fist.

Then he turns to the men, and talks two and a half times as long. We're all men here, so that's where we'll spend our time.

What being the head means

  • being the bossChrist led by washing feet
  • getting served“the Son of man came not to be ministered unto”
  • having the last wordor the loudest one
  • going first in giving yourself up“and gave himself for it”

The measure is not how she treats you. It's “even as Christ.” He loved the church before she loved Him back, when she was still dirty, and He paid with His life.

If you've failed here

Many of us have. Cheating, drinking the rent money, the words we can't take back, the years we weren't there. Some marriages are over. Some are hanging by a thread.

Look at what Christ does with a stained bride: He cleanses her, and presents her “not having spot, or wrinkle.” That's what He has done for you. A man learns to love like that by first being loved like that.

So the starting place isn't shame. It's grace. You can't undo the past, but a forgiven man can start loving the right way today.

Start today

Christ's love was not a feeling. It was a string of things He did. Here are four you can do.

  1. ✓Ask what she needs from you todayand listen without defending yourself
  2. ✓Keep one promise, however smalltrust is rebuilt one kept word at a time
  3. ✓Apologize with no “but”“I was wrong. I'm sorry.” Full stop.
  4. ✓If it's over, love still pays and prayspay the support, speak well of her to the kids, pray for her

Two words for a husband

nourisheth

Feed her, and help her grow

The same word Paul uses for raising children in 6:4, “bring them up.” Provide, and help her become all God made her to be.

cherisheth

Keep her warm

The word means to warm, like a mother holding a baby close (1 Thessalonians 2:7). Tender, safe, never cold.

No man has to be told to feed himself when he's hungry or come in out of the cold. Paul says: care for her that naturally. She's not your opponent. She's part of you.

A great mystery

Verse 31 quotes Genesis 2:24, the first wedding. A “mystery” is a secret God has now made known.

The secret: from the start, marriage was a picture of something bigger, Christ and His people.

Single, divorced, or widowed, you're not left out. You are part of the bride He gave Himself for: “the marriage of the Lamb is come” (Revelation 19:7).

Paul sums it up

Every one of you, in particular

Love your wife as yourself

Not husbands in general. You. In particular.

And the wife

Respect your husband

“Reverence” here means honour and respect.

Each one does his own part, whether or not the other does. And it starts the way this chapter started: walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us.

Points to remember

  1. Copying God“Be ye therefore followers of God, as dear children” 5:1Copy your Father the way a boy copies his dad: walk in the love Christ showed when He gave Himself for you.
  2. Sin“Let it not be once named among you, as becometh saints” 5:3Sexual sin, filth and greed don't fit who you are now. Confess them; don't let them be your name.
  3. Light“Now are ye light in the Lord: walk as children of light” 5:8You were darkness. Now you are light. Bring the secrets out, wake up, and buy back today.
  4. Filled“Be not drunk with wine… but be filled with the Spirit” 5:18The bottle filled the hole for a night. The Spirit already lives in you; yield to Him every day.
  5. Husbands“Even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it” 5:25Lead by going first in giving yourself up. If you've failed, Christ cleanses, and you can start again today.
  6. The mystery“I speak concerning Christ and the church” 5:32Marriage is a picture of Christ and His people, a love every believer is part of.

Question 1 of 6

Copying God

How do we become “followers of God, as dear children”?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Answer

By copying our Father the way a loved child copies his dad. We look at how Christ loved us and gave Himself for us, and we love others the same way, at a cost to ourselves.

Question 2 of 6

Sin

Why should these sins “not be once named among you”?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

They don't fit saints. They mark a life ruled by sin, and greed is idolatry. We were like that, but we've been washed. Confess them to God; don't let them be your name.

Question 3 of 6

Light

What does it mean that we were darkness, but now are light?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Answer

We didn't just live in the dark; we were part of it. In Christ we are now light, His light. So we walk in it: we bring our secrets into the open, we wake up, and we use today well.

Question 4 of 6

Filled

What does it mean to be filled with the Spirit instead of drunk with wine?

What verses tell us about this?

“In whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

“This I say then, Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh.”

Galatians 5:16

Answer

A drunk man is steered by the drink. A Spirit-filled man lets the Spirit steer. Every believer already has the Spirit; being filled means yielding to Him, again every day, and it shows in singing, thanks, and giving way to others.

Question 5 of 6

Husbands

How is a husband to love his wife?

What verses tell us about this?

“Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.”

John 15:13

Answer

Even as Christ loved the church: going first, giving himself up for her, feeding her and keeping her warm. If we've failed, Christ cleanses us too, and we start again today.

Question 6 of 6

The mystery

What great mystery does marriage picture?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let us be glad and rejoice, and give honour to him: for the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready.”

Revelation 19:7

Answer

Christ and the church. A man and wife made one flesh is a small picture of Christ joined to His people. Every believer, married or not, is part of that bride.
