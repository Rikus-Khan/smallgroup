James 4

Where the fights come from, and how to stand

James doesn't flatter anyone. He says where our wars really start, and then he gives a short, workable way out: submit, resist, draw near. A man who knows he is weak can use it today.

Part one

The war inside

Verses 1–6

James asks the question every man in recovery has asked: why do I keep fighting, and keep losing?

The war starts inside

  1. I want itthe craving
  2. I haven't got it“ye lust, and have not”
  3. I fight for it“ye kill, and desire to have”
  4. I can't get it“and cannot obtain”
  5. Still empty“yet ye have not”

Lusts is a strong old word for pleasures: the thing I'm sure I need. The fights outside (with the wife, the boss, the guy at the next bunk) start with a war “in your members,” in the parts of us that want. Many of us know how it feels to chase something and end up emptier than we started.

James says this to believers, “among you,” and he isn't shaming anyone. He is showing where the fire starts, because a man who knows that can put it out there.

Grab it, or ask for it

What we do

Grab it myself

Want it, scheme for it, fight for it. Then, when we do pray, we ask “amiss” (wrongly): the prayer is only a way to get the thing that feeds the craving. “That ye may consume it upon your lusts.” Still empty.

What we can do

Ask the Giver

“Ye have not, because ye ask not.” God is not hiding the good things. Ask Him for what a good Father would give: strength, a clean day, honest work, peace. And ask with an open hand: if the answer is no, it's because He loves us.

“And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us:”

1 John 5:14

Which side am I on?

Making the world's way my best friendLiving as if God isn't in the room
→
meansI've picked a side against Him.

“Adulterers and adulteresses” is a hard word, and it is a picture the Bible uses again and again: God is the faithful husband, and a heart that runs after other loves is unfaithful. Hosea lived that picture out. The world doesn't mean people (God so loved the world). It means the way of living that leaves Him out: get it, take it, numb it, look good.

This isn't about being weird or cutting off every old friend overnight. It is about whose side you are on when it counts. And for many of us, honestly, the old scene is where the old habits live, so loving those people from a distance may be the wisest thing we can do.

Grace runs downhill

“Resisteth” is an army word: God lines up against the proud. And the proud man isn't always loud. It can be a man sure he's got this, or sure he is beyond help. Both say “I know better than God.”

But first, “he giveth more grace.” More than our sin, more than we could use up, and it always goes to the low place. If you feel like you're at the bottom today, you're standing right where it pools.

Part two

Submit, resist, draw near

Verses 7–10

A plan for the minute temptation shows up, and a way back for the man who has already fallen.

When temptation comes

  1. Step 1Submit“Lord, I'm Yours. Not my way.”

    Before anything else, line up under Him, like a soldier under orders. You can't do the second step without this one.

    Whose side am I on? His.
  2. Step 2ResistSay no, and move your feet.

    Leave the room. Hang up. Put the phone down. Walk out of the store. Don't stay and argue with it.

    “He will flee from you.”
  3. Step 3Draw nearPray. Call a brother. Open the Book.

    Don't stand there alone. Get closer to God and closer to people who love Him.

    “He will draw nigh to you.”

Look at the order. Most of us try to resist first, on our own strength, and lose. James puts submit first. And “resist” means to stand your ground, not to win a fight alone. Peter says the same:

“Whom resist stedfast in the faith, knowing that the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren that are in the world.”

1 Peter 5:9

A promise, and an honest word

The promise is real: the devil does not outlast a man who stands with God. He “will flee.” Jesus Himself was tempted, and Luke ends the story this way:

“And when the devil had ended all the temptation, he departed from him for a season.”

Luke 4:13

“For a season.” He left, and he came back. So expect the craving to return, and don't read its return as proof you are failing. Each time, it's the same three steps.

And if you have already fallen, the steps still work. Draw nigh to God is an invitation to a man who has just failed, and the next verse tells you how to come.

Sorrow, not shame

Shame

“I'm no good”

Hides. Keeps it secret. Stays away from God and from the group. Then runs back to whatever numbs it. Shame is where a lot of relapses start.

Mourning that God can use

“I did it, and I'm sorry”

Says it out loud and means it. Hands are what I did; hearts are what I want. “Double minded” means two souls: one for God, one for the craving. Both get brought to Him to be cleaned.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

That is what to do with regret: bring it, don't carry it. Jesus already paid.

Humble yourselves, and he shall lift you up.

Notice who does the lifting. You don't have to prop up your own name or prove you have changed. You get low; He lifts. Peter says it this way:

“Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time:”

1 Peter 5:6

“In due time” is His time, not ours. Today's job is just the first half: get honest and get low. Your story doesn't end at the bottom, because that is where He does His lifting.

Part three

Humble in everyday life

Verses 11–17

Three everyday tests: how I talk about people, how I plan my life, and what I do about the good I already know.

Whose chair is that?

  • running a brother downgossip and put-downs when he isn't there
  • handing down my verdict“he'll never change,” “he's a fake”
  • sitting in the judge's seatthat chair belongs to the one Lawgiver
  • leaving the verdict to Him“who is able to save”: He can do what I can't

In jail and in the rooms, people size each other up all day: who's real, who's faking. James says that's not our job. This doesn't mean never saying a hard thing to a brother, because James himself will end this letter with going after a man who has wandered. The difference is whether I'm talking about him to feel big or to him to help him home.

A vapour

James isn't against plans or work: “buy and sell, and get gain” is just business. The trouble is planning as if tomorrow belongs to me and God isn't in it. Life is like the steam off a cup of coffee: real, warm, and gone before you notice.

That isn't meant to scare us. It means today is the day we actually have. “I'll get right with God later.” “I'll quit next Monday.” Later isn't promised.

“If the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that.”

This is not a magic phrase to tack onto a plan. It is a way of holding the plan, with an open hand: make it, work it, and let God have the final say.

“Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth.”

Proverbs 27:1

Think of a court date, a job application, a release date, a reconciliation you're hoping for. Do your part. Then say, honestly, “Lord, if You will.” A man who does that sleeps better, because the outcome was never really in his hands.

Verse 16 gives the opposite: “ye rejoice in your boastings: all such rejoicing is evil.” Boasting is just pride with a plan.

The sin of the good left undone

What we usually count

The wrong I did

The drink. The lie. The fight. The theft. The night I'd like to forget. We know these are sins, and they are the ones that fill our regrets.

What James adds

The good I knew to do, and didn't

The call I never made. The debt I didn't pay. The apology I put off. The man I walked past. “To him it is sin.”

Everyone has both lists, and the grace of 1 John 1:9 covers both. James isn't trying to bury you under a new list. He is pointing you at a short one for today: one good thing you already know you should do. Do it today.

Points to remember

  1. Fights“Come they not hence, even of your lusts that war in your members?” 4:1The war starts inside, in what I want. Name it there.
  2. Asking“Ye have not, because ye ask not.” 4:2Ask God, and ask for what He would give, not for what feeds the craving.
  3. The world“The friendship of the world is enmity with God.” 4:4It's about whose side I'm on, not about hating people.
  4. Grace“God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.” 4:6Grace runs downhill. The low place is where it pools.
  5. Temptation“Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you.” 4:7-8Submit first, then say no and move, then get close to God and to your brothers.
  6. Tomorrow“To him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin.” 4:17Hold tomorrow with an open hand, and do the good you know today.

Question 1 of 6

Fights

Where do our fights and cravings really come from?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

From our own wants, the cravings that war in us. We want, we grab, we fight, and we still end up empty. James says this to believers without shaming them, so we can find the fire where it starts and bring it to God.

Question 2 of 6

Asking

Why do we ask and not receive?

What verses tell us about this?

“And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us:”

1 John 5:14

Answer

Sometimes we don't ask at all. And sometimes we ask wrongly, for something that only feeds the craving. Ask God for what a good Father would give, and trust Him with a no.

Question 3 of 6

The world

What does it mean to be a friend of the world?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It means making the world's way of living, with God left out, our closest companion. It's not hating people, because God loves the world. It's picking a side. The Bible calls that unfaithfulness, and the cure is to come back to Him.

Question 4 of 6

Grace

Who gets more grace from God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Surely he scorneth the scorners: but he giveth grace unto the lowly.”

Proverbs 3:34

Answer

The humble. God sets Himself against the proud, but He gives more grace to the man who is low and honest. Grace runs downhill, so the man at the bottom is right where it pools.

Question 5 of 6

Temptation

What three things do we do when temptation comes?

What verses tell us about this?

“Whom resist stedfast in the faith, knowing that the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren that are in the world.”

1 Peter 5:9

Answer

Submit to God first. Then resist: say no and move your feet. Then draw near: pray, call a brother, open the Book. God promises the devil will flee and that He will draw near to you. And if you've already fallen, the same steps bring you back.

Question 6 of 6

Tomorrow

What should we say about tomorrow, and what is sin for the man who knows to do good?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“If the Lord will.” Make your plans, and hold them with an open hand, because life is a vapour and tomorrow isn't promised. And when you know the good you should do and don't do it, James calls that sin too. Today, do the one good thing you know.
