Genesis 30

Two sisters at war, and a boy named Joseph

Rachel envies Leah, Leah keeps score, and four women, one husband and a houseful of sons turn into a contest. Then God remembers Rachel, and Jacob starts building his own flocks.

Part one

A house at war

Verses 1–13

Two sisters, two maids, and a contest over who can give Jacob the most sons.

“Give me children, or else I die”

RachelGive me children, or else I die.

JacobAm I in God's stead, who hath withheld from thee the fruit of the womb? anger

Rachel was loved, and she was still miserable. She had the husband, she had the beauty, and she looked at her sister's children and could think of nothing else. That is what envy does: it makes you blind to what you have.

Jacob is right about one thing. Only God opens and closes the womb. But his answer is cold, and his wife is in tears. A true answer, said without kindness, still leaves a person alone. Many of us have won an argument and lost the person.

“For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.”

James 3:16

The same plan as Hagar

“And Sarai said unto Abram, Behold now, the LORD hath restrained me from bearing: I pray thee, go in unto my maid; it may be that I may obtain children by her. And Abram hearkened to the voice of Sarai.”

Genesis 16:2

Jacob's own grandmother Sarah had already tried this: if God won't, I will. It ended in jealousy and a mother and son driven into the desert (chapter 16 and 21). Now Rachel does it, and then Leah does it too.

Notice what these plans did to the maids. Bilhah and Zilpah are handed over like property, and Genesis never records what they thought. The Bible calls them wives (4, 9) and counts their sons, so God does not forget them. But the plan was a human one, and people were used to carry it out.

When we are waiting on God and it's taking too long, we make a plan of our own. It always costs somebody. Wait, and ask him, and tell a brother in Christ you are tempted to take the shortcut.

  1. Dan“he judged” (Rachel: “God hath judged me”)
  2. Naphtali“my wrestling” (“I have prevailed”)

Dan, Naphtali, Gad, Asher: sons of the maids, named in the heat of the contest. “I have prevailed” sounds like a woman who has won a match. A child is not a trophy, and the mothers were reaching for something they thought would fix their lives.

Out of all this, though, God builds a nation. These four boys will each give a tribe. Their mothers' motives were mixed, and he used them anyway. He does not wait for pure motives.

Part two

Mandrakes, and a boy named Joseph

Verses 14–24

A trade over a plant, and then God remembers Rachel.

A husband for a plant

Reuben is a small boy (the Bible doesn't give his age). He finds mandrakes in the wheat harvest and takes them home to his mother. In the ancient world they were thought to help a woman conceive.

LeahIs it a small matter that thou hast taken my husband? and wouldest thou take away my son's mandrakes also?

RachelTherefore he shall lie with thee to night for thy son's mandrakes.

Read it slowly. Two women are bargaining over a man as if he were something to swap, and neither is happy. Leah says the thing she has been carrying for years: you took my husband. Jacob doesn't say a word. He comes in from the field and is told which tent he is sleeping in.

“God hearkened unto Leah.”

Not the mandrakes. Rachel got the plant, and she wasn't the one who conceived. Leah did, because God listened to her.

That is the lesson of the mandrakes: the remedies people reach for (the trick, the bargain, the bottle) cannot do what only God does. When a thing finally happened, the Bible says who did it.

Leah's last two sons were Issachar (“hire, reward”) and Zebulun (“dwelling”; “a good dowry”). She is still saying now my husband will dwell with me. Then a daughter, Dinah. We will meet her again.

Joseph: “he adds”

יוֹסֵף said Yoseph “he adds” (“The LORD shall add to me another son”)

“God remembered Rachel.” That does not mean he had forgotten. It means he now acted. Rachel's pain: reproach, the shame of a childless wife, was lifted.

Look at her prayer in the name. She did not say “at last, enough.” She said add: Lord, another son. Rachel's faith ends where her story will: she dies giving birth to that second son (35:16-18), so her hope was answered and it cost her.

And this baby will grow up to save the whole family (50:20). The child of the long wait and the shortcuts becomes the one through whom God keeps Jacob's household alive.

Eleven sons, from four mothers, in the middle of a war. They will be the twelve tribes of Israel. The twelfth, Benjamin, is still to come. Judah (the crown) and Levi (the lamp) both came from Leah, the wife nobody wanted.

Part three

Jacob's flocks

Verses 25–43

Jacob wants to go home. Laban wants him to stay, and the two of them out-scheme each other.

Twenty years in Laban's house

  1. Years 1 to 7for Rachelthe wedding with Leah
  2. Years 8 to 14for Rachel againthe sons are born
  3. The end of fourteenJoseph is born“Send me away”
  4. Six more yearsfor the flockswages, rods and flocks (30:25-43)

“Thus have I been twenty years in thy house; I served thee fourteen years for thy two daughters, and six years for thy cattle: and thou hast changed my wages ten times.”

Genesis 31:41

By that count Joseph was born at the end of the fourteen years, and Jacob asked to go home. Laban had an answer ready.

“The LORD hath blessed me for thy sake”

Laban finally says it out loud. Fourteen years of Jacob's work had made him rich, and he knew whose God had done it.

God promised Abraham: I will bless them that bless thee (Genesis 12:3). Here is that promise at work: a man's boss is blessed by his faithful work. Jacob wasn't an easy man, and he had sins. But because God was with him, the people around him prospered.

“And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men;”

Colossians 3:23

Think of your own job. A boss who has been shortchanging you, a long shift, a wage that isn't fair. Do it “as to the Lord” anyway. Your work is a witness to the people around you, whether they say so or not.

The deal, and the cheat

The deal

Jacob's wages

Laban's flock was mostly plain. Jacob asks only for the unusual ones: the speckled and spotted goats and the brown sheep. It sounds like a small wage, and Laban says yes.

The cheat

The same day

Laban took out every animal that was striped, spotted or speckled and gave them to his sons, three days' journey away. He left Jacob with only the plain ones to breed from.

This is the Laban of chapter 29 again: a handshake, a good word, and a trick in the same afternoon. A man who has been cheated by a boss knows exactly what this feels like.

Jacob says: “so shall my righteousness answer for me.” he is saying that an honest man's record will speak for him. He had every reason to say so; he had worked for years without a wage.

The rods: clever, or God?

Jacob peeled stripes into green branches and laid them in the watering troughs. Then the flocks bred striped, speckled and spotted young, and his wages grew.

Don't take it as a farming method. Chapter 31 tells us what he later understood: the flocks were not growing because of the sticks.

“Thus God hath taken away the cattle of your father, and given them to me.”

Genesis 31:9

There it is. Jacob did something clever, and he did it hopefully. But the one who gave him the flocks was God. A man who has been cheated by his boss for twenty years and still ends up well off has something better than rods: I am with thee.

The last line of the chapter: “the man increased exceedingly.” The man who left home with a staff now has camels, servants and flocks. He didn't get there by being a better man than Laban. God kept his word.

Question 1 of 6

Envy

What does envy do to a family?

What verses tell us about this?

“For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.”

James 3:16

Answer

It turns sisters into rivals, children into a scoreboard, and a husband into something to bargain over. Rachel had a husband who loved her and could see only what her sister had. If you are comparing yourself to someone, name it to God and thank him for what you have.

Question 2 of 6

Our own plans

Why did Rachel and Leah give their maids to Jacob?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Sarai said unto Abram, Behold now, the LORD hath restrained me from bearing: I pray thee, go in unto my maid; it may be that I may obtain children by her. And Abram hearkened to the voice of Sarai.”

Genesis 16:2

Answer

To win. Rachel could not have children and Leah had stopped, and each wanted more sons than the other. It was the Hagar plan again: if God is slow, do it yourself. It cost the maids, the sisters and the home. If you are tempted to force something God hasn't given yet, tell your brothers in Christ before you act.

Question 3 of 6

God opens

Did the mandrakes give Rachel a child?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. Rachel traded for the mandrakes, but Leah was the one who conceived, and Rachel's child did not come until much later, when “God remembered Rachel.” God opens and God closes. A trick, a remedy or a bottle cannot do what only he does.

Question 4 of 6

Hope

What does the name Joseph say about Rachel's hope?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Joseph means “he adds.” Rachel was thankful for the son God gave her, and she prayed for another. A hope that looks forward doesn't stop at being rescued: it asks for more of the Lord. The shame she carried had been lifted, and she was not finished asking.

Question 5 of 6

Blessing

Who was blessed because of Jacob's work?

What verses tell us about this?

“And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men;”

Colossians 3:23

Answer

Laban, the man who was cheating him. Laban said so himself: “the LORD hath blessed me for thy sake.” A believer's honest work blesses the people around him, even a hard boss. Do today's job as to the Lord.

Question 6 of 6

Cleverness

Who made Jacob's flocks grow, the rods or God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thus God hath taken away the cattle of your father, and given them to me.”

Genesis 31:9

Answer

God. Jacob did a clever thing, but he says himself in the next chapter that God gave him the flocks. Don't build a method out of the rods. Use your head, work hard, and know where the blessing comes from.
