Luke 18

God be merciful to me a sinner

A widow who won't quit, two men at prayer, little children, a rich ruler who walks away sorrowful, and a blind beggar who won't be quiet.

Part one

Men ought always to pray

Verses 1–14

Two stories about prayer: one about not giving up, one about who God hears.

A widow who wouldn't quit

The widowAvenge me of mine adversary.

The judge(nothing)
“he would not for a while,” so she kept coming

The judge, to himselfThough I fear not God, nor regard man; yet because this widow troubleth me, I will avenge her.

Jesus gives the point first: “always to pray, and not to faint.” To faint is to lose heart and quit. Avenge me means get me justice.

How much more God

A widow in those days had no husband to stand up for her and likely no money to pay a bribe. All she had was the door, and she kept coming to it. Jesus isn't saying God is like that judge. He's saying God is nothing like him.

The unjust judge

Gave in to be rid of her

Feared not God, cared nothing for her. Helped only so she'd stop bothering him.

God

Hears His own

They are His “own elect,” His own people, crying “day and night.” He is patient with them, and He will see them righted.

Keep praying on the days nothing seems to change. The question isn't whether God will answer. It's Jesus' question: “shall he find faith on the earth?” Will He find us still praying?

“And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not.”

Galatians 6:9

Two men went up to pray

A Pharisee was the model religious man. A publican collected taxes for Rome, often cheating his own people. Everyone listening would expect God to hear the first man.

The Pharisee, “with himself”

“God, I thank thee, that I am not as other men are … I fast twice in the week, I give tithes of all that I possess.”

The publican, “afar off”

“God be merciful to me a sinner.”

He did more than the Law asked. The Law set one fast a year, on the Day of Atonement; he fasted twice a week. He tithed on everything. But his prayer was really about himself, and he looked down on the man at the back.

Comparing ourselves to a worse man always makes us look good. God doesn't grade on that curve.

“Be merciful”

ἱλάσθητι said hilasthēti “be merciful; let my sin be covered”

It's the word family of the mercy seat, the lid of the ark where the blood was sprinkled on the Day of Atonement. He couldn't lift his eyes, he beat his chest, and he asked for the one thing a sinner can ask for: mercy, his sin covered by a sacrifice. And he says it of himself, not the man in front: me a sinner.

“The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.”

Psalm 51:17

Went home justified

Justified means declared right with God: the case closed, not guilty. The cheat went home that way. The good man didn't.

The man who lifts himself up gets brought down. The man who goes low before God gets lifted up. Nobody walks into God's favour on his record. We come in on His mercy, by faith.

“Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:”

Romans 5:1

Part two

What shall I do?

Verses 15–30

Babies who can do nothing are welcomed. A rich man who has done everything goes away sad.

As a little child

The disciples thought Jesus was too busy and too important for babies. Suffer means let, allow. Jesus called the little ones to Himself.

  • earn itan infant can't work for anything
  • deserve ithe has no record to show
  • bargain for ithe has nothing to trade
  • “receive”he simply takes what he's given

That's the only way in. A man has to stop bringing his résumé and come with empty hands, trusting the One who offers it. It's the same lesson as the publican, and it's the setup for the next man, who comes asking what he can do.

“All these have I kept”

“Why callest thou me good?” Jesus isn't denying He is good. He's making the man think: if only God is good, what is he saying about Jesus, and what is he assuming about himself?

  1. ✓Do not commit adulteryhe says he kept itNo. 7
  2. ✓Do not killhe says he kept itNo. 6
  3. ✓Do not steal · Do not bear false witnesshe says he kept themNos. 8, 9
  4. ✓Honour thy father and thy motherhe says he kept itNo. 5
  5. ✗Thou shalt have no other gods before me.Exodus 20:3, the one Jesus is about to testNo. 1

Jesus listed the commandments about other people. The man ticked them all. Next, Jesus goes to the first one.

Yet lackest thou one thing.

His money was his god. Jesus put His finger right on it: let it go, and “come, follow me.” He couldn't. He went away very sorrowful, and Jesus watched him go.

Jesus doesn't tell every man to sell everything. In the very next chapter, Zacchaeus gives half. But He does ask every man to let go of whatever has his heart in place of God. For one man it's money. For another it's a bottle, a pill, a woman, a grudge, his pride.

A camel was the biggest animal in the land. A needle's eye is the smallest hole you can think of. Jesus picks the most impossible picture He can, on purpose.

Who then can be saved?

People took riches as a sign of God's blessing. If a man like that can't get in, who can?

“With men”

Impossible

No man can save himself: not by money, not by keeping rules, not by trying harder.

“With God”

Possible

Salvation is God's work, offered to anyone who will come to Him by faith. Even a rich man. Even you.

Peter says, “we have left all.” Jesus promises that no man loses by following Him. He gets “manifold more” now, a family of believers far bigger than what he left, and everlasting life to come. This isn't a promise of riches: Mark adds that it comes “with persecutions” (Mark 10:30).

This is the third time Jesus has told them (9:22, 9:44). He walked toward the cross with His eyes open. “They understood none of these things”: it was hid from them until after the resurrection, when He opened it to them (24:45).

Nigh unto Jericho

Jericho sat in the Jordan valley, the last town before the steep road up to Jerusalem: about 17 miles, climbing about 3,300 feet. Pilgrims came this way to the Passover, so the road was crowded.

Beggars sat by that road for the traffic. Mark gives this man's name: Bartimaeus (Mark 10:46).

The disciples had eyes and couldn't see what Jesus was telling them. Now a blind man is about to see who Jesus is.

So much the more

The blind manJesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me.

Those in front(rebuked him, “that he should hold his peace”)

The blind man, louderThou Son of David, have mercy on me.

The crowd said “Jesus of Nazareth.” He said “Son of David”, the King promised from David's line. He saw who Jesus was before he could see at all.

Told to be quiet, he cried louder. When the old crowd or your own head says stop crying out to Jesus, do what he did.

“Jesus stood.” On His way to the cross, with a crowd pushing, He stopped for one beggar. Then He asked, “What wilt thou that I shall do unto thee?” He knew. He wanted the man to ask. Tell Him plainly what you need.

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Question 1 of 6

Prayer

Why did Jesus tell the parable of the widow and the unjust judge?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To teach that men ought always to pray and not lose heart. If a judge who cared for no one finally gave the widow justice, how much more will God hear His own people who cry to Him day and night.

Question 2 of 6

Justified

Which man went down to his house justified, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The publican. The Pharisee trusted in himself and looked down on others. The publican owned that he was a sinner and asked only for mercy. God lifts up the man who humbles himself.

Question 3 of 6

Like a child

How must a man receive the kingdom of God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As a little child: not by earning or bargaining, but by simply receiving it, trusting the One who gives it. Whoever won't come that way can't come in at all.

Question 4 of 6

One thing

What did the rich ruler still lack?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He lacked a heart that put God first. His riches had God's place, so Jesus told him to let them go and follow Him. He wouldn't, and he went away very sorrowful.

Question 5 of 6

Possible

When they asked, “Who then can be saved?”, what did Jesus answer?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God:”

Ephesians 2:8

Answer

“The things which are impossible with men are possible with God.” No man can save himself, but God can save anyone who comes to Him by faith.

Question 6 of 6

Mercy

What did the blind man by Jericho keep crying, and what did Jesus do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Thou Son of David, have mercy on me,” louder every time they told him to be quiet. Jesus stood still, called him, asked what he wanted, and gave him his sight. He followed Jesus, glorifying God.
