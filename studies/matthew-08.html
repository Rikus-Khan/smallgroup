Down the mountain, across the lake

The sermon is over. Jesus comes down the hill with the crowds behind Him, and walks into Capernaum, His home base on the north shore (4:13).

That evening He tells the disciples to cross to “the other side.” A storm hits them on the way over.

They land on the east shore, Gentile country, where the hills drop steeply to the water. Mark and Luke call it the country of the Gadarenes, after the main town of the area. Matthew names it after the Gergesenes who lived there.

First His words, then His works

In chapters 5 to 7, Matthew showed us Jesus' authority in what He said. In chapters 8 and 9, he shows it in what Jesus did: ten miracles, one after another. Five of them are today.

  • A leper cleansed8:2
  • The centurion's servant8:5
  • Peter's wife's mother8:14
  • The storm stilled8:26
  • Two men set free8:28
  • A man sick of the palsy9:2
  • The ruler's daughter9:18
  • A woman made whole9:20
  • Two blind men9:27
  • A dumb man speaks9:32

Watch who He helps: a leper nobody would touch, a Roman soldier, a sick woman in bed, scared fishermen, and two wild men living in a graveyard. Nobody in this chapter is too unclean, too foreign, or too far gone.

Part one

If thou wilt

Verses 1–13

An outcast on his knees, and a Roman officer who understood authority.

The leper touched

The law said a leper must cry “Unclean, unclean” and “dwell alone” (Leviticus 13:45–46). He may not have felt a human hand in years.

He knew Jesus could. Would He? “If thou wilt.” Jesus touched him, “moved with compassion” (Mark 1:41).

Some of us feel untouchable. Jesus doesn't keep His distance. Touching a leper should have made Him unclean; instead, the leper was made clean.

A man under authority

A centurion was an officer over about a hundred soldiers. He was a Gentile, an outsider, and a man of the occupying power. Luke adds that the Jewish elders spoke up for him: “For he loveth our nation, and he hath built us a synagogue” (Luke 7:5). “Palsy” means paralysis.

his commandergives an orderthe centurion“Go”a soldier“he goeth”
Jesus“speak the word only”the sickness“shall be healed”

He understood how authority works. He didn't need to be there in person to get something done. He just said the word. He saw that Jesus had that kind of authority over sickness. Distance didn't matter. Jesus' word was enough.

And he came humble: “I am not worthy.” A powerful man, asking for a servant.

A seat at the table

“From the east and west”

Outsiders welcomed

Gentiles from every direction, sitting at the feast with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. They get in the same way the centurion did: by faith in Jesus.

“The children of the kingdom”

Insiders shut out

People who assumed they were in because of their family and religion, but who never trusted the King when He came.

“Outer darkness… weeping and gnashing of teeth” is Jesus' own description of hell: shut out from God's light, with grief and bitter regret. He says it plainly, because He doesn't want anyone to go there.

Part two

He bare our sicknesses

Verses 14–17

A fever in Peter's house, a town at the door, and a prophecy kept.

Raised up to serve

Jesus“touched her hand”the fever“left her”Peter's wife's mother“arose, and ministered”them

Peter was a married man, and his wife's mother lived with them in Capernaum. Peter's house became Jesus' base in town.

Mark tells us it was a sabbath (Mark 1:21, 29–32). At sundown the sabbath ended, and the whole town brought its sick to this door (v. 16).

A fever usually leaves you weak for days. This one left her well enough to get up and serve dinner right away. That's a complete healing.

And it's the pattern for every one of us. Jesus lifts a man up, and the first thing he does with his new strength is serve. Recovery isn't only getting well. It's getting well enough to help somebody else.

What Isaiah saw

Isaiah 53:4

Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted.

Matthew 8:17

That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet, saying, Himself took our infirmities, and bare our sicknesses.

Isaiah's Hebrew words mean sicknesses and pains, and Matthew saw them come true as Jesus healed. But Isaiah 53 goes on to the cross, and something heavier:

“Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed.”

1 Peter 2:24

God doesn't promise every believer healing from every sickness now. He promises forgiveness now and a new body at the resurrection. Every healing is a preview.

Part three

What manner of man is this?

Verses 18–27

Two men who almost followed, and twelve men in a sinking boat.

Count the cost

A scribeMaster, I will follow thee whithersoever thou goest.

JesusThe foxes have holes, and the birds of the air have nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head.

A scribe was a respected teacher of the law, making a big promise on the spot. Jesus told him the truth: following Me doesn't come with a soft bed.

This is the first time in Matthew that Jesus calls Himself “the Son of man,” His favorite name for Himself, from Daniel's vision of the King of an everlasting kingdom: “one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven” (Daniel 7:13). That King had nowhere to sleep.

“Suffer me first”

A discipleLord, suffer me first to go and bury my father.
“first”

JesusFollow me; and let the dead bury their dead.

“Suffer me” means let me. Many think his father hadn't died yet: let me wait until he's gone, maybe years from now, then I'll follow.

“Let the dead bury their dead”: let the spiritually dead take care of that business. You come and find life.

We all know “first.” First let me get my money right. First, one more weekend. Jesus doesn't take second place. “Follow me,” today.

Asleep in the storm

The Greek word for “tempest” also means an earthquake, a violent shaking. Winds drop into the lake's bowl of hills with little warning. Fishermen who knew that lake were scared.

Jesus was truly a man, tired enough to sleep through it. They woke Him: “Master, carest thou not that we perish?” (Mark 4:38).

Some nights it feels like He's asleep while your boat fills. They were in that storm because they obeyed Him, and He was in the boat the whole time.

A great calm

He spoke to the wind and the water the way you'd speak to a dog that's barking: “rebuked.” And they obeyed at once. Not a slow settling down. A great calm.

“He maketh the storm a calm, so that the waves thereof are still.”

Psalm 107:29

Every Jew knew only God commands the sea. So they asked the right question: “What manner of man is this?” He is a man who sleeps when he's tired, and God who tells the storm to stop. Notice He calmed them first, then the sea.

Part four

Among the tombs

Verses 28–34

Two men no one could pass, and one word: “Go.”

What the devils knew

The tombs were caves cut into the hillside. To a Jew, a graveyard was unclean. These men lived with the dead, so violent that nobody would take that road. Matthew mentions two men. Mark and Luke tell the story of the one who did the talking (Mark 5, Luke 8).

  • Who He is“thou Son of God”
  • That He's in chargethey beg Him for permission (v. 31)
  • That judgment is coming“before the time”

The demons had better theology than the crowds, and it didn't save them. “the devils also believe, and tremble” (James 2:19). Knowing facts about Jesus isn't the same as trusting Him.

Demons are real, and the Bible doesn't treat them as a joke. But watch who's afraid of whom on this beach. They're the ones trembling.

One word: “Go.”

each dot is 50 swine, “about two thousand” in all (Mark 5:13)

No Jewish farmer kept pigs. They were unclean under the law. A herd this size tells us we're in Gentile country. Jesus didn't need a ceremony or a long prayer to drive the devils out. He said one word, and they had to obey.

“Ye are of God, little children, and have overcome them: because greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world.”

1 John 4:4

Every believer has the Holy Spirit living in him from the moment he's saved. Whatever has had its grip on you, it answers to Him.

Some of us know what the tombs are like: living half-dead, scaring the people who love us. This is what Jesus does with a man like that. Nobody is too far gone for Him.

The town asked Jesus to leave their “coasts,” their borders. They cared more about pigs than men. Jesus sent the freed man home to “tell them how great things the Lord hath done for thee” (Mark 5:19).

Points to remember

  1. Willing“I will; be thou clean” 8:3Jesus is willing. He reaches out and touches the man everyone else avoids.
  2. Faith“Speak the word only” 8:8His word has all authority. Distance doesn't matter. Humble faith takes Him at His word.
  3. Prophecy“Himself took our infirmities, and bare our sicknesses” 8:17He carried our sicknesses, and on the cross He carried our sins.
  4. Following“Follow me” 8:22Following Him means no soft promises and no “first let me.” He comes first.
  5. Storms“There was a great calm” 8:26He's in the boat. Call on Him. Wind and sea obey Him.
  6. Freedom“And he said unto them, Go” 8:32Nobody is too far gone. One word from Him sets a man free.

Question 1 of 6

Willing

Is Jesus willing to touch the unclean and make them clean?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, and touched him, and saith unto him, I will; be thou clean.”

Mark 1:41

Answer

Yes. The leper wasn't sure Jesus would. Jesus answered, “I will,” and touched the man nobody else would touch. His compassion reaches into our shame, and His touch makes the unclean clean.

Question 2 of 6

Faith

What did the centurion understand about Jesus' word?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That Jesus' word carries full authority. Like an officer giving orders, Jesus only had to speak and sickness had to obey, even miles away. The centurion came humbly, trusted Jesus' word, and his servant was healed that same hour.

Question 3 of 6

Prophecy

What did Jesus carry for us?

What verses tell us about this?

“Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted.”

Isaiah 53:4

“Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed.”

1 Peter 2:24

Answer

Our sicknesses and pains, as Isaiah promised; His healings showed it. And on the cross He carried something heavier: our sins, in His own body. Forgiveness is ours now, by faith, and full healing is coming at the resurrection.

Question 4 of 6

Following

What does it cost to follow Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It may cost comfort and it costs first place. Jesus promised no soft bed, and He doesn't take second place behind our plans, even good ones. Salvation is free, but following Him means He comes first, starting today.

Question 5 of 6

Storms

What do we do when the storm hits and Jesus seems asleep?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Call on Him. He's in the boat with us, even when He seems quiet. The disciples cried, “Lord, save us,” and He got up and brought a great calm. Even “little faith” called out to the right Person.

Question 6 of 6

Freedom

Is anyone too far gone for Jesus to set free?

What verses tell us about this?

“And they come to Jesus, and see him that was possessed with the devil, and had the legion, sitting, and clothed, and in his right mind: and they were afraid.”

Mark 5:15

Answer

No. These men lived in a graveyard, so violent nobody would pass. One word from Jesus, and the demons had to go. The same man ended up sitting with Jesus, clothed and in his right mind. No one is beyond His reach.
