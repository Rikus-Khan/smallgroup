2 John 1

Truth and love, together

A short note from an old apostle: love one another, hold to the truth, and don't open the door to men who twist who Jesus is.

The second letter of John

“The elder unto the elect lady and her children, whom I love in the truth”

Who
“the elder”: John the apostle, by the oldest tradition. He never puts his name to it.
Written from
probably Ephesus, in Asia (by long tradition)
When
about AD 85 to 95
To whom
“the elect lady and her children”: a church and its members, or a believing woman and her family. Bible teachers differ, and the message is the same either way.
Why
to keep love and truth together, and to warn against teachers who travel from home to home with a different Jesus. It is one of the shortest books in the Bible: about one sheet of papyrus.

Where 2 John fits

  1. about AD 30 to 33Jesus crucified and risenJohn stood at the cross
  2. about 64Rome burns; Nero turns on the churchmost apostles gone soon after
  3. about 70Jerusalem and the temple destroyedthe world John grew up in is gone
  4. about 85 to 952 John, from Ephesus“that which we had from the beginning”
  5. about 95Revelation, from Patmosthe same old man, exiled

By now John is, by tradition, the last of the twelve still living, and an old man. Teachers who never met Jesus have started to bend the story. That's why this letter keeps saying “from the beginning” (verses 5 and 6): stay with what you heard first.

Part one

Truth and love

Verses 1–6

An old man's greeting, and the two words he can't stop saying.

Who is the “elect lady”?

One reading

A church

“Her children” are its members, and “thy elect sister” (verse 13) is a sister congregation sending greetings.

Another reading

A woman and her family

John says “thy house” (verse 10), and a believing woman could host a travelling teacher or turn him away.

Good Bible teachers differ, and the letter doesn't hang on it. Elect means chosen and beloved of God: God chose to save by faith in His Son, and He offers it to whosoever will. What matters is what John tells them to do.

Two words, all through the letter

Lit up below: every verse that has the word truth, then every verse that has love.

truth, five times

1×22×13×14×1 5 6 7 8 9 10 11 12 13 

love, four times

1×12 3×14 5×16×1 7 8 9 10 11 12 13 

Notice verse 3: “in truth and love.” John won't pull them apart. He opens with grace, mercy, and peace, three gifts from God. Grace is what you didn't earn. Mercy is not getting what you did earn. Peace is what's left when both have landed.

Truth without love, love without truth

  1. Truth without lovea hammer: right, and cold. Men leave.
  2. Love without trutha pillow: soft, and it lets a man sleep through his own fire.
  3. Truth and love“in truth and love” (verse 3)

A man in recovery needs both. He needs someone who tells him the truth about the thing that's killing him, and he needs to know that someone loves him while it's being said. A group that only has one of them doesn't help for long.

God did both at the cross: the truth about our sin, and the love that paid for it.

Love is something you walk

this is lovethat we walk after his commandments
→
and his commandment is“that we love one another”

“A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another. By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another.”

John 13:34–35

John isn't handing down something new (“not as though I wrote a new commandment”). It's the one Jesus gave him at the supper table, decades before. Love here is not a feeling you wait for. It's a walk: one step, then the next, every day.

Fell yesterday? Walking means you get up and take the next step. Nobody walks without stumbling. The step that counts is the next one.

Part two

Deceivers

Verses 7–9

Not everyone who says “Jesus” is talking about the same Jesus.

What is a deceiver saying?

  • “He only seemed to have a body”some taught that. But Jesus was hungry, tired, and bled.
  • “He was just a good teacher”a good teacher doesn't say “I and my Father are one.”
  • “He was a man who became God later”John 1:1 says the Word was God from the beginning.
  • “Jesus Christ is come in the flesh”God's own Son, a real man, a real cross, a real grave, empty.

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

John 1:14

Antichrist means against Christ, or in place of Christ: a teacher who sets up a different Jesus. If Jesus wasn't truly man, He didn't truly die for us. If He wasn't truly God, His death couldn't cover us. Everything hangs on this.

“A full reward”

Is John saying a believer can lose his salvation? No. Look at what's at risk: “those things which we have wrought” and “a full reward.” That's work and reward, not the foundation.

The foundation is Jesus Christ, and He holds the believer (John 10:28). But what a man builds on it can be wasted, even by falling for something false.

“If any man’s work abide which he hath built thereupon, he shall receive a reward. If any man’s work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss: but he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire.”

1 Corinthians 3:14–15

Stay inside what Christ taught

Transgresseth means goes beyond: a man who runs ahead of what Christ taught, to something fancier. Abideth means stays, makes his home in. Whoever stays in Christ's teaching has the Father and the Son both. The Father and the Son are never split.

“They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us: but they went out, that they might be made manifest that they were not all of us.”

1 John 2:19

John isn't describing a believer who slips and loses God. He's describing a man who walks away from the truth and shows he never really had it. A real believer who has wandered is not that man.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

If you've drifted into something that doesn't sound like Jesus, the way back is short: say so to Him, and come home.

Part three

The open door

Verses 10–13

How to welcome people, and why John wants to sit across the table.

Who gets in the house?

Churches met in homes, and a travelling teacher expected a bed, a meal and your good word. “God speed” meant a blessing on his work. Back him, and you share in what he spreads. That's “partaker of his evil deeds.”

The test is the message, not the man. Does he bring “this doctrine”: Jesus, God's Son, come in the flesh? Then welcome him.

If he brings another Jesus, don't open the house to him or back his work.

The closed door, and who it isn't for

Not the stranger

Heb. 13:2

“Be not forgetful to entertain strangers.” Hospitality stays.

Not the man who stumbled

Gal. 6:1

A man who fell isn't a deceiver. Restore him gently.

Not a brother who sees a side issue differently

v. 7

The line is Christ: who He is, and what He did.

Who it is for: today's teachers

v. 10

The preacher on your phone, the video, the podcast, the man at the door.

The real-world question for today: who do you let into your head? Anyone who says “Jesus” isn't thereby teaching the real one. Ask what he says Jesus is, and whether it matches the Gospels.

Some things need a face.

Paper and ink→wordsHe could write more. He has many things to say.
Face to face→“our joy may be full”A letter informs you. A person is with you.

John, an old man who might not make the trip, still says I trust to come. He wants to look them in the eye. That's what this group is. Not a screen full of words, but men who know your name and notice when you go quiet.

Today: one person you've only texted. Call him, or go see him. Joy gets full at a table.

2 John in one line each

  1. Love one anotherverse 5
  2. Walk in the truthverses 4 and 6
  3. Jesus came in the fleshverse 7
  4. Guard the open doorverse 10
  5. Meet face to faceverse 12

Look back at the whole letter: thirteen verses, and it covers what a new believer needs most. Love and truth belong together. A man can be fooled by a nice-sounding teacher. And no letter, text or video takes the place of people.

It's honest about how men get led astray, and it's full of grace: “grace, mercy, and peace” from the Father and the Son, and a way home if you've drifted. It ends with hope: “the truth... shall be with us for ever.” Today, love one person in a way he can see.

Points to remember

  1. Truth and love“in truth and love” verse 3Truth without love is a hammer. Love without truth is a pillow. God gives both, and so should we.
  2. Love“this is love, that we walk after his commandments” verse 6Love is not only a feeling. It's the daily walk, one step after another.
  3. Jesus“Jesus Christ is come in the flesh” verse 7A true teacher says Jesus is God's Son, truly man and truly God, who really died and rose.
  4. Careful“receive him not into your house” verse 10Welcome people. But don't back a man who brings a different Jesus.
  5. Face to face“speak face to face, that our joy may be full” verse 12Some things need a person, not a page. Go see someone today.

Question 1 of 5

Truth and love

How do truth and love belong together?

What verses tell us about this?

“But speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ:”

Ephesians 4:15

Answer

John never pulls them apart: “in truth and love.” Truth without love is cold and drives men away. Love without truth lets a man stay in trouble. Together they grow us up into Christ.

Question 2 of 5

Love

What does love look like, according to John?

What verses tell us about this?

“A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another.”

John 13:34

“My little children, let us not love in word, neither in tongue; but in deed and in truth.”

1 John 3:18

Answer

Love is walking in what Jesus commanded, and Jesus commanded us to love one another. It shows up in what you do, not only in what you feel.

Question 3 of 5

Jesus

What must a true teacher say about Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

John 1:14

Answer

That Jesus Christ is God's Son, come in the flesh: truly God and truly man, who really died for sin and really rose. If a teacher gets that wrong, the rest doesn't matter.

Question 4 of 5

Careful

How careful should a believer be about who he backs?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares.”

Hebrews 13:2

Answer

Very careful about the message, and kind to the person. Strangers are welcome, but you don't open the door to a man who brings another Jesus, or give his work your blessing.

Question 5 of 5

Face to face

Why does John want to see them in person?

What verses tell us about this?

“Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.”

Hebrews 10:25

Answer

Because joy gets full in the same room. A letter can teach you, but only a person can sit with you. That's why this group meets: don't go through this alone.
