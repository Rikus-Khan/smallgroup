Deuteronomy 25

Forty stripes, the ox at the threshing floor, a name for the dead, and honest weights

Punishment with a limit, because the guilty man is still thy brother. An ox that eats while it works. A brother who raises up a name for the one who died. One set of weights. And Amalek, who struck the weary at the back of the line.

Part one

“Forty stripes he may give him”

Verses 1–4

Punishment with a limit, and an ox that eats while it works.

Justice with a limit

  • Get the verdict right“justify the righteous, and condemn the wicked.” To justify is to declare a man in the right.
  • The judge watches“before his face”: no beating in a back room, out of sight.
  • Fit the fault“according to his fault, by a certain number”: counted, not in a rage.
  • Never past forty“and not exceed”. Stripes are lashes.

And the reason: “lest… thy brother should seem vile unto thee.” Vile means worthless, cheap. Even the man found guilty and lying on the ground is still “thy brother”. Punishment was never to strip him of that.

Stripes for us

“Of the Jews five times received I forty stripes save one.”

2 Corinthians 11:24

Paul was beaten under this very law, five times. Save one means “but one”: thirty-nine, one short of the forty the law allowed. He took it for preaching Christ.

“But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.”

Isaiah 53:5

The law gave the guilty man his stripes and no more. Christ took stripes he had not earned, for us who had. Some of us have taken a beating, in jail or on the street, and carry the shame of it. In him you are not vile. You are a brother.

The ox at the threshing floor

At harvest an ox walked round and round over the cut grain to tread out the corn, knocking the grain loose from the straw. A muzzle tied over his mouth would stop him from eating as he worked. God said: let him eat.

“For it is written in the law of Moses, Thou shalt not muzzle the mouth of the ox that treadeth out the corn. Doth God take care for oxen? Or saith he it altogether for our sakes? For our sakes, no doubt, this is written: that he that ploweth should plow in hope; and that he that thresheth in hope should be partaker of his hope.”

1 Corinthians 9:9–10

“For the scripture saith, Thou shalt not muzzle the ox that treadeth out the corn. And, The labourer is worthy of his reward.”

1 Timothy 5:18

Paul reads it “for our sakes”: the man who works should share in what he works for, and those who labour in the word should be supported. If God cares that an ox eats, he cares that you do. Work in hope.

Part two

“To raise up unto his brother a name”

Verses 5–12

The duty of a husband's brother, and the man who would not do it.

A name that is not put out

  1. 1a brother dies, no child
  2. 2his brother marries the widow
  3. 3the firstborn bears the dead man's name
  4. 4“not put out of Israel”

The widow was not to marry without, that is, outside the family, to a stranger (a man not of the family). Her husband's brother was to marry her, and their firstborn son would carry on the dead man's name and his share of the land.

This was about two people who could not help themselves: a widow with no son to care for her, and a dead man with no one to carry his name. The living brother was to stand in for the dead one.

The man who would not

A brother could say no: I like not means “I don't want to”. If he stood to it (held to it) before the elders, she pulled off his shoe and spat in his face, and his family got a name: “The house of him that hath his shoe loosed.”

He was not put to death or fined. He was shamed in public, at the gate, before the town, because he would keep his own house and let his brother's die. God takes it seriously when a man will not stand up for a brother who can't stand up for himself.

“Saying, Master, Moses said, If a man die, having no children, his brother shall marry his wife, and raise up seed unto his brother.”

Matthew 22:24

The Sadducees quoted this law to Jesus, to make the resurrection look foolish. He answered them from the Scriptures (Matthew 22:23–33). The same law that guarded a dead man's name points to a God who is not finished with the dead.

Boaz kept it

“And the kinsman said, I cannot redeem it for myself, lest I mar mine own inheritance: redeem thou my right to thyself; for I cannot redeem it.”

Ruth 4:6

“Moreover Ruth the Moabitess, the wife of Mahlon, have I purchased to be my wife, to raise up the name of the dead upon his inheritance, that the name of the dead be not cut off from among his brethren, and from the gate of his place: ye are witnesses this day.”

Ruth 4:10

The nearer kinsman said no, for fear of his own inheritance, and drew off his shoe (Ruth 4:8). Boaz said yes, at his own cost. Their son was Obed, “the father of Jesse, the father of David” (Ruth 4:17).

A Brother who stood in

Here is a picture of Christ. We had no way to save our own name. To be our near kinsman, the Son of God took our flesh and blood:

“Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil;”

Hebrews 2:14

The brother in Deuteronomy 25 could say “I like not.” Jesus never did. He did not worry about marring his own inheritance; he gave himself. And he is “not ashamed to call them brethren” (Hebrews 2:11).

Who in your life has lost someone and has no one to stand in? A widow, a fatherless boy, a brother's family when he is gone. A man in Christ can be that brother.

A hard law, briefly

Two men strive (fight). The wife of one, to help her husband, takes hold of the other man by the secrets, his private parts. The court was to cut off her hand, without pity.

This was civil law for Israel's courts under God, and Moses gives no reason. It does sit right after the law about raising up a name for the dead, where a man's line was guarded. And the law will not let even a good cause, defending her husband, excuse a shameful act.

We are not under Israel's courts. But the lesson stands: winning a fight dirty is still dirty, and God sees what is done in the heat of anger.

Part three

“A perfect and just weight”

Verses 13–19

One set of weights, and a people who must not forget Amalek.

The great weightfor buying: you get more✗
The small weightfor selling: they get less✗

Two bags of weights

Goods were weighed against stones a trader carried in his bag. Divers weights means two different sets: a heavy one for buying and a light one for selling. Each sale cheated a little. God says: one set, perfect (whole, full) and just.

“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.”

Proverbs 11:1

Our scales today

Most of us have run a crooked scale: a light bag sold as full, a cut product, a padded time sheet, a story that changes depending on who is listening. That is two weights in one bag.

“Divers weights are an abomination unto the LORD; and a false balance is not good.”

Proverbs 20:23

Abomination is a strong word: something God hates. But the same God forgives the man who owns it and turns. One weight, one story, one price: that is a new man's way of doing business, and God promises “that thy days may be lengthened in the land”.

Remember Amalek

“Then came Amalek, and fought with Israel in Rephidim.”

Exodus 17:8

“And the LORD said unto Moses, Write this for a memorial in a book, and rehearse it in the ears of Joshua: for I will utterly put out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven.”

Exodus 17:14

We saw the battle in Exodus 17. Now Moses tells what Amalek did first: he “met thee by the way” and struck “the hindmost”, the feeble (weak) ones who had fallen behind, “faint and weary”. And “he feared not God.”

God waited until Israel had rest in the land, and then sent Saul to carry out the judgment (1 Samuel 15:2–3). It was God's sentence on a people who preyed on the weak, and the Judge of all the earth does right.

Don't fall behind alone

The enemy still works the same way. He does not attack the middle of the column. He picks off the man at the back: tired, alone, worn out, the one who stopped coming.

“We then that are strong ought to bear the infirmities of the weak, and not to please ourselves.”

Romans 15:1

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

If you are the one dragging at the back today, don't stay there alone: call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor. And if you are strong today, look back down the line for the man who is missing, and go get him.

Real world: one weight, and no man left behind

  • SinTwo weights in one bag; the brother we would not stand up for; treating the guilty man as vile.
  • RegretDeals we cheated, beatings we gave or took. Bring them to God, and make right what you can.
  • Grace“With his stripes we are healed” (Isaiah 53:5). The Kinsman took our flesh and blood (Hebrews 2:14).
  • HopeGod keeps a dead man's name from being put out, and keeps his people's name in his book.
  • TodayDo one deal or one day's work with an honest weight. And text the brother who has fallen behind.

Points to remember

  1. Forty stripes“Forty stripes he may give him, and not exceed: lest… thy brother should seem vile unto thee” 25:3Even the guilty man is still thy brother.
  2. The ox“For our sakes, no doubt, this is written” 1 Corinthians 9:10The worker shares in his work: “The labourer is worthy of his reward.”
  3. A name for the dead“that his name be not put out of Israel” 25:6The brother stands in for the dead; Boaz did, and so did Christ.

Question 1 of 5

Forty stripes

Why was a guilty man to be given no more than forty stripes?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So that his brother would not seem vile, worthless, in Israel's eyes. Even when guilty, he was still their brother.

Question 2 of 5

The ox

For whose sake, Paul says, was the law about the ox written?

What verses tell us about this?

“Or saith he it altogether for our sakes? For our sakes, no doubt, this is written: that he that ploweth should plow in hope; and that he that thresheth in hope should be partaker of his hope.”

1 Corinthians 9:10

“For the scripture saith, Thou shalt not muzzle the ox that treadeth out the corn. And, The labourer is worthy of his reward.”

1 Timothy 5:18

Answer

“For our sakes”: the man who works should share in the fruit of his work, and “the labourer is worthy of his reward”.

Question 3 of 5

A name for the dead

What was a man to do when his brother died without a child?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Marry his brother's widow, so that their firstborn would carry on the dead brother's name, and his name would not be put out of Israel.

Question 4 of 5

Just weights

What weights and measures did God require, and what did he call the other kind?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A perfect and just weight and measure. Divers weights, a great and a small, and all who deal unrighteously, are an abomination to the LORD.

Question 5 of 5

Amalek

Whom did Amalek strike on the way out of Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The hindmost: the feeble who had fallen behind, faint and weary. And he feared not God.
