Malachi 1

I have loved you

God opens the last book of the Old Testament with “I have loved you.” His people answer, “Prove it,” and then bring Him their leftovers.

The book of Malachi

Who: Malachi. His name means “my messenger.” Nothing else is known about him.

When: about 430 BC, after the exile. The temple had been rebuilt for nearly a hundred years. It was around the time of Nehemiah, under Persian rule.

To whom: the Jews back home in Judah and Jerusalem, and especially their priests.

The walls were up and the altar was busy, but hearts had gone cold. Nehemiah 13 shows the same sins Malachi names.

Part one

I have loved you

Verses 1–5

God's first word is love. His people's first word back is a challenge.

“Wherein?”

The LORDI have loved you.

The peopleWherein hast thou loved us?
prove it

This is how the whole book goes. God says something plain. The people talk back: “Wherein?” That is, “Where? How? Show us.” Then God answers them. It happens again and again:

  1. 1:2“Wherein hast thou loved us?”
  2. 1:6–7“Wherein have we despised thy name?”
  3. 2:14, 17“Wherefore?” “Wherein have we wearied him?”
  4. 3:7–8“Wherein shall we return?” “Wherein have we robbed thee?”
  5. 3:13“What have we spoken so much against thee?”

Hard years, cold hearts

You can understand why they asked. They were back home, but home was a small, poor province ruled by Persia. The great promises of the prophets hadn't come true yet. Crops failed. Life was hard. So when God said “I have loved you,” it sounded empty to them.

When life is hardit's easy to look at what's wrong…

and decideGod must not care

God doesn't argue with their feelings. He points to the record: what He has actually done. For us, the record is the cross.

Romans 5:8

“We love him, because he first loved us.”

1 John 4:19

Jacob and Esau

Jacob and Esau were twin brothers. Jacob's family became Israel. Esau's became Edom, the nation just south-east of Judah. Even before they were born, God said what would come of the two nations:

“And the LORD said unto her, Two nations are in thy womb, and two manner of people shall be separated from thy bowels; and the one people shall be stronger than the other people; and the elder shall serve the younger.”

Genesis 25:23
Did God hate Esau the way a man hates?NoIn Hebrew, “love” and “hate” side by side often mean chose and did not choose, or “loved more” and “loved less.” Jacob “loved also Rachel more than Leah,” and the next verse says Leah “was hated” (Genesis 29:30–31). Jesus spoke the same way (Luke 14:26).
Was it about heaven and hell?NoIt is about two nations and the part each would play. God chose Israel to carry His promises and bring the Saviour into the world. Paul quotes this verse to show that God's choosing is His own, not earned (Romans 9:13).

Why Edom was judged

Edom was not judged for being born second. Edom was judged for what it did. When Babylon attacked Jerusalem, Edom cheered, blocked the roads, and handed the survivors over (Obadiah 10–14).

“For thy violence against thy brother Jacob shame shall cover thee, and thou shalt be cut off for ever.”

Obadiah 1:10

Esau himself had “for one morsel of meat sold his birthright” (Hebrews 12:16). He traded something holy for a bowl of stew. Many of us know that trade: the moment's hunger over what really matters.

God's choosing of Israel was never a closed door to anyone else. Today the offer stands for every man, from any nation: “whosoever believeth in him” (John 3:16).

Two nations, two endings

Edom

“We will rebuild”

By Malachi's day, Edom's homeland had been laid waste, most likely by Arab tribes pushing in from the desert. Edom vowed to rebuild. God said, “I will throw down.” Edom faded out of history.

Israel

Still here

Judah had been burned by Babylon too. But God brought them home and rebuilt them. That was His answer to “Wherein hast thou loved us?” Look around. You're still here.

A man who is still alive after everything he's been through, sitting here today with an open Bible, can ask the same question and get the same answer.

“The LORD did not set his love upon you, nor choose you, because ye were more in number than any people; for ye were the fewest of all people: But because the LORD loved you, and because he would keep the oath which he had sworn unto your fathers, hath the LORD brought you out with a mighty hand, and redeemed you out of the house of bondmen, from the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt.”

Deuteronomy 7:7–8

Part two

Where is mine honour?

Verses 6–10

The priests were supposed to lead the worship. They led the way in cutting corners.

Father and Master

God reasons with them from everyday life. A good son honours his father. A good servant respects his master. If you call God both, where is the honour?

The LORDO priests, that despise my name.

The priestsWherein have we despised thy name?

The LORDYe offer polluted bread upon mine altar.

The priestsWherein have we polluted thee?
they really didn't see it

The scary part isn't that they sinned. It's that they couldn't see it anymore. A man can drift so slowly that the drift feels normal.

Would you give that to the governor?

The “bread” and the “table of the LORD” here mean the altar and the sacrifices on it. God's law was clear about what an animal for sacrifice must be:

“But whatsoever hath a blemish, that shall ye not offer: for it shall not be acceptable for you.”

Leviticus 22:20

But the priests were taking the blind, the lame, and the sick: the animals a farmer couldn't sell anyway. So God asks: try handing that to the Persian governor as your tax or your gift. You'd never dare. You'd bring him your best.

Why did the sacrifice have to be spotless? Because it pointed ahead to the only One who truly was.

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Shut the doors

Better a locked temple than a fake one.

God says: I wish one of you would just shut the temple doors, so nobody lights another useless fire on My altar. Worship that is only for show is worse than none.

The priests were even asking God to be gracious (verse 9), with a lame lamb in their hands. That's like a man who prays for help on the way to the dealer: he wants God's blessing without giving up anything.

“But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him.”

John 4:23

Part three

A great King

Verses 11–14

If His own priests won't honour Him, the whole world will.

From sunrise to sunset

The “Gentiles” and the “heathen” are the nations: everyone who isn't Jewish. In Malachi's day hardly any of them worshipped the LORD. God says that will change, “in every place.”

  1. Malachi's dayone small temple in Jerusalem
  2. Actsthe gospel goes to the Gentiles
  3. todaymen pray in Jesus' name on every continent

“From the rising of the sun unto the going down of the same the LORD’S name is to be praised.”

Psalm 113:3

You're part of the answer to verse 11. A group of grown men praying to the God of Israel, far from Jerusalem, is exactly what God said would happen.

“What a weariness”

To “snuff at” something is to sniff at it, to huff and roll your eyes. The priests found serving God boring. It was a chore to get through.

  • “Do I have to?”worship as a chore
  • “That'll do.”the torn, the lame, the sick
  • “He is a great King.”worship that remembers who it's for

Every man in recovery knows the danger here. The meetings get routine, the prayers get short, and a man starts “snuffing at” the very things that keep him alive. The cure isn't trying harder. It's remembering who He is, and what He did for you.

Leftovers

Here is a man with a good male animal in his flock. He makes a vow to God, then swaps in a sick one and keeps the good one for himself. God calls him “the deceiver.”

The leftovers

What's left

The hours after everything else is done. The money after the bills and the fun. The energy after the job. Whatever won't cost me.

The first and best

What He's worth

“neither will I offer burnt offerings unto the LORD my God of that which doth cost me nothing” (2 Samuel 24:24).

“I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.”

Romans 12:1

Notice the order: “by the mercies of God.” We don't give God our best to earn His love. We give it because He already gave His best for us.

Points to remember

  1. God's love“I have loved you, saith the LORD” 1:2When life is hard, look at the record of what God has done. For us, the record is the cross.
  2. Jacob and Esau“Yet I loved Jacob, and I hated Esau” 1:2–3It's about God choosing a nation to carry His promise, and judging Edom for its violence. It is not a closed door to anyone.
  3. Honour“If then I be a father, where is mine honour?” 1:6If He is our Father and Master, our lives should show it.
  4. Our best“Offer it now unto thy governor” 1:8God deserves our first and best, not what's left over. He gave His best for us.
  5. Going through the motions“Behold, what a weariness is it!” 1:13When serving God feels like a chore, go back to who He is: a great King, who loves you.
  6. His name“My name shall be great among the heathen” 1:11God always meant His name for the whole world, including us.

Question 1 of 6

God's love

How can we know God loves us when life is hard?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

By what He has done, not by how we feel. Judah felt forgotten, but God pointed to the record: they were still alive, home, and His. Our record is even clearer. Christ died for us while we were still sinners. Hard days don't cancel that.

Question 2 of 6

Jacob and Esau

What does God mean when He says He hated Esau?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he went in also unto Rachel, and he loved also Rachel more than Leah, and served with him yet seven other years. And when the LORD saw that Leah was hated, he opened her womb: but Rachel was barren.”

Genesis 29:30–31

“For thy violence against thy brother Jacob shame shall cover thee, and thou shalt be cut off for ever.”

Obadiah 1:10

Answer

In Hebrew, “love” and “hate” side by side mean choosing one over the other. God chose Jacob's nation, Israel, to carry His promises and bring the Saviour. Edom's ruin came because of its own violence against Judah. It doesn't mean God shuts any man out. The gospel is for whosoever believes.

Question 3 of 6

Honour

What does it mean to honour God as our Father and our Master?

What verses tell us about this?

“And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men;”

Colossians 3:23

Answer

It means more than calling Him by those names. A son honours his father by how he lives, and a servant respects his master by how he works. The priests said the right words but treated God's altar like a dump. Honour shows up in what we actually do.

Question 4 of 6

Our best

Why does it matter what we bring to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.”

Romans 12:1

Answer

Because what we bring shows what we think He's worth. The priests would never hand the governor a sick lamb, but they handed one to God. He doesn't need our gifts, and we can't earn His love with them. But He gave His spotless Son for us, and He's worth our first and best.

Question 5 of 6

Going through the motions

What happens when serving God becomes a weariness?

What verses tell us about this?

“But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him.”

John 4:23

Answer

We start cutting corners, and soon we can't even see it. God would rather the doors be shut than have empty worship. The fix isn't just trying harder. It's remembering who He is, “a great King,” and that He loved us first. Worship in spirit and in truth comes from there.

Question 6 of 6

His name

Is God's name only for one nation?

What verses tell us about this?

“From the rising of the sun unto the going down of the same the LORD’S name is to be praised.”

Psalm 113:3

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

John 3:16

Answer

No. God chose Israel to carry His promise, but the promise was always meant for the world. Malachi saw a day when His name would be great “in every place.” Through Jesus, that day came. Any man, from any nation, can call on Him.
