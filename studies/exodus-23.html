Exodus 23

Mine Angel shall go before thee

Tell the truth, do not follow the crowd, and help your enemy's donkey. Rest the seventh day, keep three feasts, and then the promise: God sends an Angel ahead of them to bring them into the land, “by little and little.”

Part one

“Thou shalt not follow a multitude”

Verses 1–9

Truth in court, no bribes, and a kind hand for the man who hates you.

Not with the crowd

  • false reporta rumour, a lie, a story told to hurt someone
  • unrighteous witnessa man who lies in court for a friend
  • wrest judgmenttwist a fair ruling, like wringing a wet cloth until it says what you want
  • countenancefavour, side with because of who he is

Verse 2 is for any man who has ever said, “everybody was doing it.” You may not follow a crowd to do wrong. And when the crowd is gathered in a court, you may not “decline after many” and bend your own testimony toward the biggest voice.

“My son, if sinners entice thee, consent thou not.”

Proverbs 1:10

A great many of us ended up in trouble because of a crowd: the friends, the parties, the group in the truck. Verse 3 is a surprise: do not favour the poor man just because he is poor. The case must be judged on what is true, not on the crowd, the rich man or the pity.

Your enemy's ox

thenyou bring it back, you help him lift it.

Not a friend's ox. “Thine enemy's,” and “him that hateth thee.” God does not ask you to like him. He asks you to do the right thing with his animal, as if it were your own.

“Therefore if thine enemy hunger, feed him; if he thirst, give him drink: for in so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head.”

Romans 12:20

“Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.”

Romans 12:21

Who is your enemy? A man who cut you off, a boss, an ex, someone you owe an apology. Today do one small, practical good thing for him, without being asked. You do not have to feel it first.

Justice that cannot be bought

  • not the loudest voice“keep thee far from a false matter”
  • not the biggest crowd“thou shalt not follow a multitude”
  • not a gift“the gift blindeth the wise, and perverteth the words of the righteous”
  • but what is true“I will not justify the wicked”

Gift here means a bribe. Perverteth means twists. Justify means declare right: God says He will not declare a wicked man right because of a payment. A man who takes a bribe cannot see straight.

The poor man gets his case judged fairly (6), and the foreigner is not oppressed (9): “ye know the heart of a stranger.” You know how it feels to be the outsider, so do not be the one who pushes him out. At every meeting there is a new man who does not know anyone. Sit with him.

Part two

“Six days thou shalt do thy work”

Verses 10–19

Rest for the land, the ox and the servant, and three times a year all the men come before the LORD.

Rest, for everyone who works

Every seventh year

The land rests

Six years of sowing, and the seventh the land “rest and lie still.” What grows by itself is for “the poor of thy people,” and then the beasts.

Every seventh day

The people rest

“That thine ox and thine ass may rest, and the son of thy handmaid, and the stranger, may be refreshed.” A handmaid is a woman servant.

Notice who is named: the ox, the donkey, the servant's son, the foreigner. The day of rest is first for the ones who have no power to take a day off. This is a gift, not a burden.

“And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:”

Mark 2:27

Jesus said it for us: rest was made for man. A man who never stops, working, chasing or numbing himself, is running on a body and a mind that God made to need rest. Take one real rest and trust Him with the rest.

“Be circumspect”

Circumspect means careful, looking all around. “In all things that I have said unto you be circumspect.” Then a specific thing: “make no mention of the name of other gods, neither let it be heard out of thy mouth.”

Israel had lived for generations among Egypt's gods. God asks them to be careful what they carry over. Even the names were not to be on their lips. A new life usually needs new words too, and a man in recovery knows the old phrases, the old jokes, the old names that pull him back.

Three feasts a year

  1. spring (Abib)Unleavened breadseven days; “thou camest out from Egypt”
  2. early summerHarvestthe firstfruits of the grain
  3. autumnIngatheringthe harvest all stored in

Abib is the spring month, about March to April, when the barley ripens. Later it is called Nisan. The month Israel left Egypt was made the first month of their year (12:2).

The three feasts remember what God did (the going out from Egypt) and thank Him for what He gives (the first of the harvest, and the last). “None shall appear before me empty”: a man did not come to God's house empty-handed. He brought something from what God had given him.

“Thy males shall appear before the Lord GOD” (17): the men of Israel went up together. There was a place for men to stand side by side before God and thank Him. That is what you do here every day.

Blood, bread, and a kid

  • leavened breadbread with yeast. In the Bible, yeast often stands for sin that spreads
  • seetheboil
  • a kida young goat
  • remain until the morningstay overnight

The blood of the sacrifice is not to be offered with leavened bread, and the fat is not to be left until morning. Both are about doing God's worship cleanly and at once, with nothing mixed in and nothing left lying around.

Verse 19 ends with a line repeated in chapter 34: do not boil a young goat in its mother's milk. The Bible does not say why. Many think it simply forbids a cruel twist: using the very milk that gave the kid life to cook it. We do not need to claim more than the text says. What the verse shows is that God cares about tenderness, even in a kitchen.

Part three

“Behold, I send an Angel before thee”

Verses 20–33

God goes ahead of them, and promises the land, by little and little. They must not make friends with what will trip them.

An Angel with God's name in Him

Angel means messenger. This messenger is unlike the others: He has authority over sin itself (“he will not pardon your transgressions”), and “my name is in him.” Only God forgives sin. So this Angel speaks with God's own authority.

Exodus 23:21

Beware of him, and obey his voice… for my name is in him.

John 5:43

I am come in my Father’s name, and ye receive me not:

Many Christians through the centuries have read this Angel as the Son of God before He was born at Bethlehem, the One who goes ahead of His people. The text does not say His name, only that God's name is in Him. We can say this much for certain: God did not send Israel into the wilderness alone.

“Obey his voice, provoke him not.” That is a sober word: He is not to be tested. And it is the same word we find all through the Bible: listen to the One God sends.

“To bring thee into the place which I have prepared”

“Behold, I send an Angel before thee, to keep thee in the way, and to bring thee into the place which I have prepared” (20). Three things: He keeps them on the way, He brings them in, and the place is prepared before they get there.

“In my Father’s house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you.”

John 14:2

“And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also.”

John 14:3

The believer has the same kind of promise. A place is being prepared, and the One who prepares it also keeps you on the road there. A man who fell yesterday is still on the way, and he is still kept.

Why the Canaanites?

The six peoples named in verse 23 (Amorites, Hittites, Perizzites, Canaanites, Hivites, Jebusites) already lived in the land. A hard word, “I will cut them off,” so hear the whole of what the Bible says. God did not do it at once, and not for Israel's sake alone.

“But in the fourth generation they shall come hither again: for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.”

Genesis 15:16

He told Abraham, more than six hundred years earlier, that He would wait four hundred years because the Amorites' sin “is not yet full.” Their wrong had a limit, and God was patient until it was reached. Israel did not receive the land for being better: Moses will tell them they were a stiff-necked people, and it was God's promise and God's judgment.

“An enemy unto thine enemies” is a promise to people who obey His voice (22): God on their side, on the way in.

The promise, and what it does not say

  • bless thy bread, and thy waterGod provides the daily food and drink
  • take sickness awayno plague like Egypt's among them
  • cast their youngmiscarry
  • barrenunable to have children
  • fulfilfill up: He will let them live out their days

These are promises to Israel, as a nation under the covenant of Sinai, when they serve the LORD in the land. They are not a rule that every faithful man will always be healthy and rich. Job, and Paul with his thorn, were not.

What does carry over is the heart of it: “ye shall serve the LORD your God, and he shall bless thy bread.” Serve Him, and He is the one who feeds you today. Ask for today's bread and trust Him for it.

The edges of the promise

“From the Red sea even unto the sea of the Philistines, and from the desert unto the river.” South, the gulf Israel crossed. West, the Mediterranean. East and north, the desert and the great river, the Euphrates.

Bounds are borders. It is much bigger than the land they will first settle, and God says it is theirs.

Hornets and “my fear” are how He says He will drive the peoples out: terror sent ahead of Israel.

“By little and little”

one day at a time

God could have cleared the land in a day. He says: not in one year, or the land would turn to desert and the wild beasts would multiply (29). “By little and little I will drive them out… until thou be increased, and inherit the land.” God's help arrives a step at a time, at a pace the people can hold.

Every man in recovery knows this. The cravings do not leave in one year or a week. They go by little and little, a day at a time, with God driving them out before you. Do not despise the small day. It is how the land is taken.

“A snare unto thee”

A snare is a trap. The land is God's gift, but the peoples' gods and ways are the trap. “They shall not dwell in thy land, lest they make thee sin against me.”

God does not say the neighbours are worse than Israel. He says the pull is real: if you live beside it, you will end up serving it. Moses' people will find out how true that is. Judges will tell it again and again.

The real world: you know your snares. The old street, the old friend who is still using, the app, the bar you cut through on the way home. You do not make a covenant with them. You do not try to live beside them “just to see.” Change the route, delete the number, and tell a brother what the snare is.

“My son, if sinners entice thee, consent thou not.”

Proverbs 1:10

Points to remember

  1. The crowd“Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil” 23:2“Everybody was doing it” is not a defence. Stand for what is true, even alone.
  2. Enemies“thou shalt surely bring it back to him again” 23:4Do the right thing by the man who hates you. Overcome evil with good.
  3. Rest“on the seventh day thou shalt rest: that thine ox and thine ass may rest” 23:12Rest is a gift, first for the ones who work hardest. The sabbath was made for man.
  4. The feasts“Three times thou shalt keep a feast unto me in the year” 23:14Remember what God did, thank Him for what He gives, and do not come empty-handed.
  5. The Angel“Behold, I send an Angel before thee, to keep thee in the way, and to bring thee into the place which I have prepared.” 23:20God sends One ahead who carries His name, keeps you on the way, and prepares the place.
  6. Little by little“By little and little I will drive them out from before thee” 23:30God works at a pace you can hold. Take today, and do not make a deal with the snare.

Question 1 of 6

The crowd

What did God say to a man when everyone around him was going the wrong way?

What verses tell us about this?

“My son, if sinners entice thee, consent thou not.”

Proverbs 1:10

Answer

Do not follow them. Do not lie for them, and do not be bought. Stand for what is true, even if you stand alone. Tell your brothers in Christ who your crowd is and ask them to stand with you.

Question 2 of 6

Enemies

What was a man to do when his enemy's ox strayed or his donkey fell?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.”

Romans 12:21

Answer

Bring it back, and help him lift it, even though he hates you. You do not have to feel kind first. Do one practical good thing for someone you struggle with today.

Question 3 of 6

Rest

Who was the seventh day for, and who was the seventh year for?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:”

Mark 2:27

Answer

The seventh year rested the land and fed the poor. The seventh day was for the ox, the donkey, the servant and the stranger to be refreshed. Rest was made for man. Take one real rest, and trust God with the rest.

Question 4 of 6

The feasts

What three feasts did God give, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Unleavened bread, harvest and ingathering. The first remembered the going out of Egypt, the other two thanked God for the harvest. “None shall appear before me empty.” Remember what He did, and come grateful.

Question 5 of 6

The Angel

Who does God send before them, and what is special about Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am come in my Father’s name, and ye receive me not: if another shall come in his own name, him ye will receive.”

John 5:43

Answer

An Angel who keeps them in the way and brings them to the place God prepared, with God's own name in Him. Many see in Him the Son before Bethlehem. God did not send Israel out alone, and He does not send you alone either.

Question 6 of 6

Little by little

How did God say He would give them the land, and what must they not do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By little and little, a step at a time, and they were not to make a deal with the peoples or their gods, because it would be a snare. God drives out the enemy at a pace you can hold. Take today, and keep away from your snares.
