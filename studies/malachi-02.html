Malachi 2

Keep faith

Priests who stopped teaching the truth, and husbands who walked out on their wives. God calls both what they are, and asks His people to keep their promises.

Part one

The covenant of Levi

Verses 1–9

God has a word for the priests: what they were meant to be, and what they had become.

Lay it to heart

The priests heard God's word every day. They just didn't take it to heart. So God gives them a warning, with an “if.”

If ye will nothear, and lay it to heart, and give glory to My name…
→
thenI will curse your blessings

The priests lived on a share of the offerings, and when they blessed the people, they spoke God's blessing over them (Numbers 6:23–27). God says those very blessings would turn sour in their hands. In fact, He says, it has already started.

Notice that even here there is a door: “if ye will not.” God warns before He acts, so men will turn.

Carried out with the trash

This is blunt on purpose. When an animal was sacrificed, its dung and refuse were hauled outside the camp and burned.

“But the flesh of the bullock, and his skin, and his dung, shalt thou burn with fire without the camp: it is a sin offering.”

Exodus 29:14

These priests were treating God's altar like garbage. So God says He will treat them the same way: smear the refuse of their own feasts on their faces, and let them be carried out with it.

God isn't being crude. He's showing them, in a picture nobody could miss, what their careless worship looked like from heaven.

What a faithful man looks like

Levi was the tribe God set apart to serve at His altar and teach His law. Moses and Aaron came from it. God reminds the priests of what their forefathers were at their best:

  1. He feared Godand “was afraid before my name”
  2. He told the truth“iniquity was not found in his lips”
  3. He walked with God“in peace and equity,” fair to everyone
  4. He turned many“away from iniquity”
  5. He knew the word“the priest’s lips should keep knowledge”

God gave that covenant of “life and peace.” And this is a picture for every believer now:

“But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:”

1 Peter 2:9

Then and now

Levi

Turned many from sin

The law of truth in his mouth. Walked with God. People came to him to learn the way.

These priests

Caused many to stumble

Left the way themselves. Bent the law for the people they liked (“partial”). People tripped over them.

A man's example always leads somewhere. Fathers, older brothers, sponsors, the man who's been sober longest in the room: somebody is watching how you walk.

“My brethren, be not many masters, knowing that we shall receive the greater condemnation.”

James 3:1

“Masters” there means teachers. That isn't meant to scare a man off from helping others. It's meant to make him walk carefully while he does.

Part two

Dealing treacherously

Verses 10–12

Now Malachi turns from the priests to everyone. They are breaking faith with God and with each other.

The word of the chapter

“Treacherously” means breaking faith: betraying someone who trusted you. Watch how often it comes up in these few verses:

10×1 11×1 12  13  14×1 15×1 16×1

Malachi's point in verse 10: we all have one Father and one Creator. We're family. So when a man betrays his brother, he's breaking faith with God too.

Most of us have been on both ends of this. We've been betrayed, and we've done the betraying. God takes it seriously because people are His.

The daughter of a strange god

Men of Judah were marrying women who worshipped other gods. Nehemiah found the same thing in the same years:

“In those days also saw I Jews that had married wives of Ashdod, of Ammon, and of Moab: And their children spake half in the speech of Ashdod, and could not speak in the Jews’ language, but according to the language of each people.”

Nehemiah 13:23–24

This was never about race. Ruth was a woman of Moab who turned to the LORD, and she became King David's great-grandmother. It was about gods: “thy God my God” (Ruth 1:16). A home split between the LORD and an idol pulls the children away from Him.

The New Testament says the same to believers: “Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers” (2 Corinthians 6:14). But if a man was already married when he came to Christ, Paul tells him to stay: “let him not put her away” (1 Corinthians 7:12).

Part three

The wife of thy youth

Verses 13–16

Men crying at the altar, wondering why God won't listen, while they walk out on their wives.

Why won't God listen?

The men of JudahWherefore? Why won't He accept our offerings?

The LORDBecause the LORD hath been witness between thee and the wife of thy youth, against whom thou hast dealt treacherously.

They were weeping at the altar, but they would not deal with the woman they had wronged at home. God says He was a witness at that wedding. A man can't treat his wife badly and expect his prayers to go right on as if nothing happened.

“Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered.”

1 Peter 3:7

A covenant, not a contract

God gives her three names. Each one is a reason to keep faith with her:

  1. the wife of thy youthshe gave you her young years
  2. thy companionyour partner, not your servant
  3. the wife of thy covenanta promise made before God, with God as witness

A contract ends when the other side stops paying. A covenant is a promise to stay. That's the kind of promise God makes with us.

“Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;”

Ephesians 5:25

“He hateth putting away”

“Putting away” means divorce. Here's what was happening: men of Judah were sending away the Jewish wives they'd married young, so they could marry someone else. The women were left with nothing.

“Covereth violence with his garment” means wrapping cruelty in something respectable. The paperwork was legal, but the heart behind it was treachery.

God made the two “one” (verse 15), and He wanted children raised in faith: “a godly seed.” Jesus said the same:

“Wherefore they are no more twain, but one flesh. What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder.”

Matthew 19:6

“He saith unto them, Moses because of the hardness of your hearts suffered you to put away your wives: but from the beginning it was not so.”

Matthew 19:8

If yours has already broken

Many men in this room are divorced. Some walked out. Some were left. Some did things in the drinking and using years that they can't take back. Here's what this passage does and doesn't say to you.

  • “God hates divorced men.”He hates what divorce does to people. He loves the people.
  • “I'm a second-class Christian now.”“And such were some of you: but ye are washed” (1 Corinthians 6:11)
  • Own your part, receive His forgiveness, and keep faith from here.“Take heed to your spirit”

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Keeping faith from here can look like this: speaking well of your children's mother, paying what you owe, showing up for your kids, and guarding the marriage you're in now. And if you were the one left behind: “The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart” (Psalm 34:18).

Part four

Where is the God of judgment?

Verse 17

The people get tired of waiting for God to set things right, and start saying evil is good.

Tired of waiting

The LORDYe have wearied the LORD with your words.

The peopleWherein have we wearied him?

The LORDWhen ye say, Every one that doeth evil is good in the sight of the LORD … or, Where is the God of judgment?

They looked around, saw crooked men doing fine, and decided either God likes evil or He isn't watching. In chapter 1 they wore God out with their offerings. Now they wear Him out with their talk.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

God's delay is patience, not absence. And His answer to “Where is the God of judgment?” comes in the very next verse: “Behold, I will send my messenger” (3:1).

Points to remember

  1. Leaders“Ye have caused many to stumble at the law” 2:8The man who teaches God's word leads others with his life. Walk carefully.
  2. A faithful man“The law of truth was in his mouth” 2:6Fear God, tell the truth, walk with Him, and help turn others from sin.
  3. Broken faith“Why do we deal treacherously every man against his brother?” 2:10We have one Father. Betraying a brother breaks faith with God too.
  4. Marriage“Yet is she thy companion, and the wife of thy covenant” 2:14Marriage is a covenant made before God. He was there, and He's watching how we keep it.
  5. After divorce“Take heed to your spirit” 2:16God hates what divorce does, and He loves divorced men. Confess, receive forgiveness, and keep faith from here.
  6. God's justice“Where is the God of judgment?” 2:17God's delay is patience. He sees, and He will set things right.

Question 1 of 6

Leaders

Why does God hold those who teach His word to a higher standard?

What verses tell us about this?

“My brethren, be not many masters, knowing that we shall receive the greater condemnation.”

James 3:1

Answer

Because people follow them. A priest was “the messenger of the LORD of hosts,” and when he left the way, many stumbled after him. Anyone who teaches or leads, at home, at work, or in a group like this, carries other people with him. That's a reason to walk carefully, not to stay quiet.

Question 2 of 6

A faithful man

What does a faithful man of God look like?

What verses tell us about this?

“But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:”

1 Peter 2:9

Answer

He fears God. He tells the truth. He walks with God in peace and fairness. And he helps turn others away from sin. That was Levi at his best, and it's the calling of every believer now, because in Christ we are all a “royal priesthood.”

Question 3 of 6

Broken faith

Why does God take broken promises so seriously?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.”

Matthew 5:23–24

Answer

Because God is a promise keeper, and people belong to Him. We have “one father,” so betraying a brother, a wife, or a friend is breaking faith with God too. That's why God cares about how we treat people before He cares about our offerings.

Question 4 of 6

Marriage

How does God see a man's marriage?

What verses tell us about this?

“Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;”

Ephesians 5:25

Answer

As a covenant He witnessed. He made the two one, and He wants children raised to know Him. A husband is called to love his wife the way Christ loved the church: by giving himself, and by staying.

Question 5 of 6

After divorce

What hope is there for a man whose marriage has already broken?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Psalm 34:18

Answer

Real hope. God hates what divorce does, but He doesn't hate divorced men. Whatever your part was, bring it to Him honestly, and He forgives and cleanses. Then keep faith from here: deal fairly with your children's mother, show up for your kids, and guard the marriage you have now. And if you were the one left, He is near to the brokenhearted.

Question 6 of 6

God's justice

Why doesn't God judge the wicked right away?

What verses tell us about this?

“Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness; that put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter!”

Isaiah 5:20

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Because He is patient, giving men time to turn, us included. Waiting isn't the same as not caring. Judah got tired of waiting and started calling evil good. God's answer was, “Behold, I will send my messenger.” He sees, and He will set things right in His time.
