Part one

Couldn't wait

Verses 1–5

Paul is stuck in a strange city with no news, and he can't take it any more.

Timothy goes and comes back

“Forbear” means hold it in, put up with it. Paul couldn't. So he chose to be “left at Athens alone”, in a city full of idols, so that Timothy could go.

Timothy goes to Thessalonica, to “establish you, and to comfort you.”

And back, to Paul at Corinth, with Silas (Acts 18:5).

Look at how Paul describes him: “our brother, and minister of God, and our fellowlabourer.” A mentor who lifts the younger man up.

He had warned them

Paul, while he was with themTrouble is coming. It comes with the territory.

…and laterIt came, just as you said. And you were ready.

“Moved” means shaken. “Appointed thereunto” doesn't mean God wants you hurt; a new life against the grain meets pushback. Jesus said the same:

“These things I have spoken unto you, that in me ye might have peace. In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.”

John 16:33

Old friends, a hard home, a craving at ten at night. Told it would be rough, you're less likely to think something has gone wrong.

The tempter

Satan is real, and so are his darts. But look at what Paul is afraid of: not that they'd lose Jesus, but that they'd be knocked off their feet and his work come to nothing.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

A believer is held in the Father's hand. But a held man can still be shaken, scared and miserable. That's why he needs brothers beside him, and why Paul couldn't rest.

Part two

Good news

Verses 6–10

Timothy comes back. Watch what happens to Paul.

“Good tidings”

εὐαγγελίζομαι said euangelizomai “to bring good news”: the word our gospel and evangelist come from

Paul uses the gospel word for Timothy's report. Their faith was news worth announcing. “Charity” is the old English word for love.

And they missed him as much as he missed them: “desiring greatly to see us, as we also to see you.” Paul was in trouble himself at that moment, and still, hearing they were standing comforted him.

Fear, news, life

Paul was strong, and his joy still depended on his friends. Mentors, sponsors and fathers know it: you come alive when the man you care about is standing.

So your standing matters to somebody. When you show up, tell the truth, and get up after a bad day, you give life to men who pray for you. And notice: “in the Lord.” Not in yourself.

“Perfect” means mend

καταρτίσαι said katartisai “to mend, repair, put right”

“And when he had gone a little further thence, he saw James the son of Zebedee, and John his brother, who also were in the ship mending their nets.”

Mark 1:19

The same word as “mending their nets.” Paul isn't saying they have no faith; a good net has gaps that need mending. He says it “night and day, praying exceedingly.” The next two chapters are the mending.

What gap are you embarrassed about? A net being mended is not thrown away.

Part three

The prayer

Verses 11–13

Paul ends the way he does everything: by asking God.

From blocked to open

Chapter 2

“Satan hindered us”

The road was blocked, and Paul couldn't come, though he tried more than once (2:18).

Chapter 3

“God himself … direct our way unto you”

Paul doesn't give up and doesn't just complain. He takes the blocked road to God in prayer.

Years later, he did get to Macedonia and Greece again (Acts 20:1–2). The door that was shut opened, in God's time.

Love that overflows

Paul doesn't say, “try harder.” He says, “the Lord make you.” Love grows because God grows it. “Increase and abound” means grow, then overflow.

First, “one toward another.” The brothers beside you. People like this room.

Then “toward all men.” Even the hard ones, the old enemies, and the strangers at work. “Even as we do toward you,” Paul adds. He had shown them how.

What “unblameable” means

  • never failingnone of us is that, and Paul isn't asking for it
  • looking good to peoplehe says before God, who sees the heart
  • nothing left that can be charged against youbecause of Jesus, and the Lord Himself stablishes the heart

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

“Holiness” means set apart for God. And he ends where every chapter ends: “at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ with all his saints.” If you stumbled yesterday, 1 John 1:9 is still true: confess it, and He is faithful and just to forgive. Then stand up again.

“For now we live, if ye stand fast in the Lord.”

A mentor who can't rest until he knows his men are standing. That's Paul. That's what this group does for each other.

One thing to do today: send one text to a man in this group. “How are you standing?” Or, if you're the one wobbling, tell one of them. They are Timothys, and they've been waiting to hear.

Points to remember

  1. Couldn't wait“when we could no longer forbear, we thought it good to be left at Athens alone” 3:1Real care costs something. Paul gave up company to find out how they were.
  2. Warned“that no man should be moved by these afflictions” 3:3Hard times come with a new life. Knowing it helps you stand.
  3. The tempter“lest by some means the tempter have tempted you, and our labour be in vain” 3:5A held man can still be shaken, so we need each other.
  4. Good news“Timotheus came from you unto us, and brought us good tidings of your faith and charity” 3:6Your faith is good news to someone.
  5. Standing“For now we live, if ye stand fast in the Lord.” 3:8Your standing gives life to the men who love you.
  6. Paul's prayer“the Lord make you to increase and abound in love one toward another, and toward all men” 3:12Love is grown by the Lord. Ask Him.

Question 1 of 6

Couldn't wait

Why did Paul send Timothy to Thessalonica?

What verses tell us about this?

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Answer

He couldn't stand not knowing. He wanted Timothy to establish and comfort them, and to find out whether they were standing. Caring for people means going to them.

Question 2 of 6

Warned

What had Paul told them to expect?

What verses tell us about this?

“These things I have spoken unto you, that in me ye might have peace. In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.”

John 16:33

Answer

Afflictions. He had told them while he was with them that they would suffer tribulation, and it happened. Jesus said the same, and said to be of good cheer.

Question 3 of 6

The tempter

What was Paul afraid of?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour: Whom resist stedfast in the faith, knowing that the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren that are in the world.”

1 Peter 5:8–9

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. My Father, which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father’s hand.”

John 10:28–29

Answer

That the tempter had tempted them and his work come to nothing. Believers are held in the Father's hand, but the devil still prowls, so stand firm together.

Question 4 of 6

Good news

What did Timothy bring back?

What verses tell us about this?

“First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for you all, that your faith is spoken of throughout the whole world.”

Romans 1:8

Answer

Good tidings of their faith and love, and that they remembered Paul and wanted to see him. It comforted Paul in his own affliction and distress.

Question 5 of 6

Standing

What made Paul say, “now we live”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore, my beloved brethren, be ye stedfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labour is not in vain in the Lord.”

1 Corinthians 15:58

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Answer

Hearing that they stood fast in the Lord. He was glad because they were holding on, and because God finishes what He starts.

Question 6 of 6

Paul's prayer

What did Paul pray for them?

What verses tell us about this?

“And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.”

Hebrews 10:24–25

Answer

That the Lord would make their love increase and abound toward each other and all men, and stablish their hearts unblameable in holiness.
