Zechariah 12

They shall look upon me

The nations gather against Jerusalem, and God defends her. Then He pours out grace, and His people look on the One they pierced and weep.

Part one

A burdensome stone

Verses 1–9

Zechariah preached to the Jews back from Babylon, about 520 BC. Chapters 12–14 are his last “burden,” and they all look ahead to “that day.”

Who is speaking

Before God says what He will do, He says who He is. A “burden” is a heavy message from God, and this one comes with His signature:

  1. Stretcheth forth the heavensHe spread out the sky.
  2. Layeth the foundation of the earthHe set the ground under our feet.
  3. Formeth the spirit of man within himHe made the inside of you too.

The God who holds up the sky also shaped the part of you nobody else sees: your thoughts, your fears, your cravings. He knows how you're made. Nothing He promises next is too big for Him.

A cup and a stone

The whole world comes against Jerusalem. God gives two pictures of what happens to them.

Verse 2

A cup of trembling

A cup that makes the one who drinks it reel and stagger. The nations come to swallow Jerusalem, and it's they who end up unable to stand.

Verse 3

A burdensome stone

A stone too heavy to lift. Every army that tries to heave it up and carry it off gets torn up by it.

Plenty of us know what it is to pick up a cup and end up staggering. Here God turns that picture on the nations: whoever tries to swallow what belongs to Him ends up on the floor.

“In that day”

Every square below is a verse of this chapter, and the lit ones say “in that day” or “at that day.”

1  2  3×1 4×1 5  6×1 7  8×2 9×1 10  11×1 12  13  14 

This is a day still ahead, when God steps in for Jerusalem. Horses panic, riders go mad, and Judah's leaders burn through their enemies like a torch dropped in dry straw.

Look at verse 5. Judah's leaders see where their strength comes from: God's people standing together “in the LORD of hosts their God.” Not their own army. That's the turn every man needs to make too.

The feeble made strong

he that is feeble → as David David was Israel's great warrior king, the boy who killed a giant.

And notice verse 7. God saves the country people in their tents first, so the big names in the capital can't boast. He starts with the ones nobody counts.

“And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

Part two

Whom they have pierced

Verses 10–14

The strongest people on earth that day are the ones who are weeping.

Grace comes first

Watch the order. God doesn't wait for them to get sorry and clean up. He moves first.

  1. Grace pouredGod pours out “the spirit of grace and of supplications”12:10
  2. They lookat the One they pierced12:10
  3. They mournas for an only son12:10–14
  4. Washeda fountain opened for sin13:1

“Supplications” means pleading prayers. Grace is what makes a man able to pray like that. Real sorrow for sin doesn't come from being beaten down. It comes from seeing how good God has been to us, and what our sin cost Him.

“Or despisest thou the riches of his goodness and forbearance and longsuffering; not knowing that the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance?”

Romans 2:4

“Me,” then “him”

The LORD is speaking. He says they will look on me, the One they pierced, then mourn for him. God Himself is pierced, and yet He is spoken of as another Person. The New Testament tells us who:

At the cross

“And again another scripture saith, They shall look on him whom they pierced.”

John 19:37

When He comes again

“Behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him: and all kindreds of the earth shall wail because of him. Even so, Amen.”

Revelation 1:7

A Roman spear pierced Jesus' side (John 19:34). This promise belongs to Israel: one day the nation will look on Jesus and know Him. Paul says it too: “And so all Israel shall be saved” (Romans 11:26). And the same grace is open to any man who will look at the cross today and see what his sin cost.

The worst day they remembered

Megiddo is a great valley and battlefield in the north. Hadadrimmon was a place near it. About 609 BC, good King Josiah rode out there against Egypt's army and was shot.

They carried him home to Jerusalem to die, and the whole nation wept.

“His servants therefore took him out of that chariot, and put him in the second chariot that he had; and they brought him to Jerusalem, and he died, and was buried in one of the sepulchres of his fathers. And all Judah and Jerusalem mourned for Josiah.”

2 Chronicles 35:24

That was the deepest grief Judah knew. The mourning “in that day” will be like it, but for the One they pierced.

Every family apart

Zechariah names families from the very top: the kings and the priests.

The house of David

12

The royal family itself.

The house of Nathan

12

Probably David's son Nathan (2 Samuel 5:14), another royal line.

The house of Levi

13

The tribe of the priests.

The family of Shimei

13

Levi's grandson (Numbers 3:18), one family of the priestly tribe.

Each family mourns by itself, and husbands and wives each by themselves. Nobody can repent for someone else. Your wife can't do it for you, and your mother's prayers can't do it for you. Every man has to look at the cross for himself.

Points to remember

  1. Who God is“Formeth the spirit of man within him” 12:1The God who spread out the sky made the inside of you too.
  2. Protection“A burdensome stone for all people” 12:3Whoever tries to carry off what is God's gets crushed by it.
  3. Weakness“He that is feeble … shall be as David” 12:8God makes the weak strong, and He starts with the ones nobody counts.
  4. Grace“The spirit of grace and of supplications” 12:10Grace comes first. Real sorrow for sin follows it.
  5. Christ“They shall look upon me whom they have pierced” 12:10The pierced One is Jesus, and He is the LORD.
  6. Repentance“Every family apart” 12:12Nobody can repent for you. Every man looks at the cross for himself.

Question 1 of 6

Who God is

Who is the God who makes these promises?

What verses tell us about this?

“Ah Lord GOD! behold, thou hast made the heaven and the earth by thy great power and stretched out arm, and there is nothing too hard for thee:”

Jeremiah 32:17

Answer

The One who stretched out the heavens, laid the foundation of the earth, and formed the spirit inside every man. Nothing He promises is too hard for Him, and nothing in us is hidden from Him. He made us, so He knows exactly how to fix us.

Question 2 of 6

Protection

What happens to those who come against God's people?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They stagger like men who drank a cup of trembling, and they're torn up like men trying to lift a stone too heavy for them. God defends Jerusalem Himself. That's a promise to Israel, and it shows us the kind of God we have: He doesn't abandon what is His.

Question 3 of 6

Weakness

What does God do with the feeble?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

Answer

He makes them strong, like David. And He saves the humble first, so the proud can't take the credit. Being weak isn't what keeps a man from God. Pretending to be strong does. His strength is made perfect in our weakness.

Question 4 of 6

Grace

Where does true sorrow for sin come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

“Or despisest thou the riches of his goodness and forbearance and longsuffering; not knowing that the goodness of God leadeth thee to repentance?”

Romans 2:4

Answer

From God's grace. He pours it out first, and then people look, and weep, and pray. Being sorry you got caught is the world's sorrow, and it leads nowhere good. Godly sorrow comes from seeing what our sin did to Jesus, and it leads to life.

Question 5 of 6

Christ

Who is the one “whom they have pierced”?

What verses tell us about this?

“And again another scripture saith, They shall look on him whom they pierced.”

John 19:37

“Behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him: and all kindreds of the earth shall wail because of him. Even so, Amen.”

Revelation 1:7

Answer

Jesus. John says so at the cross, and Revelation says every eye will see Him when He comes back. And notice who is speaking in Zechariah: the LORD says “me.” The One pierced on the cross is God the Son.

Question 6 of 6

Repentance

Why does every family mourn apart?

What verses tell us about this?

“So then every one of us shall give account of himself to God.”

Romans 14:12

Answer

Because repentance is personal. Kings and priests, husbands and wives, each one has to face the Lord for himself. Nobody can borrow someone else's faith or someone else's tears. But the good news is just as personal: whoever looks to Jesus is received.
