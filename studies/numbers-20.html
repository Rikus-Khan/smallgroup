Numbers 20

The rock struck twice, and Aaron’s last mountain

Miriam dies, and there is no water. The LORD says to speak to the rock, and Moses strikes it twice. Edom shuts the road. And on mount Hor, Aaron goes up in the sight of all the congregation and does not come down.

Part one

“Speak ye unto the rock”

Verses 1–13

A burial, a dry place, and the best man in the camp loses his temper.

Miriam

  1. At the seaMiriam the prophetess, with a timbrelExodus 15:20
  2. Speaking against Mosesmade leprous, healed, and shut out seven daysNumbers 12
  3. “Moses, Aaron, and Miriam”sent before IsraelMicah 6:4
  4. Kadesh“died there, and was buried there”20:1

The chapter names the month, “the first month,” and not the year. Aaron dies on mount Hor “in the fortieth year” (33:38), so we are at the end of the forty. Miriam gets one verse, but the LORD himself said of her: “I sent before thee Moses, Aaron, and Miriam” (Micah 6:4).

Moses doesn’t say what her death meant to him. But the very next verse says there was no water, and he had just buried his sister.

The sons talk like the fathers

Their fathers, at Rephidim

Exodus 17:3

“And the people thirsted there for water; and the people murmured against Moses, and said, Wherefore is this that thou hast brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our cattle with thirst?”

Now, at Kadesh

Numbers 20:4–5

“And why have ye brought up the congregation of the LORD into this wilderness, that we and our cattle should die there? And wherefore have ye made us to come up out of Egypt, to bring us in unto this evil place?”

Almost word for word. “Would God that we had died when our brethren died before the LORD!” (verse 3) means they wished they had died with the brethren who died before the LORD, in the judgments of chapter 16 and before. And “no place of seed, or of figs, or of vines, or of pomegranates” remembers the pomegranates and figs the spies carried back from Canaan (13:23): the land they wanted was the one they had been promised.

Many of us can hear our fathers in our own mouths, and our own worst habits in the way we complain. It does not have to run in the family.

To the door of the tabernacle

Moses and Aaron do not answer the crowd. They leave the assembly, go to the door of the tabernacle and fall on their faces, and “the glory of the LORD appeared unto them.” That is the right first move, and the LORD answers at once.

He says: take the rod, gather the assembly, and “speak ye unto the rock before their eyes; and it shall give forth his water.” It is the rod “from before the LORD” (verse 9). Moses doesn’t say whose it is, but the rod laid up before the LORD in this book is Aaron’s (17:10).

“Speak ye unto the rock.” Remember those three words.

Two things went right and two went wrong. Moses spoke to the people, not to the rock, and he said we: “must we fetch you water.” The LORD had said he would give the water, and Moses made it sound as if he and Aaron would.

The water came anyway

Look at the end of verse 11. “The water came out abundantly, and the congregation drank, and their beasts also.” The people were not punished for Moses’ mistake. The LORD kept his word to them, even though the man he sent had not kept his.

That is how God works. What he promised does not depend on the man at the rock. And the man at the rock, who has not done what he was told, still has water to give.

Smitten once, and now we speak to him

At Rephidim

Exodus 17:6

“Behold, I will stand before thee there upon the rock in Horeb; and thou shalt smite the rock, and there shall come water out of it, that the people may drink.”

At Kadesh

Numbers 20:8

“Take the rod… and speak ye unto the rock before their eyes; and it shall give forth his water.”

“And did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ.”

1 Corinthians 10:4

Paul says the Rock was Christ, and Hebrews says “Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many” (9:28). Put the two together, as the traditional teaching does: the first rock was smitten once, and the second was to be asked. A rock smitten twice spoils the picture. Paul doesn’t say this of verse 11, so it is a reading, not a rule.

Struck once, now we ask

“So Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many; and unto them that look for him shall he appear the second time without sin unto salvation.”

Hebrews 9:28

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

Christ has been struck once, for all, and nobody has to strike him again. Now we come and ask. There is no price left to pay at the rock.

“Because ye believed me not”

The LORD names the sin, and it is not temper alone. It is unbelief, and not honouring him before the people: “to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel.” To sanctify is to treat as holy. Moses had said “we.” And the cost is the land.

“They angered him also at the waters of strife, so that it went ill with Moses for their sakes: Because they provoked his spirit, so that he spake unadvisedly with his lips.”

Psalm 106:32–33

Meribah means strife: the psalm calls it “the waters of strife.” The people provoked Moses, and he spoke with his lips what he shouldn’t. “Unadvisedly” is without thinking it through. One sentence cost him the land.

And Hebrews says of that whole generation: “So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief” (3:19).

He asked, and the LORD said no

“I pray thee, let me go over, and see the good land that is beyond Jordan, that goodly mountain, and Lebanon. But the LORD was wroth with me for your sakes, and would not hear me: and the LORD said unto me, Let it suffice thee; speak no more unto me of this matter.”

Deuteronomy 3:25–26

Wroth is very angry. Moses pleaded, and the LORD did not give way. A man can be forgiven and still carry what his sin cost. That is not the LORD being unkind. Sin has a bill, and sometimes we pay it after we are forgiven.

He saw it, and he stood in it

“And the LORD said unto him, This is the land which I sware unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, saying, I will give it unto thy seed: I have caused thee to see it with thine eyes, but thou shalt not go over thither.”

Deuteronomy 34:4

“And, behold, there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him.”

Matthew 17:3

But the LORD did not throw him away. He let him see the whole land from the mountain. And in Matthew 17, on the mount of transfiguration, Moses stands in that land, talking with Jesus. He lost the land for a while, and he did not lose the LORD.

Part two

“Thou shalt not pass by me”

Verses 14–21

The closest road to the land belongs to a brother, and he says no.

The road through Edom

Edom lay across the way to the land. Mount Hor is “in the edge of the land of Edom” (33:37), “by the coast of the land of Edom” (20:23).

We don’t put Kadesh on the map. The Bible says where it is, and no more: “in the desert of Zin” (20:1), “the wilderness of Zin, which is Kadesh” (33:36), and “a city in the uttermost of thy border” (20:16), the farthest edge of Edom’s country.

“Thy brother Israel”

Moses calls the king of Edom his brother, and that is the Bible’s own word for the people:

“Now these are the generations of Esau, who is Edom.”

Genesis 36:1

“Thou shalt not abhor an Edomite; for he is thy brother: thou shalt not abhor an Egyptian; because thou wast a stranger in his land.”

Deuteronomy 23:7

Edom came from Esau, Jacob’s brother. And Moses puts his case to him in four verses (14–17): our fathers went down into Egypt, the Egyptians vexed us, we cried to the LORD, and “he heard our voice, and sent an angel, and hath brought us forth out of Egypt” (verse 16). It is a fair, honest, humble request, and when it is refused, they offer to pay for any water (verse 19).

Asked twice, and refused twice

IsraelLet us pass… we will go by the king’s high way, we will not turn to the right hand nor to the left.

EdomThou shalt not pass by me, lest I come out against thee with the sword.

IsraelWe will go by the high way: and if I and my cattle drink of thy water, then I will pay for it.

EdomThou shalt not go through. and he came out with a strong hand

“Israel turned away from him”

Israel was a great host: when they were numbered soon after, the men able to go to war came to 601,730 (26:2, 51). They could have gone through. Verse 21 says what they did: “Israel turned away from him.” The LORD had told them to leave their brother alone:

“Meddle not with them; for I will not give you of their land, no, not so much as a footbreadth; because I have given mount Seir unto Esau for a possession.”

Deuteronomy 2:5

Israel’s road went round: a refusal that sent them the long way round Edom (21:4). They took it, and did not take revenge. “If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men” (Romans 12:18).

Part three

“Aaron shall be gathered unto his people”

Verses 22–29

The high priest goes up the mountain with his son and his brother.

The life of Aaron

  1. 83before PharaohExodus 7:7
  2. The golden calfmade at the foot of SinaiExodus 32
  3. High priestwashed, clothed, anointedLeviticus 8
  4. Nadab and Abihuhis sons dieLeviticus 10
  5. The rodbuds for the house of LeviNumbers 17

From the day he stood before Pharaoh at eighty-three to the day he died at a hundred and twenty-three is forty years. He “died there, in the fortieth year after the children of Israel were come out of the land of Egypt, in the first day of the fifth month” (33:38). And the people mourned him thirty days.

“Aaron the saint of the LORD”

“They envied Moses also in the camp, and Aaron the saint of the LORD.”

Psalm 106:16

“And the LORD was very angry with Aaron to have destroyed him: and I prayed for Aaron also the same time.”

Deuteronomy 9:20

He made the calf. He lost two sons. He rebelled at Meribah. And the psalm still calls him “the saint of the LORD,” and Moses says he prayed for him. At the end he went up the mountain when he was told, in the sight of everyone, and Moses “did as the LORD commanded.” A man’s failures are not the last word on him.

The garments go on, and the priest goes on

Moses strips Aaron of his garments on the mountain and puts them on Eleazar. The man dies; the office does not. A priest lasts only as long as his life.

“And they truly were many priests, because they were not suffered to continue by reason of death: But this man, because he continueth ever, hath an unchangeable priesthood. Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:23–25

Aaron could not stay. Our high priest, Jesus, “continueth ever.” No mountain, no last day, no one to pass his garments on to. “To the uttermost” is farthest, to the end of whatever a man has done.

Real world: when you want to strike the rock

Moses had been leading these people for forty years. He had buried his sister. They were at him again. And he did the wrong thing in front of everybody.

  • SinAnger that says “we” when it should say “he.” Fixing it ourselves, and getting the credit.
  • RegretOne loud moment can cost a man something he wanted for years. If you know that, you are not alone: Moses knew it.
  • GraceThe water still came. The LORD let Moses see the land, and Moses stood in it with Jesus. Aaron is still “the saint of the LORD.”
  • HopeThe Rock was struck once. Now we may ask, and there is a throne of grace for it.
  • TodayBefore you answer the person who is at you, do what Moses and Aaron did first: go to the door and fall on your face. Then speak, and keep it soft. “A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger” (Proverbs 15:1). And call a brother before you strike.

Points to remember

  1. No water“Miriam died there, and was buried there. And there was no water for the congregation” 20:1–2The sons said what their fathers said. A bad day makes an old habit speak.
  2. The command“speak ye unto the rock before their eyes; and it shall give forth his water” 20:8The LORD said speak. The rock was smitten once, and Christ was offered once.
  3. The rock“Because ye believed me not, to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel” 20:12Moses struck twice and said “we.” The water came, and he lost the land.
  4. Edom“Thou shalt not go through… wherefore Israel turned away from him” 20:20–21Israel was refused and turned away, as the LORD had said.
  5. Mount Hor“Moses stripped Aaron of his garments, and put them upon Eleazar his son; and Aaron died there in the top of the mount” 20:28The man dies and the office goes on. Our high priest continues ever.
  6. The reason“he shall not enter into the land… because ye rebelled against my word at the water of Meribah” 20:24Even a saint answers for his one bad day. And the LORD still holds him.

Question 1 of 6

No water

Who died at Kadesh, and what did the people say to Moses and Aaron when there was no water?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Miriam. And the people chode with Moses: “Would God that we had died when our brethren died before the LORD!” They asked why he had brought them up “unto this evil place.”

Question 2 of 6

The command

What did the LORD tell Moses to take, and what was he to do at the rock?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The rod, and he was to gather the assembly with Aaron and speak to the rock before their eyes. It would give forth its water, and the congregation and their beasts would drink.

Question 3 of 6

The rock

What did Moses do instead, and what did the LORD say it showed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He smote the rock twice, and the water came out abundantly. The LORD said, “Because ye believed me not, to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel”: Moses and Aaron would not bring the congregation into the land.

Question 4 of 6

Edom

What did Israel ask of the king of Edom, and what was his answer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To pass through his country by the king’s high way, and to pay for any water. Edom said no, and came out with much people and a strong hand. So “Israel turned away from him.”

Question 5 of 6

Mount Hor

Where did Aaron die, and what was done with his garments?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In the top of mount Hor, by the coast of the land of Edom. Moses stripped Aaron of his garments and put them upon Eleazar his son, and the congregation mourned thirty days. The man dies; our high priest “continueth ever” (Hebrews 7:24).

Question 6 of 6

The reason

Why was Aaron not to enter the land?

What verses tell us about this?

“They envied Moses also in the camp, and Aaron the saint of the LORD.”

Psalm 106:16

Answer

“Because ye rebelled against my word at the water of Meribah.” Aaron was there at the rock with Moses. Yet the psalm still calls him “the saint of the LORD.”
