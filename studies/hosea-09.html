Hosea 9

Rejoice not

The party is over. Israel will be carried off, the feasts will stop, and the people named “fruitful” will bear no fruit. Yet God remembers when He first found them, like grapes in the wilderness.

Part one

The party is over

Verses 1–6

Harvest festival, and the prophet says, “Rejoice not.”

Who paid for the harvest?

Picture harvest time: the threshing floors piled with grain, the winepresses running, a festival with music and drinking. Hosea probably stood up in the middle of it and shouted, “Rejoice not!”

What Israel thought

“Baal paid us”

They thanked the fertility god for the crop, like a harlot's wages on every threshing floor (cornfloor), and partied in his honor.

What God said

“I gave her corn”

“For she did not know that I gave her corn, and wine, and oil, and multiplied her silver and gold, which they prepared for Baal” (Hosea 2:8).

So the very things they celebrated would fail: “the new wine shall fail in her.” The party that leaves God out always runs dry. A lot of us have stood in a crowd laughing and felt empty inside. “Even in laughter the heart is sorrowful” (Proverbs 14:13). God offers a joy that doesn't fail when the bottle does.

Out of the LORD's land

The land was God's gift. Living in it, near His house and His feasts, was part of belonging to Him. Now Israel would be taken out of it.

Most would be carried off to Assyria, and that happened in about 722 BC (2 Kings 17:6). In a foreign land they'd “eat unclean things”, with no way to keep God's law about food.

Others would run to Egypt for safety, and die there: “Memphis shall bury them.” Memphis was famous for its huge burial grounds. Back home, weeds and thorns would grow in the houses they'd left.

No more feast days

In exile there would be no offerings and no feasts. Their food would be like “the bread of mourners”: food in a house where someone had died, which the law counted unclean. It might fill their stomachs, but it could never come into God's house.

God asks a question they couldn't answer: “What will ye do in the solemn day?” When the feast comes around and there's no temple, no altar, no celebration, what will you do?

Some of us know that feeling: a holiday that used to mean something, now empty, because of where our choices took us. That emptiness can be the place where we start to miss God, and turn back to Him.

Part two

The prophet is a fool

Verses 7–9

When a people hates the truth, they call the messenger crazy.

Shoot the messenger

A watchman stood on the city wall and shouted when danger was coming. God had sent Hosea as Israel's watchman, a man “with my God.”

But the people said the prophet was a fool, the man of the Spirit was crazy. They set traps for him like a bird catcher, and hated him even in God's house. When a man doesn't want to hear the truth, it's easier to call the one telling it a lunatic.

“The days of visitation” means the days when God comes to settle accounts. “Israel shall know it”: when the judgment came, they'd know who had been telling the truth.

Who in your life has told you the hard truth? A sponsor, a wife, a pastor, a brother? Maybe they weren't the crazy ones after all.

“As in the days of Gibeah.”

Every Israelite knew what that meant. Centuries before, in the town of Gibeah, a mob abused a traveler's wife all night until she died. It led to a civil war that nearly wiped out the tribe of Benjamin (Judges 19–21). It was the darkest night in Israel's memory.

“In those days there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes.”

Judges 21:25

Hosea says Israel had sunk that low again: “deeply corrupted.” When every man does what's right in his own eyes, it always ends somewhere like Gibeah. But even Gibeah wasn't the end of Israel's story. God was still at work.

Part three

Grapes in the wilderness

Verses 10–14

God remembers the day He found them, and how it went wrong.

A delight, then a shame

Imagine crossing a desert and finding a cluster of grapes. Or tasting the first ripe fig of the year. That's how God describes the day He found Israel: pure delight.

Baal, “lord” → “that shame”

Then, still in the wilderness, at a place called Peor, Israel's men slept with the women of Moab and bowed to their god, Baal (Numbers 25:1–3). Hosea won't even give Baal his name. He calls him “that shame.”

“Their abominations were according as they loved”: they became as filthy as the thing they loved. But don't miss where this verse starts. God's first thought of His people was delight. That's still how He thinks of the men He's redeemed.

The hardest verses

These are painful words, and we won't rush past them. Ephraim means “fruitful.” Their glory was their many children and strong families. Now Hosea sees that glory flying off like a startled bird.

He saw what was coming. When Assyria invaded, it was merciless, and many children died (2 Kings 15:16 tells of the same horrors in those years). Hosea knew that families raised in Ephraim would be raised “to the murderer.”

So the prophet prays a terrible prayer (v. 14): Give them... what? And he answers: better no more children than children born for slaughter. It's the prayer of a man whose heart is breaking for his people.

If you have lost a child, or lost your children through your own choices, hear this: God doesn't take that grief lightly. These verses grieve too. And the worst line here is “woe also to them when I depart from them.” The real disaster was losing God. That's the one loss Jesus came to make sure no believer ever suffers: “I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee” (Hebrews 13:5).

Part four

Wanderers

Verses 15–17

The fruitful one, dried up at the root.

Fruitful, fruitless

Ephraim, “fruitful” → “no fruit” Joseph named his son Ephraim, “For God hath caused me to be fruitful” (Genesis 41:52). Hosea turns the name inside out.

Gilgal was where Israel first camped in the promised land, and where Saul was made king. By Hosea's day it was a center of idol worship (Hosea 4:15). The place where it all began had become the place where it all went wrong.

A tree with a dead root can't bear fruit, no matter how hard it tries. Neither can a man cut off from God:

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.”

John 15:5

Not the last word

“I will love them no more” is God speaking as a husband who has been betrayed for years: He would drive them out of His house, the land. And He did. Judgment was real.

But keep reading Hosea. Two chapters later God says, “How shall I give thee up, Ephraim? ... mine heart is turned within me” (11:8). And the book ends, “I will heal their backsliding, I will love them freely” (14:4).

God keeps His word to Israel (Romans 11:1). And the same God holds on to everyone who comes to Him through Jesus. Your worst chapter isn't your last one.

Points to remember

  1. False joy“Rejoice not, O Israel, for joy, as other people” 9:1A party that leaves God out always runs dry.
  2. Exile“They shall not dwell in the LORD's land” 9:3Running from God costs a man his home, his worship, and his joy.
  3. God's messengers“The prophet is a fool, the spiritual man is mad” 9:7The one telling us the hard truth may be the one God sent.
  4. First love“I found Israel like grapes in the wilderness” 9:10God's first thought of His people was delight. We become like what we love.
  5. Fruit“Their root is dried up, they shall bear no fruit” 9:16Cut off from God, we can do nothing. Abiding in Christ, we bear much fruit.
  6. Hope“I will love them freely” 14:4Judgment was real, but God's love has the last word.

Question 1 of 6

False joy

Why did God tell Israel not to rejoice?

What verses tell us about this?

“Even in laughter the heart is sorrowful; and the end of that mirth is heaviness.”

Proverbs 14:13

Answer

Because they were celebrating the harvest as if Baal had paid for it, and God was about to take it away. Their joy had no root in God, so it couldn't last. The party without God always ends in heaviness. Real joy comes from the One who gives the harvest.

Question 2 of 6

Exile

What would Israel lose when they left the LORD's land?

What verses tell us about this?

“In the ninth year of Hoshea the king of Assyria took Samaria, and carried Israel away into Assyria, and placed them in Halah and in Habor by the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes.”

2 Kings 17:6

Answer

Their home, their worship, their feasts, and their joy. In Assyria they'd eat unclean food, with no offerings and no feast days. Those who fled to Egypt would be buried there. Running from God never takes a man anywhere better than where he started.

Question 3 of 6

God's messengers

How did Israel treat God's prophets?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They called the prophet a fool and the man of the Spirit crazy, set traps for him, and hated him in God's own house. They didn't want the message, so they attacked the messenger. When the judgment came, they found out who had been telling the truth.

Question 4 of 6

First love

How did God first see Israel, and what went wrong?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Israel joined himself unto Baal-peor: and the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel.”

Numbers 25:3

Answer

As a delight, like grapes found in the desert. But they gave themselves to Baal and became as foul as the thing they loved. We become like what we love most. That's why it matters so much what, and whom, we give our hearts to.

Question 5 of 6

Fruit

Why does a people cut off from God bear no fruit?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.”

John 15:5

Answer

Because the root is dried up. Fruit doesn't come from effort, it comes from life flowing through the root. Ephraim, “fruitful,” cut itself off from God and became fruitless. In Christ it's the other way around: abide in Him, and the fruit comes.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

Is “I will love them no more” God's last word?

What verses tell us about this?

“How shall I give thee up, Ephraim? how shall I deliver thee, Israel? how shall I make thee as Admah? how shall I set thee as Zeboim? mine heart is turned within me, my repentings are kindled together.”

Hosea 11:8

“I will heal their backsliding, I will love them freely: for mine anger is turned away from him.”

Hosea 14:4

Answer

No. God really did drive Israel out of the land. But His heart never let go of them, and the book ends with “I will love them freely.” God keeps His word to Israel, and He holds on to everyone who comes to Him through Jesus.
