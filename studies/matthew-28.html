Matthew 28

He is risen, as he said

The stone rolls away and the guards fall like dead men. The women run with fear and great joy, the priests buy a cover story, and the risen Lord sends His men to all nations.

Part one

He is not here

Verses 1–10

Two women come to see a grave. They leave running, and Jesus Himself meets them on the road.

The third day

The Jews counted any part of a day as a day. So Friday, Saturday, and Sunday morning are “the third day,” just as Jesus had said.

  1. FridayHe dies about 3 p.m. and is buried before sundown27:50–60
  2. SaturdayThe sabbath. The stone is sealed and the guard is set.27:62–66
  3. Sunday, at dawn“He is not here: for he is risen”28:1–6

The two Marys had watched Him buried on Friday (27:61). They rested on the sabbath, as the Law said. At first light on Sunday they were back.

That's why Christians gather on the first day of the week. Every Sunday is a reminder that the grave is empty.

He sat on the stone

The great stone, Rome's seal, the armed watch: an angel moves it all and then sits on the stone, like a man resting on a beaten enemy.

The keepers were there to guard a dead man. They shook and “became as dead men,” and the dead Man was alive.

The stone wasn't rolled away so Jesus could get out. He was already gone. It was rolled away so the women could look in.

“As he said”

The angel didn't bring the women a new idea. He reminded them of what Jesus had told them again and again:

  1. ✓and be killed, and be raised again the third day.Matthew 16:2116:21
  2. ✓And they shall kill him, and the third day he shall be raised again.Matthew 17:2317:23
  3. ✓to mock, and to scourge, and to crucify him: and the third day he shall rise again.Matthew 20:1920:19
  4. ✓But after I am risen again, I will go before you into Galilee.Matthew 26:3228:7, 16

Then two commands: “Come, see” and “go quickly, and tell.” That's the Christian life in four words. Look for yourself, then tell somebody. Jesus keeps His word, every word.

Still family

Three nights before, in the garden, every one of them ran:

“all the disciples forsook him, and fled” → “my brethren” Matthew 26:56, then 28:10

The first thing the risen Jesus says about them is a family name. No list of who ran. No speech about who denied Him. “He is not ashamed to call them brethren” (Hebrews 2:11).

If you've run from Him, and most of us have, this is how He talks about you when you come back. He's gone on ahead to meet you.

Part two

The story they paid for

Verses 11–15

The guards tell the truth. The chief priests pay them to tell something else.

A story with holes in it

Notice what the priests never said: “The tomb isn't empty.” Everyone knew it was. They just needed a reason.

“His disciples … stole him away”the same frightened men who had fled and were hiding (26:56)Doesn't hold
“while we slept”if you were asleep, how do you know who took Him?Doesn't hold
“we will … secure you”sleeping on guard could cost a soldier his life; they needed protection to say it (v. 14)Doesn't hold

Thirty pieces of silver bought the betrayal. “Large money” bought the cover-up. Money can buy a story. It can't fill a grave. And men who steal a body don't spend the rest of their lives, and give their lives, saying He is alive.

Part three

Go ye therefore

Verses 16–20

On a mountain in Galilee, eleven men, some of them still unsure, get their marching orders and a promise.

Back to Galilee

Three times they were told to go to Galilee (26:32; 28:7, 10). It's about a three-day walk from Jerusalem. They went, and He was there. Matthew doesn't name the mountain.

Some of them still weren't sure. Jesus didn't send the doubters home. Verse 18 says “Jesus came”: He walked toward them.

“And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.”

Mark 9:24

Marching orders

Because“All power is given unto me”
→
therefore“Go ye”
  1. Gowherever your life takes you
  2. Teach all nationsmake disciples: the main command
  3. Baptizing themin the one name of the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost
  4. Teaching themto observe all He commanded

In chapter 10 He sent them only to “the lost sheep of the house of Israel” (10:6). Now it's all nations. And notice: one name, not three names. One God, in three persons.

Believe first, then be baptized

  1. Hearthe good news about Jesus
  2. Believesaved, and the Spirit comes to live in you
  3. Be baptizedthe first step of obeying Him
  4. Be taughtlearn to observe all He said

“Then they that gladly received his word were baptized: and the same day there were added unto them about three thousand souls.”

Acts 2:41

Baptism doesn't save anybody. The thief on the cross was never baptized, and Jesus promised him paradise (Luke 23:43). “By grace are ye saved through faith” (Ephesians 2:8). Baptism is how a saved man goes public: a picture of being buried with Christ and raised to a new life (Romans 6:4). If you've believed and haven't been baptized, that's your next step. Talk to your pastor.

God with us, from start to finish

Matthew 1:23, the first chapter

“… and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us.”

Matthew 28:20, the last verse

“… and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.”

Matthew opens and closes with the same promise. Alway means all the days: the good ones, the hard ones, the day you're tempted, the day you fall and get back up.

He didn't send His men out alone, and He doesn't send you out alone. Every believer has His Spirit living in him, and that won't change, “even unto the end of the world.”

Points to remember

  1. As He said“He is risen, as he said.” 28:6Jesus told them ahead of time, and He kept His word.
  2. Fear not“Fear not ye: for I know that ye seek Jesus” 28:5Those who seek Jesus have nothing to fear. Come and see, then go and tell.
  3. Brethren“Go tell my brethren” 28:10The men who ran were still His family.
  4. The cover story“They gave large money unto the soldiers” 28:12Money bought a story. It couldn't fill the grave.
  5. Some doubted“But some doubted. And Jesus came” 28:17–18Jesus comes toward doubters, not away from them.
  6. Go“Lo, I am with you alway” 28:20Make disciples everywhere. He goes with us every day.

Question 1 of 6

As He said

How do we know Jesus meant to rise again?

What verses tell us about this?

“And shall deliver him to the Gentiles to mock, and to scourge, and to crucify him: and the third day he shall rise again.”

Matthew 20:19

“But after I am risen again, I will go before you into Galilee.”

Matthew 26:32

Answer

He said so, plainly and more than once, before it happened. He even told them where He'd meet them afterward. Even His enemies remembered it (27:63). The angel's words are the proof that Jesus keeps His word: “He is risen, as he said.”

Question 2 of 6

Fear not

What did the angel tell the women, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Fear not.” The guards shook with terror, but the women were seeking Jesus, and those who seek Him have nothing to fear. Then: come and see the empty place for yourselves, and go quickly and tell the others. See it, then say it.

Question 3 of 6

Brethren

What did Jesus call the men who had run away from Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“But all this was done, that the scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled. Then all the disciples forsook him, and fled.”

Matthew 26:56

“For both he that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one: for which cause he is not ashamed to call them brethren,”

Hebrews 2:11

Answer

“My brethren.” They had all forsaken Him and fled, and Peter had denied Him. The first time the risen Lord speaks of them, He calls them family. He isn't ashamed of them, and He isn't ashamed of a man who comes back to Him today.

Question 4 of 6

The cover story

What did the chief priests do when they heard the truth?

What verses tell us about this?

“After that, he was seen of above five hundred brethren at once; of whom the greater part remain unto this present, but some are fallen asleep.”

1 Corinthians 15:6

Answer

They heard it from their own guards, and they paid to bury it. They never claimed the tomb was full; they bought a story to explain why it was empty. But the risen Jesus was seen by hundreds of people. A man can pay to hide from the truth. He can't make it go away.

Question 5 of 6

Some doubted

What did Jesus do with the disciples who doubted?

What verses tell us about this?

“And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.”

Mark 9:24

Answer

He came to them. He didn't wait for every doubt to clear, and He didn't leave the doubters out of the commission. He walked toward them and spoke to all of them. Honest doubt brought to Jesus isn't the end of faith. Bring Him what you've got.

Question 6 of 6

Go

What did Jesus send us to do, and what did He promise?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then they that gladly received his word were baptized: and the same day there were added unto them about three thousand souls.”

Acts 2:41

Answer

To make disciples of all nations: tell people about Him, baptize those who believe, and teach them to obey Him. Baptism follows faith; it doesn't save. And the promise: He is with us always, every single day, to the end. We never go alone.
