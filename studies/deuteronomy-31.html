Deuteronomy 31

Be strong, the law written, and a song for a witness

Moses is an hundred and twenty years old. The LORD goes before, Joshua is charged, and the law is written and given to the priests to be read every seventh year. And the LORD tells Moses, before they have even crossed over, that this people will turn away, so a song is written to stand as a witness.

Part one

“I am an hundred and twenty years old this day”

Verses 1–8

Moses can go no further, and tells Israel and Joshua who will.

“I can no more go out and come in”

“I can no more go out and come in” does not mean he was worn out. At his death “his eye was not dim, nor his natural force abated” (34:7). It means what the LORD said next: “Thou shalt not go over this Jordan.”

He says it without complaint. The reason was given at Meribah: “Because ye believed me not, to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel, therefore ye shall not bring this congregation into the land” (Numbers 20:12). Even the best man answers for his sin.

Who goes over first

The LORD thy God, he will go over before thee

Moses names two leaders, in order. First the LORD, who “will destroy these nations from before thee”. Then Joshua, “as the LORD hath said”. Moses is leaving, but the one who led them out of Egypt is going in with them.

And the proof is already behind them: “as he did to Sihon and to Og, kings of the Amorites” (Numbers 21). What he has done is the ground for what he will do.

Courage, and the reason for it

The command“Be strong and of a good courage, fear not, nor be afraid of them”
→
The reason, after “for”“the LORD thy God, he it is that doth go with thee; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee”

“Be strong and of a good courage” comes three times in this chapter (6, 7, 23): to the people, to Joshua before them all, and to Joshua at the tabernacle. Each time there is a reason, and the reason is never “you can do it”. It is that the LORD goes with him.

“Fail” here means let down, give out on. A man in recovery knows what it is to be given up on, and to give up on others. This is the promise from someone who does not.

The promise passed down the line

  1. 31:6To all Israelby Moses
  2. 31:8To Joshuabefore them all
  3. Joshua 1:5To Joshua againafter Moses' death
  4. Hebrews 13:5To every believer“for he hath said”

“There shall not any man be able to stand before thee all the days of thy life: as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee: I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.”

Joshua 1:5

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

The writer of Hebrews says “he hath said”, and takes Moses' words as spoken to us. “Conversation” there means way of life, conduct. The promise holds for the man who has lost his job, his family or his standing: he “will never leave thee”.

Part two

“Moses wrote this law”

Verses 9–13

The law is written down, given to the priests, and read to all Israel every seven years.

Who wrote the law

“For had ye believed Moses, ye would have believed me: for he wrote of me. But if ye believe not his writings, how shall ye believe my words?”

John 5:46–47

The book says so itself, twice, and Jesus says so too: “he wrote of me”, and “his writings”. Jesus answered the devil from this book with “It is written” (Matthew 4:4, 7, 10). We read it as Moses wrote it, and as the Word of God.

Every seventh year

  1. 1year
  2. 2year
  3. 3year
  4. 4year
  5. 5year
  6. 6year
  7. 7year of releasethe law read aloud

Solemnity means a set time kept with ceremony. This is “the year of release” we met in chapter 15, when debts were let go, and it falls at the feast of tabernacles (we saw the feast in Leviticus 23 and chapter 16). It is read “in the place which he shall choose”, where all Israel appears before the LORD.

Think of it: a whole nation standing together, hearing this law read aloud, once in seven years, at a time of debts forgiven and booths of branches. Hearing God's Word together was part of the nation's life.

Hear, learn, fear, do

  1. hear“that they may hear”
  2. learn“and that they may learn”
  3. fear the LORD“and fear the LORD your God”
  4. do“and observe to do all the words of this law”

Everyone was to be there: “men, and women, and children, and thy stranger that is within thy gates”, and also the little ones “which have not known any thing”. A child who knows nothing yet is brought in to hear. That is how a nation, or a family, passes the Word on.

Hearing comes first, and the Word is made to be heard. That is what we do together every day: we read it out loud, and we hear it.

Part three

“Behold, thy days approach that thou must die”

Verses 14–23

At the tabernacle, the LORD charges Joshua, and tells Moses what is coming.

At the door of the tabernacle

The LORD appeared in a pillar of a cloud

Moses and Joshua “presented themselves in the tabernacle of the congregation”, and the LORD came to the door in the cloud. He did not send word. He came himself to give Joshua his charge, as he had come to Moses at the burning bush, and again on the mount.

“Charge” means a command to carry out, an office handed over. A leader is not made by a title but by the LORD's own word, spoken to him in the place of meeting.

Told before it happened

The LORD tells Moses something hard before the people have crossed Jordan. “Sleep with thy fathers” is a plain way of saying die, and after that this people “will rise up, and go a whoring after the gods of the strangers”: going a whoring is the Bible's word for a people that belongs to the LORD going after other gods, like a wife who leaves her husband.

“I will hide my face” (18) is the sentence on that day, and then the sad question in their own mouths: “Are not these evils come upon us, because our God is not among us?”

God's knowing is not his causing. “I know their imagination which they go about, even now, before I have brought them into the land” (21): they had not yet done it. He says it ahead so the warning is in their hands, and the way back (chapter 30) is already written.

How it goes wrong in a good land

  1. Brought ina land that floweth with milk and honey
  2. Eaten and filledthey have all they need
  3. Waxen fatcomfortable and sure of themselves
  4. Turn“unto other gods, and serve them”
  5. Provoke“and break my covenant”

“Waxen fat” means grown fat, well fed and settled. It is not the hard years that undo them but the good ones. We saw this in chapter 8:

“Then thine heart be lifted up, and thou forget the LORD thy God, which brought thee forth out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage;”

Deuteronomy 8:14

The song in the next chapter will sing this very road.

A song that cannot be forgotten

A song for a witness

The word song comes five times in this chapter (19 twice, 21, 22, 30), and a witness three times (19, 21, 26). The song is to “testify against them as a witness; for it shall not be forgotten out of the mouths of their seed”. A law book can be put on a shelf, and a song gets into the head and stays there.

“Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom; teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord.”

Colossians 3:16

The song is in the next chapter. And the New Testament still uses song as a way to teach and warn one another. Put the Word in a form you will still carry on the bad day: a verse, a hymn, a line you can sing in the truck.

Joshua charged twice

By Moses, in the sight of all Israel (7–8)

“thou must go with this people”

Before the whole people, Moses hands Joshua his task: bring them to the land and “cause them to inherit it”.

By the LORD, at the tabernacle (23)

“and I will be with thee”

The LORD himself gives Joshua the charge, and adds what Moses could not: his own presence.

Moses can give a man a task, a name and a blessing in front of everyone. Only the LORD can say “I will be with thee”. Every man called to something hard, a job, a family, a recovery, needs both, the brothers who stand with him and the LORD who goes with him.

Part four

“Take this book of the law”

Verses 24–30

The book goes beside the ark, as a witness, and Moses speaks the song.

A witness against thee

What the book does

“a witness against thee”

Put in the side of the ark, it stands where the LORD dwells, and says what he said. It shows what a man has done against it. “By the law is the knowledge of sin” (Romans 3:20).

What it is for

“to bring us unto Christ”

“Wherefore the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ, that we might be justified by faith” (Galatians 3:24).

The law is a witness against us, and it is meant to be. It tells the truth about what a man is, so that he will run to the one who is able to do something about it. The ark of the covenant was where the LORD met his people, and the book beside it said why they needed him to.

“How much more after my death?”

Moses speaks plainly. “Stiff neck” is the picture of an ox that will not be turned, and he has seen it for forty years: “ye have been rebellious against the LORD” (27), even while he stood with them. The question, “how much more after my death?”, is an honest one.

He asks them all to gather, elders and officers, “that I may speak these words in their ears, and call heaven and earth to record against them” (28). He calls the heavens and the earth as witnesses, just as he did in chapter 30:19, once as an offer, now as a warning. The same two witnesses stand on both sides of the choice.

It is an old man's love: he says the hard thing to the people he has led, because he would rather they heard it from him than learned it later. “The latter days” (29) means the days still to come. And he says it with the song already in hand.

Real world: be strong, and keep the Word near

  • SinWe drift when we are comfortable: “eaten and filled… waxen fat… then will they turn”. The good days are as dangerous as the bad ones, because we stop leaning.
  • RegretMoses missed the land for one sin and said so without bitterness. If you carry something you cannot undo, say it plainly and keep walking with the LORD.
  • Grace“He will not fail thee, nor forsake thee.” Moses said it to Israel, the LORD said it to Joshua, and Hebrews says it to every believer: “I will never leave thee”.
  • HopeEven when God told Moses how it would go wrong, he had already written the way back (chapter 30) and the song. His mercy comes before our failures.
  • TodayFace one thing you are afraid of today with these words: “the LORD thy God, he it is that doth go with thee.” Say the verse out loud, and read the Word with your brothers. If it is heavy, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today.

Points to remember

  1. Moses' age“I am an hundred and twenty years old this day… Thou shalt not go over this Jordan” 31:2Moses was an hundred and twenty, and the LORD had shut him out of the land.
  2. Be strong“the LORD thy God, he it is that doth go with thee; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee” 31:6The courage is borrowed from the one who goes with us, and Hebrews 13:5 says it to every believer.
  3. The law written“Moses wrote this law, and delivered it unto the priests the sons of Levi” 31:9Written by Moses, and read to all Israel every seventh year, at the feast of tabernacles.
  4. The warning“this people will rise up, and go a whoring after the gods of the strangers of the land” 31:16After Moses' death, once they were fat in the land, they would turn to other gods.
  5. The song“this song may be a witness for me against the children of Israel” 31:19A song teaches and warns, and “shall not be forgotten out of the mouths of their seed”.
  6. The book by the ark“put it in the side of the ark of the covenant of the LORD your God, that it may be there for a witness against thee” 31:26The law is a witness against us, and a schoolmaster to bring us to Christ.

Question 1 of 6

Moses' age

How old was Moses, and what had the LORD said to him about Jordan?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

An hundred and twenty years old. The LORD had said, “Thou shalt not go over this Jordan.”

Question 2 of 6

Be strong

Why were Israel and Joshua told to be strong and of a good courage?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the LORD goes with them and before them: “he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee”. The courage rests on his presence.

Question 3 of 6

The law written

What did Moses do with the law, and when was it to be read?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He wrote it and gave it to the priests the sons of Levi and the elders. It was to be read to all Israel at the end of every seven years, in the year of release, at the feast of tabernacles.

Question 4 of 6

The warning

What did the LORD say this people would do after Moses' death?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That they would go a whoring after the gods of the strangers of the land, forsake him and break his covenant: when they had eaten and filled themselves and waxen fat, they would turn to other gods.

Question 5 of 6

The song

What was the song for, and why was it to be taught to the children?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It was to be a witness for the LORD against the children of Israel, and it “shall not be forgotten out of the mouths of their seed”.

Question 6 of 6

The book by the ark

Where was the book of the law to be put, and what would it be there?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In the side of the ark of the covenant of the LORD, where it would be “for a witness against thee”.
