Titus 1

Put in order, and put in good hands

Paul leaves a young helper on an island with a bad reputation. The job: find men who are growing up, and build something solid. God does not write off a rough place, or a rough past.

The letter to Titus

“Paul, a servant of God, and an apostle of Jesus Christ … To Titus, mine own son after the common faith”

Written from
on the road, a free man again. The letter doesn't say where.
When
about AD 62 to 64, after Paul was let out of his Roman house arrest
To whom
Titus, a Greek, one of Paul's trusted helpers, left on the island of Crete (1:5)
Why
New churches in the towns, and nobody yet in charge. Titus had to set things in order.

Where Titus fits in Paul's life

  1. about AD 59 to 60Ship passes CretePaul, a prisoner, sails by (Acts 27)
  2. about 60 to 62House arrest in RomeEphesians, Colossians, Philemon
  3. about 62 to 64Paul free; Titus on Cretethis letter, with 1 Timothy
  4. about 64Rome burnsNero turns on the Christians
  5. about 66 to 67Paul's last letter2 Timothy

Paul had sailed past Crete as a prisoner. Now he is free, and he has been on the island himself, preaching. He can't stay, so he leaves Titus behind to finish the work. Nobody cheers for the man who stays and builds, and that was Titus's job.

Titus is one of three pastoral letters, with 1 and 2 Timothy: an older man writing to a younger one about how to lead a church and how to live.

Part one

A job, and the men for it

Verses 1–9

Paul's first concern for a young church is not buildings or programs. It is the kind of men who will lead it.

A son, a Greek, one faith

Titus was not a Jew. He was a Greek, and probably came to Christ through Paul, who calls him “mine own son.” Not a servant, not a hired man: a son.

The common faith. The same for a Jew and a Greek, a pastor and a man just out of prison.

Notice verse 2: the hope of eternal life was promised by “God, that cannot lie.” That is the ground under every man in the room. Your hope does not depend on how steady you feel today. It depends on a promise made before the world began, by One who can't lie.

An elder in every city

Crete was a long island with many towns, and little churches had started in them. They had believers but no leaders. “The things that are wanting” describes a house that is half built.

An elder is an older, seasoned believer who looks after the others. The Bible also calls the same job a bishop, or overseer (verse 7). The plan is not one big man at the top. It is a steady man in every town.

God builds slowly and locally, with ordinary men who show up in their own city.

What an elder looks like

  1. At homefaithful to one wife; children who are not wild
  2. Temper and habitsnot selfwilled, not quick to anger, not a drinker, not a fighter, not in it for money
  3. With peoplean open door, a friend to good men, fair and sober
  4. With the wordholds on to what he was taught, so he can encourage and correct

Look at what is not on the list: no degree, no big voice, no perfect record. It is a list of habits: how a man behaves when he is angry, when he is thirsty, when he is broke, when his kids are watching. Those are the places our old lives broke us.

A past does not bar a man

What “blameless” does not mean

A clean record

Nobody on Crete had one. These were first-generation believers, many from the very life described in verse 12. A man is not shut out of serving because of what he did before Christ.

What it does mean

Nothing to hang a charge on now

A man whose life today matches what he says. Not given to wine, not soon angry: the habits are being broken, and the people around him can see it.

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

That is Paul to a church full of men with a past. “Such were some of you.” Were. For a man who is three weeks sober, this list is not a closed door. It is a road, and he is on it.

The line about wine matters to men who know what a drink does to them. Southern Baptists have long urged believers to leave alcohol alone, and a man in recovery already has his answer.

Growing into it, one step at a time

  1. Slow to angercount to ten and mean it
  2. Soberand honest with other people's money
  3. An open doora seat at your table
  4. Justa man of his word
  5. Holding fastthe word, as taught

You don't get to the top of a staircase by staring at it. Pick the step you are standing on today, the one that has always tripped you, and ask for help with that one.

Part two

The island, and the people on it

Verses 10–16

Paul is honest about how rough Crete was. And then he sends Titus there anyway.

A rough crowd

Paul quotes one of their own: “a prophet of their own.” That is how the Greeks talked about Crete. Liars: you couldn't trust a Cretan's word. Evil beasts: wild and cruel. Slow bellies: lazy and greedy, living to eat. The Greeks even had a verb, “to Cretize,” that meant to lie.

Some men hear that and think of their own name: the drunk, the thief, the liar, the one nobody trusts. Paul does not argue with the picture. “This witness is true” (verse 13). He is honest about the sin. Look what he does next.

Called that. Sent there anyway.

liars, evil beasts, slow bellies → sound in the faith That is the goal in verse 13: “rebuke them sharply, that they may be sound.” Paul sets a rough crowd a different target, and expects them to hit it.

“Cretes and Arabians, we do hear them speak in our tongues the wonderful works of God.”

Acts 2:11

On the day the church began, Cretans were in the crowd in Jerusalem, hearing the gospel in their own language, and some may have carried it home. God was already at work on the people everybody had written off.

No place and no reputation is too rough for God to plant something in. If He started a church among the Cretans, He can start something in you.

A rebuke with a purpose

A rebuke can be a wound or a medicine. Paul's has a stated goal: “that they may be sound.” Sound means healthy, the way a body is. The point is not to shame these men. It is to get them well.

The false teachers in verse 14 were piling up “Jewish fables, and commandments of men”: myths, and made-up rules about food and washing. Rules like that make a man feel holy while changing nothing underneath. The gospel works from the inside.

Pure on the inside

Rules on the outside

“Don't touch, don't eat, don't go”

The false teachers had a list for everything. To them the problem was always out there: the wrong food, the wrong people. But the verse ends “even their mind and conscience is defiled.” The dirt was inside.

A clean heart

Pure inside, so it shows outside

A man whose heart God has washed sees things rightly. Food, a day's work, a friend: these are good gifts, and he can enjoy them without tying himself in rules about them.

One careful word: this does not mean anything goes. The same clean heart steers clear of what once ruined it. A man in recovery knows which doors to keep shut. That isn't a rule to make him holy. It's wisdom from the inside.

Say it, then live it

  • “I know God”and nothing in the week shows it
  • abominable, disobedientthe words say one thing, the hands do another
  • no good work, anywhere“reprobate” here means they fail the test
  • works that match the wordswhat Titus is sent to build

A fair question for any man: what would the guys at work say I believe, if all they had to go on was how I acted? Good works don't save a man. They are what a saved man's life starts to look like.

If yours doesn't match yet, you are not a fraud. You are early. Take one good work today: show up on time, tell the truth when a lie would be easier, pay back what you owe.

Points to remember

  1. Left behind“that thou shouldest set in order the things that are wanting, and ordain elders in every city” 1:5Titus was left to finish the work: a steady man leading in every town.
  2. Leaders“blameless, as the steward of God” 1:7Habits you can see: temper, home, money, drink, an open door. Not a clean record.
  3. Rough places“rebuke them sharply, that they may be sound in the faith” 1:13Paul called the Cretans what they were, and sent Titus anyway. Rough places are not beyond God.
  4. Holding the word“Holding fast the faithful word as he hath been taught” 1:9A leader has to know what is true, so he can encourage and correct.
  5. Pure“Unto the pure all things are pure” 1:15God makes the heart clean. Outside rules can't. A clean heart steers clear of what ruined it.
  6. Words and works“They profess that they know God; but in works they deny him” 1:16Good works don't save, but a saved life starts to show them.

Question 1 of 6

Left behind

Why did Paul leave Titus on Crete, and what was he to set in order?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Paul had started the work on the island and could not stay. He left Titus to finish it: to straighten out what was missing and to find an elder in every city. A church needs steady men, and Paul trusted Titus to find them.

Question 2 of 6

Leaders

What kind of man is fit to lead the church?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A man whose life matches what he says: a faithful husband and father, not hot-tempered, not a drinker or a fighter, not chasing money, with an open door and a love for good people. It is a list of habits, not a clean past, and it is something a man can grow into.

Question 3 of 6

Rough places

What does God do with people from a place like Crete?

What verses tell us about this?

“Cretes and Arabians, we do hear them speak in our tongues the wonderful works of God.”

Acts 2:11

Answer

He sends someone with the gospel, and He expects change. Paul does not pretend Crete is nice: its own prophet called it a liar's island. But he sends Titus to build a church there, and the aim is that they be sound. No place and no reputation is too rough for Him.

Question 4 of 6

Holding the word

Why must a leader hold fast the faithful word?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because there are always talkers who twist things, and a church with no one who knows the truth will be led off by them. A leader holds on to what he was taught, so he can encourage the weak and correct the men who oppose it, with the word and not with his own opinions.

Question 5 of 6

Pure

What does “Unto the pure all things are pure” mean?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you. A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:25-26

Answer

The trouble is not out there in the food, the places or the people. It is in the heart. God cleans a man from the inside, and then he can see things rightly. It is not permission to do anything. A clean heart keeps away from what once ruined it, because it is wise, not because it is afraid.

Question 6 of 6

Words and works

How can a man say he knows God and still deny Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.”

Matthew 5:16

Answer

By living in a way that says the opposite of what he says out loud. Words are cheap, and Paul looks at works. Good works do not save anyone. But when God has saved a man, good works begin to show. If they aren't there yet, it is not time to quit. It is time to ask for help and take the next small step.
