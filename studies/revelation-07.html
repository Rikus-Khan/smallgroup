Revelation 7

Every tear

Chapter 6 ended with a question: who shall be able to stand? Before the last seal opens, God holds back the storm and answers it. A crowd no one can count stands before the throne in robes washed white, and the Lamb wipes away every tear.

Six seals have opened, and the sixth shook the sky itself. Everyone expects the seventh. Instead, John is shown two scenes: God's servants sealed on earth, and a great crowd safe in heaven. Chapter 6 asked, “who shall be able to stand?” (6:17). Chapter 7 answers: these do.

Part one

Held and sealed

Verses 1–8

The storm is ready, and God says: not yet. First He puts His mark on His own.

The storm on a leash

“Four corners” and “four winds” are the Bible's way of saying everywhere: north, south, east and west (Daniel 7:2 and Zechariah 6:5 use the same picture). The winds of judgment are ready, and four angels hold them back.

Then a fifth angel comes from the sunrise with “the seal of the living God”, and the order is: “Hurt not … till.” Nothing touches the earth until God's people are marked as His. Judgment waits on mercy. The storm in your life is on a leash too, and it is God who holds it.

Marked as His

  1. Owneda seal said whose a thing was
  2. Keptno one broke a king's seal
  3. Realthe seal proved it genuine

Ezekiel saw it before Jerusalem fell: those who grieved over sin were marked, and judgment passed them by.

“And the LORD said unto him, Go through the midst of the city, through the midst of Jerusalem, and set a mark upon the foreheads of the men that sigh and that cry for all the abominations that be done in the midst thereof.”

Ezekiel 9:4

Every believer is sealed the day he trusts Christ: “ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise” (Ephesians 1:13). Not when you get your act together. Owned, kept and real, even the morning after a bad night.

These are the old Greek spellings: Juda is Judah, Aser is Asher, Nepthalim is Naphtali, Manasses is Manasseh, Zabulon is Zebulun. The list is unusual. Juda comes first, ahead of Reuben the firstborn: Jesus is “the Lion of the tribe of Juda” (5:5). Dan and Ephraim are missing, and no one knows for sure why. Many think it is because both led Israel into idol worship (Judges 18; Hosea 4:17).

Who are the 144,000?

Many Bible teachers

Israel, as written

Jewish believers, counted from the twelve tribes, sealed and kept by God through the trouble that is coming. God has not forgotten His promises to Israel (Romans 11).

Other Bible teachers

All God's people, pictured

Twelve times twelve times a thousand: the whole people of God, complete, with not one lost. The same people John then sees as a multitude in verse 9.

Good Baptist teachers hold both, and they hold them without fighting. Here is what is plain either way. God knows exactly who His people are, down to the last one. He marks them before trouble comes. And He loses none of them.

One more plain thing: no group today owns this number. Some have claimed only 144,000 of their own members will be in heaven. Verse 9 shuts that door: right after the number comes a crowd “which no man could number.”

“a great multitude, which no man could number”

John did the same thing in chapter 5. He heard of a Lion, and when he turned he saw a Lamb (5:5–6). Some teachers think the 144,000 and the multitude are one people, heard and then seen. Others think they are two groups, Israel and then the nations. Either way, God promised Abraham a family like the stars, “if thou be able to number them” (Genesis 15:5). Here it is, and nobody can.

And look who is in it: every nation, every family line, every people, every language. No one is too far gone, too foreign, or too late. If He has room for that crowd, He has room for you.

Part three

Washed white

Verses 13–17

An elder asks who these people are. The answer is the whole gospel in one line, and it ends with tears wiped away.

Washed white in red

You don't make anything white by washing it in blood. Except this. These people did not come to heaven in clean clothes. They came in robes that needed washing, and they washed them where the Lamb's blood was shed for them. That is the gospel, for them and for you.

“Come now, and let us reason together, saith the LORD: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool.”

Isaiah 1:18

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Out of great tribulation

Teachers differ here too. Many Baptists hold that the church is caught up before the worst comes, and that these are people saved during the great tribulation itself. Others read them as all believers, out of all the trouble of every age. Both agree on what is plain: they “came out of” it. Through, not around, and out the other side.

Beforetribulation: pressure, crushing, loss
→
Now“before the throne of God,” and He shall “dwell among them”

“Dwell among them” is literally spread His tent over them (σκηνώσει, skēnōsei), the word John used for Jesus in John 1:14, “dwelt among us.” The God who pitched His tent beside us at Bethlehem will spread it over us for good. No more sleeping outside.

The Lamb becomes the Shepherd

“Feed them” is the shepherd's word: tend them, lead them to grass. The Lamb who was slain is now the Shepherd who goes in front. Isaiah promised it about 700 years before:

“They shall not hunger nor thirst; neither shall the heat nor sun smite them: for he that hath mercy on them shall lead them, even by the springs of water shall he guide them.”

Isaiah 49:10

Not “the tears” in general. Their eyes, every one, wiped by God Himself, the way a father wipes a boy's face. He will say it again at the very end of the book: “no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying” (21:4).

Until then, not one tear is wasted. David wrote, “put thou my tears into thy bottle: are they not in thy book?” (Psalm 56:8). He keeps them now, and wipes them then.

Regret is real. Bring it to God by name; He keeps every tear in His bottle.

“are they not in thy book?”

This morningWash, and follow

Confess what stained you yesterday. The blood still washes (1 John 1:9). Then follow the Shepherd into the day.

That dayWiped away

No more hunger, no more thirst, no more crying. The Lamb Himself will see to it.

“God shall wipe away all tears”

Today: name one regret to God out loud, the one you keep carrying. Then tell one brother in this room, so you are not carrying it alone.

Points to remember

  1. Held“Hurt not the earth … till we have sealed the servants of our God” 7:3The storm is on a leash. Judgment waits until God's people are marked as His.
  2. Sealed“having the seal of the living God” 7:2A seal means owned, kept and real. Every believer is sealed by the Holy Spirit the day he trusts Christ.
  3. Counted“an hundred and forty and four thousand of all the tribes” 7:4Literal Israel or all God's people pictured: either way, God knows every one of His and loses none. No group owns the number.
  4. Multitude“a great multitude, which no man could number” 7:9From every nation, family and language. No one is too far gone to be in that crowd.
  5. Washed“made them white in the blood of the Lamb” 7:14They came with stained robes and washed them in Jesus' blood. The same blood cleans you today.
  6. Tears“God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes” 7:17The Lamb will shepherd them to living water, and God Himself will wipe every tear.

Question 1 of 6

Held

Why does God hold back the four winds?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So that His servants can be sealed first. The winds of judgment are ready, but God says “not till.” Judgment waits on mercy, and the storm only moves when God lets it. The trouble in your life is on His leash too.

Question 2 of 6

Sealed

What does it mean to be sealed by the living God?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD said unto him, Go through the midst of the city, through the midst of Jerusalem, and set a mark upon the foreheads of the men that sigh and that cry for all the abominations that be done in the midst thereof.”

Ezekiel 9:4

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

Answer

A seal says who owns a thing, keeps it safe, and proves it genuine. God marks His own before trouble comes, as He did in Ezekiel's day. Every believer today is sealed with the Holy Spirit the moment he believes, not after he has cleaned himself up.

Question 3 of 6

Counted

Who are the 144,000, and what is plain whichever way you read it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Many teachers read them as Jewish believers from the twelve tribes; others as a picture of all God's people, complete. Either way, God knows exactly who are His, marks them, and loses none. And no group today owns the number: the very next scene is a crowd no one can count.

Question 4 of 6

Multitude

Who stands before the throne, and how many are there?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he brought him forth abroad, and said, Look now toward heaven, and tell the stars, if thou be able to number them: and he said unto him, So shall thy seed be.”

Genesis 15:5

Answer

More than anyone can count, from every nation, family, people and language, in white robes with palms of victory. Not one of them takes the credit: they shout that salvation belongs to God and the Lamb. If there is room for that crowd, there is room for you.

Question 5 of 6

Washed

How are dirty robes made white?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come now, and let us reason together, saith the LORD: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool.”

Isaiah 1:18

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Answer

In the blood of the Lamb. Nobody in heaven arrived clean. They brought their stains to Jesus, whose blood was shed for them, and He made them white. The same blood still cleanses from all sin, today's included.

Question 6 of 6

Tears

What will the Lamb do for those who have suffered?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou tellest my wanderings: put thou my tears into thy bottle: are they not in thy book?”

Psalm 56:8

Answer

He will shepherd them: feed them, lead them to living water, and shelter them so hunger, thirst and heat never touch them again. And God Himself will wipe away every tear. Until that day He keeps them, every one, in His bottle.
