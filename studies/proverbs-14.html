A way that seemeth right

Builders and wreckers, short fuses and long ones, and the road that feels right but ends in death.

Part one

Builders and wreckers

Verses 1–11

The same two hands can build a house or tear it down.

With her own hands

The wise

Builds

A kind word here, a paycheck there, a promise kept. A home goes up one brick at a time.

The foolish

Plucks it down

Nobody else wrecks it. It comes down “with her hands,” one bad choice at a time.

The verse speaks of a woman, but every man in this room knows it's true of us too. Most of us tore down a home with our own hands. The good news: by God's grace, the same hands can start building again.

Verse 3 adds a picture: the fool's proud mouth is “a rod” that ends up beating him. The wise man's lips protect him.

A clean barn is an empty barn

The “crib” is the feeding trough. No ox, no mess to shovel. But also no plowing, and no harvest.

Anything that grows is messy: a marriage, kids, a church, a recovery group, real friends. A man can keep his life “clean” by keeping everybody out. He'll also keep it empty.

Fools laugh at sin.

They joke about it, brag about it, and tell the old war stories like they were the good old days. Sin becomes a punch line.

Verse 7 gives you permission to leave: when a man has nothing but foolishness on his lips, “go from the presence” of him. You don't owe that conversation your time.

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Nobody knows. Except One.

There's a bitterness every man carries that nobody else can fully feel: what you did, what was done to you, what you lost. And there's joy no outsider can share.

That's true between people. It isn't true of Jesus. He was a real man, and He was tempted in every way we are:

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:15–16

Part two

The way that seems right

Verses 12–22

Feeling right and being right are not the same thing.

It felt right

“Seemeth right” means it looks straight from where you're standing. Nobody starts down a road he thinks will kill him. The first drink, the first high, the first lie all seemed right at the time.

“The end thereof”: you only see where a road goes by where it ends. And this one doesn't end in one death, but “the ways of death”, many of them.

Our feelings are not a safe map. Jesus is: “I am the way, the truth, and the life” (John 14:6).

The laugh that hides

“Even in laughter”→the heart is sorrowfulThe life of the party, hurting the whole time.
“The end of that mirth”→is heavinessThe party ends. Then comes the morning after, and the weight comes back heavier.

A lot of us were the funny guy. Laughing loudest was a good way to keep anybody from seeing what was going on inside. You don't have to wear that mask in here.

Believing every word

The “simple” man is gullible. He swallows whatever he's told. Here are a few words every one of us has heard, from other people and from our own heads:

“Just one won't hurt.”Lie
“You can handle it this time.”Lie
“Nobody will ever find out.”Lie
“You've gone too far for God to take you back.”Lie

The prudent man “looketh well to his going”: he watches where his feet are taking him, and he checks every word against God's word.

If you've slipped back

To “backslide” is to turn back, to drift away from the Lord. Notice where it starts: “in heart.” The feet follow later.

And the result? He's “filled with his own ways.” God lets him have what he chose, until he's sick of it. A believer who drifts doesn't stop being God's child, but he does lose his peace and joy, and his Father will correct him.

The road back is always open:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Fair-weather friends

This isn't how things should be. It's how they are. When the money's flowing, everybody's your buddy. When it runs out, you find out who your friends were.

Many of us learned this the hard way, when the money, the stuff, or the supply ran out and the phone stopped ringing.

“A man that hath friends must shew himself friendly: and there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother.”

Proverbs 18:24

Verse 21 turns it around: the man who shows mercy to the poor, who expects nothing back, is the one called “happy.” Be the friend who stays.

Part three

What lasts

Verses 23–35

Work, awe of God, a calm heart, and hope that reaches past the grave.

Talk is cheap. Work pays.

“Penury” means poverty, being in want. Big plans, big talk, and big promises with nothing behind them just make a man poorer. “In all labour”, even the humble jobs, there's real gain.

That goes for the spiritual life too. Talking about change is easy. Showing up, day after day, is where the profit is.

Verse 25: a “true witness delivereth souls.” One honest man in a courtroom can save a life. And one honest man telling what Jesus did for him can point a soul toward rescue.

What “the fear of the LORD” is

  • being scared of Goda scared man runs away from Him
  • religious nervesalways wondering if you've done enough
  • awe and trusttaking God so seriously that you run to Him and do what He says

And look what it gives: “strong confidence”, solid ground under your feet. And “his children shall have a place of refuge.” A man who fears God gives his kids a safe place to run.

Said twice, on purpose

Proverbs 13:14

The law of the wise is a fountain of life, to depart from the snares of death.

Proverbs 14:27

The fear of the LORD is a fountain of life, to depart from the snares of death.

Chapter 13 said God's teaching is a fountain of life. Chapter 14 says the fear of the LORD is. They're the same fountain: you can't love His word without honoring Him, and you can't honor Him while ignoring His word.

A fountain keeps running. A snare is a hidden trap. The man who drinks from God every day finds the strength to walk around the traps instead of into them.

Short fuse, long fuse

“soon angry”
!dealeth foolishly
“slow to wrath”
!

Verse 17: “He that is soon angry dealeth foolishly.” Most of the worst things we've done took about ten seconds. A long fuse buys you time to think, to pray, to walk away.

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.”

James 1:19–20

The poor man's Maker

Every poor man was made by God. So when somebody grinds him down, cheats him, or looks down on him, he's insulting the One who made him.

Many of us have been that poor man, and we know how people look at you. Now we get to be the ones who honor God by showing mercy.

“And the King shall answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.”

Matthew 25:40

Verse 30 shows another danger: “envy the rottenness of the bones.” Wanting what the other man has rots you from the inside out. A heart at peace with God is life to the whole body.

Hope that reaches past the grave

The wicked

driven

Pushed out of this life by his own wickedness, with nothing ahead of him.

The righteous

hope

Even at his death, he has somewhere to go and Someone waiting.

“Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:”

John 11:25

Many of us have stood closer to death than we like to admit. For the man in Christ, death is not the end of hope. “For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain” (Philippians 1:21).

Points to remember

  1. The fear of the LORD“In the fear of the LORD is strong confidence” 14:26Awe of God gives a man solid ground, a fountain of life, and a refuge for his family.
  2. The wrong road“There is a way which seemeth right unto a man” 14:12Feeling right isn't the same as being right. Jesus is the way.
  3. The hidden heart“Even in laughter the heart is sorrowful” 14:13A laugh can cover a lot of pain. Jesus sees through it, and He cares.
  4. Anger“He that is soon angry dealeth foolishly” 14:17A short fuse makes fools of us. Slow to wrath is great understanding.
  5. Mercy“He that honoureth him hath mercy on the poor” 14:31How we treat the poor is how we treat the God who made them.
  6. Hope“The righteous hath hope in his death” 14:32In Christ, even death is not the end of hope.

Question 1 of 6

The fear of the LORD

What does the fear of the LORD give a man?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Strong confidence, a safe place for his children, and a fountain of life that keeps him out of death's traps. The fear of the LORD isn't cowering. It's awe and trust that sends a man running to God, and it shows up in an upright walk.

Question 2 of 6

The wrong road

Why can a way that seems right still lead to death?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6

Answer

Because “seeming right” is only how it looks from where we stand. Our feelings and our own judgment can be fooled, and the simple man believes every word. Only God sees where a road ends. Jesus is the one Way we can trust all the way home.

Question 3 of 6

The hidden heart

What do we hide behind a laugh?

What verses tell us about this?

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.”

Hebrews 4:15

Answer

Sorrow, bitterness, shame, the things we don't think anyone else could understand. A laugh can keep people from seeing it. But Jesus isn't fooled, and He isn't put off. He was tempted like we are, He feels it with us, and He invites us to come to Him boldly for help.

Question 4 of 6

Anger

What does a quick temper cost us?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.”

James 1:20

Answer

It makes fools of us. A man who is “soon angry” acts before he thinks and puts his folly on display. Some of us lost relationships, jobs, and years over a few seconds of rage. The slow fuse is “great understanding,” and God can grow it in us.

Question 5 of 6

Mercy

How does the way we treat the poor show what we think of God?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the King shall answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.”

Matthew 25:40

Answer

God made the poor man. Oppress him and you insult his Maker. Show him mercy and you honor God, and Jesus counts it as done to Himself. Our hearts toward God show up in how we treat the people who can't pay us back.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

What hope does the righteous man have that the wicked doesn't?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live:”

John 11:25

“For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain.”

Philippians 1:21

Answer

Hope in his death. The wicked man is driven out of life with nothing ahead. The man made righteous by faith in Jesus has the One who is the resurrection and the life. For him, death isn't a loss. It's gain.
