Where we are in 1 Corinthians

Paul is writing to a church he planted in Corinth, from Ephesus, about AD 55. The letter is his answer to one problem after another. In chapter 5 he told them not to shrug at a brother's open sin. Chapter 6 keeps going: more ways the church was letting the old life in.

1–4Divisions
5–6Sin the church let in
7Marriage
8–10Food and freedom
11Order in worship
12–14Gifts, and love
15The resurrection
16The offering, goodbye

Read alone, this chapter still stands: two corrections, then the best news in the letter for a man with a past, then what that news means for the way he lives in his body today.

Part one

Brothers in court

Verses 1–8

Believers were suing each other in front of unbelievers. Paul says the church is better equipped than that.

Bigger than a boundary dispute

In Corinth, a man with a complaint took it to the city's courts, and the judges were pagans: “the unjust,” Paul calls them, men outside God's people. The whole town watched. Paul is embarrassed, and asks a simple question about size.

The saints will judge the world6:2
+ we will judge angels6:3
= and you can't settle a quarrel over this life's business?“the smallest matters”

He is not saying believers are important. He is saying you belong to the One who is going to put everything right, so a dispute over money or property shouldn't need a pagan judge.

“Why do ye not rather take wrong?”

Sue him

Win the case, lose the room

You may get the money. But the town now watches two Christians at war, and they decide Jesus doesn't change anybody.

The better way

Go to him, or take the loss

Go to him alone first. Or ask a wise brother to help you both. Sometimes you simply lose, and that is cheaper than losing a brother.

“And if any man will sue thee at the law, and take away thy coat, let him have thy cloke also.”

Matthew 5:40

Two things Paul is not saying. He is not telling anyone to dodge the law: if you have a court date, fines, or something you owe, show up, tell the truth, and pay what is right. And he is not saying a man who has been beaten, robbed, or abused must keep quiet: that is a crime, and the police are God's servants for it (Romans 13:4). Paul is talking about believers with money disputes against each other.

Part two

And such were some of you

Verses 9–11

The hardest list in the letter, and the verse that answers it.

The list, plainly

Paul calls these the unrighteous. He names ten kinds. Read them soberly, and read them as a man who may find himself on it:

With the body

fornicatorsadultererseffeminateabusers of themselves with mankind

Sexual sin in several forms: sex outside the marriage of one man and one woman, as God made it.

With worship

idolaters

Giving to anything else the place that belongs to God.

With the hands

thievescovetousextortioners

Taking what isn't ours, or wanting it so badly we would.

With the bottle and the mouth

drunkardsrevilers

Drink that rules a man, and a tongue that tears people down.

Look who is on it: covetous and revilers, the respectable sins, sit beside the scandalous ones. Nobody reads this list from the outside. And “be not deceived”: a man who lives in these as his way of life, and calls it fine, is fooling himself.

This is not a list of unforgivable sins. Every one of them was in the Corinthian church's past. The very next verse says so.

“And such were some of you”

1 Corinthians 6:9–10

fornicatorsidolatersadultererseffeminateabusers of themselves with mankindthievescovetousdrunkardsrevilersextortioners
washedsanctifiedjustified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God

That is the old list. Some of those men sat in the church at Corinth, and Paul says so without flinching. Were.

Now read what is written over it. Not trying to be. Not on probation. Ye are washed, ye are sanctified, ye are justified.

Three words, all done

  • WashedMade clean. The stain is gone, and it was Jesus' blood that took it, not your effort.
  • SanctifiedSet apart as God's own. You are His, not your old habit's, from the day you believed.
  • JustifiedDeclared right with God, as if the charge had never been brought. A judge's verdict.

All three are in the past, and all three come “in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.” Not by a program, a streak, or a sober-day count. God did it, and God lives in you to keep going with it.

“For we ourselves also were sometimes foolish, disobedient, deceived, serving divers lusts and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, and hating one another. But after that the kindness and love of God our Saviour toward man appeared, Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;”

Titus 3:3–5

“But I did it again”

A man who is honestI was on that list last year. And to be straight, I was on it last Tuesday too.

The verseYe are washed. A slip does not un-wash you. You are not back on the list, you tripped on your way off it. still His

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Call it what it is, tell God and one brother, and get up. The regret is a signal to turn, not a verdict. Nothing on that old list can be used against you now.

Part three

Not under the power of any

Verses 12–20

Washed men live in a body God has bought. So what does a free man do with it?

Corinth's slogans, and Paul's answers

Many teachers think these were Corinth's own slogans, and Paul quotes them back.

Corinth“All things are lawful for me. I'm free.”

PaulTrue, but not all things are expedient (helpful), and I will not be brought under the power of any.

Corinth“Food is for the belly. The body's just plumbing. What I do with it doesn't matter.”

PaulFood and stomach will pass away. The body is for the Lord, and God will raise it up. it matters

How a thing takes over

  1. “It's allowed”no law against it
  2. “I can handle it”it's only on weekends
  3. “Just once more”and the once keeps moving
  4. “I can't stop”brought under its power

That is how Paul names addiction: “brought under the power.” The question is not whether a thing is allowed, but whether it is running you.

“Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage.”

Galatians 5:1

Today: name the one thing that has more say over you than it should, and tell one brother. “God is faithful… will with the temptation also make a way to escape” (1 Corinthians 10:13).

Joined to whom?

The old way

“One body”

Sexual union joins two people, and it can't be taken back, whatever a man tells himself. Paul says it joins members of Christ to someone He never meant them to be joined to.

The new way

“One spirit”

The man who is joined to the Lord is one spirit with Him. That is a bond that doesn't wear off, and doesn't leave anyone feeling used.

Your body isn't a separate machine you run on your own time. It belongs to Christ, so God forbid that we hand a part of it to something that was never His.

Don't fight it. Run.

Not “resist.” Flee. Joseph didn't argue with temptation. He lost his coat and kept his life. Some fights you win by moving your feet.

“Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart.”

2 Timothy 2:22

Today that looks plain: delete the number, change the route home, put the filter on the phone, call a brother before, not after. This sin is against “his own body”: it is done to the whole man.

A temple, and a price

God's presence once lived in a building, the temple at Jerusalem. Now the Holy Spirit lives in every man who trusts Christ, from the day he trusts Him, and He doesn't move out when we stumble. Paul says you are the temple.

And the price was paid: “Ye are bought with a price.” Not silver or gold, as Peter puts it:

“But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:19

Points to remember

  1. Court“Why do ye not rather take wrong?” 6:7Brothers shouldn't drag each other before unbelievers. Go to him first, or take the loss.
  2. The list“The unrighteous shall not inherit the kingdom of God” 6:9Ten kinds of sin, from the scandalous to the respectable. A man who lives in them and calls it fine is fooling himself.
  3. Such were some“But ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified” 6:11That was you, in the past. Now you're clean, set apart, and declared right, by Jesus and by the Spirit.
  4. Lawful“I will not be brought under the power of any” 6:12Freedom isn't doing whatever you like. It is not being owned by anything, even something allowed.
  5. Flee“Flee fornication” 6:18Don't argue with it. Run, like Joseph, and don't run alone.
  6. Bought“Ye are bought with a price” 6:20Your body is the Spirit's temple, and Christ paid for it with His blood. Glorify Him in it.

Question 1 of 6

Court

Why shouldn't believers take each other to court?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It brings the church's quarrels in front of unbelievers, and it treats brothers as enemies. The church has wise men who can judge between brothers. Better to settle it among them, or to take the loss.

Question 2 of 6

The list

Who will not inherit the kingdom of God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The unrighteous: men whose life is given to sins like these, and who don't turn from them. It's a list of what a man is without Christ, and verse 11 shows it isn't where any man has to stay.

Question 4 of 6

Lawful

What will Paul not let anything lawful do to him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage.”

Galatians 5:1

“For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace.”

Romans 6:14

Answer

Bring him under its power. Many things are allowed that aren't good for us, and a free man won't let even an allowed thing become his master. Christ has set us free, and He gives the strength to stay that way.

Question 5 of 6

Flee

What are we told to do about fornication?

What verses tell us about this?

“And she caught him by his garment, saying, Lie with me: and he left his garment in her hand, and fled, and got him out.”

Genesis 39:12

“Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord out of a pure heart.”

2 Timothy 2:22

Answer

Flee. Don't stand and argue with it. Get out, like Joseph, and run toward righteousness, faith, love and peace, with others who call on the Lord from a clean heart.

Question 6 of 6

Bought

Why is our body not our own?

What verses tell us about this?

“But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:19

Answer

Because it was bought with a price, the blood of Christ, and because the Holy Spirit lives in it. So we glorify God in our body, with all of ourselves.
