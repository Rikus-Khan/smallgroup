Colossians 1

Christ over everything

Paul writes from prison to a church he has never visited. Whatever has a hold on you, Jesus is bigger, and He has already moved you out of its reach.

The letter to the Colossians

“Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, and Timotheus our brother, To the saints and faithful brethren in Christ which are at Colosse”

Written from
Rome, where Paul was a prisoner in his own rented house, guarded day and night (Acts 28:30)
When
about AD 60 to 62
To whom
the believers at Colosse, a small town in Asia. Paul had never been there (2:1).
Carried by
Tychicus, with Onesimus (4:7–9)
Why
Teachers were adding things to Christ. Paul's answer: Christ is enough.

Three towns in one valley

Colosse, Laodicea and Hierapolis sat within a day's walk of each other, in the valley of the Lycus river, well over a hundred miles inland from Ephesus. Colosse was the smallest of the three.

A man named Epaphras brought the gospel up the valley (1:7). Paul wrote to Colosse, but he also had Laodicea on his heart (2:1), and he asked the two churches to swap letters (4:16).

“And this continued by the space of two years; so that all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, both Jews and Greeks.”

Acts 19:10

Paul spent about three years at Ephesus, and the gospel went out from there across the province. A few years later, in chains in Rome, he gets news of one of the little churches that grew from it. Three letters went out together: Ephesians, Colossians and a short note to a man named Philemon.

The whole letter on one line

Colossians is short: four chapters. The first half says who Jesus is. The second half says what that means for how a man lives. We'll come back to this line in every chapter.

Part one

Thanks, and a prayer

Verses 1–14

A prisoner in Rome says thank you for strangers, and prays for them. Then he says what God has already done.

Faith, love, hope

  1. Faithin Christ Jesus. It rests on Him, not on how they felt that day.
  2. Loveto all the saints. A church is people who actually show up for each other.
  3. Hopelaid up in heaven. Kept safe where nothing can touch it.

Paul starts with thanks, not a scolding. He is about to warn them about some real dangers, but first he tells them what is good in them. Notice where the hope is: “laid up for you in heaven.” It isn't in your hands, so you can't drop it. A man who fell yesterday still has a hope God is keeping.

Nobody famous

The church at Colosse was not started by Paul. It was started by Epaphras, a local man who heard the gospel while Paul was teaching at Ephesus and carried it home. We hardly know anything else about him. Paul calls him “our dear fellowservant” and “a faithful minister of Christ.”

God spread the word through ordinary men who told what they had heard. You don't need a Bible degree or a spotless record. Whoever you work next to has never heard it from you, and the gospel still travels the way it did up the Lycus valley: one man telling another.

What Paul asks God for

  1. Knowfilled with the knowledge of His will
  2. Walklive in a way that is worthy of the Lord
  3. Fruitgood work that you can see
  4. Strengthpower for patience and joy

Look at where the power comes from: “according to his glorious power.” Paul prays for strength for “patience and longsuffering with joyfulness.” That is exactly what a man needs on a bad day at work: not more willpower, but strength from outside himself, asked for in prayer.

Moved from one kingdom to another

Before

He has already moved us. “Hath delivered” and “hath translated” are past tense. Paul doesn't say God is thinking about it, or that you can earn the move. The King has changed, and the border was crossed on the cross.

Two big words

Translated

Moved to another place

The word means to move people from one land to another and settle them there. God didn't just forgive you and leave you in the dark. He gave you a new home and a new King.

Redemption

Bought back, and set free

In Paul's day it was the price paid to free a slave or a prisoner. The price was “his blood”: Jesus paid it. You didn't, and you couldn't.

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

That's what “forgiveness of sins” means: sent away, not just covered up. If yesterday is still sitting on you like a weight, bring it to Him. He has already dealt with it.

Part two

Who Jesus is

Verses 15–23

One of the most powerful pictures of Jesus in the Bible, and what He did for men who were far away.

What “firstborn” means

What it does not mean

The first thing God made

Some read it that way, as if Jesus were the first creature. But the very next verse says all things were created by him. He can't be one of the things He made.

What it does mean

First in rank: the heir

In Bible times the firstborn son held the place of honor and received the inheritance. Firstborn is about rank, not about when he was born.

“Also I will make him my firstborn, higher than the kings of the earth.”

Psalm 89:27

God said that about David, who was the youngest of his brothers. “Firstborn” meant highest. Jesus is above every king, every power and every name there is.

By him, for him, held by him

Verse 16 says it three ways: all things were created by him, through him, and for him. Even “thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers”: the rulers and the unseen forces. Nothing is outside His reach.

“By him all things consist” means hold together. The universe doesn't run on its own. He is the glue. And so is your life.

“Upholding all things by the word of his power.”

Hebrews 1:3

First in four places

  1. Over creationHe made it and holds it (15–17)
  2. Over the churchHe is the head of the body (18)
  3. Over deaththe firstborn from the dead (18)
  4. Over our peacemade through the blood of His cross (20)

“Preeminence” means first place. Many of us handed that seat to something else: a drink, a drug, a crowd, a temper, a relationship, a paycheck. Whatever sits there runs the man. Paul says the seat belongs to Jesus. He is the head, and the rest of the body follows Him.

You were. Now you are.

Then (verse 21)

Alienated, enemies, in the wrong

Alienated: a stranger, far off. Enemies in your mind: dodging God, expecting punishment. Wicked works: the things we did. Paul says it plainly. It is the “before” picture, and it is not the end.

Now (verse 22)

Holy, unblameable, unreproveable

Brought close by Christ's death, and presented to God with no charge against you. Unreproveable means nobody can bring a charge that stands.

Notice who did the work: “yet now hath he reconciled.” He did it “in the body of his flesh through death.” Your past isn't hidden from Him. It was in the price.

Enemies made friends

alienated, enemies → reconciled Two sides at war, made one. God did not wait for us to make the first move.

“For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.”

Romans 5:10

That's how peace was made: “through the blood of his cross” (verse 20). Peace between you and God didn't come from you getting your act together. It came from His Son's death, while you were still an enemy.

“If ye continue”

Some men hear verse 23 as a threat: stay good, or lose it all. That isn't what Paul means, because everything before it was what Christ did. A man who has really been reconciled stays on the foundation. The warning is about drifting away from the hope of the gospel, and Paul is about to warn them against a certain kind of teaching that would pull them off it.

“For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.”

Hebrews 10:14

So “grounded and settled” is your part: stay close to Christ and the church. He holds on to you. Fall and get back up, and you've got a Father who runs to meet you, not a judge looking for a reason.

Part three

Christ in you

Verses 24–29

Paul, in chains, explains why he is glad to suffer. A secret is out.

A hard line

Does Paul mean that Christ's suffering wasn't enough, and he has to add to it? No. Paul has just said what the cross did (verses 14, 20, 22), and Jesus said “It is finished.” Nothing is lacking in what He paid for sin.

What Paul means is that carrying Christ's message to the world costs something, and he is glad to pay a share of it. Chains in Rome were what it cost him to bring this good news to men like those in Colosse. He is suffering for them, not for his own forgiveness.

Following Jesus can be costly: a friend you lose, a job you can't take, a night you stay home. Paul rejoiced. It's worth it.

A secret, now told

Verse 26Hid for ages

God's plan was in the Scriptures, but not yet clear. No one could see the whole of it.

Verse 26Now made manifest

Christ came, died and rose, and the plan is shown. A Bible “mystery” is a secret that has been told, not something strange.

Verse 27Among the Gentiles

The secret is for everyone: not only the Jews. Whosoever believes gets in.

If you're a Gentile (anyone who isn't Jewish), this verse is about you: “the riches of the glory of this mystery among the Gentiles.” You weren't an afterthought.

Christ in you

Christ is not only over the stars and the church. He lives in the man who believes.

The Holy Spirit comes to live in every believer the moment he trusts Christ. He is with you in the truck, the kitchen, the night shift.

“I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me.”

Galatians 2:20

That is the hope: a glory already started in you. Today is not the end of your story.

Hard work, and Christ's strength

Paul's part

“I also labour, striving”

He warns, teaches, travels, sits in chains. The word striving is the one used for an athlete straining to win.

Christ's part

“his working, which worketh in me mightily”

The power is His. Paul works hard, but it's Christ's energy running through him. Perfect means complete, grown up: a man who has become what he was meant to be.

That's the pattern for a man trying to change: you do the work, and He does the powering. Try to live without Him and you'll burn out. Wait for Him to do it all while you sit still, and nothing happens. Pray, then get up and do the next right thing.

What to do with this today

  1. SinIf a power still has a hold on you, name it to God. Paul does not pretend it was never there.
  2. RegretYesterday is not the verdict. “Forgiveness of sins.” Say thank you for that.
  3. GraceHe already moved you into His kingdom. You didn't earn it, so you can't un-earn it.
  4. Hope“Christ in you.” Today isn't the end of your story.

For the real world: before you start work this morning, say one sentence to Jesus: “You're in charge today. Not the old boss.” Then do one thing today that you'd put off, and do it as a man who belongs to a King.

Question 1 of 6

Moved

What did God do when He took us out of the power of darkness?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He rescued us from the power of darkness and moved us into the kingdom of His Son. He did it by redemption, paying the price with His blood, and the result is forgiveness. It's already done. You didn't earn it, and nothing you did yesterday can take it away.

Question 2 of 6

Who Jesus is

Who is Jesus, according to Paul?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God made visible. He made everything, everything was made for Him, and He holds it all together. He is the head of the church and has first place in all things. The One who runs the universe is the One who lives in you.

Question 3 of 6

A hard word

What does “firstborn” mean when it is said of Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“Also I will make him my firstborn, higher than the kings of the earth.”

Psalm 89:27

Answer

First in rank, not first made. The firstborn son was the heir, with the place of honor. The very next verse says Jesus created all things, so He can't be one of them. He is the highest, over every king and power.

Question 4 of 6

Peace

How was peace made between God and us?

What verses tell us about this?

“For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.”

Romans 5:10

Answer

By the death of Jesus on the cross. We didn't make peace: He did, while we were still enemies. That's why the peace holds. It rests on what He did, not on how well you did lately.

Question 5 of 6

Before and after

What were these men before Christ, and what has He made them?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

Strangers and enemies, far from God and in the wrong. Christ has made them holy, without blame, with no charge that stands. That's not what they became by trying. It's what He did, and what He says is true of every man who is in Christ.

Question 6 of 6

Hope inside

What is “Christ in you, the hope of glory”?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me.”

Galatians 2:20

Answer

Christ Himself lives in every believer, by His Spirit. That's the hope: a glory that has already begun and that He will finish. You aren't doing this alone, and today is not the end of your story.
