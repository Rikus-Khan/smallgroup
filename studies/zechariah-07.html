Part one

Should I weep?

Verses 1–7

A simple question about a fast day. God answers the question under the question.

Two years later

Quick reminder: Zechariah preached in Jerusalem after the exile. Persia ruled the world, and the Jews who had come home from Babylon were rebuilding the temple. His first visions (chapters 1–6) came about 520 BC.

Now it's about two years later, the fourth year of the Persian king Darius, about 518 BC. Chisleu is the ninth month of their calendar, about our November–December. The temple walls are going back up.

Some men send two of their own, Sherezer and Regem-melech, to the house of God with a question for the priests and prophets. Their names sound Babylonian. They were probably born in exile.

Seventy years of a sad day

In the fifth month of 586 BC, Babylon burned the temple and Jerusalem (2 Kings 25:8–9). Ever since, the Jews had kept that month as a fast: a day of going without food, weeping, and mourning what was lost.

  1. 586 BCthe temple burnedthe fast begins
  2. 538exiles come homeEzra 1
  3. 520building againHaggai, Zechariah 1
  4. about 518“Should I weep?”verse 3
  5. about 516the temple finishedEzra 6:15

So the question makes sense. The temple is nearly rebuilt. Do we still have to mourn it? “Separating myself” means setting himself apart from food and ordinary life for the day.

Who was it for?

God doesn't say yes or no yet. He asks a harder question, and He asks it of everybody, not just the two men: for seventy years you fasted in the fifth and seventh months. Was any of it for Me?

“fast unto me”→“eat for yourselves”whether they fasted or feasted, it was all about them

They were mourning a building and their own losses. They weren't mourning the sin that brought the fire. A ritual can go on for years with God left out of it.

“Is not this the fast that I have chosen? to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, and to let the oppressed go free, and that ye break every yoke?”

Isaiah 58:6

Going through the motions

Every man in recovery knows you can show up and not be there. Church, meetings, prayer, Bible study, all of it can turn into a routine we do for ourselves: to look good, to feel better, to keep people off our back.

For yourself

Just the motions

Right place, right time, right words. The heart's somewhere else, and nothing changes.

Unto Him

The real thing

The same habits, done to meet God. Honest about sin, hungry for Him, and changed by it.

God isn't against fasting, or meetings, or church. Jesus fasted. He just wants it to be for Him:

“But thou, when thou fastest, anoint thine head, and wash thy face; That thou appear not unto men to fast, but unto thy Father which is in secret: and thy Father, which seeth in secret, shall reward thee openly.”

Matthew 6:17–18

You've heard this before

The “former prophets” are the ones God sent before the exile, while Jerusalem was still full and rich. We've read some of them together:

  1. Hosea“I desired mercy, and not sacrifice” (6:6)
  2. Amos“let judgment run down as waters” (5:24)
  3. Micah“do justly, … love mercy, … walk humbly” (6:8)
  4. Isaiah“Is not this the fast that I have chosen?” (58:6)

“The south and the plain” are the dry country south of Judah and the low hills toward the sea. Back then, people lived everywhere. Now, much of it was empty. God is saying: the answer to your question was preached before the fire. Do you want to hear it now?

Part two

What God wanted all along

Verses 8–14

Not a better fast. A different way of treating people, and a heart that listens.

The fast God chooses

Here is what God had said all along. Not one word of it is about food. All of it is about people:

Be fair

9

“Execute true judgment”: honest dealing, in court and out.

Be kind

9

“Mercy and compassions”: loyal kindness, a heart that feels for a brother.

Guard the weak

10

The widow, the orphan, the foreigner, the poor: people with no one to stand up for them.

Clean thoughts

10

Don't even plan harm against a brother “in your heart.”

“Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world.”

James 1:27

How a heart turns to stone

Their fathers didn't go hard in one day. Zechariah lists it step by step, going down:

  1. refused to hearkensaid no
  2. pulled away the shoulderlike an ox shrugging off the yoke
  3. stopped their earswouldn't even hear it
  4. hearts as an adamant stonenothing gets in

That's how it works with us too. First you say no to God on one thing. Then you shrug Him off. Then you stop going where you'd hear about it. One day you realize nothing touches you any more.

“While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation.”

Hebrews 3:15

Harder than flint

שָׁמִיר said shamir the hardest stone they knew: flint, or diamond

They made their own hearts that hard, so they wouldn't have to hear God's law or His prophets. Notice that the words came “in his spirit”: it was God's own Spirit speaking through those men, and they shut Him out.

So “there came a great wrath.” Not quickly. God sent prophet after prophet for hundreds of years first. But a heart that won't hear at last meets the God it refused.

They cried, too

“he cried, and they would not hear”→“they cried, and I would not hear”for years God called them; when Babylon came, they called Him

Then came the “whirlwind”: Babylon swept them off and scattered them among nations they didn't know. The “pleasant land” sat empty. And God says plainly who did it: they laid it desolate, by their sin.

“He that turneth away his ear from hearing the law, even his prayer shall be abomination.”

Proverbs 28:9

That's a hard word, and it's true. But it is spoken to the men who are back in the land, rebuilding. God is telling them the story so they won't repeat it.

Not too late.

Why tell this story to men who'd come home? Because the door was open again. Back in chapter 1, Zechariah's very first message was this:

“Therefore say thou unto them, Thus saith the LORD of hosts; Turn ye unto me, saith the LORD of hosts, and I will turn unto you, saith the LORD of hosts.”

Zechariah 1:3

And in chapter 8, God answers their question about fasting with joy. If your heart has gone hard, the very fact that you're reading this means God is still calling. A hard heart can't soften itself, but God can make it new:

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

Points to remember

  1. The heart“Did ye at all fast unto me, even to me?” 7:5God wants us, not just our rituals. Religion without Him in it is empty.
  2. For whom?“Did not ye eat for yourselves?” 7:6The same habits can be for us or for Him. Do them to meet God.
  3. Mercy“Shew mercy and compassions every man to his brother” 7:9Be fair, be kind, guard the weak, and keep your thoughts clean toward others.
  4. A hard heart“They made their hearts as an adamant stone” 7:12It happens a step at a time: no, a shrug, shut ears, then stone.
  5. Not hearing“As he cried, and they would not hear” 7:13A man who won't hear God at last finds his own cries go unheard. Listen while He calls.
  6. Hope“Turn ye unto me … and I will turn unto you” 1:3It isn't too late. God still calls, and He can give a new heart.

Question 1 of 6

The heart

What does God want more than ritual?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings.”

Hosea 6:6

“But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

Answer

He wants us. For seventy years the people kept a fast, and God asked if any of it was for Him. Rituals aren't bad, but they can't stand in for a heart that knows God and loves Him. The LORD looks past the outside to the heart.

Question 2 of 6

For whom?

Who are we really doing it for?

What verses tell us about this?

“But thou, when thou fastest, anoint thine head, and wash thy face; That thou appear not unto men to fast, but unto thy Father which is in secret: and thy Father, which seeth in secret, shall reward thee openly.”

Matthew 6:17–18

Answer

That's the question to ask ourselves. The people fasted for themselves and feasted for themselves. Church, meetings, and prayer can all be done to look good or feel better. Jesus says do it for your Father, who sees in secret. He's the one we're meeting.

Question 3 of 6

Mercy

How does God want us to treat people?

What verses tell us about this?

“He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”

Micah 6:8

“Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world.”

James 1:27

Answer

Fairly, kindly, and with an eye out for the weak: the widow, the orphan, the stranger, the poor. And it starts inside: don't even plan harm against a brother in your heart. That was the message of the prophets before the exile, and it's the same message today.

Question 4 of 6

A hard heart

How does a heart get hard?

What verses tell us about this?

“But they hearkened not, nor inclined their ear, but walked in the counsels and in the imagination of their evil heart, and went backward, and not forward.”

Jeremiah 7:24

“While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation.”

Hebrews 3:15

Answer

A step at a time. First a man refuses God on one thing. Then he shrugs Him off, then stops listening, until nothing gets through. Each “no” makes the next one easier. That's why the Bible says “to day”: the time to soften is the moment you hear Him.

Question 5 of 6

Not hearing

What happens when a man won't listen to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that turneth away his ear from hearing the law, even his prayer shall be abomination.”

Proverbs 28:9

Answer

In time, he reaps it. God called Judah for generations, and they wouldn't hear. When trouble came, they cried, and God let the judgment fall. God is very patient, but His patience is time to turn, not permission to keep going. The ruin they lived in was the fruit of their own choices.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

Is it too late for a hard heart?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore say thou unto them, Thus saith the LORD of hosts; Turn ye unto me, saith the LORD of hosts, and I will turn unto you, saith the LORD of hosts.”

Zechariah 1:3

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

Answer

Not while God is still calling, and He is. Zechariah told this sad story to men who had come home, so they could do better than their fathers. We can't soften our own hearts, but God gives a new heart to anyone who turns to Him through Jesus. Now is the day.
