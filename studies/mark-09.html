Mark 9

Hear him

On a high mountain three men see Jesus in His glory. At the bottom, a worn-out father cries, “Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.”

Part one

This is my beloved Son

Verses 1–13

Six days after Peter said, “Thou art the Christ,” three men go up a mountain and see who He really is.

An high mountain

Chapter 8 ended up north near Caesarea Philippi, where Peter said, “Thou art the Christ.”

Mark doesn't name the mountain. Many think of Mount Hermon, above Caesarea Philippi. An old tradition says Mount Tabor.

Verse 1 promised that some would see “the kingdom of God come with power.” Six days later, three of them did.

Transfigured

μετεμορφώθη said metemorphōthē He was changed in form. We get our word metamorphosis from it.

A fuller cleaned and whitened cloth for a living. No fuller could get cloth this white. This light didn't shine on Jesus from outside. It shone out of Him. For a moment the glory He always had as God's Son showed through.

“For we have not followed cunningly devised fables, when we made known unto you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, but were eyewitnesses of his majesty.”

2 Peter 1:16

Moses and Elias

Moses

the Law

He brought God's law down from Mount Sinai, about 1,400 years before.

Elias (Elijah)

the Prophets

The great prophet who was taken up to heaven, about 850 years before.

The Law and the Prophets, the whole Old Testament, stood on the mountain talking with Jesus. Luke 9:31 says they talked about the death He would soon die at Jerusalem.

“Wist” means knew. Peter didn't know what to say, so he talked anyway. His tabernacles were booths of branches. He meant well, but he was putting Jesus on the same level as two great men.

Jesus only

Two of the greatest men in Israel's history stood beside Him.

Then the Father spoke from the cloud, as He had at the Jordan (1:11). This time He added two words: “hear him.”

When the cloud lifted, Moses and Elias were gone: “Jesus only.” Many voices tell a man how to live. God points to one, and says, listen to Him.

“Elias must first come”

Malachi 4:5

Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD:

Matthew 17:13

Then the disciples understood that he spake unto them of John the Baptist.

The scribes taught from Malachi that Elijah would come before the Messiah. Jesus says he already had: John the Baptist came in the spirit and power of Elijah, and Herod “listed,” did as he pleased, and killed him (6:27).

And the Messiah would be “set at nought”: treated as nothing. Isaiah 53:3 had said it: “He is despised and rejected of men.” The glory on the mountain was real, and so was the cross ahead.

Part two

Help thou mine unbelief

Verses 14–29

From the glory on the mountain down to a crowd, an argument, and a father at the end of his rope.

Down from the mountain

On the mountain

glory and a voice from heaven

Three men saw Jesus as He really is.

At the bottom

a crowd, an argument, a boy in torment

Scribes arguing, disciples failing, a father who had run out of hope.

Nobody gets to live on the mountaintop. Life happens down in the valley, with the people and the problems. But the same Jesus came down the mountain, straight into the mess.

“O faithless generation”: Jesus is grieved at the unbelief all around Him, in the crowd, the scribes, and His own men. Still, His next words are “bring him unto me.”

“Of a child”

  1. “a dumb spirit”it took his speech (v. 17)
  2. “teareth him”threw him into fits, foaming and grinding his teeth (v. 18)
  3. “pineth away”he was wasting away (v. 18)
  4. “to destroy him”into the fire, into the water (v. 22)

This was a real evil spirit, and it had owned this boy since childhood. It wanted him dead. Some of us know what it is to have something take hold young and try to destroy us. Some of us have watched it happen to someone we love.

Listen to the father: “have compassion on us.” The whole family was suffering. He had tried the disciples. Now he barely dares to hope: “if thou canst.”

“If thou canst”

The fatherIf thou canst do any thing, have compassion on us, and help us.

JesusIf thou canst believe, all things are possible to him that believeth.

The father, with tearsLord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.

The father put the “if” on Jesus. Jesus turned it around. The question was never whether Jesus could. Then the man did the most honest thing in the chapter. He told the truth about his faith: some belief, some doubt, all of it brought to Jesus.

Both at once

Most of us live right here. We believe, and we doubt, sometimes in the same breath.

Jesus didn't send this man away to get more faith first. He took the little faith he had and healed his son. Faith isn't strong because of how big it is. It's strong because of who it's in.

That's a prayer any man can pray, on his worst day: “Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.”

Taken by the hand

The spirit left with one last fit, and the boy lay so still the crowd said he was dead. Then Jesus took his hand. Some of us know what it is to hit bottom so hard that people gave up on us. That's not the end of the story when Jesus gets hold of your hand.

“Why could not we?”

They had cast out devils before (6:13). This time it didn't work. What was missing?

  • not more effortthey tried hard, in front of everybody
  • not the right wordsthere's no magic formula
  • not what worked last timeyesterday's victory isn't today's strength
  • but prayerleaning on God, and nothing else

Prayer is a man admitting, I can't do this, and God can. Fasting goes without food to give that time to God. The power was never theirs. It never is ours either. Lean on Him daily.

Part three

Servant of all

Verses 30–41

Jesus talks about His death. The twelve argue about which of them is the greatest.

Quietly through Galilee

Jesus kept this trip out of sight. He wasn't drawing crowds now. He was teaching His men what was coming.

They didn't understand, and they were afraid to ask. Maybe they didn't want to hear the answer. Men in recovery know that kind of fear: it's easier not to ask a question when you dread what's on the other side of it.

He told them three times

  1. Mark 8:31“the Son of man must suffer many things … and be killed, and after three days rise again”
  2. Mark 9:31“The Son of man is delivered into the hands of men, and they shall kill him”
  3. Mark 10:33–34delivered to the chief priests, condemned, handed to the Gentiles, mocked, scourged, killed

Each time, the same two things: He will be killed, and “he shall rise the third day.” The cross was no accident. Jesus walked toward it on purpose, and He never once mentioned the cross without the rising.

The way up is down

  1. “first”what the twelve argued about on the road
  2. “last of all”willing to go to the back of the line
  3. “servant of all”where Jesus Himself stands

Jesus had just told them He was going to die, and they spent the walk arguing about rank. When He asked, “they held their peace.” They knew.

He didn't scold them. He sat down, like a teacher, and turned the ladder over. In God's kingdom the greatest man in the room is the one looking for someone to serve.

A child in His arms

one of such childrenreceived “in my name”receiveth meand sohim that sent me

In that day a child had no rank at all. A child couldn't do anything for you, couldn't pay you back, couldn't help your name. Jesus picked one up in His arms.

The way you treat the man nobody notices is the way you're treating Jesus. And the way you treat Jesus is the way you're treating God the Father.

“He followeth not us”

John's complaint

“he followeth not us”

He wasn't one of the twelve, so they told him to stop.

Jesus' answer

“in my name”

He was doing real work in Jesus' name. That put him on the same side.

John wanted to guard the club. Jesus cared about His name, not their group. Believers in other churches who preach the same Christ aren't the enemy.

And nothing done for Jesus is too small to count: “a cup of water … he shall not lose his reward.” Giving a man a ride to a meeting counts. Making the coffee counts.

Part four

Cut it off

Verses 42–50

Some of the strongest words Jesus ever spoke, about sin, about hell, and about salt.

A millstone

The word means a millstone so big a donkey had to turn it. Tied to a man's neck, it would take him straight to the bottom.

Offend means trip up, cause to stumble into sin. The “little ones that believe” include new, weak believers.

Jesus guards them fiercely. Never be the man who leads a new believer back into what he's getting free from.

Whatever it takes

The hand

43

what you do: the phone you pick up, the money you handle

The foot

45

where you go: the street, the bar, the old crowd's house

The eye

47

what you look at: the screen, the window, the second look

Jesus isn't telling a man to hurt his body. A hand doesn't sin on its own. He's saying: deal with sin as if your life depended on it, because it does. Cut off the number. Change the route home. Get the screen out of the bedroom.

“But put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh, to fulfil the lusts thereof.”

Romans 13:14

Hell is real

The word Jesus uses for hell is Gehenna, the valley of Hinnom just south of Jerusalem. Kings of Judah once burned children there to idols (Jeremiah 7:31), and it became the picture of God's final judgment.

Three times He repeats it, echoing Isaiah 66:24: “their worm dieth not, and the fire is not quenched.” It doesn't end. Jesus spoke about hell more than anyone else in the Bible, and He did it to keep men out of it.

Hell isn't where God wants any man to go. He sent His Son to take our judgment, and whosoever believes on Him is safe, for good:

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Salt

  1. it preservedit kept meat from rotting, with no fridge
  2. it seasonedit gave food its taste
  3. it went on every offering“with all thine offerings thou shalt offer salt” (Leviticus 2:13)

“Salted with fire” is one of the hardest lines in Mark, and good Bible teachers read it different ways. Many take it that every believer is tested and purified, the way every offering was salted before it went on the altar.

The last line is plain, and it goes back to the argument on the road: keep what makes you different, and stop fighting each other. “Have peace one with another.”

Points to remember

  1. Glory“This is my beloved Son: hear him” 9:7They saw Jesus in His glory, and the Father said to listen to Him. When the cloud lifted, it was Jesus only.
  2. Weak faith“Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief” 9:24Bring Jesus the faith you have, doubts and all. He doesn't send that man away.
  3. Prayer“By nothing, but by prayer and fasting” 9:29The power was never theirs. Lean on God daily, not on what worked last time.
  4. Greatness“Last of all, and servant of all” 9:35In God's kingdom the way up is down. The greatest is the one who serves.
  5. Stumbling“If thy hand offend thee, cut it off” 9:43Deal with sin as if your life depends on it. Cut off whatever keeps leading you back.
  6. Salt“Have salt in yourselves, and have peace one with another” 9:50Keep what makes you different, and stop fighting over who's greatest.

Question 1 of 6

Glory

What did the three disciples see on the mountain?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Jesus transfigured, shining with His own glory, with Moses and Elias talking with Him. The Father said, “This is my beloved Son: hear him.” Then they saw Jesus only.

Question 2 of 6

Weak faith

What can a man pray when his faith is weak?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The father's prayer: “Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.” He was honest about his doubts and brought them to Jesus anyway. Jesus didn't send him off for more faith. He healed his son.

Question 3 of 6

Prayer

Why couldn't the disciples cast the spirit out?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“This kind can come forth by nothing, but by prayer and fasting.” They had done it before, but the power was never theirs. Prayer is depending on God. Without it, even good men come up empty.

Question 4 of 6

Greatness

Who is the greatest in God's kingdom?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The man who makes himself last of all and servant of all. The one who welcomes a child, someone who can never pay him back, in Jesus' name. Whoever does that is receiving Jesus, and the Father who sent Him.

Question 5 of 6

Stumbling

How seriously should we deal with what trips us up?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As seriously as cutting off a hand. Jesus isn't asking us to hurt our bodies. He's telling us to cut out whatever keeps leading us into sin, and never to be the one who trips up a new believer.

Question 6 of 6

Salt

What does it mean to have salt in yourselves?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man.”

Colossians 4:6

Answer

Salt keeps things from going bad and gives them taste. Keep what makes you different, let it season how you talk and live, and instead of arguing over who's greatest, have peace with your brothers.
