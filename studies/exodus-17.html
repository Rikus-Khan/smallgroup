Exodus 17

The rock, and the raised hands

No water at Rephidim, and the people ask, “Is the LORD among us, or not?” God answers with a rock that gives water, and then with a battle that is won by a tired old man and two friends who held up his hands.

Part one

“Is the LORD among us, or not?”

Verses 1–7

They went exactly where the LORD sent them, and there was no water.

Notice verse 1: they journeyed “according to the commandment of the LORD”, and they pitched in Rephidim: and there was no water. The cloud led them there. Nobody took a wrong turn.

Sometimes a man does the right thing, goes where God sent him, and finds a dry place. That does not mean God left. It means he has something to show him in the dry place, and he knew about it before he set out.

Quarrelling with God's man

  • chideto quarrel, scold, find fault loudly
  • tempthere, to test God: to demand that he prove himself
  • thirstedwere dry with thirst, and desperate for it
  • cattletheir livestock, which they needed to live

Thirst is real, and a day or two without water in the desert is dangerous. They were not wrong to need water. They were wrong in how they asked: with a quarrel, and with an accusation that God had brought them there to kill them.

Moses has nowhere to go. “What shall I do unto this people? they be almost ready to stone me.” He did not strike back, run, or make a speech. He cried unto the LORD. When you are cornered, the first move is to take it to God.

“I will stand before thee upon the rock”

God's answer is simple: take the elders, take the rod you used on the river (the one that turned the Nile to blood), and go on ahead. And this: I will stand before thee there upon the rock.

Moses was to strike the rock, with the LORD standing on it. The water would not come from Moses' power, or from the rod. It would come from the place where God stood. The elders saw it, so that no one could say later that it was a trick or a story.

Horeb is the mountain of God, where Moses met the burning bush. The rock is near it, in the wilderness of Arabia, and the people are now within sight of where the LORD will give the law.

“That Rock was Christ”

“And did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ.”

1 Corinthians 10:4

Paul says the Rock followed them, that is, the LORD was with them the whole way, and that he was Christ. The rock was struck once, and the water ran. Later the Lord Jesus was struck for us, and the water of life ran from him.

“In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.”

John 7:37–38

Jesus said this at the feast of tabernacles. He does not say, work harder for a drink. He says, come unto me. A thirsty man does not make water. He comes to where it is.

Two names for one place

Massah + Meribah Massah means a testing (“they tempted the LORD”). Meribah means a quarrel (“the chiding of the children of Israel”).

The place is named for what the people did, not for what God did. The question that hurts is the last one: Is the LORD among us, or not? They had seen ten plagues, a sea split in two and bread from the sky. Still, the first dry day, they asked.

“Harden not your hearts, as in the provocation, in the day of temptation in the wilderness: When your fathers tempted me, proved me, and saw my works forty years.”

Hebrews 3:8–9

After he has already shown you

Most of us can say what that doubt sounds like. He rescued me last year. He got me through that night. And now it is hard, so is he really here? Doubt after a rescue is not rare, and it was not new at Rephidim.

God did not leave. He gave them water, and put the question to rest: he was among them. But the name stuck for centuries, as a warning not to do it again.

The cure is to remember. Say what he has already done, one by one: out of Egypt, through the sea, bread from heaven. Keep the list. Read it to yourself on a dry day, and to a brother in Christ when you cannot.

Part two

“His hands were steady”

Verses 8–13

Right after the water, an army. The first battle of the wilderness.

Amalek comes

The Amalekites were a desert people who came from Esau's line (Genesis 36:12). They were fast raiders. Moses later looked back on this day, and said what they had done:

“How he met thee by the way, and smote the hindmost of thee, even all that were feeble behind thee, when thou wast faint and weary; and he feared not God.”

Deuteronomy 25:18

They did not meet an army. They came up from behind, on the tired, the slow and the feeble, when thou wast faint and weary. And they did it right after the water, when the people had just been given a gift.

The enemy often comes when we are weary, and right after a good day. A man who has just been given water is not on his guard. Be careful on the evening after the high.

Two places at once

In the valley

Joshua

Moses' young helper, here for the first time. He chose the men and fought with the sword. This is the first we hear of the man who will lead Israel into the land.

On the hill

Moses, with Aaron and Hur

An old man, about eighty years old, standing with “the rod of God in mine hand”: the rod that had worked every sign in Egypt. Up there he did not swing it. He held it up.

Both were the battle. The fighting had to be done, and the praying had to be done, and neither one was enough alone. Do what you can, and ask God for the rest.

Up, down, and held

Hands upIsrael prevailed
Hands downAmalek prevailed
Hands heldsteady till sundown

The bar shows who prevailed: Israel (coloured) against Amalek (red).

“Moses' hands were heavy.” An old man standing all day with his arms up gets tired, and tired is not a sin. The answer was not to push through alone. They took a stone, and put it under him, and he sat. Then Aaron and Hur stood one on each side and held up his hands.

“Steady” means they stayed firm until the going down of the sun. Then Joshua discomfited Amalek: broke and routed them, “with the edge of the sword”.

Who is holding up your hands?

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

“Wherefore lift up the hands which hang down, and the feeble knees;”

Hebrews 12:12

Moses was the greatest man in the camp, and he could not hold his own hands up for a day. The victory did not turn on how strong he was. It turned on two friends who saw he was tired, found him a seat, and stood beside him.

Two lessons for the man in this room. When you are tired, sit down and ask; do not hold on alone until you drop. And when you see a brother's hands going down, go and stand next to him. Text him today. Hands are held up by men who show up.

Our fight, and our enemy

Amalek was a real people with swords. Our fight is not against flesh and blood, and we are not told to take up swords. But we do have an enemy, and he also comes for the weary.

“For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.”

Ephesians 6:12

It is easy to think the fight is against a bottle, a screen, a man or a woman. It is bigger than that, and so is the help. You do not fight this with willpower, alone. You fight it with God, on the hill, and with brothers, in the valley.

Part three

“Jehovah-nissi”

Verses 14–16

Write it down, build an altar, and give the victory a name.

Write it down

  • memorialsomething kept to remind: a record
  • rehearsesay it over again, so that it is learned by heart
  • utterly put outwipe out completely

This is the first time in Exodus that Moses is told to write something in a book. The Lord told him to write it, and to say it to Joshua, who would need it. (It is one of the places that show Moses wrote the book of Exodus: 17:14, 24:4, 34:27.)

God's judgment on Amalek here is the judgment of a God who sees the weak picked off from behind, and does not forget it. It was said of a people that had made itself an enemy to Israel, and God's own people were told it was God's war, not theirs to settle by private revenge.

“The LORD is my banner”

The rod held up → Jehovah-nissi Nissi means “my banner”, a flag lifted on a pole where an army gathers. Moses named the altar after the One who won, not after himself or the rod.

Moses' raised hands had been like a banner on the hill, but he did not name it that. The victory belonged to the LORD. The altar he built points to him.

A man who has got through a hard day should do the same: not “look what I did”, but build an altar in his heart, say thank you to the LORD out loud, and tell a brother who held his hands.

Real world: water, hands, banner

  • When it is dryDo not quarrel with God. Cry to him, like Moses, and go where he points.
  • When you doubtList what he has already done, and read it aloud.
  • When you are tiredSit down, ask for a stone, and let a brother stand beside you.
  • When you winBuild an altar, and give him the name.

Today: pick one brother whose hands may be going down, and call or text him before noon. And if it is your own hands, let someone know. That is how this room works.

Question 1 of 6

Thirst

Where did the LORD lead the people, and what did they find there?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To Rephidim, “according to the commandment of the LORD”, and there was no water. Doing right does not always lead to a green place. When it is dry, cry to the LORD, as Moses did, and call your brothers.

Testing

Why did Moses call the place Massah and Meribah?

What verses tell us about this?

Hebrews 3:8–9

Answer

For their quarrelling and their testing of the LORD: “Is the LORD among us, or not?” They asked it after the sea and the manna. When you doubt, list what he has already done.

Question 3 of 6

The rock

What did the LORD tell Moses to do, and where would he stand?

What verses tell us about this?

“And did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ.”

1 Corinthians 10:4

Answer

To smite the rock, with the LORD standing upon it, and water came out. Paul says that Rock was Christ. He was struck once, and living water runs from him to everyone who is thirsty and comes.

Question 4 of 6

The fight

Who attacked at Rephidim, and who did the fighting?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Amalek attacked, and Joshua led the men who fought, while Moses prayed on the hill. Do what is yours to do, and pray: both were the battle. The enemy comes for the weary, so stay close to your brothers.

Question 5 of 6

Brothers

What happened when Moses' hands went up and came down, and who held them up?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

While his hands were up Israel won; when they came down Amalek did. Aaron and Hur held them up until sundown. You are not meant to do this alone: let your brothers hold you up, and hold up theirs.

Question 6 of 6

The banner

What did Moses write, and what altar did he build?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He wrote it in a book as a memorial, and told it to Joshua. He built an altar named Jehovah-nissi: the LORD is my banner. Write down what God has done for you, and give him the name.
