Zechariah 11

Thirty pieces of silver

Part one

Howl, fir tree

Verses 1–3

Zechariah preached to the Jews back from Babylon, about 520 BC, while Persia ruled. Here he looks far ahead, and it opens with fire.

Fire from the north

The proudest trees in the land: the cedars of Lebanon, the oaks of Bashan, and the thick jungle along the Jordan, where lions lived.

Fire comes through the doors of the north and burns its way down to the river. The rulers (the “shepherds”) howl, because their glory is gone.

The rest of the chapter tells why: the people turn away the Shepherd God sends. Many see this fulfilled when Rome burned Jerusalem in AD 70, about forty years after it rejected Jesus.

Part two

Feed the flock of the slaughter

Verses 4–11

God tells Zechariah to act out a part: be the shepherd of sheep that everyone else is using.

Everybody's using them

The sheep are “the flock of the slaughter”: raised to be sold and killed. Three kinds of men handle them, and not one cares.

“Whose possessors slay them”not guiltyThe buyers kill them and sleep fine at night.
“They that sell them say, Blessed be the LORD; for I am rich”profitThe sellers thank God for the money they made off the sheep.
“Their own shepherds pity them not”no pityThe very men who should protect them don't care.

Many of us have been the sheep. Dealers, bosses, “friends” who used us and called it business. And some of us have been the sellers. God sees both, and saying His name over the money doesn't make it clean.

Two staffs

A shepherd carried a staff to guide and guard his sheep. Zechariah takes two and names them, because each one pictures something God was giving His people.

The first staff

Beauty

The Hebrew word (noam) means favour, kindness, pleasantness. God's goodness over them, holding the nations back.

The second staff

Bands

The word (chobelim) means binders, the cords that tie things together. The bond that made them one people.

He cuts off three bad shepherds, and nobody knows for sure who they were. But the flock hates the good shepherd back (“their soul also abhorred me”). So he lets go of those who want him gone. God doesn't force His care on anyone.

The few who knew

Most of the flock shrugged when Beauty broke. But a few, “the poor of the flock”, were watching the shepherd. They saw it happen just as he said, and they knew God was speaking.

That's how it went when Jesus came. The leaders turned Him away, but humble people, fishermen and tax collectors and sinners, were waiting on Him, and they knew. You don't have to be important to know God's voice. You have to be listening.

Part three

A goodly price

Verses 12–14

The shepherd asks for his wages. What they pay tells you what they think of him.

The price of a slave

pieces of silver

Thirty pieces of silver wasn't nothing, but it was an insult. In the law of Moses, that's what you paid a man if your ox gored his slave to death:

“If the ox shall push a manservant or a maidservant; he shall give unto their master thirty shekels of silver, and the ox shall be stoned.”

Exodus 21:32

So when God says “a goodly price that I was prised at” (valued at), He's speaking with bitter irony. They valued the LORD's shepherd at the price of a dead slave. God says, throw it to the potter.

Five hundred years later

Read what happened to Jesus, piece by piece:

  1. ✓they covenanted with him for thirty pieces of silverJudas sells Jesus to the chief priestsMatthew 26:15
  2. ✓he cast down the pieces of silver in the templeJudas throws the money back, in the house of the LORDMatthew 27:5
  3. ✓bought with them the potter’s fieldThe priests buy a potter's field with itMatthew 27:7

“Then was fulfilled that which was spoken by Jeremy the prophet, saying, And they took the thirty pieces of silver, the price of him that was valued, whom they of the children of Israel did value; And gave them for the potter’s field, as the Lord appointed me.”

Matthew 27:9–10

Matthew names Jeremy (Jeremiah), the prophet of the potter's house and the bought field, and weaves his words together with these from Zechariah. The point is the same: the Shepherd God sent was sold for the price of a slave, and God knew it centuries ahead.

Both staffs broken

First God's favour is cut off. The protection that held their enemies back is gone (verse 10).

Then the second staff breaks, right after the thirty pieces are thrown down. The brotherhood is broken, and the people turn on one another.

That's the order of things. When a man turns the Shepherd away, the relationships go next: brothers, wives, children, friends. The good news is that the Shepherd still takes back anyone who comes to Him.

Part four

The idol shepherd

Verses 15–17

Turn away the good shepherd, and you'll get a different one.

Two kinds of shepherd

Now Zechariah plays the opposite part, a foolish shepherd. This is the leader a people gets when it refuses God's.

The foolish shepherd

Takes from the sheep

Doesn't look for the lost, doesn't seek the young, doesn't heal the broken, doesn't feed the weak. He eats the fat ones and tears them up.

The good shepherd

Gives for the sheep

“For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost” (Luke 19:10). “I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep” (John 10:11).

Everything this world's shepherds take, Jesus gives. He goes looking for the man who wandered off. He heals what is broken. And He paid for His sheep with His own life.

Woe to the idol shepherd

“Idol” here means worthless, a nothing. He looks like a shepherd but he's empty, and when trouble comes he walks off.

If“the idol shepherd … leaveth the flock,”
→
then“the sword shall be upon his arm, and upon his right eye”

God aims at the arm that should have fought for the sheep and the eye that should have watched over them. He holds leaders to account for the people in their care.

“But he that is an hireling, and not the shepherd, whose own the sheep are not, seeth the wolf coming, and leaveth the sheep, and fleeth: and the wolf catcheth them, and scattereth the sheep.”

John 10:12

Points to remember

  1. Shepherds“Their own shepherds pity them not” 11:5A bad shepherd uses the flock. God sees it, and He will call him to account.
  2. God's name“Blessed be the LORD; for I am rich” 11:5Putting God's name on dirty money doesn't make it clean.
  3. Rejected“A goodly price that I was prised at of them” 11:13They valued God's shepherd at the price of a dead slave.
  4. Christ“Thirty pieces of silver” 11:12Jesus was sold for that very price, and the money went to a potter's field.
  5. Broken“That I might break the brotherhood” 11:14Turn the Shepherd away, and favour and brotherhood go with Him.
  6. The Good Shepherd“Woe to the idol shepherd that leaveth the flock!” 11:17The idol shepherd leaves. The Good Shepherd lays down His life.

Question 1 of 6

Shepherds

What does a bad shepherd do to the flock?

What verses tell us about this?

“The diseased have ye not strengthened, neither have ye healed that which was sick, neither have ye bound up that which was broken, neither have ye brought again that which was driven away, neither have ye sought that which was lost; but with force and with cruelty have ye ruled them.”

Ezekiel 34:4

Answer

He uses them. He doesn't look for the lost, heal the broken, or feed the weak. He feeds himself on them, and when trouble comes he leaves. God takes that personally. The sheep are His, and He holds every shepherd to account for how he treated them.

Question 2 of 6

God's name

Can a man say “Blessed be the LORD” and still be robbing people?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain; for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain.”

Exodus 20:7

“They profess that they know God; but in works they deny him, being abominable, and disobedient, and unto every good work reprobate.”

Titus 1:16

Answer

He can say it, but God isn't fooled. The sellers in verse 5 thanked the LORD for money they made off His sheep, and called themselves “not guilty.” That's taking His name in vain. Real faith shows up in how we treat people, especially the ones who can't fight back.

Question 3 of 6

Rejected

What were the people saying when they weighed out thirty pieces of silver?

What verses tell us about this?

“If the ox shall push a manservant or a maidservant; he shall give unto their master thirty shekels of silver, and the ox shall be stoned.”

Exodus 21:32

Answer

They were saying, “That's all you're worth to us.” Thirty pieces was the price of a slave killed by an ox. God took it as an insult to Himself, not just to Zechariah: “a goodly price that I was prised at.” Every man has to settle what the Shepherd is worth to him.

Question 4 of 6

Christ

How does Zechariah 11 point to Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“And said unto them, What will ye give me, and I will deliver him unto you? And they covenanted with him for thirty pieces of silver.”

Matthew 26:15

“Then was fulfilled that which was spoken by Jeremy the prophet, saying, And they took the thirty pieces of silver, the price of him that was valued, whom they of the children of Israel did value; And gave them for the potter’s field, as the Lord appointed me.”

Matthew 27:9–10

Answer

Jesus is the Shepherd God sent, and His own people turned Him away. Judas sold Him for thirty pieces of silver, threw the money down in the temple, and the priests bought a potter's field with it. Matthew says plainly that this fulfilled the prophets. God wasn't caught off guard. He wrote it down five hundred years ahead.

Question 5 of 6

Broken

What did the people lose when they turned the shepherd away?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.”

John 10:11

Answer

Two things: God's favour, which had protected them, and their brotherhood with each other. Both staffs broke. That still happens. Push the Shepherd out of your life and the protection goes, and then the relationships. But He hasn't stopped being the Good Shepherd. He still takes back anyone who comes.

Question 6 of 6

The Good Shepherd

How is Jesus different from the idol shepherd?

What verses tell us about this?

“But he that is an hireling, and not the shepherd, whose own the sheep are not, seeth the wolf coming, and leaveth the sheep, and fleeth: and the wolf catcheth them, and scattereth the sheep.”

John 10:12

“For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.”

Luke 19:10

Answer

The idol shepherd is empty, takes from the sheep, and leaves when the wolf comes. Jesus comes looking for the lost, heals what's broken, and stood His ground all the way to the cross. He didn't leave His sheep. He died for them, and He keeps them.
