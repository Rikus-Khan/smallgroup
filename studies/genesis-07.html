Genesis 7

The LORD shut him in

God calls Noah into the ark, waits seven more days, then shuts the door with his own hand. The flood that judged the world carried the ark up above it.

Part one

Come into the ark

Verses 1–10

The ark is finished. The sky is still clear. And God says, “Come.”

Grace came first

God doesn't say “Go into the ark.” He says “Come.” He speaks like a man standing in the doorway of his own house, calling his family in out of the storm.

Why Noah? Not because he was sinless. Two chapters on, we'll see him fall hard. Read the order in chapter 6:

First

“But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.”

Genesis 6:8

Then

“Noah was a just man and perfect in his generations, and Noah walked with God.”

Genesis 6:9

Grace first, then a righteous walk. Noah didn't earn the ark. He was given it, and then he lived like a man who had been given it. That's the order for us too.

Clean, and not clean

By sevens

Clean beasts and birds

The kinds God would accept as an offering: sheep, goats, cattle, doves. Noah would need extras. The first thing he does off the ark is sacrifice them (8:20).

By two

Beasts not clean

Every other kind, a male and a female, “to keep seed alive.” Enough to fill the earth again.

This is long before Moses wrote down the law of clean and unclean, but Noah already knew the difference. Bible teachers read “by sevens” two ways: seven animals of each clean kind, or seven pairs. Either way, God planned for worship before the first drop fell.

God waited: “for yet seven days”

Add up the ages in Genesis 5: Methuselah, the oldest man who ever lived, dies in the very year of the flood. God let the longest life in history run out before the waters came. Then he gave seven more days. Peter calls it “the longsuffering of God” (1 Peter 3:20).

All that the LORD commanded

Moses says it twice. Once when Noah builds (“Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he,” 6:22), and again here when he goes in.

Building took years of hammering on a boat nowhere near the sea. Going in took one step through a door. Both were obedience. For a man in recovery, most days it's not a hundred-year project. It's the next step God has already made plain.

In before the rain

Noah was six hundred years old. No one had ever seen a flood like this. He went in on God's word, with the sky still dry.

“By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear, prepared an ark to the saving of his house; by the which he condemned the world, and became heir of the righteousness which is by faith.”

Hebrews 11:7

That's what faith is: acting on what God said before you can see it. Noah didn't wait for the first drops to make up his mind.

Seven days with the door open

Noah didn't round up the animals. They came “unto Noah,” two and two, as God had said they would (6:20). Imagine the neighbours watching that parade for a week.

And still the door stood open for seven days. Jesus tells us what the world was doing that week:

“For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, And knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be.”

Matthew 24:38–39

Eating, drinking, marrying: nothing wrong with any of it. Their sin was living as if God had never spoken. Normal life went on right up to the last day.

Part two

The LORD shut him in

Verses 11–16

The waters come from above and below, and God closes the door himself.

From above and below

This was more than a long rain. Water burst up out of the earth and poured down out of the sky, both on the same day.

On the second day of creation God “divided the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament” (1:7). Now he lets them run back together. The flood is creation coming undone.

And notice the date: the six hundredth year, second month, seventeenth day. That's a ship's log, not a fable. Genesis tells this as something that happened, on a day you could write down.

Who closed the door?

Not Noah. “The LORD shut him in.” Noah's safety didn't hang on how well he latched it. God closed it himself.

That's the security of a man in Christ. You are kept by God's hand, not by your grip.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

One door, and it is open today

God told Noah to put one door in the side of the ark (6:16). Not a door for each family on the road. One. Jesus says the same of himself:

“I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”

John 10:9

The same door that kept Noah in kept the world out. There came a day when it was shut, and knocking would not open it. That day hasn't come for you. The door is open right now.

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

Part three

The waters prevailed

Verses 17–24

The same water that judged the world lifted the ark above it.

The flood carried the ark

The more the waters rose, the higher the ark rode. The thing that destroyed everything outside was the very thing that lifted Noah up.

Peter sees Christ in this. Noah's family went down into the waters of judgment inside the ark, and came up the other side alive. Baptism is a picture of the same thing: a man in Christ goes through death and judgment with him, and rises with him.

“The like figure whereunto even baptism doth also now save us (not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the answer of a good conscience toward God,) by the resurrection of Jesus Christ:”

1 Peter 3:21

The water didn't save anybody. The ark did. In the same way, baptism doesn't wash sin away; Peter says so. It's the outward picture of a man who is already safe inside Christ.

Fifteen cubits over the mountains

A cubit is about 18 inches, so the water stood about 22 feet over the highest peak. “All the high hills, that were under the whole heaven, were covered.”

The ark was thirty cubits high (6:15). Many have noticed that a loaded boat that size would ride about fifteen cubits deep. The water was just deep enough to carry it over every peak.

God meant what he said

There's no softening this. Every man outside the ark died. God had told Noah why:

“And God said unto Noah, The end of all flesh is come before me; for the earth is filled with violence through them; and, behold, I will destroy them with the earth.”

Genesis 6:13

Violence, corruption, every thought “only evil continually” (6:5). God takes sin seriously because he takes people seriously. Sin was tearing his world apart.

But he takes no pleasure in it:

“Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?”

Ezekiel 33:11

Only those in the ark

A whole world of people, and eight left alive.

What made the difference? Not that the eight were stronger swimmers or better men. They were inside. Everyone in the ark lived; everyone outside it died.

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

The waters stood a hundred and fifty days, five months, over the whole earth. Noah couldn't see land the whole time. But he was in the right place.

Question 1 of 6

Grace

Why was Noah the one God called into the ark?

What verses tell us about this?

“But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.”

Genesis 6:8

“By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear, prepared an ark to the saving of his house; by the which he condemned the world, and became heir of the righteousness which is by faith.”

Hebrews 11:7

Answer

Because he found grace. God's favour came first, and Noah believed God and walked with him. Hebrews calls it “the righteousness which is by faith.” Noah wasn't sinless; he was a man who trusted God and lived like it. The same grace calls you in today.

Question 2 of 6

God's patience

How long did God wait before the flood came?

What verses tell us about this?

“Which sometime were disobedient, when once the longsuffering of God waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing, wherein few, that is, eight souls were saved by water.”

1 Peter 3:20

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Through the building of the ark and the whole life of Methuselah, then seven more days with the door open. God still waits, because he wants men to come in, not to perish.

Question 3 of 6

Obedience

What did Noah do with what God told him?

What verses tell us about this?

“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.”

James 1:22

Answer

He did it, all of it, before he could see why. He built when there was no water, and he went in while the sky was still clear. Faith that only listens isn't faith. Today, do the next thing God has already made plain to you.

Question 4 of 6

Salvation

Who shut the door of the ark, and why does that matter?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”

John 10:9

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Answer

The LORD did. Noah's safety rested on God's hand, not his own. That's the security of every believer: Jesus is the one door, and once you are in him, he keeps you. A bad day, even a fall, doesn't wash you overboard.

Question 5 of 6

Sin

Does God take sin seriously?

What verses tell us about this?

“And God said unto Noah, The end of all flesh is come before me; for the earth is filled with violence through them; and, behold, I will destroy them with the earth.”

Genesis 6:13

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

Answer

Yes. Sin destroys people, and God will not let it go on forever. The flood shows that judgment is real. But the ark shows that God always makes a way out before the judgment falls. Sin earns death; life comes as a gift, in Christ.

Question 6 of 6

The real world

Jesus said his coming would be like the days of Noah. How do we live ready?

What verses tell us about this?

“But as the days of Noe were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be.”

Matthew 24:37

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

Answer

The people of Noah's day weren't caught out because they ate and worked and married. They were caught out because they lived as if God hadn't spoken. Living ready means being inside the ark now, trusting Christ, and then going to work today as a man who belongs to him: honest, sober, and not ashamed.
