Numbers 15

“When ye be come into the land”

The chapter after the worst day in the wilderness opens with a promise of the land, bread and wine. Then come one law for the stranger, the sin of ignorance and the sin done presumptuously, a man gathering sticks on the sabbath, and a ribband of blue to look at and remember.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. The landWhat did the LORD promise in the first words of this chapter?
  2. The strangerWhat law was there for the stranger that sojourned with Israel?
  3. IgnoranceWhat was done when a man, or the whole congregation, sinned through ignorance?
  4. PresumptionWhat did the soul that sinned presumptuously do to the LORD and to his word?
  5. The sabbathWhat did the man do on the sabbath day, and what did they do with him first?
  6. The fringeWhy were they to put a ribband of blue on the fringes of their garments?

The very next words

  1. 14:4“Let us make a captain, and let us return into Egypt”the refusal
  2. 14:29“Your carcases shall fall in this wilderness”the sentence
  3. 14:31“them will I bring in”the children
  4. 15:2“When ye be come into the land”the next words

Chapter 14 ended in tears and defeat, chased back unto Hormah. The men who would not go up were told they would die in the wilderness, forty years, “each day for a year” (14:34). Then the LORD's very next words to Moses are about the land: what they will offer “when ye be come into the land of your habitations, which I give unto you”.

“But your little ones, which ye said should be a prey, them will I bring in, and they shall know the land which ye have despised.”

Numbers 14:31

God punished the fathers, and he did not cancel the promise. The children would go in. A man who has failed badly needs to hear that: the LORD is already speaking about tomorrow.

Part one

“When ye be come into the land”

Verses 1–21

Flour, oil and wine with every offering, one law for the stranger, and the first of the dough.

Bread and wine in the land

  • an offering by firea burnt offering or a sacrifice: for a vow, a freewill offering, or the feasts (verse 3)
  • a meat offering“meat” is food: here fine flour mingled with oil, offered with the beast
  • a drink offeringwine poured out to the LORD, not drunk
  • a tenth deala tenth part of an ephah of flour, about two quarts
  • an hina measure for oil and wine, about a gallon

Think where they were standing. No fields, no vineyards, no olive trees: just sand, tents and manna. And the LORD tells them how much flour, oil and wine to bring with their offerings in the land. You don't plan the wine for a place you're never going to reach. The law itself was a promise.

“A sweet savour unto the LORD” comes six times in this chapter (verses 3, 7, 10, 13, 14 and 24). He means to be pleased with them again.

The bigger the beast, the more beside it

a lambverses 4–5
1 tenth deal¼ hin of oil¼ hin of wine
a ramverses 6–7
2 tenth deals⅓ hin of oil⅓ hin of wine
a bullockverses 8–10

The rule is plain and fair: the bigger the offering, the more flour, oil and wine went with it, and the same for every one “according to their number” (verse 12). Nobody had to guess, and nobody was left out because he brought a lamb and not a bullock.

Poured out

Look at what happened to the wine. Nobody drank it. It was poured out before the LORD with the sacrifice, “for a sweet savour unto the LORD” (verse 7).

Some of us know exactly what a bottle can do to a man. Here the same thing is not lifted to a man's lips at all. It is given away to God, all of it. That is a picture for anything that has had a hold on you: it does not get a place at your table; it gets poured out before him.

“Yea, and if I be offered upon the sacrifice and service of your faith, I joy, and rejoice with you all.”

Philippians 2:17

Paul speaks of being “offered upon the sacrifice”, as the wine was poured upon the offering. He saw his own life as wine poured out beside a sacrifice, spent for other believers, and he said he did it with joy.

One law for the stranger

“As ye are, so shall the stranger be before the LORD.”

A stranger is a man born outside Israel who lived among them. He did not get a second-class altar. If he wanted to offer to the LORD, the same offering was welcome, with the same flour, oil and wine. The word comes seven times in this chapter (verses 14, 15, 16, 26, 29 and 30; twice in 15), even where it speaks of forgiveness.

“Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God;”

Ephesians 2:19

Most of us came to God as strangers, from a long way off. In Christ there is no back row in the house of God.

The first of your dough

  • an heave offeringa portion lifted up to the LORD and given to his service
  • the first of your dougha cake from the first batch, before the family ate
  • the threshingfloorwhere the grain was beaten out; its first was given the same way

The first loaf out of the first dough went to the LORD. Not the burnt one, not what was left at the end of the week: the first. And notice how it starts: “When ye come into the land whither I bring you” (verse 18). Twice in one chapter he speaks of the land as good as theirs (verses 2 and 18).

“For if the firstfruit be holy, the lump is also holy: and if the root be holy, so are the branches.”

Romans 11:16

Paul reaches back to this very law: when the first part is given to God, the whole batch is his. Give him the first of your day, the first of your pay, before the rest is spent.

Part two

Ignorance, and presumption

Verses 22–36

The sin a man did not see, the sin he did on purpose, and a man with an armful of sticks.

When everybody missed it

To err is to go wrong, to wander off the road. Here the whole congregation has gone wrong “by ignorance without the knowledge of the congregation”: nobody noticed until later. Then all of them together bring a bullock for a burnt offering and a kid of the goats for a sin offering, and the priest makes an atonement.

8times “ignorance” or “ignorantly” (verses 24–29)
3times “it shall be forgiven” (verses 25, 26, 28)

A whole people can drift: a family, a church, a group of friends. Everyone does a thing, nobody stops to ask, and it's wrong. God made a way to put that right too. When you find out, you don't hide it. You bring it to him, together.

Two kinds of sin

Verses 27–29

Through ignorance

He did not know, or did not see it. When he found out, he brought a she goat of the first year, the priest made an atonement, “and it shall be forgiven him”.

Verses 30–31

Presumptuously

He knew, and did it anyway, as if the LORD had no say. He “reproacheth the LORD” and “hath despised the word of the LORD”. The law names no offering for him: “that soul shall utterly be cut off”.

Presumptuously means boldly, on purpose, as though you had the right. Ought in verse 30 means anything at all. To reproach is to insult. The difference is not how big the sin is, but the heart behind it: a man who stumbles in the dark, and a man who looks God in the face and says no.

“Who can understand his errors? cleanse thou me from secret faults. Keep back thy servant also from presumptuous sins; let them not have dominion over me: then shall I be upright, and I shall be innocent from the great transgression.”

Psalm 19:12–13

No offering for it, and yet

Every man here has done it: sinned with his eyes open. If that's you, from yesterday, listen. David did it too, with Bathsheba and her husband, and he knew the law had no offering for it:

“For thou desirest not sacrifice; else would I give it: thou delightest not in burnt offering. The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.”

Psalm 51:16–17

“And David said unto Nathan, I have sinned against the LORD. And Nathan said unto David, The LORD also hath put away thy sin; thou shalt not die.”

2 Samuel 12:13

David brought what the law had no altar for: a broken heart. And the LORD put away his sin.

Justified from all things

“Be it known unto you therefore, men and brethren, that through this man is preached unto you the forgiveness of sins: And by him all that believe are justified from all things, from which ye could not be justified by the law of Moses.”

Acts 13:38–39

“From all things, from which ye could not be justified by the law of Moses.” The law had no lamb for the sin done with open eyes. Christ is that Lamb. Verse 31 shows how serious sin is; the cross shows how far grace goes.

“My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:”

1 John 2:1

The man with the sticks

Moses sets this right after verses 30 and 31, and it reads as an example of them. The sabbath was the sign between the LORD and Israel, and the law already said what working on it meant:

“Ye shall keep the sabbath therefore; for it is holy unto you: every one that defileth it shall surely be put to death: for whosoever doeth any work therein, that soul shall be cut off from among his people.”

Exodus 31:14

They put him in ward, held him under guard, because “it was not declared what should be done to him”. Moses does not tell us what was still undecided. What we see is that nobody took matters into his own hands. They brought him to Moses, Aaron and the congregation, and they waited until the LORD spoke.

This was a judgment in Israel's own courts, under the covenant God made with that nation at Sinai. Read it calmly, and read it whole: God is holy, his word is not a suggestion, and a man who despises it in the open does not stand.

Our rest

The sabbath was a shadow, and Paul says what it was the shadow of:

“Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days: Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.”

Colossians 2:16–17

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.”

Matthew 11:28–29

Think of that man in the wilderness, where the manna had fallen six days a week, double on the sixth. He had what he needed, and he went out anyway. Some of us can't stop. We keep working, keep hustling, keep gathering, because stopping means facing ourselves and trusting God. Christ says come, and rest. The rest is in him.

Part three

“A ribband of blue”

Verses 37–41

Something to look at, so a man remembers before his eyes take him somewhere else.

Something to look at

  • fringestassels of threads on the border (the edge) of a man's outer garment
  • a ribbanda ribbon, a cord: one of blue on each fringe
  • the purpose“that ye may look upon it, and remember … and do them”

Moses gives the same law again later: “Thou shalt make thee fringes upon the four quarters of thy vesture” (Deuteronomy 22:12). Every time a man sat, walked or reached for something, the blue swung into sight.

Your own heart and your own eyes

Ye use to means “you are in the habit of”. To go a whoring is to be unfaithful: to leave the LORD and chase other loves, the way an unfaithful wife leaves her husband. God says it plainly: your eyes and your heart have a habit, and the habit leads away from him.

“I made a covenant with mine eyes; why then should I think upon a maid?”

Job 31:1

The eyes go first and the heart follows: the screen, the street, the old corner, the bottle on the shelf. God's answer is not just “try harder”. It is “put something in front of your eyes that reminds you who you belong to”. Look at the blue, remember, do.

The border of his garment

Jesus kept the law, and the crowds knew the border of his garment:

“And whithersoever he entered, into villages, or cities, or country, they laid the sick in the streets, and besought him that they might touch if it were but the border of his garment: and as many as touched him were made whole.”

Mark 6:56

And he warned about the other way to wear it:

“But all their works they do for to be seen of men: they make broad their phylacteries, and enlarge the borders of their garments,”

Matthew 23:5

The fringe was for the man wearing it to look at, not for others to admire. A reminder that turns into a show has lost its point. Whatever you use to remember, keep it between you and the Lord.

Real world: put the blue where your eyes go

The chapter ends the way it began, with God's own name, twice in one verse: “I am the LORD your God, which brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God: I am the LORD your God.” He brought them out, and he is bringing them in.

  • SinSome of it we stumbled into. Some of it we did with our eyes open. Name which is which.
  • RegretThe man who sinned presumptuously had no offering in the law. David knew that, and he brought a broken heart.
  • Grace“By him all that believe are justified from all things.” Even the sin done with open eyes.
  • HopeRight after the worst day, the next words God spoke in this book were about the land. He is talking about your tomorrow.
  • TodayPut a reminder where your eyes go first: a verse on your phone's lock screen, a card in your wallet, a brother's number at the top of your list. Look at it, remember, and do.

Points to remember

  1. The land“When ye be come into the land of your habitations, which I give unto you” 15:2After the judgment, the LORD's next words were about the land. He had not cancelled the promise.
  2. The stranger“as ye are, so shall the stranger be before the LORD” 15:15One law and one welcome for the outsider. There is no back row with God.
  3. Ignorance“the priest shall make an atonement … and it shall be forgiven him” 15:28The sin a man did not see, God made a way to forgive when it came to light.
  4. Presumption“the same reproacheth the LORD … he hath despised the word of the LORD” 15:30–31Sin with the eyes open insults God. The law had no offering for it; Christ is the offering.
  5. The sabbath“they put him in ward, because it was not declared what should be done to him” 15:34Nobody acted on his own. They waited for the LORD. The rest the sabbath pictured is in Christ.
  6. The fringe“that ye may look upon it, and remember all the commandments of the LORD, and do them” 15:39Put a reminder where your eyes go, so your eyes don't lead your heart away.

Question 1 of 6

The land

What did the LORD promise in the first words of this chapter?

What verses tell us about this?

“But your little ones, which ye said should be a prey, them will I bring in, and they shall know the land which ye have despised.”

Numbers 14:31

Answer

The land: “the land of your habitations, which I give unto you”. Right after the sentence on the fathers in chapter 14, the LORD spoke of what they would offer in the land. The children would go in.

Question 2 of 6

The stranger

What law was there for the stranger that sojourned with Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God;”

Ephesians 2:19

Answer

The same law: one ordinance, one law and one manner for Israel and for the stranger. He could offer the same offering and was forgiven the same way. In Christ we are no more strangers.

Question 3 of 6

Ignorance

What was done when a man, or the whole congregation, sinned through ignorance?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The congregation brought a bullock for a burnt offering and a kid of the goats for a sin offering; a single man brought a she goat of the first year. The priest made an atonement, and it was forgiven.

Question 4 of 6

Presumption

What did the soul that sinned presumptuously do to the LORD and to his word?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

He reproached the LORD and despised his word, and the law had no offering for him: he was cut off. But in Christ all that believe are justified from all things, and he forgives the sin we confess.

Question 5 of 6

The sabbath

What did the man do on the sabbath day, and what did they do with him first?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.”

Matthew 11:28–29

Answer

He gathered sticks on the sabbath. They brought him to Moses and Aaron and the congregation and put him in ward until the LORD declared what should be done. The rest the sabbath pictured, we find in Christ.

Question 6 of 6

The fringe

Why were they to put a ribband of blue on the fringes of their garments?

What verses tell us about this?

“I made a covenant with mine eyes; why then should I think upon a maid?”

Job 31:1

Answer

So that they would look on it and remember the LORD's commandments and do them, and not follow their own heart and their own eyes into unfaithfulness. A reminder where the eyes go.
