1 Peter 5

Cast it all on Him

Peter, who once denied Jesus, tells the shepherds how to lead and every man how to stand: humble, awake, and carrying nothing alone. And then he says goodbye.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

Feed the flock

Verses 1–4

A man who failed badly is the one writing to the leaders. That is exactly why he can.

“Feed”: Peter has heard that before

JesusSimon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me more than these?

PeterYea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee.

JesusFeed my lambs.

Jesus, a third timeSimon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me?

PeterLord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee.

JesusFeed my sheep.

Why Peter can say “feed”

John 21. Three denials by a charcoal fire (John 18:18), and three questions by another one (John 21:9). Jesus didn't throw Peter out. He gave him the job. Now, about thirty years later, he hands the same job to the elders. “Also an elder”: he doesn't pull rank, and he can say “a witness of the sufferings of Christ” because he was there.

Driving, or leading

Peter lists three bad reasons to lead: “by constraint” (because you were pushed into it), “for filthy lucre” (for what you can get out of it), and “lords over God's heritage” (to run people). A good shepherd walks in front: “ensamples to the flock.” You don't have to be an elder to matter here. To a man a year behind you, you are already the one he is watching.

The chief Shepherd

Every shepherd works for the Shepherd.

“I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.”

John 10:11

Leaders in the church are under-shepherds. The flock isn't theirs, and the Shepherd is coming back. The prize at the Greek games was a wreath of leaves, and it dried up in a week. His crown “fadeth not away.” So when a man serves quietly and nobody thanks him, nothing is lost: the Shepherd saw it.

Part two

Humble, and not alone

Verses 5–9

Put on humility, hand over your cares, and keep your eyes open: there is a lion about.

Tie on humility

The word “clothed” pictures a servant tying on an apron. Peter had a reason to remember one: the night Jesus tied on a towel and washed His disciples' feet, and Peter at first refused.

“If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's feet.”

John 13:14

And “ye younger, submit yourselves unto the elder”: listen to the man who has walked further down the road than you. Then “all of you be subject one to another.” Nobody is above being told something. For a man who has spent years being his own boss, with a bad boss, this is a real change.

Down, then up

  1. PrideGod stands against it
  2. Humilitythe apron, tied on
  3. Under His handnot mine
  4. In due timeHe lifts you

“Surely he scorneth the scorners: but he giveth grace unto the lowly.”

Proverbs 3:34

“Resisteth” is a battle word: God lines up against pride. And “in due time” means on His schedule, not yours. Humble doesn't mean you think you're nothing. It means you stop pretending you can run this alone, which is the same thing as asking for help. Today: ask one person for help with one thing.

Throw it on Him

“Casting” is the word for what the crowd did on Palm Sunday: “they cast their garments upon the colt” (Luke 19:35). You throw it on, and you let go. “Care” is worry that pulls the mind in pieces. And notice “all”: not the spiritual ones, but the debts, the court date, last night, the phone call you're dreading.

He careth for you

“Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee: he shall never suffer the righteous to be moved.”

Psalm 55:22

The reason is short: “for he careth for you.” He is not annoyed that you brought it. Today: name three cares on paper, say them to Him out loud, and then do the one small thing you can do about each. Leave the rest with Him.

The lion hunts the one alone

“Sober” is clear-headed, not drunk and not dulled. “Vigilant” is wide awake. A lion doesn't charge a herd. It watches for the one who wandered off, or is dozing. Men in recovery know the moments: tired, hungry, alone, angry.

“Whom resist stedfast in the faith” doesn't mean fight him by yourself. It means stand firm, and look at the next line: “the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren that are in the world.” You are not the only one. Go back to the flock, pick up the phone, get to the meeting.

He can only look

One more thing. He “walketh about, seeking”: he looks. He can't walk through a believer in Christ's hand (John 10:28). And Scripture's one-line instruction for the fight is short: “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you” (James 4:7).

And if he already got you yesterday

Peter knew about that, too. Before the night he denied Jesus, Jesus told him what was coming:

“And the Lord said, Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat: But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Luke 22:31-32

Notice what Jesus does not say. He does not say, “if you fall.” He says “when thou art converted”: when you've turned back. His prayer was not that Peter would never be sifted, but that his faith would “not fail.” And it didn't. Peter fell hard, and Jesus restored him and gave him a job.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

If you fell yesterday, tell God and tell one man today. That is the way back. The lion wants you to hide. Don't.

Part three

The God of all grace

Verses 10–14

The last word is not about the lion. It is about what God does after the suffering, and who is standing with Peter as he writes.

“After that ye have suffered a while”

“A while” is the key. The suffering is real and it can feel like it will never end, but Peter says it has an end, and then God does the work. Notice who does it: “the God of all grace” does the perfecting. You don't do it with willpower.

Four things God does

  1. Perfectthe word was used of mending fishing nets: put right what is torn
  2. Stablishset you firm, so you don't blow over
  3. Strengthengive strength you didn't have
  4. Settlelay a foundation under you

For a man who feels torn, shaky, weak and unsettled, that is a list of exactly what he needs, and Peter says God does all four. Remember Luke 22: Jesus told Peter, “strengthen thy brethren.” Now he is the one writing, and he has been through the fire himself. Today: remember that He “hath called us unto his eternal glory.” That's where this is going.

“Babylon,” and the man who carried it

The letter went by hand, with Silvanus (the Silas of Paul's second journey) carrying it out to the believers scattered through Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia and Bithynia. “Babylon” is most likely Rome: believers wrote this way when speaking openly was dangerous, and Revelation later does the same. Others read it as a real Babylon on the Euphrates.

He says “I have written briefly.” Five short chapters. And the point of it all is a single line: “this is the true grace of God wherein ye stand.” Not a place you climb to. A place you're standing in.

“Marcus my son”

Mark, who quit and went home → “Marcus my son” Acts 13:13, then 1 Peter 5:13

“Only Luke is with me. Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry.”

2 Timothy 4:11

This is the same John Mark who left Paul and Barnabas on the first journey, and caused the big argument that split them (Acts 15:37-39). Paul called him “profitable” at the end of his life, and Peter calls him “my son.” Early church writers say Mark's Gospel is built on Peter's preaching. A man who quit once became useful twice over. Don't write off the day you quit.

1 Peter on one line each

  1. Chapter 1A living hopeborn again, kept by God's power
  2. Chapter 2Living stonesfollow Christ's steps; by His stripes we are healed
  3. Chapter 3Ready with an answerfor the hope that is in you
  4. Chapter 4Enoughthe old life, love, and the fiery trial
  5. Chapter 5Cast it on Himshepherds, humility and the God of all grace

“Who are kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation ready to be revealed in the last time.”

1 Peter 1:5

Strangers here, and held by Him. It is a letter for people who don't fit anymore, and it ends where it began: they are kept. Peter wrote it, we believe, a few years before his own death. He never walked away from what he wrote.

Peace be with you all that are in Christ Jesus.

That is how the letter ends: not with a warning, and not with the lion. With peace, and a greeting. “A kiss of charity” was the ordinary greeting of the day, and it meant, you are family here.

If you carry the old life, remember that “the time past … may suffice.” If you carry regret, “cast all your care.” The grace is His, “the God of all grace,” and the hope is that suffering lasts “a while.” Today: hand one thing over to Him before you go to work.

Points to remember

  1. Shepherds“Feed the flock of God which is among you” 5:2Peter heard “Feed my sheep” from Jesus after he failed. Leaders serve the flock; it isn't theirs.
  2. Example“Neither as being lords over God’s heritage, but being ensamples to the flock” 5:3Drive from behind, or walk in front. Somebody is always watching how you walk.
  3. Humble“God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble” 5:5Tie on the apron, ask for help, and let Him lift you in His time.
  4. Care“Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you” 5:7Throw it on Him and let go. He is not annoyed that you brought it.
  5. Lion“Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about” 5:8He hunts the one who is alone or off guard. Stay with the flock and stand firm. If you fell, confess and come back.
  6. After“after that ye have suffered a while, make you perfect, stablish, strengthen, settle you” 5:10The suffering has an end. Then the God of all grace mends, steadies, strengthens and settles you.

Question 1 of 6

Shepherds

What does Peter ask of the men who lead God's people, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.”

John 10:11

Answer

Feed the flock willingly, with a ready mind, not because you were pushed into it and not for what you can get. The flock belongs to God, and the chief Shepherd will reward every shepherd who served under Him.

Question 2 of 6

Example

What is the difference between being a lord and being an example?

What verses tell us about this?

“If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's feet.”

John 13:14

Answer

A lord drives people from behind and runs their lives. An example walks in front and does it himself, as Jesus did with the towel. Anybody a step behind you is following how you live.

Question 3 of 6

Humble

What does God do with the proud, and what does He do with the humble?

What verses tell us about this?

“Surely he scorneth the scorners: but he giveth grace unto the lowly.”

Proverbs 3:34

Answer

He stands against the proud and gives grace to the humble. If you put yourself under His mighty hand, He lifts you up in His own time. You don't have to do the lifting.

Question 4 of 6

Care

What do we do with our cares, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee: he shall never suffer the righteous to be moved.”

Psalm 55:22

Answer

Throw all of them on Him, and let go. The reason is that He cares for you. He isn't annoyed that you brought them, and He can carry what you can't.

Question 5 of 6

Lion

Who is hunting us, and how do we stand against him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.”

James 4:7

Answer

The devil, our adversary, who goes looking for the man who is alone or not watching. Stay clear-headed and awake, resist him by standing firm in the faith, and remember you are not the only one in this fight. If you fall, confess it and come back.

Question 6 of 6

After

What does the God of all grace do after we have suffered a while?

What verses tell us about this?

“For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory;”

2 Corinthians 4:17

Answer

He makes us perfect (mends us), stablishes, strengthens and settles us. The suffering lasts “a while,” and it has an end. Then the God of all grace does the work, and He has already called us to His eternal glory.
