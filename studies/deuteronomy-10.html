Deuteronomy 10

What doth the LORD thy God require of thee?

The LORD makes the broken tables again and Moses puts them in the ark. Then Moses asks what the LORD requires: to fear him, walk in his ways, love him and serve him, and to circumcise the foreskin of the heart. The LORD regardeth not persons, and loveth the stranger.

Part one

“At that time the LORD said unto me”

Verses 1–11

The broken tables are made again, and put in the ark.

New tables, the same words

The first tables were broken in chapter 9. The LORD does not write something different. He wrote “according to the first writing, the ten commandments” (verse 4). Moses hewed the stone; the LORD wrote.

Then the tables went into an ark, and the mercy seat goes over it: “in the ark thou shalt put the testimony that I shall give thee” (Exodus 25:21). The law was not thrown out when it was broken. It was kept, and covered.

A second chance is not a lighter law

What did not change

“the first writing”

The same ten commandments, the same God, the same standard. The calf did not make the law any softer, and breaking it did not make it go away.

What was added

An ark, a mercy seat, and a prayer answered

Moses prayed forty days (9:25), and the LORD said: “Hew thee two tables.” The people who broke the covenant were given it again, with the mercy seat over it.

No one is saved by keeping the law: “by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified” (Romans 3:20). The law was given to show us what we are, and to bring us to Christ (Galatians 3:24). The mercy seat is where the sinner is met. That is where the men in this room live.

A priest dies, and another stands

Moses slips in two verses of travel. He names the stops (Beeroth of the children of Jaakan, Mosera, Gudgodah, Jotbath), and we leave them where the text leaves them: it fixes no place on a map. What it does say is that Aaron died, and “Eleazar his son ministered in the priest’s office in his stead”. Stead means place. Numbers says Aaron died “in the top of the mount” (Numbers 20:28); here Moses names the stage of the march.

“And they truly were many priests, because they were not suffered to continue by reason of death: But this man, because he continueth ever, hath an unchangeable priesthood.”

Hebrews 7:23–24

Aaron, whom Moses prayed for in 9:20, did not live for ever, and no priest did. Each one had to be replaced. Jesus does not die again, and he does not have to be replaced.

The tribe whose land was the LORD

Every other tribe

A share of the land

A hillside, a valley, a well, a boundary stone. Something to farm, to pass on, and to lose.

Levi

“the LORD is his inheritance”

Set apart “to bear the ark of the covenant of the LORD, to stand before the LORD to minister unto him, and to bless in his name”.

“And the LORD spake unto Aaron, Thou shalt have no inheritance in their land, neither shalt thou have any part among them: I am thy part and thine inheritance among the children of Israel.”

Numbers 18:20

Peter says every believer is now a priest: “a royal priesthood” (1 Peter 2:9). A man who has lost a house, a marriage, a licence, or ten years has still been given the thing the Levites were given: the LORD himself, who cannot be taken from him.

“Arise, take thy journey”

Arise, take thy journey before the people.

The calf, the broken tables, the forty days, the prayer, and then the LORD says: go. “that they may go in and possess the land, which I sware unto their fathers to give unto them”. He had said he would give it, and, in spite of everything, he still would.

If you are lying where you fell, that is for you too. The LORD does not say, “Think about it for another forty days.” He says, Arise. The sin was real and the prayer was heard, and now there is a road.

Part two

“What doth the LORD thy God require of thee?”

Verses 12–22

The question in the middle of Deuteronomy, and its answer.

Five things, and one reason

  • Fear“to fear the LORD thy God”. Take him seriously.
  • Walk“to walk in all his ways”. A road, taken one step at a time.
  • Love“and to love him”. This is what the other four grow out of.
  • Serve“with all thy heart and with all thy soul”. Not half.
  • Keep“the commandments of the LORD, and his statutes”, which are for “thy good”.

Moses asks “what doth the LORD… require of thee”, and the answer is not a long list of new laws. It is five things that were all there from the start. And the reason is not “because I said so” but “for thy good”.

Not heavy, and the same in every age

“He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”

Micah 6:8

“For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.”

1 John 5:3

Micah asks the same question and gets the same kind of answer: a way of living toward God and men, from the heart. John says the commandments are not heavy for those who love him.

But a man who has tried to do all this knows how it goes. Verse 16 is where Moses says what has to happen first.

Everything is his, and he delighted in them

“Behold, the heaven and the heaven of heavens is the LORD’S thy God, the earth also, with all that therein is.” He has no need of Israel. And yet: “Only the LORD had a delight in thy fathers to love them, and he chose their seed after them.”

“The LORD did not set his love upon you, nor choose you, because ye were more in number than any people; for ye were the fewest of all people: But because the LORD loved you, and because he would keep the oath which he had sworn unto your fathers, hath the LORD brought you out with a mighty hand, and redeemed you out of the house of bondmen, from the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt.”

Deuteronomy 7:7–8

It is the same ground as chapter 7: the choosing came from the LORD's love and not from their worth. You are not loved because you are impressive. The Owner of the earth delighted in men with nothing.

The heart has a foreskin

Circumcision was the mark God gave Abraham: “And ye shall circumcise the flesh of your foreskin; and it shall be a token of the covenant betwixt me and you” (Genesis 17:11). It cut away flesh. Moses asks for the same cutting on the inside: the covering that keeps a heart closed to God.

“For he is not a Jew, which is one outwardly; neither is that circumcision, which is outward in the flesh: But he is a Jew, which is one inwardly; and circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter; whose praise is not of men, but of God.”

Romans 2:28–29

Paul is not making this up. He is reading Moses. The sign on the body was never the point: a man could carry the mark and have a stiff neck, just as Israel did. What God wanted was the thing the mark stood for, a heart that turns when he pulls.

Moses commands it, and God does it

The command

“Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart”

Deuteronomy 10:16. A man cannot cut his own heart open. Moses commands it here, and later promises that the LORD himself will do it for the man who turns to him (30:6).

The promise

“the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart”

Deuteronomy 30:6: “And the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart, and the heart of thy seed, to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, that thou mayest live.”

“In whom also ye are circumcised with the circumcision made without hands, in putting off the body of the sins of the flesh by the circumcision of Christ:”

Colossians 2:11

“Made without hands”: not by a man's effort. The command shows what we cannot do, and the promise shows who does it. A man who cannot change his own heart is exactly the man Christ came for.

Old words in verses 17 to 22

  • terribleawesome, so great that a man stands in awe of it. Not “bad”: “these great and terrible things” (verse 21) are the plagues and the sea.
  • regardeth not personspays no attention to a man's rank or standing. The LORD does not take sides by who you are.
  • rewardhere, a bribe. The LORD “nor taketh reward”: nobody can buy a judge who is God.
  • the judgment of the fatherlesshe sees that they get justice: he takes up their cause.
  • the strangera foreigner who lived among Israel with no family or land there.
  • cleavehold fast to, stay stuck to (verse 20).

No favourites

“Then Peter opened his mouth, and said, Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons: But in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is accepted with him.”

Acts 10:34–35

“For there is no difference between the Jew and the Greek: for the same Lord over all is rich unto all that call upon him.”

Romans 10:12

He cannot be bought, and he cannot be bullied. It cuts both ways: no good name will help a proud man, and no bad one will keep out a man who calls on him. The same Lord, rich to all who call.

Who he looks after, and who we are to love

  • The fatherlessHe “doth execute the judgment” for them.
  • The widowThe same. Nobody left to speak for her, so he does.
  • The strangerHe “loveth the stranger, in giving him food and raiment”.

Then the turn: “Love ye therefore the stranger: for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt.” Israel is to love the stranger because that is who they used to be. The law said so before, with the same reason (Leviticus 19:34, “thou shalt love him as thyself; for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt”), and Moses says it again.

“Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God;”

Ephesians 2:19

That is where each man here came from. “Without Christ… strangers from the covenants of promise” (Ephesians 2:12), and now in the household. So when a new man walks into the room, nobody has to be told what to do.

From seventy to the stars

souls who went down into Egypt

“All the souls of the house of Jacob, which came into Egypt, were threescore and ten” (Genesis 46:27). Moses says it again: “Thy fathers went down into Egypt with threescore and ten persons; and now the LORD thy God hath made thee as the stars of heaven for multitude.” The men of war alone, counted on the plains of Moab, were “six hundred thousand and a thousand seven hundred and thirty” (Numbers 26:51).

The LORD had told Abraham, “Look now toward heaven, and tell the stars, if thou be able to number them… So shall thy seed be” (Genesis 15:5). Moses can say it is done: “He is thy praise, and he is thy God.” What he promised, he did.

Real world: a heart that turns

  • SinA stiff neck: knowing what the LORD wants and not turning. And a cold shoulder for the stranger, the man who is new, and the man with nothing to give back.
  • RegretThe first tables were broken, and the LORD wrote the same words again. What was broken is not the end of the story.
  • GraceThe command is “circumcise your heart”, and the promise is “the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart” (30:6). The law sits in the ark with the mercy seat over it.
  • Hope“Arise, take thy journey.” After the sin and the prayer, a road. Seventy became the stars, and he keeps what he swears.
  • TodayAsk the LORD to do what you cannot do to your own heart. Then speak to one man nobody sits with. And if today is heavy, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor.

Points to remember

  1. The second tables“And I will write on the tables the words that were in the first tables which thou brakest” 10:2The same words written again, and laid up in the ark; the law was kept, and covered.
  2. The Levites“the LORD separated the tribe of Levi, to bear the ark of the covenant of the LORD” 10:8No land for Levi; the LORD himself is their inheritance.
  3. The requirement“to fear the LORD thy God, to walk in all his ways, and to love him, and to serve the LORD thy God with all thy heart and with all thy soul” 10:12To fear him, walk in his ways, love him and serve him with all the heart, and keep his commandments for our good.
  4. The heart“Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart, and be no more stiffnecked” 10:16Circumcise it; the LORD himself promises to do it (30:6), and Paul says it is done in Christ.
  5. The LORD“He doth execute the judgment of the fatherless and widow, and loveth the stranger, in giving him food and raiment” 10:18God of gods, he regardeth not persons, and looks after the fatherless, the widow and the stranger.
  6. The stars“now the LORD thy God hath made thee as the stars of heaven for multitude” 10:22Seventy souls, and now the stars for multitude: the LORD keeps what he swears.

Question 1 of 6

The second tables

What did the LORD tell Moses to make and bring, and where did the tables go?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Two tables of stone, hewed like the first, and an ark of wood. The LORD wrote on them the ten commandments, according to the first writing, and Moses put them in the ark.

Question 2 of 6

The Levites

Why did the LORD separate the tribe of Levi, and what was their inheritance?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To bear the ark of the covenant, to stand before the LORD and minister to him, and to bless in his name. Their inheritance was the LORD himself, and no part of the land.

Question 3 of 6

The requirement

What doth the LORD thy God require of thee?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To fear the LORD, to walk in all his ways, to love him, and to serve him with all thy heart and with all thy soul, keeping his commandments and statutes, for thy good.

Question 4 of 6

The heart

What does Moses tell Israel to do to the foreskin of their heart?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Circumcise it, and be no more stiffnecked. Moses later says the LORD himself will circumcise the heart (30:6), and Paul says true circumcision is “that of the heart, in the spirit” (Romans 2:29).

Question 5 of 6

The LORD

What kind of God is the LORD, and whom does he love?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD is God of gods and Lord of lords, a great God, who regardeth not persons nor taketh reward. He executes the judgment of the fatherless and widow, and loveth the stranger, so Israel must love the stranger too.

Question 6 of 6

The stars

How many went down into Egypt, and what has the LORD made them now?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Threescore and ten persons, seventy, went down into Egypt; and now the LORD has made them as the stars of heaven for multitude.
