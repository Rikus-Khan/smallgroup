Proverbs 2

Dig for it

Wisdom is worth searching for like buried treasure, and the man who finds it is guarded from the roads that kill.

Part one

If you'll search

Verses 1–9

Nobody trips over buried treasure. You have to dig.

If… if… if… then

Count the “ifs” in verses 1 to 4. Each one goes deeper: from hearing, to wanting, to crying out, to digging like a man who knows there's silver down there.

Think about how hard a man works to get what he craves. Most of us know exactly how far we've gone for a high or a dollar. God says: put that kind of effort here.

Where the treasure is buried

Proverbs 2:4

If thou seekest her as silver, and searchest for her as for hid treasures;

Colossians 2:3

In whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.

Colossians is talking about Christ. Every treasure of wisdom is hidden in Him. So when you dig into Proverbs, you're really digging toward Jesus.

“Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto treasure hid in a field; the which when a man hath found, he hideth, and for joy thereof goeth and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field.”

Matthew 13:44

You dig. God gives.

Verses 1–4

Your part

Listen, want it, ask for it, keep looking. Show up every day.

Verses 5–6

God's part

He gives it, “out of his mouth.” You can't earn it or work it up. It's a gift.

“If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.”

James 1:5

“Upbraideth not” means He won't throw your past in your face for asking. Ask anyway.

A shield up close

A buckler was a small round shield, held in the fist and moved fast to catch a blow. God doesn't protect from far away. He's that close.

“His saints” means His faithful, loyal people. Not super-Christians: ordinary men who belong to Him and keep walking with Him.

And God “layeth up” wisdom ahead of time, like a father saving for his son.

Part two

What wisdom guards you from

Verses 10–19

Two voices will try to pull you off the road: a man and a woman.

When what's right starts to taste good.

Verse 10 is the turning point. Wisdom doesn't just sit in your head. It enters into thine heart, and one day you notice you actually like it.

That's real change. Not white-knuckling it forever, but God changing what you want. The man whose soul enjoys wisdom has guards on duty: discretion and understanding (v. 11).

How to spot him

Froward means twisted, bent, turned the wrong way. Here are his warning signs:

  • !
    “speaketh froward things”His talk is twisted: lies, angles, always a hustle. (v. 12)
  • !
    “leave the paths of uprightness”He used to know better, and walked away from it. (v. 13)
  • !
    “rejoice to do evil”He doesn't just fall into it. He enjoys it. (v. 14)
  • !
    “delight in the frowardness of the wicked”He loves the stories, cheers when someone else gets over. (v. 14)

You'll usually hear him before you see what he does. Listen to how a man talks, and what he laughs at.

Who is “the strange woman”?

“Strange” here means outside the bounds: a woman who isn't your wife. Verse 17 says she has walked out on “the guide of her youth” (her husband) and broken the marriage covenant she made before God.

Her weapon is flattery: smooth words that tell a man exactly what he wants to hear. Today it might be a stranger, an old flame, or a screen at two in the morning. The pull is the same.

Proverbs comes back to her again and again (chapters 5, 6, 7). That's not because men are hopeless. It's because the father knows his son, and he loves him enough to be blunt.

The house on the slope

From outside it looks warm. But it sits on a slope that runs down to death.

“None that go unto her return again” is a blunt way to say this road has no easy exit. Marriages, families, and health get wrecked on it.

But no easy exit isn't no way back. The prodigal's father ran to meet him (Luke 15:20).

If that's your story

Paul wrote a list of sins to the church in Corinth, sexual sin and drunkenness among them. Then he wrote this:

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

“Were.” Past tense. Jesus washes what you can't scrub off yourself. The warning in Proverbs 2 is there to keep you off that road, and the cross is there for the man who has already been down it.

Part three

The way of good men

Verses 20–22

Roots that hold, and roots that get pulled.

Rooted, or rooted out

“The land” was God's gift to Israel. To dwell in the land meant a settled, blessed life with Him.

The wicked may look big for a while, but a tree with no roots gets pulled up.

That's how life generally goes, not a promise that good men never suffer. The full answer comes in eternity: everyone in Christ stays with God forever.

Points to remember

  1. Seeking God“Searchest for her as for hid treasures” 2:4Put the effort you once gave your cravings into seeking God's wisdom.
  2. Who God is“For the LORD giveth wisdom” 2:6You dig, but God gives. Wisdom is a gift, and all its treasure is in Christ.
  3. God's protection“Discretion shall preserve thee” 2:11Wisdom in the heart stands guard: against the twisted man and the strange woman.
  4. Bad company“The man that speaketh froward things” 2:12Listen to how a man talks and what he enjoys. Twisted talk comes before twisted ways.
  5. Sexual sin“Her house inclineth unto death” 2:18It looks warm, but it slopes to death. Jesus washes the man who's been there.
  6. The end of the road“The upright shall dwell in the land” 2:21The upright put down roots and stay. The wicked get pulled up.

Question 1 of 6

Seeking God

How hard should a man look for wisdom?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Like a man digging for silver: listening, wanting it, crying out for it, and not quitting. Think how hard we once chased what we craved. God asks for that kind of effort, aimed at Him.

Question 2 of 6

Who God is

Where does wisdom really come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“In whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.”

Colossians 2:3

Answer

From the LORD. He gives it out of His own mouth, through His word. We search, but He gives. And every treasure of wisdom is found in Jesus Christ.

Question 3 of 6

God's protection

What does wisdom protect you from?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

From the evil man with his twisted talk, and from the strange woman with her flattering words. When wisdom gets into your heart, discretion and understanding stand guard over you.

Question 4 of 6

Bad company

How do you spot a man who will lead you wrong?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Listen to his mouth and watch what he enjoys. His talk is twisted, he's walked away from what's right, and he gets a kick out of evil. Crooked words lead to crooked roads.

Question 5 of 6

Sexual sin

Where does the strange woman's road really go?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

Downhill to death. It starts with flattering words and a warm-looking house, but there's no easy way off that road. Stay off it. And if you've been down it, Jesus can wash you clean.

Question 6 of 6

The end of the road

What happens to the upright and to the wicked in the end?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The upright put down roots and stay. The wicked are cut off and pulled up. Life doesn't always look fair along the way, but God has the last word, and those who belong to Him remain.
