Numbers 26

Counted again in the plains of Moab

Forty years on, “after the plague”, the LORD says: count them again. A new generation stands on the list, and of the men counted at Sinai there is “not left a man of them, save Caleb the son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the son of Nun.”

Part one

“Take the sum of the people”

Verses 1–4

After the plague, a second numbering.

Why count them again?

  1. Sinai603,550 numbered1:1, 1:46
  2. Kadesh“Your carcases shall fall in this wilderness”14:29
  3. Aaron diesin the fortieth year33:38
  4. The plaguetwenty and four thousand25:9
  5. Moabnumbered again26:51

The LORD gives two reasons in this chapter. They are counted as “all that are able to go to war in Israel” (verse 2), and “unto these the land shall be divided for an inheritance according to the number of names” (verse 53). The war and the land are both just ahead.

An old word: a sum is a total. “Take the sum” means count them all up. Moses does it with Eleazar the priest, now that Aaron has died, in the fortieth year (33:38): the last count of the forty years.

Part two

Tribe by tribe

Verses 5–50

Twelve tribes, a few names that stop us, and one verse that should.

“The children of Korah died not”

  • famouswell known; “men of renown” (16:2)
  • strovequarrelled and fought against
  • a signa warning set up for everyone to see
  • Notwithstandingnevertheless, even so

Numbers 16 told how Korah, Dathan and Abiram went down alive, with “all the men that appertained unto Korah” (16:32). Here, forty years on, is one small verse that chapter doesn't say: “the children of Korah died not.” His line goes on among the Levites: “the family of the Korathites” is in the list at verse 58.

“The soul that sinneth, it shall die. The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son: the righteousness of the righteous shall be upon him, and the wickedness of the wicked shall be upon him.”

Ezekiel 18:20

The text doesn't say why they were spared, and we won't guess. But Psalm 84 is one of the psalms headed “for the sons of Korah”, and it says: “I had rather be a doorkeeper in the house of my God, than to dwell in the tents of wickedness.” A father's rebellion was not the end of his sons' story. If your father's story was a ruin, it is not yours to finish.

Two names God did not erase

“The sons of Judah were Er and Onan: and Er and Onan died in the land of Canaan.” They have no families on this list, only the plain note of how they ended.

“And Er, Judah’s firstborn, was wicked in the sight of the LORD; and the LORD slew him.”

Genesis 38:7

The list is honest. Dathan and Abiram stand in it with their sin written beside them. Er and Onan stand in it with their end. The Bible does not tidy its record, even for the tribe Christ came from. And Judah is still the largest tribe, 76,500 men. God's work does not need a spotless family tree.

A census counts men. Yet Moses stops at verse 33 to write down five women by name. Their story is the next chapter. A warning about names: Noah here is a woman, a daughter of Zelophehad, and the Shechem of verse 31 is a son of Gilead, not the city. Look at who is meant, not only at the name.

A list of names, and why it is here

Dozens of names, most of them found nowhere else in the Bible. Why does God have them written down?

“He telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names.”

Psalm 147:4

“To him the porter openeth; and the sheep hear his voice: and he calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out.”

John 10:3

He knows every man by name: the famous ones, the ones who fell, the ones who stood, and a daughter of Asher called Sarah. When you feel like a number in a crowd, a name nobody knows, this chapter is for you. You are on the list. He knows yours.

603,550 at Sinai, 601,730 in Moab: 1,820 fewer, after forty years in which a whole generation fell. Seven tribes grew, five shrank. Manasseh grew most, by 20,500. Simeon fell most, by 37,100; the text doesn't say why (see chapter 25).

By number, and by lot

By number

“To many thou shalt give the more inheritance, and to few thou shalt give the less” (verse 54). A bigger tribe gets a bigger share.

By lot

“The land shall be divided by lot” (verse 55). The lot decides where: “every man’s inheritance shall be in the place where his lot falleth” (Numbers 33:54).

Two things God settled in advance: no tribe is overlooked, and no tribe picks the best ground for itself. Every portion is given, not grabbed. An inheritance is what a father leaves his family, and none of these men earned his.

Moses, Aaron and Miriam are one family. So is Korah, who “rose up before Moses” (16:2): he is “the son of Izhar, the son of Kohath, the son of Levi” (16:1). Even in the house that served the tabernacle, a man could fall through pride. Nadab and Abihu “died, when they offered strange fire before the LORD” (verse 61).

No inheritance, and a better one

The Levites were numbered apart, “all males from a month old and upward”: 23,000 here, and 22,000 at the first count (3:39). They get no share of the land. The LORD had told Aaron why:

“And the LORD spake unto Aaron, Thou shalt have no inheritance in their land, neither shalt thou have any part among them: I am thy part and thine inheritance among the children of Israel.”

Numbers 18:20

Every other tribe was counted for the land. The Levites were counted for God. They gave up a field and got the LORD himself. A man in recovery knows what it is to give things up. “I am thy part” is the best trade in the book.

“Not left a man of them”

603,550men numbered at Sinai (1:46)
2left: Caleb and Joshua (26:65)

“Doubtless ye shall not come into the land, concerning which I sware to make you dwell therein, save Caleb the son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the son of Nun. But your little ones, which ye said should be a prey, them will I bring in, and they shall know the land which ye have despised.”

Numbers 14:30–31

Verse 65 is that sentence, carried out to the last man. The LORD said it at Kadesh, and he did it to the letter. And he kept the other half of the word too, the half about the children. These are those children, standing in the plains of Moab with the land across the river.

“But with many of them God was not well pleased: for they were overthrown in the wilderness. Now these things were our examples, to the intent we should not lust after evil things, as they also lusted.”

1 Corinthians 10:5–6

Why those two

“Save Caleb the son of Jephunneh the Kenezite, and Joshua the son of Nun: for they have wholly followed the LORD.”

Numbers 32:12

The two who remained were the two who “wholly followed the LORD”. Their reward was not to skip the wilderness: they walked every one of the forty years. It was to come out the other side. Caleb, forty-five years on, said:

“And now, behold, the LORD hath kept me alive, as he said, these forty and five years, even since the LORD spake this word unto Moses, while the children of Israel wandered in the wilderness: and now, lo, I am this day fourscore and five years old.”

Joshua 14:10

“The LORD hath kept me alive.” You may have thrown away years. These two lost forty years to other men's unbelief, and the LORD kept them for the land. He is able to keep you.

Real world: you are on the list

  • SinA whole generation fell because they would not trust God when it counted. It starts small: one grumble, one look back toward Egypt.
  • RegretEvery man in the first count lost the land. Many of us feel we have lost years too.
  • GraceTheir children were counted, and the promise stood. God did not scrap his plan. In Christ he has done more: he has paid for the sin itself.
  • HopeKorah's sons lived. Judah, with Er and Onan on the record, is still the largest tribe. Your father's story, or your own old one, is not the last word.
  • TodayTell God, and a brother in Christ, that you want to follow him wholly today. Then do the next small, right thing: show up where your brothers are.

Question 3 of 6

The land by lot

How was the land to be divided among the tribes?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By number, so a larger tribe received a larger inheritance, and by lot, so the place was not chosen by men.

Question 5 of 6

The Levites

Why were the Levites not numbered among the children of Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD spake unto Aaron, Thou shalt have no inheritance in their land, neither shalt thou have any part among them: I am thy part and thine inheritance among the children of Israel.”

Numbers 18:20

Answer

Because there was no inheritance given them: the LORD himself was their part and their inheritance.
