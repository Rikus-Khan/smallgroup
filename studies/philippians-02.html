Part one

Of one mind

Verses 1–4

Paul begs them for one thing, and the way to get it is the opposite of what we'd expect.

Since you have all this

  • Consolation in ChristHe has comforted you.
  • Comfort of loveHis love has held you up.
  • Fellowship of the SpiritThe Spirit lives in every one of you.
  • Bowels and merciesOld English for a deep, gut-level kindness.
  • So“Fulfil ye my joy”: get along.

Paul isn't saying “try harder to be nice.” He's saying: look at what you've already been given, and let it show. A church that has all the same Christ and the same Spirit has no business splitting over pride.

What lowliness is, and isn't

  • not “I'm worthless”Shame keeps your eyes on yourself, just as pride does. Christ died for you, so you're worth something to Him.
  • not “be a doormat”Paul is about to say we should look at others' needs. He isn't saying let anybody walk on you.
  • but “I'm not the center”Vainglory is empty glory, wanting to be seen as somebody. Lowliness is the other road: seeing people.

One thing to do today: before you talk about your own day, ask another man about his, and listen to the answer. “Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others.”

Part two

The mind of Christ

Verses 5–11

Many Bible teachers think these verses were an early Christian hymn. Either way, they're the heart of the letter.

ἐκένωσεν said ekenōsen “emptied himself”

The King James has “made himself of no reputation”. The Greek word means He emptied Himself, poured out His privileges. He didn't stop being God. He set aside the glory and the right to be served, and took on a servant's life.

“Thought it not robbery to be equal with God” means He didn't clutch at it, like a thief with something he's grabbed. The one with the most right to be served chose to serve. That's “the mind” Paul wants in us.

Down, then up

  1. ↓ in the form of God (v. 6)
  2. ↓ no reputation (v. 7)
  3. ↓ a servant (v. 7)
  4. ↓ a man (v. 7–8)
  5. ↓ obedient unto death (v. 8)
  6. ✝ the cross (v. 8)
  7. ↑ highly exalted (v. 9)
  8. ↑ the name above every name (v. 9)
  9. ↑ every knee shall bow (v. 10)
  10. ↑ Jesus Christ is Lord (v. 11)

Six steps down, and the bottom one is a cross: the death of a slave and a criminal. Then “Wherefore”, which means because of this, God lifted Him higher than where He started.

This is the shape of the whole gospel, and the shape Paul wants for us: down to serve, and God does the lifting.

Every knee

“I have sworn by myself, the word is gone out of my mouth in righteousness, and shall not return, That unto me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear.”

Isaiah 45:23

In Isaiah, God says that about Himself, and Paul gives it to Jesus. That's a quiet way of saying Jesus is God. One day everyone will bow, those who loved Him and those who didn't. The wonderful thing is that you can bow now, gladly, and be saved:

“That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.”

Romans 10:9

Down is the way up

“For whosoever exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.”

Luke 14:11

Jesus didn't climb to the top by pushing past people. He went down, and God raised Him. You don't have to scrape for position, or prove you're somebody. Your standing doesn't depend on being noticed. It depends on Him.

“If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another’s feet. For I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you.”

John 13:14–15

Here's the real-world part for today: do one lowly thing for someone and don't mention it. Carry a load, take out the trash, make the coffee, give a ride. That's what the mind of Christ looks like in clothes.

Part three

Work out, because He works in

Verses 12–18

The most misunderstood sentence in the letter, and one of the most freeing.

Work out, not work for

Already yoursSalvationA gift, not wages. Nobody earns it (Ephesians 2:8–9).
→
God works in you“to will and to do”He gives the want-to, and the power.
→
You work it outShow up. Say no. Make the call.Living out what's already true.

Work out doesn't mean work for. Think of a man handed the deed to a farm. It's his; he didn't earn it. Now he has to farm it. Paul says to the Philippians, “ye have always obeyed”: they're already saved, and now they're to live it.

He gives the want-to

The sentence has two halves, and God does both: “to will” (the wanting) and “to do” (the doing). Many of us tried the old way: promise, grit our teeth, fail. Paul says God is working inside you, on what you want and on what you can do.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.”

Ephesians 2:8–10

“Fear and trembling” isn't being scared that God will throw you out. It's the serious, humble care of a man who knows what he's been given. Don't treat it lightly. You've fallen? Take it to Him, and get up and walk again, today.

No grumbling

Do all thingswithout murmurings and disputings,
→
so you'll beblameless and harmless, the sons of God.

Murmurings means grumbling under your breath. Disputings means arguing. Israel did both all through the wilderness. The new man has a job, a boss, a landlord, a probation officer and a family. It's easy to complain about every one.

A man who does his work without the constant moan stands out. Harmless means pure, with no mixture of anger or spite. Try it for one shift, today.

Lights in the dark

Paul doesn't say go hide from a world that's “crooked and perverse.” He says that's exactly where lights are needed. Stars only show up in the dark.

“Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid.”

Matthew 5:14

“Holding forth the word of life” can also mean holding it out, offering it to people. Your light isn't a speech. It's an honest day's work, a kept promise, a sober life.

“If I be offered… I joy, and rejoice with you all.”

The word offered means poured out, like the wine poured on an altar beside a sacrifice. Paul's picture: your faith is the sacrifice, and if his life gets poured out beside it, he's glad.

He might die in that Roman house, and he writes about joy. And he invites them to “joy, and rejoice with me.” The Christian life isn't a mood, and it isn't an act. It's gladness that can sit in a prison cell.

Part four

Two men who served

Verses 19–30

Paul has talked about the mind of Christ. Now he points at two real men who have it.

Timothy

Most people (v. 21)

“All seek their own”

Even among Paul's co-workers, most had their own plans. Paul says it plainly. Self comes first even in good company.

Timothy (v. 20, 22)

“Will naturally care for your state”

Tested over years. He joined Paul at Lystra on the trip that planted Philippi (Acts 16:1–3) and stuck with him “as a son with the father.”

This is how a man earns trust: not by one big moment, but by years of showing up. If you've burned some trust, the road back is the same: be there, again and again.

Epaphroditus's two trips

The church sent him to Paul with their gift, and to stay and help. That's a long road, many weeks, more than 700 miles, with sea in the middle.

He fell sick in Rome, nearly died, and got better. Now Paul sends him home, carrying this letter.

What Paul calls him

  • My brotherfamily, not staff
  • Companion in labourworked next to Paul
  • Fellow soldiershared the fight
  • Your messengersent by the church
  • Ministered to my wantslooked after Paul himself

Five titles in one verse. Paul doesn't brag about his own gifts. He piles up honor on a man who carried a gift and a friend's care across the sea. Real greatness in the church sounds like this.

Real men, real limits

Back in PhilippiWe heard Epaphroditus is sick.

Epaphroditus, in Rome…and I'm worried they're worried. “Full of heaviness” (v. 26).

He nearly died. The honest words are “sick nigh unto death”, and “God had mercy on him.” Paul doesn't blame him, or call it weak faith. A man can be worn out, sick, homesick and low, and still be doing the work of Christ.

Then notice what Paul tells the church to do: “Receive him therefore in the Lord with all gladness.” Not a lecture. Gladness. If a man comes back worn down, whether from illness, a rough road or a fall, that's how he should be met. And if you're that man today, that's how Christ meets you.

Points to remember

  1. Lowliness“In lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves” 2:3Don't make yourself the center. Look at other men's needs, too.
  2. The mind of Christ“He humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross” 2:8He went all the way down to the cross for us.
  3. Lifted up“Every knee should bow … Jesus Christ is Lord” 2:10–11God raised Him higher than all. Bow to Him now, gladly.
  4. Work out“Work out your own salvation … it is God which worketh in you” 2:12–13Work out, not work for. God gives the want-to and the power.
  5. Lights“Ye shine as lights in the world” 2:15A crooked world is where lights are needed. Live it, quietly.
  6. Real men“Receive him therefore in the Lord with all gladness” 2:29Worn-out men still serve, and they're welcomed back with gladness.

Question 1 of 6

Lowliness

What did Paul say should replace strife and vainglory?

What verses tell us about this?

“Likewise, ye younger, submit yourselves unto the elder. Yea, all of you be subject one to another, and be clothed with humility: for God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble.”

1 Peter 5:5

Answer

Lowliness of mind: counting others as better than ourselves and looking at their needs, not only our own. God gives grace to the humble.

Question 2 of 6

The mind of Christ

How far down did Jesus go?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

From the form of God to the form of a servant, to a man, and then to death on a cross. He gave up the right to be served, and He did it for us.

Question 3 of 6

Lifted up

What did God do for Jesus afterward, and what will every knee do?

What verses tell us about this?

“That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.”

Romans 10:9

Answer

God highly exalted Him and gave Him the name above every name. Every knee will bow and every tongue will confess that Jesus Christ is Lord. Bow now, and be saved.

Question 4 of 6

Work out

What does it mean to work out your salvation, and who is working in you?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

It means living out the salvation you've already been given, not working to earn it. God is the one working in you, giving both the wanting and the doing.

Question 5 of 6

Lights

What kind of world do believers shine in, and how?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A crooked and perverse world. Believers shine by doing everything without grumbling or arguing, living blameless and pure, and holding out the word of life.

Question 6 of 6

Real men

What did Paul say about Epaphroditus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That he was a brother, a fellow worker and soldier, and the church's messenger. He nearly died for the work of Christ, and God had mercy on him. Paul asks the church to welcome him with gladness and honor.
