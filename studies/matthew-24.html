Matthew 24

Watch therefore

On the Mount of Olives, Jesus tells His disciples the temple will fall, warns them not to be fooled, promises He will come again in glory, and tells them to be ready.

Where we are today

Jesus has spent the day teaching in the temple courts (21:23–23:39). Now He walks out, down across the Kidron valley, and up the Mount of Olives on the other side.

From that slope you look straight back at the temple. Mark says He sat “over against the temple,” and Peter, James, John and Andrew asked Him their questions there (Mark 13:3).

What He told them that evening fills chapters 24 and 25. It is often called the Olivet Discourse: the talk on the Mount of Olives.

The last week

  1. Sundaythe King rides in21:1–11
  2. the next daysteaching in the temple21:23–23:39
  3. that eveningon the Mount of Oliveschapters 24–25
  4. Thursday nightthe Last Supperchapter 26

This is about AD 30, in the last week before the cross. Many place this talk on the Tuesday. Two days later He would be arrested (26:2).

Jesus knows what is just ahead of Him. And with the cross only days away, He spends the evening getting His men ready for the long road after it.

Part one

Not one stone

Verses 1–14

The disciples are admiring the buildings. Jesus says they are coming down.

The pride of the nation

46 yearsin building, and still not finished (John 2:20)
hundreds of tonsone stone still in its western wall
about 40 yearsuntil it fell, in AD 70

King Herod rebuilt the temple on a grand scale. Its white stone and gold shone over the whole city. Nobody thought it could fall.

About forty years later, Roman armies under Titus burned Jerusalem and pulled the temple down, just as Jesus said. What men call permanent isn't. His word is.

Three questions in one breath

“When shall these things be?”the templeWhen will the temple fall?
“The sign of thy coming?”His returnHis coming: His arrival as King, in person.
“The end of the world?”the endThe world here means the age: the close of this present age.

The disciples likely thought these would all happen at once. Jesus answers all three, woven together, and that is why careful readers don't all see this chapter the same way.

How good teachers read it

This is a hard chapter, and Bible-believing men who love the Lord read it in different ways. Here are the main ones, fairly:

  1. Mostly fulfilledMuch of it came true when Rome destroyed Jerusalem in AD 70. The last part points to His final coming.
  2. Mostly still to comeMost of it describes a time of great trouble just before He returns. Many Baptists read it this way.
  3. Near and farLike the old prophets, Jesus speaks of AD 70 and the end together. The near event is a preview of the far one.

We won't settle that today. But every one of these agrees on what matters most: Jesus is coming back, in person and in glory. No man knows when. And His people must be ready. That's where this chapter keeps pointing.

Not a timetable. A warning.

They asked when. His first words were about who: men who will come in His name, saying “I am Christ.” He warns about deceivers four times in this chapter (vv. 4–5, 11, 24, 26).

About AD 132, a man named Simon bar Kokhba was hailed as the Messiah and led a revolt that ended in slaughter. Men have made the same claim ever since, and still do.

The best guard against a counterfeit is to know the real thing. A man who knows his Bible and knows his Lord is hard to fool.

Birth pains

ὠδίν said ōdin the pains of a woman in labour

Birth pains hurt, they come again and again, and they get stronger. But they point to a birth, not a death. Wars, famines and earthquakes are not a sign that God has lost control. They are the world groaning toward the day He makes it new.

So: “see that ye be not troubled.” Bad news is not a reason for a believer to panic. “The end is not yet.”

When love goes cold

wheniniquity shall abound
→
thenthe love of many shall wax cold

“Wax” means grow. Hatred from outside, betrayal from inside, and sin everywhere: it chills a man's heart until he doesn't care anymore. Most of us know what that cold feels like.

“He that shall endure unto the end” isn't a man who saves himself by grit. The ones who truly belong to Jesus keep going because He keeps them:

“Who are kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation ready to be revealed in the last time.”

1 Peter 1:5

The job in the meantime

Before the end, the good news goes out to every nation. A handful of men on a hillside heard that promise. Today the gospel is preached on every continent, in thousands of languages.

So the waiting time isn't empty time. Every man who has been found by Jesus has someone he can tell. That is part of the “witness unto all nations,” too.

Part two

Flee to the mountains

Verses 15–28

A warning sign in the holy place, and the days of the greatest trouble.

The abomination of desolation

An abomination is something God hates; here, an idol or a defiling act in His holy place. Desolation is what it brings: ruin, emptiness. Jesus points to Daniel:

“And arms shall stand on his part, and they shall pollute the sanctuary of strength, and shall take away the daily sacrifice, and they shall place the abomination that maketh desolate.”

Daniel 11:31

  2. AD 70Rome's armies take the temple and destroy itLuke 21:20
  3. still to come?many look for a final one before Jesus returns2 Thessalonians 2:3–4

Get out, and don't look back

Luke's account makes the sign plain for that first generation:

“And when ye shall see Jerusalem compassed with armies, then know that the desolation thereof is nigh.”

Luke 21:20

The church historian Eusebius writes that the believers in Jerusalem, warned beforehand, fled to Pella, across the Jordan, before the city fell.

Roofs were flat, with outside stairs. A man on his housetop was to go straight down and run.

Mercy inside the trouble

Pray it's not

winter, or the sabbath

Winter brought cold rain, mud and flooded streams. On the sabbath, town gates could be shut and travel was frowned on. Even the timing of an escape mattered to Jesus.

God's limit

“those days shall be shortened”

Tribulation means pressure, crushing trouble. But God sets a limit on it. “The elect” are His chosen people, all who belong to Him through faith, and He doesn't forget them in it.

Jesus doesn't promise His people an easy road. He promises He'll be Lord over every day of it, even the worst ones.

Don't go looking

Someone says“Lo, here is Christ!” believe it not

Someone else says“Behold, he is in the desert!” go not forth

And another“He is in the secret chambers!” believe it not

Jesus“Behold, I have told you before.”

Signs and wonders don't prove a man is from God. Test every claim by what Jesus has already said.

Like lightning

Nobody needs a news report to know lightning has struck. The whole sky lights up at once. That is how His coming will be: sudden, public, and seen by all. Not in a back room. Not in the desert.

Verse 28 is a saying from everyday life: where a dead body lies, the birds of prey gather. You can't miss it. When judgment comes, it will be just as plain.

Part three

Coming in the clouds

Verses 29–35

The lights of the sky go out, and every eye looks up.

The Son of man in the clouds

Daniel 7:13

I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him.

Acts 1:11

Which also said, Ye men of Galilee, why stand ye gazing up into heaven? this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven.

“Son of man,” Jesus' favorite name for Himself, comes from Daniel. The sun going dark is the prophets' language for the day God steps in (Isaiah 13:10).

Not one of His will be left behind or lost.

The trumpet called Israel together for feasts and for battle. “The four winds” means north, south, east and west: everywhere. From every corner of the earth, the King gathers His own.

For the men who mourn in verse 30, His coming is terrible. For those who are His, it is the best day there will ever be. Same day. What makes the difference is whether you know Him.

Learn from the fig tree

In Israel most trees stay green all winter, but the fig stands bare. When its branches go soft and its leaves come out, every farmer knows summer is close.

Jesus doesn't give a date. He gives a way of paying attention. The things He described are like leaves on the branch: they tell us the summer is coming, so live like it.

“This generation”

Another place where good teachers differ. The main readings:

The men standing thereAD 70They lived to see the temple fall, about forty years later.
The ones who see the signsthe last oneWhatever generation sees these things begin will see them finished.
This kind of peoplethe nationThe word can mean a race or kind: Israel will still be here at the end.

Whichever is right, verse 35 is the rock to stand on: “my words shall not pass away.”

Part four

Watch therefore

Verses 36–51

Nobody knows the hour. So be the kind of man who is ready at any hour.

Every date has been wrong

  • 1844William Miller's followers sold farms to wait for Him
  • 1988a book gave “88 reasons” He'd come that year
  • 2011a radio preacher put the day on billboards
  • “knoweth no man”not the angels, but the Father only

Every man who has set a date has been wrong, and anyone who sets one now is too. Jesus didn't give us a calendar. He gave us a command: be ready.

The days of Noe

Noe is Noah, spelled the Greek way.

What they were doing

ordinary life

Eating, drinking, getting married. None of that is wrong. Life just went on as if it always would.

What they missed

“knew not”

Noah built for years in plain sight, and preached. They didn't think it concerned them, until the flood came.

The danger isn't a world that's too busy sinning. It's a man too busy living to get ready.

Taken, and left

Two men at the same work, two women at the same mill (a hand mill of two round stones for grinding grain). Side by side, and parted in a moment. Teachers read “taken” two ways:

Taken in judgment

like the flood

The flood “took them all away” (v. 39). The one taken is swept off; the one left is spared.

Taken to the Lord

caught up

Believers “caught up… to meet the Lord in the air” (1 Thessalonians 4:17), while others are left behind.

Either way, the point is the same: the man beside you may be ready when you are not. Each man answers for himself.

Any watch of the night

  1. eveneveningabout 6–9
  2. midnightdeep nightabout 9–12
  3. cockcrowingsmall hoursabout 12–3
  4. morningbefore dawnabout 3–6

“Watch ye therefore: for ye know not when the master of the house cometh, at even, or at midnight, or at the cockcrowing, or in the morning:”

Mark 13:35

The goodman is the man of the house. “Broken up” means dug through: many walls were mud brick, and a thief could dig his way in. A thief doesn't send word ahead. If the owner had known which watch, he'd have stayed up. He didn't, so the only safe man is the one who is ready in every watch.

What does “watch” look like?

Not standing on a hill staring at the sky. Not reading the news for clues. It's a man living today the way he'd want to be found when Jesus comes.

“Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.”

1 John 3:2

“And every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself, even as he is pure.”

1 John 3:3

For a believer, His coming isn't a threat hanging over us. It's a hope pulling us forward. And Jesus meant this for every one of us: “what I say unto you I say unto all, Watch” (Mark 13:37).

Two servants, one house

The faithful and wise servant

“so doing”

He keeps feeding the household, “meat in due season”: food at the right time. Ordinary work, done well, every day. The lord finds him at it, and puts him over everything.

The evil servant

“My lord delayeth”

He decides there's time. So he beats the other servants and drinks with the drunks. The lord comes on a day he isn't looking, and he loses everything.

Watching isn't a feeling. It's what your hands are doing when He walks in.

“There's still time”

Every man in recovery knows that voice. I'll quit tomorrow. I'll get right with God later. The evil servant believed it, and it took him straight back to the bottle and the fist.

“And take heed to yourselves, lest at any time your hearts be overcharged with surfeiting, and drunkenness, and cares of this life, and so that day come upon you unawares.”

Luke 21:34

If the Lord seems slow, it isn't so we can play. It's mercy: He is “not willing that any should perish” (2 Peter 3:9). The extra time is for coming home, today.

“Hypocrites” were actors behind a mask. This servant wore his master's name but never had his master's heart. Jesus' words are severe because the danger is real.

Points to remember

  1. Deception“Take heed that no man deceive you” 24:4Jesus' first warning is about false christs and false prophets. Know the real thing.
  2. Troubles“See that ye be not troubled… the end is not yet” 24:6Bad news is birth pains, not a reason to panic. God is still Lord.
  3. Endurance“He that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved” 24:13When love grows cold all around, those who are His keep going, kept by His power.
  4. His coming“As the lightning cometh out of the east” 24:27He will come in person, in the clouds, in glory, and every eye will see it.
  5. The hour“Of that day and hour knoweth no man” 24:36No one knows when, and no one should set a date. Only the Father knows.
  6. Ready“Blessed is that servant, whom his lord when he cometh shall find so doing” 24:46Be found doing His work today, not putting things off till later.

Question 1 of 6

Deception

What did Jesus warn about first, and more than anything else?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Being deceived. Before He said anything about wars or signs, He warned that many would come in His name, claiming to be Christ, and that false prophets would even show signs and wonders. The guard against a counterfeit is knowing Jesus and His words.

Question 2 of 6

Troubles

How should we take the news of wars and troubles?

What verses tell us about this?

“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”

Psalm 46:1

Answer

Without panic. Jesus said these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet. They are the beginning of sorrows, like birth pains. They hurt, but they point to a new day, and God is still on His throne.

Question 3 of 6

Endurance

What happens to the love of many, and what does Jesus call us to do?

What verses tell us about this?

“Who are kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation ready to be revealed in the last time.”

1 Peter 1:5

Answer

When sin abounds, the love of many grows cold. Jesus calls us to endure to the end. Not by our own grit: those who truly belong to Him keep going because God keeps them by His power.

Question 4 of 6

His coming

How will the coming of the Son of man be seen?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Openly, by everyone, all at once, like lightning across the whole sky. Not in secret, not in a desert or a back room. The same Jesus who went up will come in the clouds with power and great glory.

Question 5 of 6

The hour

Who knows the day and hour of His coming?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No man, not even the angels of heaven, but the Father only. He will come at an hour we don't expect. So nobody should set dates, and everybody should be ready.

Question 6 of 6

Ready

What does a faithful and wise servant do while his lord is away?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He keeps doing the work his lord gave him: caring for the household and feeding them on time. He doesn't tell himself there's plenty of time and go back to his old ways. Watching means being found faithful today.
