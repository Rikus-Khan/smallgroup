2 Thessalonians 3

Keep working, keep doing good

Some in the church had quit their jobs and were living off everyone else. Paul's answer: pray, get to work, don't quit doing good, and treat a straying man as a brother.

Part one

Pray, and be kept

Verses 1–5

The great apostle asks a little church to pray for him.

Let the word run

“Have free course” is one Greek word: run. Paul pictures the gospel as a runner. It had already run from Philippi to Thessalonica to Corinth, “even as it is with you.”

At Corinth, as he writes, men were fighting him (Acts 18:6). And the Lord told him:

“Then spake the Lord to Paul in the night by a vision, Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace: For I am with thee, and no man shall set on thee to hurt thee: for I have much people in this city.”

Acts 18:9–10

Not all men, but the Lord

Verses 2 and 3 side by side: “all men have not faith. But the Lord is faithful.” People let you down. He won't.

Stablish: set firm, like a post in concrete. Keep: guard, like a sentry. Both are His job.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Part two

Work with quietness

Verses 6–12

Some had quit work and were living off the church. Paul doesn't let it slide.

A preacher with calloused hands

His right

v. 9

“Not because we have not power”: power here means the right. A preacher may be paid by the people he serves.

His choice

v. 8

He worked “night and day” so he wouldn't be “chargeable”, a burden, to anyone in a poor, new church.

His reason

v. 9

“An ensample”: an example. He wanted them to see the gospel and an honest day's work in the same man.

“And because he was of the same craft, he abode with them, and wrought: for by their occupation they were tentmakers.”

Acts 18:3

That's Corinth, where he wrote this. Disorderly is a soldier's word: out of step, a man who breaks ranks and skips his duty.

“If any would not work, neither should he eat.”

  • not the man who can't find work yeta record, a gap, no license: he's knocking on doors. That's working.
  • not the sick, the old, the disabledthe early church took care of them (Acts 6:1)
  • but the man who won'twho could work, chooses not to, and lets others carry him

Paul aims at the will, not the circumstances. If you're looking for work today, don't let anyone shame you with this verse. If you're dodging it, let it shake you awake.

Not busy, but busybodies

Verse 11 is a play on words in Greek: “working not at all, but are busybodies.” Not busy at work, but very busy with everyone else's business. A man with nothing to do finds trouble to stir.

Why had they quit? Many Bible teachers think the scare of chapter 2 had something to do with it: if Jesus is coming any day, why bother with work? Paul's answer is that waiting for Christ never means sitting down. It means getting up and working “with quietness.”

“And that ye study to be quiet, and to do your own business, and to work with your own hands, as we commanded you;”

1 Thessalonians 4:11

From taking to giving

the old lifeTaking

Living off others: stealing, hustling, borrowing with no plan to pay.

“Let him that stole steal no more”

the new startWorking

Eating your own bread. A job, even a hard or humble one.

“but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good”

where it leadsGiving

Enough left over to help the next man.

“that he may have to give to him that needeth”

That's Ephesians 4:28, and it's this chapter's road. For a man rebuilding a life, work isn't a side issue. Showing up on time, doing the job right and taking home an honest check is recovery. Today, do your work as if Jesus were your boss, because He is.

Part three

Don't quit, and peace

Verses 13–18

A word for the tired, a word about the stubborn, and Paul's own signature.

“But ye, brethren, be not weary in well doing.”

The faithful ones were tired: tired of carrying the lazy, tired of being mocked, tired of doing right and seeing no change. Paul's word to them is short: don't quit.

“And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not.”

Galatians 6:9

Maybe you're tired today: of the same job, the same meetings, the same fight with the same craving. Sober days add up even when they feel like nothing. The harvest comes “in due season.” Keep doing the next right thing.

Not an enemy, a brother

What to do (v. 14)

“Note that man… have no company with him”

Don't pretend all is well. Step back, so he feels the weight of what he's doing: “that he may be ashamed.”

How to do it (v. 15)

“Admonish him as a brother”

Not as an enemy. The point isn't to throw him away. It's to win him back.

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Good recovery groups know this: tough love with the door left open. If you've been the man people had to step back from, that wasn't the end. It was an invitation to come home.

“With mine own hand”

Paul usually spoke his letters to a helper who wrote them down. At the end he took the pen and signed. That signature was “the token in every epistle”: the mark that the letter was really his.

Remember the trouble in 2:2, “by letter as from us”? Now they can tell. If it doesn't carry Paul's own hand, don't believe it.

“Ye see how large a letter I have written unto you with mine own hand.”

Galatians 6:11

“The Lord of peace himself give you peace always by all means.”

Persecution outside, a scare inside, idle men stirring things up: and Paul's last prayer is for peace. Not peace when everything settles down, but “always”, and “by all means”, in every way and every circumstance. And it's not a feeling he hands out. It's the Lord “himself.”

“Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid.”

John 14:27

Looking back over 2 Thessalonians

The whole letter, in three chapters, written to a scared, suffering young church:

1

Rest for the troubledGod sees, and He will set it rightto you who are troubled rest with us1:7

2

Don't be shakenThat day hasn't come yet, and when it does, Christ winsThat ye be not soon shaken in mind2:2

3

Keep working, keep doing goodPray, work quietly, and don't quitbe not weary in well doing3:13

One thread runs through all three: the Lord Jesus is coming. Because He's coming, the troubled will get rest. Because He hasn't come yet, don't panic. And until He comes, get up and do good.

If you fell yesterday, this letter was written for people like you: shaken, tired, under pressure. “The Lord is faithful.” Take one thing from it today: do your work well, and do one good thing for someone else.

Points to remember

  1. Pray for us“Pray for us, that the word of the Lord may have free course” 3:1Even Paul needed prayer. Ask for it, and pray for those who preach.
  2. Faithful“But the Lord is faithful, who shall stablish you, and keep you from evil” 3:3People let you down. He doesn't. Keeping you is His job.
  3. Example“To make ourselves an ensample unto you to follow us” 3:9The gospel and an honest day's work belong in the same man.
  4. Work“If any would not work, neither should he eat” 3:10Aimed at the man who won't, not the man who can't yet. Work is part of recovery.
  5. Weary“But ye, brethren, be not weary in well doing” 3:13Tired is not the same as done. Keep doing the next right thing.
  6. A brother“Count him not as an enemy, but admonish him as a brother” 3:15Tough love with the door left open.

Question 1 of 6

Pray for us

What did Paul ask the Thessalonians to pray for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That the word of the Lord would run freely and be glorified, as it had been with them, and that Paul and his friends would be delivered from unreasonable and wicked men.

Question 2 of 6

Faithful

What did Paul say the Lord would do for them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The Lord is faithful: He will set them firm and keep them from evil, and direct their hearts into the love of God and the patient waiting for Christ.

Question 3 of 6

Example

What example did Paul set by the way he worked?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He didn't eat anyone's bread for free. He worked night and day so he wouldn't be a burden, even though he had the right to be supported, to give them an example to follow.

Question 4 of 6

Work

What rule did Paul give about work and eating?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

If any would not work, neither should he eat. It's aimed at those who won't work, not those who can't. They were to work quietly and eat their own bread.

Question 5 of 6

Weary

What did Paul tell the faithful brethren not to grow weary in?

What verses tell us about this?

“And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not.”

Galatians 6:9

Answer

In well doing. Don't quit doing good, even when you're tired and see no change. The harvest comes in due season.

Question 6 of 6

A brother

How were they to treat a brother who would not obey?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Note him and keep away from him, so he feels ashamed. But not as an enemy: warn him as a brother, to win him back.
