Exodus 40

The glory filled the tabernacle

On the first day of the second year, Moses raises the tabernacle, piece by piece, as the LORD commanded. Then a cloud covers the tent, the glory of the LORD fills it, and God comes to live among the people he brought out of Egypt.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. The dayWhen was the tabernacle set up?

Part one

“Set up the tabernacle”

Verses 1–16

The LORD tells Moses the day, the order, and the washing and anointing of the priests.

The first day of a new year

  1. first monthThe Passoveryear one, 12:2 and 12:6
  2. third monthMount Sinai19:1
  3. forty daysand a calf of goldchapters 24 to 34
  4. first day, year twoThe tabernacle reared up40:17

Two weeks short of a year after the Passover. “Reared up” means raised, set standing. The LORD had said at the Passover that this month would be “the beginning of months”:

“This month shall be unto you the beginning of months: it shall be the first month of the year to you.”

Exodus 12:2

A people who had a calf of gold in chapter 32 start a new year with the house of God. It is a first day. If you have a first day of your own, a day you got clean or came to Christ, mark it. God picks first days to build on.

Washed, clothed, anointed

  1. washedwith waterverse 12
  2. clothedin the holy garmentsverses 13–14
  3. anointedwith oilverses 13, 15

Hallow and sanctify mean make holy, set apart for God. To anoint is to pour or rub on oil, the sign that a man or a thing is set apart. And look who does what. Aaron does not wash himself, dress himself or anoint himself. Moses does it all, at the LORD's command. A priest is made, he does not make himself.

“But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:”

1 Peter 2:9

The New Testament says this of every believer: a royal priesthood. Washed, clothed and set apart by Another. You did not qualify yourself. You were brought to the door, and washed.

Part two

“So Moses finished the work”

Verses 17–33

The tent goes up, one piece at a time, “as the LORD commanded Moses.”

Raising the tent

  • reared upraised, stood upright
  • socketsthe silver bases, set first, that the boards stood in (38:27)
  • boardsthe gold-covered frames of the walls, and bars to hold them straight
  • pillarsthe posts for the vail and the door
  • spread abroadstretched out, over the top
  • the covering of the tentthe layers of goats' hair, rams' skins and leather on the roof

Notice the order: the foundation first, the walls second, the roof last. And all of it was made by other hands, in the last chapters, to a pattern. Moses did not design one board. He put up what God showed him.

The law, under the lid

Inside the ark

the testimony

The two tables of stone. God's law, the ten commandments we have all broken.

On top of the ark

the mercy seat

A lid of pure gold. Once a year the blood was sprinkled on it, and the LORD met his people there.

The law was not thrown away. It was put in the ark, and then covered. Between a holy God and the broken law stood a lid with blood on it. “Propitiation” in Romans 3:25 is the same idea: the mercy seat.

“Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus: Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God;”

Romans 3:24–25

If you have broken it, and you have, the answer is not to try harder to keep it. The answer is the mercy seat, and the blood on it is Jesus'.

Bread, light and incense

  • the table, northtwelve loaves set in rows before the LORD: bread, always in his presence
  • the candlestick, southseven lamps lit: light in a room with no window
  • the golden altarin front of the vail, where sweet incense burned: smoke that rises
  • the hanging at the doorthe curtain that let the priests in
  • the altar of burnt offeringat the door outside, with the burnt offering and the meat offering
  • meat offeringhere it means a grain offering: fine flour and oil. Meat once meant any food

Every piece is placed “as the LORD commanded Moses.” The bread and the light and the incense are the daily things: food, light and prayer. God's house had all three, and they did not run out.

Inside, one piece at a time

The picture brings them in the way a priest meets them, walking in from the door: bread, light and incense first, then the vail, then the ark. Moses set them up from the other end, the ark first, as the LORD had told him in verse 3.

The court rises, and the work is finished

The laver went between the tent and the altar, and the priests washed there “when they went into the tent of the congregation, and when they came near unto the altar.” Withal means “with it.” Then the court, and the gate. Verse 33: “So Moses finished the work.”

As the LORD commanded

Verse 16 says Moses did “according to all that the LORD commanded him.” Then seven times, from verse 19 to verse 32, the same words come again:

  • verse 16Moses did all
  • verse 19the tent over the tabernacle
  • verse 21the ark and the vail
  • verse 23the bread
  • verse 25the lamps
  • verse 27the incense
  • verse 29the burnt offering
  • verse 32the washing

After the golden calf, God had a people that did it his way, down to the last peg. That is the work of grace: he does not throw away people who failed. He forgives them, and their hearts are stirred to willing work.

Part three

“The glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle”

Verses 34–38

The cloud comes down, and God moves in.

Too holy to enter

The tent is not finished when the last peg goes in. It is finished when God comes. “A cloud covered the tent… and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle.”

And Moses, the man who had spoken to the LORD “face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend” (33:11), could not go in. The glory was too much. The same thing happened at Solomon's temple (1 Kings 8:10–11). (Abode means stayed. The tent of the congregation is the tent of meeting, where the assembly of the people met with God.)

Not able to enter, and now able

This is where the Old Testament leaves a man: standing outside the glory, even a man like Moses. The New Testament picks up exactly there.

“And, behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom; and the earth did quake, and the rocks rent;”

Matthew 27:51

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh; And having an high priest over the house of God; Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.”

Hebrews 10:19–22

When Jesus died, the vail between the people and the holiest of all was torn in two from the top to the bottom, by God, not by men. We may go in “by the blood of Jesus.” The glory that stopped Moses at the door has made a way in, and the way is a man.

A tent that moves

By day the cloud, by night the fire. When it lifted, they packed the tent and followed. When it stayed, they stayed, a day, a month, or a year.

“Or whether it were two days, or a month, or a year, that the cloud tarried upon the tabernacle, remaining thereon, the children of Israel abode in their tents, and journeyed not: but when it was taken up, they journeyed.”

Numbers 9:22

They did not choose the road. They followed. It is the same cloud that led them out of Egypt (13:21), and now it rests on the tent.

Looking back over Exodus

  1. 1–2Slaves“bitter with hard bondage”
  2. 3–12The blood“I will pass over you”
  3. 13–18The seabread, water, a cloud
  4. 19–34Sinaithe law, a calf, mercy
  5. 35–40God among themthe glory filled it

Read it as one story. A people crushed in slavery, and a God who heard them: “they made their lives bitter with hard bondage” (1:14). A people covered by blood, led through a sea, fed with bread from heaven, given a law they could not keep, and forgiven when they broke it. And at the end, a house where God himself comes to live.

Why he brought them out

Pharaoh was never the point. The point was always where they were going, and who would be there:

“Wherefore say unto the children of Israel, I am the LORD, and I will bring you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians, and I will rid you out of their bondage, and I will redeem you with a stretched out arm, and with great judgments: And I will take you to me for a people, and I will be to you a God: and ye shall know that I am the LORD your God, which bringeth you out from under the burdens of the Egyptians.”

Exodus 6:6–7

“And I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God. And they shall know that I am the LORD their God, that brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them: I am the LORD their God.”

Exodus 29:45–46

“That I may dwell among them.” He did not set them free and say, goodbye, good luck. He freed them and then pitched his tent in the middle of their camp. Rescue was never meant to end at the shore of the sea.

The tent that was a man

John 1:14

And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

Revelation 21:3

And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.

The word John uses for “dwelt” means to pitch a tent. The Word became flesh, and set up his tent among us. The glory that Moses could not walk into, “we beheld his glory.” And at the end of the Bible, no tent is needed: “the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them.”

Exodus ends with a cloud over a tent. The Bible ends with God and his people together, with nothing between.

Real world: the glory lives with you now

The glory filled a tent of wood and curtains. For a man who has received Christ, God has done something closer:

“What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own?”

1 Corinthians 6:19
  • SinYou will still fall. But you fall as a man the Spirit lives in, not a man outside the door.
  • RegretThe people who made the calf in chapter 32 are the people God moves in beside in chapter 40. What you did does not decide where God lives.
  • HopeA people at a calf ended at a tent with the glory in it. Today is a first day too.
  • TodayTake the washing seriously: confess it to him and a brother, and go on by what he says, a step at a time, like the cloud.

Next after Exodus is Leviticus. It begins with the LORD calling to Moses “out of the tabernacle”: now that God lives among them, how do sinners live near him? Exodus ends with the glory in the camp. Much of what follows is the answer to that question.

Points to remember

  1. The day“in the first month in the second year, on the first day of the month, that the tabernacle was reared up” 40:17A new year and a new beginning, almost a year after the Passover, and God picks first days.
  2. The ark“he took and put the testimony into the ark… and put the mercy seat above upon the ark” 40:20The broken law is covered by the mercy seat, and the blood on it is Jesus'.
  3. The washing“Moses and Aaron and his sons washed their hands and their feet thereat” 40:31The laver stood between the altar and the tent. The priests washed before they went near.
  4. The oil“their anointing shall surely be an everlasting priesthood throughout their generations” 40:15Washed, clothed and anointed by another, and all believers are a royal priesthood.
  5. The glory“a cloud covered the tent of the congregation, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle” 40:34God moves in. Even Moses could not enter, and now the way is open by the blood of Jesus.
  6. The cloud“the cloud of the LORD was upon the tabernacle by day, and fire was on it by night” 40:38They followed where he led, and stayed where he stayed. And one day the tabernacle of God will be with men.

Question 1 of 6

The day

When was the tabernacle set up?

What verses tell us about this?

“This month shall be unto you the beginning of months: it shall be the first month of the year to you.”

Exodus 12:2

Answer

On the first day of the first month of the second year, almost a year after the Passover. God made it a beginning. If you have a first day, mark it.

Question 2 of 6

The ark

What did Moses put into the ark, and what did he put on top of it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus: Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God;”

Romans 3:24–25

Answer

The testimony, the tables of the law, went into the ark, and the mercy seat went on top of it. The law we broke is covered by the mercy seat, and the blood on it is the blood of Jesus.

Question 3 of 6

The washing

What stood between the tent and the altar, and who washed there?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The laver, full of water. Moses, Aaron and his sons washed their hands and feet there before going into the tent or near the altar. Come clean, then go in.

Question 4 of 6

The oil

What did the LORD say about the anointing of Aaron's sons?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That their anointing would be “an everlasting priesthood throughout their generations.” Moses washed, clothed and anointed them: a priest is made by Another, and in Christ every believer is a royal priesthood.

Question 5 of 6

The glory

What happened when Moses finished the work?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A cloud covered the tent, the glory of the LORD filled it, and Moses was not able to go in. Today there is boldness to enter by the blood of Jesus. God came to live among them.

Question 6 of 6

The cloud

What told Israel when to move and when to stay?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The cloud on the tabernacle. When it lifted they went, when it stayed they stayed, by day and by night, in sight of everyone. They followed where he led.
