Paul left Timothy at Ephesus, about AD 62 to 64, to put a troubled church in order. Chapter 4 told Timothy how to live. Chapter 5 is about how to treat the people around him: older and younger, widows, and elders.

Read alone, chapter 5 still makes sense. It answers one question that every new believer has to answer: what do I owe the people God puts next to me?

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

The church is a family

Verses 1–2

Four kinds of people, and one way to treat each.

Who is who

Older man

Like a father

“Intreat” him: ask, appeal, plead. Don't blast him.

Younger man

Like a brother

Equals. Side by side, no pecking order.

Older woman

Like a mother

With the honour you'd give your own mother.

Younger woman

Like a sister

“With all purity”: a brother's care, nothing else.

If your own father was missing, mean, or drunk, this is good news. God gives you fathers and brothers in the church. Some of the men in your group are going to be those for you.

“With all purity”

Paul adds two words to this one line and no other: “with all purity.” He knew how fast a man's mind can run, and he knew his young friend was a man too.

Many of us have a past with women that we aren't proud of. This is not a trap. It is a fence, and it is a kindness. A sister is not someone you size up. She is someone you protect.

  • not flirtingeven as a joke
  • not alone behind a closed doorwith a woman who isn't your wife
  • not a second lookthe first look is an accident, the second is a choice
  • a brother's respect“with all purity”

Part two

Widows, and your own house

Verses 3–16

Who looks after the people who have no one? Family first.

Who helps first?

There was no welfare office. A woman who lost her husband and had nobody could starve. The early church kept a list and took care of the ones who were truly alone, “widows indeed.”

First

Her own family

Children and grandchildren “shew piety at home”: they pay back what she gave them.

→
Then

Believing relatives

“If any man or woman that believeth have widows, let them relieve them” (v. 16).

→
Last

The church

So the church can care for the ones with truly nobody (v. 16).

The order matters. The church isn't a place to dump your own mother. Family first, then the church. And if you're the one who dropped the ball, you can start picking it up.

Two ways to live alone

The widow indeed

Trusts in God, prays night and day

Desolate, with nobody, and her hope is in the Lord. Alone and not alone.

The one in pleasure

“Dead while she liveth”

Chasing the next feeling. Breathing, but empty inside: dead on her feet.

Some of us know the second life well. The high that promised everything and left a hole. Paul isn't shaming her, or you. He is saying where real life is: not in the chase, but in trust.

The hardest verse in the chapter

Read it slowly. Many of us have not provided for our own. Child support unpaid. A house we left. A mother we let down. This verse is not a cheap shot. It names a failure, and a failure can be turned around.

“Provide” is a word that means think ahead for: to plan for them. “An infidel” just means an unbeliever. Even men who don't believe in God look after their families. When a believer doesn't, his life says one thing and his Saviour says another. That's what “denied” means: his actions are contradicting his faith.

Notice what it does not say. It does not say he's lost, or that God has quit on him. It says his life is out of line with what he says he believes, and that can be fixed.

Rebuilding a provider

  1. TodayShow up to workon time, sober, the whole shift
  2. SoonPut something ineven if it's small: money, groceries, a ride
  3. This monthOne promise, kepta call to the kids that actually happens
  4. This yearSteadypayments made, a steady job, no excuses
  5. In timeTrusted againnot demanded: earned

If you're locked up, in treatment, or out of work right now, you can't do all this today. That's not the end of it. The direction is what counts: you're thinking ahead for them again. And the grace is already yours: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins.” (1 John 1:9)

The widows' list

Verses 9 and 10 are about a special list: older widows the church supported, who in turn gave themselves to prayer and helping. “Threescore” is sixty. A good reputation was part of the job.

  1. ✓Over sixty, “the wife of one man”a faithful woman: one marriage that she honoured
  2. ✓“Brought up children”she'd raised a family, or been a mother to someone
  3. ✓“Lodged strangers, washed the saints' feet”hospitable, willing to do the lowest job in the house
  4. ✓“Relieved the afflicted”helped the ones in trouble: she knew what it was

None of this is about earning God's love. It is about who the church trusted to be its prayer team. Her good works were “well reported”: people could see them.

Idle hands

Why not the younger widows? Not because they were bad. It was about what happens to anyone with an empty day and no direction.

Nothing to do“idle”
→
then“wandering about from house to house”
Wandering“tattlers and busybodies”
→
then“speaking things which they ought not”

A tattler is a gossip; a busybody is someone busy with everybody's business but his own. Men do this too. The road from nothing to do to trouble is short, and many of us know it by heart.

The answer isn't guilt. It's a day with shape: work, a meeting, a call to a brother, a chore, a chapter. An empty day is where the old life moves back in.

Don't give the enemy a handle

“The adversary” is anyone watching to find a reason to mock the faith (and, behind them, Satan, v. 15). Paul's advice is practical: live a settled, ordinary, honest life, and they have nothing to point at.

Think about the men who will watch you. Your old crowd, the family, a parole officer, a boss. They will not read the Bible. They'll read you. A steady life gives the adversary nothing to say.

Verse 15 is blunt: “some are already turned aside after Satan.” They turned aside; they weren't thrown out. A man who has turned aside can turn back, today.

Part three

Elders, and Timothy's own life

Verses 17–25

Honour them, be fair, go slowly, and remember that time tells.

Don't muzzle the ox

An ox walked round and round a threshing floor, treading out the grain. The law said: let him eat as he goes (Deuteronomy 25:4). Paul takes it as a rule about people.

“Double honour” means respect and pay, for the elders who work hard at teaching God's word. Jesus said the same: “The labourer is worthy of his reward.”

Today: if a man teaches you, feeds you, drives you to the meeting or fixes your car, thank him, and when you can, pay him. Honour with words and with money.

Fair to everyone

Leaders get accused, and some of it is false. Paul protects them with the old rule of two or three witnesses, and protects the flock with the next verse: if the sin is real, deal with it in the open.

  • not one man's say-soa rumour is not a witness
  • not what I hearddon't pass it on
  • not going easy on a friend“nothing by partiality”
  • the same rules for everyonewitnesses, honesty, and no favourites

In a recovery house, talk runs fast. Today: don't repeat what you can't prove. If you've been hurt by gossip, you know why Paul wrote this.

Not too fast

“Lay hands” means setting a man apart for leadership with prayer (see 4:14). “Suddenly” means in a hurry. Don't promote a man before he has been tested. If you do, you share in what he does wrong: “neither be partaker of other men's sins.”

  1. SavedToday: a new man in Christwelcome, not a title
  2. MonthsSteadysame man on the bad days
  3. A yearProven in small thingssets up chairs, shows up, pays what he owes
  4. ThenTrusted with morenot rushed

If you're newly saved, this protects you. Don't take on a big job because you feel you owe God. Wait. And when no one makes you a leader for a while, it isn't rejection. It's the time test, and it's kind.

Timothy's stomach

This is a verse some men grab. Read it exactly. Paul is giving medical advice to a sick man who had given up wine, probably to be a clean example in a city full of drunkenness.

What it says

A little wine, for a sick stomach

Timothy had often been ill, and the water in ancient cities was not always safe. Paul tells a man who was drinking only water to take a little wine as medicine.

What it doesn't say

Nothing about cravings or nerves

It isn't permission to drink. It isn't a cure for stress. And it isn't written to a man who can't stop at one.

If you're in recovery

You aren't Timothy. For a man with an addiction, the wise path is to leave alcohol alone, and many Bible teachers say the same. Southern Baptists have long urged believers to leave alcohol alone.

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

“Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging: and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise.”

Proverbs 20:1

If you're ill, there are other medicines. Ask your doctor, and tell him you're in recovery. Paul told Timothy to look after his health. He wouldn't tell you to put your life at risk.

Sooner or later, it shows

Some sins are obvious now. Some wait years, and then catch up. And that's why Moses warned, “be sure your sin will find you out” (Numbers 32:23). Hiding a sin doesn't work. Telling the truth does.

Now read verse 25. Good works also show, and “they that are otherwise cannot be hid”. A changed life can't be hidden either. People who knew the old you will notice the new one. Give it time.

Points to remember

  1. Family“The younger as sisters, with all purity” 5:2Treat the church like a family: fathers, brothers, mothers, and sisters you protect.
  2. Widows“Let them learn first to shew piety at home” 5:4Family cares first, then the church. Don't leave your own on the church's doorstep.
  3. Provide“If any provide not for his own… he hath denied the faith” 5:8Your life should match your faith. Start rebuilding today, one small step at a time.
  4. Leaders“Lay hands suddenly on no man” 5:22Don't rush a man into leadership, and don't rush yourself. Let time test.
  5. Wine“A little wine for thy stomach's sake” 5:23Medical advice to a sick man who abstained, not permission to drink. Leave it alone if it has had you.
  6. Sin“Some men's sins are open beforehand… and some men they follow after” 5:24Sin shows sooner or later. So does a changed life. Tell the truth and wait.

Question 1 of 6

Family

How should we treat older and younger men and women?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Like family. Older men as fathers (appeal to them, don't rebuke), younger men as brothers, older women as mothers, and younger women as sisters, with all purity.

Question 2 of 6

Widows

Who should look after a widow first?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Her own children and relatives. They are to repay what they were given, and the church cares for the ones who have nobody.

Question 3 of 6

Provide

What does Paul say about a man who won't provide for his own family?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

He has denied the faith by his actions, and is worse than an unbeliever. But it's a failure that can be turned around: confess it, be forgiven, and start providing again, one step at a time.

Question 4 of 6

Leaders

How quickly should a man be put into leadership?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not quickly. Test him first, treat every man fairly, and don't share in another man's sins by promoting him too soon. Time shows the man.

Question 5 of 6

Wine

What did Paul tell Timothy about wine, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

“Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging: and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise.”

Proverbs 20:1

Answer

To use a little wine for his stomach and his frequent illnesses. It was medical advice for a man who had given it up, not permission to drink. For a man with an addiction, the wise path is to leave it alone.

Question 6 of 6

Sin

Which sins show up right away, and which follow later?

What verses tell us about this?

“But if ye will not do so, behold, ye have sinned against the LORD: and be sure your sin will find you out.”

Numbers 32:23

Answer

Some sins are plain at once, and some turn up later. Either way it comes out in the end, and so do good works. So be honest now, and let the changed life show.
