Exodus 6

I am the LORD

The people are crushed and Moses is discouraged, so the LORD tells Moses who he is and what he will do: seven times, “I will.” Then the chapter stops to name the family that Moses and Aaron came from.

Part one

“I am the LORD”

Verses 1–13

The people cannot listen, and Moses cannot see how, so the LORD says who he is.

A name to be known by

God Almighty → JEHOVAH God's own name, which the KJV prints as “the LORD”, and here in full as JEHOVAH. It comes from “I AM” (3:14).

Moses had just asked God why. The answer begins here: Now shalt thou see. Pharaoh will drive them out “with a strong hand”, the same Pharaoh who said “I know not the LORD.”

The name was not new. Abraham heard it in Genesis:

“And he said unto him, I am the LORD that brought thee out of Ur of the Chaldees, to give thee this land to inherit it.”

Genesis 15:7

So “by my name JEHOVAH was I not known to them” means they had the promise, and not yet the seeing of it. They knew him as God Almighty, who has power. Their children will know the name by what he does. A name is not a magic word. It is a character you find out by trusting.

“I have remembered my covenant”

  • He establishedHis covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob: the land of Canaan (verse 4)
  • He heard“the groaning of the children of Israel” under the Egyptians (verse 5)
  • He remembered“I have remembered my covenant” (verse 5)

Pilgrimage here is not a trip to a holy place. It is a life lived as a stranger in a land that is not yours. Abraham, Isaac and Jacob lived that way in Canaan: strangers.

God does not say he had forgotten and now recalls. In the Bible, when God remembers, he acts. Four hundred years of affliction (Genesis 15:13) were not four hundred years of being ignored.

“I die: and God will surely visit you, and bring you out of this land unto the land which he sware to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.”

Genesis 50:24

Joseph's last words. Chapter 6 is the answer. If you have been groaning, he has heard the groaning. He remembers his word, and he keeps it.

Seven times, “I will”

  • 1. I will bring you outfrom under the burdens
  • 2. I will rid youout of their bondage
  • 3. I will redeem youwith a stretched out arm
  • 4. I will take youto me for a people
  • 5. I will be to you a God“ye shall know”
  • 6. I will bring you inunto the land I swore
  • 7. I will give it youfor an heritage

Three in verse 6, two in verse 7, two in verse 8, wrapped in “I am the LORD” at its start and its end. There is not one “you must” in it. The people have nothing to do but be rescued.

Rid means rescue. A stretched out arm is God's own power reaching in. Great judgments are the plagues he will send on Egypt. An heritage is an inheritance, given, not earned.

“I will redeem you”

To redeem is to buy back: a slave or a captive set free by a price paid on his behalf. In Egypt, Israel could not buy itself out. Someone had to come in with a stretched out arm.

“Who gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works.”

Titus 2:14

The price in Exodus will be the blood of a lamb. The price for us was Christ himself. A man cannot redeem himself with willpower, any more than Israel could make bricks without straw and walk out of Egypt.

And look at the next promise: I will take you to me for a people, and I will be to you a God. The point of being brought out is not just to be out. It is to belong to him, and to his people. For a man who has spent years alone with his habits, that is as good as the freedom.

Too crushed to hear

Moses told them every word. They “hearkened not,” which means they did not listen: “for anguish of spirit, and for cruel bondage.” In chapter 4 they believed and bowed. Now, after the bricks, they cannot take it in. Anguish here is the picture of a spirit with no room to breathe.

Notice what God does not do. He does not scold them. There is no rebuke for people who were too tired to hear good news. They had a crushing life, and he knew it.

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Psalm 34:18

If you are too heavy to take in what you hear, you are in company. The promise stands even when we cannot hold it. And for those days, he gave us brothers who can hold it for us. Call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor. Tell them the truth about the day.

“Uncircumcised lips”

Circumcision cut away what covered. To say Moses has uncircumcised lips is to say his lips are closed up and not working: he can't speak well. It is the same fear as “slow of speech” in chapter 4.

Moses' reasoning sounds sensible. If Israel will not listen, how will Pharaoh? But it begins with a setback and argues its way out of obeying. God does not debate it. In verse 13 he gives Moses and Aaron a charge: a command, to bring Israel out.

The fear had already been answered (4:11–12), and here it is again. That is how it goes: a fear that God has answered can come back the next time things go wrong. Go back to his answer. Read it again, and ask a brother to read it with you.

The list begins with Reuben, the oldest, and Simeon, then stops at Levi, because Levi is the family of Moses and Aaron. Heads of their fathers' houses are the family heads. You do not have to remember a name: each was a real man with a mother.

Levi, and the line of anger

Look at Levi, and at what Jacob once said of him and Simeon:

“Cursed be their anger, for it was fierce; and their wrath, for it was cruel: I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel.”

Genesis 49:7

Moses comes from that line. The man who killed an Egyptian in a rage (2:12) came from the line of an angry man. And out of it God raises up the deliverer and the priests. Your family's worst trait does not decide what God can do with you.

The Bible counts the years of only three men here: Levi (137), Kohath (133) and Amram (137). A family list like this gives the line, and not every link in it, as the family list of Jesus in Matthew 1 also does. It is the line that matters.

There they are, in a single line: she bare him Aaron and Moses (bare is bore: gave birth to). The man who had asked “Who am I?” (3:11) turns out to be one name in a family of Levites, like Izhar's sons or Uzziel's. God did not wait for someone special. He took the sons of a family of slaves.

Jochebed was Amram's father's sister, his aunt. The law that came later forbade that marriage (Leviticus 18:12); it was not yet given. Moses is listed after Aaron here because Aaron was older: Aaron was eighty-three and Moses eighty (7:7). Their sister Miriam is not named here, but is in Numbers 26:59.

Elisheba was “sister of Naashon”, whom Numbers 1:7 spells Naashon, a prince of Judah. A man of that family is in the line of Jesus (Matthew 1:4 spells it Naasson). The priestly house and the royal house were joined by marriage in a slave camp.

Some of these men will fail

Nadab and Abihu die offering fire the LORD had not commanded (Leviticus 10). Korah, named in verse 21 and again in verse 24, will lead a rebellion (Numbers 16). But look at the end of verse 24: “the families of the Korhites.” Korah's sons did not die with him (Numbers 26:11), and the heading of several psalms says “for the sons of Korah.”

A father's sin was not his son's verdict. If your father was a drunk, or a thief, or never there, you may still be the start of something good.

Why a list of names?

Twice, in two verses: These are that Aaron and Moses. The writer has stopped the story to say who these two men are: real men, with real fathers and grandfathers, whom the LORD named to bring Israel out “according to their armies,” in ranks, like a host and not a mob.

A list of names feels like the dullest part of the Bible. But every name on it was a man the LORD knew. He knows the names of the men in this room, and every one of them is written down where he keeps his own.

“Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.”

Isaiah 43:1

Real world: say your own name to God today. Not the one you were called in the worst years. The one he gave you. You are known, and you are his.

Part three

“I am of uncircumcised lips”

Verses 28–30

The chapter ends where the lesson began: Moses afraid, and the LORD still sending him.

Five times, “I am the LORD”

2×1 6×1 7×1 8×1 29×1

Verses 28–30 take up the story again where verse 13 left it. The LORD says the same thing once more, “I am the LORD: speak thou unto Pharaoh king of Egypt all that I say unto thee.” And Moses says the same thing once more: I am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall Pharaoh hearken unto me?

The chapter closes with Moses still afraid. No speech has fixed it. God does not wait for him to feel ready. The very next verse, in chapter 7, is the answer:

“And the LORD said unto Moses, See, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh: and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet.”

Exodus 7:1

The fear is real, and so is “I am the LORD.” Which of them are you going to repeat to yourself today?

Question 1 of 6

Name

What name did God give himself, and what did he say about it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“I am the LORD,” and the name Jehovah, which they had not yet known in the seeing. It is a name you find out by trusting, as he brings you out. Not a magic word: a character.

Question 2 of 6

Promise

What did God say he would do for Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Seven times, “I will”: bring out, rid, redeem, take, be your God, bring in, give. There is not one “you must”. He does the rescue, from first to last, and it ends with belonging to him.

Question 3 of 6

Despair

Why would the people not listen to Moses?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Psalm 34:18

Answer

They were crushed, “anguish of spirit, and… cruel bondage.” It was not unbelief so much as being too worn out to hear. God did not scold them. If that is you, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today. He is near.

Question 4 of 6

Fear

What was Moses afraid of, and what did he say twice?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He was afraid he could not speak, and said it twice: “uncircumcised lips.” God had already answered it (4:11–12), and the fear came back. Go back to his answer, and read it with a brother.

Question 5 of 6

Family

Whose family does the list in verses 14–27 follow, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“Cursed be their anger, for it was fierce; and their wrath, for it was cruel”

Genesis 49:7

Answer

The family of Levi, which leads to Moses and Aaron. It is from the line whose anger Jacob cursed, and God raised up the deliverer and the priests from it. He knows every name, and your family's worst does not decide your story.

Question 6 of 6

Covenant

His covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, to give them the land. He had not forgotten it. He heard the groaning, and was about to act, as Joseph had said he would.
