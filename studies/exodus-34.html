Exodus 34

Merciful and gracious

New tables of stone, and the LORD passes by and says his own name: “merciful and gracious, longsuffering.” The covenant broken at the calf is made again, and Moses comes down with his face shining.

Part one

“The LORD, The LORD God”

Verses 1–9

Two new stones, and God says who he is.

“Like unto the first”

Hew means cut, chisel out, and brakest is broke. The first tables were God's work from start to finish (32:16). This time Moses cuts the stone, and God writes on it again: the same words, “the words that were in the first tables, which thou brakest.”

God does not lower the standard after a fall. He does not hand out a softer law for people who failed the first one. He gives the same words again, and he writes them himself.

That is the second chance. Not a smaller calling, but the same one, given again to the same people, by the same God. Moses had to climb the mountain one more time to get it.

His name

  • The LORD, The LORD GodHis own name, said twice: “I AM THAT I AM” (3:14), the God who keeps his covenant.
  • mercifultender-hearted. The Hebrew word is close to the word for a mother's womb: the feeling a mother has for her child.
  • graciouskind to those who don't deserve it, freely
  • longsufferingslow to anger, patient for a long time
  • abundant in goodness and truthoverflowing with faithful love, and true to his word

Think about when he says this. Not after Israel did well. Soon after the calf, on the mountain where the covenant was broken. When God tells Moses his own name, mercy comes first.

Forgiving, and just

  • keeping mercy for thousandsmercy kept in store, for thousands (of generations)
  • forgiving iniquity and transgression and sinevery kind: the crooked thing, the rebellion, the missed mark
  • will by no means clear the guiltyHe does not pretend sin is nothing
  • visiting the iniquity of the fatherssin's damage runs down a family, to the third and fourth generation (see 20:5)

Count them: mercy to thousands, damage to three or four. His mercy outruns the wreckage by far. And if the pattern in your family has come down to you, it can stop with you.

But how can God forgive the guilty and also “by no means clear the guilty”? Both cannot be true… unless someone else pays.

Where the two meet

“By no means clear the guilty”

Sin is paid for

God does not wink at it. Every sin is dealt with.

“Forgiving iniquity”

The sinner goes free

Because the payment fell on Someone else.

“Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God; To declare, I say, at this time his righteousness: that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus.”

Romans 3:25–26

Propitiation: the sacrifice that turns away God's wrath. At the cross God is both: just, because sin is paid, and the justifier, because the man who believes goes free.

The name, said again and again

  1. Exodus 34God says iton Sinai
  2. Numbers 14Moses prays it backafter another rebellion
  3. Nehemiah 9“a God ready to pardon”after the exile
  4. Psalm 103David sings it“slow to anger”
  5. Joel 2“turn unto the LORD”come back
  6. Jonah 4even for NinevehJonah knew it
  7. John 1“full of grace and truth”the Word made flesh

“The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy.”

Psalm 103:8

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

John 1:14

Israel held on to these words for a thousand years, every time they fell. Moses asked to see God's glory; John says we have seen it, in Jesus, “full of grace and truth.”

“Pardon our iniquity”

Moses' first answer to the name is not a word. He made haste, hurried, bowed his head to the ground, and worshipped.

Then he prays on the strength of what he just heard. “If now I have found grace… go among us; for it is a stiffnecked people.” Their stubbornness is the reason he gives for needing God close, not a reason to keep him away.

Look at the small word: “pardon our iniquity and our sin.” Moses never made the calf, and he puts himself in with the guilty. And he asks the biggest thing: “take us for thine inheritance”: keep us as Your own.

Use God's own name when you pray after a fall: “Lord, You said You are merciful and gracious, slow to anger, forgiving iniquity. I need that today.”

Part two

“Thou shalt worship no other god”

Verses 10–17

The covenant made again, and a warning about the company they keep.

The covenant, made again

  • marvelswonders, miracles
  • terribleawesome, something that fills men with fear and wonder
  • Observekeep, do carefully

“Behold, I make a covenant.” After the calf, after the broken tables, God makes the covenant again. Not because Israel had earned it back, but because of who he had just said he is.

And he promises to fight for them: “I drive out before thee” the peoples of the land. He has not given up on getting them home.

The snare at the table

  • snarea trap that catches an animal by the foot
  • groveswooden poles set up for a false goddess
  • go a whoringbe unfaithful: the Bible's plain word for leaving God for other gods, like a wife leaving her husband

Watch how it happens in verses 15–16. Make a deal with them. One of them invites you. You eat at his table. Your son marries his daughter. And then your house is worshipping his gods. Nobody plans it. It starts with an invitation.

Whose name is Jealous: God will not share you with what is killing you. And after the calf, he says it straight: “Thou shalt make thee no molten gods.”

Real world: who you sit down with

“Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.”

1 Corinthians 15:33

Evil communications means bad company. You know the old friends, the old places, the old phone numbers. The calf did not start with a calf. It started with the crowd.

  • Break it down“Destroy their altars”: get rid of what you used, and where you hid it.
  • Don't sit downTurn down the invitation that leads back to the old table.
  • Find new companySit with men who are walking with God. Today.

Part three

“Thrice in the year”

Verses 18–28

The feasts, the firstborn, and forty more days on the mount.

Bought back with a lamb

  • openeth the matrixthe first to open the womb: the firstborn
  • firstlingthe first young one born to an animal
  • redeembuy back, by paying a price
  • emptyempty-handed

Every firstborn belonged to the LORD, because he spared Israel's firstborn on the Passover night (13:15). A donkey could not be offered, so it was bought back with a lamb, or it died. And every firstborn son was bought back.

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

A life bought back with a lamb, or lost. That is every man's choice, and the Lamb has already paid.

Rest, and three feasts

  1. Unleavened breadthe month Abib, spring“thou camest out from Egypt”
  2. Weeksthe wheat harvestlater called Pentecost (Acts 2:1)
  3. Ingathering“the year's end”, autumnthe harvest gathered in

Earing is ploughing. Rest on the seventh day even in ploughing and harvest, when a farmer can least afford to stop. Rest is trust: God runs the world while you stop.

“And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:”

Mark 2:27

Thrice is three times; men children are the males. And “neither shall any man desire thy land” while you are gone: God guards the home of the man who goes to worship.

The first, and forty days again

Verses 25–26 repeat what was given in chapters 12 and 23: no leaven with the blood of the sacrifice, nothing of the Passover left till morning, the first of the harvest brought to God, and no young goat seethed (boiled) in its mother's milk. The first of everything goes to God, not the leftovers.

After the tenor of these words means on the terms of these words. Moses writes them down, and the covenant stands on them.

Then forty more days and nights on the mountain, the second time, “he did neither eat bread, nor drink water.” And the ten commandments were written on the new stones. Verse 1 says who wrote them: “I will write upon these tables.”

Part four

“The skin of his face shone”

Verses 29–35

Moses comes down, and everyone can see where he has been, except him.

He didn't know

Wist not: he didn't know. Forty days in the presence of God, and it showed on him. Everyone could see it but Moses.

The Hebrew word for shone means sent out rays, and it is close to the word for a horn. An old Latin Bible read it “horned,” which is why some old statues of Moses have horns. The KJV has it right: his face shone.

This is not something Moses worked at. He had been with God, and it rubbed off. The men who walk closest to God are usually the last to notice the change in themselves. Others see it first.

The vail

A vail is a vail, a cloth over the face. Moses took it off to speak with the LORD and to pass on his words, and put it back on afterward.

Paul tells us why: the glory on his face was fading, and he covered it so they would not watch it go.

“And not as Moses, which put a vail over his face, that the children of Israel could not stedfastly look to the end of that which is abolished:”

2 Corinthians 3:13

But for the man who turns to the Lord, the vail is taken away for good, and the glory does not fade. It grows.

With open face

“Nevertheless when it shall turn to the Lord, the vail shall be taken away.”

2 Corinthians 3:16

“But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.”

2 Corinthians 3:18

As in a glass means as in a mirror. Moses alone went in, and his glow faded. Every believer goes in, with no vail, and is changed, little by little, from glory to glory, by the Holy Spirit who lives in him.

That is the end of the calf story. Not a people destroyed, but a God who says his name is merciful, and who changes stubborn men into the likeness of his Son. It started for Israel at a broken covenant. It can start for you at a broken promise.

Real world: from the calf to the shining face

  • Climb againNew stones, same words. Take up the calling you dropped. God has not changed it.
  • Learn his nameWrite Exodus 34:6–7 down and carry it. Pray it back to him when you fall.
  • Sit with the right menTurn down the old table. Find new company.
  • Spend time with himTime with God shows on a man, even if he doesn't know it. Others will see it first.

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Points to remember

  1. The new tables“Hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first: and I will write upon these tables.” 34:1Moses cut the stone; God wrote the same words again. After a fall, the same calling.
  2. The name“The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth.” 34:6When God proclaimed his name after the calf, he led with mercy. And he does not wink at sin.
  3. The prayer“Pardon our iniquity and our sin, and take us for thine inheritance.” 34:9Moses worshipped, counted himself with the guilty, and prayed God's own name back to him.
  4. The warning“Take heed to thyself, lest thou make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land.” 34:12The old gods come back by way of the old table. Watch your company.
  5. The feasts“the firstling of an ass thou shalt redeem with a lamb… All the firstborn of thy sons thou shalt redeem.” 34:20Keep the feasts, rest, bring the first to God. A life is bought back with a lamb.
  6. The vail“Moses put the vail upon his face again, until he went in to speak with him.” 34:35Moses' glory faded behind a vail. In Christ the vail is gone, and we are changed from glory to glory.

Question 1 of 6

The new tables

Who cut the new tables, and who wrote on them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Moses cut them, and God wrote on them the same words that were on the first tables. God did not lower the standard after the calf. He gave the same calling again, and wrote it himself.

Question 2 of 6

The name

What did the LORD proclaim when he passed by before Moses?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and plenteous in mercy.”

Psalm 103:8

Answer

His name: merciful, gracious, longsuffering, abundant in goodness and truth, forgiving every kind of sin, and yet not clearing the guilty. At the cross both are true: the sin is paid, and the man who believes in Jesus goes free.

Question 3 of 6

The prayer

What did Moses do, and what did he ask, when he heard the name?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He hurried to bow down and worship. Then he asked God to go among them, to pardon “our” sin, and to take them as his own. Pray God's own name back to him, and count yourself in with the people who need mercy.

Question 4 of 6

The warning

What did the LORD warn Israel not to do with the people of the land?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.”

1 Corinthians 15:33

Answer

Not to make covenants with them, eat at their sacrifices, or marry into their worship, because it would be a snare and pull them after other gods. The old life comes back by the old table. Choose your company.

Question 5 of 6

The feasts

What did the LORD tell them to keep, to bring, and to redeem?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To keep the feasts and the seventh-day rest, to bring the first of the harvest, and to redeem every firstborn, a donkey with a lamb. We were bought back the same way: with the blood of Christ, the Lamb.

Question 6 of 6

The vail

Why did Moses put a vail on his face?

What verses tell us about this?

“But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.”

2 Corinthians 3:18

Answer

His face shone from being with God, and the people were afraid. He covered it after he spoke, and Paul says the glory was fading. In Christ there is no vail: we see his glory and are changed, from glory to glory, by the Spirit.
