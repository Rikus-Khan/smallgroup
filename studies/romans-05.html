Paul is writing from Corinth, about AD 57, to believers in Rome he hasn't met yet.

Chapters 1–3 ended with every man guilty: “all have sinned.” Chapter 4 showed Abraham made right with God by believing, not by working.

Now chapter 5 asks: so what does that give me? Today's answer is good news for any man who's tired of fighting God.

Part one

We have peace with God

Verses 1–11

The war is over. What a believer has now, what hard days do in him, and how far down God's love reached to get him.

A verdict, not a feeling

Guilty → Justified Justified is a courtroom word. The Judge declares the man who trusts in Jesus right with God. Not “improving.” Right.

“Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus:”

Romans 3:24

You might not feel justified this morning. You might feel like yesterday. But a verdict doesn't change with your mood. God said it, on account of what Jesus did, and the case is closed.

What you have, starting now

  1. Peace with GodThe war is over. Not just calm feelings: God is no longer against you.
  2. AccessA door that stays open. You can come to Him any hour, by faith.
  3. A place to stand“this grace wherein we stand.” You stand on grace, not on your record.
  4. HopeOf the glory of God. Where this ends is already settled.

“Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.”

Hebrews 4:16

Notice the tense: we have. Not we hope to have, if we do well enough. A man who has fought God, the law, his family and himself for years can put the weapons down. The war with God is over.

A chain that ends in hope

Tribulation means pressure, being pressed like grapes. Patience is staying under the load without quitting. Experience is what's left when a man has been through the fire and come out proven, like tested metal.

Paul doesn't say we glory because of the trouble. We glory in it, because we know what God is making out of it. The chain doesn't end in bitterness. It ends in hope.

Hope that doesn't let you down

Some of us have hoped before and been made fools of. This hope maketh not ashamed: it won't leave you standing there embarrassed, because it doesn't rest on you. It rests on the love of God.

That love is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost. Every believer has the Spirit from the moment he trusted Christ. You don't have to wait for more of Him.

“My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience.”

James 1:2–3

The detox, the cell, the job you lost, the hard morning you're in now: God wastes none of it. A man who came through hard days with God has something to give the next man going through them.

How far down His love reached

  1. a righteous man“scarcely” would anyone die for him
  2. a good man“peradventure” (perhaps) someone would dare
  3. without strengthcouldn't help ourselves (5:6)
  4. ungodly sinners(5:6, 8)
  5. enemiesand Christ died for us (5:10)

Most of us would think hard before dying for a good man. Christ went lower than that. He died for men with no strength left, men who had turned their backs on God, men at war with Him.

“Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.”

John 15:13

Jesus said that. Then He did more than that: He died for His enemies. Commendeth means He proved it. God doesn't just say He loves you. He showed it at the cross.

He didn't wait for you to clean up first.

Christ didn't die for the man you were going to become. He died for the man you were: while we were yet sinners. He knew every one of your worst days before you lived them, and He went to the cross anyway.

So whatever you did yesterday can't surprise Him or make Him take it back. Regret is real. Take it to the One who already paid for it. Don't let it tell you He's done with you.

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

“Much more”

Paul argues from the hard thing to the easy thing. If God did the hard thing for His enemies, He will surely do the easier thing for His children.

If, when we were enemiesChrist died to reconcile us
→
much more, now we're HisHe will keep us, “saved by his life”

Reconciled means enemies made friends again. Atonement here means the same thing: made at one with God. It's already received, a done deal.

Jesus didn't save you and then leave you to keep it up on your own. He is alive, and He keeps what He saves.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Part two

By one man

Verses 12–19

Two men stand at the head of the human race. What one man broke, the other more than mended.

From Adam to today

  1. AdamSin enters, and death with itGenesis 3
  2. Adam to MosesDeath reigned, with no written law5:13–14
  3. MosesThe law given at Sinai5:20
  4. Jesus Christ“Him that was to come”5:14
  5. TodayGrace reigns5:21

“But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.”

Genesis 2:17

Before Moses there were no Ten Commandments on stone, but people still died. Sin was there all along. The law just wrote out the charges. And Adam was a figure, a pattern, of the One who was coming: another man whose one act would reach everyone.

Not an even trade

“Not as the offence, so also is the free gift.” Grace isn't just Adam's sin cancelled out. It is much more.

Adam's one offence brought condemnation. The free gift deals with many offences: not one sin but every one, yours included, and turns them into justification.

And look who reigns now. Death reigned over us. Those who receive the gift shall reign in life. You're not just let off. You're raised up.

Two heads, two outcomes

Every man is born in Adam's line. Nobody had to teach us to sin. But there's a second Head, and His obedience, all the way to the cross, reaches as far as Adam's fall.

The gift came “upon all men”: offered to everyone. Whoever receives it by faith (5:17) is moved out of Adam's line into Christ's.

“For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive.”

1 Corinthians 15:22

His obedience, counted for you

Your recordwhat you did

His recordwhat He did

Disobediencethe things you regret most
Obedience“unto death, even the death of the cross”

At the cross the records traded places. He took what you did. You're given what He did.

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Part three

Grace did much more abound

Verses 20–21

However high the sin rises, grace rises higher.

Grace rises higher

“The law entered, that the offence might abound.” The law is like switching on the light in a dirty room. It doesn't make the dirt. It shows you how much there is.

“Wherefore the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ, that we might be justified by faith.”

Galatians 3:24

Then the best sentence in the chapter: where sin abounded, grace did much more abound. However deep your sin went, you can't get under the reach of grace. No man in this room has out-sinned it.

A new King on the throne

Sinreignedunto deaththe old king, and where he always takes a man
Gracereignsunto eternal lifethe new King, through Jesus Christ our Lord, and where He takes you

Someone always asks: if grace is that big, why not keep sinning? Paul asks it himself in chapter 6. Hold that one.

For today: when the hard thing comes, name the link. This is tribulation, and God is working patience. Then tell one brother what you're carrying, so you don't carry it alone.

Points to remember

  1. Peace“We have peace with God” 5:1Justified by faith: the war with God is over, the door is open, and you stand on grace.
  2. Hard times“Tribulation worketh patience” 5:3Trouble, patience, proven character, hope. God wastes none of your hard days.
  3. Love“While we were yet sinners, Christ died for us” 5:8He didn't wait for you to clean up. He died for you as you were.
  4. Kept“Much more … we shall be saved by his life” 5:10If He saved you as an enemy, He will surely keep you as His child.
  5. Two men“By the obedience of one shall many be made righteous” 5:19Adam brought sin and death. Christ brings righteousness and life to whoever receives Him.
  6. Grace“Grace did much more abound” 5:20However high your sin rose, grace rises higher. No one has out-sinned it.

Question 1 of 6

Peace

What do we have now that we are justified by faith?

What verses tell us about this?

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Answer

Peace with God: the war is over. Access into His grace any time, a place to stand that isn't our record, and a sure hope of the glory of God. We have it now, not someday if we do well enough.

Question 2 of 6

Hard times

What does tribulation work in a believer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Patience, the strength to stay under the load. Patience makes experience, a man proven true. And experience makes hope, a hope that won't let us down, because God's love has been poured into our hearts by the Holy Ghost.

Question 3 of 6

Love

When did Christ die for us?

What verses tell us about this?

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

Answer

While we were still sinners, without strength, ungodly, even enemies. He didn't wait for us to get better first. That is how God proved His love.

Question 4 of 6

Kept

If God saved us while we were His enemies, how sure is it now?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Answer

Much more sure. If Christ's death made peace with His enemies, His life will surely keep His friends. A believer is saved from wrath and kept by a living Saviour. He never lets go.

Question 5 of 6

Two men

What came through Adam, and what came through Jesus Christ?

What verses tell us about this?

“For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive.”

1 Corinthians 15:22

Answer

Through Adam's one disobedience came sin, condemnation and death for all. Through Christ's obedience came the free gift: righteousness, justification and life, offered to all and given to everyone who receives it by faith.

Question 6 of 6

Grace

What happens to grace where sin abounds?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Grace does much more abound. However much sin piles up, grace is bigger. And now grace reigns, through righteousness, unto eternal life by Jesus Christ our Lord.
