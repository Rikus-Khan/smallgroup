Galatians 1

No other gospel

Paul writes in a hurry to young churches being talked out of grace. Then he tells his own story: the worst enemy the church had, called by God's grace.

The letter to the Galatians

“Paul, an apostle … And all the brethren which are with me, unto the churches of Galatia”

Written from
Antioch in Syria, many think, soon after Paul came home from his first journey (Acts 14:26–28)
When

Many think Galatians is the first letter Paul ever wrote. The churches were only a year or two old. Paul had barely got home when the news came: they were being talked out of grace.

The same fight reached Jerusalem a few months later. The apostles met and settled it: Gentiles are saved by grace, without becoming Jews first (Acts 15:11). Galatians says it first, and louder.

Part one

Who gave himself

Verses 1–5

Paul packs the whole gospel into his greeting, because the gospel is what's under attack.

What He gave, and what for

What He did

“Gave himself for our sins”

Not a donation. Not good advice. Himself, on a cross, in our place. Our sins are the reason He went there.

Why He did it

“That he might deliver us”

Deliver means rescue: pulling a man out of something he can't get out of on his own.

“This present evil world” is the age we live in, with its pull and its lies. Jesus doesn't lift us out of it yet; you still go to work in it today. He breaks its grip on you. And notice: Paul says “not of men” (v. 1). No man sent him. Jesus did, and the Father “who raised him from the dead.”

Part two

Which is not another

Verses 6–12

No thank-you, no small talk. Paul goes straight to the trouble.

Jesus, plus something

What the troublers taught (Acts 15:1)Jesus+circumcision+keeping the law of Moses=saved
What Paul preachedJesus+nothing=saved

They never said, “Drop Jesus.” They said, “Jesus, and.” Paul says that isn't a second version of the good news; it's no good news at all. The moment you add what you must do, it's back on you, and grace is gone. In nearly every other letter Paul starts by thanking God for the people. Not here: “I marvel.”

Accursed

ἀνάθεμα said anathema “accursed”: handed over to God's judgment

Paul says it twice, on purpose. Men's souls are at stake. A doctor who swaps the medicine for sugar water isn't being kind.

This curse is for the man who changes the gospel, not for the struggling believer. And look who's included: “we, or an angel from heaven.” Check the message, not the messenger. A preacher on TV, a voice in your head, a smooth talker at work: if it isn't grace through faith in Jesus, it isn't the gospel.

Whom are you working for?

IfI yet pleased men,
→
thenI should not be the servant of Christ.

Somebody had been saying Paul watered the gospel down to win Gentiles. His answer: a man who curses false teachers isn't trying to be liked. You can't serve two bosses.

Most of us know what it is to live for other people's approval: the crowd, the old friends, the family who's never satisfied. It wears a man out. Serving Christ means one opinion settles it, and His is already given in grace.

Where Paul got his message

  • not after mannobody made it up
  • neither received it of manit wasn't passed down to him like a family custom
  • neither was I taught itno school or teacher gave it to him
  • but by the revelation of Jesus ChristJesus Himself showed him

Certify means I want you to know for sure. A revelation is an unveiling: something hidden, uncovered. Now Paul is going to prove it with his own story. Nobody who knew him would ever have guessed where he ended up.

A terrible past

Conversation in old English means way of life. Wasted means laid waste, like an army wrecking a town. When believers were put to death, Saul voted yes.

And he did it as the most religious man in the room: top of his class, “exceedingly zealous.” Religion without Christ didn't make Saul good. It made him dangerous. That's the whole point of this letter.

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

But God

1:13What I was

“I persecuted the church of God, and wasted it.”

1:15–16What God did

“Called me by his grace, to reveal his Son in me.”

1:23What I am now

“…now preacheth the faith which once he destroyed.”

Everything turns on one word: “But.” Saul didn't clean himself up and then come to God. God came to him, on the road, in the middle of the hunt. And God had a plan for him “from my mother's womb,” long before Saul went wrong. Your past didn't surprise God either.

Part four

They glorified God in me

Verses 18–24

Quiet years, a short visit, and a story that made the churches praise God.

The years after the road

On the road to Damascus to arrest believers, Saul met the risen Jesus (Acts 9).

He went off into Arabia, the desert kingdom east and south of Damascus, then back to Damascus.

After three years, Jerusalem: fifteen days with Peter. Of the others he saw only James, the Lord's brother, who hadn't believed in Jesus at first either (John 7:5).

Then home to Syria and Cilicia. Tarsus, his hometown, is in Cilicia (Acts 9:30).

Paul's years, the way he tells them

  1. about AD 34 to 35The Damascus road1:15–16
  2. soon afterArabia, then Damascus1:17
  3. three years onJerusalem: fifteen days with Peter1:18
  4. yearsSyria and Cilicia, out of sight1:21–22
  5. fourteen years onJerusalem again, with Barnabas and Titus2:1
  6. about 46 to 48The first journey, to GalatiaActs 13–14

Paul tells it this carefully for a reason: the apostles in Jerusalem didn't hand him the gospel. He hardly saw them. “Before God, I lie not.”

There's something else here. Most of those years were quiet, far from any spotlight. God wasn't in a hurry with Paul. If your new life feels small and slow right now, that isn't wasted time. Be faithful where you are.

What people heard about him

The churches of JudaeaDid you hear? The man who hunted us down…

…and the answer…now preaches the faith he tried to destroy.

They didn't say, “We'll never trust him.” They didn't keep score. “They glorified God in me.” Paul's past became the proof of how good God is.

People will hear about your past. Let them also hear what God has done since. Paul never pretended his record didn't happen, but he didn't live there either:

“Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before,”

Philippians 3:13

“They glorified God in me.”

Regret is real. Paul carried the names of the people he hurt. But he took his regret to the cross, where Jesus “gave himself for our sins” (v. 4), and left it there.

Here's one thing to do today: tell one person one thing God has done for you. Not a sermon. Just the before and after. That's how the churches of Judaea heard about Paul, and they ended up praising God.

Points to remember

  1. The rescue“Who gave himself for our sins, that he might deliver us” 1:4Jesus gave Himself, to pull us out of what we couldn't get out of alone.
  2. Another gospel“Which is not another” 1:7Jesus plus anything isn't good news. Grace is all of it, or it isn't grace.
  3. Pleasing men“If I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ” 1:10Live for one opinion: His.
  4. The past“Beyond measure I persecuted the church of God, and wasted it” 1:13A violent man, and very religious. No man is too far gone.
  5. Called by grace“But when it pleased God … called me by his grace” 1:15God came to Saul before Saul ever changed. That's grace.
  6. Glory to God“And they glorified God in me” 1:24Your story, told honestly, can make people praise God.

Question 1 of 6

The rescue

What did Jesus give, and what did He give it for?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

He gave Himself, for our sins. He did it to deliver us, to rescue us from this present evil world, by the Father's will. He did it while we were still sinners, not after we got better.

Question 2 of 6

Another gospel

Why is a different gospel not really another gospel at all?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Because a “gospel” of Jesus plus your own law-keeping isn't good news. It perverts the gospel of Christ and puts the weight back on you. There is only one gospel: grace, received by faith. Anyone who changes it, even an angel, is accursed.

Question 3 of 6

Pleasing men

Whom was Paul trying to please?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God, not men. He was sent by Jesus, not by any man, and his message came from Jesus too. A man who lives to please people can't be Christ's servant. Paul answered to one Master.

Question 4 of 6

The past

What kind of man was Paul before God called him?

What verses tell us about this?

“As for Saul, he made havock of the church, entering into every house, and haling men and women committed them to prison.”

Acts 8:3

Answer

A persecutor who tried to wreck the church, and a top religious man, zealous for the traditions of his fathers. His religion didn't make him good; it made him violent. If God could save Saul, nobody is too far gone.

Question 5 of 6

Called by grace

What turned Paul around?

What verses tell us about this?

“Howbeit for this cause I obtained mercy, that in me first Jesus Christ might shew forth all longsuffering, for a pattern to them which should hereafter believe on him to life everlasting.”

1 Timothy 1:16

Answer

God did. He called Paul by His grace and revealed His Son to him. Paul didn't earn it or work up to it. And God says Paul is a pattern: what He did for Paul, He'll do for anyone who believes.

Question 6 of 6

Glory to God

What did the churches do when they heard what had happened to Paul?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

They glorified God. They didn't hold his past over him; they praised God for what He had done. A changed life points people to the One who changed it.
