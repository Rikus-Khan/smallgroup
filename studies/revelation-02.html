Revelation 2

I Know Thy Works

Four letters from the risen Jesus to four real churches: one that lost its love, one that was poor and hunted, one that made deals with the world, and one that let a false teacher stay. To each He says, “I know.”

Four letters, four cities

  • Ephesusthe great port and the biggest city of Asia. Paul spent three years there (Acts 19–20).
  • Smyrnaa proud, rich harbour city, fiercely loyal to Rome.
  • Pergamosthe old capital, crowded with temples, including one to the emperor.
  • Thyatiraan inland trade town of guilds: dyers, potters, bronze workers. Lydia came from here (Acts 16:14).

A messenger landing at Ephesus would carry the book along this road, reading each church its letter, and every church would hear all seven.

Who is the overcomer? John tells us: “Who is he that overcometh the world, but he that believeth that Jesus is the Son of God?” (1 John 5:5). The promises belong to every true believer. They are not prizes for super-Christians.

Part one

Ephesus: the church that lost its love

Verses 1–7

Hard-working, sound in doctrine, tough on false teachers, and slowly going cold.

A church that did a lot right

About forty years earlier, Paul had warned the Ephesian elders:

“For I know this, that after my departing shall grievous wolves enter in among you, not sparing the flock.”

Acts 20:29

They listened. They worked hard, they put up with a lot, they never quit, and they checked every preacher who came through town against the truth. Jesus notices every bit of it: “I know thy works.” Nothing you do for Him is wasted or unseen.

The way back to your first love

Look backRemember

Remember how it was when He first saved you: the gratitude, the hunger for His word, the joy.

Turn aroundRepent

Call the coldness what it is. Tell Him. Turn back. Not more effort; a changed heart.

Start againDo the first works

Do what you did then: pray, read, show up, tell somebody. Feelings follow feet.

You can be busy for God, right about everything, and still not love Him much. That drift is slow and quiet. The cure is not guilt. It is going back to the One who loved you first.

A lampstand can go dark

The lampstand is the church (1:20). Jesus is not threatening to take any believer's salvation: “they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand” (John 10:28). He is warning that a church that stops loving stops shining, and He will not keep a dark lamp in its place.

Today Ephesus is a field of ruins. The same can happen to a man's witness. The fix is in the verse: except thou repent. The door back is open.

Nicolaitanes, and the tree of life

The Nicolaitanes: nobody knows for sure who they were. Verses 14–15 set them right beside Balaam's teaching, so many think they taught that a believer could join in idol feasts and sexual sin and it wouldn't matter. Jesus hates their deeds. Notice: their deeds, not the people.

Genesis 2:9

… the tree of life also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of knowledge of good and evil.

Revelation 22:2

In the midst of the street of it, and on either side of the river, was there the tree of life, which bare twelve manner of fruits …

Man lost the tree of life in Eden because of sin. Jesus promises it back. The Bible starts in a garden and ends in a garden city, and the way back in is Him.

Poor, but rich

What the city saw

Poverty

The word means dirt poor, nothing at all. Christians who wouldn't join the guild feasts or honour Caesar lost work. Lies were told about them.

What Jesus saw

Rich

Forgiven, loved, with a crown waiting and a Saviour who had been dead and is alive. Rich where it counts.

Some of you are starting over with nothing: no money, no car, a record. Jesus doesn't measure you by your bank account or your past. If you belong to Him, you are a rich man today.

Faithful unto death

Jesus doesn't promise them a way out. He promises to be with them through it. Ten days: a short, limited time. The devil can throw them in prison, but God sets how long. The crown is the wreath given to the winner of a race.

“Blessed is the man that endureth temptation: for when he is tried, he shall receive the crown of life, which the Lord hath promised to them that love him.”

James 1:12

About AD 155, Polycarp, the old pastor of Smyrna, was told to curse Christ or burn. The story says he answered: “Eighty and six years have I served Him, and He never did me any wrong. How then can I blaspheme my King and my Saviour?” He was faithful unto death.

The second death is the lake of fire (20:14). A man who belongs to Jesus may die once. He will never die that death.

Part three

Pergamos: where Satan's seat is

Verses 12–17

Brave under fire, but letting the enemy in through the side door.

“I know where thou dwellest”

Satan's seat is his throne. Pergamos was the Roman capital of the region, with a huge altar to Zeus on its hill and temples where people bowed to the emperor. Many think one of these is what Jesus means. Either way, the town was a stronghold of the enemy.

They held on anyway. Antipas, a believer we know only from this verse, was killed for Jesus right there, and Jesus calls him “my faithful martyr.” Nobody remembers his killers. Jesus remembers his name.

You may live or work where Satan's seat is: the block you grew up on, a job site full of the old life, a cell block. Jesus knows exactly where you live. He doesn't tell them to move. He tells them to hold fast where they are.

If he can't curse you, he'll invite you

King Balac hired the prophet Balaam to curse Israel (Numbers 22–24). Every time Balaam opened his mouth, God turned it into a blessing. So Balaam gave the king another plan: invite Israel to the party, with idol feasts and Moabite women.

“Behold, these caused the children of Israel, through the counsel of Balaam, to commit trespass against the LORD in the matter of Peor, and there was a plague among the congregation of the LORD.”

Numbers 31:16

The enemy who can't knock you down with persecution will try to lure you with a party. For a man in recovery that sounds like: “Come on, one drink with the old crew won't hurt.” Pergamos stood up to the sword and fell to the invitation. Jesus says: Repent.

A new name for a man with a bad one

junkie · inmate · loser · failure → a new name written on a white stone, known only to Him and to you

Men in Pergamos were tempted to eat at idol feasts. Jesus offers better food: hidden manna, the bread from heaven. He is that bread (John 6:51). A white stone was used in courts to vote “not guilty,” and as a ticket into a feast; many teachers think Jesus means one or both.

God promised it long ago: “thou shalt be called by a new name, which the mouth of the LORD shall name” (Isaiah 62:2). Other people may still use your old name. Jesus doesn't.

Part four

Thyatira: growing, and tolerating Jezebel

Verses 18–29

The longest letter goes to the smallest town, and it ends with the morning star.

A church that was growing

Ephesus had cooled off. Thyatira was going the other way: more love, more service, more faith now than at the start. Jesus sees it: the last to be more than the first.

Thyatira was a town of trade guilds. To work as a dyer, a potter or a bronze smith, you joined a guild, and guild meetings meant meals offered to idols and often drunkenness and worse. Saying no could cost you your trade.

So Jesus comes to them as the Son of God with eyes like fire and feet like fine brass: the town's bronze workers made polished brass, and His is purer than theirs. And He sees through every excuse.

Jezebel, and space to repent

The first Jezebel was the queen who led Israel into Baal worship: “none like unto Ahab, which did sell himself to work wickedness in the sight of the LORD, whom Jezebel his wife stirred up” (1 Kings 21:25). This one called herself a prophetess and taught believers that idol feasts and sex outside marriage were fine.

Even for her, Jesus held back the clock: “I gave her space to repent.” He is “not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance” (2 Peter 3:9). If you are still breathing, you still have space. Don't waste it, as she did.

The morning star

The morning star is the bright one you see in the last, darkest part of the night. When it shows up, dawn is coming. At the end of the book, Jesus tells us who it is: “I am the root and the offspring of David, and the bright and morning star” (22:16).

The prize is not a thing. It is Jesus Himself. And to the faithful who refused Jezebel, He says: “I will put upon you none other burden”. No new rules. Just hold fast till I come.

Which one sounds like you right now? Every letter ends the same way: “He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.” All of them are for all of us.

Something to do today: pick the letter that hits closest, and do its command today. If you've gone cold, do one “first work”: call the man who led you to Jesus, or read the chapter you read when you first believed.

Points to remember

  1. First love“Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and repent, and do the first works” 2:5Busy and right isn't enough. Go back to loving Jesus the way you did at first.
  2. Poor but rich“I know thy works, and tribulation, and poverty, (but thou art rich)” 2:9Jesus sees your troubles, and in Him you are rich.
  3. Faithful“be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life” 2:10Suffering has a limit; the crown of life does not.
  4. Compromise“thou hast there them that hold the doctrine of Balaam” 2:14What can't curse you will try to invite you. Don't make room for sin.
  5. A new name“a white stone, and in the stone a new name written” 2:17Jesus gives a new name to men with a bad one.
  6. Space to repent“I gave her space to repent” 2:21God is patient, even with the worst. Use the space He gives.

Question 3 of 6

Faithful

What does Jesus promise the one who is faithful unto death?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A crown of life. And the overcomer will not be hurt of the second death. Death can take his body; it cannot touch his life with God.

Question 4 of 6

Compromise

What did Jesus have against Pergamos and Thyatira?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Both let false teaching stay: Balaam's doctrine in Pergamos and Jezebel in Thyatira, leading believers into idol feasts and sexual sin.

Question 6 of 6

Space to repent

What did Jesus give Jezebel before He judged her?
