Romans 10

Whosoever shall call

Paul prays for his people, and shows how near salvation really is: as near as your own mouth and your own heart.

Chapter 9 showed God's right to give mercy on His own terms. It ended with the reason Israel missed it: they “sought it not by faith, but as it were by the works of the law” (9:32).

Chapter 10 keeps going from there. If God's terms are faith, what does faith look like? How close is it? Who can have it? And how does anyone ever hear about it? This is one of the plainest chapters in the Bible on how a man is saved.

Part one

My heart's desire

Verses 1–5

Paul prays for people who want him dead, and names the mistake he once made himself.

He kept praying

Paul had just spent a chapter on God's choosing. It didn't make him stop praying for the lost. It made him pray harder. He wanted every one of them saved, and he told God so.

And who was he praying for? The same people who had run him out of town after town, stoned him, and plotted to kill him. Brethren, he still calls the church, and he still calls Israel his own.

Is there someone you've given up on? A brother, your father, the man who got you started? Say his name to God today. Paul's prayer for Israel is still being answered.

Zeal, but not according to knowledge

Zeal is burning energy for a cause. Israel had plenty of it for God. Paul knew exactly what that looked like, because he'd been the most zealous man of all:

“Concerning zeal, persecuting the church; touching the righteousness which is in the law, blameless.”

Philippians 3:6

Verse 3

Their own righteousness

“Going about to establish” it: building it, brick by brick, out of rules kept and good deeds done. It's never finished, and it's never enough.

Verse 3

The righteousness of God

Not built, but submitted to: a right standing God gives as a gift. Paul gave up his own “to be found in him” (Philippians 3:9).

You can be sincere and still be sincerely wrong. Israel didn't fail because they didn't try hard enough. They failed because they were trying to earn what can only be received.

The end of the law

Here are two ways to be right with God. Verse 5 gives the first, from Leviticus 18:5:

Ifa man does all those things, every day of his life,
→
thenhe shall live by them.

Nobody has ever done it, except one Man. “For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God” (3:23). Jesus kept the law perfectly, and then died for the men who didn't.

End here is the Greek telos: the goal and the finish line. The law was always pointing to Him. Once you trust Him, the race to earn your standing with God is over. He ran it.

Part two

The word is nigh thee

Verses 6–13

You don't have to climb to heaven or dig into the grave. It's as close as your own mouth.

Paul borrows Moses' words. Nobody has to climb up to heaven to fetch Christ: He already came down. Nobody has to go down into the grave to raise Him: He already rose.

The hard part is done. What's left is close enough to touch: “the word is nigh thee, even in thy mouth, and in thy heart.”

“But the word is very nigh unto thee, in thy mouth, and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it.”

Deuteronomy 30:14

Nigh thee

Nigh means near. Moses said it about God's word to Israel at the edge of the promised land. Paul says it's even truer of the gospel: “the word of faith, which we preach.”

There's no pilgrimage to make, no program to finish first, no level of clean you have to reach. You don't have to get your life together to come. Salvation isn't far away from the man in a cell, a rehab bed, or a truck at a job site at five in the morning. It's right where he is.

Mouth and heart

In thine heart

Believe

“that God hath raised him from the dead.” Trust that Jesus died for your sins and rose again, and put your whole weight on Him.

With thy mouth

Confess

“the Lord Jesus”: say it and mean it. Jesus is Lord. Own Him out loud as your Lord and Saviour.

These aren't two hoops to jump through. They're one faith, inside and out. The heart trusts Him, and the mouth says so. Verse 10 even turns the order around. Nothing else is added: not a ceremony, not a fee, not a cleanup period.

“For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures:”

1 Corinthians 15:3–4

Thou shalt be saved

Paul doesn't say might be saved, or could be, if you hold out long enough. “Thou shalt be saved.” That's a promise from God, and it's said to “thou”: one man at a time.

The jailor at Philippi, Acts 16:30Sirs, what must I do to be saved?

Paul and Silas, 16:31Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house.

Baptism comes after, as the first step of obeying the Lord you've confessed. It pictures what has already happened. But the saving happens when a man believes and calls on Him. A man who did that years ago can stop wondering. A man who never has can do it today.

No difference

Paul uses the same two words twice in this letter, and they cut both ways:

Romans 3:22–23

No difference in the problem

“for there is no difference: For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God”

Romans 10:12

No difference in the offer

“for the same Lord over all is rich unto all that call upon him.”

The religious man and the addict, the Jew and the Greek, stand in the same line. Nobody's too good to need Him, and nobody's too far gone for Him. He's rich toward everyone who calls: there's no shortage of mercy.

And “shall not be ashamed”: a lot of us know shame better than our own names. Trust Him, and He will never leave you standing there embarrassed that you did.

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Paul is quoting Joel 2:32, and Peter preached the same verse at Pentecost (Acts 2:21). Look at some of the men who called:

  1. A dying thief“Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom.” (Luke 23:42)
  2. A crooked tax man“God be merciful to me a sinner.” (Luke 18:13)
  3. A jailor at midnight“Sirs, what must I do to be saved?” (Acts 16:30)
  4. A persecutor“Lord, what wilt thou have me to do?” (Acts 9:6)
  5. Youtoday, in your own words. Whosoever has your name in it.

No one in this room is outside that word. Not the worst thing you've done, not the time you went back, not the family you hurt. If you call on Him, He saves. That's His promise.

Part three

How beautiful are the feet

Verses 14–21

If calling saves, somebody has to tell them who to call. And God's hands stay open all day long.

The chain

Paul asks it backward, one link at a time: call, believe, hear, preacher, sent. Watch the chain build from the end.

  1. Sent“except they be sent”
  2. Preach“without a preacher”
  3. Hear“of whom they have not heard”
  4. Believe“in whom they have not believed”
  5. Call“call on him”
  6. Saved“shall be saved” (10:13)

Now run it forward. God sends someone. He tells it. A man hears it, believes it, calls on the Lord, and is saved. Every man who has ever been saved got there by this chain.

A preacher here isn't only a man in a pulpit. The Greek word means a herald, anyone who announces news. If one link is missing, the chain breaks. Some of us are that link for somebody.

“How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace; that bringeth good tidings of good, that publisheth salvation; that saith unto Zion, Thy God reigneth!”

Isaiah 52:7

Beautiful feet

Isaiah pictured a runner coming over the hills to a city under siege, carrying news that the war was won. His feet were cut and dusty. To the people on the wall, they were beautiful, because of the news they carried.

Whose feet brought it to you? A chaplain, a sponsor, a grandmother, a man at a meeting. Today, your feet can be the beautiful ones. Tell one man what Jesus did for you.

“So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

Not everyone who hears believes. Isaiah grieved the same thing seven hundred years earlier: “Lord, who hath believed our report?” (Isaiah 53:1). The word doesn't force anyone. It can be refused.

But faith doesn't come from feelings, or from trying to work it up. It comes from hearing what God has said. Faith grows the way it started: by hearing the word.

So if your faith feels thin, don't go looking inside yourself for more. Get under the word. That's why we meet every day. Read a chapter today, even if it's only one. Faith comes that way.

Into all the earth

Has Israel not heard? They have. Paul borrows Psalm 19:4, where the sky preaches God's glory everywhere, and says the gospel has gone just as far.

At Pentecost, about AD 30, Jews from all over the empire heard Peter, “strangers of Rome” among them (Acts 2:10). They carried it home. By about AD 57, when Paul wrote this letter, there was a church in Rome he had never even visited.

Hands stretched out all day long

Verse 20, the Gentiles

Found by men who weren't looking

“I was found of them that sought me not.” That's most of us. We weren't seeking God. He came looking for us.

Verse 21, Israel

Hands held out to men who wouldn't come

“All day long I have stretched forth my hands unto a disobedient and gainsaying people.” Gainsaying means talking back.

Look at the last picture Paul leaves us. God isn't turning His back. He's standing with His arms open, all day, to people who keep saying no. When they're lost, the fault is not on His side.

And chapter 11 opens: “Hath God cast away his people? God forbid.” His hands are still open, to Israel, and to you, today.

Points to remember

  1. Prayer“My heart’s desire and prayer … that they might be saved” 10:1Keep praying for the lost, even the ones who hurt you.
  2. Zeal“A zeal of God, but not according to knowledge” 10:2They tried to build their own righteousness instead of receiving God's.
  3. Near“The word is nigh thee” 10:8Christ already came and rose. Salvation is as close as your mouth and heart.
  4. Saved“Confess with thy mouth … believe in thine heart” 10:9Trust that God raised Jesus, own Him as Lord, and you shall be saved.
  5. Whosoever“Whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord” 10:13No difference: the offer has every man's name in it.
  6. Sent“Faith cometh by hearing” 10:17Someone is sent, the word is told and heard, and faith is born.

Question 1 of 6

Prayer

What was Paul's heart's desire for Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

“Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth.”

1 Timothy 2:4

Answer

That they might be saved. He prayed for it, for the very people who persecuted him. God wants all men saved, and so should we. Keep praying for the ones you love who don't know Him yet.

Question 2 of 6

Zeal

What was wrong with Israel's zeal?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It was real, but it wasn't according to knowledge. They didn't know God's way of making a man right, so they tried to build their own righteousness and wouldn't submit to the one God gives. Paul had done the same thing, until he met Christ.

Question 3 of 6

Near

How near is the word of faith?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As near as your own mouth and heart. Nobody has to climb to heaven or go down to the dead. Christ already came down, died and rose. All that's left is to believe it and say so, right where you are.

Question 4 of 6

Saved

What must a man do to be saved?

What verses tell us about this?

“And they said, Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house.”

Acts 16:31

Answer

Believe in your heart that God raised Jesus from the dead, and confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord. It's one faith, inside and out. Nothing else is added to it, and God promises: thou shalt be saved.

Question 5 of 6

Whosoever

Who can call upon the name of the Lord?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Whosoever. Jew or Greek, religious or not, clean or still struggling. There's no difference: the same Lord is rich to everyone who calls. That includes every man in this room, today.

Question 6 of 6

Sent

How does a man come to faith?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By hearing the word of God. Someone is sent, the gospel is told, a man hears it, believes, and calls on the Lord. Faith comes, and grows, by hearing God's word. And somebody has to be the feet that bring it.
