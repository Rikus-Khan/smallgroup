Matthew 10

Sent out

Jesus names twelve ordinary men, gives them His power, and sends them out like sheep among wolves, with a Father who counts every hair on their heads.

Part one

The twelve

Verses 1–4

Fishermen, a tax man, a zealot, and a traitor.

From learners to messengers

disciples → apostles A disciple is a learner, a student who follows his teacher around. An apostle (Greek apostolos) is one sent, like an ambassador who speaks for his king. Verse 1 calls them disciples. Verse 2 calls them apostles.

At the end of chapter 9, Jesus told these men to pray for labourers. Now He sends them. And the power was His, handed to them: “he gave them power.” He never sends a man without giving him what he needs for the job.

Look at the two lit up. Matthew had worked the tax booth for Rome's side. “Canaanite” here comes from an Aramaic word for zealous: Simon was a Zealot, one of the men who wanted Rome thrown out, by force if it came to that. Jesus put both of them at the same table.

No priests, no scholars, no rich men. Working men, several with a past. That's who Jesus picked to carry His message. He still does.

Where they came from

Most of the twelve were men of Galilee, the farm and fishing country in the north. Peter, Andrew, and Philip came from Bethsaida (John 1:44). James and John fished near Capernaum. Nathanael came from Cana (John 21:2).

Iscariot probably means “man of Kerioth,” a town in the south of Judaea. If so, Judas was the one southerner in the group.

Judaeans looked down on Galileans as country people with a funny accent (26:73). Jesus built His church on them.

Where to go, what to say, and what not to pack.

Israel first, then the world

Gentiles are everyone who isn't Jewish. The Samaritans lived in Samaria, between Judaea and Galilee, and Jews and Samaritans had been enemies for centuries. On this first trip, Jesus kept the twelve among their own people. God's promises had come to Israel first.

Nobody charged us for grace, so they weren't to charge for healing. What we got for nothing, we give for nothing: our time, a ride, our story.

Travel light

“Purses” were money belts. “Brass” means copper coins. A “scrip” was a traveller's bag for food, and “staves” are walking sticks.

  • gold, silver, or copperno money belt
  • a scrip for the roadno packed lunch
  • two coats, spare shoes, extra stavesno backups
  • “the workman is worthy of his meat”God would feed them through the people they served

That was the plan for this trip. Later, Jesus told them to take a purse and a scrip (Luke 22:35–36). The lesson stays the same: He takes care of the men who work for Him.

Offer peace, and let them choose

To “salute” a house is to greet it: “Peace be to this house” (Luke 10:5). A “worthy” house is one willing to take them in.

Shaking off the dust said, “We've done our part. The choice is yours.” Sodom and Gomorrha were burned for their wickedness (Genesis 19), yet a town that turns away Christ's own messengers will have it worse. More light means more to answer for.

You can't make another man get saved, or get sober. You offer. If he says no, your peace comes back to you, and you go on.

Part three

Sheep among wolves

Verses 16–31

Hard days ahead, and a Father who counts every hair.

Wise and harmless

A sheep can't outfight a wolf. Jesus sends His men out anyway, and He doesn't hide the danger. “Behold, I send you.” He goes with what He sends.

Wise as serpents: see the trap before you step in it. A man in recovery doesn't go back to the old bar “to witness.”

Harmless as doves: no hidden angle, no payback. When they bite, you don't bite back.

It happened just as He said

You don't need a script to tell what Jesus did for you. The Holy Spirit lives in every believer from the day he's saved, and He gives the words.

He that endureth

Does holding on earn salvation?NoSalvation is a gift, received by faith in Jesus. Enduring doesn't buy it. It shows it. Real faith keeps going when it costs something.
Will God keep the ones He saves?Yes“He which hath begun a good work in you will perform it” (Philippians 1:6). We hold on because He holds us.

“Till the Son of man be come” (v. 23) is a hard verse, and good Bible teachers read it different ways. What's plain: there will always be more towns to reach, right up until He comes.

What they called Him

Baal-zebub the god of Ekron (2 Kings 1:2) “lord of flies”

By Jesus' day the name had become a name for Satan, “the prince of the devils.” The Pharisees had already said Jesus worked for him (9:34). If they called the Master of the house that, His servants shouldn't be surprised when the insults come their way.

When the old crowd mocks your new life, you're in good company.

Part four

Whose side are you on?

Verses 32–42

Owning Him in public, what it may cost at home, and a cup of cold water.

Confess or deny

Confess

“He's mine, and I'm His”

To say it openly, at work, at home, in front of the old crowd. And Jesus says the same about you to His Father.

Deny

“I don't know Him”

Jesus means a man who disowns Him for good, who wants no part of Him.

Peter denied Him three times in one night. Then:

“And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.”

Luke 22:61

Peter wept, and Jesus restored him (John 21:15–17). A believer who stumbles isn't thrown out. He's brought back.

A sword through the family

Micah 7:6

For the son dishonoureth the father, the daughter riseth up against her mother, the daughter in law against her mother in law; a man’s enemies are the men of his own house.

Matthew 10:35–36

For I am come to set a man at variance against his father, and the daughter against her mother… And a man’s foes shall be they of his own household.

Jesus quotes Micah. The “sword” isn't violence. It's a dividing line. Jesus gives peace with God (Romans 5:1), but following Him can split a home when some follow and some don't.

Some of you have family who don't understand your faith, or who liked you better the old way. Love them and honor them. Just don't let anyone talk you out of Christ.

Take up the cross

Men in Galilee had seen Romans crucify rebels, each condemned man carrying his own crossbeam to the place he'd die. To take up your cross is to stop running your own life and follow Him. It isn't how a man gets saved. It's how a saved man follows.

“findeth his life”→“shall lose it”hold on tight to the old life, and it slips away
“loseth his life for my sake”→“shall find it”hand it over to Him, and find what life was meant to be

“And he said to them all, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me.”

Luke 9:23

A cup of cold water

a man who welcomes youwelcomesJesusandthe Father who sent Him

Jesus stands so close to His people that how you treat them is how you treat Him. “These little ones” are His plain, ordinary followers, the least of them.

Not even a cup of water given for His sake is forgotten. A phone call to the new guy, a ride to a meeting, a cup of coffee and an hour of listening. God keeps the record, and “in no wise” means it won't be lost.

Question 1 of 6

Called

What kind of men did Jesus choose to send?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Ordinary working men: fishermen, a tax collector who had served Rome, a Zealot who wanted Rome gone. Not religious leaders, not rich men. He gave them His own power and sent them. He still uses men with a past.

Question 2 of 6

Grace

Why should we give freely?

What verses tell us about this?

“Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus:”

Romans 3:24

Answer

Because that's how we received. God forgave us freely, by grace, and Jesus paid what we owed. So we pass it on the same way, without a price tag: our time, our help, our story. And God doesn't forget even a cup of cold water.

Question 3 of 6

Wisdom

How should a believer act among people who are against him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Wise as a serpent: see the danger and don't walk into traps, and move on when it's time. Harmless as a dove: no hidden motives, no striking back. And don't panic about what to say. The Spirit of our Father lives in us and gives the words.

Question 4 of 6

Fear

Why don't we have to be afraid?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because men can only touch the body, and our souls are in God's hands. Not one cheap sparrow falls without the Father, and He has counted every hair on our heads. We're worth more to Him than many sparrows. The man who fears God has nobody else to fear.

Question 5 of 6

Confession

What does it mean to confess Jesus before men?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To own Him openly: to say in front of people that you belong to Jesus, and not hide it. Whoever owns Him, He owns before His Father in heaven. Peter failed at it once and was restored, so a stumble isn't the end. Keep owning Him.

Question 6 of 6

The cross

What does it mean to take up our cross and follow Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said to them all, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me.”

Luke 9:23

Answer

It means dying to running our own lives, every day, and following Him wherever He leads. It's not how we're saved. It's how a saved man lives. The life we cling to, we lose. The life we hand to Jesus, we find.
