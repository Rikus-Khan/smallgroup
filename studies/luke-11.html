Luke 11

Lord, teach us to pray

Jesus teaches His men to pray and to keep on asking, warns about a house swept clean and left empty, and tells the Pharisees the inside of the cup is what counts.

Part one

Lord, teach us to pray

Verses 1–13

The disciples watched Him pray, and they wanted what He had.

The one thing they asked Him to teach

They'd seen Him heal and preach, but what they asked to learn was prayer. They'd watched Him pray at His baptism (3:21), alone (5:16), all night (6:12), and on the mount (9:29).

  1. Our Fatherwho we talk to
  2. Hallowed be thy nameHis name kept holy
  3. Thy kingdom come, thy will be doneHis rule, His way
  4. Daily breadtoday's needs
  5. Forgive usas we forgive
  6. Lead us notkeep us from the trap

It starts with God, then asks for what we need. Matthew gives a longer form of the same prayer from the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 6:9–13). Jesus likely taught it more than once.

One day at a time

Not a year's supply. Bread for today. Israel learned that in the wilderness:

“Then said the LORD unto Moses, Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you; and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no.”

Exodus 16:4

Every man in recovery knows the phrase one day at a time. It's in the Lord's prayer. You don't have to carry next month today. Ask for today's bread, today's strength, and come back tomorrow.

Forgive us, and keep us

“For we also forgive.” We don't earn forgiveness by forgiving. But a man who knows how much he's been forgiven can't keep holding a grudge against everyone else.

“Lead us not into temptation” asks God to steer us away from the test we'd fail, and to pull us out when the evil one comes. Pray it every morning. Then don't walk into the places you just asked Him to keep you from.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

A knock at midnight

Outside, knockingFriend, lend me three loaves; for a friend of mine in his journey is come to me, and I have nothing to set before him.

Inside, in bedTrouble me not: the door is now shut, and my children are with me in bed; I cannot rise and give thee.
he keeps knocking

A guest at your door had to be fed; that was a point of honour. Village families baked each day's bread that morning, so by midnight it was gone. A poor family slept together on mats in one room, and the door was barred for the night. Getting up meant waking everybody.

Importunity

ἀναίδεια said anaideia “shamelessness”: not ashamed to keep asking

Jesus isn't saying God is a grumpy neighbour you have to wear down. He's arguing from the lesser to the greater. If a tired man in bed will get up for a friend who won't quit knocking, how much more will your Father, who never sleeps and loves you?

The words in Greek mean keep on asking, keep on seeking, keep on knocking. That's the midnight friend again: don't quit.

And it's for “every one that asketh.” Not just the good pray-ers or the men who've been at it for years. Whosoever.

How much more

An earthly father, “being evil”

Still gives good things

Bread, not a stone. A fish, not a snake. An egg, not a scorpion.

“Your heavenly Father”

Gives the Holy Spirit

The best gift there is: God Himself, living with His people.

The disciples were told to ask. Since Pentecost (Acts 2), every believer receives the Spirit the moment he believes. Today we don't beg for Him to come. We ask Him to lead, to fill and to change us.

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

Part two

A stronger than he

Verses 14–28

A mute man talks, and the critics say the devil did it.

Beelzebub

בַּעַל זְבוּב said Baal-zebub “lord of the flies”

“And Ahaziah fell down through a lattice in his upper chamber that was in Samaria, and was sick: and he sent messengers, and said unto them, Go, enquire of Baal-zebub the god of Ekron whether I shall recover of this disease.”

2 Kings 1:2

He was an old Philistine idol. By Jesus' day the name stood for the prince of devils himself. A dumb man (one who couldn't speak) was talking, and the critics couldn't deny it. So they called it the devil's work, and others asked for a sign, right after seeing one.

The finger of God

First, it doesn't make sense: Satan doesn't tear down his own house. A gang that fights itself falls apart. And their own sons, Jewish men who cast out devils, would judge that charge.

Then Jesus borrows words from Egypt. When Moses' plagues went past anything Pharaoh's magicians could copy, even they had to admit it:

“Then the magicians said unto Pharaoh, This is the finger of God: and Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, and he hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had said.”

Exodus 8:19

Pharaoh's own magicians saw it. These men wouldn't.

The strong man and the Stronger

Satan is the strong man, armed, guarding what he's taken. His goods are people he holds. Jesus is the Stronger. He breaks in, strips the armour, and takes back the spoils.

Some of us have had a strong man in the house for years: a bottle, a pipe, a rage, a habit we couldn't break. There is a Stronger. “Greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world” (1 John 4:4).

And no one stays neutral. With Him, or against Him.

Swept and garnished

The spirit leaves, wanders, and comes back to check. The house is clean, put in order (garnished), and empty. Nobody new moved in. So he brings seven worse, and the man ends up worse off than before.

Many of us have lived this. We cleaned up: quit, got sober for a while, stopped the old crowd. But nothing filled the space, and the old thing came back with friends.

Clean is not the same as filled. A believer's house isn't empty: the Holy Spirit lives there. Feed that, daily. “Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh” (Galatians 5:16).

“Yea rather”

A woman in the crowd called out a blessing on Jesus' mother. Jesus didn't put Mary down. She herself said, “all generations shall call me blessed” (Luke 1:48). But He pointed past her to a blessing any of us can have.

It isn't who you're related to or who you know. It's hearing God's word and doing it.

“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.”

James 1:22

Part three

A greater than Jonas is here

Verses 29–36

The crowd wants a sign. Jesus says they already have more than enough.

The sign of Jonas

Jonah came to Nineveh as a man who'd been brought back from the belly of the deep. That was his sign. Matthew records Jesus spelling it out:

“For as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale’s belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth.”

Matthew 12:40

The sign Jesus gave wasn't a trick on demand. It was His own death and rising from the grave. That's the sign we still point to.

Two outsiders who listened

  1. The queen of the southtravels to hear Solomonabout 950 BC
  2. Ninevehrepents at Jonah's preachingabout 760 BC
  3. “A greater … is here”Jesus, among His own peopleabout AD 29

The queen of Sheba, likely from southern Arabia, came well over a thousand miles to hear wise words (1 Kings 10:1). The Ninevites were heathen enemies of Israel who believed one short sermon from a reluctant prophet (Jonah 3:5).

Both had far less than this crowd had. On judgment day they'll stand up as witnesses. The Wisdom of God and the Prophet of prophets was standing right there, and the crowd wanted a sign.

The light is there. What about the eye?

A candle was a small clay oil lamp. Nobody lights one to hide it under a bushel, a grain basket. Jesus wasn't hiding. The light was out in the open. The trouble was in the people looking.

“When thine eye is single”

Full of light

Clear and undivided, set on one thing. The light gets in.

“When thine eye is evil”

Full of darkness

Clouded by greed, pride or a hard heart. It shuts the light out.

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Part four

Woe unto you

Verses 37–54

Dinner at a Pharisee's house, and Jesus doesn't wash up the way His host expects.

Not about clean hands

This washing wasn't about germs. It was a religious ritual, pouring water over the hands a certain way before eating. The Law of Moses didn't require it of ordinary people. It came from rules the teachers had added.

“For the Pharisees, and all the Jews, except they wash their hands oft, eat not, holding the tradition of the elders.”

Mark 7:3

The host was shocked that Jesus skipped it. Jesus used the moment to talk about a different kind of clean.

The outside of the cup

Nobody wants a cup that's polished outside and filthy inside. But that's what they were: careful about what people could see, and full of ravening (greed, grabbing) underneath.

God made both the outside and the inside, and He looks at both. “Man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart” (1 Samuel 16:7).

Then give from the inside out: alms, gifts to the poor, from a changed heart. A man can clean up his outside for court or for his family. Only God can clean the inside.

Counting leaves

Counted carefully

Mint and rue

A tenth of every garden herb, leaf by leaf, given to God.

Passed right over

Judgment and the love of God

Doing right by people, and loving God with all the heart.

The law said to give God a tenth, a tithe. They tithed even the kitchen herbs and felt holy doing it. Jesus doesn't tell them to stop. “These ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.” The small things don't make up for skipping the big ones.

They also loved the best seats and being greeted with respect in the markets, the public squares.

Graves you can't see

Under the Law, touching a grave made a man unclean:

“And whosoever toucheth one that is slain with a sword in the open fields, or a dead body, or a bone of a man, or a grave, shall be unclean seven days.”

Numbers 19:16

So graves were marked to warn people off. An unmarked grave was a trap: you could walk over one and never know you'd been defiled. The men who looked the holiest were the ones spreading the uncleanness, and the people following them had no idea.

Heavy loads, and fine tombs

The lawyers, experts in the Law, piled rule on rule. To lade is to load a pack animal. They loaded men down and wouldn't lift a finger to help them carry it.

Compare what Jesus offers the same tired men:

“For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.”

Matthew 11:30

They also built handsome sepulchres, tombs, for the prophets their fathers had killed. It's easy to honour a dead prophet. They were planning to kill the living One standing in front of them.

From Abel to Zacharias

  1. Abelthe first murderGenesis 4:8
  2. Zachariasstoned in the temple court2 Chronicles 24:21, about 800 BC
  3. “This generation”the Son Himself is rejectedabout AD 30
  4. Jerusalem fallsRome destroys the templeAD 70

The Hebrew Bible starts with Genesis and ends with Chronicles. So “from Abel to Zacharias” means from the first book to the last: every innocent man killed in the whole Old Testament.

“And they conspired against him, and stoned him with stones at the commandment of the king in the court of the house of the LORD.”

2 Chronicles 24:21

About forty years after Jesus said this, Roman armies destroyed Jerusalem and its temple.

The key of knowledge

They held the Scriptures, the key to knowing God. They wouldn't go through the door themselves, and they stood in the doorway so nobody else could either.

From here on, they lay wait for Him, hunting for a slip of the tongue to use against Him. The road to the cross is getting shorter. And He kept telling the truth anyway.

Religious people can be the hardest to reach, and sometimes the ones who hurt us most. Don't let a hypocrite stand between you and Jesus. The door is open: He is the door.

Question 1 of 6

Prayer

What did Jesus teach His disciples to ask for when they pray?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

First for God: that His name be kept holy, His kingdom come, His will be done. Then for us: bread day by day, forgiveness as we forgive others, and to be kept from temptation and delivered from evil.

Question 2 of 6

Keep asking

What did Jesus promise to every one that asketh, seeketh, and knocketh?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That he receives, he finds, and the door is opened to him. Jesus tells us to keep on asking, like the friend at midnight who wouldn't quit knocking. And the promise is for every one, not a special few.

Question 3 of 6

A good Father

What does the heavenly Father give to them that ask Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

Answer

The Holy Spirit. If an earthly father won't hand his son a scorpion, how much more will our Father give His best. Today every believer receives the Spirit when he believes, and is sealed by Him.

Question 4 of 6

The empty house

What happened to the house that was swept clean but left empty?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The unclean spirit came back, found it swept and garnished, and brought seven worse spirits with him. The man ended up worse than before. Cleaning up isn't enough; the life has to be filled with Christ and His Spirit.

Question 5 of 6

Truly blessed

Who did Jesus say is truly blessed?

What verses tell us about this?

“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.”

James 1:22

Answer

“They that hear the word of God, and keep it.” Not just the people closest to Him by family, but anyone who listens to God's word and does what it says.

Question 6 of 6

Inside out

What did Jesus say about the outside and the inside of the cup?

What verses tell us about this?

“Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.”

Psalm 51:10

Answer

The Pharisees kept the outside spotless, but inside they were full of greed and wickedness. God made the inside too, and He looks at the heart. Only He can make it clean.
