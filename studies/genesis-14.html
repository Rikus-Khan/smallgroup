Genesis 14

The rescue, and the priest-king

Four kings from the east sweep down on Sodom and carry off Lot. Abram goes after him and wins, and a mysterious priest meets him with bread and wine, a picture of Jesus.

Part one

War comes to the plain

Verses 1–12

Lot chose the green valley. Now the valley is at war, and Lot is in the middle of it.

Four kings against five

From the east (verse 1)

Four kings

Amraphel of Shinar (Babylonia), Arioch of Ellasar, Tidal, and Chedorlaomer of Elam, the leader. For twelve years the five cities served him.

The cities of the plain (verse 2)

Five kings

Bera of Sodom, Birsha of Gomorrah, Shinab of Admah, Shemeber of Zeboiim, and the king of Bela, “which is Zoar.” In the thirteenth year they rebelled.

A vale is a valley. The vale of Siddim is where the five kings made their stand. “Which is the salt sea” says that when Moses wrote, the valley was already part of the Dead Sea. Many think it lay at the south end.

The kings' sweep

Chedorlaomer didn't just attack the five cities. He first beat the peoples on the way: the Rephaims, Zuzims and Emims (old peoples of the land, known for their size), the Horites in Mount Seir, and the Amalekites and Amorites.

Not every place can be pinned down today. This is the likely path. Smote means struck, defeated. Hazezon-tamar was later called En-gedi.

He was clearing every enemy off his flank before turning on Sodom. Sodom's turn was last.

Slimepits

חֵמָר said chemar “tar,” “asphalt”: a slimepit is a tar pit

It is the same word as the “slime” the builders of Babel used for mortar:

“And they had brick for stone, and slime had they for morter.”

Genesis 11:3

The valley was full of sticky black pits (morter is mortar, the glue between bricks). The two kings who ran fled right into them, and the soldiers who were left ran for the hills. A beaten man runs, and doesn't always look where.

Lot wasn't fighting. He just lived where the fighting was.

Verse 12 has a quiet detail: Lot “dwelt in Sodom.” In chapter 13 he pitched his tent toward it. Now he lives inside it, and when Sodom lost the war, he lost with it. Victuals are food and supplies, and the kings took them, and Lot's goods, and Lot.

A believer can end up carried off by someone else's war, simply by where he chose to stand. Sin has a way of dragging in the people around it. That includes the believer who never meant any harm.

“He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed.”

Proverbs 13:20

Part two

Abram goes after Lot

Verses 13–16

Lot had walked away from Abram. Abram went after him anyway.

“Abram the Hebrew”

עִבְרִי said Ivri “Hebrew”: perhaps “from the other side” (of the river), or a descendant of Eber

It is the first time the word Hebrew appears in the Bible. To the Amorites around him Abram was the outsider from across the river, a man who lived in tents and kept an altar.

He had friends anyway. Mamre, Eshcol and Aner were confederate with him, which means allies, bound by a treaty. And he had a household of fighting men, “trained servants, born in his own house.” He called Lot his brother (verse 14), not “that fool.”

Three hundred and eighteen

Abram's trained men, against four kings

Four kings had just beaten the whole country. Abram had 318 men and the allies from Mamre's camp. He divided his men and hit them by night, and chased them “unto Hobah, which is on the left hand of Damascus,” that is, north of Damascus, a long way north of Sodom's valley.

The chase north

Red: the four kings come down to the vale of Siddim and take Lot.

Blue: Abram goes up from Hebron by night after them, as far as Dan and on past Damascus, and brings back everything, “and also brought again his brother Lot.”

Lot's losses, brought back

What Lot lost (14:11–12)

BROUGHT BACKall of it, verse 16

Abram went after the man who had walked away.

Lot had chosen his own road. Abram risked his life for him anyway. That is a small picture of a bigger rescue:

“For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.”

Luke 19:10

There's a real-world job in this one. Is there a brother who has drifted, who stopped coming, or who is back in some Sodom? Call or text him today. Don't lecture him. Tell him you've been thinking about him. And if you're the one who has drifted: let somebody come after you.

Part three

Two kings, two offers

Verses 17–24

A king of peace brings bread. A king of Sodom brings a deal.

Melchizedek

מַלְכִּי־צֶדֶק said Malki-tsedeq “king of righteousness”

Salem means peace, and it is an old name for Jerusalem. A dale is a valley. The writer of Hebrews gives the meaning of both names. By interpretation means when they are translated:

“To whom also Abraham gave a tenth part of all; first being by interpretation King of righteousness, and after that also King of Salem, which is, King of peace;”

Hebrews 7:2

A priest, and a king, who just appears

What Genesis tells us

Very little

He is king of Salem and priest of the most high God. He brings bread and wine, blesses Abram, and takes a tenth. Then he's gone. No parents, no family tree and no death are recorded.

What Hebrews 7 adds

A picture of Jesus

Because Genesis names no beginning and no end, he pictures a priest who never ends. And the greater one blesses the lesser, so Abraham's own priest-king stands above Abraham.

“And without all contradiction the less is blessed of the better.”

Hebrews 7:7

Contradiction means dispute: nobody argues about it.

Priest for ever

“The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.”

Psalm 110:4

King David wrote that centuries later, about the coming Messiah. Jesus came from the tribe of Judah, not from the priests' tribe of Levi. So how could he be a priest? Hebrews 7 answers: after the order of Melchizedek, not Aaron. His priesthood doesn't end with death. (Will not repent means will not change his mind.)

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Wherefore means therefore, intercession is pleading for someone, and to the uttermost means completely. Right now Jesus is alive and speaking to the Father for you. A man who fell last night has a priest at the right hand of God who knows what temptation is (Hebrews 4:15).

Bread and wine

Abram's men had fought all night and chased for miles. The first thing the priest-king did was feed them and bless them. Hebrews doesn't say what the bread and wine mean, but they are a picture of the Lord's Supper, which Jesus gave his disciples (brake means broke):

“And as they were eating, Jesus took bread, and blessed it, and brake it, and gave it to the disciples, and said, Take, eat; this is my body.”

Matthew 26:26

Wine is a hard word for many of us. This verse isn't about drinking. It's about a priest meeting worn-out men with a meal and a blessing, before anything else. Don't read a permission slip into it.

A tenth of the winnings

Tithes means “tenths.”

Hebrews 7:4 says it was a tenth “of the spoils,” out of the battle's haul, not his paycheck. And the law came four hundred and thirty years after the promise (Galatians 3:17). Nobody told Abram to. He gave because of what he said in the same breath: God “hath delivered thine enemies into thy hand.” Possessor means owner: the most high God owns heaven and earth, so the victory was his. In the verse below, purposeth means decides.

Argue about how much, and when, some other day. Abram's heart was thanks. God doesn't need our money, and there's no deal where we give and he pays us back.

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7

Not a thread

The king of SodomGive me the people. You keep the goods.

AbramI have lifted my hand to the LORD, the most high God. I won't take a thread or a shoelatchet of yours, so you can never say, “I made Abram rich.” no thanks

“Lift up mine hand” is the old way of swearing an oath. A shoelatchet is a sandal strap, the smallest thing there is. God had already made Abram rich (13:2), and he wouldn't let Sodom's king claim the credit.

Notice the manner. He was firm about himself and fair to others: his allies Aner, Eshcol and Mamre were free to take their share, and his young men had eaten what they'd eaten (verse 24).

Some offers come with strings.

Sodom's king meant it kindly, and it would have cost Abram something. Today something will be offered to you: easy money, a favour, a loan, a ride, a drink from the old crowd. If it ties you back to your old life, you can say no, politely, like Abram.

“But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition.”

1 Timothy 6:9

Perdition is ruin. And if you've already taken what you shouldn't have, you're not finished. You have a priest forever who is able to save to the uttermost.

Points to remember

  1. Choices“They took Lot… who dwelt in Sodom” 14:12Where we live and who we stand with drag us into their wars, even when we never meant any harm.
  2. Rescue“He… pursued them” 14:14Abram went after a brother who had walked away. Go after yours.
  3. God's help“Hath delivered thine enemies into thy hand” 14:20The victory belongs to the LORD, the owner of heaven and earth.
  4. Christ“Priest of the most high God” 14:18Melchizedek pictures Jesus, our priest forever, who ever lives to pray for us.
  5. Giving“He gave him tithes of all” 14:20Abram gave out of thanks to the God who gave the victory.
  6. Integrity“Lest thou shouldest say, I have made Abram rich” 14:23Take nothing that ties you to Sodom, and let God have the credit.

Question 1 of 6

Choices

What did Lot's choice cost him?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed.”

Proverbs 13:20

Answer

His goods, his freedom, and his place: he was carried off with the losers of the war. Lot was living in Sodom by then, so when Sodom lost, Lot lost. His choice in chapter 13 had its price.

Question 2 of 6

Rescue

What did Abram do when he heard Lot was taken?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.”

Luke 19:10

Answer

He armed 318 trained men and went after him by night. He called Lot his brother even though Lot had walked away, and brought back him and everything he had. Jesus did the same for us, and he sends us after our brothers.

Question 3 of 6

God's help

Who gave Abram the victory?

What verses tell us about this?

“The horse is prepared against the day of battle: but safety is of the LORD.”

Proverbs 21:31

Answer

God did. 318 men, even by night, against four kings, could not have done it alone. Abram and Melchizedek both said so. We plan, we prepare, we show up. The victory is the LORD's.

Question 4 of 6

Christ

Who is Melchizedek, and who does he point to?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.”

Psalm 110:4

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Answer

A king of Salem (peace) and priest of the most high God, who met Abram and blessed him. Genesis gives no beginning or end, so he pictures a priest who never ends. That is Jesus, our priest forever, who lives to pray for those who come to God through him.

Question 5 of 6

Giving

What did Abram give to Melchizedek?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now consider how great this man was, unto whom even the patriarch Abraham gave the tenth of the spoils.”

Hebrews 7:4

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7

Answer

A tenth of everything he had won. This was long before the law of Moses, and nobody had told him to. It was thanks to the God who gave the victory, and it showed who he thought was greater.

Question 6 of 6

Integrity

Why would Abram take nothing from the king of Sodom?

What verses tell us about this?

“But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many foolish and hurtful lusts, which drown men in destruction and perdition.”

1 Timothy 6:9

Answer

Because he had sworn to the LORD, and because God had made him rich, not Sodom. He wouldn't owe a thing to a wicked city, or let its king take credit for his wealth. He was still fair to his allies.
