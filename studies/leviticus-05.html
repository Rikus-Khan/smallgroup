Leviticus 5

Guilty when you didn't know, and a way in for the poor man

Saying nothing, touching what you didn't know was unclean, and speaking an oath too quickly: each is sin, and each has the same road back. Confess, bring what you can, and “it shall be forgiven him.”

Part one

“He shall confess that he hath sinned”

Verses 1–13

Sins of silence, sins that hide, and an offering at every price.

The sin of saying nothing

A man hears an oath sworn aloud. He is a witness: “he hath seen or known of it.” And he says nothing. “If he do not utter it,” the LORD says, “he shall bear his iniquity.”

  • swearinga solemn oath spoken aloud
  • utterspeak out, say it
  • bear his iniquitycarry his own guilt

Nobody here lied or stole. The man did nothing, and the LORD still calls it sin. He knew a thing, and he kept it back.

“Whoso is partner with a thief hateth his own soul: he heareth cursing, and bewrayeth it not.”

Proverbs 29:24

“Therefore to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin.”

James 4:17

Many of us have kept quiet: about a friend using, about harm done to someone weaker, about their own part in a thing. The way out of silence is to speak, to the right person: a pastor, a brother you trust.

Guilty, and he did not know

Verses 2 and 3

Touching what was unclean

A dead beast, or any uncleanness of man. “It be hid from him”: he did not know he had touched it. Afterward, “when he knoweth of it, then he shall be guilty.”

Verse 4

A word said too quickly

Swearing with his lips “to do evil, or to do good,” and it was “hid from him.” He spoke it and forgot, or never thought what it meant, and when he knows, he is guilty.

Carcase is a dead body, and creeping things are the small creatures that crawl. Withal means with it. The point of the verses is the same in all three: it was hid from him, and then he knew.

Look at when guilt arrives: “when he knoweth of it.” God shows a man what he has done. Conviction is not an enemy. It is the first step of the road home.

Men in recovery know the rash promise, made in a moment of shame and broken by the weekend. When you see the word you broke, don't hide from it: own it before the LORD, and take the next step.

Confess first

  1. he knows“when he knoweth of it”verses 3 to 4
  2. he confesses“that he hath sinned in that thing”verse 5
  3. he bringsa lamb or a kidverse 6

The confession comes first, before the animal. “In that thing” means he names it: not “I'm a sinner, generally,” but the thing he did. The offering is for a particular sin, and so is the confession.

“I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin.”

Psalm 32:5

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

The word trespass in verse 6 means a sin that wrongs someone, here the LORD himself. The chapter calls the same offering a “trespass offering” and a “sin offering” in the same verse. The text uses both names for it, so we do not split them any further than it does.

Two birds

  • turtledovessmall wild doves
  • young pigeonsthe young of the pigeon
  • wring offtwist the head from the neck (verse 8)
  • asunderapart: the head was not cut right off

If a man could not bring a lamb, he brought two birds, which cost far less. One was the sin offering, and one the burnt offering. “According to the manner” (verse 10) means the way it is commanded: the sin first, then the whole offering to the LORD.

And the verse ends as the others do: “the priest shall make an atonement for him for his sin which he hath sinned, and it shall be forgiven him.” A small offering, the same forgiveness.

An offering at every price

fine floura tenth part of an ephahverse 11, about two quarts

If a man had no lamb and no two birds, he brought a handful of the flour, with no oil and no frankincense, “for it is a sin offering.” The priest burned a handful as a memorial: a reminder before the LORD. The remnant, what was left, was the priest's, as a meat offering: meat here means a food offering of flour.

And the end of verse 13: “it shall be forgiven him.” The poorest man who could bring nothing but flour was forgiven as surely as the man with a lamb.

When Jesus was a baby, Mary and Joseph brought the offering of the poor: two birds (Luke 2:24). “Though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich” (2 Corinthians 8:9). He came down to where the poor man stands.

Part two

“Make amends for the harm”

Verses 14–19

The holy things, and the guilt of the sin you did not know.

Make it right, and add a fifth

A man sinned “in the holy things of the LORD”: something set apart for the LORD, which he treated as common. The offering was a ram without blemish, valued in silver, “after the shekel of the sanctuary.” And he also paid back what he had taken from the holy thing, and a fifth more.

Amends means making up for it. Estimation means the value put upon the animal: priced in shekels of silver by the shekel of the sanctuary, the sanctuary's own standard. A fifth part is one part in five, so two more for every ten.

Notice that the offering and the repair go together. A man cannot be forgiven in his heart and leave the damage where it lies. Chapter 6 shows this most clearly.

“Though he wist it not”

Wist is an old word for knew. Verse 17 says it flat out: a man “though he wist it not, yet is he guilty, and shall bear his iniquity.” Guilt is not what a man feels. It is what is true about what he did.

Verse 17

Guilty

“Though he wist it not.” Not knowing does not make the thing not have happened.

Verse 18

Forgiven

“Concerning his ignorance wherein he erred and wist it not, and it shall be forgiven him.” The very next verse names the remedy.

“He hath certainly trespassed against the LORD” (verse 19). Every sin, even one done to a man, is first against the LORD: “Against thee, thee only, have I sinned” (Psalm 51:4). That is why a man can be forgiven even when the person he wronged is gone.

“Yet it pleased the LORD to bruise him; he hath put him to grief: when thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin, he shall see his seed, he shall prolong his days, and the pleasure of the LORD shall prosper in his hand.”

Isaiah 53:10

Isaiah, looking ahead to Christ, says his soul would be made an offering for sin. The final offering for the guilty was prepared from the beginning.

Real world: you have something to bring

This chapter was written for the man who says, “I have nothing to bring.” Not a lamb, not two birds. The LORD made a way even for flour.

  • Name itConfess “that thing” by name, not just “I'm a mess.” Say it to the LORD today (1 John 1:9).
  • Speak, don't stay silentIf you know something that is hurting someone, tell a brother in Christ or your pastor.
  • Make it rightWhere you took something, pay it back and add to it. Ask a brother to help you work out how.

You do not have to be able to pay for it. Christ already did. What he asks is the honest confession, and a heart that wants to make it right.

Points to remember

  1. Silence“if he do not utter it, then he shall bear his iniquity” 5:1Keeping quiet about what you know is a sin, and the way out is to speak.
  2. Hidden“it be hid from him; when he knoweth of it, then he shall be guilty” 5:3The LORD shows a man what he did. That is the first step home.
  3. Confess“he shall confess that he hath sinned in that thing” 5:5Name the thing, first, and then bring the offering.
  4. The poor man“the tenth part of an ephah of fine flour for a sin offering” 5:11An offering at every price. The man with flour was forgiven as surely as the man with a lamb.
  5. Amends“make amends for the harm that he hath done… and shall add the fifth part thereto” 5:16Put right the harm, and a fifth more.
  6. Guilty anyway“though he wist it not, yet is he guilty” 5:17Guilt is what is true, not what he felt, and the same chapter gives the remedy.

Question 1 of 6

Silence

What sin does a man commit who sees or knows something, and says nothing?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin.”

James 4:17

Answer

He is a witness who does not “utter it,” and the LORD says “he shall bear his iniquity.” Silence about what we know can be a sin, and the way out is to speak to the right person.

Question 2 of 6

Hidden

What if a man did something wrong and it was hid from him? When does he become guilty?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“When he knoweth of it, then he shall be guilty.” The LORD brings it to his knowledge, and that is when he is to confess and bring his offering.

Question 3 of 6

Confess

What must a man do before he brings his offering?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

He must confess that he hath sinned “in that thing”: name it, and then bring the offering. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins.”

Question 4 of 6

The poor man

What could a man bring if he could not afford a lamb, or even two birds?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Two turtledoves or young pigeons, and if he could not afford those, a tenth part of an ephah of fine flour. And the poorest was forgiven too: “it shall be forgiven him.”

Question 5 of 6

Amends

What did a man have to add when he had wronged the holy things of the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He had to make amends for the harm and add “the fifth part thereto,” and give it to the priest, and bring a ram without blemish for a trespass offering.

Question 6 of 6

Guilty anyway

Is a man guilty if he did not know he had sinned?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Yes: “though he wist it not, yet is he guilty.” And the next verse gives the remedy: a ram, the priest's atonement, and “it shall be forgiven him.”
