Obadiah 1

The pride of thine heart

The shortest book in the Old Testament: a proud nation cheers while its brother falls, and God says the high will come down and deliverance will come on mount Zion.

The book of Obadiah

Who, when, and to whom

Obadiah's name means “servant of the LORD.” That's all we know about him. His book is one chapter, 21 verses. He speaks about Edom, the nation next door, but he speaks for Judah, the southern kingdom, after Jerusalem had been looted and its people dragged away.

  1. Genesis 25Twins: Jacob (Israel) and Esau (Edom)
  2. Moses' dayEdom blocks Israel's way to the promised land
  3. 586 BCBabylon burns Jerusalem. Edom cheers and joins in
  4. soon after?Obadiah speaks
  5. after AD 70Edom is gone from history

Most Bible teachers put Obadiah soon after Babylon took Jerusalem, about 586 BC, because verses 11–14 sound just like that day. Some put him earlier, after a raid on Jerusalem in King Jehoram's day. Either way the message is the same: God saw what Edom did to its brother, and He will answer it.

Part one

Brought down

Verses 1–9

Edom thinks it's out of reach. God says nobody is.

Two brothers, two nations

Edom came from Esau. Esau's twin brother was Jacob, whose family became Israel. Before they were born, God told their mother:

“…Two nations are in thy womb, and two manner of people shall be separated from thy bowels…”

Genesis 25:23

Esau swore to kill Jacob (Genesis 27:41). They made peace, but the grudge outlived them. When Israel came out of Egypt, Edom met them with the sword (Numbers 20:18).

Now God sends word among the nations: rise up against Edom. The proud brother will be made small.

A nest among the stars

Edom lived high in red sandstone mountains, with homes and storerooms cut into the cliffs and only narrow gorges leading in. Their stronghold was called Sela, “the rock.” Many think it's the place later called Petra. They felt untouchable.

God's answer: as high as an eagle, as high as the stars, “thence will I bring thee down.” No height is out of His reach.

Pride lies

Notice who pride fooled first. Not the neighbors. “The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee.” Pride lies to the man who has it. Here are some of its favorite lines:

  • “Nobody can touch me.”Edom said it from the rocks
  • “I can handle it.”the line before a lot of falls
  • “I don't need anybody.”said by men who end up alone
  • “God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.”James 4:6

“Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.”

Proverbs 16:18

Plenty of us remember the day we said, “I've got this.” The way back up starts low: admitting we can't, and asking the God who gives grace to the humble.

Nothing left hidden

Thieves stop when they have enough. Grape pickers leave a few bunches behind. What's coming on Edom is worse than either.

  • Thieves by night“stolen till they had enough,” then gone
  • Grape gatherers“leave some grapes” behind
  • God's judgment“searched out,” every hidden thing “sought up”

Edom hid its treasure in caves deep in the rock. God says all of it will be found. Most of us know about hidden things too: the stash, the second phone, the account nobody knows about.

“…be sure your sin will find you out.”

Numbers 32:23

What comes into the light can finally be dealt with. Better to bring it out yourself, to God and to a brother, than to wait for it to be found.

Friends who weren't

A “confederacy” is an alliance. Edom counted on its allies and on its famous wise men (Teman was one of its regions, known for wisdom). Both let it down.

  1. allieswalked thee to the border and left
  2. men at peace with theedeceived thee
  3. men who eat thy breadlaid a trap under thee
  4. wise men“none understanding”

A “wound under thee” is a snare set where you'll step. The people at your own table set it. A lot of men know this one: the friends you used with, the crowd that was there for the party and gone for the crash.

Edom trusted its friends, its brains, and its rocks. None of them could hold it up. Only the LORD can carry the weight of a man's trust.

Part two

Thy brother Jacob

Verses 10–16

The day Jerusalem fell, Edom watched, cheered, and then joined in.

On the other side

When foreign armies broke into Jerusalem, Edom didn't lift a hand to help. A psalm remembers what they said that day:

“Remember, O LORD, the children of Edom in the day of Jerusalem; who said, Rase it, rase it, even to the foundation thereof.”

Psalm 137:7

Jesus told of a man beaten half to death, and a religious man who saw him and kept walking:

“And by chance there came down a certain priest that way: and when he saw him, he passed by on the other side.”

Luke 10:31

God doesn't count standing aside as staying neutral. “Even thou wast as one of them.” When your brother is going down, watching is a choice.

Eight steps down

God lists what Edom did, one “shouldest not” at a time. Watch where it starts, and where it ends.

  1. looked on(12)
  2. rejoiced(12)
  3. spoke proudly(12)
  4. went inthe gate (13)
  5. staredat their pain (13)
  6. lootedtheir goods (13)
  7. blockedthe road (14)
  8. handed overthe survivors (14)

It started with looking. It ended at the crossroads (the “crossway”), catching refugees as they ran and handing them to the enemy.

“Rejoice not when thine enemy falleth, and let not thine heart be glad when he stumbleth:”

Proverbs 24:17

Sin walks downstairs one step at a time. Stop at the top step, back when it was “only looking.”

As thou hast done

“The day of the LORD” is the day God steps in to judge and to set things right. And when it comes, Edom's own actions will come back around:

Edom→did→to his brotherLooked, laughed, looted, and handed him over.
it→shall be done→unto thee“Thy reward shall return upon thine own head.”

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

That's a warning about what we plant. It's also why God tells us to leave payback to Him. He sees it all, and He settles every account.

The cup

Edom partied on the ruins of God's holy mountain. Now God turns their party around: the nations will “drink” too, but what they drink is His judgment. The Bible often pictures God's judgment as a cup a man has to drink to the bottom.

That's not a picture to take lightly. It's also not the end of the story. The night before the cross, Jesus said this:

“Then said Jesus unto Peter, Put up thy sword into the sheath: the cup which my Father hath given me, shall I not drink it?”

John 18:11

Jesus drank the cup that sinners like us had coming, all of it. That's why anyone who believes on Him doesn't have to.

Part three

On mount Zion

Verses 17–21

Edom's mountain falls. God's mountain stands.

Two mountains

The mount of Esau

Brought down

High and proud. “Cut off for ever.” Stubble in a fire. “As though they had not been.”

Mount Zion

Deliverance

Holiness, and Jacob's family getting back what was taken from them: they “shall possess their possessions.”

That promise belongs to Israel, and God will keep it to Israel. What it shows us is God Himself: He gives back what was stolen from His people. Joel promised the same deliverance on mount Zion to “whosoever shall call on the name of the LORD” (Joel 2:32), and Paul opened that door to everyone:

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

God kept His word

  1. by about 300 BCArab tribes (the Nabataeans) take Edom's mountains
  2. about 125 BCThe Edomites, now called Idumeans, are swallowed up into Judaea
  3. about 37 BCHerod the Great, an Idumean, becomes king
  4. after AD 70Edom is never heard of again

Herod, the king who tried to kill the baby Jesus, came from Edom's line. After the Romans destroyed Jerusalem in AD 70, the Edomites vanish from history. Nobody today calls himself an Edomite. Jacob's people are still here.

“For the LORD hath spoken it.” When God says something, you can build your life on it. That goes for His warnings and for His promises.

Every border given back

Verses 19–20 walk around the map. Judah's people from the south take Edom's mountains. The lowlands take the Philistine coast. The north comes back: Ephraim and Samaria. Benjamin crosses the Jordan to Gilead. Exiles come home from as far as Zarephath and Sepharad, a far-off place of exile.

Everything the enemies took, God gives back to His people. Not one corner is forgotten.

And the kingdom shall be the LORD’S.

“Saviours” here means deliverers, like the judges God raised up in the old days, Gideon and the rest. Obadiah ends with God's people safe and God on the throne.

“For the kingdom is the LORD’S: and he is the governor among the nations.”

Psalm 22:28

“And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.”

Revelation 11:15

Edom had its day of laughing. Babylon had its day of burning. Neither had the last word. The last word belongs to the LORD and His Christ, and that's the side to be on.

Points to remember

  1. Pride“The pride of thine heart hath deceived thee” 1:3Pride lies to the man who has it first. God brings the high down and gives grace to the humble.
  2. Brothers“Even thou wast as one of them” 1:11Standing aside while a brother falls is taking part. And it starts with “only looking.”
  3. Reaping“As thou hast done, it shall be done unto thee” 1:15What we sow comes back. Jesus drank the cup of judgment for all who believe.
  4. Deliverance“Upon mount Zion shall be deliverance” 1:17God gives back what was taken. Whosoever calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved.
  5. The kingdom“The kingdom shall be the LORD’S” 1:21Proud nations rise and vanish. God keeps His word, and He has the last word.

Question 1 of 5

Pride

What does pride tell a man about himself?

What verses tell us about this?

“Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.”

Proverbs 16:18

“But he giveth more grace. Wherefore he saith, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.”

James 4:6

Answer

Pride tells a man he's out of reach: nobody can bring him down, he can handle it, he doesn't need anyone. It's a lie, and the first person it fools is the proud man himself. God brings down the high, but He gives grace to the humble. Getting low before God is the safest place a man can stand.

Question 2 of 5

Brothers

What does God think of standing by while a brother falls?

What verses tell us about this?

“Rejoice not when thine enemy falleth, and let not thine heart be glad when he stumbleth:”

Proverbs 24:17

“And by chance there came down a certain priest that way: and when he saw him, he passed by on the other side.”

Luke 10:31

Answer

God counts it as taking part: “even thou wast as one of them.” Edom didn't start the fire, but it watched, cheered, and helped itself. God wants the opposite from us: when a brother is going down, we step in, not back. And we never celebrate anyone's fall, not even an enemy's.

Question 3 of 5

Reaping

Does what a man does come back on him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

“Then said Jesus unto Peter, Put up thy sword into the sheath: the cup which my Father hath given me, shall I not drink it?”

John 18:11

Answer

Yes. What Edom did to Jacob came back on its own head. A man reaps what he sows. But here's the good news: Jesus drank the cup of judgment our sins earned. Whoever believes on Him is forgiven. Some earthly consequences may remain, but the judgment is gone, and from here on we can sow something better.

Question 4 of 5

Deliverance

Where is deliverance found?

What verses tell us about this?

“And it shall come to pass, that whosoever shall call on the name of the LORD shall be delivered: for in mount Zion and in Jerusalem shall be deliverance, as the LORD hath said, and in the remnant whom the LORD shall call.”

Joel 2:32

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

Not in the rocks, not in allies, not in our own smarts. Edom tried all three. Deliverance is found with the LORD, on His mountain. Paul takes Joel's promise and holds it out to everyone: whosoever calls on the name of the Lord shall be saved. That's anyone, and it's today.

Question 5 of 5

The kingdom

Who has the last word?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the kingdom is the LORD’S: and he is the governor among the nations.”

Psalm 22:28

“And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.”

Revelation 11:15

Answer

God does. Edom laughed and is gone. Babylon burned Jerusalem and is gone. What the LORD said came true, down to the last Edomite. One day every kingdom will belong to our Lord and His Christ. Whatever has the loudest voice in your life right now, it doesn't get the last word. He does.
