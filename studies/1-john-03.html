1 John 3

Sons of God

Look what kind of love this is: God calls us His sons, now. John is plain that a son of God doesn't live in sin as his way of life, and just as plain that when your own heart condemns you, God is greater than your heart.

Part one

Sons of God

Verses 1–3

John stops and points: “Behold.” Look at this. Look at what God calls you.

A new name

addict · inmate · failure → sons of God What manner of means what kind of, as if John found it foreign: love from another country. Nobody on earth loves like this.

Not servants who might be let go. Not guests on probation. Sons. “But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his name” (John 1:12).

The world knoweth us not: if old friends don't get the new you, don't be surprised. They didn't get Jesus either.

Now, not yet, then

nowSons of Godnow are we the sons of God
not yetStill being madeit doth not yet appear what we shall be
when He appearsLike Himwe shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is

You live in the middle panel. The struggle is real, the old habits still pull, and you don't look like much yet. But you're already a son, and the ending is already written.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Hope makes a man clean up.

A man whose bride is coming tomorrow doesn't leave the house a wreck. He's not cleaning to earn her; she already said yes. He cleans because she's coming.

That's the order: you're already His son (v. 1), He's coming (v. 2), so you get ready (v. 3). Hope isn't a reason to coast. It's the best reason there is to throw out what doesn't belong in a son's house.

Part two

Born of God

Verses 4–10

The hardest verses in the letter, and the most freeing, once you hear what John means.

He came to take it away

Sin is the transgression of the law: at bottom, sin is lawlessness, a man saying, “Nobody tells me what to do, not even God.”

And here's why Jesus came: to take away our sins. Not to manage them, or help us feel better about them: to take them away. He could, because in him is no sin. A drowning man can't save another; only someone on solid ground can pull you out.

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

“Destroy” means untie

λύσῃ said lysē, from luō to loose, untie, break up what was bound

It's the word Jesus used at Lazarus's tomb: “Loose him, and let him go” (John 11:44). The devil's works are knots: habits, chains, lies tied tight over years. Jesus came to untie them.

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

One frame, or the whole film?

Born of Godfalls, but fights and gets up

a fall1:9

Lives in itthe whole film dark, and fine with it

The verbs here are present tense: they mean keeps on doing it, as a way of life. Doth not commit sin means doesn't go on living in sin. It does not mean never sins. John already said, “If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves” (1:8), and “if any man sin, we have an advocate” (2:1).

God isn't judging you by one frame. John is talking about the whole film.

A word for two different men

The man who fell and hates it

Don't despair

A sheep can fall in the mud. It hates it and wants out. That misery is God's seed, His own life, remaining in you. It doesn't leave. You're not a fake: you're a son in a fight.

The man who's comfortable in it

Don't excuse it

A pig lies down in the mud and calls it home. If sin is your settled way of life, with no fight and no grief, John says plainly: let no man deceive you (v. 7). Come to Jesus for real, today.

“O wretched man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death? I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 7:24–25

Paul wrote that as a believer. The fight itself is a sign of new life: dead men don't struggle.

Part three

Love one another

Verses 11–18

The first murder, the cross, and a brother who needs a ride to work.

Cain's staircase

  1. jealoushis brother's offering was accepted (Genesis 4:4–5)
  2. very wroth“his countenance fell” (4:5)
  3. warned“sin lieth at the door” (4:7)
  4. slew himin the field (4:8)

“If thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted? and if thou doest not well, sin lieth at the door. And unto thee shall be his desire, and thou shalt rule over him.”

Genesis 4:7

God stopped Cain on the third step and told him he could still rule over it. Cain walked past the warning. Anger toward a brother is a staircase too. Get off at the top, while God is still talking to you.

From death unto life

Love for other believers isn't how we cross over. It's how we know we did. Jesus said the man who believes “is passed from death unto life” (John 5:24). Loving the brothers is the fruit you can see.

Hateth his brother is a murderer: Jesus taught the same, that anger and contempt are murder at the root (Matthew 5:21–22). Many of these men know what the outside of that looks like. God says the inside matters as much.

Here's good news hidden in it: if you find you actually love these brothers, men you'd never have chosen, that love didn't come from you. It's the proof you've crossed over.

Love you can see

In word and tongue

“I'll pray for you, brother”

And walks off. He shutteth up his bowels of compassion: bowels is the old word for the gut, where you feel for someone.

In deed and in truth

Shows up

A ride to work. A meal. Answering the phone at 2 a.m. Sitting with him in court. Laying down your life, an hour at a time.

“If a brother or sister be naked, and destitute of daily food, And one of you say unto them, Depart in peace, be ye warmed and filled; notwithstanding ye give them not those things which are needful to the body; what doth it profit?”

James 2:15–16

Most of us will never be asked to die for a brother. We're asked to give up a Saturday. That's where love gets real.

Part four

Greater than our heart

Verses 19–24

For the man whose own heart keeps telling him he's no good.

When your heart won't stop accusing

Some men confess a sin and still hear it at 3 a.m. for years. Their own heart becomes the prosecutor and never rests its case.

God is greater than our heart. Your heart only knows part of the story. God knoweth all things: every sin you did, and every drop of blood that paid for it. He saw it all before He saved you, and He saved you anyway.

When your heart and God disagree, God wins. Answer your heart with His words, out loud if you have to.

Conviction or condemnation?

From the Holy Spirit

Conviction

Specific: “That was wrong. Make it right.” It points you to Jesus, and once you confess, it lifts.

From the accuser, or old shame

Condemnation

Vague and endless: “You're garbage. You always will be.” It drives you away from Jesus, and it keeps coming back after you've confessed.

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Obey the first right away. Refuse the second by name: “That's already confessed, and the blood covered it.” Then confidence toward God (v. 21) comes back.

One command, two sides

All of chapter 3 in one sentence: believe on Jesus, and love one another. Faith toward God, love toward men. You can't keep one and drop the other.

And how do we know He lives in us? By the Spirit which he hath given us. Given, not earned; given to every believer, the day he believes. A son who obeys his Father doesn't get a bigger answer to his prayers by earning it. He asks the way a son asks, because he's close.

“The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God:”

Romans 8:16

Behold, what manner of love: sons of God.

You're a son now, and you'll be like Him when He comes. A son doesn't make his home in sin; when he falls, he hates it and gets up. He loves his brothers with his hands, not just his mouth. And when his heart condemns him, he remembers: God is greater, and God knows everything.

Something to do today: do one thing for a brother in need with your hands, not just your words. And the next time your heart says, “You're a fake,” answer it out loud with 1:9.

Points to remember

  1. Sons of God“that we should be called the sons of God” 3:1The Father's love has made us His own sons, right now.
  2. Like Him“when he shall appear, we shall be like him” 3:2We aren't finished yet, but when Jesus comes we'll be like Him, and that hope makes us clean up.
  3. Born of God“Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin” 3:9A son of God doesn't keep living in sin as a way of life. He falls, fights, and gets up.
  4. Why He came“that he might destroy the works of the devil” 3:8Jesus came to take away our sins and untie what the devil has bound.
  5. Love in deed“he laid down his life for us” 3:16The cross shows what love is. We love with our hands, not just our words.
  6. A condemning heart“God is greater than our heart, and knoweth all things” 3:20When your heart accuses you, God knows it all, and His word overrules it.

We will see Him as He is, and we will be like Him. Every man with that hope starts getting ready now.

Question 3 of 6

Born of God

What does it mean that whoever is born of God “doth not commit sin”?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”

1 John 1:8

“My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:”

1 John 2:1

Answer

He doesn't keep on living in sin as his way of life. It doesn't mean he never sins. God's seed stays in him, so he can't be at home in it: he fights, confesses, and gets up.

Question 4 of 6

Why He came

Why was the Son of God manifested?

What verses tell us about this?

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

Answer

To take away our sins, and to destroy, untie, the works of the devil. He came to set us free.

Question 5 of 6

Love in deed

How do we know what love is, and how should we love?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Jesus laid down His life for us; that's what love is. So we lay down our lives for our brothers: we meet real needs, in deed and in truth, not just in words.
