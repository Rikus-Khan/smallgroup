Mark 16

He is risen

Three women find the stone rolled away and the tomb empty. The risen Lord appears to a woman He set free, sends His doubting men into all the world, and goes up to the right hand of God.

Part one

He is risen

Verses 1–8

Three women go to anoint a dead body, and find an empty tomb.

The third day

A Jewish day began at sundown, not midnight. Count the days the way they did:

  1. Fridaycrucified, buried before sundown15:42–47
  2. The sabbathSaturday: they rest
  3. Sabbath pastSaturday evening: spices bought16:1
  4. First daySunday, at sunrise: the tomb16:2

Jesus had said it plainly: “and the third day he shall rise again” (Mark 10:34). The women didn't come expecting that. They came with sweet spices to anoint a dead body. Their love was real. Their hope was buried with Him.

Too heavy for them

All the way there they worried about one thing: the stone. It was “very great,” far too heavy for three women. They went anyway.

“And when they looked,” it was already done. Matthew tells us an angel of the Lord had rolled it back (Matthew 28:2).

Some of us carry a stone we can't move: a habit, a past, a grave we can't climb out of. Keep walking toward Jesus. The biggest stone of all, death itself, is already rolled away.

Not here

The “young man” in a long white garment was an angel (Matthew 28:5 calls him “the angel”). Listen to the order of what he says:

“which was crucified”He died

You've come to the right place, for the right Jesus. He really was dead.

“he is risen”He lives

Not a spirit, not a memory. Risen, body and all.

“behold the place”Come and see

The proof is right there: an empty shelf where they laid Him.

Christianity doesn't rest on good advice. It rests on an empty tomb. If He is risen, then everything He said is true, including every promise He made to you.

Back where it started

The night He was arrested, Jesus had told them: “But after that I am risen, I will go before you into Galilee” (14:28).

Galilee was home: the lake, the boats, the place where He first said “Follow me.” He goes ahead of them to start again with the same men in the same place.

“As he said unto you.” Every word He spoke before the cross held true after it.

“And Peter”

Mark 14:72, Thursday night

“And Peter called to mind the word that Jesus said unto him, Before the cock crow twice, thou shalt deny me thrice. And when he thought thereon, he wept.”

Mark 16:7, Sunday morning

“But go your way, tell his disciples and Peter that he goeth before you into Galilee:”

Peter, who had sworn he never knew Jesus, may have thought he was finished. The risen Lord sent word to him by name. Mark's Gospel likely came from Peter's own preaching: imagine him telling this part.

If you've failed Him badly, maybe after promising you never would, hear it: He hasn't crossed your name off. “And Peter.” And you.

Part two

They believed not

Verses 9–14

The risen Lord shows Himself, and His own men can't bring themselves to believe it.

First to see Him

seven devils → first witness Luke tells us she was one of the women “which had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities, Mary called Magdalene, out of whom went seven devils” (Luke 8:2).

Of everyone He could have shown Himself to first (Pilate, the high priest, Peter), the risen Lord chose a woman whose life had once been owned by seven devils.

Your past doesn't put you at the back of the line with Jesus. The man who knows how much he's been forgiven often loves Him the most. And she didn't keep it to herself: she “went and told them.”

Slow to believe

The two walking into the country were on the road to Emmaus (Luke 24:13–35). Count how often believe or unbelief shows up from here to the end:

9  10  11not 12  13not 14not ×2 15  16yes / not 17yes 18  19  20 

These were the men who would turn the world upside down, and they didn't believe the first reports. “Upbraided” means He rebuked them sharply. Unbelief isn't a small thing.

But look at what He did next: He gave those same men the greatest job in the world (v. 15). He corrects doubters. He doesn't throw them away. You can pray what a desperate father once prayed: “Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief” (Mark 9:24).

Part three

Into all the world

Verses 15–20

The commission, the warning, the promise, and a King who sits down at God's right hand.

Every creature

Eleven ordinary men are sent to take the good news to the whole world: not one nation or one kind of sinner, but “every creature.”

“But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.”

Acts 1:8

It spread ring by ring until it reached us. Your Jerusalem is the people right in front of you.

Saved or damned

Jesus puts the two ends side by side. Look closely at what makes the difference:

He thatbelieveth (and is baptized)
→
shall besaved
He thatbelieveth not
→
shall bedamned

The second line doesn't say “is not baptized.” It says “believeth not.” Believing is what divides the saved from the lost. Damned means condemned: lost, under God's judgment, for ever. Jesus said it plainly, because He loves men too much to soften it.

“He that believeth on him is not condemned: but he that believeth not is condemned already, because he hath not believed in the name of the only begotten Son of God.”

John 3:18

Where baptism fits

What saves

Faith in Jesus

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.” (Ephesians 2:8–9)

What follows

Baptism

“Then they that gladly received his word were baptized” (Acts 2:41). A believer's first step of obedience: a public picture of being buried with Christ and raised to walk in newness of life (Romans 6:4).

In the New Testament, men who believed were baptized, and soon. So Jesus names the two together. But the water doesn't wash away sin; the blood of Jesus does. The thief on the cross was never baptized, and Jesus told him, “To day shalt thou be with me in paradise” (Luke 23:43).

If you've trusted Christ, you're saved. If you haven't been baptized yet, that's your next step. Talk to the pastor about it.

The viper bit Paul by accident while he gathered sticks. Nobody handled snakes or drank poison to prove his faith; Jesus said, “Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God” (Matthew 4:7). Nor is this a promise that believers never get sick or poor. The signs were God confirming His word: not a show, and not a payday.

From the tomb to the throne

  1. Risen“he is not here”v. 6
  2. Seenby Mary, two, the elevenvv. 9–14
  3. Sending“Go ye into all the world”v. 15
  4. Received upinto heavenv. 19

A priest in the temple never sat down; his work was never done. Jesus sat, because the work of paying for sin was finished. The right hand is the place of honor and power.

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

The beginning of the gospel

Mark's book opened with “The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God” (1:1). It ends with men going out “every where”. The story was only beginning.

Look who went: men who had run away, denied Him, and refused to believe the first reports. And look who went with them: “the Lord working with them.” He was in heaven at God's right hand, and at work beside them on the road at the same time.

That's the same Lord we follow today. He doesn't send a man out alone, and He doesn't need you to be finished before He uses you.

“Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.”

Matthew 28:20

Question 1 of 6

The stone

What happened to the problem the women couldn't fix?

What verses tell us about this?

“And, behold, there was a great earthquake: for the angel of the Lord descended from heaven, and came and rolled back the stone from the door, and sat upon it.”

Matthew 28:2

Answer

God had already taken care of it. The stone was far too heavy for them, but they went anyway, and when they looked it had been rolled away. It wasn't moved to let Jesus out, but to let them see the tomb was empty. Death, the heaviest stone of all, has been rolled away.

Question 2 of 6

And Peter

Why did the angel single out Peter by name?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the second time the cock crew. And Peter called to mind the word that Jesus said unto him, Before the cock crow twice, thou shalt deny me thrice. And when he thought thereon, he wept.”

Mark 14:72

“And that he was seen of Cephas, then of the twelve:”

1 Corinthians 15:5

Answer

Because Peter had denied Him three times and wept bitterly, and he may well have thought he was out for good. The Lord made sure Peter knew he was still wanted. Paul tells us Jesus even appeared to Peter (Cephas) personally. Failure doesn't cancel His call.

Question 3 of 6

First

Who did the risen Jesus appear to first?

What verses tell us about this?

“And certain women, which had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities, Mary called Magdalene, out of whom went seven devils,”

Luke 8:2

Answer

Mary Magdalene, a woman He had set free from seven devils. Not a ruler, not a priest, not even an apostle. The Lord honored the one with the darkest past, and she went straight to tell the others. Nobody's history disqualifies him from knowing Jesus.

Question 4 of 6

Unbelief

How did Jesus deal with disciples who would not believe?

What verses tell us about this?

“And straightway the father of the child cried out, and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help thou mine unbelief.”

Mark 9:24

Answer

He rebuked them honestly for their unbelief and hard hearts, and then in the same breath He sent them into all the world. He didn't excuse their doubt, and He didn't discard them for it. A man who struggles to believe can bring that struggle straight to Him.

Question 5 of 6

Saved

What decides whether a man is saved or damned?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that believeth on him is not condemned: but he that believeth not is condemned already, because he hath not believed in the name of the only begotten Son of God.”

John 3:18

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Believing on Jesus. Whoever believes is saved; whoever does not believe is damned. Salvation is a gift received by faith, not earned by works, and baptism doesn't save. A believer is baptized as his first step of obedience, to show the world he belongs to Christ.

Question 6 of 6

With us

Where is Jesus now, and how does He work with His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Answer

He is in heaven, seated at the right hand of God, His work of salvation finished. From there He prays for His people and works with them wherever they go, confirming His word. He's never far from the believer He sends out.
