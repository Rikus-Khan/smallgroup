Leviticus 17

The life of the flesh is in the blood

Every offering comes to one door, and none to the devils in the open field. Then God tells Israel why the blood is never to be eaten: the life is in it, and he gave it on the altar to make an atonement for their souls.

Part one

“Unto the door of the tabernacle”

Verses 1–9

One place to bring an offering, for Israel and the stranger alike, and no more sacrifices to devils.

Blood imputed

  • killeth an ox, or lamb, or goatany animal fit for the altar, killed in the camp or out of it
  • imputedcounted, put on his account: as if he had shed blood wrongly
  • cut off from among his peopleput out of Israel, no longer counted among God's people

In the wilderness, an ox or a lamb or a goat was not killed for a man's table without first being brought as a peace offering to the door, where the priest put its blood on the altar and the fat went up to God. Later, when Israel was spread out in the land, Moses let them kill meat at home, and the blood was still poured out (Deuteronomy 12:20–24).

The point is not the meat. It is the blood. A life was given up, and God would not have that treated as nothing.

Out of the open field

In the open field nobody saw what a man offered, or to whom, and Israel had been offering to devils, the evil spirits behind the idols. Gone a whoring is God's own picture: Israel running after other gods like a wife running after other men.

“But I say, that the things which the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to devils, and not to God: and I would not that ye should have fellowship with devils.”

1 Corinthians 10:20

Worship in the open field

The open field is worship your own way: private, nobody watching, nobody to answer to. It sounds free. Verse 7 says where it ended up. A man in recovery knows this road. “Me and God are fine, I don't need church, I don't need anybody.” Out there, alone, the wrong spirits get the offering.

God called them in, to the door, to the priest, where the blood was handled his way and everybody could see. He was not fencing them out. He was calling them home.

One door, for everybody

The stranger, the foreigner living among Israel, came to the same door as the Israelite. No back way in for anybody, and no separate way for outsiders. That is still true:

John 10:9

I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.

John 14:6

Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.

Some men hear “one door” and feel shut out. Read it again: “by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved.” One door, and it is open to any man.

Part two

“The life of the flesh is in the blood”

Verses 10–16

Why the blood is never eaten, what the hunter does with it, and what to do when a man is made unclean.

The life of the flesh is in the blood

  1. the lifeis in the bloodwhat blood is
  2. “I have given it”upon the altarwhose idea it was
  3. an atonementfor your soulswhat it is for

This verse is the reason for the whole book. “Blood” comes thirteen times in these sixteen verses. Notice who gives. Atonement was not man's idea for calming God down. God says, I have given it to you. The way back was his gift before it was ever their offering.

A life for a life

The altara life poured out

Back to his tentforgiven

The man“the soul that sinneth” (Ezekiel 18:4): his life is owed
The lambwithout blemish, nothing owed

The blood is the life. When it is poured out on the altar, a life has been given in the place of the man's soul, and he walks home. That is what atonement costs: not effort, but a life.

“And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.”

Hebrews 9:22

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Pour it out, cover it

A hunter far from the altar still did not eat the blood. He poured it out and covered it, the way you would bury something precious. Even away from the altar, a life was a life.

This law is older than Moses. God told Noah, the day he gave him meat to eat:

“But flesh with the life thereof, which is the blood thereof, shall ye not eat.”

Genesis 9:4

And it is not left behind in the New Testament. The apostles wrote to the Gentile believers to “abstain… from blood, and from things strangled” (Acts 15:29).

The blood that gives life

From Noah to Moses and on, God's people were not to eat blood, because the life was in it. Then Jesus said this in the synagogue at Capernaum, and many walked away (John 6:59, 66):

“Whoso eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, hath eternal life; and I will raise him up at the last day.”

John 6:54

He was not handing them a cup of blood to drink. A few verses earlier he said what he meant: coming to him, believing on him.

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

“For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins.”

Matthew 26:28

The life is in the blood. His blood was poured out so that his life could be yours.

Wash, or bear it

If he washes

clean by evening

“Unclean until the even: then shall he be clean.” The even is the evening. A bad day, but the day ends clean.

If he will not

he bears it

“Then he shall bear his iniquity.” Nobody carries it away for him. He carries it himself.

An animal that died of itself, or was torn with beasts, still had its blood in it. A man could eat it and end up unclean, not even meaning to. The law did not throw him out for that. It told him what to do: wash. The difference between the two men is not the fall. It is the washing.

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Real world: come to the door

“And from Jesus Christ, who is the faithful witness, and the first begotten of the dead, and the prince of the kings of the earth. Unto him that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood,”

Revelation 1:5
  • SinWhere is your open field: the private place where you handle things your own way, where nobody sees?
  • RegretIsrael had been offering to devils. God's answer was not “you're finished.” It was “no more,” and “bring it to the door.”
  • Grace“I have given it to you.” The blood that makes atonement is God's gift, not your payment.
  • HopeUnclean until the even, then clean. A fall today does not have to be carried into tomorrow.
  • TodayWash. Confess it to God, and bring it into the light with a brother in Christ before the day is out. Don't carry it to bed.

Points to remember

  1. The door“unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, unto the priest” 17:5Every offering came to one door. Jesus said, “I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved.”
  2. The open field“they shall no more offer their sacrifices unto devils” 17:7Worship done your own way, out of sight, ends up with the wrong spirits. God calls us in.
  3. The life“For the life of the flesh is in the blood” 17:11Blood is life. Pouring it out is giving up a life.
  4. The atonement“I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls” 17:11God gave the blood as the way back. A life for a life, and in the end, Christ's life for ours.
  5. The dust“he shall even pour out the blood thereof, and cover it with dust” 17:13Even away from the altar, a life was treated as precious.
  6. The washing“But if he wash them not, nor bathe his flesh; then he shall bear his iniquity” 17:16The fall did not decide it; the washing did. Wash, and be clean by evening.

Question 1 of 6

The door

Where did every offering have to be brought?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”

John 10:9

Answer

To the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, to the priest, for Israel and the stranger alike. There was one door, and Jesus says he is the door, open to any man.

Question 2 of 6

The open field

To whom had Israel been sacrificing out in the open field?

What verses tell us about this?

“But I say, that the things which the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to devils, and not to God: and I would not that ye should have fellowship with devils.”

1 Corinthians 10:20

Answer

To devils. Worship out of sight, done their own way, had gone to the wrong spirits. God called them in to the door, where it was done his way.

Question 3 of 6

The life

What does God say is in the blood?

What verses tell us about this?

“But flesh with the life thereof, which is the blood thereof, shall ye not eat.”

Genesis 9:4

Answer

The life. To pour out blood is to give up a life, which is why it was never eaten, from Noah on.

Question 4 of 6

The atonement

Why did God give the blood upon the altar?

What verses tell us about this?

“And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission.”

Hebrews 9:22

Answer

To make an atonement for their souls: a life given in place of the sinner's. God gave it; it was his way back, and it points to the precious blood of Christ.

Question 5 of 6

The dust

What was a hunter to do with the blood of what he caught?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Pour it out on the ground and cover it with dust, and never eat it. Even far from the altar, a life was treated as precious.

Question 6 of 6

The washing

What was a man to do if he ate what died of itself, and what if he would not?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Wash his clothes and bathe, and he was clean by evening. If he would not wash, he bore his own iniquity. The fall did not decide it; the washing did. “The blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin” (1 John 1:7).
