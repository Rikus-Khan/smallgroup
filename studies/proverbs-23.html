Proverbs 23

Who hath woe?

A rich man's table, riches with wings, a father's plea for his son's heart, and an honest picture of the drunkard's trap, and the way out.

Things to watch for

Part one

At the ruler's table

Verses 1–8

Good food, and a hook underneath it.

Bait on a fancy plate

A powerful man invites you to dinner. The food is amazing. But he isn't feeding you for free. He wants something: your loyalty, your silence, a favor later.

“Put a knife to thy throat” is a strong way of saying: get a firm grip on your appetite. If you know you're “a man given to appetite,” take it seriously. Treat it like life and death.

Every craving has a hook in it somewhere. Wisdom looks for the hook before it takes the bite.

Money with wings

“Labour not to be rich” doesn't mean don't work. It means don't make getting rich the goal you wear yourself out for. You'll chase it and it'll fly off.

“That which is not”: money looks solid, but it can be gone in a day. A layoff, a hospital bill, one bad weekend.

“But godliness with contentment is great gain.”

1 Timothy 6:6

What he says, and what he thinks

You may have heard verse 7 used as a slogan for positive thinking. In context it's a warning about a stingy host, a man with “an evil eye.”

What he says→“Eat and drink”Smiles, generous words, help yourself.
What he thinks→counting every bite“His heart is not with thee.” And the man really is what he thinks, not what he says.

Some “friends” are like that. Their words say one thing, their heart says another, and afterward you feel sick about it (v. 8). Watch the heart, not the show.

Part two

My son

Verses 9–28

A father pleads for his son's heart.

The orphan's Redeemer

גֹּאֵל said go'el the family member who buys back what was lost

If a man lost his land or was sold into slavery, his closest relative was supposed to step in, pay the price, and set things right. Boaz did it for Ruth. A fatherless child had no one to do that, so a greedy neighbor could just move the stones and take his field.

But God says: I am their Redeemer, and I'm mighty. That's the same hope Job held: “For I know that my redeemer liveth” (Job 19:25). In Jesus, the Redeemer paid the price for us Himself.

Correction that saves

“He shall not die” means correction won't kill him. Letting a child run wild without any boundaries might. The goal is to “deliver his soul”, to turn him off a road that leads to death.

This was never permission for rage. A father who corrects in anger is just passing along his own wounds. If you grew up with that kind of “rod,” it wasn't God's way, and God can break that chain with you.

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

Don't envy the fast lane

The old crowd can look like they're having all the fun: cash, cars, no rules. It's easy to feel you're missing out.

“Surely there is an end.” That road has an end, and it isn't pretty. Yours doesn't get cut off. Stay “in the fear of the LORD all the day long.”

The company and the cost

Winebibbers are heavy drinkers. Riotous eaters of flesh are gluttons, men who party and stuff themselves. Notice the first command isn't about the bottle. It's “Be not among” them. Where you sit decides a lot.

And the bill always comes: poverty, and a fog that dresses a man in rags. Not just empty pockets. A life that slowly falls apart.

Verse 19 is the way out: “guide thine heart in the way.” You steer your heart by choosing where your feet go.

Buy the truth, and sell it not.

Truth costs something: pride, old friends, comfortable lies about yourself. Pay it. And once you have it, don't trade it back for anything.

Verses 22–25 add: honor your father and mother, even when they're old. Some of us have hard histories with our parents. Honoring them doesn't mean pretending. It means not despising them, and living in a way that could one day make them glad.

Give Him your heart

Here's the center of the whole chapter. Not “give me your behavior” or “give me your Sunday.” “Give me thine heart.” When the heart belongs to God, the eyes follow: “let thine eyes observe my ways.”

Right away comes the danger that competes for that heart: the woman who isn't your wife is “a deep ditch” and “a narrow pit.” Easy to slip into, hard to climb out of. And “she also lieth in wait as for a prey.” The trap is set on purpose.

Rules alone won't hold a man. A heart that's been given to God will.

Part three

Who hath woe?

Verses 29–35

The most honest picture of addiction in the Bible, and a door out.

Six questions, one answer

  • woea heavy, “why me?” kind of misery
  • sorrowregret that won't leave
  • contentionsfights, arguments, burned bridges
  • babblingcomplaining, saying things you can't take back
  • wounds without causebruises you can't explain
  • redness of eyesbloodshot mornings

Who has all that? “They that tarry long at the wine,” the ones who stay late and go looking for “mixed wine”, probably wine mixed with spices to make it stronger. Solomon isn't guessing. He's describing a man a lot of us have been.

Sweet first, bite last

The wine is red, it sparkles in the cup, it “moveth itself aright”: it goes down smooth. That's how it gets you. It never shows the fangs up front.

Notice where the fight is won: “Look not.” The battle isn't at the bottom of the bottle. It's at the first look, before the first sip. Whatever your drug of choice is, the same is true.

The cycle

Three thousand years ago, Solomon drew addiction exactly. The eyes go wrong and the mouth says crazy things (v. 33). He's like a man asleep at the top of a ship's mast, rocking, no footing (v. 34).

He gets beaten and doesn't feel it. Then he wakes up, and the very first thought is: “I will seek it yet again.”

If you've lived that, the Bible isn't shocked by you. It tells the plain truth about the trap so you can see it for what it is.

There is a way out

That circle feels like it'll run forever. It doesn't have to.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

How the way out works

  • shameit keeps you hiding, and hiding keeps you using
  • willpower alonea man who fights by himself loses by himself
  • the Lord, His people, and real helpJesus to forgive and change you, brothers to walk with you

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed.”

James 5:16

Tell someone the truth today. Go to the meeting. Get a brother or sponsor you can call at 2 a.m. Let the church carry you (Galatians 6:2). And if you've been drinking heavily, talk to a doctor: quitting suddenly can be dangerous.

Paul wrote to a church full of former drunkards and worse: “And such were some of you: but ye are washed” (1 Corinthians 6:11). That's the end of the story God wants to write for you.

Points to remember

  1. Appetite“Put a knife to thy throat” 23:2Take your cravings seriously. Every one has a hook in it.
  2. Riches“Riches certainly make themselves wings” 23:5Money flies. Godliness with contentment stays.
  3. Our Redeemer“For their redeemer is mighty” 23:11God takes up the cause of the fatherless. In Jesus, He redeemed us.
  4. Hope“Thine expectation shall not be cut off” 23:18The fast lane has an end. A life with God doesn't get cut off.
  5. The heart“My son, give me thine heart” 23:26God wants the heart. When He has it, the eyes and feet follow.
  6. Addiction“I will seek it yet again” 23:35The cycle is real, and it can be broken: the Lord, His people, and real help.

Question 1 of 6

Appetite

How should a man handle what he craves?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Seriously, like it's life and death, because it can be. Know that every craving has a hook hidden in it. Win the fight early, at the first look, not at the bottom of the glass. And guide your heart by choosing where your feet go.

Question 2 of 6

Riches

Why shouldn't we wear ourselves out to get rich?

What verses tell us about this?

“But godliness with contentment is great gain.”

1 Timothy 6:6

“But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust doth corrupt, and where thieves do not break through nor steal:”

Matthew 6:20

Answer

Because riches grow wings and fly away. Work hard, but don't make money the thing you live for. It looks solid and isn't. Contentment with God is real gain, and treasure in heaven can't be taken.

Question 3 of 6

Our Redeemer

Who defends the fatherless?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth:”

Job 19:25

Answer

God Himself. He is the Redeemer of those with no family to stand up for them, and He is mighty. Mess with the fatherless and you're dealing with Him. Jesus is our Redeemer too. He paid the price to buy us back.

Question 4 of 6

Hope

Why shouldn't we envy people who seem to get away with sin?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end.”

Jeremiah 29:11

Answer

Because “surely there is an end.” The fast lane looks fun, but it runs off a cliff. Stay in the fear of the LORD all day long. Your hope in Him won't be cut off.

Question 5 of 6

The heart

What does God ask us to give Him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Our heart. Not just good behavior or an hour on Sunday, but the whole inside of us. When the heart belongs to Him, the eyes follow His ways. Rules alone won't keep a man out of the pit. A heart given to God will.

Question 6 of 6

Addiction

What does the drunkard's cycle look like, and how do we get out?

What verses tell us about this?

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

“Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed.”

James 5:16

Answer

It looks good, goes down smooth, then bites. The mind bends, the footing goes, the pain goes numb, and the first thought on waking is “I will seek it yet again.” The way out isn't shame or willpower alone. It's Jesus, who makes us free indeed, and His people walking with us, plus real help like meetings, a sponsor, and a doctor when needed.
