Jonah 2

Salvation is of the LORD

From inside a fish, at the bottom of the sea, a runaway prophet prays. And God hears him.

Things to watch for

Jonah, the prophet from Israel who ran from God's call to Nineveh, has been thrown into the sea and swallowed by a great fish. Keep these questions in mind as we read.

  1. PrayerCan God hear a man who prays from the bottom?

Part one

Out of the deep

Verses 1–6

The man who ran from God's presence prays his way back to it.

“The belly of hell”

שְׁאוֹל said sheol the grave, the world of the dead

Jonah felt as good as dead and buried. And he prayed anyway, from inside the fish, with nothing in his hands. No clean-up first, no temple, no offering. Just a cry.

“In my distress I cried unto the LORD, and he heard me.”

Psalm 120:1

Psalms at the bottom of the sea

Jonah 2:3

…and the floods compassed me about: all thy billows and thy waves passed over me.

Psalm 42:7

Deep calleth unto deep at the noise of thy waterspouts: all thy waves and thy billows are gone over me.

Jonah's prayer is stitched together from the Psalms. He had them in his memory, and at the bottom, they came back. The Scripture you put in today is what you'll have when everything else is gone.

And look at the word “yet.” “I am cast out of thy sight; yet I will look again toward thy holy temple.” He feels thrown away, and he looks toward God anyway.

Rock bottom has a floor

Seaweed around his head. Down to where the mountains start. “The earth with her bars was about me for ever”: like a cell door slammed and locked behind him. “Corruption” is the pit, where bodies rot.

Then one word turns it all around: “yet.” Lower than any man can reach is not lower than God can reach. Plenty of us have been to a bottom like that. God's hand goes under it.

Part two

Salvation is of the LORD

Verses 7–10

With nothing left to hold, Jonah remembers who holds him.

“When my soul fainted within me I remembered the LORD.”

When did Jonah remember God? Not on the dock at Joppa. Not on the ship. When his strength was gone. A lot of us didn't remember the LORD until we'd run out of everything else.

That's not something to be ashamed of. It's a door. And God didn't say, “Now you remember Me?” The prayer went straight in:

“In my distress I called upon the LORD, and cried unto my God: he heard my voice out of his temple, and my cry came before him, even into his ears.”

Psalm 18:6

“Lying vanities” are idols: empty things that promise everything and deliver nothing. To “observe” them is to cling to them. And the man who clings to them walks away from the mercy that was his for the asking.

“For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water.”

Jeremiah 2:13

Salvation is of the LORD

This is the center of the whole book. Look at who couldn't save Jonah:

  • the sailorsthey rowed hard, “but they could not” (1:13)
  • Jonah himselfhe was sinking with weeds around his head (2:5)
  • the idolslying vanities (2:8)
  • of the LORDonly He can bring a man up

Jonah promises to give thanks and keep his vows. That's not paying God back for saving him. It's what a rescued man does. The rescue itself is a gift.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Everything obeys

God speaks, and a fish obeys. Look how everything in this book does what God says:

  • the windsent out (1:4)
  • the lotfell on Jonah (1:7)
  • the seaceased (1:15)
  • the fishswallowed, then let go (1:17, 2:10)
  • the prophet?not yet, but chapter 3 is coming

God had an easier time with the fish than with His prophet. And still He didn't give up on him. Jonah lands on dry ground, not where he wanted to be, but alive, with a second chance waiting for him in the very next verse.

Points to remember

  1. Prayer“Out of the belly of hell cried I, and thou heardest my voice” 2:2No place is too low to pray from. God hears a man at the bottom.
  2. Rock bottom“Yet hast thou brought up my life from corruption” 2:6The bottom has a floor, and God's hand reaches under it.
  3. Remembering“When my soul fainted within me I remembered the LORD” 2:7When everything else runs out, remembering God is a door, not a disgrace.
  4. Idols“They that observe lying vanities forsake their own mercy” 2:8Clinging to idols means walking away from mercy that was ours.
  5. Salvation“Salvation is of the LORD” 2:9Not of sailors, self, or idols. Rescue is God's gift.
  6. A fresh start“It vomited out Jonah upon the dry land” 2:10God brings a man up and gives him another chance.

Question 1 of 6

Prayer

Can God hear a man who prays from the bottom?

What verses tell us about this?

“In my distress I cried unto the LORD, and he heard me.”

Psalm 120:1

“This poor man cried, and the LORD heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles.”

Psalm 34:6

Answer

Yes. Jonah prayed from inside a fish, at the bottom of the sea, after running from God, and God heard him. You don't have to clean up, dress up, or get to a church first. The cry of a man in trouble goes straight to God's ears.

Question 2 of 6

Rock bottom

What did Jonah find at the bottom?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Psalm 34:18

Answer

He found what it's like to be locked in, with no way out on his own. And he found that God was already there. “Yet hast thou brought up my life.” The bottom isn't too deep for God. He is near to the broken, and He brings men up.

Question 3 of 6

Remembering

When did Jonah remember the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

“In my distress I called upon the LORD, and cried unto my God: he heard my voice out of his temple, and my cry came before him, even into his ears.”

Psalm 18:6

Answer

When his soul fainted: when he had nothing left. That's when a lot of us first turned to God too. God didn't hold it against Jonah that it took a fish to get his attention. The prayer went straight into His holy temple. It's never too late to remember Him.

Question 4 of 6

Idols

What do lying vanities cost a man?

What verses tell us about this?

“For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water.”

Jeremiah 2:13

“Little children, keep yourselves from idols. Amen.”

1 John 5:21

Answer

They cost him the mercy that was his. An idol is anything we cling to in place of God: a bottle, a pill, a woman, money, a reputation. It promises to hold us and it leaks dry. The man who clings to it walks away from the Fountain. Let go of the cistern and come back to the spring.

Question 5 of 6

Salvation

Where does salvation come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“Salvation belongeth unto the LORD: thy blessing is upon thy people. Selah.”

Psalm 3:8

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

From the LORD, and only from Him. The sailors couldn't row Jonah to safety, Jonah couldn't swim out, and idols can't save anybody. Salvation is God's gift, received by faith in Jesus, not earned by works. Our thanks and our obedience come after, as the response of a rescued man.

Question 6 of 6

A fresh start

What does God do with a man He brings up?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

He sets him back on solid ground and gives him another chance. It wasn't pretty, but Jonah was alive and on dry land. And the next chapter opens with God speaking to him “the second time.” God doesn't rescue a man just to shelve him. He brings him up to give him a new start.
