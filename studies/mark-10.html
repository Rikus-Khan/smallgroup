Mark 10

The road up to Jerusalem

Marriage, little children, a rich man who walks away sad, and a blind beggar who won't keep quiet, all on the road to the cross.

Part one

What God hath joined

Verses 1–16

Jesus leaves Galilee for the last time. A trick question about divorce, and little children in His arms.

The last road south

Jesus leaves Galilee and goes down the east side of the Jordan, the land called Perea. From there He'll cross to Jericho and climb to Jerusalem, and the cross.

Perea belonged to Herod Antipas, the man who killed John the Baptist for saying Herod's marriage was unlawful (6:17–18).

So the Pharisees' question was a trap: “tempting him.” Answer one way and the crowd turns on Him. Answer the other and Herod might.

What Moses allowed

“When a man hath taken a wife, and married her, and it come to pass that she find no favour in his eyes, because he hath found some uncleanness in her: then let him write her a bill of divorcement, and give it in her hand, and send her out of his house.”

Deuteronomy 24:1

“Suffered” means allowed. Moses didn't command divorce. He put limits on something that was already happening. The written bill protected a woman who had been sent away, so she could marry again.

In Jesus' day some teachers said a man could divorce his wife for almost any reason. Jesus says the law allowed it only because of “the hardness of your heart.” Then He goes back further than Moses.

From the beginning

Jesus answers from Genesis 1 and 2. God made them “male and female,” and a man leaves his parents to “cleave to his wife”: to stick fast, like glue.

“Twain” means two. In marriage two people become one. God Himself does the joining.

Pull apart two boards that have been glued, and both come away torn. That's why divorce hurts so much. It was never meant to happen.

A hard word, and grace

Alone with the disciples, Jesus says it plainly. Notice verse 12: the same rule for the wife as for the husband. In Matthew 19:9 Jesus adds one exception, “except it be for fornication.”

Many of us have been through a divorce, or grew up in the wreckage of one. This passage shows us what God intended for marriage. It isn't here so anyone can rule on your life. If you have questions about your own situation, talk with the pastor one on one.

And whatever is behind you, this promise is for you:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

As a little child

The disciples thought the children were in the way. Jesus was “much displeased,” angry, and took them up in His arms. “Suffer” here means let.

  • not earneda child has nothing to pay with
  • not deserveda child hasn't proved anything yet
  • not by being importanta child had no rank at all
  • but receivedheld out empty hands, and taken as a gift

That's the only way anybody gets in. A tough man has to come the same way a child does: with empty hands, trusting Jesus.

Part two

One thing thou lackest

Verses 17–31

A man comes running to Jesus, and leaves walking, with his head down.

“Thou knowest the commandments”

  1. ✓Do not commit adultery, Do not kill, Do not steal, Do not bear false witnesscommandments 7, 6, 8 and 9
  2. ✓Defraud not, Honour thy father and motherdon't cheat (close to 10, don't covet), and 5

Every one Jesus named is about how we treat other people. He left out the first: “Thou shalt have no other gods before me.” That was the one this man was about to break.

“Why callest thou me good?” Jesus isn't denying He is God. He's asking the man to think about what he just said, and who he's really talking to.

He loved him

Mark is the only one who tells us: Jesus looked at him and loved him. What He said next came from love.

Selling everything wasn't a way to earn heaven. Jesus put His finger on the one thing that owned this man. His money was his god.

Jesus let him walk away. He didn't lower the price. Every man has his “one thing.” What's yours?

A camel and a needle

The camel was the biggest animal in the land. The eye of a needle was the smallest hole anyone knew.

You may hear a story about a small gate in Jerusalem called “the needle's eye.” There's no good evidence for it. Jesus meant exactly what it sounds like: impossible.

Money isn't sin. Trusting it is (v. 24). A man who trusts what he has can't see he needs a Saviour.

Who then can be saved?

With men

impossible

No one, rich or poor, can save himself. Not with money, not with good works, not with willpower.

With God

all things are possible

God does what we can't. He saves whosoever comes to Him through His Son.

The same words Jesus said to the father in 9:23. Salvation is God's work, offered to every man, and received by faith:

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

An hundredfold

What a man leaves

house, brethren, sisters, father, mother, wife, children, lands

“for my sake, and the gospel's” (v. 29)

What he receives

houses, brethren, sisters, mothers, children, lands

“with persecutions; and in the world to come eternal life” (v. 30)

Look around this room. Some of us lost family to the life we lived. In God's family a man finds brothers, mothers, homes he's welcome in. That's the hundredfold, “now in this time.”

Notice “with persecutions.” Jesus never sells the Christian life as easy. And notice who's missing from the second list: fathers. A believer already has one.

Part three

A ransom for many

Verses 32–45

Jesus walks out in front toward Jerusalem. Two of His men ask for the best seats.

He went before them

  1. deliveredto the chief priests and scribes
  2. condemnedto death
  3. handed overto the Gentiles (Rome)
  4. mockedand spit upon
  5. scourgedwhipped
  6. killed
  7. rise again“the third day”

The third time He tells them, and the fullest. Only the Romans could put a man to death, so the Jewish leaders would hand Him to the Gentiles. Every piece came true, just as He said (chapters 14–16).

And He walked out in front. Nobody dragged Jesus to the cross. The disciples were afraid, and He led the way.

“Ye know not what ye ask”

What they asked

“one on thy right hand, and the other on thy left hand”

the places of honour beside a king on his throne (v. 37)

Who was there, at His cross

“two thieves; the one on his right hand, and the other on his left”

Mark 15:27

The cup and the baptism mean suffering, being plunged under it. In the garden Jesus would pray, “take away this cup from me” (14:36).

They said, “We can,” and later they did. James was the first of the twelve to be killed for Jesus (Acts 12:2). John was sent into exile on Patmos (Revelation 1:9).

“So shall it not be among you”

The other ten were angry, likely because they wanted those seats too. Jesus pointed at Rome: rulers there “exercise lordship”, they boss people around.

shall beyour minister
whosoever will bethe chiefest

shall beservant of all

A minister was a servant who waited on others. Greatness in the church isn't a title. It's a towel and a pair of willing hands.

A ransom

Boundheld until the price is paid

Freeowing nothing

A ransom was the price paid to set a captive or a slave free. Jesus paid it with His own life, and took our place.

“For many” means the price was for a great number, not only a few. Paul puts it this way:

“Who gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time.”

1 Timothy 2:6

Part four

Thou Son of David

Verses 46–52

The last stop before Jerusalem, and a blind man nobody could shut up.

At the gate of Jericho

Jericho was the last town before the climb to Jerusalem. Crowds of pilgrims passed through on their way to the Passover. A blind man sat where the crowds were, and begged.

“Son of David” means the promised King from David's line. Bartimaeus is the first man in Mark to call Jesus that, out loud, in public. A blind man saw who He was.

He cried the more

“Many charged him that he should hold his peace.” The crowd told him to shut up. He only got louder. And “Jesus stood still.” On the way to the cross, He stopped for one blind beggar.

Maybe people have told you you're too far gone, or you've told yourself. Don't stop calling. He hears.

blind, “by the highway side begging” → “followed Jesus in the way”

A beggar's cloak was his bed at night and the cloth he spread for coins by day. Bartimaeus threw it off. He wasn't planning to come back.

The same question

Mark 10:36, to James and John

What would ye that I should do for you?

“Grant unto us that we may sit, one on thy right hand …”

Mark 10:51, to Bartimaeus

What wilt thou that I should do unto thee?

“Lord, that I might receive my sight.”

Two disciples asked for glory. A blind beggar asked for mercy. The beggar went away seeing. He followed Jesus up the road to Jerusalem, toward the cross.

Jesus asks you the same question today. What do you want Him to do for you?

Points to remember

  1. Marriage“What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder” 10:9God made marriage one man and one woman, joined by Him for life. And His forgiveness reaches every failure.
  2. Children“Receive the kingdom of God as a little child” 10:15Nobody earns it. We receive it with empty hands, trusting Jesus.
  3. Riches“With God all things are possible” 10:27A man who trusts his riches can't save himself. God saves whosoever comes to Him.
  4. Leaving all“He shall receive an hundredfold now in this time” 10:30Nothing given up for Jesus is lost. He gives a family now, and eternal life to come.
  5. Serving“To give his life a ransom for many” 10:45Jesus came to serve, and to pay the price to set us free.
  6. Crying out“He cried the more a great deal” 10:48Don't let anyone quiet you. Jesus stops for the man who keeps calling.

Question 1 of 6

Marriage

What did God intend marriage to be?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

From the beginning, God made them male and female. A man leaves his parents and cleaves to his wife, and the two become one flesh. God does the joining, and it's meant for life. Moses allowed divorce only because of hard hearts.

Question 2 of 6

Children

How must a man receive the kingdom of God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As a little child: not by earning it or deserving it, but by receiving it as a gift. A child comes with empty hands and trusts. That's how every man has to come to Jesus.

Question 3 of 6

Riches

Why is it so hard for a rich man to be saved?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because he trusts his riches, and they become his god. Like the man who went away grieved, he won't let go of them to follow Jesus. With men it's impossible, but not with God. He saves whosoever turns to Him.

Question 4 of 6

Leaving all

What does a man get for what he gives up for Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

An hundredfold, now: a family of brothers and mothers and homes in God's people, with persecutions too. And in the world to come, eternal life. Nothing given up for Jesus is wasted.

Question 5 of 6

Serving

Why did the Son of man come?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not to be served, but to serve, and to give His life a ransom for many: the price paid to set us free. So the great man among His people is the one who serves.

Question 6 of 6

Crying out

What happens when a man keeps crying out to Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Jesus stops. The crowd told Bartimaeus to be quiet, but he cried the more, and Jesus stood still and called him. He received his sight and followed Jesus in the way.
