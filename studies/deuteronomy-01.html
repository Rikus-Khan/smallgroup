Deuteronomy 1

These be the words which Moses spake

Forty years on, at the edge of the land, an old man looks back over the road and tells the children what happened to their fathers at Kadesh-barnea, and why they must not do the same.

The book of Deuteronomy

Where: “on this side Jordan, in the land of Moab” (1:5), across the river from Jericho.

Who wrote it: Moses. “And Moses wrote this law, and delivered it unto the priests the sons of Levi” (31:9). Jesus answers the devil from it: “It is written” (Matthew 4:4).

To whom: the new generation, the children of the men who fell in the wilderness: “not with our fathers, but with us, even us, who are all of us here alive this day” (5:3).

When: the fortieth year, the eleventh month

  1. 15th, 1st monthOut of EgyptNumbers 33:3
  2. 3rd monthIsrael comes to Sinai (Horeb)Exodus 19:1
  3. 1st day, 1st month, 2nd yearThe tabernacle reared upExodus 40:17
  4. 20th day, 2nd month, 2nd yearThe cloud lifts; they leave HorebNumbers 10:11
  5. Kadesh-barneaThe spies; “ye would not go up”Deuteronomy 1:19–46
  6. Thirty and eight yearsFrom Kadesh-barnea to the brook ZeredDeuteronomy 2:14
  7. 1st day, 5th month, 40th yearAaron dies on mount HorNumbers 33:38
  8. 1st day, 11th month, 40th yearMoses begins to speakDeuteronomy 1:3
  9. 10th day, 1st monthIsrael crosses JordanJoshua 4:19

Moses speaks after Sihon and Og are slain (1:4; Numbers 21). He is “an hundred and twenty years old” when he dies (34:7), and the people weep for him “in the plains of Moab thirty days” (34:8). The Bible does not say on which day he died, so we do not guess.

The shape of the book: Moses’ sermons

  1. 1 to 4: looking backthe road from Horeb, Kadesh-barnea, the thirty and eight years, Sihon and Og
  2. 5 to 26: the law, given againthe ten commandments, “Hear, O Israel”, and the laws for the land
  3. 27 to 30: blessing and cursethe covenant in the land of Moab: “choose life”
  4. 31 to 34: Moses’ last daysJoshua charged, the song, the blessing, and Moses’ death

The big idea comes in two verses that Jesus himself called the greatest commandment (Mark 12:29–30):

“Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD: And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might.”

Deuteronomy 6:4–5

Remember what he did, love him, obey him from the heart, and choose life (30:19). And all of it rests on his grace first: “because the LORD loved you” (7:8).

Part one

Ye have dwelt long enough in this mount

Verses 1–8

An old man on the edge of the land begins to speak, and goes back to the day God said, “Move.”

Eleven days, and forty years

11 daysfrom Horeb to Kadesh-barnea, by the way of mount Seir (1:2)
40 yearsbefore Moses speaks: “in the fortieth year” (1:3)

Moses tucks the eleven days into a bracket, and the rest of the chapter is why a road of eleven days took forty years. Horeb is the Bible’s own other name for mount Sinai. Kadesh-barnea was where they stood at the edge of the land (verse 20).

The verse also names the places of the wilderness, Paran, Tophel, Laban, Hazeroth and Dizahab. The Bible names them and does not tell us more, so we leave them as it leaves them.

Moses begins to declare

Declare means to make plain, to set a thing out so that it can be understood. Moses is not adding a new law. He is explaining the one they have, to people who were children, or not yet born, when it was given.

Notice when he speaks. Sihon and Og are already dead (verse 4). The LORD has begun to keep his word before Moses opens his mouth, and the men listening have seen it with their own eyes.

He is an old man, speaking to men who will go on without him. A man who has been through the worst has something to say to the ones coming up behind. One day that will be you.

Time to move on

Horeb was a good place. The LORD had met them there, given the law, and set up the tabernacle. But a mountain is not the promise. The land was the promise, and it was an old one:

“In the same day the LORD made a covenant with Abram, saying, Unto thy seed have I given this land, from the river of Egypt unto the great river, the river Euphrates:”

Genesis 15:18

Verse 7 lists the country by its hills, its plain, its vale and its south, “unto the great river, the river Euphrates”, and verse 8 says “I have set the land before you.” God did not hold the land back. He set it before them, and told them to go in.

A man in recovery can mistake a good place for the whole road. The group, the church, the good feeling, all of it is a gift. But God moves a man on: to work, to forgive, to serve. Ask him today what your next step is.

Part two

I am not able to bear you myself alone

Verses 9–18

One man cannot carry a people: captains, judges, and a judgment that belongs to God.

Hard words in verses 9 to 15

  1. cumbrancea heavy load that weighs a man down
  2. strifequarrelling, the people’s disputes
  3. known among your tribesmen whose lives the people already knew
  4. captains over thousandsofficers over a thousand men, then hundreds, fifties and tens
  5. officersmen who carried out the judges’ orders

Moses says “at that time” three times in this chapter (9, 16, 18). He keeps taking them back to what was done in their own days.

Not able alone

We saw the start of this at the mount of God, when Moses’ father in law watched him judge all day:

“Thou wilt surely wear away, both thou, and this people that is with thee: for this thing is too heavy for thee; thou art not able to perform it thyself alone.”

Exodus 18:18

If you try to carry everything yourself, you will wear away. Name the one man you can call when it gets heavy, and let him help.

Captains of thousands, hundreds, fifties and tens

  1. Thousandsone captain over a thousand men
  2. Hundredsa captain over each hundred
  3. Fiftiesa captain over each fifty
  4. Tensa man over every ten, close to every family

The men were “known among your tribes”. Nobody was a stranger. The biggest job in the camp was cut down until a man could know the ten he looked after.

The judgment is God’s

Moses charged the judges with four rules:

  1. Hear the causelisten before you judge, between brethren and “the stranger that is with him”
  2. Judge righteouslythe same for every man
  3. Respect no persons“the small as well as the great”; no favourites, no fear
  4. Bring the hard cases“the cause that is too hard for you, bring it unto me”

A judge who is afraid of a man’s face will judge wrong. The reason is in one line: “the judgment is God’s.” The judge only sits in God’s seat for a day. And Moses, the greatest man in the camp, says there are causes too hard for the judges, and he gives them somewhere to take them.

We are not judges in a court, but we judge all day, in our heads, about the men beside us. Be as slow to condemn a brother as you hope God is with you.

Part three

Kadesh-barnea

Verses 19–33

The good land in sight, twelve men, and a people who would not go up.

Hard words in verses 19 to 28

  1. terribleto be feared; the wilderness was vast and awful
  2. discouragedhaving the courage taken out of you
  3. murmuredgrumbled, complained in a low voice
  4. Anakimsthe sons of Anak, tall men of the land; the spies named them (Numbers 13:33)
  5. walled up to heavenwalls so high that, to frightened men, they touched the sky

We read the spies and the unbelief in Numbers 13 and 14. Now we hear the same day from Moses’ mouth, and he adds what the people said when no one was listening.

Who asked for the spies?

The people, every one of youWe will send men before us, and they shall search us out the land.

MosesThe saying pleased me well: and I took twelve men of you, one of a tribe.

Numbers 13 gives the LORD’s word to Moses to send them. Here Moses reminds the sons that the asking began with the people. Both are true: the people asked, Moses agreed, and the LORD gave the word.

The report that came back began with “It is a good land.” The trouble was never the land, and in the end it was never the giants. It was what the people did with the report.

What they said in their tents

Look where it happened: “ye murmured in your tents.” Not in front of Moses, but in private. And look what they said about the LORD:

In the tents (verse 27)

“The LORD hated us”

He brought us out of Egypt, they said, to hand us to the Amorites and destroy us. The God who had just freed them, accused of wanting them dead.

The truth (verses 30–31)

“As a man doth bear his son”

The LORD “goeth before you, he shall fight for you”. He had carried them every step of the way, as a father carries a son.

Fear is a liar, and the first thing it lies about is God. A man who has failed lies awake and says, “God is done with me. He hates me.” That is the voice of the tent. Write down the truth beside it: “Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him” (Psalm 103:13).

Four things he had done, and “yet ye did not believe”

  1. He went before“which goeth before you” (30), in fire by night and a cloud by day (33)
  2. He fought for them“according to all that he did for you in Egypt before your eyes” (30)
  3. He carried them“as a man doth bear his son, in all the way that ye went” (31)
  4. He found the place“to search you out a place to pitch your tents in” (33)

And the sentence on their hearts: “Yet in this thing ye did not believe the LORD your God” (32). They had the plagues, the sea and the manna behind them. Unbelief is not a lack of evidence. It is a heart that will not lean its weight on him.

“The eternal God is thy refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms…”

Deuteronomy 33:27

Part four

Moses too, and the little ones

Verses 34–46

The oath, the two who followed fully, the children who go in, and a sorrow that would not turn.

Who goes in, who does not

  1. The men who refused“not one of these men of this evil generation” (35)
  2. Caleb“he shall see it” (36)
  3. Moses“Thou also shalt not go in thither” (37)
  4. Joshua“he shall go in thither” (38)
  5. The little ones“they shall possess it” (39)

Wholly followed means he did not hold part of himself back. Caleb was one man against a whole camp, and he was right. God keeps his word to the man who follows him all the way.

Even Moses answers for his sin

The reason is given at the waters of Meribah, years later:

“And the LORD spake unto Moses and Aaron, Because ye believed me not, to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel, therefore ye shall not bring this congregation into the land which I have given them.”

Numbers 20:12

The people’s sin dragged Moses down with it, and Moses sinned too, as Psalm 106:32–33 says. Even the best man in Israel answers for his own sin.

Encourage him

Look at what Moses does with his sentence. He does not sulk. He turns to Joshua, the man who will do what he cannot, and says: encourage him. A man who is shut out can still build up the next man.

Your sins may cost you something, a job, a trust, a year. They cannot cost you his love. And God did not leave Moses outside: he showed him the whole land from the mount (34:1–4), and Moses stood in the land, with Christ, on another mount (Matthew 17:3).

Sorry, and wrong again

The peopleWe have sinned against the LORD, we will go up and fight.

The LORDGo not up, neither fight; for I am not among you.

The peopleThey “went presumptuously up into the hill.”

Presumptuously means pushing ahead as if you had the right. They said “we have sinned,” and then did their own thing. Yesterday it was “I will not go,” today “I will go my way.” Neither was “Lord, what do you say?”

Two kinds of sorrow

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Godly sorrow turns and does what God says. The sorrow of the world only wants to feel better, and goes off to fix itself with a bigger promise to try harder. After you fall, go back to him and ask what he says:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Bees, tears, and many days

The Amorites came out of the hill “as bees do” and drove them down to Hormah. Then the people wept before the LORD, and he “would not hearken”. Tears had come too late for that road. It was the door they had refused the day before.

Moses does not say the LORD stopped loving them. He says the way up was closed that day, and the camp stayed on in Kadesh “many days”, then turned back toward the Red sea (2:1). The story goes on in chapter 2.

The little ones go in

Verse 39: “your little ones… they shall go in thither”. Moses is speaking to those little ones now, grown men, with the land in front of them. The LORD had said it in the wilderness:

“But your little ones, which ye said should be a prey, them will I bring in, and they shall know the land which ye have despised.”

Numbers 14:31

What your father or your old life threw away, God can still give to you. The sentence fell on them. Their children walked in.

Today, on this side Jordan

  1. Sinunbelief that said God hated us, and then went its own way
  2. Regretdo not stay at Kadesh-barnea for many days. The sentence is not yours to fix, but the turning is
  3. Gracehe bare them as a man doth bear his son, and his arms were underneath
  4. Hopethe little ones went in. A new generation, and a new day
  5. Todaysay out loud one thing God has done for you; then do the next thing he says, not the next thing you feel

“While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation.”

Hebrews 3:15

If it is heavy, call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor. You were not made to carry it by yourself.

Points to remember

  1. The place and the day“On this side Jordan, in the land of Moab, began Moses to declare this law” 1:5The fortieth year, the eleventh month, the first day: an old man tells the new generation the road.
  2. Not alone“I am not able to bear you myself alone” 1:9One man cannot carry a people. Share the load with men you know.
  3. The judgment is God’s“Ye shall not respect persons in judgment… for the judgment is God’s” 1:17Hear the small as well as the great, and fear no man’s face.
  4. What they said in their tents“Because the LORD hated us, he hath brought us forth out of the land of Egypt” 1:27Fear lies about God first. The truth was “he shall fight for you.”
  5. He bare thee“the LORD thy God bare thee, as a man doth bear his son” 1:31He went before, fought, and carried them, and still they did not believe.
  6. Who goes in“your little ones… they shall go in thither” 1:39Caleb and Joshua, and the children. Even Moses answered for his own sin.

Question 1 of 6

The place and the day

Where and when did Moses begin to declare this law?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Moses wrote this law, and delivered it unto the priests the sons of Levi, which bare the ark of the covenant of the LORD, and unto all the elders of Israel.”

Deuteronomy 31:9

Answer

In the land of Moab, on this side Jordan, in the fortieth year, in the eleventh month, on the first day of the month, after Sihon and Og were slain. Moses spoke it, and Moses wrote it.

Question 2 of 6

Not alone

Why did Moses say he could not bear the people by himself, and what did he do about it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou wilt surely wear away, both thou, and this people that is with thee: for this thing is too heavy for thee; thou art not able to perform it thyself alone.”

Exodus 18:18

Answer

The people had grown to be as the stars of heaven, and the load of their burden and strife was too heavy for one man. He took wise men, known among their tribes, and made them captains over thousands, hundreds, fifties and tens.

Question 3 of 6

The judgment is God’s

What were the judges told never to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They were never to respect persons in judgment, never to favour the great over the small, and never to be afraid of the face of man, “for the judgment is God’s”. The hard cases they were to bring to Moses.

Question 4 of 6

What they said in their tents

What did Israel say about the LORD when they would not go up?

What verses tell us about this?

“Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.”

Psalm 103:13

Answer

They murmured in their tents and said the LORD hated them, and had brought them out of Egypt to give them over to the Amorites to be destroyed. It was a lie about the God who had carried them, and only fear could tell it.

Question 5 of 6

He bare thee

How had the LORD carried them, and what would they not do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He went before them in fire by night and a cloud by day, fought for them, and bare them as a man bears his son, all the way they went. And still they would not believe him, so they would not go up.

Question 6 of 6

Who goes in

Which men would see the land, and who would possess it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Of the men who refused, not one, except Caleb, who wholly followed the LORD, and Joshua. Moses himself was shut out. But the little ones, whom their fathers said would be a prey, would go in and possess it.
