Paul is answering a troubled church he started in Corinth, about AD 55. Chapters 8 to 10 answer one question: should a believer eat meat that was offered to an idol? In chapter 8 he said knowing you're free isn't enough. Love comes first, and if my freedom makes a brother stumble, I'll give it up (8:13).

Chapter 9 is Paul proving he means it. He lays out what he gave up, then runs a race he doesn't intend to lose.

Part one

The man with rights

Verses 1–14

Paul has every right to be paid for his work. He lists them.

Four questions, four yeses

“Am I not an apostle?”YesSome in Corinth ran him down. An apostle was a man sent out by Jesus Himself.
“Am I not free?”YesFree to be paid, rested and looked after, like any worker.
“Have I not seen Jesus Christ our Lord?”YesOn the Damascus road (Acts 9), about AD 34 to 35.
“Are not ye my work in the Lord?”YesThe Corinthian church began under his hands (Acts 18).

A seal was a stamp pressed in wax to prove a letter or contract was real. Paul says the Corinthians themselves are his seal. Their changed lives are the proof, and a changed life is still the best proof there is.

What Paul was free to claim

“Power” here means right. A preacher on the road could ask a church for three things:

Food and drink, at the church's costverse 4

His wife along on the roadverse 5: the other apostles did, and the Lord's brothers, and Cephas (Peter)

Time off from his tent-makingverse 6: Paul and Barnabas worked with their hands to pay their own way

None of it was greedy. These were fair claims, and Paul says so. Hold on to that: he gives up things that were his.

The working ox

Paul gives four pictures: a soldier gets wages, a vineyard-keeper eats the grapes, a shepherd drinks the milk, and an ox that tramples out the grain is allowed to eat as he goes.

That last one is straight from the law (Deuteronomy 25:4). “Doth God take care for oxen?” means surely the law was about more than cattle. If God looks after the ox, He means the man who works for Him to be looked after too.

Fair pay for the work

“Carnal things” in verse 11 means ordinary, material things: food, a roof. Paul says: we gave you the things of the Spirit, and it isn't much to ask for bread in return. The priests in the temple ate from the sacrifices the same way (verse 13).

So a church that looks after its pastor is doing right, and a man who works hard has earned what he's paid. Nobody should feel guilty for fair wages.

Notice the end of verse 12: “Nevertheless we have not used this power.” The point of this whole list is what comes next.

Part two

Made all things to all men

Verses 15–23

Paul lays every one of those rights down, and tells us why.

“I have used none of these things”

Paid by the churcha right, and he asked for none of itnot used

Looked after on the roadhe paid his own waynot used

What he got instead“the gospel of Christ without charge”: no one could say he was in it for the money

Why? So that nothing he did would give anyone a reason to wave the gospel away. He wasn't trying to be a hero. He says he couldn't stay quiet: “woe is unto me, if I preach not.” A steward carries out a trust his Master gave him (Acts 9), and Paul wanted to carry it out cleanly.

All things to all men

With Jews he kept to their customs. With Gentiles he didn't make them keep Jewish ones. With the weak he was gentle. Free from every man, yet servant to all, so more might be won.

Somebody once did that for you. He sat down where you were, talked like you talked, and didn't make you clean up first. Now it's your turn to do it for the next man.

Beside them, not back with them

What he did

met them where they were

Ate at their tables, learned how they thought, spoke so they could understand, and never made them feel small.

What he never did

joined them in their sin

“Not without law to God.” He stayed under Christ's rule the whole time. Meeting a man isn't the same as going back to what ruined him.

That line matters for us. You can sit with the man who's still using, and be a friend to him. You can't go back to his place to prove you're not above it. Know where your own line is, and keep to it.

Part three

So run

Verses 24–27

Corinth's own games were a few miles from the city wall. Paul picks his picture from there.

Corinth's games

Every two years or so, crowds came to the Isthmus, the narrow neck beside Corinth, for the Isthmian Games: foot races, wrestling, boxing, chariots. They were among the biggest games in the Greek world, and every man in the Corinthian church had seen them.

In a race, everyone runs and one wins. Paul isn't saying only one Christian gets a prize. He's saying: run like the one who means to win.

A crown that doesn't wither

The winner at the Isthmus was handed a wreath of leaves. It was cut fresh and was dead in days. “Temperate in all things” (verse 25) means every runner held back from food, drink and late nights while he trained, for a prize like that.

We run for one that lasts. It is not salvation: that's a gift, never earned. This crown is a reward for how a saved man serves.

Running on purpose

Paul has two pictures. A runner who can see the finish line and heads for it, and a boxer whose punches land, not one throwing blows at the air. “I keep under my body” is the language of a boxer: he hits his own body hard enough to make it obey.

Our bodies want the easy road. A drifting man never runs a straight line. He only follows whatever pulls him next.

Do one thing today. Name your hardest hour (when, where, with whom), and decide before it comes what you'll do. A man with a plan isn't trusting his mood to hold.

What “castaway” means

Not this

losing his salvation

Jesus says of His own: “they shall never perish.”

This

disqualified from the prize

The Greek word is adokimos: tested and not approved. An athlete who broke the rules was sent off the field, with no crown. A saved man can still lose his usefulness and his reward.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

“If any man’s work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss: but he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire.”

1 Corinthians 3:15

If you fell yesterday, you haven't been put out of the stadium. Paul himself writes later, “forgetting those things which are behind… I press toward the mark.” Get up, and run today's stretch.

Points to remember

  1. Rights“Nevertheless we have not used this power” 9:12Paul laid down rights that were really his, so nothing would block the gospel. Love will do that.
  2. Paying preachers“They which preach the gospel should live of the gospel” 9:14Yes. The worker is worth his wages, and a church that supports its pastor does right.
  3. Reaching people“I am made all things to all men, that I might by all means save some” 9:22Meet a man where he is, without going back to where he was.
  4. The prize“They do it to obtain a corruptible crown; but we an incorruptible” 9:25A wreath dies in days. We run for a reward that stays, given to a saved man for faithful service.
  5. Discipline“I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection” 9:27Run with a plan. A man who drifts never runs straight.
  6. Castaway“Lest… I myself should be a castaway” 9:27Disqualified from the prize, not lost again. Get up and run.

Question 1 of 6

Rights

What rights did Paul have, and why did he lay them down?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore, if meat make my brother to offend, I will eat no flesh while the world standeth, lest I make my brother to offend.”

1 Corinthians 8:13

Answer

He could have been paid, fed, and travelled with his wife. He used none of it, so that nothing would get in the gospel's way and no one could say he was in it for money. A right is something you can give up for love.

Question 2 of 6

Paying preachers

Should a man who preaches the gospel be supported?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou shalt not muzzle the ox when he treadeth out the corn.”

Deuteronomy 25:4

Answer

Yes. The law fed the ox that worked, and the Lord ordained that those who preach the gospel live of the gospel. Paul's choice to go without was his own, not a rule for everyone else.

Question 3 of 6

Reaching people

What does “I am made all things to all men” mean?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He went to people where they were, with respect for their customs, so that he could win them. He never joined them in sin: he stayed “under the law to Christ.” Meet a man where he is without going back to where he was.

Question 4 of 6

The prize

What are we running for?

What verses tell us about this?

“I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 3:14

Answer

An incorruptible crown: a lasting reward from Christ for a saved man's faithful service. A wreath at the Isthmus died in days. This one won't.

Question 5 of 6

Discipline

Why does Paul “keep under” his body?

What verses tell us about this?

“All things are lawful unto me, but all things are not expedient: all things are lawful for me, but I will not be brought under the power of any.”

1 Corinthians 6:12

Answer

So that he runs on purpose and finishes, and so that no appetite is his master. A man who trains is a man who has decided ahead of time.

Question 6 of 6

Castaway

What does Paul mean by “castaway”?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

“If any man’s work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss: but he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire.”

1 Corinthians 3:15

Answer

Disqualified from the prize, like an athlete sent off the field, and not lost again. A believer is safe in Christ's hand, but he can lose his reward and his usefulness. That's reason to stay in the race, not to quit it.
