Exodus 2

Drawn out of the water

A baby in a basket, a prince who kills a man and runs, a shepherd in the desert for forty years, and a God who hears groaning and remembers a promise.

Part one

A baby in the river

Verses 1–10

Pharaoh's order puts a baby in the river. God puts him in Pharaoh's house.

Three months

The parents are not named here (we learn in 6:20 that they were Amram and Jochebed). “Goodly” is an old word: fine, beautiful. She saw something in him and she hid him for three months, in a land where the order was to throw every son into the river (1:22).

“By faith Moses, when he was born, was hid three months of his parents, because they saw he was a proper child; and they were not afraid of the king’s commandment.”

Hebrews 11:23

Hebrews calls it faith. She had no plan and no army. She had a baby, a promise her people had been holding for centuries, and a God she believed in more than the king. “They were not afraid of the king's commandment.”

An ark

תֵּבָה said tevah “ark”: a chest, a box that floats. Used in the whole Bible only for Noah's ark and this one.
  1. daubedsmeared all over
  2. slime and pitchtar, to make it waterproof
  3. flagsreeds growing in the shallows

In both cases a boat carried God's rescue through the water. It carried one family in Noah's day, and in Moses' day it carried one baby, the man God would use to bring a nation through.

The baby cried

Of everyone who could have found the basket, it was Pharaoh's own daughter. A king who ordered every Hebrew boy into the river, and his daughter lifts one out. She knew exactly what he was: “This is one of the Hebrews' children.” And she had pity anyway.

Notice that nothing here is a miracle. The river didn't part, the basket didn't glow. A girl went down to wash, and a baby cried. God does most of his work like that: in the ordinary, with the right person at the right place.

“But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive.”

Genesis 50:20

That is how Joseph put it about his own life. Pharaoh meant the river for death. God used the same river for life.

Paid to nurse her own son

His sisterShall I go and call to thee a nurse of the Hebrew women?

Pharaoh's daughterTake this child away, and nurse it for me, and I will give thee thy wages.

The baby goes home to his own mother, and the king's daughter pays her to nurse him. Nobody planned that but God.

He grew up at home first, likely hearing about the God of Abraham.

Moses: drawn out

מֹשֶׁה said Mosheh from the Hebrew “to draw out”: “Because I drew him out of the water”

The name Moses means drawn out. Stephen says that in the palace he “was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and was mighty in words and in deeds” (Acts 7:22). The best education in the world. For forty years he was a prince.

“He sent from above, he took me, he drew me out of many waters.”

Psalm 18:16

David wrote that, and any man who has been pulled out of something can say it. God drew Moses out of the water. He is the same God who draws men out of the deep.

Part two

A prince on the run

Verses 11–15

The same man who was drawn out of the water gets into deep water of his own.

“This way and that way”

Moses was forty (Acts 7:23). He went out to his own people, saw how they suffered, and cared. That was good. Then he saw an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, checked that no one was watching, and killed him. “Slew” is an old word for killed. He hid the body in the sand.

Stephen says what was in his heart: “he supposed his brethren would have understood how that God by his hand would deliver them” (Acts 7:25). He wanted to help, in his own way and his own strength. That is anger doing the work, and it cost a man his life.

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath: For the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God.”

James 1:19–20

Right goal, wrong way. Many of us have wanted to protect someone and used our fists or our temper. The text doesn't excuse it.

Hidden, and then known

“intendest thou to kill me, as thou killedst the Egyptian?” (do you mean to). The secret was out, and Pharaoh “sought to slay Moses”.

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

Hidden sin follows a man. Confess: lift the cover.

Forsake it, and where the sin was, there is mercy.

The same question, forty years on

Forty years beforerefused

Forty years latersent

“Who made thee a ruler and a judge?”the Hebrew who was beating his fellow
“The same did God send to be a ruler and a deliverer”Acts 7:35

“This Moses whom they refused, saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge? the same did God send to be a ruler and a deliverer by the hand of the angel which appeared to him in the bush.”

Acts 7:35

The man his own people rejected is the man God sent. God had a better way to make him a judge. Moses had to lose everything first, to learn that he was not the deliverer; God was.

Into the desert

Midian was in Arabia, east of the gulf. The Bible doesn't give Moses' road; the line is one likely way, round the head of the gulf: weeks of walking, from a palace to a shepherd's life.

The Midianites came from Abraham by Keturah (Genesis 25:1–2): Moses ran to distant family.

And he came to the mountain where God would meet him.

Part three

A stranger in a strange land

Verses 16–25

Forty years of nothing, and then the cry of a nation.

At the well

Shepherds drove the seven daughters away from the water. Moses stood up and helped them, and watered their flock. The same quick sense of right and wrong that got him into trouble in Egypt is still in him. Now it's used to help, and no one is hurt.

The priest of Midian is called Reuel here and Jethro in 3:1: the same man, with two names. “Reuel” means friend of God. “Call him, that he may eat bread” is how welcome works in a desert: a meal first, and questions later.

God does not delete who a man is. He takes the strength, even the temper, and puts it to work in the right place.

Gershom

גֵּרְשֹׁם said Gershom “a stranger there”: “I have been a stranger in a strange land”

Moses had a wife, Zipporah, and a son, and he was “content to dwell” there. That word means he was willing, and settled. But he named his son after the ache: a stranger in a strange land. A prince of Egypt and a shepherd in the desert, belonging to neither place.

A man who has blown up his own life knows that feeling. This is where God finds him.

Moses' three forties

  1. 0–40A prince in EgyptActs 7:23
  2. 40–80A shepherd in MidianActs 7:30
  3. 80–120Leading Israel7:7; Deuteronomy 34:7

Forty years in Egypt, forty in Midian, forty in the wilderness (Acts 7:23, 30, 36). Moses was eighty when God called him (7:7). He lived a hundred and twenty years, and the part he is remembered for began after eighty.

“By faith Moses, when he was come to years, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh’s daughter; Choosing rather to suffer affliction with the people of God, than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season; Esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures in Egypt: for he had respect unto the recompence of the reward.”

Hebrews 11:24–26

The years in Midian were not wasted. In forty years Moses learned the desert, the same ground that Israel would cross. And somewhere in those years he stopped being the man who thought he was the answer.

Four things God did

  1. heardtheir groaning
  2. rememberedHis covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob
  3. looked uponthe children of Israel
  4. had respecttook notice, and cared

The king of Egypt died, and nothing changed. The slavery went on. Then the people “sighed”, and “cried”, and the cry “came up unto God”. Nothing is said about good prayers. It was groaning. And God heard.

He had said it would take this long

“And he said unto Abram, Know of a surety that thy seed shall be a stranger in a land that is not theirs, and shall serve them; and they shall afflict them four hundred years; And also that nation, whom they shall serve, will I judge: and afterward shall they come out with great substance.”

Genesis 15:13–14

God told Abraham four hundred years of this, ahead of time, and that he would judge the nation that enslaved them and bring them out “with great substance”. “Remembered” doesn't mean he had forgotten. It means he moved, in his own time, to do what he had promised.

If you feel forgotten, remember: four hundred years looked like silence to Israel. It was not. Tell a brother in Christ, or your pastor, what you are carrying. God hears, and he sends help.

Points to remember

  1. Providence“She called his name Moses: and she said, Because I drew him out of the water” 2:10Pharaoh's order, Pharaoh's river, Pharaoh's daughter: God used all of it.
  2. Faith“By faith Moses, when he was born, was hid three months of his parents” Hebrews 11:23A small hiding place, a big faith.
  3. Sin“he slew the Egyptian, and hid him in the sand” 2:12Anger and hiding: the sin of a man who meant well.
  4. Regret“I have been a stranger in a strange land” 2:22He ran, and it cost him forty years. It did not cost him God.
  5. God hears“God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant” 2:24God hears groaning, and keeps promises.
  6. Hope“the same did God send to be a ruler and a deliverer” Acts 7:35A man's worst day is not his last word.

Question 1 of 6

Providence

How did Pharaoh's own daughter end up raising the baby Pharaoh wanted dead?

What verses tell us about this?

“But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive.”

Genesis 50:20

Answer

A baby cried, a girl pitied him, and a sister spoke up. Pharaoh meant the river for death, and God used it for life, in Pharaoh's own house. No miracle was seen, but God was in all of it: “God meant it unto good.”

Question 2 of 6

Faith

What did Moses' mother do, and what does Hebrews say about it?

What verses tell us about this?

“By faith Moses, when he was born, was hid three months of his parents, because they saw he was a proper child; and they were not afraid of the king’s commandment.”

Hebrews 11:23

Answer

She hid him for three months, and when she couldn't any longer she made a basket and put him in the river, in the hands of God. Hebrews calls it faith: they “were not afraid of the king's commandment”. Faith isn't a feeling. It's doing the next right thing, trusting God with the rest.

Question 3 of 6

Sin

What did Moses do when he was forty, and how did he handle it?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

Answer

He killed a man in anger, hid the body in the sand, and ran when it came out. The Bible does not dress it up. The way out is not to hide: “whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Question 4 of 6

Regret

Where did Moses end up after he ran, and what did he say about his life?

What verses tell us about this?

“And when he was full forty years old, it came into his heart to visit his brethren the children of Israel.”

Acts 7:23

Answer

In a desert in Midian, as a shepherd, a prince with no palace, naming his son for the ache: “a stranger in a strange land”. A man with a past knows that feeling. It cost him forty years. It did not cost him God.

Question 5 of 6

God hears

What did God hear, and what did he remember?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Psalm 34:18

Answer

He heard their groaning, and remembered his covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. He looked on them and cared. God is never deaf to a man at the end of his strength, and he keeps his promises when the silence seems endless.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

What does Moses' story say to a man whose worst day is behind him, but still weighs on him?

What verses tell us about this?

“This Moses whom they refused, saying, Who made thee a ruler and a judge? the same did God send to be a ruler and a deliverer by the hand of the angel which appeared to him in the bush.”

Acts 7:35

“Choosing rather to suffer affliction with the people of God, than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season;”

Hebrews 11:25

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

Answer

That God uses men with a past. The man who killed and ran is the man God sent, and Hebrews counts him as a man of faith. Christ came to save sinners, even the worst. Your worst day is not your last word.
