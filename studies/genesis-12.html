Genesis 12

Get thee out

God calls a seventy-five-year-old man to leave everything he knows and promises to bless every family on earth through him. Then Abram gets scared and lies, and God keeps His promise anyway.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. God's callWhat did God ask Abram to leave, and what did He promise?
  2. FaithHow did Abram answer God's call?
  3. God's promiseWho is the promise really for, in the end?
  4. WorshipWhat did Abram do wherever he pitched his tent?

Part one

The call

Verses 1–3

God speaks to a man of seventy-five and asks him to leave everything, with nothing in his hand but a promise.

A call that took years

  1. UrThe God of glory appears to AbramActs 7:2–3
  2. HaranThe family stops halfway; Terah diesGenesis 11:31–32
  3. Haran, age 75“So Abram departed”12:4
  4. Canaan“Unto thy seed will I give this land”12:7

“Now the LORD had said.” The call came earlier. Stephen says God first appeared to Abram in Mesopotamia, “before he dwelt in Charran,” and that Abram moved on “when his father was dead” (Acts 7:2–4).

Abram's father Terah had started for Canaan and stopped at Haran. Maybe you've heard God's voice, started toward something, and stalled halfway. Abram's story says God is patient with a man who stalls, and glad when he finally gets up and goes.

Leave, and I will

What God asked him to leave

Three things

His country, his kindred (his relatives), and his father's house: home, family and security. Everything a man leans on.

What God promised

“I will, I will, I will”

Show him the land. Make him a great nation. Bless him. Make his name great. Make him a blessing. In the whole list, God does the work. Abram's part is to go.

God asks for the leaving first and shows the land later. Abram set out with a promise and no map.

“By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out into a place which he should after receive for an inheritance, obeyed; and he went out, not knowing whither he went.”

Hebrews 11:8

Every man here has left something: the old crowd, the old places, the old habits. God didn't tell you to leave and then walk off. The One who says “get thee out” says “I will.”

All families of the earth

Abramhis seedChristthe blessing reachesall families of the earth

God's answer to everything that went wrong in the first eleven chapters starts with one old man and his tent. The New Testament says plainly what the promise was about:

“And the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the heathen through faith, preached before the gospel unto Abraham, saying, In thee shall all nations be blessed.”

Galatians 3:8

“Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.”

Galatians 3:16

“Heathen” means the nations, everyone who wasn't a Jew, and “justify” means to declare righteous. That includes you. The promise that God made to Abram, in a tent, four thousand years ago, was aimed at you too.

Part two

Into the land

Verses 4–9

He went, and wherever he stopped, he worshipped.

Haran to Canaan

Abram was seventy-five. This was about 2090 BC. From Haran to Canaan was several hundred miles on foot, with a whole household, flocks and tents.

The line starts at Ur because that is where the call first came. The family went on together to Haran, and Abram left from there.

Lot, his dead brother's son, comes along (12:4). Remember his name. He is the whole story of the next two chapters.

Four old words

  1. substanceeverything they owned: flocks, tents, silver
  2. the souls they had gottenthe people they had gathered at Haran: servants and workers who joined the household
  3. kindredrelatives
  4. seeddescendants: children, grandchildren, and on down

Abram was not a poor wanderer. He was a wealthy chief with a large household. In chapter 14 he can call out 318 trained men from it. And still God asked him to leave it all behind and trust Him.

Wherever he pitched a tent

  1. ShechemThe LORD appears; Abram builds an altar12:6–7
  2. BethelA tent, an altar, and he calls on the LORD12:8
  3. The southA famine in the land12:9–10
  4. EgyptNo altar is mentioned12:10–20

The tent was for Abram. The altar was for God. In the land of promise he did both, in that order, everywhere he stopped. Notice what happens on the timeline when the altar disappears.

Sichem is the old spelling of Shechem. The “plain of Moreh” was a grove of big oaks. Bethel means “house of God,” and Hai is the town the book of Joshua calls Ai.

A guest in the land

It wasn't an empty country, and the promise was for his seed, later. Abram lived there as a guest and waited, and what he built was altars, not houses. (In the quote, tabernacles are tents, and sojourned means lived as a stranger.)

“By faith he sojourned in the land of promise, as in a strange country, dwelling in tabernacles with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise: For he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God.”

Hebrews 11:9–10

Here is the real-world question: where is your altar? A chair, a time, a spot where every day you stop and talk to God before the day takes over.

Part three

Down to Egypt

Verses 10–20

Abram's faith was real. So was his fear. Here is what happens when the two meet.

How a good man slides

  1. a faminethe hard time was real (10)
  2. afraid“they will kill me” (12)
  3. a plan“Say… thou art my sister” (13)
  4. Sarai takeninto Pharaoh's house (15)

Genesis does not say God told Abram to go to Egypt, and it does not say he asked. He went down to sojourn, to live there for a while as a stranger. The famine was grievous, heavy and hard to bear. This was a real squeeze, not an excuse. But Abram started doing the thinking that God had not asked for.

“The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.”

Proverbs 29:25

A snare is a trap, and whoso means whoever.

The half-truth

Abram's thinkingThey'll see you, kill me, and keep you. Say you're my sister, so it goes well with me.

What the plan didIt put his wife in harm's way to cover himself, and it put the promise in danger. “I will make of thee a great nation” needs a living Abram and Sarai as his wife. the promise at risk

Genesis 20:12 says Sarai really was his half-sister, so it was a half-truth. A true fact used to deceive is still a lie.

Say it plainly, without shame: Abram was a scared man with God's promise in his pocket (12:7), and he went for a plan of his own. Most of us know the feeling.

Paid for the lie

What Abram got (16)

Sheep, oxen, donkeys, camels

Sheep and oxen, he asses and she asses (donkeys), camels, menservants and maidservants. Entreated means treated: Pharaoh treated him well.

What it cost (15)

His wife

Sarai was taken into Pharaoh's house, and Abram stood by with a bigger herd. The gain was real and so was the price.

Money that comes out of a lie is not a blessing. It is a bill. The herds in the next chapter get too big to share, and some of those animals came from this gift. Hagar, the Egyptian servant girl in chapter 16, may have joined his household in Egypt. Genesis doesn't say.

Rebuked by Pharaoh

PharaohWhat is this that thou hast done unto me? Why didn't you tell me she was your wife? Here she is. Take her, and go.

Abram… no answer is recorded

The man who was to bless all the families of the earth stood speechless while a pagan king lectured him. He had it coming, and it was fair.

“Plagues” is the old word for blows. Verse 17 doesn't say what they were. It says the LORD did it, unasked. Abram never prayed. God stepped in on His own.

Up, down, and God's hand

Look at who turns the line around. Abram doesn't repent out loud in this chapter, and he doesn't fix a thing. God does.

God's own comment

Centuries later, a psalm looks back on exactly this story (reproved means rebuked, anointed means chosen and set apart):

“When they were but a few men in number; yea, very few, and strangers in it. When they went from one nation to another, from one kingdom to another people; He suffered no man to do them wrong: yea, he reproved kings for their sakes; Saying, Touch not mine anointed, and do my prophets no harm.”

Psalm 105:12–15

That is grace: God keeps His promise because of who He is, not because Abram deserved it. It isn't a license to lie. It is the reason a man who has fallen can get up, and next chapter, Abram does.

Fear writes a plan. Faith builds an altar.

The squeeze will come today: money, a boss, a woman, a temptation, a temper. Before you start making a plan, do two things.

  1. Say it to God“Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee” (Psalm 55:22). Sustain means hold up
  2. Say it to a brotherTell a brother in Christ, or your pastor, the real thing before you act

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

And if you've already done what Abram did, the lie told, the plan made, the cost running, you can bring it into the light today. God doesn't ask you to fix it first.

Points to remember

  1. God's call“Get thee out… unto a land that I will shew thee” 12:1God asks us to leave the old life, and He goes ahead into the new one with promises.
  2. Faith“So Abram departed, as the LORD had spoken unto him” 12:4Faith is getting up and going on God's word, before the map comes.
  3. God's promise“In thee shall all families of the earth be blessed” 12:3The promise reaches every nation through Abram's seed, Christ.
  4. Worship“There builded he an altar unto the LORD” 12:7A tent for himself, an altar for God. Keep a place and a time for Him every day.
  5. Sin“Say, I pray thee, thou art my sister” 12:13Fear leads to plans, and plans lead to half-truths. Bring it to God and a brother first.
  6. Grace“The LORD plagued Pharaoh and his house” 12:17God protected Abram and the promise when Abram didn't. He does not give up on His men.

Question 1 of 6

God's call

What did God ask Abram to leave, and what did He promise?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God.”

Hebrews 11:10

Answer

His country, his relatives, and his father's house: everything a man leans on. In return God promised a land, a great nation, a blessing, and a great name. Abram was really being offered a city whose builder is God. God never asks a man to leave something without promising Himself in its place.

Question 2 of 6

Faith

How did Abram answer God's call?

What verses tell us about this?

“By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out into a place which he should after receive for an inheritance, obeyed; and he went out, not knowing whither he went.”

Hebrews 11:8

Answer

He obeyed, and he went. He was seventy-five and didn't know where he was headed. Faith in the Bible is not a feeling. It is getting up and doing what God said before you can see how it ends.

Question 3 of 6

God's promise

Who is the promise really for, in the end?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the heathen through faith, preached before the gospel unto Abraham, saying, In thee shall all nations be blessed.”

Galatians 3:8

“Now to Abraham and his seed were the promises made. He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ.”

Galatians 3:16

Answer

Every family on earth, through one descendant of Abram: Jesus Christ. Paul says this was the gospel, preached ahead of time to Abraham. It was never only about one nation or one man. Anyone who comes to God through faith in Jesus is inside the blessing.

Question 4 of 6

Worship

What did Abram do wherever he pitched his tent?

What verses tell us about this?

“Commit thy way unto the LORD; trust also in him; and he shall bring it to pass.”

Psalm 37:5

Answer

He built an altar and called on the name of the LORD. A tent for himself, an altar for God, at Shechem and again at Bethel. It is a simple pattern a man can copy: keep a place and a time every day where you stop and talk to God.

Question 5 of 6

Sin

What did fear make Abram do in Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

“The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.”

Proverbs 29:25

“It is better to trust in the LORD than to put confidence in man.”

Psalm 118:8

Answer

It made him lie. He told Sarai to say she was his sister, so Pharaoh would not kill him for her. That put his wife in harm's way and put God's promise at risk. A believer can still be a coward when he is afraid. The way out is to trust the LORD and to tell a brother the truth before the plan gets made.

Question 6 of 6

Grace

Did God give up on Abram after he lied?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

No. God stepped in, brought Sarai back, and sent Abram out safe with everything he had. The promise did not depend on Abram being perfect. It depended on God. That is not permission to sin. It is the reason a man who failed yesterday can come back to the altar today.
