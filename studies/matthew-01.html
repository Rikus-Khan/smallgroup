The Gospel of Matthew

Who: Matthew (Levi), one of the twelve: a tax collector at Capernaum, despised by his own people, until Jesus said, “Follow me” (9:9).

When: probably about AD 50 to 70.

To whom: Jews first, then all nations. He keeps showing from the Old Testament that Jesus is the promised King.

The New Testament opens with two thousand years of history in seventeen verses: Abraham, David, Babylon, and then Christ.

The road on the map runs the same way: born in Bethlehem, raised in Nazareth, preaching around Capernaum, then up to Jerusalem, the cross, and the empty tomb.

Israel had waited for its King for centuries. For about four hundred years since Malachi, God had sent no new prophet. Matthew's first line says the wait is over.

Part one

The family tree

Verses 1–17

Forty-two generations, and every one of them was leading to Him.

Two promises in one line

To Abraham, about 2000 BC

A blessing for the world

“In thee shall all families of the earth be blessed” (Genesis 12:3).

To David, about 1000 BC

A King for ever

“Thy throne shall be established for ever” (2 Samuel 7:16).

Every Jew knew these promises. Matthew's first sentence says: here is the Man they were about. He is David's heir, the rightful King. And He is Abraham's seed, the blessing for all families, not one nation only. That includes yours.

Grace in the family tree

Family lists usually named only men. Matthew names four women, and each one is a surprise.

Thamarthe King's lineHer father-in-law Judah got her with child, thinking she was a harlot (Genesis 38).
Rachabthe King's lineA Canaanite harlot of Jericho who hid Israel's spies and trusted their God (Joshua 2).
Ruththe King's lineFrom Moab, a people shut out of Israel's assembly (Deuteronomy 23:3).
the wife of Uriasthe King's lineBathsheba. David took her and had her husband killed (2 Samuel 11).

If your story has shame in it, so does His family tree. Jesus wasn't ashamed of it. He came for people like these.

Good kings, bad kings, and Babylon

Real kings, with real records. The worst was Manasses: idols, and “innocent blood very much” (2 Kings 21:16). Yet in chains in Babylon, he turned back:

“And when he was in affliction, he besought the LORD his God, and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers, And prayed unto him: and he was intreated of him, and heard his supplication …”

2 Chronicles 33:12–13

The pattern breaks

Count the word “begat” in verses 2 to 16:

2×3 3×3 4×3 5×3 6×2 7×3 8×3 9×3 10×3 11×1 12×2 13×3 14×3 15×3 16×1

Thirty-nine times, father begat son. Then it stops. Joseph didn't beget Jesus: Mary is the one “of whom was born Jesus.”

Joseph gave Jesus His legal place in David's royal line. But Jesus had no human father. Verse 18 tells us why.

Three sets of fourteen

Abraham to Davidthe promise
David to Babylonthe kings
Babylon to Christthe waiting

Matthew sets the names out in three groups to make them easy to learn, and he skips a few names to do it. That was a normal way to write a family line. In Hebrew, the letters of David's name add up to fourteen.

The point: history wasn't random. God was carrying His promise through every generation, through the good years and the shameful ones, right up to Christ.

Part two

God with us

Verses 18–25

A good man gets news that breaks his heart, and then a word from God.

“Espoused”

  1. Espousedbound by law, called husband and wife
  2. Found with childbefore they came together
  3. The weddingoften about a year later; she moves into his house

Espousal was more than our engagement: it took a divorce to end it. That's why Joseph is already “her husband” (v. 19).

Then Mary was expecting, and Joseph knew it wasn't his. As far as he could tell, she had been unfaithful.

A just man, and a kind one

Joseph believed he'd been wronged. The law gave him the right to expose her in public. He could have made her pay.

He chose to divorce her “privily”: quietly, with no public trial, so she wouldn't be shamed.

That's real strength: doing right without being cruel, even when you're hurting.

A name that says what He does

All three mean “the LORD saves.” God chose the name before the baby was born, and the angel explained it: “for he shall save his people from their sins.”

Not from Rome. Not just from trouble. From sins: from their guilt, and from their grip. That's the rescue every one of us needs most.

Written about 700 years before

Isaiah 7:14, about 735 BC

Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.

Matthew 1:23

Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us.

This is the first of many times Matthew says “that it might be fulfilled.” God said it ahead of time, and then God did it. A virgin birth is a sign only God could give.

Emmanuel

עִמָּנוּ אֵל said immanu El immanu = with us  +  El = God

Not God far off, watching. God with us: born as a baby, in a real family, in a hard world.

Matthew ends his book the same way. Jesus' last words in it are, “Lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world” (28:20). In a hospital, in a cell, at 3 a.m. when the craving hits, He is with you.

Joseph doesn't say a word. He obeys.

The angel of the Lord (v. 20)“Fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife.”

Joseph (v. 24)“… and took unto him his wife.”

The angel of the Lord (v. 21)“Thou shalt call his name JESUS.”

Joseph (v. 25)“… and he called his name JESUS.”

The Bible never records a word Joseph said, only what he did. He took on the gossip, because God said so. He'll do it three more times in chapter 2.

Points to remember

  1. Who Jesus is“The son of David, the son of Abraham” 1:1Jesus is the rightful King, with a real family in real history.
  2. God's promises“That it might be fulfilled” 1:22God kept His word across two thousand years. He keeps it still.
  3. Grace“Of her that had been the wife of Urias” 1:6Sinners and outsiders are in His line. Nobody is beyond His grace.
  4. A godly man“Being a just man” 1:19Right without being cruel, and quick to obey when God speaks.
  5. Salvation“He shall save his people from their sins” 1:21Jesus saves from sin itself: its guilt and its grip.
  6. God with us“God with us” 1:23God didn't stay far off. He came close, and He stays.

Question 1 of 6

Who Jesus is

Why does Matthew start with a family tree?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To show who Jesus is: the son of David, the rightful King, and the son of Abraham, the promised blessing for all nations. His family line is real history, and God moved through every generation of it to bring Him.

Question 2 of 6

God's promises

Did God keep the promises He made to Abraham and David?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Yes. A blessing for the world, a King for ever, a virgin's Son. It took centuries, through exile and silence, but every word came true in Jesus. The God who kept those promises will keep His promises to you.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

What kind of people are in Jesus' family line?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Sinners and outsiders: a scandal in Judah's house, a Canaanite harlot, a woman of Moab, David's adultery, a king as wicked as Manasses. God put them on the first page. Jesus came for people just like that.

Question 4 of 6

A godly man

What kind of man was Joseph?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A just man who was also merciful: wronged, as he thought, he still wouldn't shame her. And when God spoke, he got up and obeyed, whatever it cost his name.

Question 5 of 6

Salvation (soteriology)

What did Jesus come to save us from?

What verses tell us about this?

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

John 3:16

Answer

From our sins: their guilt before God and their grip on our lives. His very name means “the LORD saves.” And the offer is for whosoever believes in Him.

Question 6 of 6

God with us

What does the name Emmanuel mean for us?

What verses tell us about this?

“Lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.”

Matthew 28:20

Answer

“God with us.” In Jesus, God came near: a real baby, in a real family. He didn't come to watch from far off. He is with His people always, on the worst days too.
