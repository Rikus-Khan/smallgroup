Part one

“Remember this day”

Verses 1–10

They are still hearing the words of the night they left. God tells them what to keep, and what to tell their children.

“It is mine”

  • sanctifyset apart for God, as his own
  • firstbornthe first son born in a family (and the first young of the flock)
  • openeth the wombthe one who is born first

Only days before, God had struck every firstborn in Egypt and passed over the houses marked with the blood (12:12–13). Israel's firstborn lived. So God says plainly: those sons are mine. They were rescued, so they belong to the one who rescued them.

That is not a threat. It is how a rescue works. A man who has been pulled out of the water does not say the lifeguard has no claim on him. He is the lifeguard's friend for the rest of his life.

The month Abib

Abib → Nisan Abib means a young ear of grain, the barley just turning green: spring, about March into April. After the exile the Jews called the same month Nisan.

God gave Israel a new calendar when he gave them a new life (12:2). The year began with the day they were set free.

Remember this day. The first thing God asks of a rescued people is to remember the rescue: “by strength of hand the LORD brought you out.” Not by their strength, and not by Moses'. If you know the day you came to Christ, or the day you last picked up, write it down somewhere you will see it.

Leaven

Leaven is yeast: a little piece of old dough that works through the whole lump and makes it rise. For seven days Israel ate flat bread, and there was to be no leaven “seen with thee in all thy quarters”, which means all through the house and the borders of the land.

“Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us:”

1 Corinthians 5:7

The old leaven

Malice and wickedness

A little of the old life, left in the house, spreads.

The unleavened bread

Sincerity and truth

“Therefore let us keep the feast” (1 Corinthians 5:8). Christ our passover is already sacrificed.

Hand, eyes and mouth

  • upon thine handwhat you do: the work of your hands carries the memory
  • between thine eyeswhat you see: a memorial, a reminder in front of you
  • in thy mouthwhat you say: “the LORD’S law may be in thy mouth”

Later Jewish teachers took the words of verse 9 and verse 16 literally, and wore small boxes holding these verses on the arm and forehead. Whether or not it was meant literally, the point is clear in the verse: God's word should be on a man's hands, before his eyes and in his mouth, so he cannot go a day without remembering what the LORD did.

An ordinance is a standing rule. “From year to year” means every spring, not once. Rescue is remembered again and again, because men forget fast. A man in recovery knows that: the meetings, the verses, the phone calls are all ways of keeping a memorial in front of his eyes.

Part two

“Thou shalt redeem”

Verses 11–16

What belongs to the LORD can be bought back, and when the son asks why, there is a story to tell.

Matrix, firstling, redeem

  • matrixthe womb
  • firstlingthe first young one born to an animal
  • redeembuy back with a price, so what was owed is paid
  • assa donkey

Clean animals, like a firstborn lamb, were given to the LORD. A donkey was not an animal for the altar, so a lamb was given in its place, to redeem it. If a man would not pay, the donkey was not to be kept as his own, and its neck was to be broken. God was serious that what is his is not taken back.

Notice the last words of verse 13: all the firstborn of man among thy children shalt thou redeem. Not one son was to be offered up. Each one was bought back with a price.

The lamb takes the place of the son. That was the lesson of the whole night in Egypt, and every family was to learn it again with every firstborn.

The men in this room have been in debt to sin. Someone had to pay. God does not say “try harder.” He says: here is the price, already paid.

Redeemed with the blood of a lamb

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Peter says it outright. We were redeemed, bought back, not with silver or gold but with the blood of Christ, the Lamb. Corruptible means perishing, and conversation means way of life.

“(As it is written in the law of the Lord, Every male that openeth the womb shall be called holy to the Lord;)”

Luke 2:23

When Jesus was a baby, Mary and Joseph brought him to the temple because of this very law. The firstborn son who was presented to the LORD grew up to be the Lamb who paid everyone else's price. If that is who bought you, you are not your own.

The father does not answer with a rule. He tells a story, and the story is: he did it, and he did it for us. Children will ask, and so will younger men in the church. Be ready to say what the LORD did for you, in two or three plain sentences.

Part three

“God led the people about”

Verses 17–22

The short road would have been the quick one. God chose the long road, and he went in front.

Two roads

The short road ran along the coast from Egypt to Gaza, in the land of the Philistines. It was “near”, and it would have been quick.

God took them the other way: across the Sinai, to the sea at Nuweiba, and on to the mountain of God in Arabia. The map follows the route as Ron Wyatt draws it.

“Lest peradventure the people repent”

  • peradventureperhaps, in case
  • repenthere: change their mind and turn back
  • harnessedin battle order: armed and in ranks, not a mob

God knew these men. Slaves for generations, they were not ready to fight a war in their first days of freedom. They would have run back to Egypt. So the shortest road was the wrong road for them, and God took them the long way.

A man in recovery wants the fast route: be fixed by Friday. God is kinder than that, and wiser. If your road is longer than you hoped, it does not mean God has lost the way. He knows which battles you can face today, and he goes in front of you the whole distance.

The bones of Joseph

Joseph had made the sons of Israel swear: carry my bones out of Egypt (Genesis 50:25). “And Joseph died, and all his brethren, and all that generation” (1:6). Moses was born, and was eighty before Israel went out (7:7). Then, on the way out, Moses picked up the bones (13:19). A whole generation had gone, and the promise had not been forgotten.

“And the bones of Joseph, which the children of Israel brought up out of Egypt, buried they in Shechem, in a parcel of ground which Jacob bought of the sons of Hamor the father of Shechem for an hundred pieces of silver: and it became the inheritance of the children of Joseph.”

Joshua 24:32

The promise was kept to the end. In the middle of leaving, Moses did not leave a dead man's request behind. Keep your word to the dead and the living both. And take comfort: what God has promised, he will do, even when it takes longer than a lifetime.

Succoth, Etham and on

First they went to Succoth, then to Etham, “in the edge of the wilderness”: where Egypt's land ends and the desert begins. We do not know exactly where Etham was. From there the road ran out across the Sinai toward the sea.

No one in the camp had been this way before. No one needed to have, because the one leading them knew every step.

A pillar of cloud, and a pillar of fire

The LORD was not behind them or above them, pointing the way. He went before them (verse 21). The cloud shaded them from the desert sun, and at night the same pillar was a fire that gave them light.

“To lead them the way… to go by day and night.” There was no hour when they were walking without him.

“He took not away”

Day and night, the whole road. Israel could not see where the road was going, but they could always see who was leading.

“For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God.”

Romans 8:14

A believer has no pillar to look at. He has something nearer: the Holy Spirit, who lives in him from the day he believed, and who leads. Ask him to lead you today, and then do the next right thing that is in front of you.

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Points to remember

  1. Redemption“all the firstborn of man among thy children shalt thou redeem” 13:13What belongs to the LORD is bought back with a price, and for us the price was the blood of the Lamb.
  2. Remembering“By strength of hand the LORD brought us out from Egypt” 13:14Tell the story of what he did, again and again, because men forget.
  3. The long way“God led them not through the way of the land of the Philistines” 13:17The shortest road is not always the best one. God knows what you are ready for.
  4. A promise kept“Moses took the bones of Joseph with him” 13:19God keeps his word to the end, and so should we.
  5. Guidance“He took not away the pillar of the cloud by day, nor the pillar of fire by night” 13:22He leads, day and night, and he does not leave.
  6. Grace“by strength of hand the LORD brought you out from this place” 13:3The rescue is his work, not ours.

Question 1 of 6

Redemption

What belonged to the LORD, and how could it be bought back?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Every firstborn, of man and of beast, was the LORD's, because he had spared Israel's firstborn in Egypt. Each one was bought back with a price. For us, the price was “the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot” (1 Peter 1:19). We were bought, so we are his.

Question 2 of 6

Remembering

What were Israel to tell their sons, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That the LORD brought them out of Egypt by strength of hand. They kept the feast every year so that the story would be told to every child. Your story of what the LORD did is worth telling too.

Question 3 of 6

The long way

Why did God not lead them by the short road?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because they would have seen war and run back to Egypt. The short way was the wrong way for them. If your road is longer than you hoped, he is still leading.

Question 4 of 6

A promise kept

Whose bones did Moses carry out of Egypt, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Joseph's. He had made Israel swear to carry his bones out, because he believed God would bring them home. Joseph's whole generation died (1:6), and still Moses kept the oath. God's promises do not run out, and neither should our word.

Question 5 of 6

Guidance

How did the LORD lead his people by day and by night?

What verses tell us about this?

“For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God.”

Romans 8:14

Answer

He went before them in a pillar of cloud by day and a pillar of fire by night, and he never took it away. Today the Holy Spirit lives in every believer and leads. Ask him to lead you today.

Question 6 of 6

Grace

Who brought Israel out of Egypt, and how?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD did, by strength of hand. Your rescue is the same: his work from start to finish, and you can lean on that today.
