Part one

Moab, then Judah

Verses 1–5

Two more names, and the second one hits close to home.

Even an enemy's bones

A quick reminder: Amos, a herdman from Judah, is preaching at Bethel in Israel, about 760 BC. In chapter 1 he named five of Israel's enemies. Now he names a sixth: Moab, east of the Dead Sea.

Who did Moab wrong?EdomNot Israel. A pagan king, and no friend of Israel's either. Moab dug up his bones and burned them to powder.
Did God care?YesWrong is wrong, no matter who it's done to. God isn't only the God of one side.

To burn a man's bones was to try to wipe him out completely, even in death: no grave, no memory. Hatred that won't quit even at the grave. God saw it, and Moab's own cities, like Kirioth, would burn.

A different kind of charge

The six nations

Sins against conscience

Slave raids, broken treaties, murder. They never had God's law, but they knew these things were wrong.

Judah

Sins against God's word

Judah had the law, the temple, and the priests. Their charge: they despised it. They knew what God said and shrugged it off.

Lies handed down

“Their lies” are the false gods and false ideas Judah had picked up. And they didn't invent them. Their fathers had walked the same road.

Plenty of us grew up in homes with drinking, drugs, rage, or lies, and then found ourselves walking the same road our fathers walked. That pattern is real. But it doesn't have the last word.

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Then the eighth

The faint line is chapter 1: five enemy nations, all around Israel. The crowd at Bethel has been cheering.

Moab, the sixth. Still cheering.

Judah, the seventh. Their southern rivals! Seven is the number of a full set. Surely the sermon is over.

It isn't. “For three transgressions of Israel, and for four.” The eighth name is their own, and their charge sheet is the longest of all.

That's how sin works on us. We spot it fast in other people. God's word has a way of turning around and pointing at the man who's listening.

Part two

A pair of shoes

Verses 6–8

Rich, religious, and crushing the poor.

  • A poor man's coatkept as security

What God sawpeople sold cheap

A man who fell behind on a small debt, even what a pair of sandals cost, could be sold as a slave. Judges took silver to rule against the innocent.

“Pant after the dust of the earth on the head of the poor”: a mourner threw dust on his head. These men were so greedy they begrudged the poor even the dirt. And a father and his son were both using the same young servant girl for sex. A girl with no power to say no. God calls it what it is: profaning His holy name.

Lying on a poor man's coat

A poor man's coat was often the only blanket he had. God's law let a lender hold it as security, but only during the day:

“If thou at all take thy neighbour’s raiment to pledge, thou shalt deliver it unto him by that the sun goeth down: For that is his covering only, it is his raiment for his skin: wherein shall he sleep? and it shall come to pass, when he crieth unto me, that I will hear; for I am gracious.”

Exodus 22:26–27
the coat→God's way: back to the poor man by sundownso he can sleep warm
the coat→Israel's way: a cushion to lie on at the altar“and they drink the wine of the condemned in the house of their god”

They kept the coats, lay on them at worship, and paid for their drinks with fines squeezed out of people. Religion on the outside, cruelty underneath. And God says, “when he crieth unto me, I will hear.”

Part three

Yet I…

Verses 9–12

God reminds them who brought them this far.

Grace remembered

Before God lists any more of their sin, He lists His kindness. Notice who does every one of these things: I.

  1. “I brought you up”out of slavery in Egyptverse 10
  2. “and led you”forty years in the wildernessverse 10
  3. “Yet destroyed I”the Amorite, tall as cedarsverse 9
  4. “And I raised up”prophets and Nazaritesverse 11
  5. “But ye…”verse 12

The Amorites stand for the peoples of Canaan, some of them giants who made Israel's spies feel like grasshoppers (Numbers 13:33). God cut them down, root and fruit. Israel didn't win the land. They were given it.

Before you look at your failures, look at what God has done: brought you out, carried you through, given you people to speak His truth to you. Sin looks different against that.

Silencing the voices

A Nazarite was a man who made a vow to belong to God in a special way. One part of the vow: no wine, no strong drink, nothing from the grapevine (Numbers 6:3).

Israel, to the NazariteCome on. One drink won't hurt.

The NazariteI made a vow to God.

Israel, to the prophetProphesy not.
shut up

God had given them two kinds of men to keep them straight: one who lived it, one who said it. Israel pressured the first to break his vow and told the second to keep quiet.

Some of us know exactly what that feels like, from both sides. Guard the men who stay sober beside you. And when somebody tells you a hard truth, don't shut him up. “Faithful are the wounds of a friend” (Proverbs 27:6).

Part four

Nowhere to run

Verses 13–16

The fastest, the strongest, and the bravest, and none of them gets away.

A cart groaning under the load.

At harvest, farmers piled the sheaves high on a wooden ox-cart, until the axle creaked and the wheels sank in. God says that's what Israel's sin feels like to Him: a weight He has carried, groaning, for a long time.

Think about that. Our sin isn't nothing to God. It weighs on Him. And still, the same God invites every man who is worn out under his own load to come to Him.

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Who can save himself?

  • The swifthis speed won't save him
  • The strong, the mightyhis strength won't save him
  • The archer, the runner, the horsemanhis skill won't save him
  • The bravesthe runs away naked
  • “Safety is of the LORD”Proverbs 21:31

Seven kinds of strong men, and not one gets away “in that day.” About forty years later, Assyria's army came, and Israel's best soldiers couldn't stop it.

Every man in recovery has tried white-knuckling it: I'm strong enough, I can handle this. We can't save ourselves, not from sin and not from judgment. That's not bad news. It's the start of turning to the One who can.

Question 1 of 5

God's word

Why did God judge Judah for despising His law?

What verses tell us about this?

“For as many as have sinned without law shall also perish without law: and as many as have sinned in the law shall be judged by the law;”

Romans 2:12

“For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required: and to whom men have committed much, of him they will ask the more.”

Luke 12:48

Answer

Because they had it. The other nations were judged by their conscience; Judah had God's own law, the temple, and the prophets, and treated it all as nothing. The more of God's word a man has heard, the more he is responsible for. That's a reason to listen, not to stop listening.

Question 2 of 5

Justice

What sins did God name in Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Selling people over small debts, bribed courts, crushing the poor, a father and son using the same servant girl, keeping the poor man's coat and lounging on it at worship. Not one of these was a secret ritual. They were everyday ways of treating people cheaply, done by religious men. God named every one.

Question 3 of 5

God's grace

What had God already done for Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Everything. He brought them out of slavery, carried them through the wilderness, beat enemies they could never have beaten, and gave them men to show and speak His truth. Over and over God says, “I.” What God has already done for us is the best reason to stop running from Him.

Question 4 of 5

Temptation

What happens when we silence the voices that keep us straight?

What verses tell us about this?

“He, that being often reproved hardeneth his neck, shall suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy.”

Proverbs 29:1

“Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.”

Proverbs 27:6

Answer

We end up with nobody to warn us. Israel pushed the Nazarites to drink and told the prophets to hush, and the next thing in the chapter is judgment. Keep the men around you who live clean and tell you the truth. Encourage the sober man; never tempt him. A hard word from a friend can save your life.

Question 5 of 5

Self-reliance

Can a man's own strength save him in the day God judges?

What verses tell us about this?

“There is no king saved by the multitude of an host: a mighty man is not delivered by much strength. An horse is a vain thing for safety: neither shall he deliver any by his great strength.”

Psalm 33:16–17

Answer

No. Speed, strength, skill, and courage all fail “in that day.” That's true of judgment, and it's true of sin's grip on a man. The good news is that we don't have to save ourselves. Safety is of the LORD, and He saves everyone who calls on Him.
