Hosea 12

Turn thou to thy God

Ephraim chases the wind and cheats on the scales. God points them back to their father Jacob: a schemer who wept, held on, and came out changed.

Part one

Feeding on wind

Verses 1–2

Hosea is still warning the northern kingdom before Assyria comes. Its big plans are a bag of air.

Playing both sides

The east wind is the hot, dry wind off the desert that scorches crops. Ephraim was living on it: lies, schemes, and deals that came to nothing.

The planSwear loyalty to Assyria, and send olive oil, a costly gift, to Egypt
→
The resultIsrael's king in an Assyrian prison

“And the king of Assyria found conspiracy in Hoshea: for he had sent messengers to So king of Egypt, and brought no present to the king of Assyria, as he had done year by year: therefore the king of Assyria shut him up, and bound him in prison.”

2 Kings 17:4

Any man who has lived a double life knows how much work it takes, and how it ends. Hosea calls it feeding on wind: you chew all day and never get full.

Part two

Remember Jacob

Verses 3–6

God tells Ephraim to look at their father: a schemer who learned to hold on to God.

From heel-grabber to prince

The whole nation was named after Jacob. His name sounds like the Hebrew word for heel. He was born holding his twin Esau's heel, and he spent years grabbing what wasn't his.

“And after that came his brother out, and his hand took hold on Esau’s heel; and his name was called Jacob: and Isaac was threescore years old when she bare them.”

Genesis 25:26
Jacob → Israel heel-grabber became he strives with God

“And he said, Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed.”

Genesis 32:28

He won by holding on

One night at the river Jabbok, Jacob wrestled until dawn with the One Hosea calls “the angel” (Genesis 32). Hosea tells us something Genesis doesn't: “he wept, and made supplication.” He begged.

“And he said, Let me go, for the day breaketh. And he said, I will not let thee go, except thou bless me.”

Genesis 32:26

That's how Jacob “prevailed.” Not by out-muscling God, but by refusing to let go and asking to be blessed. A man isn't changed by fighting God. He's changed by holding on to Him.

And “he found him in Bethel”: the place where God first met Jacob on the run, and promised to stay with him (Genesis 28). In Hosea's day, Bethel had a golden calf. Ephraim stood on the ground where their father met God, and bowed to idols.

Three steps back to God

Step 1Turn

“Turn thou to thy God.” Face Him. Stop running.

Step 2Keep mercy and judgment

Treat people with kindness and fairness.

Step 3Wait continually

Keep looking to Him, today and every day.

Notice it says “thy God.” After everything, He's still theirs. Turning back isn't one big moment and done. It's a turn, then a way of living, then a daily waiting on Him.

“He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”

Micah 6:8

Part three

Crooked scales

Verses 7–11

Ephraim got rich and was sure nobody could prove a thing.

A thumb on the scale

Hosea's word for merchant is kena'an, the same word as Canaan. God's people had become just like the Canaanites they had replaced: cheating customers and squeezing the poor.

Then Ephraim's boast: “Yet I am become rich … they shall find none iniquity in me.” In other words: nobody can prove anything.

“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.”

Proverbs 11:1

Plenty of us have lived by the hustle. God sees the thumb, even when nobody else can prove it. And He delights in the man who deals straight.

Both sides of the Jordan

Gilead lay east of the Jordan; Gilgal, west of it near Jericho. Gilgal was where Israel first camped in the Promised Land (Joshua 4–5). Now it was a center for idol worship.

There's a play on words: Gilgal sounds like the Hebrew for heaps. Their altars would end up as piles of rocks in a plowed field.

“Vanity” means emptiness. All that worship, and nothing in it.

Back to tents

Every year, at the Feast of Tabernacles, Israel lived for a week in shelters made of branches, to remember the years in the wilderness. God says He will put them back in tents.

It's a judgment: they'll lose their fine houses. But it's also a fresh start: back to the days when it was just them and God, and He fed them every morning.

And God never stopped talking. Prophets, visions, and similitudes, word pictures. Hosea's own marriage was one of them. Then God sent His best Word:

“God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds;”

Hebrews 1:1–2

Part four

Kept by a prophet

Verses 12–14

Jacob kept sheep for a wife. God kept Israel by a prophet.

Jacob's road

  1. Bornholding Esau's heelGen. 25
  2. The tricktakes his brother's blessingGen. 27
  3. On the runflees to Syria; God meets him at BethelGen. 28
  4. Shepherdserves for a wife, keeps sheep twenty yearsGen. 29–31
  5. The wrestleholds on, and is renamed IsraelGen. 32

Verses 12 and 13 sit side by side on purpose. Jacob kept sheep to win the woman he loved. By a prophet, Moses, Israel was preserved; in Hebrew it's the same word, kept. Jacob worked hard for the one he loved. God worked harder for His people.

It comes back.

Ephraim's guilt and shame would land back on Ephraim. “Leave his blood upon him” means he'll answer for his own wrongs. Sin has a way of coming home.

“But if ye will not do so, behold, ye have sinned against the LORD: and be sure your sin will find you out.”

Numbers 32:23

But keep verse 6 in view. The same chapter that says sin comes back also says “turn thou to thy God.” What meets the man who stays in his sin is very different from what meets the man who turns. Hosea is heading for that word: return (14:1).

Points to remember

  1. Chasing wind“Ephraim feedeth on wind” 12:1Schemes, lies, and double deals never fill a man. They only dry him out.
  2. Jacob's story“He wept, and made supplication unto him” 12:4Jacob was changed when he held on to God and begged for a blessing.
  3. Turning back“Turn thou to thy God” 12:6Turn, deal with people kindly and fairly, and keep waiting on Him.
  4. Honest dealing“The balances of deceit are in his hand” 12:7God sees the thumb on the scale, even when nobody can prove it.
  5. God speaks“I have also spoken by the prophets” 12:10God kept speaking to His people, and now He has spoken by His Son.

Question 1 of 5

Chasing wind

What does it mean to feed on wind?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the king of Assyria found conspiracy in Hoshea: for he had sent messengers to So king of Egypt, and brought no present to the king of Assyria, as he had done year by year: therefore the king of Assyria shut him up, and bound him in prison.”

2 Kings 17:4

Answer

It means living on things that can't feed you: lies, schemes, playing both sides, chasing money. Israel did it for years and ended up with a king in prison and a land in ruins. A man can chew on wind his whole life and stay hungry. Only God fills.

Question 2 of 5

Jacob's story

What changed Jacob?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said, Let me go, for the day breaketh. And he said, I will not let thee go, except thou bless me.”

Genesis 32:26

“And he said, Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed.”

Genesis 32:28

Answer

An encounter with God. Jacob the schemer wrestled all night, and when he couldn't win, he wept and held on: “I will not let thee go, except thou bless me.” God blessed him and gave him a new name. Men aren't changed by trying harder. They're changed by clinging to God and asking.

Question 3 of 5

Turning back

What does God ask of a man who turns back to Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”

Micah 6:8

“But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint.”

Isaiah 40:31

Answer

Turn to Him first. Then live it out: kindness and fairness toward people. Then keep waiting on Him, not once but continually. That's not a list to earn your way back. It's what life looks like for a man who has come home, and God gives the strength to walk it.

Question 4 of 5

Honest dealing

Why does God care about honest scales?

What verses tell us about this?

“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.”

Proverbs 11:1

Answer

Because cheating is a sin against people, and God cares about people. Ephraim thought that if nobody could prove it, it didn't count. God saw every crooked weight. Walking with God shows up at work and in every deal: a just weight is His delight.

Question 5 of 5

God speaks

How did God keep speaking to His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds;”

Hebrews 1:1–2

Answer

Through prophets, visions, and word pictures, again and again. A prophet, Moses, led them out of Egypt, and prophets like Hosea kept calling them home. Then God spoke His last and clearest word: His Son, Jesus. We have that word today, in our hands.
