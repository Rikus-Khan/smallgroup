Luke 12

Rich toward God

Before a crowd of thousands, Jesus warns against masks and greed, tells worried men to look at the ravens, and calls His servants to be ready when He comes.

Part one

Fear him, and fear not

Verses 1–12

A crowd so big they trample each other, and Jesus speaks first to His own men.

A mask that spreads

The innumerable multitude is, word for word, “myriads”: tens of thousands. In front of all of them, Jesus turns to His disciples first of all.

ὑπόκρισις said hupokrisis “playing a part on a stage”

A hypocrite was an actor, and Greek actors wore masks. Leaven is yeast: a pinch works quietly through the whole lump of dough. A little pretending spreads the same way, through a man and through a church.

Nothing stays covered

Houses had flat roofs, and news was shouted from them. What was whispered behind a closed door will one day be proclaimed upon the housetops. A mask doesn't last. God sees behind it now, and one day everyone will.

That's a warning, and it's also good news. You don't have to wait to be found out. Bring it into the light yourself, to God and to a brother you trust.

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

Who to be afraid of

“Them that kill the body”

Can only go so far

Men can take your job, your freedom, even your life. After that, no more that they can do.

“Fear him”

Has the last word

God holds both this life and the next. Hell here is Gehenna, the place of final judgment.

Fear of people runs a lot of lives: what they'll think, what they'll say, what they'll do. Jesus calls these men my friends, and tells them to settle the bigger question. The man who fears God rightly doesn't have to be ruled by anyone else. And in the very next breath He says, twice: “Fear not.”

The fifth sparrow

2 for 1Matthew: “two sparrows sold for a farthing”
5 for 2Luke: spend two farthings, get one more thrown in
1 farthinga small copper coin, a fraction of a day's pay

“Are not two sparrows sold for a farthing? and one of them shall not fall on the ground without your Father.”

Matthew 10:29

Sparrows were the cheapest meat in the market, food for the poor. Buy four, and the seller tossed in a fifth for nothing. That's the one Jesus is talking about: the bird that wasn't even worth charging for. Not one of them is forgotten before God.

Every hair counted

hairs, give or take, on one head

Nobody knows that number about himself. God does, for every man in this room, and He's kept count all along. If He bothers with that, He hasn't lost track of your life.

Some of us have been told, or told ourselves, that we're worthless. Jesus says: ye are of more value than many sparrows. That's the Son of God putting a price on you, and He paid it at the cross.

Confess, deny, and the sin that isn't forgiven

To confess Jesus is to say openly that He's yours and you're His. To deny Him is to say, “I don't know Him.” Peter did that three times, and wept, and Jesus restored him:

“And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice.”

Luke 22:61

So verse 9 isn't about a man who fails in a weak moment. It's about a life that refuses Jesus to the end. Even words against the Son of man can be forgiven.

To blaspheme against the Holy Ghost is to look at the Spirit's plain work, pointing to Jesus, and call it evil, and keep refusing Him. It's not a slip of the tongue. A man who's worried he's done it shows by his worry that the Spirit is still working on him.

Words when you need them

Synagogues were the local meeting places with their own courts. Magistrates, and powers were city and Roman officials. These men would stand in front of all of them. In Acts, they did:

“Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled; and they took knowledge of them, that they had been with Jesus.”

Acts 4:13

You don't need to be a speaker to tell what Jesus did for you. The Holy Ghost lives in every believer, and He'll give you the words: at work, at the courthouse, at the family table.

Part two

Rich toward God

Verses 13–34

A fight over an inheritance, a farmer with bigger barns, and birds that never worry.

A man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth.

Under the Law, the oldest son got a double share and the rest was split (Deuteronomy 21:17). Teachers were often asked to settle these fights. This man wasn't asking Jesus to teach him. He wanted Jesus on his side of the money.

Jesus won't be used that way. He goes under the question to the real problem: covetousness, wanting more than you have. Greed tells you life is in the having. Jesus says it isn't.

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

I, I, my, I

Count how often the rich man says I or my:

16  17×3 18×6 19×2 20  21 

Eleven times in three verses. He talks to himself, plans with himself, and in the end preaches to himself: “Soul, … take thine ease.” He never once thanks God for the rain, never once thinks of a hungry neighbour. Getting rich wasn't his sin. Leaving God out of it was.

And notice who finally speaks in verse 20. It isn't him.

“Many years,” or “this night”

  1. this night“thy soul shall be required”God's plan
  2. year onepull down, build greater
  3. year twobarns full
  4. year threetake thine ease
  5. many yearseat, drink, and be merryhis plan

Required is a word for a loan called in. His soul was never his to begin with. Then the question that hangs over every estate sale: whose shall those things be? You can't take a barn with you.

A fool in the Bible isn't a man who can't think. It's a man who lives as if God isn't there. To be rich toward God is to hold what He gives with an open hand: thankful, generous, and ready to give account.

Of all birds, ravens

Take no thought means don't be eaten up with worry. Then Jesus points at the raven. Under the Law it was an unclean bird (Leviticus 11:15), a scavenger nobody wanted. It doesn't plant, doesn't harvest, has no barn, and God feeds it anyway:

“He giveth to the beast his food, and to the young ravens which cry.”

Psalm 147:9

Worry and fear drive a lot of relapses. The bird nobody wanted still eats today. So will you.

Solomon in all his glory

40 years

the richest king Israel ever had, in royal robes

One wildflower

1 day

no work, no spinning, dressed better than the king

Worry can't add a cubit (about a foot and a half) to your height, or an hour to your life. If it can't do the smallest thing, why let it run the rest? The lilies were the wildflowers on the Galilee hillsides. After they dried up, people cut the grass and burned it as fuel in their clay ovens. If God dresses up something that only lasts a day, how much more will He take care of you?

O ye of little faith isn't a put-down. Small faith in a big God still works, and He was growing theirs.

μετεωρίζομαι said meteōrizomai “hung up in the air, tossed up and down”

That's worry: a ship in a storm, up one wave and down the next, never settled. Jesus gives two things that settle it. First: your Father knoweth. You're not an orphan scrambling for scraps. Second: put first things first.

“Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God.”

Philippians 4:6

A gift, not a wage

A little flock: a handful of ordinary men, not many and not strong. And the Father is pleased to give them the kingdom. Nobody earns it. He gives it, because He wants to.

So hold money loosely. Alms are gifts to the poor. A purse wears out, a thief breaks in, a moth eats your good coat. What's given away for God's sake goes into a bag that never wears out.

Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.

Part three

Be ye therefore ready

Verses 35–48

Servants up late with the lamps lit, waiting for their master to come home.

Dressed for work, lamps lit

Men wore long robes. To work or run, you pulled the robe up and tucked it into your belt: loins girded. Oil lamps had to be filled and trimmed, or they went out. These servants aren't in bed. They're up and ready to open the door.

  1. first watchabout 6–9 p.m.
  2. second watchabout 9 to midnightstill watching?
  3. third watchabout midnight to 3still watching?
  4. fourth watchabout 3–6 a.m.

A wedding feast could run late. The second and third watches are the dead of night, when the house is dark and a tired man nods off. Faithfulness is mostly staying awake when it's late and nothing seems to be happening.

The master serves the servants

Any other master

“Make ready … and serve me”

He comes home and the servants wait on him (17:8). That's how a house works.

This Lord

“He shall gird himself”

He ties on the apron, seats them at His table, and brings the food Himself.

Nobody had ever heard of a master like that. Then, the night before the cross, they saw it:

“He riseth from supper, and laid aside his garments; and took a towel, and girded himself.”

John 13:4

Ready, not guessing

The goodman of the house is the man who owns it. Walls were often mud brick, and a burglar could dig right through one. No thief sends word ahead of time.

Jesus will come again. He didn't give the date, and He says plainly it will be at an hour when ye think not. So the Bible never asks us to work out when. It asks us to be ready.

“For yourselves know perfectly that the day of the Lord so cometh as a thief in the night.”

1 Thessalonians 5:2

Being ready isn't living scared. It's living today the way you'd want Him to find you: trusting Him, doing the work He gave you, with nothing you'd need to hide.

The faithful steward

Peter asks who this is for. Jesus answers with another picture. A steward was a servant put in charge of the rest of the household: he handed out everyone's food, in due season, on time, every day.

When the lord comes, he finds him“so doing”
→
So the lord makes him“ruler over all that he hath”

Not doing something spectacular. Just so doing: the ordinary job, done faithfully, day after day. Showing up, feeding the people put in your care, keeping your word. That's what the Lord is looking for.

“My lord delayeth”

The slide starts with one thought: He isn't coming any time soon. Then the steward beats the people he was meant to feed, and the drinking comes back out. Men in recovery know that pattern. It starts when you stop expecting the Master.

Cut him in sunder means a severe punishment, and he ends up with the unbelievers. He wore the title of servant but never really belonged to the master. This isn't a believer losing his salvation. It's a man showing what he was all along.

Unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required.

Stripes are lashes with a whip. God judges fairly. The man who knew what his lord wanted and ignored it answers for more than the man who never knew.

That cuts close for us. We've been given a lot: the gospel, an open Bible, brothers, another day sober. That's not a reason to be afraid. It's a reason to be faithful with it, and to hand it on to the next man.

Part four

Discern this time

Verses 49–59

Fire, a baptism still to come, a house split down the middle, and men who can read the sky but not the times.

The baptism ahead of Him

Bible teachers read the fire a few ways: judgment, or the Spirit's fire at Pentecost (Acts 2:3). The baptism is plainer: His suffering and death, the flood He would go under for us. Straitened means hemmed in. It weighed on Him until it was done.

  1. 9:22“must suffer many things”
  2. 9:51sets His face toward Jerusalem
  3. 12:50“how am I straitened”today
  4. 13:33“out of Jerusalem”
  5. 18:31“we go up to Jerusalem”
  6. 23:33Calvary
  7. 24:6“he is risen”

He wasn't dragged to the cross. He walked toward it on purpose, for you. And there He said it was done:

“When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost.”

John 19:30

One house, two sides

Jesus does bring peace: peace with God (2:14). But that peace divides. When one man in a family follows Jesus and others don't, the line runs right through the living room. The prophet saw it long before:

“For the son dishonoureth the father, the daughter riseth up against her mother, the daughter in law against her mother in law; a man's enemies are the men of his own house.”

Micah 7:6

Some of you know this. You got sober and saved, and people at home didn't celebrate. Keep loving them, and keep following Jesus. Your new family in Christ stands with you.

Reading the sky

In Israel, the Great Sea lies to the west. A cloud coming in off the water meant rain was on the way. A wind from the south came off the desert, and that meant a scorcher. Every farmer could read it.

They could forecast tomorrow's weather, but they couldn't see what was standing in front of them: the Son of God, healing, forgiving, calling them to come. That's what this time means.

A man can be sharp about sports, money, and the weather, and blind about his own soul. Jesus asks: why even of yourselves judge ye not what is right?

Settle it on the way

  1. on the wayyou can still settle
  2. the magistratethe case is filed
  3. the judgethe case is decided
  4. the officerhe takes you away
  5. prisontill the last mite

An adversary is the man taking you to court. A mite was the smallest copper coin there was. Any man knows: settle before you face the judge.

Jesus means more than lawsuits. Every one of us is on the way to stand before God. Right now, on the road, you can be made right with Him through Jesus, who paid every last mite of what we owe. Don't wait for the courtroom.

Points to remember

  1. Hypocrisy“The leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy” 12:1A mask spreads like yeast. Everything comes to light, so live in the light now.
  2. Worth“Not one of them is forgotten before God” 12:6He counts sparrows and hairs. He hasn't lost track of you.
  3. Greed“Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required” 12:20Life isn't in what you own. Be rich toward God.
  4. Worry“Rather seek ye the kingdom of God” 12:31Your Father knows what you need. Seek Him first.
  5. Ready“He shall gird himself … and serve them” 12:37The Lord we wait for comes to serve. Be found faithful at your post.
  6. Settle it now“As thou art in the way, give diligence” 12:58Get right with God while you're still on the road. Today is the day.

Question 1 of 6

Hypocrisy

What did Jesus say the leaven of the Pharisees is?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Hypocrisy: wearing a mask, playing a part. Like yeast, a little of it works through everything, and it never stays hidden. Better to bring it to God now and live honestly.

Question 2 of 6

Worth

How did Jesus show that God never forgets us?

What verses tell us about this?

“Are not two sparrows sold for a farthing? and one of them shall not fall on the ground without your Father.”

Matthew 10:29

Answer

God doesn't forget even the cheap sparrow thrown in for free, and He has numbered every hair on your head. If He keeps track of that, He hasn't lost track of you. You are of more value than many sparrows.

Question 3 of 6

Greed

What did God call the man who built bigger barns, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Thou fool.” He planned for many years and left God out of every plan, and his soul was required that same night. He was rich in barns and poor toward God. A man's life isn't in what he owns.

Question 4 of 6

Worry

What does Jesus tell us to seek instead of worrying?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The kingdom of God. Worry leaves a man tossed up and down. But our Father knows what we need, and He feeds the ravens and dresses the lilies. Put Him first, and the rest will be added.

Question 5 of 6

Ready

What will the lord do for the servants he finds watching?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He will gird himself, sit them down at his table, and serve them himself. The Lord we wait for is a Servant. He blesses the man He finds faithfully doing the ordinary work He gave him.

Question 6 of 6

Settle it now

What does Jesus tell a man to do while he is still on the way?

What verses tell us about this?

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

Answer

Settle it before you reach the judge. We're all on the way to stand before God. Now, while we're still on the road, we can be made right with Him through Jesus, who paid what we owe.
