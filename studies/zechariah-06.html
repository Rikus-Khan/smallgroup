Zechariah 6

A priest upon his throne

The last night vision sends God's chariots out across the earth. Then Zechariah puts a crown on Joshua the high priest: a living picture of the BRANCH, who will be both priest and King.

Part one

Four chariots

Verses 1–8

The night that began with horses ends with horses, and this time they ride out to act.

The last vision

Quick reminder: Zechariah was a priest and prophet in Jerusalem about 520 BC, after the exile, while the temple was being rebuilt. This is the eighth and last of his night visions.

  1. First chariotred horses
  2. Second chariotblack horses
  3. Third chariotwhite horses
  4. Fourth chariotgrisled and bay: spotted, and strong

In the first vision (1:8–11), horsemen came back to report that the whole earth sat still. Now chariots, the war machines of the day, roll out to act.

“Brass” is bronze: strong and unbreakable. The two bronze mountains seem to be the gateway out of God's presence. The Bible doesn't tell us what each color means, so we won't guess. The point is where they go, and who sends them.

Sent out by the Lord of all the earth

“Spirits” can also mean winds: God's servants going out to the four corners of the world, sent by “the Lord of all the earth.”

The black and the white go to “the north country.” That's Babylon, whose armies always came at Israel from the north, around the desert.

The grisled go south, to Egypt. The bay patrol the whole earth.

Persia ruled most of the known world. But the world has one Lord, and it isn't the king of Persia.

They have quieted my spirit.

“My spirit” here means God's anger. Babylon had crushed Jerusalem and burned the temple. Now God says His judgment on the north country is carried out, and His anger is at rest. Babylon fell to Persia in 539 BC, about twenty years before this vision.

God's anger at evil is real, and it is never out of control. He doesn't stay restless forever. He deals with evil completely, and then it's done.

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

Some of us carry a lot of anger at people who hurt us. We don't have to carry the job of paying them back. God sees, and He settles accounts.

Part two

A crown for the priest

Verses 9–15

The visions are over. Now Zechariah has to do something in broad daylight.

The wrong head?

Heldai, Tobijah and Jedaiah had just arrived from the Jews still in Babylon with a gift of silver and gold. Zechariah is to meet them at the house of Josiah son of Zephaniah (not the prophet), and make a crown.

And he's to put it on Joshua, the high priest, not on Zerubbabel from David's royal line. That would have stopped people in their tracks:

The king

From Judah

David's family. He sits on the throne and rules.

The priest

From Levi

Aaron's family. He serves at the altar and offers the sacrifices.

When King Uzziah tried to act as a priest, God struck him with leprosy (2 Chronicles 26:18–19). Joshua isn't made king. He's made a sign, as chapter 3 said: “men wondered at.”

Out of his place

The crown on Joshua points past him, to the BRANCH promised in chapter 3: the new shoot from David's cut-down family tree.

He “shall grow up out of his place”: not from a palace, but from humble ground.

“For he shall grow up before him as a tender plant, and as a root out of a dry ground: he hath no form nor comeliness; and when we shall see him, there is no beauty that we should desire him.”

Isaiah 53:2

Jesus grew up in Nazareth, a nowhere town, the carpenter's son. Out of His place.

A priest upon his throne

No man in Israel was allowed to be both King and priest. In the BRANCH the two fit together perfectly: “the counsel of peace” is between them.

“Now of the things which we have spoken this is the sum: We have such an high priest, who is set on the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens;”

Hebrews 8:1

“For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood.”

Hebrews 7:14

Hebrews says it outright: Jesus is from Judah, the king's tribe, and yet He is our High Priest, seated on the throne. He offered Himself for our sins, and now He reigns.

He shall build the temple

God says it twice, in verse 12 and again in verse 13. Zerubbabel was building a temple of stone (4:9). The BRANCH will build one too, and bear the glory for it.

The New Testament shows Jesus building a house of a different kind, out of people:

“And I say also unto thee, That thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.”

Matthew 16:18

“Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house, an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ.”

1 Peter 2:5

Every believer is a “lively stone,” a living stone, set into the house by the Builder Himself. Even the ones the world threw out.

Those far off

The crown didn't stay on Joshua. It was kept in the temple as a “memorial”: a reminder to everyone who saw it that the real King-Priest was still coming. (Helem and Hen seem to be other names for Heldai and Josiah.)

And people “far off” would come to help build. Heldai and his friends had come from far off in Babylon. The prophets also say the nations will come. Paul uses the very same words for people like us:

“But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.”

Ephesians 2:13

“Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God;”

Ephesians 2:19

The last line is a call to Zechariah's people: “if ye will diligently obey.” The promise was certain; whether they would live to see its blessing depended on listening to God. Their part was to obey while they waited, and that's ours too.

Points to remember

  1. God's rule“The Lord of all the earth” 6:5Empires run the map for a while. God runs the world.
  2. Judgment“These … have quieted my spirit in the north country” 6:8God deals with evil completely. We don't have to carry the job of revenge.
  3. The BRANCH“Behold the man whose name is The BRANCH” 6:12The promised King grows up from humble ground: Jesus.
  4. Priest and King“He shall be a priest upon his throne” 6:13Jesus is both. He offered the sacrifice for us, and He reigns.
  5. Far off“They that are far off shall come and build” 6:15No one is too far off to be brought near by the blood of Christ.
  6. Obeying“If ye will diligently obey the voice of the LORD” 6:15We're saved by grace, and while we wait for the King, we listen and obey.

Question 1 of 6

God's rule

Who rules over all the earth?

What verses tell us about this?

“The earth is the LORD’S, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein.”

Psalm 24:1

“The LORD hath prepared his throne in the heavens; and his kingdom ruleth over all.”

Psalm 103:19

Answer

The Lord. Persia looked like it ran the world, and in our day other powers look that way. But God sends out His servants to every corner of the earth, and nothing happens outside His rule. That includes the things that frighten us.

Question 2 of 6

Judgment

Does God ever finish dealing with evil?

What verses tell us about this?

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Yes. Babylon's judgment came, and God's anger was quieted. If He seems slow, it's because He's patient, giving people time to turn to Him. But evil does not get the last word. That frees us from carrying revenge: God will settle every account.

Question 3 of 6

The BRANCH

Who is the man whose name is The BRANCH?

What verses tell us about this?

“Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper, and shall execute judgment and justice in the earth.”

Jeremiah 23:5

“For he shall grow up before him as a tender plant, and as a root out of a dry ground: he hath no form nor comeliness; and when we shall see him, there is no beauty that we should desire him.”

Isaiah 53:2

Answer

The promised King from David's line, growing up out of a cut-down stump. Joshua wore the crown for a day, as a sign. The real BRANCH is Jesus: born of David's family, raised in a humble town, and crowned by God as King.

Question 4 of 6

Priest and King

Why does it matter that Jesus is both priest and King?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now of the things which we have spoken this is the sum: We have such an high priest, who is set on the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens;”

Hebrews 8:1

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Answer

Because we need both. As our Priest, He offered Himself for our sins and still prays for us. As our King, He has all power, and nobody can overrule Him. The One who loves us enough to die for us is the One on the throne. That's why He can save “to the uttermost,” and keep the ones He saves.

Question 5 of 6

Far off

Is there room in God's house for those who are far off?

What verses tell us about this?

“But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.”

Ephesians 2:13

“All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.”

John 6:37

Answer

Yes. Zechariah saw people coming from far away to help build God's house. Paul says the blood of Christ brings the far-off near. However far you went, Jesus will not turn away a man who comes to Him. And once you're in, you're not just a guest. You get to help build.

Question 6 of 6

Obeying

What does God ask of us while we wait?

What verses tell us about this?

“If ye love me, keep my commandments.”

John 14:15

“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.”

James 1:22

Answer

To listen to Him and do what He says. We don't obey to get saved; that's grace. We obey because we love the One who saved us. Day by day, a man who hears God's word and does it is building, and he'll see the blessing of it.
