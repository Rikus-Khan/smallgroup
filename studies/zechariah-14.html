King over all the earth

Part one

Then shall the LORD go forth

Verses 1–5

Zechariah preached to the Jews back from Babylon, about 520 BC. His book ends here, at the very end of the story.

The shape of the chapter

“The day of the LORD” is the day God steps in to judge His enemies and set the world right. This chapter is still ahead of us. Here's how it unfolds:

  1. Nations gatherJerusalem falls1–2
  2. The LORD fightsHe goes forth3
  3. His feet standon the Mount of Olives4–5
  4. Light, waterfrom Jerusalem6–8
  5. The Kingover all the earth9
  6. Worshipthe nations come up16

It gets darkest before He comes. Verse 2 doesn't soften what happens to the city. Then verse 3 turns on one word: “Then.”

Christians who love the Bible read the timing of these last things in different ways, so we won't draw charts today. What all of them agree on: this is still ahead, Jesus is coming back in person, and He will reign.

The mountain splits

The Mount of Olives is the ridge just east of Jerusalem. When the LORD's feet touch it, it splits in two.

A great valley opens running east, and God's people escape through it, the way their fathers ran from the earthquake in King Uzziah's day.

That quake was so big that Amos dated his book by it: “two years before the earthquake” (Amos 1:1).

The same mountain

Real feet, on a real mountain. Forty days after He rose, Jesus went back to heaven from exactly this place:

The angels' promise

“Which also said, Ye men of Galilee, why stand ye gazing up into heaven? this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven.”

Acts 1:11

Where they were standing

“Then returned they unto Jerusalem from the mount called Olivet, which is from Jerusalem a sabbath day’s journey.”

Acts 1:12

And He won't come alone: “all the saints with thee.” Paul says the same thing:

“To the end he may stablish your hearts unblameable in holiness before God, even our Father, at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ with all his saints.”

1 Thessalonians 3:13

Part two

At evening time it shall be light

Verses 6–11

A day like no other day, and a King like no other king.

Light when you'd expect dark

Nobody knows all of what this day will be like. Only “the LORD” knows. But it moves the opposite way from every other day.

verse 6Not clear, nor dark

A dim, strange light. Nothing like an ordinary day.

verse 7Not day, nor night

“One day which shall be known to the LORD.”

verse 7At evening, light

When the sun should be going down, it gets brighter.

Some of us feel like our life is at evening time: too late, too far gone, the light going out. That's exactly when God likes to turn the light on.

Living waters

The Hebrews faced east to give directions, so the “former” (front) sea is the Salt Sea to the east, and the “hinder” (back) sea is the Great Sea to the west. “Living” water means running water.

“In summer and in winter”: most streams in that land dry up in the summer. This one never stops.

Jesus stood up at the Feast of Tabernacles, the same feast this chapter ends with, and offered that kind of water to anyone:

“In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink. He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.”

John 7:37–38

King over all the earth

His first coming

Lowly

“Behold, thy King cometh unto thee: he is just, and having salvation; lowly, and riding upon an ass” (Zechariah 9:9). Jesus rode into Jerusalem just like that.

His coming again

King of all

No more rival gods, no more competing kings. “One LORD, and his name one.”

“That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.”

Philippians 2:10–11

Every knee will bow one day. The only question is whether a man bows now, willingly, and finds a Saviour, or later, and finds a Judge.

Safe at last

The land around is leveled into a plain, from Geba (at Judah's north end) to Rimmon (far to the south), and Jerusalem is lifted up above it. Zechariah names real places in the real city:

  1. Benjamin's gateon the north side
  2. The first gateand the corner gate
  3. The tower of Hananeelon the north wall
  4. The king's winepressesin the south
  5. Safely inhabited“no more utter destruction”

Jerusalem has been burned, besieged and torn down again and again. God promises a day when that ends for good. That promise is Israel's, and it tells us God finishes what He starts. He means to make things whole.

Part three

Holiness unto the LORD

Verses 12–21

The enemies are judged, the nations come to worship, and even the cooking pots belong to God.

A hard judgment

These verses are hard to read: flesh, eyes and tongues wasting away while men are still standing, panic so great that soldiers turn on each other, even their animals struck. God doesn't make judgment sound pretty.

Why

They fought against God

These armies came to destroy what was His, and the LORD Himself had gone out to fight them (verse 3). They chose their side.

Until then

He waits

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance” (2 Peter 3:9).

The day is coming, and it is real. The reason it hasn't come yet is that God is still holding the door open. Today it's still open.

Enemies turned worshippers

Look who comes up to Jerusalem: “every one that is left of all the nations which came against Jerusalem.” The same people who marched on the city now come every year to worship.

The Feast of Tabernacles came at the end of harvest. For a week, families lived in shelters made of branches to remember how God cared for Israel in the wilderness (Leviticus 23:42–43). It was the happiest feast of the year.

If“whoso will not come up … to worship the King,”
→
then“even upon them shall be no rain.”

Egypt didn't depend on rain. The Nile watered it. So God names a plague for Egypt too. Nobody gets around the King by having a water supply of his own.

Holy pots

The high priest wore a gold plate on his forehead: “grave upon it, like the engravings of a signet, HOLINESS TO THE LORD” (Exodus 28:36). Only one man, on one spot, carried those words.

the high priest's forehead → horse bells and cooking pots

In that day the most ordinary things are as holy as the temple: the bells on a work horse, every kitchen pot in the city. No line between “church” things and everyday things.

Where the book ends

“Canaanite” was also a word for a trader. In that day no one is left making a profit off God's house.

“Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God.”

1 Corinthians 10:31

That's how Zechariah ends: a whole world where everything belongs to the King. We don't have to wait to start living that way. A man's job, his kitchen, his truck and his phone can all say holiness unto the LORD today.

Points to remember

  1. The day of the LORD“Then shall the LORD go forth, and fight” 14:3The darkest hour ends when the LORD Himself steps in.
  2. His coming“His feet shall stand in that day upon the mount of Olives” 14:4Jesus left from the Mount of Olives, and He's coming back in person.
  3. Hope“At evening time it shall be light” 14:7When it looks too late, God turns the light on.
  4. The King“The LORD shall be king over all the earth” 14:9Every knee will bow. Bow now and find a Saviour.
  5. Worship“Go up from year to year to worship the King” 14:16God turns enemies into worshippers.
  6. Holiness“Every pot in Jerusalem … shall be holiness unto the LORD” 14:21Everything, even the ordinary, can belong to God.

Question 1 of 6

The day of the LORD

What will happen when the day of the LORD comes?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

The nations gather against Jerusalem and the city suffers terribly. Then the LORD Himself goes out and fights for His people, judges His enemies, and takes His throne. It's a real day, still ahead. It hasn't come yet because God is patient and wants men to turn to Him first.

Question 2 of 6

His coming

Where will the LORD's feet stand in that day?

What verses tell us about this?

“Which also said, Ye men of Galilee, why stand ye gazing up into heaven? this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven.”

Acts 1:11

Answer

On the Mount of Olives, just east of Jerusalem, the very place Jesus went up into heaven. The angels promised He'd come back the same way He left: in person, in a body, for real. And the mountain will split under His feet.

Question 3 of 6

Hope

What does “at evening time it shall be light” promise?

What verses tell us about this?

“For his anger endureth but a moment; in his favour is life: weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning.”

Psalm 30:5

Answer

That when the world expects the dark, God brings light. In that day it will happen for real. And it shows how God works with us now: the man who thinks it's too late, that his evening has come, is exactly the man God loves to surprise with light.

Question 4 of 6

The King

Who will be King over all the earth?

What verses tell us about this?

“Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion; shout, O daughter of Jerusalem: behold, thy King cometh unto thee: he is just, and having salvation; lowly, and riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass.”

Zechariah 9:9

“That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.”

Philippians 2:10–11

Answer

The LORD, and the New Testament shows us His name: Jesus. He came the first time lowly, on a donkey. He'll come the second time as King over all, with no rivals. Every knee will bow to Him. Better to bow now, willingly, and find Him a Saviour.

Question 5 of 6

Worship

What will the nations do in that day?

What verses tell us about this?

“In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink.”

John 7:37

Answer

They'll come up to Jerusalem year after year to worship the King and keep the Feast of Tabernacles, even nations that once marched against it. God turns enemies into worshippers. He did it with every one of us who has come to Jesus.

Question 6 of 6

Holiness

What will be holy in that day?

What verses tell us about this?

“And thou shalt make a plate of pure gold, and grave upon it, like the engravings of a signet, HOLINESS TO THE LORD.”

Exodus 28:36

“Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God.”

1 Corinthians 10:31

Answer

Everything. Words once worn only by the high priest will be on horse bells and cooking pots. Nothing will be “just ordinary” anymore. We can start now: whatever we eat, drink, or do, we can do it for the glory of God.
