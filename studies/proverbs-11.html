Proverbs 11

Honest weights and open hands

God cares how a man does business, how he handles a secret, who he listens to, and what he does with what he has.

Part one

Honest scales

Verses 1–11

God is watching the marketplace, not just the church house.

A thumb on the scale

Goods were weighed on a balance against stones of a set weight. A crooked merchant kept two stones in his bag: a heavy one for buying, a light one for selling (Deuteronomy 25:13 forbids it).

God calls that an “abomination”, something He finds disgusting. An honest weight is “his delight.” Shorting a customer, padding your hours, selling junk as good: God sees it. And an honest day's dealing pleases Him.

First pride. Then shame.

In Hebrew the two words even sound alike, zadon and qalon. Pride walks in the door, and shame is right behind it. Solomon says the same thing later:

“Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.”

Proverbs 16:18

But “with the lowly is wisdom.” The humble man, the one who can say “I was wrong” and “I need help,” is the one who keeps learning. A lot of us had to hit bottom to get low enough to listen. Staying there on purpose is wisdom.

Money can't buy you out of this

Proverbs 10:2

Treasures of wickedness profit nothing: but righteousness delivereth from death.

Proverbs 11:4

Riches profit not in the day of wrath: but righteousness delivereth from death.

Solomon says it twice, word for word. Money can get you out of a lot of things. It can't get you out of standing before God. Only righteousness can, and that's the righteousness God gives through faith in Jesus, “who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption” (1 Corinthians 1:30).

Old word in verse 6: “naughtiness” isn't cute mischief. In 1611 it meant real wickedness. The wicked get caught in their own trap.

The trap snaps the other way

The book of Esther tells a story just like this. Haman built a gallows to hang Mordecai, a righteous Jew. By the end of the story, Haman was hanged on it himself (Esther 7).

That doesn't always happen on our timetable. But again and again the man digging a pit for someone else ends up falling into it.

Verse 9 shows the weapon: “with his mouth.” The hypocrite wrecks his neighbor with words, with flattery and lies. The righteous get out through “knowledge”: knowing the truth, and seeing through the talk.

Nobody sins alone

A good man is a blessing to his whole town. When the wicked go down, people cheer. Your life sends out ripples, whether you want it to or not.

That's sobering for a man who has hurt people. But it works the other way too. Every day you live upright, the ripples are blessing your house, your neighbors, your town.

Part two

Secrets, counsel, and kindness

Verses 12–22

Who you talk about, who you listen to, and who you sign for.

What happens in the group…

The talebearer

“revealeth secrets”

He goes from house to house with the latest. Tell him something, and it'll be all over town by dark.

A faithful spirit

“concealeth the matter”

You can trust him with your worst. It stops with him.

A group like ours only works if what's shared here stays here. Verse 12 adds: the man of understanding “holdeth his peace.” He doesn't run his neighbor down, even when he could.

Don't decide alone

The worst decisions most of us ever made, we made alone, or with only the voices that told us what we wanted to hear.

Wisdom surrounds itself with counsel: a pastor, a sponsor, an older believer, the brothers in this room. Before the big move (the job, the money, the relationship), you run it past people who love you enough to say no.

There's safety in many counsellors because no one man sees everything, least of all the man in the middle of it.

Co-signing for a stranger

Promissory note

Lendera man in town

Borrower“a stranger”

Surety (if he doesn't pay)you

YOU PAY

To be “surety” was to put up a pledge, shake on it, and promise to pay another man's debt if he didn't. Today we'd call it co-signing. To “smart” is to get hurt.

This isn't a ban on helping people. It's a warning against promising what you can't cover, for someone you don't really know. Proverbs warns about this again and again (6:1–5, 17:18, 22:26). Give if you can. Don't sign away your family's rent.

Kindness pays its own wages

Verse 16 sets two kinds of strength side by side. Strong, ruthless men grab and keep riches. A gracious woman keeps something better: honour. Grace wins what force can't.

Verse 17: mercy comes back around to the man who shows it. Cruelty does too. A hard, bitter man is hurting “his own flesh” first: his own body, his own peace, his own family.

What God can't stand, and what He loves

The chapter uses the same pair twice. Look at what God calls an abomination, and what He calls His delight:

“Abomination to the LORD”

a false balance (11:1)

a froward (twisted) heart (11:20)

“His delight”

a just weight (11:1)

such as are upright in their way (11:20)

The God of the universe takes delight in an honest man walking straight. Your ordinary, upright Tuesday makes Him glad. And verse 18 adds: the man who sows righteousness has “a sure reward.” The wicked man's paycheck is fake.

A gold ring in a pig's nose.

Women wore gold rings in their noses as jewelry. Picture one on a hog rooting in the mud. To Israel, a pig was an unclean animal. The gold doesn't make the pig beautiful; the pig ruins the gold.

“Discretion” means good sense, good taste, good judgment. Good looks without it are wasted. That's true of a woman and of a man. Whatever God has given you (looks, strength, smarts, charm), without wisdom it ends up in the mud.

Verse 21's “hand join in hand” means even if the wicked team up and shake on it, they still won't get away with it.

Part three

Open hands

Verses 23–31

The upside-down math of generosity.

God's upside-down math

The farmer who throws his seed away ends up with a field. The man who clutches every kernel ends up with less every year.

“The liberal soul” is the generous man. “Made fat” means well fed, refreshed. Water other people's dry ground, and you'll find yourself watered.

Generosity, not a gimmick

Some preachers turn verses like this into a deal: send money, get rich. That's not what this says. God isn't a slot machine. But the pattern is real:

“But this I say, He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully. Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:6–7

Addiction makes a man a taker: everything goes toward the next fix. Generosity is one of the clearest signs God is changing him. Start small. Buy a brother lunch. Give your time. Help someone move. Watch what God does with an open hand.

Riches fall. Branches grow.

Money is something you hold. A branch is something alive, connected to a root. One can slip through your fingers; the other keeps growing.

Verse 26: a man hoards grain in a famine, waiting for the price to climb. People curse him for it.

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.”

John 15:5

A wise man wins people

A good man's life is like “a tree of life”: people come to it and are fed. And “he that winneth souls is wise.” In Hebrew that's literally takes or captures souls: the wise man wins people over and draws them toward life.

For us, the greatest way to do that is to point a man to Jesus. You don't need a seminary degree. You need a changed life and the willingness to say what He's done for you.

Verse 29 is the opposite picture: a man who brings trouble on his own family “shall inherit the wind.” All that work, and he ends up holding air.

God settles accounts

If God disciplines His own people here on earth, how much more will the wicked face Him? Peter picks up this verse:

“And if the righteous scarcely be saved, where shall the ungodly and the sinner appear?”

1 Peter 4:18

“Scarcely” doesn't mean we barely squeak in. It means salvation is no small thing: it took the cross. Nobody gets in on their own. That's why the only safe place to stand on that day is in Christ.

  1. Honesty“A just weight is his delight” 11:1God watches how you do business, and an honest deal makes Him glad.
  2. Pride“When pride cometh, then cometh shame” 11:2Shame follows pride in the door. Stay low, and keep learning.
  3. The tongue“A faithful spirit concealeth the matter” 11:13Be the man a secret stops with.
  4. Counsel“In the multitude of counsellors there is safety” 11:14Run the big decisions past people who love you enough to say no.

Question 1 of 6

Honesty

Why does God care whether my deals are fair?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou shalt not have in thy bag divers weights, a great and a small.”

Deuteronomy 25:13

Answer

Because cheating someone is lying with your hands, and it wrongs your neighbor. God calls a false balance an abomination and an honest one His delight. Our faith isn't just for Sunday; it shows up in how we work, buy, sell, and keep our word. Integrity guides the upright man.

Question 2 of 6

Pride

What comes right after pride?

What verses tell us about this?

“Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.”

Proverbs 16:18

Answer

Shame. Pride walks in, and shame is right on its heels. But wisdom is “with the lowly”: the humble man can admit he's wrong, ask for help, and keep learning. Humility isn't thinking you're worthless. It's being honest about who you are before God.

Question 3 of 6

The tongue

What should I do with a secret?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Keep it. A talebearer spreads what he hears; a man with “a faithful spirit” lets it stop with him. Words can destroy a neighbor, so the man of understanding holds his peace. (The exception is when someone is in danger. Then love gets help.)

Question 4 of 6

Counsel

Why shouldn't I make big decisions alone?

What verses tell us about this?

“If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.”

James 1:5

Answer

Because no one man sees everything, least of all the man in the middle of it. Where there's no counsel, people fall; with many counsellors there's safety. Pray first, then run it past a pastor, a sponsor, the brothers who'll tell you the truth. It could save you from a promise like co-signing for a stranger.

Question 5 of 6

Generosity

How can a man give and still end up with more?

What verses tell us about this?

“But this I say, He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully. Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:6–7

Answer

Like a farmer scattering seed: what he lets go of comes back as a harvest. The generous man is refreshed; the man who waters others gets watered. It's not a get-rich deal with God. It's how His world works, and God loves a cheerful giver.

Question 6 of 6

Salvation

What will hold up in the day of wrath?

What verses tell us about this?

“But of him are ye in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption:”

1 Corinthians 1:30

Answer

Not money. Riches can't buy anyone out on the day we stand before God. Righteousness delivers from death, and Christ Himself is made our righteousness when we trust Him. The man who trusts his riches falls; the man rooted in Christ flourishes like a branch.
