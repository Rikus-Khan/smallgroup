Part one

The mocker and the mirror

Verses 1–9

The bottle lies to us, and our own hearts can't wash themselves.

The face it shows, and the face it has

Wine shows you a friendly face: relief, fun, courage, sleep. That's the mask.

Behind the mask is a mocker, someone who laughs at you. It promised peace and gave you shakes, debts, and a wrecked family. Strong drink is “raging”: loud, brawling, out of control.

“Deceived” is the honest word. Nobody plans to become an addict. Everyone who got hooked was lied to first.

Deceived is not the same as hopeless

If you've been fooled by the bottle (or the pipe, or the pills), this verse isn't here to shame you. It's here to name the liar. You're not stupid and you're not beyond help. You were deceived, and God is in the business of setting deceived people free.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

The answer isn't just “stop.” It's be filled. The empty place the bottle used to fill, the Holy Spirit fills. He lives in every believer, and He is stronger than any craving.

“And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken.”

Ecclesiastes 4:12

Not by willpower alone

Solomon warned, “woe to him that is alone when he falleth” (Ecclesiastes 4:10). Christians have long pictured the Lord as the third strand.

Build the rope: pray daily, stay in this group, go to your meetings, and have a brother you'll call before you pick up.

Drinking hard every day? See a doctor before you quit. Withdrawal can be dangerous, and getting help is wisdom.

Too cold to plow

In Israel, plowing and planting came in the cold, rainy months. The sluggard always had a reason to stay in: too cold, too wet, not today.

Months later, everybody else brings in a harvest. He goes around begging, and gets nothing. The hard season was the planting season.

Early recovery is a cold season. Do the work now. Verse 3 adds: it's an honour to walk away from a fight. Any fool can start one.

Drawing it out

What's really in a man's heart is like water at the bottom of a deep well. It's there, but it takes patience to draw it up. A wise friend asks good questions and waits for the answer.

That's part of what we do in this group. Nobody has to spill everything at once. But we keep letting the bucket down.

Verse 6: lots of men brag about how good they are. A man who is actually faithful, who does what he says, is rare. Be that man. Verse 7: his children are blessed after him.

Who can say it?

  • “I have made my heart clean”no amount of trying scrubs it
  • “I am pure from my sin”no man can honestly say it
  • “the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin”1 John 1:7

The answer to verse 9 is nobody. “For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God” (Romans 3:23). That's not bad news for addicts only. It's true of every man in the room, the pastor included.

What we can't do, He did

“Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus:”

Romans 3:24

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

You can't make your heart clean. Jesus can, and He does it freely, for anyone who trusts Him. And when a believer falls, he doesn't start over. He confesses, and the Father cleans him up again.

Part two

Honest weights

Verses 10–19

God watches the scale, the haggling, and the gossip.

Two weights in the bag

A cheat kept two stone weights marked the same: a heavy one for buying, a light one for selling.

“Thou shalt not have in thy bag divers weights, a great and a small” (Deuteronomy 25:13). God calls it an abomination, something He hates.

Today it's a hustle or padded hours. The God who made the eye (v. 12) sees it.

“It's junk, it's junk,” then he brags.

“Naught” means worthless. The buyer runs the item down to get a lower price, then walks off bragging about the deal he got. Proverbs has a sense of humor, and it sees right through us.

Verse 15: gold and rubies are fine, but a man who speaks with real knowledge is the rare jewel. And verse 13: love sleep, and you'll end up poor. Get up, open your eyes, and you'll have bread.

Sweet, then gravel

Bread of deceit

“sweet”“gravel”

Honest bread

hard worksatisfied

Anything gotten by lying or stealing tastes great at first. Later it's like chewing a mouthful of rocks. Every hustle ends that way. Verse 16: if a man vouches for a stranger's debt, take his coat as security, because a man that careless is a bad risk.

What's said here stays here

A “talebearer” goes around telling secrets. And the flatterer who tells you what you want to hear usually tells others what he heard from you. Keep your distance from both.

For a group like this one, that's a rule worth keeping: what a brother shares in confidence doesn't leave the room.

Verse 18: big decisions need good counsel. Even a war was planned with advice. Don't make your next big move alone.

Part three

Wait on the LORD

Verses 20–30

Leave payback to Him, let Him guide your steps, and let His light search you.

Say not, I'll pay him back.

Somebody did you wrong: robbed you, lied on you, hurt your family. Everything in you wants to settle it. God says: don't. Wait on Him. He will save you, and He'll handle the score.

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

“Who, when he was reviled, reviled not again; when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously:”

1 Peter 2:23

That's exactly what Jesus did on the cross. Waiting isn't weakness. It's trust.

A lamp put out, and money too fast

Verse 20: cursing your father or mother ends in darkness. God takes the fifth commandment seriously. Honoring them doesn't mean pretending they were perfect. It means you don't curse them.

Verse 21: an inheritance grabbed early, like the prodigal son who demanded his share (Luke 15:12), doesn't end blessed. Fast money usually goes as fast as it came.

Steps you can't see, and deals with God

Verse 24: we don't see the whole road. God does. You won't understand every turn of your life, but you can trust the One who's leading. “The steps of a good man are ordered by the LORD” (Psalm 37:23).

Verse 25 warns about rash promises to God: grabbing something to give Him, then having second thoughts afterward. A lot of us have prayed from a jail cell, “God, get me out of this and I'll…” God doesn't want bargains. He wants your heart, and He keeps His word even when we don't.

Verse 23 repeats verse 10 on purpose: God really does hate the crooked scale.

A lamp in the dark rooms

God made something in us, our spirit and conscience, that He uses like a lamp. He carries it through the back rooms of the heart and shows us what's there.

That uneasy feeling when you know you're slipping? That's often the lamp. Don't blow it out. Pray it like David: “Search me, O God, and know my heart” (Psalm 139:23).

Verse 26: “the wheel” was a threshing wheel run over grain to separate it from the chaff. A wise king sorts out the wicked.

Strength and grey hair

Young men

strength

Their glory. Energy to work, build, and fight for what's right.

Old men

grey

Their beauty. Grey hair is a sign of years survived and lessons learned.

Both are gifts. Young men, listen to the grey heads. Older men, use what you learned the hard way to help the younger ones. Verse 30: sometimes the bruise is what finally cleans a man up. The hard correction that hurt you may be what God used to heal you.

Question 1 of 6

Drink

What does wine promise, and what does it really do?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

Answer

It promises relief, fun, and peace. Then it mocks you, makes you rage, and leaves you with gravel in your mouth. Anyone fooled by it was deceived, not hopeless. The way out isn't willpower alone: be filled with the Spirit, and let the Lord and your brothers make a rope that doesn't break.

Question 2 of 6

Work

What happens to the man who won't work when it's hard?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

When harvest comes, he has nothing. He skipped the planting because it was cold. The hard season is the planting season. Get up, open your eyes, do today's work, and you'll be satisfied with bread.

Question 3 of 6

Sin and grace

Can any man make his own heart clean?

What verses tell us about this?

“For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;”

Romans 3:23

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Answer

No. Not one of us. All have sinned. But what we can't do, Jesus did. His blood cleanses from all sin, and God justifies freely, by grace, anyone who trusts in Christ. Clean hearts are a gift, not an achievement.

Question 4 of 6

Honesty

Why does God care about honest weights?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because cheating people is lying with your hands, and God hates it. He says it twice in one chapter. He made the eye and the ear and sees every deal. Our doings show who we really are, so deal straight even when nobody's checking.

Question 5 of 6

Revenge

What should we do when someone wrongs us?

What verses tell us about this?

“Recompense to no man evil for evil. Provide things honest in the sight of all men.”

Romans 12:17

“Who, when he was reviled, reviled not again; when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously:”

1 Peter 2:23

Answer

Don't pay it back. Wait on the LORD, and He will save you. Jesus didn't threaten or hit back. He trusted the Judge who judges rightly. Walking away from revenge isn't weakness. It's trusting God with the score.

Question 6 of 6

Who God is

Who directs a man's steps?

What verses tell us about this?

“The steps of a good man are ordered by the LORD: and he delighteth in his way.”

Psalm 37:23

Answer

The LORD. We can't see the whole road, but He can, and He leads the people who trust Him. He also carries a lamp through our hearts, showing us what needs to change. Follow His lead, one step at a time, today.
