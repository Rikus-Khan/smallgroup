Zechariah 4

Not by might, nor by power

Zechariah sees a golden lampstand fed by two olive trees. The word to a tired builder: the work will be finished, not by human strength, but by God's Spirit.

Part one

The golden lampstand

Verses 1–5

The angel wakes Zechariah up for another vision, and this one is full of light.

A lamp that never runs dry

Quick reminder: Zechariah was a priest and prophet in Jerusalem about 520 BC, after the exile, when the returned Jews were trying to rebuild the temple. This is the fifth of his night visions.

A “candlestick” here is a lampstand that burns olive oil. In the tabernacle, priests had to fill the lamps by hand every day. This one has a bowl of oil on top, and pipes to seven lamps.

And the oil comes straight from two living olive trees beside it (v. 12). Nobody pours. Nobody presses olives. The light just keeps burning.

“No, my lord”

ZechariahWhat are these, my lord?

The angelKnowest thou not what these be?

ZechariahNo, my lord.

Here's a prophet who isn't afraid to say “I don't know.” He doesn't fake it. He asks, and he gets an answer.

That's a good model for a new believer. You don't have to have it all figured out. Ask the question, in prayer and in this group. That's how a man learns.

Part two

Not by might, nor by power

Verses 6–10

The vision is a message for one tired man with a half-built temple.

A builder who stalled

Zerubbabel was the governor of Judah, from King David's family line. He led the first exiles home from Babylon and laid the temple's foundation. Then enemies opposed the work, the people lost heart, and it sat unfinished for about sixteen years.

  1. 538 BCPersia lets the exiles go home
  2. about 536the foundation is laid
  3. about 16 yearsthe work stops
  4. 520Haggai and Zechariah speak
  5. about 516the temple is finished

So the lampstand is God's picture for Zerubbabel: I will supply what this needs. You don't have to squeeze it out of yourself.

So how does it get done?

  • Not by mightnot by numbers, money, or an army
  • nor by powernot by one man's strength or willpower
  • but by my spiritby God Himself, at work

Zerubbabel had few people, little money, and a Persian king who could shut him down with one letter. God says that's not the point. The work is His, and His Spirit will see it through.

Many of us tried to quit on willpower alone: “by power.” It didn't hold. God doesn't ask a man to do it on muscle. He gives His Spirit.

Filled, not drunk

Paul puts the same choice in front of every believer: what is going to fill you, and run you?

Drunk with wine

Run by a bottle

It promises strength and leaves a man weaker. It ends in “excess,” and it always wears off.

Filled with the Spirit

Run by God

He gives real strength, and grows real fruit: love, joy, peace, self-control (Galatians 5:22–23). He doesn't wear off.

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

“What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own?”

1 Corinthians 6:19

Every believer has the Holy Spirit living in him from the day he's saved. You're not waiting for Him to show up. You're learning to lean on Him.

The mountain goes flat

The “great mountain” is everything piled up in front of Zerubbabel: enemies, letters to the king, no money, tired people, sixteen years of discouragement.

God speaks to the mountain directly: Who do you think you are? Before Zerubbabel, it will be a flat plain.

The “headstone” is the last stone, the one that tops off the building. When it goes in, the people will shout “Grace, grace unto it!” From the first stone to the last, the whole thing is God's grace.

The day of small things

When the foundation was laid, the old men who remembered Solomon's temple cried. This one looked so small.

What the old men saw

“As nothing”

A small foundation, a poor people, and memories of something better (Haggai 2:3).

What God saw

The plummet in his hand

A “plummet” is a plumb line, a weight on a string for building straight walls. If it's in Zerubbabel's hand, the walls are going up. God rejoices to see it.

“But many of the priests and Levites and chief of the fathers, who were ancient men, that had seen the first house, when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes, wept with a loud voice; and many shouted aloud for joy:”

Ezra 3:12

One day sober. One week. One honest prayer. It may look small to you. Don't despise it. God started it, and He is watching it grow.

The eyes of the LORD run to and fro.

The seven eyes on the stone in chapter 3 are explained here. Seven means complete. God sees everything, everywhere, all the time. Nothing in the whole earth is out of His sight, and that includes a half-finished temple in a little province, and a man trying to stay clean in a hard place.

“The eyes of the LORD are upon the righteous, and his ears are open unto their cry.”

Psalm 34:15

He doesn't just see. His ears are open too.

Part three

Two olive trees

Verses 11–14

Zechariah asks about the trees twice before he gets his answer.

Two anointed ones

“Anointed ones” is literally “sons of oil.” They are most likely the two leaders God had just spoken about: Joshua the high priest (chapter 3) and Zerubbabel the governor (this chapter). A priest and a ruler.

the LORD of the whole earththe olive trees: Joshua and Zerubbabel
the olive treesthe golden pipesthe lamp: God's people, shining

The trees don't make oil by straining. They're planted where God put them and they stand near Him: “that stand by the LORD.” The oil runs through them to keep the light burning.

In chapter 6, God will put the priest's role and the king's role on one man's head. That's a hint of Someone still to come.

Points to remember

  1. God's Spirit“Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit” 4:6God's work is done by His Spirit, not by our muscle, money, or numbers.
  2. Mountains“Before Zerubbabel thou shalt become a plain” 4:7Nothing can stand in the way of what God has set out to finish.
  3. Grace“Grace, grace unto it” 4:7From the first stone to the last, God's work is grace.
  4. Finishing“His hands shall also finish it” 4:9What God starts, He finishes.
  5. Small things“Who hath despised the day of small things?” 4:10A small beginning is still a beginning, and God rejoices to see it.
  6. God's eyes“The eyes of the LORD, which run to and fro” 4:10God sees the whole earth, and He sees you.

Question 1 of 6

God's Spirit

How does God's work get done?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.”

John 15:5

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

Answer

By God's Spirit. Not by numbers, money, or willpower. That doesn't mean we sit and do nothing: Zerubbabel still had to pick up the plumb line. But the strength comes from God. A man who stays close to Jesus, like a branch on the vine, bears fruit he could never squeeze out of himself.

Question 2 of 6

Mountains

What happens to the mountain in front of you?

What verses tell us about this?

“Every valley shall be exalted, and every mountain and hill shall be made low: and the crooked shall be made straight, and the rough places plain:”

Isaiah 40:4

“And Jesus said unto them, Because of your unbelief: for verily I say unto you, If ye have faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye shall say unto this mountain, Remove hence to yonder place; and it shall remove; and nothing shall be impossible unto you.”

Matthew 17:20

Answer

When it stands in the way of what God has promised to do, it comes down. This isn't a promise that we get whatever we want. It's a promise that nothing can stop God's work. The mountain that looks too big for you is not too big for Him.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

What is God's work built on, start to finish?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

“And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

Answer

Grace. When the last stone goes in, nobody shouts “Zerubbabel!” They shout “Grace, grace!” Our salvation is the same: a gift from first to last, not something we earned. And God's grace is enough for the weak days in between.

Question 4 of 6

Finishing

Will God finish what He starts?

What verses tell us about this?

“And this house was finished on the third day of the month Adar, which was in the sixth year of the reign of Darius the king.”

Ezra 6:15

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Answer

Yes. God said Zerubbabel's hands would finish the temple, and about four years later they did. What God begins in a man who trusts Jesus, He keeps working on, and He will finish it. A believer is never God's abandoned project.

Question 5 of 6

Small things

Why shouldn't we despise small beginnings?

What verses tell us about this?

“But many of the priests and Levites and chief of the fathers, who were ancient men, that had seen the first house, when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes, wept with a loud voice; and many shouted aloud for joy:”

Ezra 3:12

“Yet now be strong, O Zerubbabel, saith the LORD; and be strong, O Joshua, son of Josedech, the high priest; and be strong, all ye people of the land, saith the LORD, and work: for I am with you, saith the LORD of hosts:”

Haggai 2:4

Answer

Because God doesn't. The old men cried over how small it looked, but God rejoiced to see the plumb line in Zerubbabel's hand. A first day clean, a first honest prayer, a first step back toward your family: they're small, but God is in them. Keep building, because He is with you.

Question 6 of 6

God's eyes

Does God see what is happening to me?

What verses tell us about this?

“The eyes of the LORD are upon the righteous, and his ears are open unto their cry.”

Psalm 34:15

“Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do.”

Hebrews 4:13

Answer

Yes. His eyes run through the whole earth, and nothing is hidden from Him. That's sobering, because we can't hide anything from God. It's also a comfort: He sees the hard place you're in, and His ears are open when you cry out to Him.
