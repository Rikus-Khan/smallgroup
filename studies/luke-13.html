Luke 13

This year also

Bad news from Jerusalem, a fig tree given one more year, a woman bent for eighteen years set free, and Jesus weeping over a city that would not come.

Part one

Except ye repent

Verses 1–9

Two pieces of bad news, and a tree that has had three years to bear fruit.

“Sinners above all”?

Pilate was the Roman governor of Judaea, from about AD 26 to 36, known for being harsh. Some men from Galilee had come to the temple to offer sacrifices, and his soldiers cut them down right there. Everybody was asking the same question: what did they do to deserve that?

  • they must have been worse sinners“I tell you, Nay”
  • God was paying them backdisaster isn't a scorecard
  • except ye repentthe question is about you

The tower in Siloam

Then Jesus brings up a second disaster everyone knew about. A tower near the pool of Siloam, at the south end of Jerusalem, fell and killed eighteen people. No soldiers this time. Just an accident.

One cruelty, one accident, the same answer: they weren't worse than anybody else. And nobody is safe just because nothing has fallen on him yet.

When trouble hits, we ask, “What did he do?” or “Why me?” Jesus turns it into a call to turn to God, while there's time.

Except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish.

Jesus says it twice, word for word (verses 3 and 5). To repent is to change your mind about sin and about God, and turn around: away from going your own way, toward Him. It isn't cleaning yourself up first. It's turning to the One who can.

That's a hard word, but it's said in love. God isn't eager to judge. He's waiting for men to come home:

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

One more year

  1. year oneno fruit
  2. year twono fruit
  3. year three“cut it down”
  4. this year alsodig about it, and dung itmercy
  5. after thatfruit, or the axe

Three years was plenty of time to expect figs. This one just took up space: why cumbereth it the ground? It was using up soil and water a good tree could have had. Then the man who tended the vineyard steps in and pleads for it.

He doesn't just ask for time. He gets to work: loosen the hard ground around the roots, and pack in manure. That's what grace looks like. It's not God shrugging at a barren life. It's God working on it.

Don't waste the year

Some of us have had a lot of years with nothing to show for them. If you're here today, the Lord has given you this year also. He's been digging around your roots: the hard times, the people He's put in your life, this very study.

Grace gives time, and time has a purpose: fruit. The tree isn't saved by its figs. The figs show it's alive. Turn to Jesus, and let Him grow something in you.

“Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near:”

Isaiah 55:6

Part two

Thou art loosed

Verses 10–17

A woman who hadn't stood up straight in eighteen years, and a ruler who was angry she did.

Eighteen years bent over

Bowed together: bent double, unable to straighten up. For eighteen years she'd seen the ground and other people's feet. A spirit of infirmity was behind it: Satan had bound her (verse 16).

She didn't ask for anything. Jesus saw her, called her over, and said, “Woman, thou art loosed.” He laid His hands on her, and she stood up straight, praising God.

Some of us have been bent over by something that long or longer. Jesus still sees the one in the back of the room.

An ox, or a daughter of Abraham

The ruler of the synagogue ran its services. He wouldn't face Jesus, so he scolded the crowd: come be healed on a workday. Jesus answers with what that man did himself every sabbath:

youloose his ox or his assfrom the stallto watering
a beast, or a daughterone night, or 18 yearsa drink, or freedom
Jesusthis woman, a daughter of Abrahamwhom Satan hath boundbe loosed from this bond

He'd untie a donkey without thinking twice. Was she worth less than his donkey? The sabbath was God's gift of rest. What better day to set a prisoner free? The crowd saw it, and all the people rejoiced.

“A daughter of Abraham”

Nobody else in Scripture is called that. To the ruler she was a problem on his schedule. To Jesus she was family, an heir of God's promise to Abraham, and she had a right to be free.

Notice the word Jesus keeps using: loose, loosed, bound, bond. He doesn't just ease what hurts. He breaks what binds. Whatever has had you tied in a stall for years, He calls you over, and He can loose you.

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

Part three

The strait gate

Verses 18–30

A tiny seed, a pinch of yeast, and a door that will not stay open forever.

From almost nothing

A mustard seed was about the size of a pinhead, the smallest seed a Galilee farmer sowed. In one season the plant could grow taller than a man, big enough for birds to perch in.

The kingdom of God started like that: one Man and a few fishermen and tax collectors in a corner of the Roman world. Look at it now, with people from every nation resting in its branches.

What God starts small in a man's life can grow the same way. Don't despise the small beginning.

A pinch of leaven

3 measuresof meal: about 20 quarts of flour
1 pinchof leaven, hidden inside
the wholewas leavened

That's a huge batch, enough bread to feed a crowd. Abraham told Sarah to bake that much for three visitors:

“And Abraham hastened into the tent unto Sarah, and said, Make ready quickly three measures of fine meal, knead it, and make cakes upon the hearth.”

Genesis 18:6

In chapter 12, leaven was hypocrisy spreading. Here it's the kingdom spreading: quiet, out of sight, working from the inside until the whole lump is changed. God works in a man like that too.

On the road to Jerusalem

Since 9:51, Jesus has stedfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem. Luke follows Him town by town, likely through Herod's lands of Galilee and Perea, toward the city where He would die.

On the way, a man asks a head-count question: are there few that be saved? It's the kind of thing men like to argue about. Jesus doesn't give a number. He turns it back on the man: you make sure you get in.

ἀγωνίζεσθε said agōnizesthe “strain, like a man in a contest”

It's where we get agony. The strait gate is the narrow door. Striving here isn't earning your way in: nobody works his way through this door. It's the effort of a man who quits making excuses, lays down his pride, and goes in, now.

The door is narrow because there's only one, and it's a Person. But it's open to whosoever will:

“I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”

John 10:9

When the door is shut

OutsideLord, Lord, open unto us.

The master of the houseI know you not whence ye are.

OutsideWe have eaten and drunk in thy presence, and thou hast taught in our streets.

The master of the houseDepart from me, all ye workers of iniquity. near Him, never known

Why could many seek and not be able? They came after the door was shut. Being near Jesus isn't coming to Him. Today the door is open.

From every direction

The feast in the kingdom, with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob at the table. The listeners assumed they had reserved seats. Jesus says some will be outside, while people from every corner of the world come in.

Weeping and gnashing of teeth is grief and bitter anger at being shut out: a sober warning.

And hope for the man who feels last in line. Nobody gets in on his family name, and nobody is kept out by his past.

Part four

O Jerusalem, Jerusalem

Verses 31–35

A threat from Herod, and Jesus' answer: He's going to Jerusalem anyway.

“Tell that fox”

This is Herod Antipas, son of Herod the Great, who ruled Galilee and Perea for Rome. He had already beheaded John the Baptist (9:9). A fox is sly and destructive, a small-time killer that sneaks around at night. Jesus isn't afraid of him.

  1. to daycast out devils, do cures
  2. to morrowkeep on walking
  3. the third day“I shall be perfected”His work finished

Herod doesn't set Jesus' calendar. God does. Perfected means brought to the finish line: His work done at the cross and the empty tomb. And it would happen in Jerusalem, the city that had a long record of killing the prophets God sent it.

As a hen gathers her brood

When danger comes, a hen spreads her wings and calls, and her chicks run underneath. She'd take the hit herself to keep them safe. That's His heart toward the city that would crucify Him.

How often would I … and ye would not! They weren't kept out by His unwillingness, only by theirs.

“He shall cover thee with his feathers, and under his wings shalt thou trust: his truth shall be thy shield and buckler.”

Psalm 91:4

A house left desolate

Desolate means emptied and abandoned. About forty years later, Roman armies destroyed Jerusalem and its temple. Turning the Lord away has a cost.

But He ends with a line from the Psalms, the words pilgrims sang as they came up to the temple:

“Blessed be he that cometh in the name of the LORD: we have blessed you out of the house of the LORD.”

Psalm 118:26

The crowds sang it to Him when He rode into Jerusalem (19:38), and He still wept over the city (19:41). One day every knee will bow to Him. For you, today, the wings are still open. Whosoever will may come.

Points to remember

  1. Repentance“Except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish” 13:3Trouble isn't a scorecard. Every one of us needs to turn to God.
  2. Another year“Let it alone this year also” 13:8Grace gives time and works the soil. Don't waste the year.
  3. Set free“Woman, thou art loosed from thine infirmity” 13:12No bondage is too old for Jesus to break.
  4. Small beginnings“It grew, and waxed a great tree” 13:19God's kingdom starts small and hidden, and it spreads.
  5. The door“Strive to enter in at the strait gate” 13:24Don't argue about how many. Go in yourself while the door is open.
  6. Love refused“How often would I have gathered thy children” 13:34His wings are open. The only thing that keeps a man out is “ye would not.”

Question 1 of 6

Repentance

When people brought Jesus bad news, what did He say they needed to do?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Repent. The people who died weren't worse sinners than anyone else. Jesus told the listeners to turn to God themselves, and God is patient, not willing that any should perish.

Question 2 of 6

Another year

What did the dresser ask for the fig tree that bore no fruit?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

One more year: “let it alone this year also.” And he promised to dig around it and feed it. That's grace: more time, and God working on a barren life so it can bear fruit. But the year isn't forever.

Question 3 of 6

Set free

What did Jesus say to the woman who had been bound for eighteen years?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Woman, thou art loosed from thine infirmity.” He called her to Him, laid His hands on her, and she stood up straight and glorified God. She was a daughter of Abraham, worth far more than an ox, and He set her free.

Question 4 of 6

Small beginnings

What do the mustard seed and the leaven teach about the kingdom of God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The kingdom starts small and hidden, and it grows. A tiny seed becomes a tree birds can rest in. A little leaven works through the whole batch of dough. God's work in the world, and in a man, can start almost unseen.

Question 5 of 6

The door

When a man asked if few would be saved, what did Jesus tell him to do?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”

John 10:9

Answer

Strive to enter in at the strait gate. Jesus didn't give a number. He told the man to make sure he went in himself, now, before the door is shut. Jesus is the door, and any man who enters by Him shall be saved.

Question 6 of 6

Love refused

How did Jesus want to gather Jerusalem, and what stopped Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“And when he was come near, he beheld the city, and wept over it,”

Luke 19:41

Answer

As a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, and often. What stopped Him was their own refusal: “ye would not.” He didn't shut them out, and He wept over them.
