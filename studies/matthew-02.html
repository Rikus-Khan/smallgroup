Part one

The wise men

Verses 1–12

Strangers come a long way to worship. The people next door don't bother.

Who were the wise men?

The Greek word is magoi: learned men who studied the stars and advised kings in the lands east of Israel.

Daniel had once been put over “the wise men of Babylon” (Daniel 2:48), and many Jews still lived out there. The East may have known Israel's hope of a coming King.

A trip of months. They weren't Jews, and they came to worship.

What the Bible does, and doesn't, say

  • three kingsIt never says they were kings, or that there were three. There were three gifts.
  • at the mangerThey came “into the house” and found “the young child” (v. 11), maybe months later.
  • named Caspar, Melchior and BalthazarThose names came hundreds of years later.
  • wise men from the eastwho followed God's sign a long way, to worship the King

Christmas cards add a lot. The real story is better: God brought outsiders from the far side of the desert to bow before His Son.

Two kings

Made king by Rome

Herod the king

Of Edomite blood, ruling about 37 to 4 BC. A great builder: he made the temple huge. He also killed a wife and three of his own sons to keep his throne.

“Born King of the Jews”

A young child

Born in David's line, in David's town. He would never grab for a throne. He would give His life for His people.

No wonder Herod was troubled, “and all Jerusalem with him.” When a man like Herod got nervous, everybody did.

They knew the answer

Micah 5:2, about 700 BC

But thou, Beth-lehem Ephratah, though thou be little among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.

Matthew 2:6

And thou Bethlehem, in the land of Juda, art not the least among the princes of Juda: for out of thee shall come a Governor, that shall rule my people Israel.

The chief priests and scribes answered right away. They knew the verse cold. Bethlehem was about six miles down the road, and not one of them went.

What Herod said, and what he meant

Herod to the wise men (v. 8)“Bring me word again, that I may come and worship him also.”
a lie

The angel of the Lord to Joseph (v. 13)“Herod will seek the young child to destroy him.”

Herod used the language of worship to cover a plan to kill. People can say all the right words. God sees the heart, and He wasn't fooled for a minute.

“Exceeding great joy”

Stars don't move ahead of travelers and stop over one house. This was God's own sign, leading men who were looking for His Son.

Matthew piles the words up: they rejoiced with exceeding great joy. That's what it's like when a long search finally ends at Jesus.

Gifts fit for Him

  • Goldthe gift you bring a king
  • Frankincensesweet incense, burned in the worship of God (Exodus 30:34–37)
  • Myrrha costly spice, later brought to wrap His body for burial (John 19:39)

Many have seen the whole story in them: a King, God with us, and a Saviour who would die. Notice too: they saw Mary, but they worshipped him. They fell down, and they gave. That's worship.

They went home another way.

God warned them, and they listened. They didn't go back to Herod.

It's true for us too. Once you've met Jesus, you don't go home the way you came. Some old roads, and some old company, you just don't take anymore.

Part two

Out of Egypt

Verses 13–18

A family runs for its life in the night, and a town weeps.

Run, now

Joseph didn't wait for morning. He got up and left “by night,” with a wife and a small child.

Egypt was a Roman province outside Herod's reach, and many Jews lived there. It was a long walk, through the desert by the sea.

The King of the Jews began His life as a refugee. Jesus knows what it is to have no safe place to stay.

Israel's story, lived again

Hosea 11:1

When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt.

Matthew 2:15

… that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Out of Egypt have I called my son.

Hosea was looking back at the Exodus. God called Israel “my son” and brought the nation out of Egypt. Israel went on to fail in the wilderness.

Now God's true Son walks the same road: down into Egypt and back out. Where Israel failed, Jesus would obey all the way.

A jealous, dying king

Herod was about seventy and near death. He could not stand the thought of another King.

Bethlehem was a small town, so these may have been a handful of baby boys, maybe twenty or fewer. Every one of them had a name and a mother.

It fits the man. The historian Josephus says that as Herod lay dying, he ordered leading men of the nation to be killed the day he died, so the land would weep. That order was never carried out.

The Bible doesn't hide evil or pretty it up. Matthew writes it down plainly.

Weeping, and what comes after

Jeremiah 31:15Rachel weeps

“Rahel weeping for her children refused to be comforted.”

Rachel, Israel's mother, was buried near Bethlehem (Genesis 35:19).

Jeremiah 31:16God answers

“Refrain thy voice from weeping, and thine eyes from tears.”

The very next verse promises hope.

Revelation 21:4The last word

“And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes.”

The Child who escaped would die and rise, and end death.

God lets grief be grief. He doesn't tell a mother to stop crying. But He doesn't leave her there either.

Part three

Home to Nazareth

Verses 19–23

Herod is dead, and God leads a family home, one step at a time.

Four dreams, four times he got up

  1. 1:20“Fear not to take unto thee Mary”he took her
  2. 2:13“Flee into Egypt”he left by night
  3. 2:20“Go into the land of Israel”he arose and came
  4. 2:22warned about Judaeahe turned aside to Galilee

God didn't give Joseph the whole map. He gave him the next step, and Joseph took it, every time.

That's how most of us get through: not by seeing everything ahead, but by doing the next thing God shows us, today.

Herod's sons

When Herod died, about 4 BC, Rome split his kingdom among his sons.

Archelaus got Judaea and Samaria. He was as cruel as his father, and Rome threw him out about ten years later.

Antipas got Galilee. He's the Herod who will later behead John the Baptist. So Joseph took his family north, to the little town of Nazareth.

“Spoken by the prophets”

No one verse says these exact words. Matthew says “the prophets,” more than one, and gives their message:

נֵצֶר said netzer a branch, a shoot. “A Branch shall grow out of his roots” (Isaiah 11:1).

Nazareth was a small, looked-down-on place: “Can there any good thing come out of Nazareth?” (John 1:46). The prophets said the Messiah would be “despised and rejected of men” (Isaiah 53:3). Jesus grew up a nobody from nowhere, on purpose.

Two chapters, five fulfillments

  1. ✓Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a sonIsaiah 7:14Matthew 1:23
  2. ✓out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in IsraelMicah 5:2, of Beth-lehemMatthew 2:6
  3. ✓called my son out of EgyptHosea 11:1Matthew 2:15
  4. ✓Rahel weeping for her childrenJeremiah 31:15Matthew 2:18
  5. ✓a Branch shall grow out of his rootsIsaiah 11:1, and the prophetsMatthew 2:23

Written by different men, hundreds of years apart, and all of it came together around one Child. God's word doesn't fail.

Points to remember

  1. Seeking Jesus“Where is he that is born King of the Jews?” 2:2Outsiders searched hard. The insiders knew the verse and stayed home.
  2. Worship“Fell down, and worshipped him” 2:11Real worship bows low, gives freely, and is about Him.
  3. God's word“For thus it is written by the prophet” 2:5Prophets hundreds of years apart all pointed to this one Child.
  4. God's protection“Being warned of God in a dream” 2:12No king could touch God's Son before His time.
  5. Grief“Rachel weeping for her children” 2:18God sees the suffering of the innocent, and He has the last word.
  6. Obedience“And he arose” 2:14, 21Joseph took the next step God showed him, every time.

Question 1 of 6

Seeking Jesus

Who came looking for Jesus, and who didn't?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Wise men from far away, who weren't even Jews, followed God's sign for months to find Him. The chief priests and scribes knew exactly where He would be born, six miles away, and never went. Knowing about Jesus isn't the same as coming to Him.

Question 2 of 6

Worship

What does real worship look like?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Herod used worship words to hide a plan to kill. The wise men came a long way, rejoiced, bowed down, and gave their best. Real worship is honest, humble, and generous, and it's all about Jesus.

Question 3 of 6

God's word

How did the Scriptures point to Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They said where He would be born, that God's Son would come out of Egypt, and that He would be a despised Branch. Different men, centuries apart, all pointing to one Child. God says it, then God does it.

Question 4 of 6

God's protection

How did God protect His Son?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He warned the wise men away from Herod, sent the family to Egypt before the soldiers came, and brought them home when the danger was dead. Herod had an army. God had the timing.

Question 5 of 6

Grief

Does God see it when the innocent suffer?

What verses tell us about this?

“Refrain thy voice from weeping, and thine eyes from tears: for thy work shall be rewarded, saith the LORD …”

Jeremiah 31:16

“And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death …”

Revelation 21:4

Answer

Yes. He wrote it down so it would never be forgotten, and He doesn't wave grief away. But the Child who escaped that night grew up to die and rise again, and one day God will wipe away every tear.

Question 6 of 6

Obedience

What did Joseph do each time God spoke?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then Joseph being raised from sleep did as the angel of the Lord had bidden him, and took unto him his wife”

Matthew 1:24

Answer

He got up and did it, right away, even at night, even when it meant leaving everything. God showed him only the next step, and Joseph took it, every time.
