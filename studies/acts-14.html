Acts 14

Gods, stones, and the long way home

Paul and Barnabas are called gods at Lystra, then Paul is stoned and left for dead. He gets up, goes back into the city, and keeps going.

Part one

Iconium

Verses 1–7

A city splits in two, and the missionaries move on before the stones fly.

One message, two answers

Verse 1

A great multitude believed

Jews and Greeks, in the same synagogue, hearing the same words.

Verse 2

“Evil affected”

The unbelieving Jews “made their minds evil affected against the brethren”: they poisoned the well, turning the crowd against them.

“Long time therefore abode they speaking boldly in the Lord.” They stayed, and they kept speaking, even with trouble brewing. Their boldness was in the Lord, not in themselves. And the Lord “gave testimony unto the word of his grace”: He put His stamp on the message of grace, with signs through the apostles' hands.

The gospel never leaves a place the way it found it. “Part held with the Jews, and part with the apostles.”

Seen it coming, and left

When a plot to stone them came out in Iconium, they “were ware of it” and slipped away to Lystra and Derbe, cities of Lycaonia.

That wasn't cowardice. Jesus had told His men: “When they persecute you in this city, flee ye into another” (Matthew 10:23). And “there they preached the gospel.” They changed the address, not the message.

If a place will wreck you, a house, a corner, a crowd, leaving isn't weakness. It's wisdom.

Part two

Lystra: the gods come down

Verses 8–18

A man who never walked, a crowd that sees gods, and two men who say no.

A man who had never walked

lame from his mother's wombheard Paul speakPaul sees faith to be healed“Stand upright on thy feet”he leaped and walked

He'd never taken one step in his life, and he listened. Faith came by hearing, and Paul, “stedfastly beholding him,” saw it. Luke tells it almost word for word like Peter's lame man at the temple gate (Acts 3): the same Lord working through both apostles.

That doesn't mean a sick man today is short on faith. Here God is putting His stamp on the apostles' message, as He did in Jerusalem. What it does show is that a life stuck since the very beginning can still stand up when Jesus says so.

Lost in translation

The crowd, in the speech of LycaoniaThe gods are come down to us in the likeness of men!
probably lost on Paul and Barnabas

The priest of Jupiter…brought oxen and garlands unto the gates, and would have done sacrifice with the people.
now they got it

Most likely Paul and Barnabas had been speaking Greek, which everyone knew. When the crowd shouted in its own Lycaonian, the two men probably had no idea what was being said until the oxen and flower garlands arrived at the gate.

Jupiter and Mercurius

Barnabas→JupiterGreek name Zeus, the king of the gods.
Paul→MercuriusGreek name Hermes, the gods' messenger: “because he was the chief speaker.”

There was an old story told in that part of the world: Zeus and Hermes once came down disguised as men, and nobody took them in except one poor old couple. The Roman poet Ovid tells it. Many think that's why Lystra jumped to this conclusion: this time it wasn't going to be caught out.

Jupiter and Mercurius are the Roman names, and “the priest of Jupiter” had a temple “before their city.” A whole town was ready to worship two tired travelers.

“We also are men”

Not gods. Men, like you.

To “rend” your clothes was the strongest way to show horror at blasphemy. Barnabas and Paul ran into the crowd, tearing their robes. “Like passions” means the same nature and the same feelings: tired, hungry, tempted, and mortal.

Think how easy it would have been to enjoy it. A whole city bowing to you. They said no, instantly. Good men don't take the glory. And any man God uses, that's what he is: a man, pointing to somebody else.

Turn from vanities

“Vanities” means empty things: things that promise life and can't give it.

  • Jupiter, king of the godsa name on a statue
  • Mercurius, the gods' messengera story, not a savior
  • whatever you gave yourself toit promised life, and it took it
  • the living God, which made heaven, and earth, and the seathe One who hears, answers, and gives

An idol doesn't have to be carved. It's whatever you bow to: a bottle, a pill, a paycheck, a person. Paul's whole message to Lystra is two words: turn, and living.

Never without a witness

  1. Rain“from heaven”
    v. 17
  2. Seasons“fruitful seasons”
    v. 17
  3. Food“filling our hearts with food”
    v. 17
  4. Gladness“and gladness”
    v. 17

Paul doesn't quote Scripture here, because this crowd had never heard it. He starts with what they can see: rain, harvest, food, and gladness. Every good thing in a man's life is God's kindness, whether or not he's ever said thank you.

“For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse:”

Romans 1:20

Even this barely worked: “scarce restrained they the people.” A crowd that's made up its mind is hard to turn. Paul and Barnabas just did their part.

Part three

Stoned, and still standing

Verses 19–20

Left for dead outside the city wall.

From “gods” to stones

Jews from Antioch (about a hundred miles away) and from Iconium followed them to Lystra and talked the crowd round. The people who had wanted to worship them were now throwing stones. A crowd's love is thin. Live on applause and you'll live in fear of the next stone.

“Once was I stoned”

“Thrice was I beaten with rods, once was I stoned, thrice I suffered shipwreck, a night and a day I have been in the deep;”

2 Corinthians 11:25

Years before, a young man named Saul stood by, approving, while Stephen was stoned (Acts 7:58; 8:1). Now the stones fell on him. We aren't told whether he thought of it. We are told God didn't write him off. A forgiven man can still be used.

“From henceforth let no man trouble me: for I bear in my body the marks of the Lord Jesus.”

Galatians 6:17

Many think those “marks” include scars from this day. He never hid what the road had cost him, or who he had been.

He rose up

Proverbs 24:16

“For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.”

2 Corinthians 4:8–9

“We are troubled on every side, yet not distressed; we are perplexed, but not in despair; Persecuted, but not forsaken; cast down, but not destroyed;”

Notice who was there: “the disciples stood round about him.” Then he got up. He didn't get up because he felt fine. He got up because he wasn't alone. And he walked back into the same city, then left for Derbe the next day.

If you fell yesterday, get up today. Then call one of your brothers and let him stand around you.

Part four

The long way home

Verses 21–28

Back through every town that threw them out.

Back to the towns that threw them out

After Derbe they turned around and walked back through Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch in Pisidia: the very towns that had thrown them out and stoned them.

Why? New believers were there, weeks old, with the whole town against them. Paul and Barnabas went back to “confirm” (strengthen) their souls.

Who's new in your life? Text him today.

The honest word

Through much tribulation into the kingdom.

Paul doesn't say suffering earns the kingdom. Grace did that (13:39). He says this is the road. We live in a world that's against Him, so the way to His kingdom runs through hard places.

“These things I have spoken unto you, that in me ye might have peace. In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.”

John 16:33

Paul told new believers this to their faces. A man promised an easy life quits when it gets hard. A man who was told the truth knows a rough day isn't a sign God has left. Expect hard days. Plan for them: who will you call?

How you build a church

  1. Preachthe gospelv. 21
  2. Teach“many”v. 21
  3. Returnand strengthen themv. 22
  4. Appoint eldersin every churchv. 23
  5. Pray and fastand commend them to the Lordv. 23

“Ordained” here means appointed. The elders were older, proven men, put there to look after the rest. A church is people, not a building, and no new believer should be left to stand alone.

And then they let go. They “commended them to the Lord, on whom they believed.” It's God who keeps His church, not Paul. If you don't have a church yet, start looking today, and ask for the older men.

Perga again

On the way home they came back through Pamphylia to Perga, the town where John Mark had turned back. This time Luke says they “preached the word in Perga.”

Then down to the port of Attalia, and by ship all the way back to Antioch in Syria, “from whence they had been recommended to the grace of God for the work which they fulfilled.” The church that sent them out had prayed them through.

Home, and what came next

  1. about AD 34–35Saul turned aroundActs 9
  2. about AD 46Sent out from AntiochActs 13:2
  3. about AD 46–48The first journeyActs 13–14
  4. about AD 48–49Galatians, written to these churches
  5. about AD 49The Jerusalem councilActs 15

“They rehearsed all that God had done with them.” The report wasn't about what they did. It was about what God did, and how He “opened the door of faith unto the Gentiles.” That door is open to anyone who'll walk through it.

Today: tell one person one thing God has done for you. That's all “rehearsing” is.

Points to remember

  1. Humility“Sirs, why do ye these things? We also are men of like passions with you.” 14:15Give God the glory. Whatever good is in you is His, and you're only a man.
  2. The living God“Nevertheless he left not himself without witness, in that he did good, and gave us rain from heaven, and fruitful seasons, filling our hearts with food and gladness.” 14:17God has been good to you all along, even in the years you weren't looking for Him.
  3. Getting up“Howbeit, as the disciples stood round about him, he rose up, and came into the city:” 14:20Being knocked down isn't the end. Get up, with your brothers around you.
  4. Hard times“…and that we must through much tribulation enter into the kingdom of God.” 14:22The road has hard stretches. They don't mean God has left. Jesus has overcome the world.
  5. The church“And when they had ordained them elders in every church, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord, on whom they believed.” 14:23Nobody is meant to do this alone. Find a church, and find your brothers.
  6. Giving God the credit“…they rehearsed all that God had done with them, and how he had opened the door of faith unto the Gentiles.” 14:27Look back and name what God has done. It was Him, not you, and the door of faith is open to everyone.

Question 1 of 6

Humility

What did Paul and Barnabas do when the crowd at Lystra tried to worship them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They tore their clothes, ran into the crowd, and shouted, “We also are men of like passions with you.” They refused the praise and pointed to the living God who made everything. When people want to make you more than you are, say no, and point to Jesus.

Question 2 of 6

The living God

What has the living God done to show Himself to every nation?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He made everything, and He has never left Himself without a witness. He does good: rain from heaven, fruitful seasons, food, and gladness. Every harvest and every rain says that Someone made all this, and He is good.

Question 3 of 6

Getting up

What happened to Paul at Lystra, and what did he do next?

What verses tell us about this?

“We are troubled on every side, yet not distressed; we are perplexed, but not in despair; Persecuted, but not forsaken; cast down, but not destroyed;”

2 Corinthians 4:8–9

Answer

Jews from Antioch and Iconium won over the crowd, and they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, thinking he was dead. The disciples stood around him, and he got up, went back into the city, and left for Derbe the next day. Cast down, but not destroyed.

Question 4 of 6

Hard times

What did Paul tell the new believers to expect on the way to the kingdom of God?

What verses tell us about this?

“These things I have spoken unto you, that in me ye might have peace. In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.”

John 16:33

“Persecutions, afflictions, which came unto me at Antioch, at Iconium, at Lystra; what persecutions I endured: but out of them all the Lord delivered me.”

2 Timothy 3:11

Answer

“That we must through much tribulation enter into the kingdom of God.” He didn't promise an easy road, but an honest one. Jesus said the same: “In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.” Hard times don't mean God has left you.

Question 5 of 6

The church

How did Paul and Barnabas look after the new believers?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They went back to every town, even the ones that had thrown them out, to strengthen the new believers and urge them to keep going. They appointed elders in every church, prayed with fasting, and handed them over to the Lord. New believers don't have to stand alone.

Question 6 of 6

Giving God the credit

What did Paul and Barnabas say when they got home to Antioch?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They gathered the church and told them all that God had done with them, and how He had opened the door of faith to the Gentiles. They gave God the credit for the whole trip, and so should we.
