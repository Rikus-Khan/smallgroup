2 Timothy 4

Finishing well

Paul's last chapter: a charge, a finish line, a crown, and a lot of empty chairs.

Where we are in 2 Timothy

1Don't be ashamedfan the gift into flameI am not ashamed 1:12
2Be strongsoldier, athlete, farmerbe strong in the grace 2:1
3Hold to the Bookhard times are comingcontinue thou in the things 3:14
4Finish wellPaul's last wordsI have kept the faith 4:7

This is the last chapter Paul ever wrote. He is in a Roman prison, about AD 66 to 67, near the end of his life, and he writes to Timothy, the young man he trained, who is leading the church at Ephesus.

Read alone, this chapter still stands. A man near the end tells the one he's leaving behind how to finish, who let him down, who didn't, and what he's looking forward to. It's the best answer in the Bible to how do I not quit?

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

The charge

Verses 1–5

Paul's last orders to the man who'll carry on.

Preach the word: in plain words

Paul begins with weight: “I charge thee… before God, and the Lord Jesus Christ, who shall judge the quick and the dead.” (The quick are the living.) Timothy is answering to the Lord, not to the crowd.

“Preach” isn't only for a pulpit. It means tell it out. “Instant” means ready on the spot, and “in season, out of season” means when it's easy and when it's not. And do it “with all longsuffering”: patiently, never like a bully.

Twenty seconds at work

Guy at workYou've been different lately. What happened?

YouI was in a bad place. Jesus got hold of me. I'm not perfect, but I'm not who I was.

That's the whole sermon: twenty seconds, true, and no speech. Who around you has noticed that something has changed? Be ready, today, to say it plainly.

Itching ears

Teacher 1“You deserve it. Go ahead.”
Teacher 2“Nothing's really a sin.”
Teacher 3“Just feel good about yourself.”
Teacher 4“Send me money and God will bless you.”

Sound doctrine

The word means healthy: teaching that makes you well. Like medicine it doesn't always taste good, but it's the thing that heals.

Notice “heap to themselves.” People pile up teachers who scratch where it itches. Nobody forces them. And the stories they end up on, “fables”, sound good and go nowhere.

For a man trying to stay clean, this one is personal. You'll always find someone who'll tell you what you want to hear. Find the man who tells you what you need to hear, and keep him close.

But you: four things

  1. 1Watch thou in all thingskeep a clear head, stay awake and sober-minded
  2. 2Endure afflictionstake the hard parts without quitting
  3. 3Do the work of an evangelisttell the good news to the man in front of you
  4. 4Make full proof of thy ministrycarry out in full the job you were given

The ears are itching, so Paul says but you. Everyone else may drift. You don't have to. Pick one of the four for today and do it.

Part two

I have finished my course

Verses 6–8

The goal for every man in the room.

Departure is untying

Paul uses two pictures for his coming death. “Ready to be offered” means being poured out, like the wine poured on an altar at the end of a sacrifice. “Departure” is a ship's word: analusis, the untying of the ropes so it can sail.

So Paul is not talking about getting caught by death. He sees a ship, loaded and ready, with the ropes coming off, headed home.

A man who has made peace with Jesus has nothing to be afraid of at the dock. If that's not you yet, it can be today.

The finish line

  1. The fightA good fight

    Not a perfect one. Paul stumbled and got up, and the fight went on.

  2. The courseFinished

    The race track. He didn't quit at the hard turn, and he finished.

  3. The faithKept

    The trust he was handed (1:14). He guarded it and passed it on.

  4. The crownGiven

    By the righteous judge, as the winner's wreath is handed over.

  5. Not me onlyAll who love Him

    Everyone who is longing for Him to come.

The man who held the coats

Remember who is saying this. Years earlier, Paul stood by while a mob killed Stephen, and kept their coats:

“And cast him out of the city, and stoned him: and the witnesses laid down their clothes at a young man’s feet, whose name was Saul.”

Acts 7:58

“And Saul was consenting unto his death” (Acts 8:1). He carried that, and he never pretended otherwise. He called himself the chief of sinners. And how did he run, with a past like that?

“Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before, I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 3:13–14

Forgetting doesn't mean pretending it didn't happen. It means not letting it be the thing you run toward. Your worst day is behind you. Face forward.

Not to me only. To you.

This isn't about how a man gets saved. That is by grace, a gift (Ephesians 2:8–9). It's about what a saved man does with the rest of the race, and the Lord has a prize for every runner who is looking for Him, not just apostles.

“And every man that striveth for the mastery is temperate in all things. Now they do it to obtain a corruptible crown; but we an incorruptible.”

1 Corinthians 9:25

A man six days sober is in the same race as Paul. You don't have to run the whole thing today, only this stretch. Finishing well is made of ordinary days.

Part three

Who is left

Verses 9–15

A lonely old man, a cold cell, and a list of names.

One by one they went

Paul asks Timothy to come soon (v. 9). Look at who has gone:

  1. DemasTo Thessalonica“loved this present world”: he walked out
  2. CrescensTo Galatianot called a deserter
  3. TitusTo Dalmatiamost likely on the Lord's work
  4. TychicusTo Ephesus“have I sent” (v. 12)

Paul even gives his helpers away. “Only Luke is with me.”

Demas

  1. “my fellowlabourers”Philemon 1:24
  2. just “Demas”no title now (Colossians 4:14)
  3. “loved this present world”and departed (2 Timothy 4:10)

“Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas, greet you.”

Colossians 4:14

He drifted in stages. Years earlier he was Paul's fellowlabourer. Then just a name. Then he was gone. Paul doesn't say he hated Jesus. He says he loved this present world: the comfort, the freedom, a life without chains.

We aren't told how Demas's story ended, and we shouldn't guess. The same Lord who got Mark back can get Demas back. And if the old world is pulling at you today, call a brother before you go, not after.

Mark: the long way round

  1. Leg 1Joins the teamwith Paul and Barnabas (Acts 13)
  2. Leg 2Quits at Pergagoes home (Acts 13:13)
  3. Leg 3Barnabas takes himto Cyprus (Acts 15:39)
  4. Leg 4“Bring him”to Rome (2 Timothy 4:11)

Paul remembers a man who quit

“But Paul thought not good to take him with them, who departed from them from Pamphylia, and went not with them to the work.”

Acts 15:38

Paul wrote that man off, and the quarrel was bad enough that Paul and Barnabas split up (Acts 15:39). Now, near the end, Paul says “he is profitable to me.” Useful. The man he refused is the one he asks for.

How did that happen? Someone gave Mark a second chance, and Mark took it and proved himself. If you've quit on a job, a program or a person, this is the most hopeful verse in the chapter for you.

Barnabas did it twice

“But Barnabas took him, and brought him to the apostles, and declared unto them how he had seen the Lord in the way, and that he had spoken to him, and how he had preached boldly at Damascus in the name of Jesus.”

Acts 9:27
Barnabas“took him”Saul, the man the church fearedbecamePaul
Barnabas“took Mark”Mark, who'd quitbecame“profitable”
Youtakethe new guy, the one who failedand he becomes?

Paul was a Barnabas project too. When no one in Jerusalem would trust him, Barnabas did. So when Paul asks for Mark, he is giving someone else what he once got himself. Be the Barnabas for somebody today.

A cloak, books and parchments

for the body

The cloak

A heavy outer wrap. He's cold in a damp cell, and winter is coming (v. 21).

for the mind

The books

Scrolls of papyrus. Paul, near the end, still wants something to read.

for the soul

The parchments

Animal-skin scrolls, perhaps Scripture or his own papers. “Especially,” he says.

Warmth, something to read, and the Word. A man about to die asks for these three, and every one is a plain need. Paul was not too spiritual to be cold.

He asked for help, and a man who asks is not a weak man. What do you need today? Say it to one person.

Alexander, and a grudge

We don't know which Alexander this is, or exactly what he did. “Did me much evil” and “greatly withstood our words” sounds like he turned on Paul, possibly at the trial. What matters is what Paul does with it:

Carry it

“I'll never forget what he did”

Resentment. It feels like strength and it eats the man who carries it. It has pulled many a man back to the bottle.

Hand it over

“The Lord reward him”

Be careful of him (“be thou ware”), but leave the payback to God, who sees everything.

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

Who is your Alexander? Say his name to God today, and hand it over.

Part four

Alone, but not abandoned

Verses 16–22

The room empties out. Someone is still standing there.

“My first answer”

The first answer was Paul's first defense at his trial in Rome. Friends who had promised to be there weren't. “All men forsook me.” It must have been the loneliest day of his life.

“Notwithstanding the Lord stood with me, and strengthened me.” He had done the same thing once before, in the army barracks at Jerusalem:

“And the night following the Lord stood by him, and said, Be of good cheer, Paul: for as thou hast testified of me in Jerusalem, so must thou bear witness also at Rome.”

Acts 23:11

Alone. Not abandoned.

Some of you know that empty room: everybody gone when you needed them. The promise here is not that people never fail you. It's that He doesn't.

“for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

And look at what Paul says about the people who left him: “I pray God that it may not be laid to their charge.” He is saying Stephen's last words, the ones he heard while holding the coats:

“And he kneeled down, and cried with a loud voice, Lord, lay not this sin to their charge.”

Acts 7:60

Paul heard that prayer at the worst moment of his old life. It followed him for thirty years, and now he is praying it for his own friends. Forgiveness is learned, and it can be handed on.

Through, not around

“Out of the mouth of the lion” is probably a picture of deadly danger, whether the emperor, the court, or the enemy behind them. Paul knew he'd probably die soon (v. 6). So “deliver me from every evil work” isn't “keep me from dying.”

It's the same word as chapter 3: “out of them all the Lord delivered me.” Here the Lord brings him through, all the way, “unto his heavenly kingdom.” For a believer, death is the last road home, not the end. And a man who belongs to Jesus can't be lost.

The last names

Prisca and Aquilathe tentmakers who took Paul in at Corinth (Acts 18)
The house of Onesiphorushe “oft refreshed me, and was not ashamed of my chain” (1:16)
Erastusstayed on at Corinth
Trophimusleft at Miletum, sick
Eubulus, Pudens, Linus, Claudiaand “all the brethren”: the Christians in Rome
Timothy“Come before winter.”

Trophimus, left sick. Paul, who had seen God do remarkable things, didn't heal everyone, and he left a friend ill. Sickness isn't always a sign of sin or of weak faith, and prayer is not a vending machine. It's also why “Do thy diligence to come before winter” is so human: I need you, and time is short.

Notice the ending: “Grace be with you.” The last words in Paul's last letter are about grace. He needed it every day, and so do we.

The whole letter, in four lines

1Don't be ashamedI am not ashamed: for I know whom I have believed 1:12

Fan the flame. Don't hide.

2Be strongbe strong in the grace that is in Christ Jesus 2:1

Strength is a gift. Take it daily.

3Hold to the Bookcontinue thou in the things which thou hast learned 3:14

The Word keeps you through hard times.

4Finish wellI have kept the faith 4:7

One ordinary day at a time.

If you wanted the whole letter in one sentence: Don't quit. Hold on to Jesus and His Word, and keep going until the end.

A life in one line

  1. Damascus roadabout AD 34 to 35the persecutor stopped
  2. Three journeysabout AD 46 to 57stoned, shipwrecked
  3. Prison and Romeabout AD 57 to 62then free again
  4. This letterabout AD 66 to 67“I have finished”
  5. That daythe crown“at that day”

By tradition Paul was put to death in Rome not long after this, about AD 67 to 68. Thirty-odd years from the Damascus road to the finish line, and he never turned back.

He held the coats. He kept the faith.

That's the whole story of Paul in one line, and it is open to every man in this room. It wasn't talent or a clean start. He took the second chance Jesus gave him, and kept taking it, one day at a time, to the end.

Two things to do today. First: name one man who could be your Barnabas, someone who'd take a chance on you. Second: name one man you could be a Barnabas to. Then reach out to one of them.

You don't have to be ready for the whole race. You only have to run the next stretch.

Points to remember

  1. The charge“Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season” 4:2Be ready to say plainly what Jesus did for you, patiently, to the man in front of you.
  2. Itching ears“After their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears” 4:3Find the man who tells you what you need to hear, and keep him close.
  3. Finishing“I have finished my course, I have kept the faith” 4:7The crown is for everyone who loves His appearing. Run the next stretch.
  4. Demas“Demas hath forsaken me, having loved this present world” 4:10Drift happens in stages. Call a brother before you go, not after.
  5. Mark“Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me” 4:11A man who quit can be useful again. Be a Barnabas for someone.
  6. Not alone“Notwithstanding the Lord stood with me, and strengthened me” 4:17People fail. He doesn't. Forgive, and let Him bring you through.

Question 1 of 6

The charge

What did Paul charge Timothy to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Preach the word, in season and out, patiently. Keep a clear head, endure the hard parts, tell the good news, and fully do the work he was given.

Question 2 of 6

Itching ears

What will people want instead of sound doctrine?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Teachers who tell them what they want to hear, and fables. Sound doctrine is the healthy teaching they need, even when it doesn't taste good.

Question 3 of 6

Finishing

What had Paul done, and what was waiting for him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He had fought a good fight, finished his course and kept the faith. A crown of righteousness was laid up for him, from the Lord the righteous judge, and not for him only but for all who love His appearing.

Question 4 of 6

Demas

What verses tell us about this?

“Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas, greet you.”

Colossians 4:14

“Marcus, Aristarchus, Demas, Lucas, my fellowlabourers.”

Philemon 1:24

Answer

He loved this present world. He started as Paul's fellowlabourer and drifted in stages. Call a brother before you go back, not after.

Question 5 of 6

Mark

Who did Paul say was profitable to him for the ministry?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now when Paul and his company loosed from Paphos, they came to Perga in Pamphylia: and John departing from them returned to Jerusalem.”

Acts 13:13

“But Paul thought not good to take him with them, who departed from them from Pamphylia, and went not with them to the work.”

Acts 15:38

Answer

Mark, the man who quit at Perga and was refused a second trip. Restored, he became useful, and Paul asked for him by name. A man who quit can be useful again.

Question 6 of 6

Not alone

Who stood with Paul when everyone else left?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The Lord. When all men forsook him, He stood with Paul and strengthened him, delivered him, and will preserve him to His heavenly kingdom. People fail, and He doesn't.
