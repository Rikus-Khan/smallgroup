Deuteronomy 27

Two mountains, twelve curses, and one Amen

When Israel crosses Jordan, great stones are to be set up with the law written on them, an altar built, and the tribes divided: six on one mount to bless, six on the other to curse. And “all the people shall say, Amen.”

Part one

“Great stones… and an altar”

Verses 1–10

Moses looks past Jordan to the day Israel is in the land.

Across Jordan, on mount Ebal

Moses speaks on the plains of Moab, east of Jordan. The stones are for the other side: “when ye be gone over Jordan… in mount Ebal.” He has already named the two mounts:

“And it shall come to pass, when the LORD thy God hath brought thee in unto the land whither thou goest to possess it, that thou shalt put the blessing upon mount Gerizim, and the curse upon mount Ebal.”

Deuteronomy 11:29

Joshua kept it, and the book of Joshua says so (Joshua 8:30, 32): an altar “in mount Ebal”, and “a copy of the law of Moses” written on the stones.

An altar no man shaped

“And if thou wilt make me an altar of stone, thou shalt not build it of hewn stone: for if thou lift up thy tool upon it, thou hast polluted it.”

Exodus 20:25

Whole stones, as the field gave them: no iron, no chisel. The LORD would have nothing of man's cutting and polishing on the place where a sinner came to him. It is the altar-law given at Sinai, and Joshua followed it (Joshua 8:31).

First the burnt offering, then the peace offering

Firstburnt offerings: the whole offering, given up to the LORD
→
Thenpeace offerings: “shalt eat there, and rejoice before the LORD”

The order matters. The burnt offering is laid on the altar, and then the family eats and rejoices before the LORD. It is a picture of the order in Christ: first “he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever” (Hebrews 10:12), and then, “being justified by faith, we have peace with God” (Romans 5:1).

The law in plain sight

Plaister is plaster: the stones were given a smooth coat, and the words written on it, “all the words of this law… very plainly” (verses 3 and 8). Nobody could say he had not heard. Paul says what that plainness does:

“Now we know that what things soever the law saith, it saith to them who are under the law: that every mouth may be stopped, and all the world may become guilty before God.”

Romans 3:19

“Therefore by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight: for by the law is the knowledge of sin.”

Romans 3:20

The stones show a man where he stands. They never made him right. That is why the altar stood beside them.

First his people, then his commands

“This day thou art become the people of the LORD thy God.” Moses says it first, and then “thou shalt therefore obey”. The “therefore” is the hinge: they obey because they belong. They were never told to obey in order to be chosen.

“The LORD did not set his love upon you, nor choose you, because ye were more in number than any people; for ye were the fewest of all people: But because the LORD loved you…”

Deuteronomy 7:7–8

It is the same order for a man who has come to Jesus. He does not clean himself up and then come. He comes as he is, and he is made one of God's own, and then he learns to walk like it.

Six and six: the tribes are named in the order the verses give them, and the mothers are named from Genesis 35:23–26.

Both mounts heard the whole law

Moses does not tell us why the tribes were divided as they were, and we should not guess. What Joshua's day shows is that nobody missed a word:

“…as well the stranger, as he that was born among them; half of them over against mount Gerizim, and half of them over against mount Ebal;”

Joshua 8:33

As we saw in chapter 11, Joshua read every word to all of them, the stranger too (Joshua 8:35). Six and six, every tribe heard both the blessing and the curse.

Amen: so be it

After every curse the people answer, and the word comes twelve times (counted over the chapter): Amen. It means “so be it.” Each man said it of himself. He agreed out loud that if he did this, the curse was just.

Sins no one sees

Two of the twelve name the secret outright: an idol “in a secret place” (15), and a neighbour smitten “secretly” (24). The landmark (17) and the reward taken to slay the innocent (25) are the same kind of thing: easy to do while no one is looking. The people were made to say Amen to the very sins no one would see.

“Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do.”

Hebrews 4:13

That is hard news for a man with a hidden sin, and it is the reason to bring it out. What a man covers stays on him; what he confesses is forgiven: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9).

The twelfth curse catches everyone

The first eleven name particular sins. The last names every man: whoever does not stand by all the words of the law, to do them. Paul quotes it:

“For as many as are of the works of the law are under the curse: for it is written, Cursed is every one that continueth not in all things which are written in the book of the law to do them.”

Galatians 3:10

One failure is enough

“For whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one point, he is guilty of all.”

James 2:10

So the mountain of cursing answers every man who thinks he can stand on his own keeping. Not one of us has kept it. That is why the altar stood there, and the altar is where the story turns.

Who took the curse

Verse 26under the curse

Us“that confirmeth not all the words of this law to do them”
Christ“being made a curse for us”

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree: That the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ; that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.”

Galatians 3:13–14

Paul puts the two curses side by side. The law curses the man who fails to do it all. Christ, who did not fail, hung on the tree and took that curse on himself, and the one who trusts him is out from under it. It ends where the Bible ends: “And there shall be no more curse” (Revelation 22:3).

Real world: bring it out of the secret place

  • SinThe curses fall on what is done in secret: the thing no one saw, the thing you hide from your brothers and your wife. Name it. You have carried it long enough.
  • RegretSaying Amen to every curse was not meant to crush Israel. It was meant to make a man honest about what is in him.
  • Grace“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law” (Galatians 3:13). Your hidden sin is not outside what he bore.
  • HopeThe altar stood on the mount of cursing. God put the place of sacrifice on Ebal, where the curse was spoken.
  • TodayTell one brother in Christ the thing you have kept in the dark, and ask him to pray for you. If it is heavier than that, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today.

Points to remember

  1. The stones“thou shalt write upon the stones all the words of this law very plainly” 27:8The law was set up in the open, on great stones on mount Ebal, for all to read.
  2. The altar“Thou shalt build the altar of the LORD thy God of whole stones” 27:6No man's tool on it: burnt offerings first, then peace offerings and rejoicing.
  3. The two mounts“These shall stand upon mount Gerizim to bless the people” 27:12Six tribes on Gerizim, six on Ebal, and the Levites speaking.
  4. The people's answer“And all the people shall say, Amen.” 27:16Twelve times they agreed out loud: so be it.
  5. Hidden sins“putteth it in a secret place” 27:15The idol in secret (15) and the blow struck secretly (24). The LORD sees what no court sees.
  6. The last curse“Cursed be he that confirmeth not all the words of this law to do them.” 27:26It catches every man, and Christ took it for us (Galatians 3:10, 13).

Question 1 of 6

The stones

What was Israel to write on the great stones, and where were they to be set up?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All the words of this law, “very plainly”, on great stones plastered with plaister, set up in mount Ebal when they had gone over Jordan.

Question 2 of 6

The altar

Of what was the altar to be built, and what was offered on it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Of whole stones, with no iron tool lifted upon them. Burnt offerings were offered on it, and peace offerings, eaten there with rejoicing before the LORD.

Question 3 of 6

The two mounts

Who stood on mount Gerizim to bless, and who on mount Ebal to curse?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On Gerizim, to bless: Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Joseph and Benjamin. On Ebal, to curse: Reuben, Gad, Asher, Zebulun, Dan and Naphtali.

Question 4 of 6

The people's answer

What did all the people say after each curse?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Amen”, so be it, twelve times over, once after every curse.

Question 5 of 6

Hidden sins

Which curses name a sin done in secret?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The idol set up “in a secret place” (verse 15) and the neighbour smitten “secretly” (verse 24). The LORD sees what no court sees.

Question 6 of 6

The last curse

Who is cursed in the last curse?

What verses tell us about this?

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

Answer

Every man who does not confirm all the words of this law to do them, which is every one of us. Christ redeemed us from that curse, being made a curse for us.
