Deuteronomy 9

Not for thy righteousness

Moses tells the new generation why they are getting the land: not for their goodness, but for the wickedness of the nations and the oath to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Then he reads out their record, from the golden calf to Kadesh-barnea, and tells how he lay on his face forty days and forty nights for them.

Part one

“Not for thy righteousness”

Verses 1–6

Giants ahead, and a warning about the day after the victory.

The same giants, the same walls

Moses speaks with Jericho in sight. Ahead are walled cities and the children of Anak, whom their fathers saw at Hebron:

“Nevertheless the people be strong that dwell in the land, and the cities are walled, and very great: and moreover we saw the children of Anak there.”

Numbers 13:28

That report sent the fathers back. Moses says the question is not who can stand before the Anakims, but who goes in front.

“He which goeth over before thee”

“For our God is a consuming fire.”

Hebrews 12:29

The men do not have to be big enough. The LORD goes over first, and fire is not afraid of walls. They still have to go in: “so shalt thou drive them out”.

Why the nations go, Moses says in verse 4: “for the wickedness of these nations”. It was a judgment, and not a hasty one: when he promised Abraham the land, “the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full” (Genesis 15:16). He waited until it was.

Why they are getting the land

Moses names the thought before it can grow: Speak not thou in thine heart. The word righteousness stands three times in verses 4 to 6, and every time it is struck out.

  • not for thy righteousnessyou did not earn it
  • nor for the uprightness of thine heartnot even your good intentions
  • but for the wickedness of these nationsa judgment on them, after the LORD had waited
  • and the word he sware to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacoba promise, kept to children who had not earned it

Nothing in the list is about Israel being good. The land comes from the LORD's judgment on the one side, and his promise on the other.

Old words in this part

  • fencedwalled, fortified. “Cities great and fenced up to heaven”: walls so high a man could not picture getting over them.
  • Anakimsthe children of Anak, a tribe of tall men whom the spies saw at Hebron (Numbers 13:22, 28). The fathers called them giants and gave up.
  • stiffneckedlike an ox that locks its neck and will not turn when the driver pulls. It is how Moses describes Israel here (verse 6), how the LORD describes them at the calf (verse 13), and once more in the next chapter (10:16).

Moses does not leave the men to find the point for themselves. He says it in so many words: “thou art a stiffnecked people”. He is not insulting them. He is telling them what the land cannot be a reward for.

The danger after a good run

Chapter 8 already named it. When the harvest is in and the houses are built, a man says in his heart what Moses warns against here:

“And thou say in thine heart, My power and the might of mine hand hath gotten me this wealth. But thou shalt remember the LORD thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth, that he may establish his covenant which he sware unto thy fathers, as it is this day.”

Deuteronomy 8:17–18

Here the same thought puts on church clothes: not my hand got me this wealth but my righteousness got me this land. It is the same lie either way: I did this.

Not of works

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

“And the publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner. I tell you, this man went down to his house justified rather than the other: for every one that exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.”

Luke 18:13–14

Israel got the land by promise, and we get Christ the same way. The man who comes empty-handed, with nothing to say for himself, goes down justified. That is good news for the man who knows exactly what his record looks like, and Moses is about to read Israel's out loud.

Part two

“Ye have been rebellious”

Verses 7–17

Moses reads the record, and the golden calf is the worst page in it.

A hard thing to say to a man's face

Remember, and forget not.

The men listening were children, or not yet born, when most of it happened. Moses says thou anyway, as one people with one record: “from the day that thou didst depart out of the land of Egypt, until ye came unto this place”.

We saw the calf in Exodus 32. Moses does not tell it again for the story. He tells it so that nobody on the plains of Moab can think the land is a prize for good behaviour. Horeb is where he begins, because it was the first great break of the covenant.

Forty days, and forty days

The first forty

The law written

Moses goes up, and “the LORD delivered unto me two tables of stone written with the finger of God” (verse 10). He was “in the mount forty days and forty nights” (Exodus 24:18).

The second forty

The people prayed for

“And he was there with the LORD forty days and forty nights; he did neither eat bread, nor drink water. And he wrote upon the tables the words of the covenant, the ten commandments” (Exodus 34:28).

No bread and no water for forty days is not something a man does. The LORD held him up, twice. The words forty days and forty nights come five times in chapters 9 and 10 (9:9, 11, 18, 25; 10:10), as Moses tells it again to people who were there.

Moses broke the tables “before your eyes”. He did not break them by temper alone: the people had broken the covenant first, and the pieces on the ground said so. A covenant is a promise between two sides, and one side had walked out of it.

Part three

“I fell down before the LORD”

Verses 18–29

What Moses did about it, and what he said when he prayed.

Forty days on his face

Moses does not pray a short prayer and go back to his tent. He lies down before the LORD for forty days and forty nights, with no bread and no water, “because of all your sins which ye sinned”. They were not his sins. He had no part in the calf.

He was afraid, too: “I was afraid of the anger and hot displeasure, wherewith the LORD was wroth against you to destroy you”. The man who spoke with God face to face feared his anger more than anyone in the camp, because he understood it best.

And the answer: “But the LORD hearkened unto me at that time also.” Hearkened is more than heard; it is listened to, and acted on.

Somebody has lain down for you in the same spirit: a mother, a wife, a grandmother, a brother in Christ. And there is somebody you can lie down for. Name him before the LORD today.

“And I prayed for Aaron also”

Aaron had made the calf (Exodus 32:2–4), and “the LORD was very angry with Aaron to have destroyed him”. His own brother prayed for him. Moses does not say Aaron was blameless, and he does not say Aaron was finished.

“And Aaron the priest went up into mount Hor at the commandment of the LORD, and died there, in the fortieth year after the children of Israel were come out of the land of Egypt, in the first day of the fifth month.”

Numbers 33:38

Aaron lived on and served as the priest until the fortieth year, when he died on mount Hor. The man who failed so badly at the foot of the mount was not set aside. Somebody prayed, and the LORD heard.

“Your sin, the calf”

Moses does not call it an idol, or a statue, or a god. He calls it your sin. And he gets rid of it, one step at a time:

  1. Burntwith fire“burnt it with fire”
  2. Stampedbroken up“and stamped it”
  3. Groundas small as dust“very small”
  4. Cast awayinto the brook“that descended out of the mount”

He did not keep a piece. A man in recovery knows what a piece is: the one number left in the phone, the bottle in the back of the cupboard, the old friend who still has a key. Whatever your calf is, the LORD is not asking you to manage it. He is asking you to be done with it, and a brother in Christ will help you carry it out.

Five times, and the last was the worst

Moses names places, and the men know every one. He does not give them in order; here they are in the order they happened:

  1. Massah“Is the LORD among us, or not?”Exodus 17:7
  2. Horebthe golden calfExodus 32
  3. Taberah“the fire of the LORD burnt among them”Numbers 11:3
  4. Kibroth-hattaavah“they buried the people that lusted”Numbers 11:34
  5. Kadesh-barnea“ye believed him not”9:23; Numbers 14

At Kadesh-barnea the LORD said, “Go up and possess the land which I have given you”, and they would not (verse 23). The LORD himself counted it: “have tempted me now these ten times” (Numbers 14:22). They refused, forty years ago, the very thing they are being offered again today.

Then Moses says it in one line, and does not soften it: “Ye have been rebellious against the LORD from the day that I knew you.” An honest list is not the end of a man. It is where Moses starts the prayer.

What Moses did not say

Read the prayer again for what is missing. There is not one word of “but they are sorry”, or “they will do better”, or “they deserve another chance”. Moses asks the LORD to look away from their sin (verse 27) and pleads three other things:

  • They are thine“thy people and thine inheritance, which thou hast redeemed” (verse 26). “Yet they are thy people” (verse 29). What the LORD brought out of Egypt is his.
  • The oath“Remember thy servants, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob” (verse 27). The same promise that gives them the land (verse 5) is the reason they are not destroyed.
  • His name“Lest the land whence thou broughtest us out say, Because the LORD was not able to bring them into the land” (verse 28). The LORD's name was on the line.

It is the same ground as verse 5: not their righteousness, but his promise. A man who prays like that is not pretending he is good. He is saying where his hope is, and it is outside himself. You can pray that way today.

One who lives to ask for us

Moses prayed forty days and had to come down off the mount. There is another who pleads for his people and never has to come down:

“Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died, yea rather, that is risen again, who is even at the right hand of God, who also maketh intercession for us.”

Romans 8:34

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Moses stood in the gap with nothing but a promise to plead. Jesus pleads the same promise, and he is the one who died to keep it. “If any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous” (1 John 2:1). If you fell yesterday, there is a man at the right hand of God asking for you right now.

Real world: whose is it?

  • SinThe thought that says I did this, and the stiff neck that will not turn when the LORD pulls. Moses names both in one chapter.
  • RegretIsrael's record was bad, and Moses read it out. Say yours honestly to the LORD and to a brother, without dressing it up. A list written in the light cannot hold you in the dark.
  • GraceThe land came “not for thy righteousness”, and Christ comes the same way: “not of works”. He lives to make intercession for you.
  • HopeAfter forty days of prayer, “the LORD hearkened unto me at that time also”. The people who had made a calf went on to the land.
  • TodayWhen something goes well, say out loud whose it is. Then pray, by name, for one brother who is struggling. And if the weight of your record is heavy today, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor.

Points to remember

  1. The one who goes first“the LORD thy God is he which goeth over before thee; as a consuming fire he shall destroy them” 9:3Their fathers feared the giants; the LORD goes in front, so the question is who is leading.
  2. Not for thy righteousness“Not for thy righteousness, or for the uprightness of thine heart” 9:5It is judgment on the nations and a promise to the fathers; ours, too, is a gift and not a wage.
  3. The record“Ye have been rebellious against the LORD from the day that I knew you” 9:24Moses read out the record, without softening it, so that none would take the land for a prize.
  4. The calf“I took your sin, the calf which ye had made, and burnt it with fire” 9:21He broke the tables before their eyes, then burnt the calf, ground it to dust and cast it away.
  5. The prayer“Remember thy servants, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob; look not unto the stubbornness of this people” 9:27Forty days and forty nights, pleading that they are his, his oath, and his name; and Christ lives to ask for us.
  6. The other places“at Taberah, and at Massah, and at Kibroth-hattaavah, ye provoked the LORD to wrath” 9:22Taberah, Massah and Kibroth-hattaavah, and last of all at Kadesh-barnea, where they believed him not.

Question 1 of 6

The one who goes first

Who goes over before Israel, and what will he do to the nations?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD thy God. As a consuming fire he shall destroy the nations and bring them down before Israel's face, so that they drive them out and destroy them quickly.

Question 2 of 6

Not for thy righteousness

Was the land given to Israel for their righteousness?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. “Not for thy righteousness”: it was for the wickedness of the nations and so that the LORD might perform the word he sware to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Israel was “a stiffnecked people”.

Question 3 of 6

The record

How long had Israel been rebellious against the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

From the day they departed out of the land of Egypt until they came to the plains of Moab: “from the day that I knew you”.

Question 4 of 6

The calf

What did Moses do with the tables, and with the calf?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He cast the two tables out of his hands and brake them before their eyes. Then he took “your sin, the calf”, burnt it with fire, stamped it, ground it as small as dust, and cast the dust into the brook that descended out of the mount.

Question 5 of 6

The prayer

How long did Moses fall down before the LORD, and what did he plead?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Forty days and forty nights. He pleaded that they were the LORD's people and inheritance, that he had sworn to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and that his name would be put to shame among the nations.

Question 6 of 6

The other places

Where else did Israel provoke the LORD to wrath?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

At Taberah, and at Massah, and at Kibroth-hattaavah. And when the LORD sent them from Kadesh-barnea to go up and possess the land, they rebelled against his commandment, and believed him not.
