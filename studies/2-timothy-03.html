2 Timothy 3

Hard times, and the Book that holds

Paul sees the pressure coming and tells Timothy what to hold on to: a life people can watch, and the Scriptures.

This is the last letter Paul ever wrote, about AD 66 to 67, from a cold Roman prison, and he knows the end is close. He is writing to Timothy, the younger man he trained, who is leading a church in Ephesus.

Read alone, chapter 3 still stands. An old man tells a younger one what the pressure will look like and what will hold him up when it comes. Every man in this room can use both.

Part one

Perilous times

Verses 1–9

Paul gives Timothy a list. Read it slowly, and don't point it at anybody else.

The last days are now

“The last days” does not mean some far-off year. In the Bible it means the whole stretch between Jesus' first coming and His return. Paul says “perilous”, which means hard to live through and dangerous, and he says it to a man who is already in the middle of it.

  1. The crossabout AD 30 to 33
  2. Paul writesabout AD 66 to 67
  3. Todayyou are here
  4. He returnsno one knows the day

So this isn't a puzzle about the future. It's a description of the world a man has to get up and walk through this morning, and of what to stand on while he does.

Several of those hit close for a man coming out of addiction: no self-control, broken promises, cold toward family. That is not an insult. It is an honest picture of where a life ends up when self runs it.

That list was us, too

It would be easy to read verses 2 to 4 and think of the news, or the people at the next table. Don't. Paul wrote the same sort of list about himself and his friends:

“For we ourselves also were sometimes foolish, disobedient, deceived, serving divers lusts and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, and hating one another.”

Titus 3:3

Notice the word were. Most of us could tick several boxes on Paul's list, and some of us ticked them last week. Name them to God, plainly, without making excuses and without whipping yourself.

“But after that the kindness and love of God our Saviour toward man appeared, Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;”

Titus 3:4–5

The list is not there to shame you. It's there so you know exactly what Jesus came to rescue you from, and what He is working out of you now.

Church clothes, nobody home

A “form of godliness” is the look: the words, the habits, the face you wear at the meeting. “Denying the power” means none of it connects to anything real.

The cure isn't trying harder to look better. The power is Christ Himself, and He lives in anyone who trusts Him. The honest start is the opposite of a show: “I can't do this on my own.”

Hiding something behind the right words? Tell one brother the truth today.

Who they go after

Verse 6 is a hard one, so let's take it straight. Paul is describing predators, smooth teachers who slip into homes and go after people who are already weighed down. The old word “silly” means little and easily led, and these women were “laden with sins”: carrying guilt nobody had told them how to put down.

He is not insulting women, and he is not saying they were stupid. He is saying the con man always hunts the same way: find the guilty, the lonely and the hungry, and sell them an answer.

Men in recovery get hunted like this too: the slick preacher, the new group that wants all your time and money, the guy with a secret system. A quick test: does he point you to Jesus and His finished work and the open Bible, or to himself and your wallet?

Ever learning, never arriving

“Ever learning” is not praise. Some people collect ideas and never settle on one. They read it all and obey none of it. Another book, another speaker, another question, and the circle never opens.

Knowing about the truth is not the same as coming to it. Jesus said it plainly:

“I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6

Pick one truth you already know, and do it today.

Jannes and Jambres: a copy only goes so far

The Old Testament never names them. Jewish tradition says Jannes and Jambres were Egyptian magicians who stood against Moses before Pharaoh (Exodus 7–8). For a while they matched him:

Part two

A life Timothy could watch

Verses 10–13

Against the list, Paul puts a man you can watch up close.

What Timothy saw up close

“Thou hast fully known” means you've followed me closely and watched. Seven things, and every one is something a man can see:

  1. 1Doctrinewhat I taught
  2. 2Manner of lifehow I lived, day to day
  3. 3Purposewhat I was aiming at
  4. 4Faithhow I trusted God
  5. 5Longsufferinghow I put up with difficult people
  6. 6Charitylove that does something
  7. 7Patiencehow I held on when it dragged

Teaching is cheap. The proof that it works is a life someone can watch up close. Somewhere a younger man is watching you: a nephew, a coworker, the guy two weeks behind you in the program. Live today like he is.

Three towns, three beatings

Verse 11 lists places Timothy knew, from the first journey (about AD 46 to 48).

Antioch (in Pisidia): “expelled them out of their coasts” (Acts 13:50).

Iconium: a plot “to stone them”, and they fled (Acts 14:5–6).

Lystra, Timothy's own town: Paul was stoned and dragged out for dead, and “as the disciples stood round about him, he rose up” (Acts 14:19–20). Timothy may well have stood in that circle.

Not spared. Delivered.

Look at the verse again: “out of them all the Lord delivered me.” Delivered out of them, not kept away from them. Paul still got stoned. God did not prevent the trouble. He brought him through it, and he got up and walked back into the city.

That's the promise a man in a hard spot can lean on. It doesn't say the pain won't come. It says the Lord will be there on the other side of it. He got Paul up, and He can get you up.

When doing right costs you

Ifyou start to live godly in Christ,
→
thenexpect some pushback.

Most of us will never be stoned. Persecution looks smaller where we live: old friends who drop you or mock you, a family member who rolls their eyes and waits for you to fail, the guys at work who say “you're no fun now.”

One honest warning. Being treated badly because you are acting like a jerk is not persecution. Pushback for doing right is. It does not mean you did something wrong, and it does not mean God is gone. It's a sign you've turned a corner.

Down the other way

  1. evil menand seducers
  2. wax(grow) worse and worse
  3. deceivingother people
  4. being deceivedby their own lies

The same road that costs the godly man something does something worse to the other man. The word “wax” means grow. Nobody stands still. A man who deceives others ends up believing his own story, and then he can't see the way out.

Everyone in this room knows some of that. The way off the stairs is simple and hard: stop telling yourself the story, and say the true thing out loud to God and one brother.

Part three

Continue in the Book

Verses 14–17

With the world getting worse, here is what holds.

From a child

“Knowing of whom thou hast learned them,” Paul says. Timothy learned the Scriptures at home. His father was a Greek, but his mother was a Jewish believer, and his grandmother was too (Acts 16:1; 2 Timothy 1:5).

“When I call to remembrance the unfeigned faith that is in thee, which dwelt first in thy grandmother Lois, and thy mother Eunice; and I am persuaded that in thee also.”

2 Timothy 1:5
LoistaughtEunicetaughtTimothyPaul: “commit thou to faithful men” (2:2)the next man

If you had no Lois, this is for you: you can be the first link. The man sitting beside you may be the one who never had anyone. Somebody has to start the chain, and you've already got more to pass on than you think.

The Book points to a Person

“The holy scriptures” here means the Old Testament, the only Bible Timothy had as a boy. And the Old Testament was already aimed at one man. After His resurrection, Jesus walked two disciples through it:

“And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.”

Luke 24:27

It makes you “wise unto salvation”, and notice how: “through faith which is in Christ Jesus.” The Book does not save anyone by being read. It points you to the Saviour, and He saves anyone who trusts Him.

Not Paul's opinion. God's breath.

“Given by inspiration of God” is one Greek word, theopneustos: God-breathed. Men wrote it, with their own voices and their own lives, and God's breath was behind every word.

That is why you can put your weight on it. When you are shaky, you want to stand on something that doesn't depend on how you feel today.

What the Word does in a man

  1. DoctrineWhat's trueteaching: the road itself

    Who God is, what Jesus did, how I'm meant to live.

  2. ReproofWhere I'm offshows me the ditch

    It tells me when my life has left the road, and it does it honestly.

  3. CorrectionHow to get backsets me upright again

    It doesn't leave me in the ditch. It points the way back to the road.

  4. InstructionHow to stay on ittraining in doing right

    Day by day, coaching me into a new way of living.

Not perfect. Equipped.

Verse 17 says “perfect”, and it does not mean never makes a mistake. It means complete, fitted out, like a ship with everything it needs for the sea or a workman with a full toolbox. And “throughly furnished unto all good works” means ready for whatever the day throws at you.

Jesus Himself used the Book that way when He was tempted and hungry, in a wilderness, and the enemy came for Him:

“But he answered and said, It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.”

Matthew 4:4

A craving is a hunger. Feed on something that lasts. When the pull comes, reach for the Word before you reach for the phone.

Four questions for any chapter

You don't need to be a scholar. Open the Bible to one chapter today, and ask Paul's four things in order:

  1. What's true here?Doctrine: what does this say about God and about me?
  2. Where am I off?Reproof: what do I see in this that I don't like in me?
  3. What do I change?Correction: what's the way back, in plain steps?
  4. What do I practice today?Instruction: one small thing, done today.

If you miss a day, you haven't failed. Open it tomorrow and start again.

Points to remember

  1. Hard times“In the last days perilous times shall come” 3:1It all grows from love turned inward. Read the list as your own, then take it to Jesus.
  2. Pretending“Having a form of godliness, but denying the power thereof” 3:5A show has no power. Be honest with God and one brother, and the real thing gets in.
  3. The cost“All that will live godly in Christ Jesus shall suffer persecution” 3:12Pushback doesn't mean you're wrong. The Lord delivers, and He'll get you up.
  4. Roots“From a child thou hast known the holy scriptures” 3:15Be the first link if nobody was yours. Pass it on.
  5. Salvation“Wise unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus” 3:15The Book points to the Saviour. Trust Him, and it all comes together.
  6. The Word“All scripture is given by inspiration of God” 3:16Open it today and ask the four questions. You will be equipped.

Question 1 of 6

Hard times

What will men be like in the last days?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Lovers of their own selves, lovers of money, proud and unthankful, with no self-control, and lovers of pleasure more than lovers of God. It all grows from love pointed at self, and we were part of it. The cure is Jesus.

Question 2 of 6

Pretending

What is missing in a form of godliness?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The power. It has the look and the words, but nothing real behind them. Honesty with God and with one brother is where the real thing starts, and a copy never gets far.

Question 3 of 6

The cost

What will happen to all that will live godly in Christ Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They will suffer persecution, some pushback from a world that doesn't like it. But the Lord delivered Paul out of every one of his, and he does the same for us. The deceivers only get worse.

The Word
