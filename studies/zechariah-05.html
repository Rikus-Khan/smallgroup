Zechariah 5

The flying roll

Two strange visions: a giant flying scroll that brings God's curse into the houses of thieves and liars, and a woman called Wickedness, sealed in a basket and carried far away.

Part one

The flying roll

Verses 1–4

God has cleaned His priest and promised His Spirit. Now He deals with the sin among His people.

A scroll you can't miss

Quick reminder: Zechariah was a priest and prophet in Jerusalem about 520 BC, after the exile. This is the sixth of his night visions.

A “roll” is a scroll. This one is spread wide open, flying, and enormous.

30 ftlong (20 cubits)
15 ftwide (10 cubits)

The same size as the porch of Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:3): God's word, big enough for everybody to read.

Written on both sides

The scroll is “the curse”: the penalty God's law said would fall on those who broke it. Like the two stone tablets of the Ten Commandments, it's written on both sides.

On this side

“Every one that stealeth”

A sin against your neighbor. “Thou shalt not steal” (Exodus 20:15).

On that side

“Him that sweareth falsely by my name”

A sin against God: using His name to back up a lie. “Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain” (Exodus 20:7).

“Remove far from me vanity and lies: give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with food convenient for me: Lest I be full, and deny thee, and say, Who is the LORD? or lest I be poor, and steal, and take the name of my God in vain.”

Proverbs 30:8–9

The two go together. A man who steals usually ends up lying about it, and often swearing to God that he didn't.

It shall remain in the midst of his house.

The scroll doesn't just fly by. It flies in the front door, settles down inside, and eats the house from within, beams and stones and all, like rot.

“But if ye will not do so, behold, ye have sinned against the LORD: and be sure your sin will find you out.”

Numbers 32:23

Many of us know this one from the inside. The stash hidden at home, the lies about where the money went. It didn't stay hidden, and it ate at the whole family. Sin kept in the dark doesn't sit still.

A thief can change

The curse is real. But it isn't the end of the story for any man who turns to God. Paul wrote to believers who used to steal:

a thief → a giver “working with his hands … that he may have to give”

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28

“Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another.”

Ephesians 4:25

Part two

Wickedness in a basket

Verses 5–11

The seventh vision: God doesn't just judge sin. He packs it up and sends it away.

Shut in

An “ephah” was a grain basket holding about six gallons: the tool of the market, and a favorite way to cheat (Amos 8:5).

Inside sits a woman: “This is wickedness.” (The Hebrew word is feminine, so the picture is a woman. It isn't a statement about women.)

She tries to climb out. The angel shoves her back and slams down a lid of lead, a “talent,” about 75 pounds.

Then the basket is lifted up and carried off. Wickedness isn't allowed to stay among God's people.

Sent back to Babylon

A stork is a strong bird that flies far, and an unclean one under the law (Leviticus 11:19): an unclean carrier for an unclean load.

“Shinar” is the old name for Babylon's land, where men built the tower of Babel (Genesis 11:2, 9). God's people came home from Babylon. Now their wickedness is sent back.

“Upon her own base” is how you'd describe an idol on its stand. Wickedness gets a house far away, and God's people get theirs back clean.

What God does with sin

Put the two visions side by side, and you see the whole of how God deals with sin among His people.

  1. Exposedthe open scroll, there for all to read
  2. Judgedthe curse comes into the house
  3. Shut insealed under a lid of lead
  4. Removedcarried far away

Every one of those happened for us at the cross. Jesus took the curse on Himself, so it would never land on the man who trusts Him. And He carries the sin away.

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

Points to remember

  1. Hidden sin“It shall remain in the midst of his house” 5:4Sin hidden at home doesn't stay hidden. It eats the house from the inside.
  2. Stealing“Every one that stealeth shall be cut off” 5:3God takes stealing seriously, and in Christ a thief can become a giver.
  3. Truth“Him that sweareth falsely by my name” 5:4God's name is never a cover for a lie.
  4. Wickedness“This is wickedness” 5:8God names it, shuts it in, and won't let it stay among His people.
  5. Removed“To build it an house in the land of Shinar” 5:11God carries sin far away from His people.
  6. The curse“This is the curse” 5:3Christ became a curse for us, so the man who trusts Him is free of it.

Question 1 of 6

Hidden sin

Can a man hide sin inside his own house?

What verses tell us about this?

“But if ye will not do so, behold, ye have sinned against the LORD: and be sure your sin will find you out.”

Numbers 32:23

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Answer

No. God's word flies right in the front door. Sin kept secret at home doesn't stay put; it eats away at the man and at everyone under his roof. The way out is to bring it into the light, with God and with someone you trust. In the light, the blood of Jesus cleans it.

Question 2 of 6

Stealing

What does God say about stealing?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou shalt not steal.”

Exodus 20:15

“Let him that stole steal no more: but rather let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.”

Ephesians 4:28

Answer

It's a sin against your neighbor that God judges. But God's goal for a thief isn't only that he stops. It's that he changes: works honestly, and ends up with something to give. A man who used to take can become a man who gives.

Question 3 of 6

Truth

Why does God care about a sworn word?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain; for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain.”

Exodus 20:7

“Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another.”

Ephesians 4:25

Answer

Because a man who swears “by God” is putting God's name on his words. If they're a lie, he's dragging God's name into it. God is truth, and His people are meant to be men whose word can be trusted. Trust is rebuilt one true word at a time.

Question 4 of 6

Wickedness

What does God do with wickedness among His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.”

Psalm 51:10

Answer

He calls it what it is, shuts it in so it can't run loose, and takes it away. God doesn't leave wickedness living among His people as if it didn't matter. And He doesn't just box up our sin; He gives us a clean heart in its place.

Question 5 of 6

Removed

How far can God carry our sin away?

What verses tell us about this?

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

“He will turn again, he will have compassion upon us; he will subdue our iniquities; and thou wilt cast all their sins into the depths of the sea.”

Micah 7:19

Answer

As far as the east is from the west: farther than any map. For Zechariah it was hundreds of miles to Babylon. For the man who trusts Jesus, the sin is gone so far that God will never hold it against him. When the old shame comes back, remember where God put it.

Question 6 of 6

The curse

How does a man get out from under the curse?

What verses tell us about this?

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Answer

Not by trying harder to keep the law; the law is what pronounces the curse. Jesus took the curse on Himself when He hung on the cross. Anyone who trusts Him is redeemed, bought out from under it, and there is no condemnation left for him.
