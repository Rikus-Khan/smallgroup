Leviticus 8

As the LORD commanded Moses

What the LORD commanded in Exodus 29, Moses now does in front of all the people: Aaron and his sons are washed, clothed, anointed and covered with blood, and they stay at the door for seven days.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. At the doorWho was gathered at the door of the tabernacle, and what did Moses say about what was being done?
  2. Washed and clothedWhat was done to Aaron before he was clothed, and what was put in the breastplate?
  3. The oilWhat did Moses anoint with oil, and who was anointed last?
  4. The handsWhat did Aaron and his sons do to the bullock and the two rams?
  5. The bloodWhere did Moses put the blood of the ram of consecration on Aaron?
  6. Seven daysWhere were Aaron and his sons to stay for seven days, and why?

Part one

“Gather thou all the congregation”

Verses 1–5

The whole people at the door, to see their priests made.

Done in the open

Moses gathered the whole congregation “unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation” and told them: “This is the thing which the LORD commanded to be done.”

Nothing about the priesthood was done in secret. The people could see that Aaron did not take the office himself. He was brought, washed, clothed and anointed by the word of the LORD.

Part two

Washed, clothed, anointed

Verses 6–13

First the water, then the garments, then the oil.

“Girded” means tied on with a belt. “Mitre” is the turban, and on its front, on a golden plate, was “the holy crown.” The breastplate, with its twelve stones, sat over Aaron's heart.

The place first, then the priest

  1. the tabernacleand all in itverse 10
  2. the altarsprinkled seven timesverse 11
  3. the laverand his footverse 11
  4. Aaronoil on his headverse 12

Everything the priest would touch was made holy before the priest was. Moses was doing what the LORD had said in Exodus 40:9–11: anoint the tabernacle, the altar and the laver.

Poured, not dabbed

The oil ran down. Psalm 133 uses that very picture, and it is a picture of brothers:

“Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity! It is like the precious ointment upon the head, that ran down upon the beard, even Aaron’s beard: that went down to the skirts of his garments; As the dew of Hermon, and as the dew that descended upon the mountains of Zion: for there the LORD commanded the blessing, even life for evermore.”

Psalm 133:1–3

The oil ran from Aaron's head down to the skirts of his garments, covering the whole man. And the psalm says it is good for brothers to dwell together in unity. Men who are being made new in the same Lord do well to do it.

Aaron, and the sons who stood with him

  • Aaronanointed on the head
    • Nadab
    • Abihu
    • Eleazar
    • Ithamar

This chapter says only “Aaron and his sons.” The Bible gives their names in Exodus 6:23 and Exodus 28:1: Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar. All four stood at this door, and every one was washed, clothed, anointed and covered with the same blood.

Hold on to that. The same blood, the same oil, the same word of the LORD, on every one of them. Chapter 10 comes soon.

Priests, by the blood

“And from Jesus Christ, who is the faithful witness, and the first begotten of the dead, and the prince of the kings of the earth. Unto him that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood, And hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father; to him be glory and dominion for ever and ever. Amen.”

Revelation 1:5–6

Washed, and made priests. The order is the same as at the door of the tabernacle: first the washing, then the office.

“But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:”

1 Peter 2:9

Peter says it to every believer, not to a class of men. You do not need a priest in a turban to come near to God. You are a royal priesthood, because of the one who loved you and washed you from your sins in his own blood.

Part three

Three offerings

Verses 14–29

The sin offering, the burnt offering and the ram of consecration.

A sin offering for the priests first

The first offering was “the bullock for the sin offering.” It was for Aaron and his sons themselves. The men who would stand between the people and God had to be cleansed of their own sin before anything else.

“For such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and made higher than the heavens; Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people’s: for this he did once, when he offered up himself. For the law maketh men high priests which have infirmity; but the word of the oath, which was since the law, maketh the Son, who is consecrated for evermore.”

Hebrews 7:26–28

Aaron needed a sin offering first, and so did every priest after him. Jesus did not. He is “holy, harmless, undefiled,” and he offered himself once, for the people's sins and not his own.

If your own sin has been heavy on you, notice where the priests began. Not with the people's sins. With their own.

Outside the camp

The bullock's body, its hide and its dung were not burned on the altar. “He burnt with fire without the camp; as the LORD commanded Moses” (verse 17).

Paul writes of the sin offerings burned without the camp, and says Jesus suffered outside the gate, to sanctify the people with his own blood.

And then comes a call to us: “Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach.” A man who follows him may be looked down on by the people he used to run with. He is in good company.

Three times, the same words

Count it. “Aaron and his sons laid their hands upon the head” of the bullock (verse 14), of the first ram (verse 18) and of the second ram (verse 22): three times in this chapter.

The bullock

A sin offering

My sin needs to be dealt with. The body was burned outside the camp (verse 17).

The first ram

A burnt offering

“The whole ram upon the altar”: all of it given to God, “a sweet savour” (verse 21).

Savour means smell. First the sin is dealt with, then the man gives himself, all of him, to God. The order is the same as in Exodus 29, and it is the same order for you.

The same blood on Aaron and on his sons

Look at verses 23 and 24. The blood goes on the tip of Aaron's right ear, the thumb of his right hand, the great toe of his right foot. Then Moses brings Aaron's sons, and puts it in the same three places on them.

  • Aaronthe high priest: ear, thumb, toe
  • each of his sonsthe same blood, the same three places

The man at the top and the least of his sons were covered alike. Nobody stood on his own record that day. In Christ it is no different: the new believer and the old saint stand on the same blood.

Part four

“Ye shall abide at the door”

Verses 30–36

The meal, and seven days and nights in the court of the tabernacle.

Blood and oil, together

In verse 30 Moses takes “of the anointing oil, and of the blood which was upon the altar” and sprinkles both on Aaron, his garments, his sons and their garments. Blood and oil, together on the same men.

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

“And such were some of you.” Paul had just listed the sins that shut a man out of the kingdom. Then he said: such were some of you. Past tense. Washed, sanctified, justified, by the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of our God.

If you are carrying what you were, hear the other half of the sentence. You are washed.

Seven days and nights at the door

  1. this daythe offerings, then the meal“As he hath done this day” (34)
  2. seven daysday and night at the door“keep the charge of the LORD” (35)
  3. the eighth daythe next chapterLeviticus 9:1

Abide means stay. The charge of the LORD is what he told them to do. They were not to leave the door of the tabernacle for seven days and nights, until the days of their consecration were at an end.

“That ye die not.” The LORD is holy, and approaching him was not casual. Moses says it was “so I am commanded.” Nothing was his own idea.

“As the LORD commanded” in this chapter

Verse by verse, here is where the chapter says “as the LORD commanded”: in verses 4, 9, 13, 17, 21 and 29. Six times.

Then verse 36 sums it up: “So Aaron and his sons did all things which the LORD commanded by the hand of Moses.” Moses did what he was told, and Aaron and his sons did what they were told. That is how a priesthood was begun.

Real world: stay near the door

The priests were not allowed to go out for seven days and nights. They were in the place where the LORD had met them, and where the blood had just been applied.

  • The first daysA new believer is the most likely to drift back to the old places. Stay near where God has met you: his word, his people, your church.
  • BrothersYou do not have to carry the first days alone. Call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor, before the pull gets strong.
  • If you have been outYou did not forfeit the blood. Come back to the door. The sin offering came first, and it is still for you.

The picture is a week at the door. For you it may mean a first week, or the hard evenings. The text only says: they stayed, and they did what the LORD told them.

Points to remember

  1. At the door“This is the thing which the LORD commanded to be done” 8:5The priesthood was made in the open, by the LORD's word.
  2. Washed and clothed“Moses brought Aaron and his sons, and washed them with water” 8:6The washing came first. So it is for us: “ye are washed.”
  3. The oil“he poured of the anointing oil upon Aaron’s head, and anointed him” 8:12The place was made holy first, then the man, last of all.
  4. The hands“Aaron and his sons laid their hands upon the head of the bullock” 8:14Three times, and the priests began with their own sin.
  5. The blood“put it upon the tip of Aaron’s right ear, and upon the thumb of his right hand, and upon the great toe of his right foot” 8:23Aaron and each of his sons got the same blood.
  6. Seven days“ye abide at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation day and night seven days” 8:35Stay near where God met you, and near his people.

Question 1 of 6

At the door

Who was gathered at the door of the tabernacle, and what did Moses say about what was being done?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All the congregation, with Aaron and his sons. Moses told them: “This is the thing which the LORD commanded to be done.”

Question 2 of 6

Washed and clothed

What was done to Aaron before he was clothed, and what was put in the breastplate?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Moses washed him with water. Then he put on him the coat, the girdle, the robe, the ephod and the breastplate, and put the Urim and the Thummim in the breastplate.

Question 3 of 6

The oil

What did Moses anoint with oil, and who was anointed last?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The tabernacle and everything in it, the altar and its vessels, and the laver and its foot. Aaron was anointed last of these, with oil poured on his head; then in verse 30 Moses sprinkled the oil and the blood on Aaron, his sons and their garments.

Question 4 of 6

The hands

What did Aaron and his sons do to the bullock and the two rams?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They laid their hands on its head each time: the bullock for the sin offering, the ram for the burnt offering and the ram of consecration.

Question 5 of 6

The blood

Where did Moses put the blood of the ram of consecration on Aaron?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On the tip of his right ear, the thumb of his right hand and the great toe of his right foot. Then the same on each of his sons.

Question 6 of 6

Seven days

Where were Aaron and his sons to stay for seven days, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

At the door of the tabernacle, day and night, until the days of their consecration were at an end. They were to keep the charge of the LORD, “that ye die not.”
