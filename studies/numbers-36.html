Numbers 36

The inheritance stays in the tribe, and the book ends

The daughters of Zelophehad may marry whom they think best, only within the tribe of their father, so that no inheritance moves from tribe to tribe. “These are the commandments and the judgments, which the LORD commanded by the hand of Moses… in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho.”

In chapter 27 the LORD said: “The daughters of Zelophehad speak right” (27:7). Now the chief fathers of Gilead, their own family, come back with a second question. The five are named three times in Numbers (26:33; 27:1; 36:11).

The fear, step by step

If a daughter marries into another tribeher inheritance goes with her
→
And at the jubileit is “put unto the inheritance of the tribe whereunto they are received”
→
So“taken away from the inheritance of the tribe of our fathers”

The jubile is the fiftieth year, when every man returned to his possession (Leviticus 25:10), Israel's safeguard against land lost for good. A marriage across tribes would break it: the land would come home to the wrong tribe, and stay there.

They do not complain about the daughters. They ask Moses openly, before the damage. That is how a man brings a worry to the LORD's people.

Part two

“The tribe of the sons of Joseph hath said well”

Verses 5–9

Moses answers by the word of the LORD, and the daughters stay free.

Free, within one line

Left to them

“to whom they think best”

The LORD did not choose their husbands. Each daughter was free to choose, by her own judgment: nobody was marrying them off.

The one limit

“only to the family of the tribe of their father”

So the land stayed where the LORD had given it. “Neither shall the inheritance remove from one tribe to another tribe” (verse 9).

The ruling answers the fear exactly, and it does not take the daughters' freedom away. Moses does not rule on his own wisdom: the answer is “according to the word of the LORD” (verse 5). He tells the men their words were good: “hath said well”. A true worry, honestly brought, is heard.

An inheritance to keep

The word inheritance stands seventeen times in these thirteen verses. Every family is to “keep himself to the inheritance of the tribe of his fathers” (verse 7). What the LORD gave must not drift away.

“Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, which according to his abundant mercy hath begotten us again unto a lively hope by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead, To an inheritance incorruptible, and undefiled, and that fadeth not away, reserved in heaven for you,”

1 Peter 1:3–4

Israel's inheritance was a piece of ground, and it could be lost, by a marriage or a bad year. Peter says ours is incorruptible, undefiled and fadeth not away, and “reserved in heaven for you”. It is kept for us. We are still to guard what we have been given: the walk, the Word, the brothers.

They married their father's brothers' sons, their own cousins, within Manasseh. And when the land was divided, Joshua gave them their share. What God said to them in the plains of Moab, they lived to see in Canaan.

“By the hand of Moses”

Moses wrote this book, as the book itself says of the journeys: “Moses wrote their goings out according to their journeys by the commandment of the LORD” (33:2). The last verse says it again of its commandments: by the hand of Moses. The words “near Jericho” close the book, and they come eight times in it: 26:3, 26:63, 31:12, 33:48, 33:50, 34:15, 35:1 and now 36:13. The people have been camped on that plain since chapter 22 (22:1), and what they heard in these last chapters was spoken there.

There on the plain, east of the Jordan, over against Jericho: the promised land is in sight, and they are still outside it.

Looking back over Numbers

  1. 1–10:10At Sinaicounted and set in order
  2. 10:11–14The march, and Kadesh“Let us return into Egypt”
  3. 15–20The wandering yearsKorah, the rod, the rock
  4. 21–36The march to Moabthe serpent, Balaam, Peor, the new count

It opened “on the first day of the second month, in the second year after they were come out of the land of Egypt” (1:1), with a people counted and ordered round the tabernacle. It ends in “the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho”, late in the fortieth year. From Kadesh-barnea to the brook Zered had been “thirty and eight years” (Deuteronomy 2:14).

Between those two places is every kind of failure these men know: lusting for the old life, wanting to go back, fear of giants, grumbling, a leader's anger, a sin in the camp that found them out. And all the way, the cloud, the manna and the water.

Nearly the same number, and, save Caleb and Joshua, not one of the same men. “But among these there was not a man of them whom Moses and Aaron the priest numbered, when they numbered the children of Israel in the wilderness of Sinai. For the LORD had said of them, They shall surely die in the wilderness. And there was not left a man of them, save Caleb the son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the son of Nun” (26:64–65).

Forty years, “each day for a year” (14:34), and a whole generation had fallen. And God kept his word to their children: the people on the plain are nearly as many as the people who left Sinai.

Written for us

“But with many of them God was not well pleased: for they were overthrown in the wilderness. Now these things were our examples, to the intent we should not lust after evil things, as they also lusted.”

1 Corinthians 10:5–6

“So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief.”

Hebrews 3:19

That is the book in two verses: a people the LORD led, fed and kept, and those who would not believe did not enter in. Paul adds why it is written: “for our admonition” (1 Corinthians 10:11).

The grace of it: the LORD never stopped leading a murmuring people. He is the same God to the man who failed yesterday: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9).

And then Deuteronomy

“And it came to pass in the fortieth year, in the eleventh month, on the first day of the month, that Moses spake unto the children of Israel, according unto all that the LORD had given him in commandment unto them;”

Deuteronomy 1:3

Numbers stops on the plain, and Deuteronomy begins there, in the same place: “on this side Jordan” (Deuteronomy 1:1). Moses is a hundred and twenty years old at the end (Deuteronomy 34:7), and he spends his last days speaking to the new generation: he tells them again where they came from, what the LORD commanded, and what lies ahead.

The people had failed once at the edge of the land, and now they stand at the edge again. The difference is that they have heard, in this book, what it cost their fathers. The book of Numbers leaves them listening.

Real world: hold what he gave you

  • SinMen drift out of their inheritance slowly: a compromise, a marriage that pulls the wrong way, a place they had no business in. The men of Gilead saw it coming, and spoke.
  • RegretWe have already lost some of what we were given: years, trust, a family's good name. The jubile says that what was lost can be returned to a man (Leviticus 25:10).
  • GraceOurs is “incorruptible, and undefiled, and that fadeth not away” (1 Peter 1:4). It does not depend on how well we guard it.
  • HopeThe children went in. The generation that failed was replaced by the one that heard, and the God who counted them the first time counted them again.
  • TodayBring one worry to a brother in Christ before it becomes damage, the way the chief fathers came to Moses. And if it is heavier than a worry, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today.

Question 1 of 6

The worry

What did the chief fathers of the children of Gilead fear?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That the inheritance of the daughters of Zelophehad would pass to another tribe if they married into it, and at the jubile be lost for good to their fathers' tribe.

Question 2 of 6

The word of the LORD

Whose word did Moses speak when he answered them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The word of the LORD. “This is the thing which the LORD doth command”, and Moses told the men of Joseph that they had said well.

Question 3 of 6

Free, within a line

Whom were the daughters free to marry?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Whom they thought best, only within the family of the tribe of their father.

Question 4 of 6

The inheritance

Why must no inheritance remove from tribe to tribe?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So that every tribe kept the share the LORD gave it, “that the children of Israel may enjoy every man the inheritance of his fathers”.

Question 5 of 6

The obedience

What did the daughters of Zelophehad do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

As the LORD commanded Moses, so they did: all five were married to their father's brothers' sons, and their inheritance remained in the tribe of their father.
