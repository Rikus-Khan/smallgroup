Numbers 29

The seventh month

The trumpets, the day of atonement, and a feast of seven days where the bullocks come down by one each day, from thirteen to seven, and then one on the eighth. A goat for sin on every one of the ten days.

Part one

“A day of blowing the trumpets”

Verses 1–11

The first day of the seventh month, and the tenth, when they afflicted their souls.

Old words in these verses

  • blowing the trumpetsa day that began with the sound of trumpets
  • afflict your soulshumble yourselves before God, and sorrow for sin
  • according unto their mannerby the rule already given
  • servile workthe ordinary labour of the week

The same offering stands on both days: one young bullock, one ram, seven lambs, and one kid of the goats for a sin offering (verses 2–5, 8–11). Nothing on the first day is bigger because it is a trumpet, and nothing on the tenth is bigger because it is the day of atonement. What marks the tenth day is the instruction to the people: “ye shall afflict your souls.” The offering of the day itself is told in Leviticus 16, and verse 11 says this goat comes beside it: “beside the sin offering of atonement.”

Part two

“Ye shall keep a feast unto the LORD seven days”

Verses 12–34

Thirteen bullocks, then twelve, then eleven: one fewer every day.

Part three

“On the eighth day ye shall have a solemn assembly”

Verses 35–40

One bullock, and then the whole month's offerings are told.

Beside your vows, and Moses told them

  • set feaststhe feasts the LORD himself had set, with their days fixed
  • vowspromises a man made to the LORD
  • freewill offeringsgifts brought because a man wanted to, and no one had asked

Everything in these two chapters is what the LORD required. Verse 39 adds that a man still had his own offerings “beside” them: the vow he had made, and the gift he gave because his heart wanted to. The required was the floor. A man who loved the LORD brought more.

And then: “Moses told the children of Israel according to all that the LORD commanded Moses.” He did not shorten it, did not add to it, did not tell them only the easy part. The next chapter is about the vow: what a man says to the LORD, and what it means to keep it.

A goat for sin on every one of the ten days. Moses does not say why the count comes down by one each day, and we won't guess.

A feast of joy on a foundation of blood

The LORD had said of this feast, in Leviticus 23: “ye shall rejoice before the LORD your God seven days.” Numbers 29 shows what the joy stood on: seventy bullocks, and a goat for sin every day. Joy and the altar went together. A man is not joyful because he has no sin to answer for. He is joyful because it has been answered.

“And not only so, but we also joy in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom we have now received the atonement.”

Romans 5:11

Paul says it in one sentence: “we also joy in God… by whom we have now received the atonement.” The joy is real, and it is paid for.

So many bulls and goats

“For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins.”

Hebrews 10:4

Seventy-three bullocks and ten goats in this one chapter, and Hebrews says plainly: it could not take away sins. It is not the LORD's word that failed. The law, it says, had “a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things” (Hebrews 10:1). The sacrifices kept the question in front of the people. They could not settle it.

“For then must he often have suffered since the foundation of the world: but now once in the end of the world hath he appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.”

Hebrews 9:26

Seventy-three bullocks in one month, and the altar was never done. Hebrews takes us past them: many sacrifices that could never finish the work, and one that did.

Real world: a joy that has been paid for

  • SinA goat for sin on every one of the ten days, even in the feast of joy. Sin does not take a holiday, and neither does the answer to it.
  • RegretA man can carry regret into a good day and spoil it. The goat was already given. Put it down.
  • Grace“By whom we have now received the atonement.” Not will receive. Have received.
  • HopeSeventy bullocks in a week could not finish it; “one offering” did. The long work of sacrifice is over, and a man can rejoice.
  • TodayFind one clean joy in the day: a meal with a brother, a song, a walk with your kids, work done well. Take it, and thank the LORD for it by name.

  1. The trumpets“And in the seventh month, on the first day of the month, ye shall have an holy convocation… it is a day of blowing the trumpets unto you.” 29:1The first day of the seventh month, with one bullock, one ram, seven lambs and a goat for a sin offering.
  2. The tenth day“ye shall afflict your souls: ye shall not do any work therein” 29:7On the tenth day they humbled themselves and did no work.
  3. The bullocks“thirteen young bullocks, two rams, and fourteen lambs of the first year” 29:13Thirteen on the first day of the feast, twelve on the second, down one each day.
  4. The count“on the seventh day seven bullocks” 29:32Seven on the seventh day, and one bullock on the eighth, a solemn assembly. Seventy in the seven days.
  5. Beside“These things ye shall do unto the LORD in your set feasts, beside your vows, and your freewill offerings” 29:39The required offerings stood beside the vows and freewill offerings a man brought himself.
  6. Moses“And Moses told the children of Israel according to all that the LORD commanded Moses.” 29:40Moses told them all of it, as the LORD commanded.

Question 1 of 6

The trumpets

On what day of the seventh month is a day of blowing the trumpets?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The first day of the seventh month. They offered one young bullock, one ram and seven lambs, with a kid of the goats for a sin offering.

Question 2 of 6

The tenth day

What were the people to do on the tenth day of the seventh month?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They had an holy convocation, afflicted their souls, and did not do any work. The offering was one bullock, one ram, seven lambs and a kid of the goats for sin, beside the sin offering of atonement.

Question 3 of 6

The bullocks

On the fifteenth day how many young bullocks were offered, and how many on the second day?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Thirteen young bullocks on the fifteenth day, the first day of the feast, and twelve on the second day. Each day, two rams, fourteen lambs, and a goat for a sin offering.

Question 4 of 6

The count

How many bullocks on the seventh day, and how many on the eighth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Seven bullocks on the seventh day, and one on the eighth, which was a solemn assembly. Thirteen, twelve, eleven, ten, nine, eight and seven make seventy for the seven days.

Question 5 of 6

Beside

What were these set feasts kept beside?

What verses tell us about this?

“And not only so, but we also joy in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom we have now received the atonement.”

Answer

Beside their vows and their freewill offerings, which a man brought of his own. Beside the continual burnt offering too (verses 11, 16 and on). What the LORD required was the floor, and a man could give more.
