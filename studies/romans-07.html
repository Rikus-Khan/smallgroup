Paul wrote this letter from Corinth, about AD 57, to the believers in Rome. Chapters 1–3: all have sinned. Chapters 4–5: God makes a man right with Himself by faith. Chapters 6–8: how a saved man lives free.

  1. Dead to sinsin is no longer your master
  2. Dead to the lawrules can't change you, and the war inside is real
  3. Alive in the Spiritno condemnation, no separation

A new believer asks two honest questions. If I'm not under the law, what's the law for? And if I'm free, why do I still fall? Chapter 7 answers both.

Part one

Dead to the law

Verses 1–6

A marriage only death can end, and a new Husband.

Till death do us part

Everyone knew this much: a marriage is for life, and only a death ends it. “Dominion” means rule.

You “are become dead to the law by the body of Christ.” When Jesus died, you died with Him (6:6). The law's claim on you ended at the cross.

And you're “married to another,” the risen Christ. You don't belong to a rulebook now. You belong to a living Person.

Old fruit, new fruit

Married to the law · v. 5

“fruit unto death”

“The motions of sins” are sin's urges and cravings, at work in “our members,” our bodies. The law said no, and the cravings only got louder.

Married to Christ · vv. 4, 6

“fruit unto God”

A new life working from the inside: “newness of spirit,” not “the oldness of the letter,” rules written on a page.

Rules alone never kept any of us sober. A list of don'ts can't change a heart. A relationship can. You serve God now, not out of fear of the rulebook, but because you belong to Him.

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Part two

Is the law sin?

Verses 7–13

A good law, and what sin did with it.

A light in a dark room

So is the law the problem? “God forbid”: never.

The law is a light switched on in a dark room. The mess was there all along. Paul thought he was fine until he met “Thou shalt not covet.” You can keep your hands clean. You can't keep your wanting clean.

“Covet” is wanting what isn't yours. “Concupiscence” is that wanting running wild. Until the commandment came, sin lay quiet, “dead.”

How sin uses a good rule

  1. The commandment camev. 9
  2. Sin took occasionused it to attack, v. 8
  3. The wanting grew“all manner of concupiscence,” v. 8
  4. Sin revivedit woke up, v. 9
  5. It deceived mepromised life, v. 11
  6. It slew medelivered death, v. 11

“Taking occasion” is a soldier's word: a base to launch an attack from. Sin grabbed the commandment and turned it against him. Tell a man he can't, and something in him wants it more. Every addict knows that pull.

So whose fault is it? Not the law's: it “is holy… and just, and good” (v. 12). The law did its job. It showed sin to be “exceeding sinful,” as bad as it really is. The problem isn't God's rules. It's the sin in us.

Part three

The war inside

Verses 14–20

“What I would, that do I not.”

Whose struggle is this?

Watch the verbs. Verses 7–13 look back: “I was alive… I died.” From verse 14 on, it's right now: “I am… I do… I find.”

7had8did9died10found11slew12—13made 14am15do16do17dwells18know19do 20do21find22delight23see24am25serve

So most Bible teachers, Baptist pastors among them, hear Paul the believer here, telling the truth about himself. A lost man doesn't “delight in the law of God” (v. 22). And late in life Paul still wrote of sinners, “of whom I am chief” (1 Timothy 1:15). Present tense.

“Carnal”: fleshly. “Sold under sin”: like a slave at market. “I allow not”: I don't approve of what I do. No excuses. Just honesty.

A war every believer knows

You wake up meaning to do right, and by night you've done the thing you swore you wouldn't. Paul knew that feeling.

But you hate it. Before Christ you didn't fight. The fight is a sign of new life.

“For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh: and these are contrary the one to the other: so that ye cannot do the things that ye would.”

Galatians 5:17

Willpower goes round in circles

“Flesh” here is the old sinful self still in you, still pulling the wrong way. In it “dwelleth no good thing.”

“To will is present”: I want to. “How to perform… I find not”: I can't pull it off. Willpower is real. It just isn't enough.

“No more I that do it” isn't blaming someone else; the new man in you doesn't want this. And the way out isn't a better promise. It's a Person (v. 25).

Part four

O wretched man

Verses 21–25

“Who shall deliver me?”

A law that pulls every time

“A law” here is a rule that always works, like gravity. Drop a stone, and it falls. Every time.

“The law of my mind” loves God's law. “The law of sin… in my members” pulls the other way, to drag him into “captivity,” like a prisoner of war.

Gravity never takes a day off, and neither does the flesh. But chapter 8 names a stronger law (8:2).

From the cry to the thanks

verse 24The cry

“O wretched man that I am!”

Honest. No excuses left.

verse 24The question

“Who shall deliver me…?”

Not what. Who.

verse 25The answer

“I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

The rescue is a Person.

“Wretched” means worn out, miserable, at the end of yourself. “The body of this death” is this body, where sin still lives, the body that will one day die. Paul is tired of the fight, and he says so.

He doesn't ask what will deliver him: a new program, a better plan, more willpower. He asks who. And he knows the answer before he finishes the sentence.

Still fighting, already free

Romans 7:25→the fight goes onUntil the day our bodies are made new (8:23), the flesh will keep pulling.
Romans 8:1→the verdict is already in“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus.”

Both are true at once. You'll fight sin as long as you live in this body. But a man in Christ fights from “no condemnation,” not toward it. That's chapter 8.

Points to remember

  1. The law“The law is holy… and just, and good” 7:12The law shows sin like a light in a dark room. It can't wash it away.
  2. Freedom“Married to another, even to him who is raised from the dead” 7:4Your old bond to the law ended at the cross. Now you belong to the risen Christ.
  3. Sin“Sin, taking occasion by the commandment, deceived me” 7:11Sin twists a good “no” into a “want.” The problem isn't the rule. It's the sin in us.
  4. The struggle“What I would, that do I not; but what I hate, that do I” 7:15The war is real in every believer. Hating your sin is a sign of new life, not proof you're lost.
  5. Honesty“O wretched man that I am!” 7:24Own it. No excuses, no hiding, no giving up. Bring it to God.
  6. Deliverance“I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord” 7:25The rescue is a Person, not a program. And the next words are “no condemnation.”

Question 1 of 6

The law

What can God's law do, and what can't it do?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight: for by the law is the knowledge of sin.”

Romans 3:20

Answer

The law is holy, just and good. It shows us right from wrong, and it shows sin for what it really is, like a light switched on in a dark room. But it can't make a man good, and it can't set him free. It can show you the mess. Only Christ can clean it up.

Question 2 of 6

Freedom

What does the marriage picture in verses 1–4 teach us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Only a death ends a marriage. When Christ died, we died with Him, and the law's claim on us ended. Now we belong to the risen Christ, and we serve Him out of love, not fear of a rulebook.

Question 3 of 6

Sin

How does sin use even a good commandment against us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Sin uses the commandment as a launch pad. Tell a man “thou shalt not,” and the sin in him wants it more. Then it lies, promising life and paying out death. The fault isn't God's law. It's the sin in us.

Question 4 of 6

The struggle

Why does a saved man still do the things he hates?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the flesh, the old sinful self, still lives in him and pulls the other way, and willpower alone can't win. But his “inward man” delights in God's law. Hating the sin is a sign of new life. It isn't an excuse to keep sinning, and it isn't proof you were never saved.

Question 5 of 6

Honesty

What should a believer do with his failures?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Answer

Tell the truth about them, like Paul did: no excuses and no hiding. Bring them to God, who forgives and cleanses. The world's kind of sorrow says, “I'm hopeless, why try,” and it leads to death. Godly sorrow turns back to God, gets up, and keeps walking.

Question 6 of 6

Deliverance

Who will deliver us from the body of this death?

What verses tell us about this?

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Answer

Jesus Christ our Lord: a Person, not a program. He has set us free from condemnation, His Spirit lives in us to fight the flesh, and one day He'll give us bodies with no sin in them. What He started in you, He will finish.
