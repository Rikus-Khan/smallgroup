Deuteronomy 7

Because the LORD loved you

Moses tells Israel what to do with the nations of Canaan and their idols, and why God chose them: not because they were many or good, “but because the LORD loved you.” He will drive the nations out “by little and little.”

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

“Thou shalt make no covenant with them”

Verses 1–5

Seven nations stand in the land, and Moses tells Israel not to make peace with them or with their gods.

Seven nations, greater and mightier

  • Hittites
  • Girgashites
  • Amorites
  • Canaanites
  • Perizzites
  • Hivites
  • Jebusites
  • “greater and mightier than thou”

Moses names them, and counts them: seven nations, and all of them “greater and mightier than thou.” Their fathers had looked at these same people from Kadesh-barnea and said, “we were in our own sight as grasshoppers” (Numbers 13:33; we saw that in Numbers 13–14).

Watch who does the work in these two verses: “the LORD thy God shall bring thee… and hath cast out… shall deliver them before thee.” Israel's part was to go in and obey.

A hard command: why “utterly destroy”?

This is hard to read, and it should be. It was God's own sentence on those nations, carried out by Israel in that land. And God had waited a long time. Long before, he told Abraham his children could not have the land yet:

“But in the fourth generation they shall come hither again: for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full.”

Genesis 15:16

“Not for thy righteousness, or for the uprightness of thine heart, dost thou go to possess their land: but for the wickedness of these nations the LORD thy God doth drive them out from before thee…”

Deuteronomy 9:5

It was judgment for their wickedness (18:12 names it), and God holds his own people to the same measure: “As the nations which the LORD destroyeth before your face, so shall ye perish” (8:20). And where one of them believed, she was spared: “By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not” (Hebrews 11:31). “Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?” (Genesis 18:25).

Who will turn your heart?

The reason is in verse 4, and it is not about blood or race: “they will turn away thy son from following me.” The danger was the gods that would come home with the marriage. It came true in the wisest king Israel ever had:

“For it came to pass, when Solomon was old, that his wives turned away his heart after other gods: and his heart was not perfect with the LORD his God, as was the heart of David his father.”

1 Kings 11:4

“Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness?”

2 Corinthians 6:14

Every man here knows how this works. Nobody went back to the old life alone; somebody was waiting for him there. The people closest to you will either pull you toward the Lord or away from him. “Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners” (1 Corinthians 15:33).

Break it, cut it, burn it

  • altarsthe places where they sacrificed to their gods
  • imagesstanding stones and statues set up for worship
  • groveswooden poles and planted places set up for the worship of false gods
  • graven imagesidols carved from wood or stone

Not one of these was to be kept as a souvenir. Four verbs, and none of them gentle: destroy, break down, cut down, burn. When the gospel came to Ephesus, the new believers did the same with what had held them:

“Many of them also which used curious arts brought their books together, and burned them before all men: and they counted the price of them, and found it fifty thousand pieces of silver.”

Acts 19:19

The stash, the numbers in the phone, the bottle kept “just in case”: a man doesn't put those on a shelf. He breaks them.

Part two

“Because the LORD loved you”

Verses 6–11

Why Israel? Moses answers the question before anyone can boast.

“An holy people… a special people”

Holy means set apart for God, belonging to him. Special here means his own, his “peculiar treasure” (Exodus 19:5). That is the reason for verse 5: a people set apart for God can't keep the altars of other gods.

Peter writes the same words to believers in Christ:

“But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:”

1 Peter 2:9

Christ “gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people” (Titus 2:14). A man who belongs to the Lord is not his own property any more, and not the old life's.

Why did he choose them?

  • not because they were many“ye were the fewest of all people” (verse 7)
  • not because they were good“thou art a stiffnecked people” (9:6)
  • “because the LORD loved you”and because he would keep the oath to their fathers (verse 8)

God chose a nation to be his own, and Moses says plainly that it rested on his love, not on their worth. He does not explain the love any further: he loved them because he loved them. Israel was never allowed to think they had earned it.

That is how God's love has always come. Not to the strong or the clean, but freely: “For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life” (John 3:16).

Loved before he was clean

Israel was loved while they were still slaves, making bricks for Pharaoh. God didn't wait for them to get free first; he got them free. That is redeemed: bought out of bondage.

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

A man who has been in bondage to a bottle or a pipe knows what a house of bondmen is. The love came first; the freedom came out of it. “We love him, because he first loved us” (1 John 4:19).

“Know therefore”: the faithful God

To them that love him

“a thousand generations”

He “keepeth covenant and mercy”. He doesn't break his word, and his mercy outlasts every family line we can count.

To him that hateth him

“to his face”

He “will not be slack”: he is not slow or careless about sin. He repays it to the man himself, “to his face”.

We heard this in the commandments: mercy “unto thousands of them that love me” (5:10; Exodus 20:6). Both columns are true of the same God. That is why verse 11 follows: “Thou shalt therefore keep the commandments.” Keeping them doesn't buy his love (verse 8 already settled that); it is how a loved man answers.

“God is faithful, by whom ye were called unto the fellowship of his Son Jesus Christ our Lord.”

1 Corinthians 1:9

Part three

“Thou shalt be blessed above all people”

Verses 12–16

What the LORD promised Israel in the land, if they would hearken.

A promise to Israel, in the land

Children, crops, wine and oil, calves and lambs, and none of “the evil diseases of Egypt”: these were the covenant blessings for the nation of Israel in the land the LORD “sware unto thy fathers” (verse 13). Chapter 28 will set them out in full, with the curses.

This is not a promise that a believer today who obeys will get money and good health. It was God's covenant with one nation in one land. What God has promised us in Christ is better, and it cannot be lost:

“Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ:”

Ephesians 1:3

And watch what verse 16 sets beside the blessing: “neither shalt thou serve their gods; for that will be a snare unto thee.” A snare is a trap with bait in it. The blessing is God's; the bait is the old gods.

Part four

“Thou shalt not be afraid of them”

Verses 17–26

Moses answers the fear before they have even crossed the river.

The cure for fear is memory

If thou shalt say in thine heart“These nations are more than I; how can I dispossess them?”
→
Then“shalt well remember what the LORD thy God did unto Pharaoh, and unto all Egypt”

Moses doesn't tell them the nations are small. He tells them to remember. Egypt had held them as slaves, and they had watched God bring them out of Pharaoh's hand. Temptations in verse 19 means the trials and tests God brought on Egypt, the plagues their own eyes saw.

That is the question every man asks the morning after he's clean: how can I possibly beat this? Remember what he has already done. The God who got you out will get you through.

The hornet, and a God among you

  • horneta large stinging wasp; God sends it ahead of Israel (Exodus 23:28)
  • affrightedfrightened, terrified
  • terribleto be feared, awesome: a God men tremble before

Years later Joshua told the people how it had gone:

“And I sent the hornet before you, which drave them out from before you, even the two kings of the Amorites; but not with thy sword, nor with thy bow.”

Joshua 24:12

“The LORD thy God is among you.” That is the reason not to be afraid. Not that the enemy is weak, but that God is in the camp.

“By little and little”

held by the nationsby little and littlepossessed

Why not all at once? “Lest the beasts of the field increase upon thee.” An empty land would go wild before Israel could fill it. God had said it at Sinai: “I will not drive them out from before thee in one year… By little and little I will drive them out from before thee, until thou be increased, and inherit the land” (Exodus 23:29–30).

The victory was sure: “there shall no man be able to stand before thee” (verse 24). The pace was God's. He gave them only as much land as they could hold.

Your own little and little

Most men want the whole thing gone on the first day: every craving, every habit, every old way of talking. Usually God doesn't work like that. He takes ground a piece at a time, and he gives a man only as much as he can hold.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

“But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.”

2 Corinthians 3:18

Slow isn't the same as losing. If some of the old enemies are still in the land today, keep walking and keep taking ground. Little and little is still forward.

The gold on the idol

The idols were burned, but the silver and gold on them was worth money. Moses saw the temptation coming: keep the gold, burn the wood. No: “thou shalt not desire the silver or gold that is on them.” The gold was part of the abomination, and whatever came into the house with it would bring the curse with it.

“When I saw among the spoils a goodly Babylonish garment, and two hundred shekels of silver, and a wedge of gold of fifty shekels weight, then I coveted them, and took them; and, behold, they are hid in the earth in the midst of my tent, and the silver under it.”

Joshua 7:21

Achan saw, coveted, took and hid, and it cost him everything. The old life always has some gold on it: the money, the friends, the fun we remember. A man doesn't keep the good parts of an idol. “Thou shalt utterly detest it, and thou shalt utterly abhor it.”

Real world: clear the house

  • SinWhat we let stay in the house, and the people who pull us back to it. Verse 4 says where that road goes.
  • RegretYou may have kept something you should have burned, or a friendship that turned your heart. Break it down today; don't just regret it.
  • GraceHe didn't set his love on you because you were much. “Because the LORD loved you.” You didn't earn it, and you can't out-sin a love you never earned.
  • HopeLittle and little is still forward. “He which hath begun a good work in you will perform it.”
  • TodayGo through your house and your phone and get rid of one thing that belongs to the old gods. Then tell a brother in Christ you did it.

Points to remember

  1. The judgment“for the wickedness of these nations the LORD thy God doth drive them out” 9:5It was God's sentence on their wickedness, after long patience, and a guard against their gods.
  2. The company“For they will turn away thy son from following me” 7:4The danger was not their blood but their gods, coming home with the marriage.
  3. Chosen and loved“But because the LORD loved you” 7:8Not for their number or their goodness, but for his love and his oath.
  4. The faithful God“which keepeth covenant and mercy… to a thousand generations” 7:9He keeps his word and his mercy to them that love him, and he is not slack about sin.
  5. Fear“shalt well remember what the LORD thy God did unto Pharaoh” 7:18The cure for fear is remembering what God has already done.
  6. Little by little“by little and little: thou mayest not consume them at once” 7:22So that the land would not go wild: God gives only as much ground as a man can hold.

Question 1 of 6

The judgment

Why were the seven nations to be utterly destroyed?

What verses tell us about this?

“Not for thy righteousness, or for the uprightness of thine heart, dost thou go to possess their land: but for the wickedness of these nations the LORD thy God doth drive them out from before thee…”

Deuteronomy 9:5

Answer

For their wickedness: God had waited until “the iniquity of the Amorites” was full (Genesis 15:16). It was his judgment, and a guard to keep Israel from their gods.

Question 2 of 6

The company

Why was Israel not to make marriages with them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because they would turn their sons away from following the LORD to serve other gods, as Solomon's wives later turned his heart (1 Kings 11:4).

Question 3 of 6

Chosen and loved

Why did the LORD set his love upon Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not because they were many (they were the fewest) or good, but because the LORD loved them, and to keep the oath he swore to their fathers.

Question 4 of 6

The faithful God

What does the faithful God keep, and for how long?

What verses tell us about this?

“God is faithful, by whom ye were called unto the fellowship of his Son Jesus Christ our Lord.”

1 Corinthians 1:9

Answer

His covenant and his mercy, with them that love him and keep his commandments, to a thousand generations.

Question 5 of 6

Fear

What were they to remember when the nations looked too strong?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

What the LORD did to Pharaoh and to all Egypt: the signs and wonders their own eyes saw. And that the LORD was among them.

Question 6 of 6

Little by little

Why would the LORD put the nations out by little and little?

What verses tell us about this?

“By little and little I will drive them out from before thee, until thou be increased, and inherit the land.”

Exodus 23:30

Answer

So the beasts of the field would not overrun an empty land. God gave them the land as fast as they could fill and hold it.
