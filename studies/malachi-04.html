Malachi 4

The Sun of righteousness

The last chapter of the Old Testament: a day that burns like an oven, a sun that rises with healing, and a promise that Elijah will come first.

Things to watch for

Malachi spoke about 430 BC, to the Jews back home after the exile. After him, heaven went quiet for about four hundred years. Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

The day cometh

Verses 1–3

One day, two very different experiences of it: fire for some, sunrise for others.

One day, two outcomes

This is the “day of the LORD,” the day God finally sets everything right. The people had asked, “Where is the God of judgment?” (2:17). Here is the answer.

The proud and the wicked

Stubble in an oven

Stubble is the dry stalks left in a field after harvest. It burns in seconds. “Neither root nor branch” means nothing left to grow back.

You that fear My name

A sunrise

The same day that burns the proud brings light and healing to those who fear Him.

Verse 3 sounds harsh, but notice who does it: “in the day that I shall do this.” God's people don't take revenge. They walk free on the far side of a judgment God carried out.

“For God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ,”

1 Thessalonians 5:9

Healing in his wings

Picture the sun coming up and its rays spreading across the sky like wings. Everywhere its light reaches, it brings warmth and healing. The New Testament uses this same picture of Jesus:

Luke 1:79In darkness

“them that sit in darkness and in the shadow of death”

Luke 1:78The dayspring

“the dayspring from on high hath visited us”

John 8:12The light of life

“I am the light of the world”

Zacharias sang Luke 1:78–79 at the birth of his son, John the Baptist, about the Lord whom John would go before. Malachi's Sun was rising.

Calves out of the stall

Young calves were kept in a stall to be fed and fattened. When a farmer finally opened the gate in the morning, they'd burst out into the sun, kicking and leaping.

Ye shall go forth.

Many men in this room know what a stall feels like: a cell, a habit, years when you couldn't get out. This is God's picture of what's ahead for those who fear Him: the gate open, the sun up, and room to run.

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Part two

Remember, and behold

Verses 4–6

The last words of the Old Testament: look back to Moses, look ahead to Elijah.

Look back, look ahead

Look back

“Remember … Moses”

Horeb is Mount Sinai, where God gave the law. While you wait, hold on to what God has already said.

Look ahead

“Behold … Elijah”

Elijah was the bold prophet who called Israel back from idols (1 Kings 18). God will send one like him before the great day.

Moses stands for the law. Elijah stands for the prophets. Hundreds of years later, both of them showed up on a mountain, talking with Jesus, whose face shone like the sun:

“And after six days Jesus taketh Peter, James, and John his brother, and bringeth them up into an high mountain apart, And was transfigured before them: and his face did shine as the sun, and his raiment was white as the light. And, behold, there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him.”

Matthew 17:1–3

Who is “Elijah”?

In the New Testament, Elijah's name is spelled “Elias,” from the Greek.

Was John the Baptist Elijah come back from heaven?NoWhen asked, “Art thou Elias?” John said, “I am not” (John 1:21).
Did John fulfill this promise?YesHe came “in the spirit and power of Elias” (Luke 1:17). Jesus said, “this is Elias, which was for to come” (Matthew 11:14).

“And Jesus answered and said unto them, Elias truly shall first come, and restore all things. But I say unto you, That Elias is come already, and they knew him not, but have done unto him whatsoever they listed. Likewise shall also the Son of man suffer of them. Then the disciples understood that he spake unto them of John the Baptist.”

Matthew 17:11–13

John was a rough man from the wilderness, like Elijah, calling a nation to repent. Because Jesus also says Elias “shall first come,” many Bible teachers believe there is more of this promise still to be fulfilled before Jesus returns.

Fathers and children

Of everything Elijah's coming could fix, God names this one: broken families. The angel who told Zacharias about John quoted these very words:

“And he shall go before him in the spirit and power of Elias, to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children, and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just; to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.”

Luke 1:17

When a man truly turns to God, it shows up at home. Some of us have children who don't know us, or don't want to. Some of us still carry wounds from our own fathers. Repentance starts healing both directions.

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

It is never too late to start turning your heart toward your kids. You can't control how they answer. But you can pray, keep your word, and keep the door open.

The last word

In the King James Bible, the very last word of the Old Testament is “curse.” The law showed what sin deserves, and it couldn't take that away. The Old Testament ends waiting for Someone who could.

The last word of the Old Testament

“a curse”

Malachi 4:6

The last chapter of the New Testament

“no more curse”

Revelation 22:3

What happened in between? Jesus took the curse on Himself.

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

Four hundred quiet years

After Malachi, no prophet spoke for God for about four hundred years. Empires rose and fell over the little land of Judah, and God's people waited.

  1. about 430 BCMalachi
  2. 330sAlexander and the Greeks
  3. 160sthe Maccabees revolt
  4. 63 BCRome takes Jerusalem
  5. about 5 BCJesus born
  6. about AD 28John the Baptist

Silence isn't absence. God was getting the world ready: one common language, Roman roads, and a people longing for the Messiah. Then, right on time:

“But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law,”

Galatians 4:4

The silence breaks

When God finally spoke again, He picked up right where Malachi stopped. Turn the page from Malachi 4 to the Gospels:

For this is he that was spoken of by the prophet Esaias, saying, The voice of one crying in the wilderness, Prepare ye the way of the Lord, make his paths straight.”

Matthew 3:1–3

And when the messenger saw the Lord coming, this is what he said:

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

Points to remember

  1. The day“The day cometh, that shall burn as an oven” 4:1God will set everything right. The proud will burn like stubble.
  2. Healing“The Sun of righteousness arise with healing in his wings” 4:2For those who fear Him, the same day is a sunrise: light, healing, and freedom.
  3. Remember“Remember ye the law of Moses my servant” 4:4While we wait, we hold on to what God has already said.
  4. Elijah“I will send you Elijah the prophet” 4:5John the Baptist came in the spirit and power of Elijah, to prepare the way for Jesus.
  5. Fathers and children“He shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children” 4:6When a man turns to God, it shows up at home. It's never too late to start.
  6. The curse“Lest I come and smite the earth with a curse” 4:6The Old Testament ends with a curse. Jesus took it on Himself, so there will be “no more curse.”

Question 1 of 6

The day

What is coming on the day of the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

“For God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ,”

1 Thessalonians 5:9

Answer

Judgment. God will finally set everything right, and the proud and wicked will burn like dry stubble. It's real, and it's serious. But nobody has to face it unprepared. God has not appointed those who trust in Jesus to wrath, but to salvation.

Question 2 of 6

Healing

What does God promise to those who fear His name?

What verses tell us about this?

“Through the tender mercy of our God; whereby the dayspring from on high hath visited us, To give light to them that sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the way of peace.”

Luke 1:78–79

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Answer

A sunrise. The day that burns the proud brings light and healing to those who fear Him, and they go out free like calves let out of the stall. That light came into the world in Jesus, and whoever follows Him won't walk in darkness.

Question 3 of 6

Remember

What do we hold on to while we wait for God to act?

What verses tell us about this?

“God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, Hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds;”

Hebrews 1:1–2

“But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law,”

Galatians 4:4

Answer

What God has already said. Israel waited four hundred years with the law of Moses in their hands, and God came right on time. We have even more: God has spoken to us by His Son. When heaven seems quiet, open the Book.

Question 4 of 6

Elijah

Who is the Elijah that God promised to send?

What verses tell us about this?

“And if ye will receive it, this is Elias, which was for to come.”

Matthew 11:14

“And they asked him, What then? Art thou Elias? And he saith, I am not. Art thou that prophet? And he answered, No.”

John 1:21

Answer

John the Baptist. He wasn't Elijah come back from heaven, but he came “in the spirit and power of Elias,” and Jesus said, “this is Elias, which was for to come.” His job was to get people ready for Jesus.

Question 5 of 6

Fathers and children

What does it mean to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he shall go before him in the spirit and power of Elias, to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children, and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just; to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.”

Luke 1:17

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

Answer

It means families mended by repentance. When a man turns to God, his heart turns toward the people he's hurt or neglected, starting with his kids. We can't make anyone come back. But we can pray, keep our word, and keep the door open. It's never too late to start.

Question 6 of 6

The curse

How is the curse taken away?

What verses tell us about this?

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

“And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him:”

Revelation 22:3

Answer

By Jesus taking it on Himself. The Old Testament ends with the word “curse,” because the law could show sin but couldn't remove it. On the cross, Christ was made a curse for us. Everyone who believes in Him is redeemed, and one day there will be “no more curse” at all.
