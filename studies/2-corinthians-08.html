2 Corinthians 8

The grace of giving

A church with almost nothing begs for the chance to give. Paul shows us why: Jesus gave Himself first.

Where we are in 2 Corinthians

About AD 56, Paul writes from Macedonia. The hard letter has worked, and Corinth has turned around. Now he turns to a job they had started a year before and left half done: a gift for the poor believers in Jerusalem.

1–2Comfort in trouble
3–4The new covenant
5Reconciled to God
6Open hearts
7Titus brings news
8–9The collection
10–13Paul answers his critics

Two chapters on money, but not a fundraising letter. Paul never says “give or else.” He talks about grace, joy, and Jesus. Read alone, this chapter still makes sense: here is a church giving more than it can afford, and here is why.

Part one

Out of deep poverty

Verses 1–7

The poorest churches begged for the chance to give.

A gift for Jerusalem

Many believers in Jerusalem were poor, and Paul asked the Gentile churches to send help: “it hath pleased them of Macedonia and Achaia to make a certain contribution for the poor saints which are at Jerusalem” (Romans 15:26).

Philippi and Thessalonica are in Macedonia, in the north. Corinth is Achaia, in the south. It's a gift of thanks: the gospel came to the Gentiles through Jewish believers.

What a poor church was made of

A great trialafflictionThese churches were being pressed hard for believing (Acts 16–17).

Notice the arithmetic doesn't work. Poverty plus trouble should add up to “we can't.” Paul says grace is the thing that changes the sum. They gave because of what God had done for them, not because they had cash to spare.

That's good news if you're broke. Nobody has to wait until he's comfortable to become a giver. The poorest church in the Bible was the most generous.

“First gave their own selves”

Paul had hoped for a donation. He got more: “not as we hoped, but first gave their own selves to the Lord.” The money was the smallest circle. It followed the person, and the person followed the Lord.

If you're out of money, this verse is for you. You can still do the first part: give yourself. An hour. A ride. A hand with a move. Listening to a man who's struggling. Next to the Macedonians, that is real giving, and nobody is too poor for it.

“In this grace also”

Corinth was gifted: faith, utterance, knowledge, diligence, love (v. 7). They were the church that loved to talk and to know things. Paul adds one more to the list.

Giving isn't an extra for the rich. Paul counts it as one of the marks of a healthy believer, right next to faith and knowledge. And he calls it grace: a thing God gives you to do, not a bill He sends you.

Verse 8 says it plainly: “I speak not by commandment.” Paul won't squeeze. He wants it to come from love, and love can't be forced.

Part two

The grace of our Lord Jesus

Verses 8–15

Why a man gives: because of what Jesus did for him.

He who had everything, down to nothing

  1. rich“the glory which I had with thee before the world was” (John 17:5)
  2. a manger“no room for them in the inn” (Luke 2:7)
  3. no home“hath not where to lay his head” (Matthew 8:20)
  4. the cross“obedient unto death, even the death of the cross” (Philippians 2:8)

That's the “grace of our Lord Jesus Christ.” He didn't give out of leftovers. He gave up heaven's side of the table and went down every step, to a cross for sins He never committed.

The trade

Christholds

Youhold

Heaven's richesglory, life, the Father's side
Our povertysin, guilt, a debt we couldn't pay

He took our poverty on Himself. And what we get is His riches: forgiven, a child of God, the Spirit in you, a home that's coming.

What kind of riches? Not money. Paul writes this to a church whose very next job is to give money away, and the Macedonians stayed poor. This isn't a promise that Jesus will make you wealthy. It's the best deal ever made, and it's not in dollars. A man with nothing in his pocket can be fully rich in Christ.

What a gift is worth

“For all they did cast in of their abundance; but she of her want did cast in all that she had, even all her living.”

Mark 12:44

That's verse 12: “it is accepted according to that a man hath, and not according to that he hath not.” God never asks you for what you don't have. He weighs the heart. A willing mind counts, and so does a small gift from a poor man.

Verse 11 adds a hard push. A year ago they wanted to give, and nothing happened. Good intentions that never get done are a thing every man knows. Paul says: finish it, with what you have.

What you have to give

“According to that a man hath.” Most of us don't have money. Here's what many men in this room do have.

To a meeting, a job interview, church. A ride has saved people.

Prayer

Pray for one man by name today. Then tell him you did.

“I have shewed you all things, how that so labouring ye ought to support the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he said, It is more blessed to give than to receive.”

Acts 20:35

Equality: a level scale

“He that had gathered much had nothing over; and he that had gathered little had no lack.”

2 Corinthians 8:15

Paul is quoting the manna in the wilderness (Exodus 16): everybody gathered what he needed. The point of the gift isn't to take from you to make you poor. It's that “at this time” your plenty meets their need, and one day it may go the other way.

And it did go both ways. Jerusalem gave the Gentiles the gospel. Now Gentiles gave back the bread. Nobody in the family is on the take.

Part three

Honest hands

Verses 16–24

Who carries the money, and why it's done in the open.

Three men, and nothing hidden

Titus

“earnest care”

Paul's partner. He went of his own accord, not because he was told.

The brother

chosen by the churches

Well known in all the churches. The churches picked him; Paul didn't.

Our brother

proved diligent

Tested “oftentimes” in many things, and found faithful.

Paul could have carried the money himself. He didn't, because someone might say he skimmed it (v. 20). So he put three men on it, picked by others, so everyone could see it was honest. If you have a reputation, even a good one, nobody should have to just take your word.

For a man in recovery that's gold. If you handle money, a bank card, or a key, let somebody see it. Not because you're a crook. Because nothing hidden is safe. Hiding is where relapse starts.

Points to remember

  1. Deep poverty“Their deep poverty abounded unto the riches of their liberality” 8:2Grace and joy, not money, made them generous. You don't have to wait until you're comfortable.
  2. Themselves“First gave their own selves to the Lord” 8:5The person comes first and the gift follows. You can give time and help even with an empty wallet.
  3. Grace“Though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich” 8:9Jesus gave up everything to give you a Father and forgiveness. That's the riches, and not a promise of money.
  4. A willing mind“If there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath” 8:12God never asks for what you don't have. Finish what you start, with what's in your hand.
  5. Sharing“He that had gathered much had nothing over; and he that had gathered little had no lack” 8:15Family shares. Today your plenty meets someone's need, and one day it'll be the other way round.
  6. Honesty“Providing for honest things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men” 8:21Handle money in the open, with others watching. Nothing hidden is safe.

Question 1 of 6

Deep poverty

How could people in deep poverty give so generously?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By the grace of God. Trouble and poverty didn't stop them, because their joy was deeper than their need. They didn't have to be asked twice: they begged for the chance to give. A poor man can be generous too.

Question 2 of 6

Themselves

What did the Macedonians give before they gave any money?

What verses tell us about this?

“I have shewed you all things, how that so labouring ye ought to support the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he said, It is more blessed to give than to receive.”

Acts 20:35

Answer

Themselves. They gave their own selves to the Lord first, and then the gift followed. If you have no money, you can still give yourself today: your time, your hands, your help.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

What did Jesus give up for us, and what do we get?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus: Who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men: And being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.”

Philippians 2:5–8

“He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?”

Romans 8:32

Answer

He gave up heaven's glory, a home, and His life. In exchange, we get forgiveness, a Father, and everything that comes with being His. That is the riches of verse 9: not money, but Him.

Question 4 of 6

A willing mind

What counts with God when you have little to give?

What verses tell us about this?

“For all they did cast in of their abundance; but she of her want did cast in all that she had, even all her living.”

Mark 12:44

Answer

A willing mind. God accepts a gift by what a man has, not by what he doesn't. A widow's two mites counted for more than a rich man's pile. Don't wait for plenty: do the doing of it, with what you have.

Question 5 of 6

Sharing

What does Paul mean by “equality” in giving?

What verses tell us about this?

“For if the Gentiles have been made partakers of their spiritual things, their duty is also to minister unto them in carnal things.”

Romans 15:27

Answer

Not that everyone ends up with the same pile. It's that nobody in the family goes without: this time your plenty meets their need, and next time it may be the other way round. The Gentiles owed the Jewish church for the gospel, and this was a way to give some of it back.

Question 6 of 6

Honesty

How did Paul keep the money honest?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He didn't carry it alone. The churches chose men to travel with it, so everything was in the open and nobody could say he took a cut. Whatever you handle, let someone see it. That's how a man stays clean.
