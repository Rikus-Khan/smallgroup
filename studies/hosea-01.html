Hosea 1

A marriage that preached

God tells a prophet to marry a woman who will break his heart, so a whole nation can see what its sin has done to God, and how far His mercy reaches.

The book of Hosea

Who: Hosea, son of Beeri, a prophet of the northern kingdom. His name means salvation, from the same Hebrew root as Joshua and Jesus.

When: about 755 to 715 BC, before the exile. He saw Assyria swallow up the north in 722, and kept preaching after.

To whom: Israel, the ten northern tribes, also called Ephraim or Samaria. Rich on the outside, rotten inside: Baal worship, crime, broken homes.

God sent Hosea to say: you have been unfaithful to Me, and it will cost you. And then, over and over: come home. I still love you.

Notice Hosea names four kings of Judah, but only one king of Israel. After Jeroboam died, the north had six kings in about thirty years. Four of them were murdered by the next man (2 Kings 15).

When Hosea started, times were good: money, peace, full barns. Nobody felt like they needed a warning. That's often when we need it most.

A living sermon

“Whoredom” is the old word for selling yourself, or for sexual unfaithfulness. Bible teachers differ on whether Gomer was already living that way when Hosea married her, or fell into it later. Either way, God told Hosea ahead of time how it would go, and he married her anyway.

Part two

Three names

Verses 4–9

Every time Hosea called his children in for supper, he preached a sermon.

Jezreel

יִזְרְעֶאל said Yizre‘el “God sows,” or “God scatters”

Jezreel was a rich valley and a town in the north. About ninety years before, a soldier named Jehu had seized the throne there. God had sent him to end wicked King Ahab's family, but Jehu went at it with a butcher's joy, and then kept the golden calves Israel worshipped (2 Kings 9–10).

God gave Jehu's family four generations on the throne. Here is how that went:

Jehuhis sonJehoahazhis sonJoashhis sonJeroboam IIhis sonZachariah, killed after six months

“This was the word of the LORD which he spake unto Jehu, saying, Thy sons shall sit on the throne of Israel unto the fourth generation. And so it came to pass.”

2 Kings 15:12

Lo-ruhamah

לֹא רֻחָמָה said Lo-ruchamah “no mercy,” “not pitied”

The Hebrew word for this kind of mercy is close kin to the word for a mother's womb. It's the tender love a mother has for her baby. Imagine naming your little girl Unloved.

This wasn't God losing His temper. The north had been turning away for nearly two hundred years, ever since it split from Judah. God sent prophet after prophet: Elijah, Elisha, Jonah, Amos, now Hosea. Judgment came late, and only after long patience.

Not by bow, nor by sword

  • not by bownot by their own weapons
  • nor by sword, nor by battlenot by fighting harder
  • by horses, nor by horsemennot by the best army money could buy
  • by the LORD their Godsalvation comes from Him

It came true about fifty years later. Assyria had Jerusalem surrounded, and Judah had no army that could stop it. Then God stepped in:

“And it came to pass that night, that the angel of the LORD went out, and smote in the camp of the Assyrians an hundred fourscore and five thousand: and when they arose early in the morning, behold, they were all dead corpses.”

2 Kings 19:35

A man can't fight his way out of sin with the bow and sword of his own willpower. The LORD saves. Our part is to trust Him.

Lo-ammi

  1. Jezreel“God scatters”: judgment is coming
  2. Lo-ruhamah“no mercy”: the pity has run out
  3. Lo-ammi“not my people”: the bond is broken

Notice verse 3: Gomer “bare him a son.” With the next two children, the word “him” is missing. Many think Hosea was left wondering whether they were his.

Part three

Yet

Verses 10–11

Judgment is not God's last word.

From “not my people” to sons

Lo-ammi, “not my people” → sons of the living God

In the very place where God said, “Ye are not my people,” He will say, “Ye are the sons of the living God.” The same mouth that gave the sentence gives the pardon.

Paul quotes this verse to show that God calls people to Himself from among the Jews, and from among the Gentiles too:

“As he saith also in Osee, I will call them my people, which were not my people; and her beloved, which was not beloved. And it shall come to pass, that in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people; there shall they be called the children of the living God.”

Romans 9:25–26

One people again

For nearly two hundred years, God's people had been split in two, north and south, often at war with each other. God promises to bring them back together under one head.

IsraelJudah“gathered together, and appoint themselves one head”

And “Jezreel” turns around. The name that meant God scatters now means God sows: He plants His people back in the ground to grow.

That promise belongs to Israel, and God will keep it. What it shows us is who God is: He doesn't only tear down. He rebuilds. If He can do that for a nation that ran from Him for centuries, He can rebuild a man.

Points to remember

  1. God's heart“Departing from the LORD” 1:2Our sin isn't just a broken rule. It's a broken marriage, and it grieves Him.
  2. Sin“The land hath committed great whoredom” 1:2Chasing other gods is unfaithfulness to the One who loves us.
  3. God's word“And so it came to pass” 2 Kings 15:12What God says, He does, both His warnings and His promises.
  4. Salvation“Will save them by the LORD their God” 1:7Not by bow or sword. The LORD saves.
  5. Mercy“Ye are the sons of the living God” 1:10Those who were “not my people” can be called His sons.
  6. Hope“Great shall be the day of Jezreel” 1:11After the scattering, God sows again.

Question 1 of 6

God's heart

How does God feel when His people turn away from Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“How shall I give thee up, Ephraim? how shall I deliver thee, Israel? how shall I make thee as Admah? how shall I set thee as Zeboim? mine heart is turned within me, my repentings are kindled together.”

Hosea 11:8

Answer

Like a husband whose wife has walked out. God doesn't shrug off our sin. It grieves Him, and He takes it personally. Later in this same book He cries out, “How shall I give thee up?” His judgment is real, but His heart still aches for His people to come home.

Question 2 of 6

Sin

Why does God compare idolatry to adultery?

What verses tell us about this?

“Ye adulterers and adulteresses, know ye not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God? whosoever therefore will be a friend of the world is the enemy of God.”

James 4:4

Answer

Because God's bond with His people is like a marriage. He gave Himself to them and asked for their whole heart. When they gave that heart to Baal, it was betrayal. Anything we run to in place of God, a bottle, a pill, a person, money, does the same thing: it takes the love that belongs to Him.

Question 3 of 6

God's word

Does God do what He says He will do?

What verses tell us about this?

“This was the word of the LORD which he spake unto Jehu, saying, Thy sons shall sit on the throne of Israel unto the fourth generation. And so it came to pass.”

2 Kings 15:12

“God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: hath he said, and shall he not do it? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good?”

Numbers 23:19

Answer

Yes, every time. He told Jehu four generations, and it was four. He said Assyria would take the north, and it did. He said He would save Judah without a sword, and He did. That cuts both ways: His warnings are real, and so are His promises. The God who keeps His word about judgment will keep His word about mercy.

Question 4 of 6

Salvation

Who does the saving: us, or the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

“Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.”

Psalm 20:7

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

The LORD. Judah couldn't fight off Assyria, and we can't fight off sin and death by our own strength. God saves, and we receive it by faith. That doesn't make us passive. It means we stop trusting our own bow and sword and start trusting Him.

Question 5 of 6

Mercy

Can people who were not God's people become His sons?

What verses tell us about this?

“As he saith also in Osee, I will call them my people, which were not my people; and her beloved, which was not beloved. And it shall come to pass, that in the place where it was said unto them, Ye are not my people; there shall they be called the children of the living God.”

Romans 9:25–26

“Which in time past were not a people, but are now the people of God: which had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy.”

1 Peter 2:10

Answer

Yes. God promised it to Israel, and Paul and Peter show He opens the same door to anyone, Jew or Gentile, who believes. Whatever you were called before, “not my people,” “no mercy,” God can give you a new name: a son of the living God.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

What hope does God hold out after judgment?

What verses tell us about this?

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

Answer

That He will gather, plant, and rebuild. The same chapter that names judgment ends with “Yet” and a promise. God keeps that promise for Israel. For us, it shows His heart: when we come back to Him, a scattered life can be planted again.
