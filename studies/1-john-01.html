1 John 1

Walking in the Light

An old man who once touched Jesus with his own hands writes to his “little children.” God is light. Nobody is fooled by pretending. And for every man who fell yesterday there is one promise that never fails: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive.”

The first letter of John

No name, no greeting. It opens with a witness: “That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes … and our hands have handled, of the Word of life”

Written by
the apostle John, by long tradition: the fisherman, “the disciple whom Jesus loved.” Now an old man, he calls his readers “my little children.”
From, when
Ephesus, about AD 85 to 95, near the end of his life.
To whom
believers he knew and loved, most likely the churches of Asia around Ephesus.
Why
Some men had walked out of the church teaching lies about Jesus (2:19, 26), and the believers were shaken. John writes so they can be sure: “that ye may know that ye have eternal life” (5:13).

“These things have I written unto you that believe on the name of the Son of God; that ye may know that ye have eternal life, and that ye may believe on the name of the Son of God.”

1 John 5:13

By now Peter and Paul were gone. John may have been the last man alive who had walked with Jesus. He had heard every new idea come and go for sixty years, and he writes to people he loves with one aim: not to scare them, but to let them know.

From thunder to love

Boanerges → “my little children” Jesus nicknamed John and his brother the sons of thunder (Mark 3:17). Sixty years later the same man writes like a grandfather.

“And when his disciples James and John saw this, they said, Lord, wilt thou that we command fire to come down from heaven, and consume them, even as Elias did?”

Luke 9:54

Young John wanted to burn a village down. Old John's favorite word is love. If you've got a temper, a record, a reputation, look at what Jesus did with this man. Give Him sixty years, or six months.

Part one

What we have seen

Verses 1–4

John doesn't open with his name. He opens with his ears, his eyes and his hands.

Closer and closer

Each word gets nearer. Heard Him teach. Seen Him with their own eyes. Looked upon: studied Him, for three years. Handled: touched Him with their hands.

“Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.”

Luke 24:39

Some teachers were already saying Jesus only seemed to have a body. John says: I leaned on Him at supper. Our faith isn't a feeling or a theory. It rests on a real Man who really died and really rose.

What is “fellowship”?

κοινωνία said koinōnia sharing life in common, like partners in one business

“And so was also James, and John, the sons of Zebedee, which were partners with Simon.”

Luke 5:10

John knew this word from the fishing boats: partners who share one boat, one net, one catch. Fellowship isn't coffee after church. It's being in on the same life: with the Father, with His Son, and with each other. And John says that's where full joy is found.

Part two

God is light

Verses 5–7

The message in one line, and what it means for a man with something to hide.

Turn on the light. It shows the mess, and that's why men run from it: “every one that doeth evil hateth the light” (John 3:20). But you can't clean a room in the dark.

And the same light shows the door. God isn't partly good and partly shady, kind on Sunday and moody on Monday. No darkness at all. You can trust every word He says to you.

Walking in the light isn't being perfect

Verse 6

Say one thing, live another

Say we're close to God, but walk in the dark: hiding, covering, living a second life. John's word for that is plain: we lie.

Verse 7

Live in the open

Nothing hidden from God. Honest with Him, honest with a brother. And look what comes with it: the blood cleanseth.

If walking in the light meant never sinning, nobody in the light would need cleansing. But verse 7 says the blood is cleansing the very people who walk there. Cleanseth means keeps on cleansing, like a river running over a stone. Not one bath, a steady stream.

Fellowship one with another: the light includes other men. Have one brother who knows the real you. “Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed” (James 5:16).

Not some sin. All sin.

Why blood? Because sin costs a life. “Without shedding of blood is no remission” (Hebrews 9:22). Jesus paid with His own.

“Unto him that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood,”

Revelation 1:5

Make your own list: the worst night, the thing you've never told anyone, the people you hurt. Then read verse 7 over the top of it. All covers your list. There is no sin too dark for the blood of God's own Son.

Part three

If we confess

Verses 8–10

Two ways to lie about sin, and the one verse every believer needs on the morning after.

The man who says, “I don't have a problem”

Every man in recovery knows this one. As long as he says he doesn't have a problem, nobody can help him. Not because God won't, but because he won't come.

Verse 8

“No sin”

Denies the pull: the old nature still in us. Saved men still feel it. Pretending you don't is how you get blindsided.

Verse 10

“Not sinned”

Denies the acts: I haven't really done wrong. That calls God a liar, because He says, “all have sinned” (Romans 3:23).

John isn't scolding. He's clearing the ground. You can't be forgiven for what you won't admit. The way out always starts with telling the truth.

  1. 1If we confess our sinshomologeō: to say the same thing. Call it what God calls it. No excuses, no softer name.
  2. 2he is faithful and justFaithful: He promised, and He keeps His word. Just: Jesus already paid. God won't collect the same debt twice.
  3. 3to forgive us our sinsThe word means to send away. The debt is gone, and He doesn't bring it back up.
  4. 4and to cleanse us from all unrighteousnessNot just forgiven, washed. Even the stains you didn't know to name.

What confession is, and what it isn't

  • not paying for itJesus already paid in full (2:2)
  • not getting saved againa son who does wrong is still a son
  • not going through a middlemanyou go straight to the Father, by Jesus
  • but agreeing with God, and coming homethe fellowship is restored

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

John is writing to believers: “my little children.” A saved man's sin doesn't cancel his salvation; it clouds his fellowship. Confession clears the sky. And God isn't reluctant: He is faithful. He has never once turned down an honest confession.

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

Regret says, “You'll never be clean.” God says, “I already cleansed you.” Don't keep confessing the same forgiven sin every morning as if He didn't hear you the first time. Thank Him, and walk.

Every lie about sin is a dead end. Every honest answer leads to Jesus. God doesn't want you to pretend. He wants you to come.

God is light. Walk in it.

Light shows the mess, and it shows the door. The blood keeps cleansing. And when you fall, 1:9 is waiting, every single time. The next chapter opens with what happens next: “if any man sin, we have an advocate.”

Something to do today: if there's something you've been keeping in the dark, say it to God by name before you leave for work. Then tell one brother you trust. Walk out the door clean.

Points to remember

  1. Eyewitnesses“which we have seen with our eyes … and our hands have handled” 1:1John heard, saw, studied and touched Jesus. Our faith rests on a real Man.
  2. Fellowship“that ye also may have fellowship with us” 1:3He wrote so we'd share life with the Father, the Son, and each other, and have full joy.
  3. God is light“God is light, and in him is no darkness at all” 1:5God is completely good and true. His light shows the mess and the way out.
  4. The blood“the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin” 1:7All sin, and it keeps on cleansing those who walk in the light.
  5. Honest about sin“If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves” 1:8Pretending only fools yourself. The way out starts with the truth.
  6. Confession“he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” 1:9Agree with God, and He forgives and cleanses, every time, because Jesus paid.

Question 1 of 6

Eyewitnesses

How did John know that Jesus was real?

What verses tell us about this?

“Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have.”

Luke 24:39

Answer

He was there. He heard Jesus, saw Him with his own eyes, watched Him for years, and touched Him with his hands, even after He rose from the dead.

Question 2 of 6

Fellowship

Why did John write down what he had seen and heard?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So that we could share in the same life he had: fellowship with the Father, with His Son Jesus Christ, and with other believers. And so that our joy would be full.

Question 3 of 6

God is light

What is the message John heard from Jesus about God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Answer

God is light, with no darkness at all. He is completely good and true, so we can trust Him fully, and we can't fake our way into His company while we live in the dark.

Question 4 of 6

The blood

What does the blood of Jesus Christ cleanse us from?

What verses tell us about this?

“In whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace;”

Ephesians 1:7

Answer

From all sin. Not some, not the small ones: all of it. And it keeps on cleansing the man who walks in the light with God.

Question 5 of 6

Honest about sin

What are we doing if we say we have no sin?

What verses tell us about this?

“For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;”

Romans 3:23

Answer

Fooling ourselves, and even calling God a liar. Everyone has sinned, and the pull is still in us. Admitting it is the first step to being cleansed.

Question 6 of 6

Confession

If we confess our sins, what will God do?

What verses tell us about this?

“I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. Selah.”

Psalm 32:5

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

Answer

He will forgive our sins and cleanse us from all unrighteousness. He is faithful, because He promised, and just, because Jesus already paid. He does it every time.
