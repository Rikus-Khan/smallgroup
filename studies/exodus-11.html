Exodus 11

One plague more

The LORD says there is one plague left, and then Pharaoh will thrust them out. Moses is told what will happen “about midnight,” and that God will “put a difference” between Egypt and Israel.

Part one

“He shall surely thrust you out”

Verses 1–3

The LORD tells Moses how this ends, before the last plague has started.

From “see my face no more” to “thrust you out”

  • thrust outpushed out, driven away: they will be the ones begging
  • altogethercompletely, all of them at once
  • borrowthe word means ask for: they asked, and were given
  • jewelsarticles of silver and gold: cups, ornaments, valuables

Pharaoh had said the next time Moses showed his face, he would die. God says: after the last plague, Pharaoh will not just let you go. He will drive you out himself. The man who bargained for every hoof will be begging them to leave.

The wages, centuries late

Why ask the neighbours for silver and gold? Because God had promised Abraham, more than four hundred years earlier, how this would end:

“And also that nation, whom they shall serve, will I judge: and afterward shall they come out with great substance.”

Genesis 15:14

The people had worked as slaves for generations, with nothing. Now “the LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians,” and the same neighbours who watched them work put gold in their hands. God keeps accounts, and He keeps promises, even long ones.

Verse 3 adds: “the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt.” This is the man who ran from Egypt at forty with a killing behind him. God took him back, and made him great in the very place that had been his shame. A past does not decide where God can use you.

Part two

“About midnight”

Verses 4–10

The hardest words in the book so far, and the line God draws between two houses.

Under each plague, what the text says about Pharaoh's heart: Pharaoh hardened it himself “was hardened” (no one named) the LORD hardened it

The firstborn is the first son born in a family, the one who carried the family's place. The LORD himself says: “About midnight will I go out into the midst of Egypt.” This one is not carried by the wind, or by Moses' rod. God says He will go.

Notice nobody is skipped, and nobody is left out: from the firstborn of Pharaoh on his throne to the firstborn of the servant girl “behind the mill” (the hand mill, where she ground the grain). A king's house and the lowest house get the same night.

Is this fair? What the text says

This is a hard chapter, and we should not hurry past it. Three things the text itself says:

  1. 1I will slay thy son, even thy firstborn.God gave Moses this warning on the way back, before he ever reached Egypt.4:23
  2. 2Every son that is born ye shall cast into the riverIt was Pharaoh who first made the sons of Israel his target. The nation was answering for what it had done.1:22
  3. 3Have I any pleasure at all that the wicked should die?God says He takes no delight in it. He gave nine plagues, and many chances.Ezekiel 18:23

Justice is real, and God is patient before it falls. He does not wave sin away, and He does not rush to punish. The question the next chapter answers is how any house, Egyptian or Israelite, is spared.

“A difference”

The houses of Egypt

A great cry

“A great cry throughout all the land of Egypt, such as there was none like it, nor shall be like it any more.”

The houses of Israel

Not a dog will bark

“Against any of the children of Israel shall not a dog move his tongue.” The animals themselves stay quiet. The night passes by them.

What is the difference? It is not that Israel was better. Joshua says later that Israel too had served other gods in Egypt (Joshua 24:14). The LORD says He is the one who “doth put a difference.” The difference is something He does, and in the next chapter it is a lamb and blood on a door.

“In a great anger”

Verse 8 ends with Moses leaving Pharaoh “in a great anger.” Many readers take verses 4 to 8 as the speech Moses made as he walked out, after Pharaoh had said “see my face no more.” Pharaoh's own servants, Moses says, will come down and bow to him, and that happens in 12:31–33.

Moses was angry, and nobody blames him. He had waited a long time, been refused nine times, and been threatened. But note who this is: a man who once killed in anger and ran for forty years. He had to learn to carry his anger without being carried by it. He is learning, a step at a time, and his temper will cost him again (Numbers 20).

If anger is one of your things, the same God who used a man with a temper has not given up on you. Be angry about the right things. Put it in His hands, and call a brother in Christ before you act on it.

“That my wonders may be multiplied”

  • hearkenlisten, and obey
  • wonderssigns and acts of God that no one can explain away
  • multipliedmade many: one wonder after another

God told Moses back in chapter 7 that Pharaoh would not listen. Now it has come true nine times. Remember how it went: Pharaoh hardened his own heart again and again, and then the LORD confirmed him in it (chapter 10). The wonders were not wasted. Israel and the nations were meant to see who the LORD is.

There is a sad line in the middle of this: Moses and Aaron “did all these wonders before Pharaoh.” A man can see everything and still say no. Seeing is not the same as turning. Don't wait for one more sign from God before you do the thing He has already told you to do.

Before the last night

“Have I any pleasure at all that the wicked should die? saith the Lord GOD: and not that he should return from his ways, and live?”

Ezekiel 18:23

Everything in this chapter says: judgment is coming, and it is real. The same God says He wants the man to turn and live. Both are true at once.

Points to remember

  1. One more“he shall surely thrust you out hence altogether” 11:1God knows how it ends. The man who bargained for every hoof will beg them to go.
  2. Favour“the LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians” 11:3God keeps His promise of wages, even centuries late, and Moses is great where he once fled.
  3. Midnight“About midnight will I go out into the midst of Egypt” 11:4Judgment is real, and no house is skipped. God warned of it from the start (4:23), and waited through nine plagues.
  4. A difference“the LORD doth put a difference between the Egyptians and Israel” 11:7The difference is not that Israel was better. It is what God provides, and next it is blood on a door.
  5. A hard heart“Pharaoh shall not hearken unto you” 11:9A man can see nine wonders and still say no. Don't wait for one more sign before you obey.

Question 1 of 5

One more

What did the LORD say would happen after the last plague?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Pharaoh will let them go, and will drive them out himself. The man who bargained for every hoof will beg them to leave. God knew it before the last plague began. Your story is in the same good hands.

Question 2 of 5

Favour

How did the Egyptians now treat Israel and Moses?

What verses tell us about this?

“And also that nation, whom they shall serve, will I judge: and afterward shall they come out with great substance.”

Genesis 15:14

Answer

With favour: they gave gold and silver when asked, and Moses was very great in their eyes. It is the promise to Abraham, kept. God can give a man favour in the very place he once ran from.

Question 3 of 5

Midnight

What was coming at midnight, and who would it reach?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I say unto thee, Let my son go, that he may serve me: and if thou refuse to let him go, behold, I will slay thy son, even thy firstborn.”

Exodus 4:23

Answer

Every firstborn in Egypt, from the king's house to the servant at the mill. It was warned of from the start, and came only after nine plagues. God takes no delight in death, and next chapter shows how a house was spared.

Question 4 of 5

A difference

How would the LORD show the difference between Israel and Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A great cry in Egypt, and not so much as a dog barking against Israel. The difference was not that Israel was better. It was what God provided, and next chapter we see it: a lamb, and blood on the door.

Question 5 of 5

A hard heart

Why did Pharaoh still not listen, after all these wonders?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He had hardened his own heart again and again, and the LORD confirmed him in it. A man can see nine wonders and still say no. Don't wait for one more sign: do what God has already shown you.
