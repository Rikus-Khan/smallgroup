The writer of Hebrews wrote, about AD 64 to 68, to Jewish believers under pressure to go back to the old ways. For ten chapters he has shown them that Jesus is better than everything they would be going back to. Chapter 10 ended with a line from Habakkuk: “the just shall live by faith.” Then this:

“But we are not of them who draw back unto perdition; but of them that believe to the saving of the soul.”

Hebrews 10:39

So what does a life of faith look like? Chapter 11 answers with people, not a definition.

“The elders” means the people of old. The writer walks through the whole Old Testament, one life at a time. And the last frame is still open.

Faith starts at the beginning

Nobody watched God make the world. We know it the way Abraham knew his promise: God said so. He spoke, and what wasn't there was there.

That matters for you. The God who made something out of nothing isn't stuck with the mess you made. He can start with nothing.

“While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal.”

2 Corinthians 4:18

Two men before the flood

11:4 · Genesis 4

Abel

Cain brought some of his crops. Abel brought the firstlings of his flock, and he brought them by faith. God accepted Abel and his gift. Cain killed him for it. But Abel “being dead yet speaketh”: his story still preaches.

11:5 · Genesis 5

Enoch

Three hundred years of ordinary days, walking with God. Then one day he wasn't there. God took him home without dying.

“And Enoch walked with God: and he was not; for God took him.”

Genesis 5:24

One was murdered young; one never died. Both pleased God the same way: by faith.

Noah worked for years on something nobody else could see coming. He was “moved with fear”, the right kind: he took God seriously. And his whole household came through.

You may be building something your family can't see yet: a clean record, steady work, trust. Day after day of hammer and nails. Keep building. It's “to the saving of his house.”

Part two

Strangers and pilgrims

Verses 8–22

Abraham walked out with no map, lived in tents, and died before the promise came. He thought it was worth it.

No map

About 2000 BC God told Abraham to leave Ur and his father's house “unto a land that I will shew thee” (Genesis 12:1). He didn't say where. Abraham went anyway.

His grandson Jacob's family went down to Egypt in a famine.

Centuries later, Moses led their children out, through the Red sea, and Joshua led them in at Jericho. The whole chapter happens on this road.

Tents and a city

God promised Abraham the land, yet he lived in it in tents, like a visitor, and so did Isaac and Jacob. Tents go up and come down.

He could live like that because he was looking past it, for a city God Himself built. What he had in his hands was temporary. What he had on his deed was not.

From one man, as good as dead

Sara laughed when she heard she'd have a son at ninety (Genesis 18:12). Yet Hebrews remembers her faith: she “judged him faithful who had promised.” She wasn't trusting her body. She was trusting Him.

From that one old couple came a nation like the stars and the sand. When God records your story, He can write the faith in bigger letters than the laugh.

Seen from far off

They died before the promises came true. They saw them coming the way you see a town's lights from miles down the highway, and they kept driving toward them.

“Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad.”

Jesus, John 8:56

A pilgrim is a man on his way somewhere. He doesn't put down roots in a motel. This world isn't the place God is taking you; it's the road.

The road back is always open

If they had kept their minds on the old country, the writer says, they could have gone back. Ur was still there. Nobody was stopping them.

The old life is still out there for you too: same streets, same numbers in your phone. You stay off that road by setting your heart on a better one. And God is “not ashamed to be called their God.” Not ashamed of you.

Abraham told his servants, “I and the lad will go yonder and worship, and come again to you” (Genesis 22:5). He expected to come back with Isaac, even if God had to raise him. Then God stopped his hand and gave a ram in Isaac's place.

That's the “figure”: a picture. About two thousand years later, near those same hills, another Father gave His only begotten Son. No one stopped that hand. Jesus took our place, and on the third day God raised Him from the dead.

  2. Jacobthe old schemer, dying, worshipping on his staff (11:21)

“And Joseph took an oath of the children of Israel, saying, God will surely visit you, and ye shall carry up my bones from hence.”

Genesis 50:25

Joseph had every reason to stay bitter: sold by his brothers, framed, jailed. He died sure God would keep His word, and about four hundred years later Israel carried his bones home (Joshua 24:32). Jacob started as a cheat and finished as a worshipper. How you started isn't how you have to finish.

Part three

Choosing

Verses 23–31

Moses could have had a palace. A harlot could have stayed on the losing side. Both chose God.

Weighed up

Raised in Pharaoh's palace, Moses had money, rank, and every pleasure Egypt could give. When he was grown, he walked away from it to stand with a nation of slaves.

To Egypt it looked like a fool's choice. But Moses weighed it with eternity on the scale: “he had respect unto the recompence of the reward.”

The pleasures of sin

The Bible doesn't pretend sin isn't fun. It calls it pleasure. Every man in recovery knows that's true, or he wouldn't have needed recovery.

But it's pleasure “for a season.” The high lasts a night. The bill comes for years: the marriage, the job, the kids, the record.

Faith doesn't deny the pleasure. It reads the whole line, to the end.

Blood on the door, a road through the sea

11:28 · Exodus 12

The passover

Lamb's blood on the doorposts. The destroyer passed over every house under the blood. A picture of Jesus, “the Lamb of God”, whose blood covers us.

11:29 · Exodus 14

The same water

Israel walked through on dry ground. The Egyptians tried the same thing and drowned. Same sea, same path. The difference was faith in God, not nerve.

And Moses himself? At forty he killed a man and ran for his life (Exodus 2:12–15). Forty years later God sent him back. He “endured, as seeing him who is invisible.” Your worst day doesn't disqualify you either.

Rahab the harlot, in the hall of faith

Rahab the harlot → in the family of Jesus

A Canaanite prostitute in a doomed city, she believed Israel's God: “he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath” (Joshua 2:11). She hid His spies, and God spared her house.

“And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth; and Obed begat Jesse;”

Matthew 1:5

She became King David's great-great-grandmother. The Bible still says what she was. It also says what grace made her.

Read their stories and you'll wince. Fear, lust, a violent past, adultery, murder. Yet when God writes their names in this chapter, He doesn't list their sins. He lists their faith.

Not excused, but forgiven

Their sins were real, and they paid for them: Samson lost his eyes, and David's house was never the same. God doesn't pretend. But He forgives, and He uses men who come back to Him.

“And David said unto Nathan, I have sinned against the LORD. And Nathan said unto David, The LORD also hath put away thy sin; thou shalt not die.”

Samson's last prayer came from a prison. God heard it. If you are in Christ, your file says forgiven and by faith, and “out of weakness were made strong” is about men like you.

Faith that won, and faith that suffered

11:33–35a

Delivered

Lions' mouths shut (Daniel). Fire that didn't burn (the three Hebrews). Sons raised from the dead for their mothers (Elijah, Elisha).

11:35b–38

Not delivered

Tortured. Mocked, whipped, jailed. Stoned, like the prophet Zechariah (2 Chronicles 24:21). Sawn in two, Isaiah by old tradition. Hiding in caves (1 Kings 18:4).

Same faith, both columns. Faith is not a deal where God promises you an easy life if you believe hard enough. Some were rescued from it, and some were carried through it, all the way to death and “a better resurrection.”

“But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up.”

Daniel 3:18

The world wasn't worthy of them.

Homeless, in animal skins, living in holes in the ground. The world looked at them and saw nothing. God looked at them and said the world didn't deserve them.

People may have written you off: an inmate number, an addict, a waste. That's not how God reads a man who trusts Him.

Question 1 of 6

Faith

What is faith, and why does God require it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Faith is being sure of what God has promised before you can see it, like holding the deed before you live in the house. God requires it because coming to Him means trusting that He is, and that He is good to those who seek Him. Without that trust, nothing we do pleases Him.

Question 2 of 6

Pilgrims

Why did these people live like strangers on the earth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because they were looking for a better country, a heavenly city God Himself built. They could have gone back to the old country, but they set their hearts ahead instead. And God is not ashamed to be called their God.

Question 3 of 6

Moses

What did Moses give up, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He gave up being called the son of Pharaoh's daughter, with the treasures and pleasures of Egypt. He chose to suffer with God's people, because he weighed it to the end: sin's pleasures last only for a season, and the reward lasts forever.

Question 4 of 6

Rahab

Why is a harlot in this list?

What verses tell us about this?

“for the LORD your God, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath.”

Joshua 2:11

“And Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth; and Obed begat Jesse;”

Matthew 1:5

Answer

Because she believed God. The list is about faith, not a clean past. Rahab trusted Israel's God, sided with His people, and was spared when Jericho fell. Grace took her all the way into the family line of Jesus.

Question 5 of 6

Flawed

Why does God list men like Samson and Jephthae?

What verses tell us about this?

“O Lord GOD, remember me, I pray thee, and strengthen me, I pray thee, only this once”

Judges 16:28

Answer

Because they trusted God, and He forgives and uses weak men who come to Him. Their sins were real and costly, but God writes down their faith. Out of weakness they were made strong. There's room in this list for men with ugly chapters.

Question 6 of 6

Rescued?

Does faith always mean God rescues us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. Some were delivered from lions and fire. Others, just as faithful, were tortured and killed. Faith trusts God either way, and it looks to a better resurrection. God has something better for all of them, and for us.
