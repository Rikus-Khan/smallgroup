1 John 2

An Advocate with the Father

“That ye sin not. And if any man sin…” John aims high and catches us when we fall. A lawyer who has never lost a case, a payment big enough for the whole world, and three doors every temptation still walks through.

Part one

An advocate

Verses 1–6

The best news a believer who just fell could ever hear, and what real knowing looks like.

The aim, and the net

The aim

“That ye sin not”

John isn't handing out a license. He writes so you'll stay out of it. Grace never says, “Go ahead.”

The net

“If any man sin”

And when you do fall, you don't fall to the bottom. We have, right now, someone with the Father on our side.

“And the Lord said, Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat: But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Luke 22:31–32

Jesus prayed for Peter before Peter fell, and planned his comeback in the same breath. He does the same for you.

A lawyer who has never lost a case

Advocate is paraklētos: one called to your side, a defense lawyer. The accuser is right about the facts. Your Lawyer doesn't claim you're innocent. He stands beside you and points to His own blood.

Case closed, not because the sin was small, but because it's paid. “Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died” (Romans 8:34).

Big enough for the whole world

Propitiation: the sacrifice that satisfies God's justice and turns His anger away. Once a year the high priest sprinkled blood on the lid of the ark, the mercy seat. Jesus is that, in person.

And how far does it reach? Not for ours only. Nobody is outside it: not the worst man in the cell block, not the man you can't forgive, not you.

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

John 3:16

Paid for everyone; received by faith. The payment is already big enough. It becomes yours the moment you take it.

Check for a pulse

A doctor doesn't ask whether you've ever been sick. He checks for a pulse. Some days a believer's pulse is faint or uneven, but a living man has one: he wants to obey, he gets back up, he's learning to walk like Jesus. That's not how you get saved. It's how you can know you are.

Part two

The light is already shining

Verses 7–14

An old command made new, the darkness of a grudge, and a word for every stage of the walk.

Old command, new light

The command is old: love. Moses wrote it. But Jesus made it new by showing what it looks like: “A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you” (John 13:34).

before ChristNight

Living in the dark, and not even knowing it.

nowDawn

The sun is up. Some shadows are left, but the light is winning.

when He comesDay

No more night at all.

That's where you live: at dawn. If you still see dark spots in yourself, that's normal at sunrise. Don't mistake a shadow for the night coming back. The true light now shineth.

A grudge is a blindfold

Hates his brother

Walks in the dark

Doesn't know where he's going. Can't see straight. Every decision gets made through the grudge.

Loves his brother

Nothing to trip on

None occasion of stumbling: no hidden stone in the path, for him or for anyone following him.

Men in recovery learn this the hard way: resentment is one of the fastest roads back to the bottle. You can't hold a grudge and see clearly at the same time.

“Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath: Neither give place to the devil.”

Ephesians 4:26–27

A word for every stage

stage oneLittle childrenyour sins are forgiven you for his name's sake

The starting line. You know the Father, and the record is clear, because of His name, not yours.

stage twoYoung menye are strong, and the word of God abideth in you

The fight years. The Word is in you, and you've beaten the wicked one more than once.

stage threeFathersye have known him that is from the beginning

Long years of knowing Him. Quiet, steady, deep. They're the men who help the rest up.

These aren't ages in years; they're stages of growth. A 50-year-old saved last month is a little child, and that's fine. Every stage starts at the same place: your sins are forgiven you. Ask yourself today: who is a “father” I can learn from, and who is a “little child” I can help?

Part three

Love not the world

Verses 15–17

Three doors that every temptation since the garden has walked through.

Which “world”?

  • not the people in it“God so loved the world” (John 3:16)
  • not the earth and its good thingsGod made them, and called them good
  • but life arranged without Godits wants, its rules, its idea of who matters

And it doesn't last. The world passeth away, and the lust thereof. Every high wears off. Every prize rusts. But he that doeth the will of God abideth for ever. Don't trade forever for a weekend.

Part four

Abide in him

Verses 18–29

Fakes who walked out, a Teacher who moved in, and a reason to stand tall when Jesus comes.

Who “went out”?

The antichrists, verse 22

Walked out denying Jesus

Teachers who sat in the church, then left it, saying Jesus is not the Christ. Their leaving showed they had never truly been of us.

Not the same man

A believer who falls

He stumbles, hates it, and comes back to Jesus. That isn't “going out.” That's a son coming home, and John wrote 1:9 and 2:1 for him.

Real believers continue. Not because they're strong, but because God keeps them: “neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand” (John 10:28). If you're worried that you've wandered too far, the worry itself is a sign the Spirit is still pulling you back.

The Christ shares His anointing with His people. Every believer has it, from the day he's saved: no second experience to chase, no secret level to unlock. “If any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his” (Romans 8:9). If you're His, the Spirit is in you.

Nothing new is needed. Stay with what you heard.

The false teachers said, “You need something more, something deeper that we have.” John says the opposite: the old gospel you heard at the start, Jesus crucified and risen for sinners, is the real thing. Let it abide, live in you, like a man who moves in and stays.

And it comes with a promise God made, not a hope we invented: eternal life. That's not something you might get someday if you hold on tight enough. He promised it.

A Teacher who moved in

Ye need not that any man teach you doesn't mean, “Skip church, you don't need teachers.” John is teaching them right now! It means you don't need these new “experts” with secret knowledge. The Spirit in you knows the real Jesus when He hears Him.

“Howbeit when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth:”

John 16:13

Abide in him: stay close. And here's the hope: one day He will appear. A man who has stayed close to Jesus won't hide his face that day. He'll look up, like a son when his father's truck pulls into the driveway.

If any man sin, we have an advocate.

Aim high: that ye sin not. Love your brother. Shut the three doors. And when you fall anyway, don't run from the Judge. Your Lawyer is already standing beside you, and the payment covers the whole world, so it covers you.

Something to do today: name the door you're weakest at (flesh, eyes, or pride) and pick one Bible verse to keep at that door. Write it on your phone's lock screen. Then, if there's a grudge in you, pray for that man by name before you clock in.

Points to remember

  1. Our advocate“we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous” 2:1When a believer sins, Jesus stands beside him before the Father, pointing to His own blood.
  2. The whole world“not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world” 2:2His sacrifice is big enough for everyone. It's received by faith.
  3. Knowing Him“hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments” 2:3Obeying Him doesn't save us. It's the pulse that shows we're alive.
  4. Light and love“he that hateth his brother is in darkness” 2:11A grudge blinds a man. Love keeps the path clear.
  5. Love not the world“the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life” 2:16Three doors. Jesus won at all three with the Word, and the world is passing away.
  6. The anointing“the anointing which ye have received of him abideth in you” 2:27Every believer has the Holy Spirit, teaching him the truth about Jesus.

Question 1 of 6

Our advocate

If any man sin, who stands up for us with the Father?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

Answer

Jesus Christ the righteous, our Advocate. He stands with us before the Father, not claiming we're innocent, but pointing to the price He paid.

Question 2 of 6

The whole world

Whose sins was Jesus the propitiation for?

What verses tell us about this?

“Who gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time.”

1 Timothy 2:6

Answer

Ours, and the sins of the whole world. No one is outside the reach of His sacrifice. Whoever believes receives it.

Question 3 of 6

Knowing Him

How do we know that we know Him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

If we keep His commandments and walk as He walked. Not perfectly, but really: a growing desire to obey is the pulse of a living faith.

Question 4 of 6

Light and love

Where is the man who hates his brother?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In darkness, still. He walks blind and doesn't know where he's going. The man who loves his brother stays in the light, with nothing to trip over.

Question 5 of 6

Love not the world

What three things in the world are not of the Father?

What verses tell us about this?

“And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat.”

Genesis 3:6

Answer

The lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life. They're passing away; the man who does God's will lasts for ever.

Question 6 of 6

The anointing

What has every believer received from Him that abides in him?

What verses tell us about this?

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

Answer

The anointing: the Holy Spirit, given to every believer when he believes. He stays, and He teaches us the truth about Jesus.
