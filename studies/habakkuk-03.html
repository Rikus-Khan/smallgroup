Habakkuk 3

Yet I will rejoice

Habakkuk ends with a song. He remembers what God has done, trembles at what's coming, and decides to rejoice in the LORD even if everything is lost.

Part one

In wrath remember mercy

Verses 1–2

Habakkuk heard God's answer, and it scared him. So he prays.

A prayer to sing

Quick reminder: Habakkuk lives in Judah, about 610–600 BC, with Babylon on the way. He asked God hard questions in chapter 1. God answered in chapter 2. Chapter 3 is his reply, and it's a song.

  1. Shigionotha kind of song; the word is rare, and probably means a stirring, deeply felt one
  2. Selahsaid three times here; probably a pause, a moment to stop and think
  3. the chief singerverse 19: the choir leader, who used it in worship

Habakkuk's prayer was written to be sung by God's people together. Like the psalms, it was meant for men to carry in their mouths when times got hard.

Mercy outweighs it

Habakkuk doesn't argue anymore. He agrees that judgment is coming and that Judah has earned it. He asks two things: “revive thy work” (keep Your work alive, do it again in our day), and “in wrath remember mercy.”

That's the prayer of a man who knows he has nothing to bargain with. It's the same prayer Jesus praised:

“And the publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner.”

Luke 18:13

Part two

God came

Verses 3–15

To face the future, Habakkuk looks back: the God who came to rescue His people before.

Where God came from

Habakkuk pictures God marching up from the desert south, as He did after the exodus: from Sinai, through the wilderness of Paran, past Edom (Teman), to the land. Moses used the same names (Deuteronomy 33:2).

Blazing light, rays (“horns”) flashing from His hand, plague and fire before Him, mountains crumbling, desert tribes trembling in their tents. This is the God we're dealing with.

He's done it before

Was God angry at the rivers and the sea? No. Habakkuk is retelling Israel's story as a song, with God riding out in His “chariots of salvation.” Each line calls up something God really did:

  1. the seaparted, Exodus 14verses 8, 15
  2. the mountainstrembled at Sinai, Exodus 19verses 6, 10
  3. the riverthe Jordan stopped, Joshua 3verses 8, 9
  4. the sun and moonstood still, Joshua 10verse 11

When a man is scared of what's ahead, it helps to remember what God has already done, in the Bible and in his own life. The God who did that is still God.

“I will remember the works of the LORD: surely I will remember thy wonders of old.”

Psalm 77:11

He went out to save

Why did God march through the land and “thresh the heathen”? Not for sport. Verse 13 gives the reason: “for the salvation of thy people.” He crushed the head of the wicked house, the way He broke Pharaoh's army, and the enemy who came “as a whirlwind” to scatter His people was the one scattered.

“Thine anointed” is God's chosen king and people. The word in Hebrew is mashiach, the word that gives us “Messiah.”

That's who God is: He goes out to rescue. He did it at the Red Sea, and He did it most of all when He sent His Son for sinners.

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Part three

Yet I will rejoice

Verses 16–19

Nothing outside has changed. Babylon is still coming. But the man has changed.

Shaking, then still

Habakkuk is honest: his stomach churns, his lips shake, his legs feel like they're rotting out from under him. The invasion is coming, and he knows it.

But he decides to wait quietly for the day of trouble, trusting God to deal with the invader. Fear doesn't have to be gone for faith to be there. A man can tremble and trust at the same time.

“Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee.”

Isaiah 26:3

Everything gone

For a farmer in Judah, this list is everything he owns and everything he eats. Habakkuk takes it all away, one by one:

  1. the fig treeno blossom
  2. the vinesno fruit
  3. the olivethe work fails
  4. the fieldsno food (“meat”)
  5. the flockcut off
  6. the herdempty stalls

Some of us know what that list feels like: the job, the house, the family, the health, the name. Habakkuk doesn't say it won't happen. He says even if it does.

Yet.

That one word turns the whole book around. His joy isn't in the harvest, so the locusts and the Babylonians can't take it. It's in the LORD, “the God of my salvation,” and nobody can take Him.

This isn't pretending to be happy. It's a choice: “I will.” Paul wrote the same thing while he was a prisoner:

“Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, Rejoice.”

Philippians 4:4

“Not that I speak in respect of want: for I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content.”

Philippians 4:11

Sure feet on high ground

A “hind” is a female deer. She can run up a steep, rocky mountain without slipping. The “high places” here are those heights, not the idol shrines we've seen in other prophets.

Habakkuk's groundshaking, rocky, steep, and Babylon on the way
→
what God givesfeet that don't slip, and a place up high

God doesn't always flatten the mountain. He gives a man the feet to climb it. “The LORD God is my strength,” not my willpower.

“He maketh my feet like hinds’ feet, and setteth me upon my high places.”

Psalm 18:33

From “how long?” to “yet I will rejoice”

Look at the whole book. Babylon was still coming at the end, just as at the start. What changed was Habakkuk. He started out asking why, and ended up singing Who.

That's the road for us too. Bring God the hard questions. Wait for Him. Live by faith. And on the worst day, choose this: “yet I will rejoice in the LORD.”

Points to remember

  1. Mercy“In wrath remember mercy” 3:2We have nothing to bargain with. We ask God for mercy, and He is rich in it.
  2. Remembering“His ways are everlasting” 3:6Remember what God has already done. The God who did it is still God.
  3. Salvation“Thou wentest forth for the salvation of thy people” 3:13God goes out to rescue His people. He did it most of all in Jesus.
  4. Fear“That I might rest in the day of trouble” 3:16A man can tremble and trust at the same time. Rest in God while you wait.
  5. Joy“Yet I will rejoice in the LORD” 3:18Joy in the Lord can't be taken, because He can't be taken. It's a choice: “I will.”
  6. Strength“The LORD God is my strength” 3:19God may not flatten the mountain, but He gives the feet to climb it.

Question 1 of 6

Mercy

What can we ask for when we deserve judgment?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner.”

Luke 18:13

“But God, who is rich in mercy, for his great love wherewith he loved us, Even when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together with Christ, (by grace ye are saved;)”

Ephesians 2:4–5

Answer

Mercy. Habakkuk didn't argue that Judah deserved better. He agreed judgment was right and asked God to remember mercy. That's the prayer God always hears. Jesus said the tax collector who prayed “God be merciful to me a sinner” went home right with God.

Question 2 of 6

Remembering

Why look back at what God has already done?

What verses tell us about this?

“I will remember the works of the LORD: surely I will remember thy wonders of old.”

Psalm 77:11

Answer

Because it reminds us who He is. Habakkuk faced a frightening future by remembering the Red Sea, Sinai, the Jordan, and the sun standing still. God's ways are everlasting: He hasn't changed. Remembering what God has done in your own life works the same way on a hard day.

Question 3 of 6

Salvation

Who goes out to rescue God's people?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

God Himself. Israel didn't save itself at the Red Sea; God went out for them. That's still how salvation works. We didn't climb up to God. He came down to us, and Christ died for us while we were still sinners.

Question 4 of 6

Fear

What do we do when we're afraid of what's coming?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee.”

Isaiah 26:3

“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”

Psalm 46:1

Answer

Be honest about it, and rest in God anyway. Habakkuk shook from head to toe, and still chose to wait quietly for the day of trouble. Fear doesn't cancel faith. Keep your mind on the Lord, and He keeps you in peace.

Question 5 of 6

Joy

Can a man rejoice when everything is gone?

What verses tell us about this?

“Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, Rejoice.”

Philippians 4:4

Answer

Yes, if his joy is in the Lord. Habakkuk listed every crop and animal gone, and then said, “Yet I will rejoice in the LORD.” Things can be lost. God can't. Rejoicing in Him isn't pretending things are fine. It's a choice to hold on to the God of our salvation.

Question 6 of 6

Strength

Where does a man's strength come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“He maketh my feet like hinds’ feet, and setteth me upon my high places.”

Psalm 18:33

“Then he said unto them, Go your way, eat the fat, and drink the sweet, and send portions unto them for whom nothing is prepared: for this day is holy unto our Lord: neither be ye sorry; for the joy of the LORD is your strength.”

Nehemiah 8:10

Answer

From the Lord. “The LORD God is my strength,” Habakkuk sang, not his own grit. God gave him sure feet like a deer's for steep and rocky ground. Willpower runs out. The Lord doesn't, and the joy He gives becomes our strength.
