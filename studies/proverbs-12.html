Proverbs 12

Love correction, guard your mouth

A wise man can take a rebuke, hold his temper, tell the truth, and finish the job. His words heal instead of cut.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

Rooted

Verses 1–12

What a man loves, who he marries, and what he's rooted in.

Love it or hate it

Nobody enjoys being corrected. But Solomon says the man who wants to know things has to love being taught, including the hard part.

The man who hates reproof is “brutish”: the Hebrew word is about cattle. He's like a dumb animal that keeps walking into the same fence. Every correction he refuses, he stays right where he is.

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

What holds you up

You can build something fast on wickedness: a hustle, a reputation, a bankroll. But “a man shall not be established” by it. There's nothing underneath.

The righteous man's real strength is underground, where nobody sees it: time with God, His Word, a life slowly rooted in Him. When the wind comes, roots are what count. Verse 12 says it too: “the root of the righteous yieldeth fruit.”

A crown, or rot in the bones

A virtuous woman is a woman of strength and good character: the same word used of the wife in chapter 31 and of Ruth. She makes her husband look like a king.

“Who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is far above rubies.”

Proverbs 31:10

If God gives you a woman like that, honor her. The other side is “rottenness in his bones”: a marriage can rot a man from the inside. And that runs both ways. Be the kind of husband who is a crown to his wife too.

Verses 5–6 turn to words: the wicked use them like an ambush, “to lie in wait for blood.” The upright use theirs to rescue people.

Better a nobody with bread

Man one

Talks bigNice clothes, big plans, everybody's buddy. “Honoureth himself.”
“Lacketh bread”Behind the show, the cupboard's empty and the rent is late.

Man two

A nobodyPlain truck, plain job. People don't think much of him. “Despised.”
“Hath a servant”Bills paid, food on the table, enough to hire help.

Flip the cards. The show-off is broke; the nobody is doing fine. Solomon says the quiet man with real bread is better off than the big talker with none.

Pride makes a man spend money he doesn't have to impress people he doesn't like. Humility lets you live honest and small, and build something real.

Kind to animals, careful with friends

Verse 10: a good man is even kind to his animals. How a man treats something that can't pay him back says a lot. The wicked man's “tender mercies”, his best behavior, is still cruel.

Verse 11: work your own land and you'll eat. Follow “vain persons” (empty, worthless men with get-rich-quick schemes and nothing behind them) and you'll end up with nothing too.

You know those men. Maybe you used to be one. Part of a new life is choosing new company and putting your hands to honest work.

Part two

Swords and medicine

Verses 13–22

The same tongue can cut a man open or help him heal.

“It seemed like a good idea at the time”

The fool never thinks he's being a fool. His way is “right in his own eyes.” That's the problem: his own eyes are the only ones he checks with.

Proverbs 12:15

The way of a fool is right in his own eyes: but he that hearkeneth unto counsel is wise.

Proverbs 14:12

There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.

If your best thinking got you where you were, it's wise to let somebody else look at the map. Verses 13–14 add: a man gets “snared” by his own lips, and he eats the “fruit of his mouth”, good or bad.

A short fuse, a long fuse

The fool
BANG: “presently known”
The prudent man
put out: “covereth shame”

“Presently” in 1611 meant right away. The fool's anger shows the second he's insulted. Everybody in the room knows.

The prudent man “covereth shame”: when someone insults him, he lets it go instead of making a scene. That isn't weakness. It takes more strength to hold it than to throw it.

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:”

James 1:19

Words that cut, words that heal

Some men talk like they're swinging a blade: sarcasm, put-downs, the one comment that finds the sore spot. Most of us were cut that way growing up, and a lot of us learned to cut back.

But the wise man's tongue is “health”, literally healing, medicine. The same mouth that wounded can learn to mend. Ask God to change the way you talk to your wife, your kids, and the men in this room.

How long does a lie last?

A lying tongue“but for a moment”
The lip of truth“established for ever”

A lie buys you a minute: out of trouble, past the question, through the night. Then it has to be fed with more lies. The truth is slower, and it holds.

Verse 22 says “lying lips are abomination to the LORD”, but “they that deal truly are his delight.” For a man who used to lie for a living (to cover the using, the money, the where-were-you), learning to tell the truth is one of the clearest marks of a new life.

If you've been a liar

Don't hear this and give up. God hates lying lips because He loves the truth, and He loves you. He doesn't leave a liar stuck. He offers a clean start:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

“Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour: for we are members one of another.”

Ephesians 4:25

Confess it to God. Then start practicing truth with each other, beginning in this room.

Part three

Finish the job

Verses 23–28

A heavy heart, a lazy hunter, and a road with no death on it.

A heavy heart

“Heaviness” is worry, dread, anxiety. It bends a man over. Some of us carry it every day: the court date, the bills, the kids, the shame.

And what lifts it? Sometimes, just “a good word.” A brother who says “I'm praying for you” or “you're doing better than you think.” You can be that word for someone today.

And take the weight to the One who can carry it: “Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you” (1 Peter 5:7).

Boss, or forced labor

The diligent man ends up able to “bear rule”: trusted, in charge, running things. The slothful ends up “under tribute”: forced labor, working for somebody else on their terms.

Laziness promises you freedom and delivers the opposite. Steady work, done well, is what actually sets a man free.

Verse 23 adds a quiet word about wisdom: the prudent man doesn't show off everything he knows. The fool broadcasts his foolishness to anyone who'll listen.

The lazy hunter

  • track it
  • hunt it
  • catch it
  • roast it never

Here's a man who did the hard part. He went hunting and caught something. Then he couldn't be bothered to cook it. All that work, and he goes to bed hungry.

That's the slothful man: he starts strong and doesn't finish. Ninety days clean and then coasting. Half the program. The job with no follow-through. Diligence is finishing. “The substance of a diligent man is precious.”

Your friends are a road

The first line is hard to translate from Hebrew. The KJV reads it as the righteous man standing above his neighbor; others read it as the righteous man guiding his neighbor, or choosing friends with care. The second line is plain: “the way of the wicked seduceth them.” It leads people off the road.

“He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed.”

Proverbs 13:20

Who you walk with decides where you end up. Pick men who are headed where you want to go.

A road with no death on it

Every road a man can walk ends at a grave, except one. “In the pathway thereof there is no death.” Solomon saw it from a distance. Jesus stepped onto the road and named it:

“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6

A believer's body may still die, but death isn't the end of his road. It's a door. In Christ, the road keeps going, and it ends in life.

Points to remember

  1. Correction“Whoso loveth instruction loveth knowledge” 12:1Learn to love correction. Hating it keeps you stuck.
  2. The tongue“The tongue of the wise is health” 12:18The same mouth that cut people can learn to heal them.
  3. Truth“A lying tongue is but for a moment” 12:19A lie buys a minute. The truth lasts forever, and God delights in it.
  4. Anger“A prudent man covereth shame” 12:16Let the insult go. Holding your temper takes more strength than losing it.
  5. Work“The substance of a diligent man is precious” 12:27Diligence is finishing. Don't leave the meal uncooked.
  6. Life“In the pathway thereof there is no death” 12:28Jesus is the Way. On His road, death is a door, not the end.

Question 1 of 6

Correction

How do I respond when someone corrects me?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

Answer

Listen, and learn to love it. The fool thinks his own way is right and stays stuck, like an animal walking into the same fence. The wise man hearkens to counsel. Correction hurts in the moment, but afterward it grows the fruit of righteousness in us.

Question 2 of 6

The tongue

Do my words cut, or do they heal?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That's the question to ask yourself. Careless, cutting words pierce like a sword; a wise man's words bring health. Something as small as a good word can lift a heavy heart. And what we say comes back to us: a man is filled by the fruit of his own mouth.

Question 3 of 6

Truth

Why is a lie never worth it?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Because it only lasts a moment, and then it needs more lies to keep it alive. Truth is established forever. God calls lying lips an abomination and honest dealing His delight. If lying has been your habit, confess it; He forgives and cleans us up, and then we practice the truth with each other.

Question 4 of 6

Anger

What does a wise man do with an insult?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:”

James 1:19

Answer

He covers it. The fool's anger shows up right away for everyone to see. The prudent man lets the insult pass without making a scene. He's quick to listen and slow to blow up, and he doesn't need to say everything he's thinking.

Question 5 of 6

Work

What's the difference between the diligent man and the slothful man?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The diligent man works his own field and finishes the job, and he ends up trusted and in charge. The slothful man starts things and doesn't finish them (he won't even cook what he caught), and he ends up working on someone else's terms. Diligence isn't flashy. It's showing up and following through.

Question 6 of 6

Life

Where is the road with no death on it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6

Answer

In the way of righteousness, and Jesus is that Way. The wicked are overthrown and gone, but the house of the righteous stands. Anyone who trusts Christ is on a road where death isn't the end. It's a door into life with Him.
