Luke 24

He is risen

An empty tomb, a stranger on the road to Emmaus, and the risen Lord eating fish with His friends before He sends them out and goes up into heaven.

Part one

He is not here, but is risen

Verses 1–12

The women come with spices for a dead man. The stone is already gone.

Very early, the first day

The women who watched Him buried (23:55) rested through the sabbath, then came at first light on Sunday with their spices. They came to care for a body. No one was expecting a resurrection.

The stone was already rolled away. It wasn't moved to let Jesus out. It was moved to let the witnesses in. And the body was gone.

Among the dead

The two men in shining garments were angels (v. 23 calls it “a vision of angels”). Their question is almost gentle: why are you looking for a living man in a graveyard?

Then: “remember how he spake unto you.” He had told them plainly, more than once, back in Galilee:

“Saying, The Son of man must suffer many things, and be rejected of the elders and chief priests and scribes, and be slain, and be raised the third day.”

Luke 9:22

Some of us spent years looking for life among dead things: the bottle, the old crowd, the old places. There's no life there. He's risen.

The first witnesses

  1. Mary Magdalenelikely from Magdala, by the lake. Jesus had cast seven devils out of her (8:2).
  2. Joannawife of Chuza, the steward of Herod's household (8:3). A woman of some standing.
  3. Mary the mother of Jamesone of the women from Galilee who watched the cross from a distance (Mark 15:40).

In those days a woman's word didn't count for much in court. If someone were making up a resurrection story, he'd never pick women as the first witnesses. Luke names them because that's what happened.

And notice who got there first: a woman who'd been tormented by devils. The people Jesus rescued were the ones who stayed closest.

Idle tales

Idle tales means nonsense, the talk of someone not thinking straight. The apostles didn't believe it. These weren't gullible men waiting for a miracle. They'd seen Him die, and they were sure it was over.

But Peter ran. The man who had denied Him three times only days before ran to the tomb and stooped to look. He saw the linen clothes, the burial wrappings, lying there by themselves. No body. No thief would have stopped to unwrap it.

He went home “wondering.” Not believing yet, but not dismissing it. Honest questions are a fine place to start. Jesus will meet a man there.

Part two

Did not our heart burn?

Verses 13–35

Two sad men walk home, and a stranger falls in step beside them.

Seven miles out

60furlongs (“threescore”)
≈ 7miles, about 11 km
2–3hours on foot

Two followers of Jesus, not of the eleven, walk home on Sunday afternoon. A furlong was about an eighth of a mile.

After supper they walk the whole way back, in the dark, that same hour (v. 33).

What things?

The strangerWhat manner of communications are these that ye have one to another, as ye walk, and are sad?

CleopasArt thou only a stranger in Jerusalem, and hast not known the things which are come to pass there in these days? the only man who knew

The strangerWhat things?

Their eyes were holden: kept from knowing Him. He doesn't announce Himself. He asks, and lets two hurting men tell Him their story. You can tell Him exactly how it looks from where you stand. He isn't offended by a sad man's honesty.

“We trusted”

Past tense. They'd hoped He was the One to redeem Israel: to set the nation free, most likely from Rome. Then the cross. Then three days of silence. Even the women's report only confused them more.

They had the right Man and the wrong idea of what He came to do. He did redeem, far deeper than they dreamed, and not with an army:

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Beginning at Moses

O fools here means men without understanding, not idiots. Their problem wasn't a lack of evidence. It was slow hearts: they believed the parts of the prophets they liked, about a glorious King, and skipped the parts about a suffering servant.

Ought not: it had to be this way. First the suffering, then the glory. The cross wasn't the plan failing. It was the plan.

“But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed.”

Isaiah 53:5

Then the best Bible study ever taught, on a dusty road: Jesus going through the whole Old Testament, showing them Himself on every page.

Abide with us

At the village He made as though He'd keep walking. He didn't force Himself on them. They constrained Him, urged Him strongly: it's getting dark, stay with us.

“Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me.”

Revelation 3:20

The guest took the host's place. He took the bread, blessed it, broke it, and handed it to them, and suddenly they knew Him. Maybe it was the way He did it. Maybe they saw the nail wounds in His hands. Then He was gone.

From sad to burning

“Did not our heart burn within us”: it was already happening on the road, while He opened the Scriptures, before they knew who He was. He still meets men that way.

He appeared to Simon

Back in Jerusalem the eleven have news of their own. Of all the disciples, the risen Lord made a point of seeing Simon Peter, alone. Remember the last time their eyes met:

“And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. And Peter went out, and wept bitterly.”

Luke 22:61–62

We aren't told what was said. But the man who failed worst got a private visit first (1 Corinthians 15:5). If you've let Him down badly, the risen Jesus isn't avoiding you. He's coming to find you.

Part three

Handle me, and see

Verses 36–43

The room goes quiet. He's standing in the middle of them.

Flesh and bones

Before anything else He says “Peace be unto you,” to the men who ran away. No rebuke first. Then they panic: a spirit, a ghost. So He holds out the hands and feet with the nail wounds, the ones the psalm foretold: “they pierced my hands and my feet” (Psalm 22:16).

Not a vision. Not a spirit. The same Jesus, in a real body, raised and changed, that could be touched. John was in that room, and spent the rest of his life saying so:

“That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we have looked upon, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life;”

1 John 1:1

Too good to be true

“Believed not for joy”: it seemed too good to be true. So He asked for meat, which in the KJV means any food, and ate a piece of fish in front of them. Every other way of explaining it away falls flat:

  • They went to the wrong tombthe women saw where He was laid (23:55)
  • Someone stole the bodythe grave clothes were left behind (24:12)
  • They saw a ghosta ghost hasn't flesh and bones (24:39)
  • They wanted to believe itthey called it idle tales (24:11)
  • “The Lord is risen indeed”24:34

Part four

Ye are witnesses

Verses 44–53

He opens their minds, hands them a message for the world, and blesses them as He goes.

The whole Old Testament

The Jews divided their Bible into three parts: the Law, the Prophets, and the Writings, which began with the Psalms. Jesus names all three. Every part points to Him:

The law of Moses

Genesis 3:15

“it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel”: the first promise of a Saviour.

The prophets

Isaiah 53:5

“he was wounded for our transgressions”: the suffering Servant.

The psalms

Psalm 16:10

“neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption”: a grave that couldn't hold Him.

Then opened he their understanding. We need the same help. Ask Him before you read, and the Book starts to open.

Repentance: a turning around. Remission: sins sent away, the debt cancelled. In His name, by His authority, to all nations.

And “beginning at Jerusalem,” the city that had just crucified Him. If there was grace for them, there's grace for anyone.

Acts 1:8 traces the same rings: Jerusalem, Judaea, Samaria, the ends of the earth. That's how the news reached us.

The promise of the Father

  1. Fridaythe cross23:33
  2. Sundaythe third day: risen24:6
  3. 40 daysseen alive, “by many infallible proofs”Acts 1:3
  4. taken upfrom near Bethany24:50–51
  5. about 10 days laterPentecost: the Spirit comesActs 2:1–4

The promise of my Father is the Holy Spirit. Luke tells the rest in his second book: they waited, and at Pentecost the Spirit came with power. Luke 24 tells it short; Acts 1 tells us the forty days in between.

We don't have to wait like they did. Every believer receives the Spirit the moment he trusts Christ: “after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise” (Ephesians 1:13).

With great joy

He led them out toward Bethany, on the Mount of Olives, a couple of miles from the city. The last thing they saw was His hands, with the nail marks, lifted up over them in blessing.

Luke 24:17

“as ye walk, and are sad”

Sunday afternoon, walking away from Jerusalem with dead hopes.

Luke 24:52

“with great joy”

Back in Jerusalem, in the temple, praising God. Nothing about their danger had changed. They had.

“…this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven.”

Acts 1:11

Points to remember

  1. The empty tomb“He is not here, but is risen” 24:6The stone was gone and the body was gone. He rose, as He said.
  2. Doubt“Their words seemed to them as idle tales” 24:11The apostles weren't easy to convince. Jesus was patient with them.
  3. The Scriptures“Beginning at Moses and all the prophets” 24:27The whole Old Testament points to Jesus, who suffered and rose.
  4. Restored“Hath appeared to Simon” 24:34The man who denied Him got a visit from the risen Lord.
  5. A real body“Handle me, and see” 24:39Nail-scarred hands, flesh and bones, and fish eaten in front of them.
  6. The mission“Repentance and remission of sins … among all nations” 24:47The good news goes out to everyone, starting where we are.

Question 1 of 6

The empty tomb

What did the women find at the tomb?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The stone rolled away, no body, and two angels asking why they were looking for the living among the dead. He had risen on the third day, just as He'd told them.

Question 2 of 6

Doubt

How did the apostles first take the news?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They called it idle tales and didn't believe it. Even with Him standing in front of them, it seemed too good to be true. Jesus didn't throw them out. He showed them His hands, ate in front of them, and opened the Scriptures until they believed.

Question 3 of 6

The Scriptures

What did Jesus show the two on the road to Emmaus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Himself, in all the Scriptures. Starting with Moses and going through the prophets, He showed them that the Christ had to suffer and then enter His glory. Their hearts burned while He opened the Bible to them.

Question 4 of 6

Restored

Which disciple did the risen Lord appear to by name?

What verses tell us about this?

“And that he was seen of Cephas, then of the twelve:”

1 Corinthians 15:5

Answer

Simon Peter, the man who had denied Him three times. Peter ran to the tomb, and then the Lord Himself came to him. Failure isn't the end of a man who belongs to Jesus.

Question 5 of 6

A real body

How did Jesus show He wasn't a ghost?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He showed them His hands and feet, told them to touch Him, because a spirit hasn't flesh and bones, and He ate a piece of broiled fish in front of them. He rose in a real body.

Question 6 of 6

The mission

What did Jesus send His disciples out to preach?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Repentance and remission of sins in His name, to all nations, starting at Jerusalem. They were His witnesses, and the Holy Spirit would give them the power. Every believer is part of that same mission today.
