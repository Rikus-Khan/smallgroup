Zechariah 10

I will bring them again

Sheep without a shepherd, scattered to far countries. God whistles for them, gathers them, and brings them home as though He had never cast them off.

Part one

Ask the LORD

Verses 1–5

Rain comes from God, not from idols. And sheep need a real shepherd.

Ask for rain

Quick reminder: Zechariah preached to the Jews back from exile in Babylon, about 520–518 BC. In chapter 9 he promised a lowly King. Now he turns to everyday needs, starting with rain.

  1. autumnthe former rainplow and sow
  2. winterthe main rainsthe crop grows
  3. springthe latter rainthe grain fills out
  4. early summerharvest
  5. summerdry monthsno rain at all

In a land with no rivers to water the fields, the spring rain made or broke the year. The neighbors prayed to Baal for it. God says: ask Me. He makes the storm clouds and sends the showers, “to every one grass in the field.”

“Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you:”

Matthew 7:7

Comfort in vain

Instead of asking God, people asked their idols and the fortune-tellers (“diviners”). Here's what they got:

  • the idols“spoken vanity”: empty talk
  • the diviners“seen a lie”
  • the dreamers“told false dreams”
  • all of it“they comfort in vain”
  • the LORDHe sends the rain, and He shepherds His people

We have our own: the bottle, the pills, the screen, the next relationship. They promise comfort, and they leave a man wandering like a sheep with nobody to lead him.

“Are there any among the vanities of the Gentiles that can cause rain? or can the heavens give showers? art not thou he, O LORD our God? therefore we will wait upon thee: for thou hast made all these things.”

Jeremiah 14:22

From sheep to war horse

The “shepherds” and “goats” are the leaders: the rams and billy goats that push to the front of the flock. They led God's people into idols and then left them. God is angry with them, and He comes to tend the flock Himself.

scattered sheep → “his goodly horse” When God takes over, the same frightened flock becomes His fine war horse, strong and unafraid. The difference isn't the sheep. It's who's leading them.

“But when he saw the multitudes, he was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd.”

Matthew 9:36

Out of Judah

For years foreigners had ruled them. God says their leaders will come from Judah itself, and He uses four pictures:

  1. the cornerthe cornerstone that holds a building square
  2. the nailthe tent peg that holds the whole tent up
  3. the battle bowstrength to win the fight
  4. every rulerhere “oppressor” means a ruler who drives others: leaders of their own

The New Testament doesn't quote this verse. But it does tell us the greatest Leader came out of Judah, and it calls Him “the chief corner stone” (Ephesians 2:20):

“For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood.”

Hebrews 7:14

Verse 5: they'll fight like mighty men, “because the LORD is with them.” Not their own strength. His.

Part two

I will bring them again

Verses 6–12

Listen to how many times God says “I will.”

As though it never happened

“Joseph” and “Ephraim” mean the northern tribes, carried off by Assyria about 722 BC, two hundred years earlier. People had given them up for lost. God hadn't.

God doesn't restore halfway, or keep a man on probation. “As though I had not cast them off.” Why? “For I have mercy upon them.”

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

Rejoice in the LORD.

Ephraim's joy is compared to the glad feeling wine gives. Many of us know that feeling, and what it cost. But look where the verse ends: “their heart shall rejoice in the LORD.”

The joy God gives is the real thing that wine only imitates. It doesn't wear off by morning, it doesn't wreck a home, and the children see it and are glad too.

“Rejoice in the Lord alway: and again I say, Rejoice.”

Philippians 4:4

The Shepherd's whistle

To “hiss” here is to whistle, the way a shepherd calls his flock. God doesn't send an army to drag them home. He whistles, and they know His voice. “For I have redeemed them”: He bought them back. They're His.

“My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me:”

John 10:27

Verse 9: “I will sow them among the people.” Scattered like seed, not thrown away like trash. A seed is put in the ground to come up again. And out there, far from home, “they shall remember me … and turn again.”

“And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father’s have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee,”

Luke 15:17–18

From both ends of the map

Egypt and Assyria were the two places God's people had been carried off to, or run off to, for centuries.

God will bring them back “out of the land of Egypt” in the south-west…

…and gather them “out of Assyria” in the north-east, home to Gilead (east of the Jordan) and Lebanon (the mountains to the north), the old edges of the land.

So many will come that “place shall not be found for them.” There won't be room. God's promise to Israel is not small, and He will keep it.

Through the sea again

This sounds like the exodus, and it's meant to. Long ago God split the Red Sea and brought Israel out of Egypt. He says He'll do it again: whatever sea of trouble (“affliction”) stands between His people and home, He'll strike it and dry it up.

And the powers that held them, “the pride of Assyria” and “the sceptre of Egypt,” will be brought down. No master is strong enough to keep God's people when He calls them home.

“When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow thee: when thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned; neither shall the flame kindle upon thee.”

Isaiah 43:2

Count the “I will”s

Look at who does the work in verses 6–12:

  1. strengthenverse 6
  2. saveverse 6
  3. hiss, gatherverse 8
  4. sowverse 9
  5. bring againverse 10
  6. strengthenverse 12“in the LORD”

The chapter starts and ends with “I will strengthen.” Their part is to walk: “they shall walk up and down in his name.” Living every day as His people, under His name.

That's recovery the way God does it. Not white-knuckling it alone. He gathers, He saves, He strengthens, and we walk.

“Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might.”

Ephesians 6:10

Points to remember

  1. Prayer“Ask ye of the LORD rain” 10:1Bring everyday needs to God. He's the One who sends the rain.
  2. False comfort“They comfort in vain” 10:2Idols and every other substitute promise comfort and leave us wandering.
  3. The Shepherd“They were troubled, because there was no shepherd” 10:2Sheep without a shepherd scatter. With the LORD leading, the same flock is strong.
  4. Taken back“As though I had not cast them off” 10:6God restores all the way, because He has mercy.
  5. Far country“They shall remember me in far countries” 10:9However far a man has gone, he can remember God and turn back.
  6. Strength“I will strengthen them in the LORD” 10:12God does the strengthening. Our part is to walk in His name.

Question 1 of 6

Prayer

What should we ask God for?

What verses tell us about this?

“Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you:”

Matthew 7:7

“Be patient therefore, brethren, unto the coming of the Lord. Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, and hath long patience for it, until he receive the early and latter rain.”

James 5:7

Answer

Whatever we need, even something as ordinary as rain. God told a nation of farmers to ask Him, not the idols, for the thing their whole year hung on. Nothing is too small to bring Him. He's the One who actually sends it.

Question 2 of 6

False comfort

Where do men look for comfort instead of God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Are there any among the vanities of the Gentiles that can cause rain? or can the heavens give showers? art not thou he, O LORD our God? therefore we will wait upon thee: for thou hast made all these things.”

Jeremiah 14:22

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

In idols, fortune-tellers, and dreams then. In bottles, pills, screens, and people now. They all talk big and “comfort in vain.” Only God can do what they promise. Jesus invites the worn-out man to come to Him for rest.

Question 3 of 6

The Shepherd

What happens to sheep without a shepherd?

What verses tell us about this?

“But when he saw the multitudes, he was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd.”

Matthew 9:36

“I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.”

John 10:11

Answer

They wander, get scared, and get hurt. Bad leaders had left Judah that way. But God came to tend His flock Himself, and turned them into a war horse. Jesus looked at crowds like that with compassion, and He is the Good Shepherd who gave His life for them.

Question 4 of 6

Taken back

Can God take back a man He once had to cast off?

What verses tell us about this?

“Return, ye backsliding children, and I will heal your backslidings. Behold, we come unto thee; for thou art the LORD our God.”

Jeremiah 3:22

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

Answer

Yes, and all the way. The northern tribes had been gone two hundred years, and God said they'd be “as though I had not cast them off.” That's what His mercy does. He doesn't take a man back on probation. He removes the sin as far as the east is from the west.

Question 5 of 6

Far country

Is it ever too late to turn back?

What verses tell us about this?

“And when he came to himself, he said, How many hired servants of my father’s have bread enough and to spare, and I perish with hunger! I will arise and go to my father, and will say unto him, Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee,”

Luke 15:17–18

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

Answer

No. God said His scattered people would “remember me in far countries” and “turn again.” The lost son remembered his father's house in a far country, and his father ran to meet him. As long as you have breath, you can remember Him and come home.

Question 6 of 6

Strength

Where does real strength come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might.”

Ephesians 6:10

Answer

From the LORD. Judah fought like mighty men “because the LORD is with them,” and God says, “I will strengthen them in the LORD.” Willpower runs out. His strength doesn't. We lean on Him, and walk in His name, one day at a time.
