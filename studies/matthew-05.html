Matthew 5

The Sermon on the Mount

On a hillside by the lake, Jesus tells who is truly blessed, and shows that God's law reaches all the way down into the heart.

Part one

Who is blessed

Verses 1–12

Jesus sits down on a hillside and turns the world's idea of a good life upside down.

A hillside by the lake

Chapters 5 to 7 are called the Sermon on the Mount. Tradition places it on a hillside above the north shore of the lake, near Capernaum. The crowds from every direction (4:25) are below.

“When he was set”: teachers sat down to teach. Moses brought God's law down from a mountain. Now Jesus sits on a mountain and opens it up.

“Blessed”

μακάριοι said makarioi “happy, favoured by God”

Not lucky. Not doing well by the world's measure. Blessed means God's favour is on you, whatever your bank account says.

And look who Jesus calls blessed: the broke in spirit, the grieving, the gentle, the hungry. The world calls these men losers. Jesus says they are the ones God is pleased to help.

Down before up

  1. poor in spiritI have nothing to bring
  2. they that mournI grieve over my sin
  3. the meekI bow to God
  4. hunger and thirstI want His righteousness

Poor in spirit is a man who knows he's bankrupt before God. Every man who ever got well started there: I can't fix this myself. That isn't the end. It's the door in: “theirs is the kingdom.”

“And the publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner.”

Luke 18:13

Meek isn't weak. Moses was called “very meek” (Numbers 12:3). It's strength that has bowed to God. And the hungry man “shall be filled.”

Mercy, a clean heart, and peace

the merciful

7

Men who have been forgiven much, and forgive. They “shall obtain mercy.”

the pure in heart

8

Not double-minded. One heart, washed clean and turned toward God. They “shall see God.”

the peacemakers

9

Not just peace-keepers who avoid trouble. Men who go and make things right. Like their Father.

None of these are ways to earn heaven. They are what the kingdom looks like in a man God has changed. Jesus is describing His people, and Himself.

Eight blessings, one kingdom

the poor in spirit→theirs is the kingdom of heaven
they that mourn→shall be comforted
the meek→shall inherit the earth
they which hunger and thirst→shall be filled
the merciful→shall obtain mercy
the pure in heart→shall see God
the peacemakers→shall be called the children of God
the persecuted→theirs is the kingdom of heaven

The first and last promises match, like bookends: “theirs is.” The kingdom is theirs now, and the rest is coming.

Then Jesus turns to His disciples: “Blessed are ye.” Following Him will cost you. Some old friends will mock you for changing. You're in good company: they did the same to the prophets.

Part two

Salt and light

Verses 13–16

What a changed man is for.

Salt

What salt did

Kept and seasoned

With no fridges, salt kept meat and fish from rotting. And it gave food its taste.

“Lost his savour”

Good for nothing

Salt then was rarely pure. If the salt washed out, the rest was just dust, thrown on the path.

Jesus doesn't say try to be salt. He says you are. A believer in a rough place, a job site, a jail, a halfway house, slows the rot just by being there and staying true.

But salt only works when it touches the meat. You can't help anyone from a distance.

Light

A candle here is a small clay lamp that burned olive oil. A bushel is a basket for measuring grain. A candlestick is a lampstand. Nobody lights a lamp just to cover it.

At night you could see the lamps of the hill towns around the lake for miles. “A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid.”

Notice who gets the credit: men “glorify your Father.” We don't do good to be saved, or to be seen. We do it because we've been saved, so people look past us to Him.

Part three

Not to destroy, but to fulfil

Verses 17–20

Jesus didn't come to throw out God's law. He came to keep every bit of it.

“The law, or the prophets”

  1. Mosesthe law at Sinaiabout 1400s B.C.
  2. Davidthe psalmsabout 1000 B.C.
  3. The prophetsIsaiah to Malachiabout 800–430 B.C.
  4. Silenceno new prophetabout 400 years
  5. Jesus“to fulfil”about A.D. 28

“The law, or the prophets” was a way of saying the whole Old Testament.

Fulfil means fill it full. Jesus kept God's law perfectly, every command, His whole life. And He is what it all pointed to: the sacrifices, the promises, the prophecies. He didn't set the Old Testament aside. He completed it.

A jot and a tittle

יyod, the smallest letter: the jot
בbeth
כkaph

The jot is yod, the smallest Hebrew letter, hardly bigger than an apostrophe. A tittle is a tiny stroke, like the little corner at the bottom right that tells beth from kaph.

Jesus says not even a pen stroke of God's Word will fail. Every promise in it will be kept. You can stand your whole weight on it.

Better than the Pharisees?

The scribes copied and taught the law. The Pharisees were the strictest religious party in Israel. If anyone was good enough, people thought, they were.

The scribes and Pharisees

Their own

Earned, outward, from the outside in. Careful about rules, cold in the heart.

The kind that exceeds

A gift

Given by God to whoever believes, and worked out from the inside, by His Spirit.

“And be found in him, not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith:”

Philippians 3:9

Part four

But I say unto you

Verses 21–48

Six times Jesus takes a command they knew and follows it down into the heart.

“Ye have heard… But I say unto you”

  1. Murderand anger · 21–26
  2. Adulteryand lust · 27–30
  3. Divorce31–32
  4. Oathsand plain honesty · 33–37
  5. An eye for an eye38–42
  6. Love thy neighbourand thine enemy · 43–48

“Ye have heard that it was said”: what they heard read in the synagogue, often with the teachers' own add-ons.

“But I say unto you.” The prophets said, “Thus saith the LORD.” Jesus speaks on His own authority, as the One who gave the law. The crowds noticed: He taught “as one having authority” (7:29).

Murder starts smaller

  1. angry without a causethe judgment: the town court
  2. “Raca”the council: the Sanhedrin, the high court
  3. “Thou fool”hell fire

Raca is an Aramaic insult: empty-head, worthless. Hell fire is literally Gehenna, the valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem, where idol worshippers once burned their children (Jeremiah 7:31). Jesus used it for the place of final judgment.

Jesus traces murder back to its root: the anger you feed and the contempt that says a man is worthless. Not all anger is sin:

“Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath:”

Ephesians 4:26

Go make it right

Ifyour brother “hath ought against thee,”
→
thenleave the gift, go, and first be reconciled.

The altar was in the temple, where a man brought his offering to God. Jesus says God would rather have you fix things with your brother first. Worship and a broken relationship you could mend don't go together.

Some of us owe a lot of apologies. Make amends where you can, wisely, and where it won't hurt them more. Settle things “quickly,” before they grow. A farthing was the smallest Roman coin. Unsettled wrongs get paid down to the last cent.

“If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.”

Romans 12:18

Adultery starts in the eyes

What they had heard

The act

“Thou shalt not commit adultery” (Exodus 20:14). Many thought: if I haven't done it, I'm clean.

What Jesus says

The heart

The look that wants her is where it starts. God sees the heart, not just the hands.

Noticing a woman is not sin. Looking to lust, the second look and the replay in your head, is. Job knew it long ago:

“I made a covenant with mine eyes; why then should I think upon a maid?”

Job 31:1

Cut it off

Jesus isn't asking any man to hurt his body. A blind man can still lust. He's telling us to be ruthless with whatever feeds the sin.

  • keep it close and be carefulthat's how most of us fell
  • manage it, cut back a littlesin doesn't stay managed
  • “cut it off, and cast it from thee”the app, the number, the shortcut home past the bar

Whatever it costs to cut it off, it costs less than keeping it. And if you've already fallen here, hear this too:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Marriage is meant to last

The law of Moses had a writing of divorcement (Deuteronomy 24:1). Some teachers in Jesus' day said a man could send his wife away for almost any reason, so long as he handed her that paper.

Jesus raises the bar. Marriage is for life, and He names one exception here: fornication, sexual unfaithfulness. In that day a woman put away often had little way to live except to marry again, and Jesus holds the man who pushed her out responsible.

“Wherefore they are no more twain, but one flesh. What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder.”

Matthew 19:6

Many of us have a broken marriage behind us. This text isn't here to pile on shame or to rule on your story. Bring it to the Lord, and talk it through with your pastor. There is real forgiveness with Him.

A man of his word

Let your yes mean yes.

Men swore by heaven, by Jerusalem, by their own heads, to make a promise sound solid. Then they argued their way out on a technicality: “I didn't swear by God's name.” Jesus says all of it belongs to God. Just tell the truth.

We've all said, “I swear, this is the last time.” A changed man makes fewer promises and keeps the ones he makes. (Jesus isn't forbidding an oath in court. He Himself answered under oath before the high priest, 26:63–64.)

An eye for an eye

“Eye for eye, tooth for tooth” (Exodus 21:24) was a rule for judges. It kept punishment fair and stopped feuds from growing. It was never a license for a man to get even.

A blow on the right cheek from a right hand is a backhand: an insult more than an attack. Jesus says don't hit back to defend your pride.

The coat was the shirt-like tunic. The cloke was the heavy outer robe a poor man slept in. The law said a cloak taken in pledge had to be given back by sundown:

“If thou at all take thy neighbour’s raiment to pledge, thou shalt deliver it unto him by that the sun goeth down:”

Exodus 22:26

Jesus says: let him have even that. Your rights aren't worth more than your witness.

The second mile

A Roman soldier could make any man in an occupied land carry his gear for one mile. That's what happened to Simon of Cyrene, “compelled to bear his cross” (27:32).

The first mile is forced. The second is a gift. When you choose to go further, nobody owns you anymore: you're serving because you decided to. “Give to him that asketh thee” comes from the same free heart.

Love your enemies

The law said, “love thy neighbour as thyself” (Leviticus 19:18). It never said hate thine enemy. Men added that part.

God sends sunshine and rain on the evil man's field and the good man's field alike. That's what your Father is like. Be His son: bless, do good, and pray for the man who used you.

Jesus did it Himself. On the cross He prayed, “Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do” (Luke 23:34). And we were His enemies when He died for us.

“Perfect”

Publicans collected taxes for Rome and often skimmed extra. People saw them as traitors and crooks. Matthew, who wrote this book, was one, until Jesus called him (9:9). Even publicans love their own friends.

τέλειοι said teleioi “complete, whole, full-grown”

Here it means love that isn't lopsided, that reaches enemies too, like the Father's. No man gets there on his own. This standard sends us to Christ for His righteousness, and then His Spirit grows us toward it day by day.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Points to remember

  1. Blessed“Blessed are the poor in spirit” 5:3God's favour rests on the men who know they need Him.
  2. Salvation“Except your righteousness shall exceed…” 5:20The only righteousness good enough is the one God gives by faith in Christ.
  3. Salt and light“Ye are the light of the world” 5:14Let your good works show, so men glorify your Father.
  4. The heart“Already in his heart” 5:28Sin starts inside: anger, contempt, the look that wants. Cut off what feeds it.
  5. Making peace“First be reconciled to thy brother” 5:24Go and make it right, quickly, as far as it depends on you.
  6. Enemies“Love your enemies” 5:44Bless, do good, and pray for them, like your Father does.

Question 1 of 6

Blessed

Who does Jesus call blessed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not the strong and successful, but the poor in spirit, the grieving, the meek, the hungry for righteousness, the merciful, the pure, the peacemakers, and the persecuted. God's favour rests on men who know they need Him.

Question 2 of 6

Salvation

How can anyone's righteousness exceed the righteousness of the scribes and Pharisees?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be found in him, not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith:”

Philippians 3:9

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Answer

Not by trying harder. The only righteousness that exceeds theirs is God's own, given to whoever believes in Jesus. He took our sin, and we receive His righteousness by faith.

Question 3 of 6

Salt and light

What does Jesus want the world to see in us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Our good works, done in the open, so that people see the Father behind them and glorify Him. Like salt, we slow the rot around us by staying true. Like a lamp on a stand, we aren't meant to hide.

Question 4 of 6

The heart

Where does sin start, according to Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.”

James 1:14–15

Answer

In the heart. Murder starts with anger and contempt. Adultery starts with the look that wants. So Jesus says to deal with sin at the root, and to cut off whatever feeds it.

Question 5 of 6

Making peace

What should a man do when his brother has something against him?

What verses tell us about this?

“If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.”

Romans 12:18

Answer

Go to him first and make it right, before you bring your gift to God, and do it quickly. You can't control how he answers, but you can do your part.

Question 6 of 6

Enemies

How does Jesus tell us to treat people who hurt us?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

Love them, bless them, do them good, and pray for them. Don't hit back. God is good even to the evil, and Christ died for us while we were still sinners.
