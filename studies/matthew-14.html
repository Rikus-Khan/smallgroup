Matthew 14

It is I; be not afraid

A king's foolish oath costs John the Baptist his life. Jesus feeds five thousand men with five loaves, walks on a stormy sea, and catches Peter when he sinks.

Part one

For the oath's sake

Verses 1–12

A king who couldn't stand to look weak, a woman with a grudge, and a prophet who told the truth.

Herod the tetrarch

This is Herod Antipas, a son of the Herod who killed the babies of Bethlehem (2:16). When the old king died, about 4 BC, Rome split his kingdom among his sons. Antipas got Galilee and Perea. A tetrarch was a ruler of part of a kingdom.

He ruled from Tiberias, on the lake, for over forty years. Jesus did most of His work in Antipas' backyard.

When he hears about Jesus, his first thought is “This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead.” He had killed John, and he couldn't get away from it. A guilty conscience hears footsteps everywhere.

She left her husband, and Antipas sent away his own wife, so the two could marry.

John didn't whisper about it. He told the ruler of the land to his face: “It is not lawful for thee to have her.” God's law was plain:

“Thou shalt not uncover the nakedness of thy brother’s wife: it is thy brother’s nakedness.”

Leviticus 18:16

Herod wanted John dead, but he was afraid of the crowds who counted John a prophet. So John sat in prison. The historian Josephus says it was Machaerus, a fortress east of the Dead Sea.

Herod knew it was wrong. He was sorry. But his guests were watching, and he couldn't stand to look weak. A charger was a large serving platter. The girl was the daughter of Herodias, Salome by name according to Josephus, and her mother put her up to it.

“The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.”

Proverbs 29:25

A foolish promise isn't made right by keeping it. Plenty of us have done something we knew was wrong because of who was watching. It's never too late to say, “I was wrong,” and stop.

Part two

Give ye them to eat

Verses 13–21

Jesus goes looking for a quiet place to grieve, and finds a crowd that needs Him.

Looking for a quiet place

John's disciples buried him, then went and told Jesus. John was His kinsman (Luke 1:36) and His forerunner. Jesus got in a boat to get away and be alone. Luke says the place was near Bethsaida (Luke 9:10). A desert place means a lonely, empty one, not sand dunes.

The crowds saw the boat and walked around the shore. When He landed, they were already there.

He didn't send them off. He was moved with compassion, and He healed their sick. Even grieving, He had room for them. And when you're grieving, you can do what John's disciples did: go and tell Jesus.

the disciples

“send the multitude away”

Let them fend for themselves. They can buy victuals, food, in the villages.

Jesus

“give ye them to eat”

You do it. Then He showed them how.

“We have here but five loaves, and two fishes.” Notice the word but. They looked at what they had, then at the crowd, and it was nothing. They were right. It wasn't enough.

Jesus didn't ask them for enough. He asked them to bring what they had to Him. Your strength, your time, your clean days so far: it's not enough for what's in front of you. Bring it to Him anyway.

“But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus.”

With women and children, the crowd may have been two or three times that. Nobody went home with just a crumb. They “were filled.” And there was more left over than He started with: a basket for each of the twelve who had said it couldn't be done.

The disciples didn't make the bread. They carried it. That's our job too: to take what He gives and hand it on. The same four words come back at the Last Supper (26:26).

Part three

It is I; be not afraid

Verses 22–36

A night on the mountain, a boat fighting the wind, and a man who stepped out onto the water.

A long night

Constrained means made them. John tells us the crowd wanted to take Jesus by force and make Him king (John 6:15). He sent His disciples off before that fever could catch them, sent the crowd home, and went up the mountain alone to pray.

  1. eveningJesus alone on the mountain, prayingv. 23
  2. first watchabout 6 to 9 p.m.rowing
  3. second watchabout 9 to midnightstill rowing
  4. third watchabout midnight to 3“the wind was contrary”
  5. fourth watchabout 3 to 6 a.m.“Jesus went unto them”

The disciples rowed most of the night and got almost nowhere. They were right where He had sent them, and they were still in trouble. Obeying Jesus doesn't keep the wind away.

But He knew where they were the whole time. Mark says He saw them toiling in rowing (Mark 6:48). He came in the darkest, coldest stretch, when they were most worn out.

Walking on the sea

Grown fishermen cried out for fear. In the dark, a figure walking on the waves could only be a spirit, a ghost. They knew no man could do this. The Old Testament says only God can:

“Which alone spreadeth out the heavens, and treadeth upon the waves of the sea.”

Job 9:8

“It is I” is two words in Greek, egō eimi, literally “I am.” It's how God named Himself to Moses at the burning bush (Exodus 3:14). Jesus walks where only God walks and speaks the way God speaks.

And the first thing He says is “Be of good cheer.” Take heart. Not because the wind has stopped, because He has come.

Eyes on Jesus

Give Peter credit. Eleven men stayed in the boat. He asked, Jesus said “Come,” and he climbed over the side and walked on the water.

The wind hadn't changed. Peter's eyes had. He looked at the wind, boisterous, wild and strong, and down he went.

He had breath for three words: “Lord, save me.” And immediately Jesus caught him.

“Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is set down at the right hand of the throne of God.”

Hebrews 12:2

Lord, save me

Three words, and a hand.

Peter didn't make a speech or promise to do better. He was going under, and he cried out. That's a prayer God always hears. Plenty of men first prayed it from the bottom: a cell, a detox bed, a bathroom floor.

“O thou of little faith” isn't a slap. Jesus says it with Peter already in His grip. Little faith is still faith, and it reached for the right Man. And “wherefore didst thou doubt?” is a gentle question: Why doubt, when I was right here?

“Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.”

Isaiah 41:10

Who is this?

  • “It is a spirit”what they thought in the dark (v. 26)
  • a teacher, a healer, a prophettrue, but not enough
  • “Of a truth thou art the Son of God”what they knew when He climbed into the boat (v. 33)

This is the first time in Matthew that the disciples together call Him the Son of God, and they worshipped Him. Jesus didn't stop them. A good man would have. God's Son accepts worship.

The moment He stepped into the boat, the wind stopped. Some storms He calms with a word. This one just quit when He arrived.

The land of Gennesaret

When the night was over, they came ashore at Gennesaret, a small, rich farming plain on the northwest shore, a few miles south of Capernaum.

Word spread, and people carried their sick in from all around. They only asked to touch the hem of his garment, the tassel the Law told every Jewish man to wear on the corners of his cloak (Numbers 15:38). Like the woman in 9:20–22, they believed one touch would be enough.

And “as many as touched were made perfectly whole.” Not one was turned away. Nobody comes to Him too sick, too far gone, or too late.

Points to remember

  1. Conscience“This is John the Baptist; he is risen from the dead” 14:2Unconfessed sin doesn't stay buried. Only Jesus can clean a guilty conscience.
  2. Fear of man“Nevertheless for the oath's sake, and them which sat with him” 14:9Herod did what he knew was wrong because people were watching. It's never too late to stop.
  3. Grief“Was moved with compassion toward them” 14:14He took His grief to a quiet place, and still had room for the hurting. Take yours to Him.
  4. Little is much“Bring them hither to me” 14:18What you have isn't enough. Bring it to Him anyway, and watch what He does with it.
  5. Eyes on Jesus“When he saw the wind boisterous, he was afraid” 14:30Peter sank when he looked at the wind instead of at Jesus.
  6. Rescue“Immediately Jesus stretched forth his hand” 14:31“Lord, save me” is a prayer He always answers, and He doesn't wait.

Question 1 of 6

Conscience

What does a guilty conscience do to a man?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It haunts him. Herod killed John, and when he heard of Jesus, his first thought was that John had come back from the dead. Hiding sin doesn't make it go away. Confessing it to the Lord does, because He forgives and cleanses.

Question 2 of 6

Fear of man

Why did Herod keep a promise he was sorry he made?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because of his pride and the people watching. He had sworn in front of his guests and couldn't stand to look weak. He feared men more than God. A wrong promise isn't made right by keeping it.

Question 3 of 6

Grief

What did Jesus do when He heard John was dead?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He went away by boat to a quiet place to be alone. When the crowd followed, He didn't send them away. He was moved with compassion and healed their sick. Later He went up a mountain alone to pray (v. 23). Grief is real, and it's right to take it to God.

Question 4 of 6

Little is much

What can Jesus do with what little we bring Him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

More than enough. Five loaves and two fishes fed about five thousand men, besides women and children, and twelve baskets were left over. He doesn't ask us to have enough. He asks us to bring what we have to Him.

Question 5 of 6

Eyes on Jesus

Why did Peter begin to sink?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He took his eyes off Jesus and looked at the wind. The water was just as rough when he was walking on it. What changed was where he was looking. Keep your eyes on Jesus, not on the storm.

Question 6 of 6

Rescue

What does Jesus do when a man cries, Lord, save me?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He reaches out immediately and catches him. He doesn't wait for the man to clean up or prove himself. Then He brings him back into the boat, the wind stops, and the men worship Him as the Son of God.
