3 John 1

Three men

A friendly note to a man named Gaius, and three portraits: one who welcomes, one who bosses, and one everybody trusts.

The third letter of John

“The elder unto the wellbeloved Gaius, whom I love in the truth.”

Who
“the elder”: John the apostle, by the oldest tradition
Written from
probably Ephesus, in Asia (by long tradition)
When
about AD 85 to 95
To whom
Gaius, a believer in one of the churches there. We don't know which town. Gaius was a common name, so he may not be any Gaius we meet elsewhere in the New Testament.
Why
to thank Gaius for taking in travelling believers, and to deal with a man in the church named Diotrephes who wouldn't. It is one of the shortest books in the Bible: a note to one friend about a church problem.

Where 3 John fits

  1. about AD 30 to 33Jesus crucified and risenActs 1–2
  2. about 49The Jerusalem councilActs 15
  3. about 64Rome burns; Nero turns on the churchthe church is spread out
  4. about 70Jerusalem and the temple destroyedthe old centre is gone

There was no church building, no Bible in every pocket and no phone. The churches were small groups in homes, held together by travelling teachers and letters. When one man in one church closed his door, the whole chain was in trouble. That's what this note is about.

Part one

Gaius

Verses 1–8

A man who opened his door.

Verse 2 is a greeting

Letters of John's day often opened with a wish for good health, a bit like “hope you're well.” That's what this is: “I wish... that thou mayest prosper and be in health.” John is saying: I hope things go as well with the rest of you as they are going with your soul.

Prosper is the word behind “a prosperous journey” in Romans 1:10: to go well on the road. It isn't about money. And it's a wish, not a promise to every believer.

God can heal, and we ask Him to. But the Bible never promises a believer wealth and health. Believers get sick, lose jobs, and bury people. What can go well is the soul, and that part is the one that counts:

“For what is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul?”

Matthew 16:26

No greater joy.

John isn't proud of a number or a building. His greatest joy is hearing that people he led to Christ are walking in the truth. Not that they were perfect. Walking: still going, still on the road.

“I rejoiced greatly that I found of thy children walking in truth, as we have received a commandment from the Father.”

2 John 4

Somebody has been waiting to hear that about you: a mother, a wife, a kid, a pastor, an old friend who never gave up. They may have heard bad news about you for years. Today could be the day they hear different news.

And if you've got someone you've been helping along, this is the verse. Your biggest payday is hearing that he's still walking.

How the travelling brothers got by

These were believers who “went forth” to teach, and John says they took “nothing of the Gentiles” (here, unbelievers). They didn't want anyone to say they preached for money. So they had to count on believers to feed them, house them and send them on their way.

“Bring forward on their journey after a godly sort” means: pack them food and money and send them off as if you were sending God Himself. Gaius did, to men he didn't even know (“and to strangers”).

“Fellowhelpers to the truth”: when you help the one who carries the message, you're on the team. Who needs a meal, a ride or a spare room right now? That's your version of Gaius.

Part two

Diotrephes

Verses 9–11

A man who loved to be first.

He loved to be first

φιλοπρωτεύων said philoprōteuōn “loves to be first”

One word in Greek, and it appears only here. Preeminence is first place. John doesn't accuse Diotrephes of false teaching. He accuses him of pride and control: my church, my rules, nobody gets in without me.

“But ye shall not be so: but he that is greatest among you, let him be as the younger; and he that is chief, as he that doth serve.”

Luke 22:26

Pride is not only for preachers. It looks like never taking correction, always needing the last word, and cutting off anyone who sees you clearly. Who is allowed to tell you the truth about yourself?

How pride grows

  1. loves to be firstv. 9
  2. won't receiveJohn's letter or messengers (v. 9)
  3. talks them down“malicious words” (v. 10)
  4. shuts the dooron the brothers (v. 10)
  5. throws outthose who would help (v. 10)

“Neither as being lords over God’s heritage, but being ensamples to the flock.”

1 Peter 5:3

It never starts at the bottom. It starts with wanting to be first, and each step makes the next one easier. If you see it in yourself, deal with it at the top of the stairs: tell a brother, and take the lowest seat on purpose.

Follow what is good

he whokeeps doing good, as his way of life
→
shows he is“of God”

John isn't saying you earn God by doing good. He says what a man does shows who he follows. One bad day is not the same as a bad direction, and a man who stumbles hasn't “not seen God.”

“My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:”

1 John 2:1

So pick whom you're following, because you'll become like him. A man with a bad crowd gets a bad habit; a man with good friends gets better. Beloved, follow not that which is evil.

Part three

Demetrius

Verses 12–14

A man everyone speaks well of.

Three men, three choices

Demetrius had three witnesses: all men, the truth itself, and John. Many think he was the man carrying this letter, vouched for in advance. Nothing else is known of him, and that's enough: a good name, and nobody has to explain him.

One of these three is who you want to be, and none of them is perfect on paper. Which one are you on a bad day? Which one do you want to be on a good one?

Greet the friends by name.

Like 2 John, it ends with “face to face.” John won't squeeze it all onto a page. And his last line isn't greet everyone. It's by name. A name says: I know you, I noticed you, you're not a crowd.

“To him the porter openeth; and the sheep hear his voice: and he calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out.”

John 10:3

That's how Jesus does it. Today, say a man's name out loud, to someone who hasn't heard his own name said kindly in a while.

3 John in one line each

  1. A soul that is wellverse 2
  2. Walking in truthverses 3 and 4
  3. An open doorverses 5 to 8
  4. A proud man shuts itverses 9 and 10
  5. Follow what is goodverses 11 and 12

The whole note in one look: a good man doing a quiet thing (opening his door), a proud man doing a loud thing (closing it), and a third man who just has a good name. Real faith shows in how you treat people, especially the ones who can't pay you back.

If you've been a Diotrephes, it isn't the end of you. Pride is a sin like any other, and 1 John 1:9 covers it. The way back is to take the lowest seat and open the door.

Points to remember

  1. Prosper“I wish above all things that thou mayest prosper and be in health, even as thy soul prospereth” verse 2A friendly greeting, not a promise of health and wealth. The soul doing well is what counts most.
  2. Joy“I have no greater joy than to hear that my children walk in truth” verse 4The best news is a brother still walking. Someone is hoping to hear it about you.
  3. Open door“thou doest faithfully whatsoever thou doest to the brethren, and to strangers” verse 5Welcoming people who can't repay you is real faith in action, and it makes you a partner in the truth.
  4. Pride“Diotrephes, who loveth to have the preeminence among them” verse 9Loving to be first shuts doors and hurts people. Take the lowest seat on purpose.
  5. Follow“Beloved, follow not that which is evil, but that which is good” verse 11You become like what you follow. Pick Gaius and Demetrius, and stay out of Diotrephes's seat.

Question 1 of 5

Prosper

What is John really wishing for Gaius in verse 2?

What verses tell us about this?

“For what is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul?”

Matthew 16:26

Answer

A friendly greeting: that things go as well for Gaius in body and life as they already are for his soul. It's a wish, as in any letter, not a promise that God makes every believer rich and healthy. The soul doing well matters most.

Question 2 of 5

Joy

What gives John his greatest joy?

What verses tell us about this?

“I rejoiced greatly that I found of thy children walking in truth, as we have received a commandment from the Father.”

2 John 4

Answer

Hearing that those he led to Christ are walking in the truth. Not perfect, but walking. That is the best news a believer can bring to the people who love him.

Question 3 of 5

Open door

Why does John praise the way Gaius welcomes strangers?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I was an hungred, and ye gave me meat: I was thirsty, and ye gave me drink: I was a stranger, and ye took me in:”

Matthew 25:35

Answer

Because those brothers went out for Christ and took nothing from unbelievers, so they depended on believers to take them in. Gaius's open door made him a partner with the truth. Hospitality is real faith at work.

Question 4 of 5

Pride

What is wrong with Diotrephes?

What verses tell us about this?

“But so shall it not be among you: but whosoever will be great among you, shall be your minister: And whosoever of you will be the chiefest, shall be servant of all.”

Mark 10:43–44

Answer

Pride and control. He loved to be first, so he shut the door on the brothers and threw out anyone who disagreed. Jesus said the great one among you is the servant.

Question 5 of 5

Follow

Whose example should a believer follow?

What verses tell us about this?

“Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.”

Proverbs 27:17

Answer

Those who do good, like Gaius and Demetrius, and not those who do evil. You become like the men you follow, so choose friends who sharpen you.
