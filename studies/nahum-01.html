Nahum 1

A strong hold in the day of trouble

More than a hundred years after Nineveh repented at Jonah's preaching, it has gone back to its cruelty. God's patience is long, but it has an end, and so does the yoke Assyria laid on His people.

The book of Nahum

Who: Nahum “the Elkoshite,” from a town called Elkosh. Nobody knows today where it was. His name means comfort.

When: about 650 BC, before the exile. Assyria had already carried off the northern kingdom in 722. Judah, in the south, was still standing, but paying Assyria to be left alone.

About whom: Nineveh, the capital of Assyria, the strongest and cruelest empire on earth. For whom: Judah, who had lived in its shadow for a hundred years.

This is the same Nineveh Jonah preached to, more than a hundred years before. Back then it repented, and God spared it. Since then it went back to its old ways, and worse. Nineveh fell in 612 BC, just as Nahum said.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

The LORD revengeth

Verses 1–6

Before Nahum says one word about what will happen to Nineveh, he shows us who God is.

A heavy word, and a comfort

נַחוּם said Nachum “comfort”

A “burden” is a load, something lifted and carried. The prophets used the word for a heavy message God laid on them to deliver.

The same message is heavy for Nineveh and comfort for Judah. For a hundred years Judah had lived with a bully on its back. God's word to them was: I see it, and it will not last.

Nineveh, before and after

  1. about 760 BCJonah preaches; Nineveh repentsJonah 3
  2. 722Assyria takes Samaria and the north2 Kings 17
  3. 701Sennacherib surrounds Jerusalem; God saves it2 Kings 19
  4. about 650Nahum: Nineveh will fall
  5. 612Nineveh falls to the Babylonians and Medes

Nineveh's repentance under Jonah was real, and God spared the city. But it didn't last past that generation. Their grandsons built an empire on terror, and their own kings carved their cruelty into stone to boast about it.

One generation's turning doesn't carry the next. Every man has to answer to God for himself.

Jealous, and avenging

These words sound harsh to us, because with people they usually mean something petty. With God they don't.

Jealous

Not envy

Not wanting what someone else has. It's a husband's protective love for his wife: she is mine, and no one else gets her heart. God will not share His people with idols.

Revengeth

Not a grudge

Not a man losing his temper and getting even. It's a just Judge making wrong things right, for people who have been crushed with no one to stand up for them.

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

That's freedom for a man who's been wronged. You don't have to carry the score. God keeps it, and He will settle it.

Slow is not never

Jonah, about 760 BC, angry that God spared Nineveh

“…for I knew that thou art a gracious God, and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repentest thee of the evil.”

Jonah 4:2

Nahum, about 650 BC, a hundred years later

“The LORD is slow to anger, and great in power, and will not at all acquit the wicked…”

Nahum 1:3

Same God, same words. Jonah was angry that God was slow to anger with Nineveh. A hundred years on, God has given that city every chance. Slow to anger, but not blind, and not never.

“Acquit” means to declare a man not guilty. God won't pretend sin didn't happen. He either judges it, or (as the New Testament shows) He pays for it Himself at the cross for everyone who believes.

When the green hills wilt

  1. Bashanrich grazing land east of the Jordan, famous for fat cattle and oaks
  2. Carmelthe mountain by the sea; its name means “garden land”
  3. Lebanonthe mountains of cedar and flowers to the north

“Languisheth” means wilts, withers. Nahum names the greenest, richest places anyone in Judah knew. If those dry up when God moves, what are Nineveh's walls?

“Who can stand before his indignation?”No oneNot the sea, not the mountains, not the greatest army on earth.

That's a frightening question. But Nahum doesn't leave it there. The very next verse tells you where to stand.

Part two

A strong hold

Verses 7–11

In the middle of the storm, one verse of shelter.

The LORD is good

After six verses of storm, earthquake and fire, this. The same power that shakes mountains is a wall around the men who trust Him.

A “strong hold” was a fortress on high ground. When an army came, the people in the open fields ran to it and shut the gates.

“In the day of trouble”: He doesn't promise there won't be one. He promises a place to run when it comes.

“He knoweth them”: not just knows about them. He knows them personally, the way a man knows his own family.

Where do you run?

Every man has somewhere he runs when trouble hits: the bad news, the craving at two in the morning, the court date, the phone call. For a lot of us, it used to be a bottle, a pill, a woman, or just shutting down.

Those places promised to be a strong hold. They turned out to be the flood.

“The name of the LORD is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe.”

Proverbs 18:10

Running to Him looks like ordinary things: praying out loud in the truck, calling a brother, opening the Book, showing up here. It isn't weakness. It's going to the one place that holds.

The second time

About fifty years earlier, Sennacherib of Assyria had Jerusalem surrounded, and his spokesman mocked the LORD:

Sennacherib's man, outside JerusalemWho are they among all the gods of the countries, that have delivered their country out of mine hand, that the LORD should deliver Jerusalem out of mine hand?
2 Kings 18:35

The LORD, by NahumWhat do ye imagine against the LORD? he will make an utter end: affliction shall not rise up the second time.

That night God struck the Assyrian camp, and Sennacherib went home. Verse 11's “wicked counsellor” sounds like him. Nineveh would never get a second try.

Verse 10: tangled thorns, men too drunk to stand, dry straw that burns in a moment. Many of us know that picture from the inside: sure of ourselves, and unable to stand.

Part three

Good tidings

Verses 12–15

For a hundred years Judah wore Assyria's yoke. God says: no more.

I will break his yoke

A yoke is the wooden beam laid across an ox's neck so it can be driven. Assyria had Judah yoked: paying tribute, doing as it was told. “Though I have afflicted thee”: God admits He allowed it, to discipline His people. Now He says, enough.

Notice who breaks it. Not Judah. “I will.”

That promise was to Judah, and God kept it. The principle holds for us: there are yokes a man can't break by trying harder. God can.

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

A city that disappeared

  1. 612 BCNineveh falls and burns
  2. 401 BCA Greek army marches past the ruins. Its writer doesn't know the city's name.
  3. the 1840sDug out of grassy mounds by the Tigris

“No more of thy name be sown” means no more sons to carry the name on. Within two hundred years, people walking past the ruins didn't know what they had been. The greatest city in the world became a grave.

Its gods couldn't help. “Out of the house of thy gods will I cut off the graven image and the molten image.” Carved idols and metal idols, the work of men's hands. When the day came, they burned with everything else.

A runner on the hills

Picture the watchman on Jerusalem's wall. A runner comes over the mountains, and you can tell by the way he runs that the news is good: the enemy is finished. It's over.

Isaiah, about the LORD saving His people

“How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace…”

Isaiah 52:7

Paul, quoting Isaiah, about the gospel

“…How beautiful are the feet of them that preach the gospel of peace, and bring glad tidings of good things!”

Romans 10:15

Nahum uses Isaiah's words for Nineveh's fall. Paul uses them for the gospel: Jesus has beaten the enemy that held us, sin and death. That's the best news ever carried over a hill.

“Keep thy solemn feasts, perform thy vows.” With the enemy gone, go back to worship, and keep the promises you made to God when you were in trouble. A lot of us made a few of those.

Points to remember

  1. Who God is“The LORD is good” 1:7His jealousy is a husband's protective love, and His anger is a just Judge's. Both come from His goodness.
  2. Patience“The LORD is slow to anger” 1:3He gave Nineveh a hundred years. Slow to anger, but not never.
  3. Refuge“A strong hold in the day of trouble” 1:7Run to Him, and He knows you by name.
  4. Justice“Will not at all acquit the wicked” 1:3Evil doesn't get away with it. God settles every account.
  5. Freedom“I will break his yoke from off thee” 1:13God breaks yokes a man can't break himself.
  6. Good news“Him that bringeth good tidings” 1:15The enemy is beaten. Go back to worship, and keep your vows.

Question 1 of 6

Who God is

How can the same God be jealous and furious, and also good?

What verses tell us about this?

“For thou shalt worship no other god: for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God:”

Exodus 34:14

Answer

Because His jealousy and His anger come out of His goodness, not against it. A good husband is jealous for his wife. A good judge is angry at men who crush the weak. A God who shrugged at Nineveh's cruelty wouldn't be good. The same God who is a fire to His enemies is a strong hold to those who trust Him.

Question 2 of 6

Patience

Why does God wait so long before He judges?

What verses tell us about this?

“And God saw their works, that they turned from their evil way; and God repented of the evil, that he had said that he would do unto them; and he did it not.”

Jonah 3:10

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

Because He wants people to turn and live. He spared Nineveh when it repented under Jonah, and then waited another hundred years. His patience isn't weakness, and it isn't permission. It's room to come home. Today is part of that room.

Question 3 of 6

Refuge

Where can a man run in the day of trouble?

What verses tell us about this?

“The name of the LORD is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe.”

Proverbs 18:10

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

To the LORD. He is good, He is a strong hold, and He knows the men who trust Him. The old places we ran to promised safety and turned out to be the flood. Running to Him looks like prayer, His Word, and His people, and He doesn't turn anyone away.

Question 4 of 6

Justice

Will the wicked get away with it forever?

What verses tell us about this?

“Fret not thyself because of evildoers, neither be thou envious against the workers of iniquity. For they shall soon be cut down like the grass, and wither as the green herb.”

Psalm 37:1–2

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

Answer

No. Nineveh looked untouchable for a hundred years, and then it was gone. God keeps the books. That frees us from having to get even with the people who hurt us. It also reminds us that our own sin needs dealing with, and the only safe place for it is at the cross.

Question 5 of 6

Freedom

What does God do with the yoke on His people's neck?

What verses tell us about this?

“If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed.”

John 8:36

“Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage.”

Galatians 5:1

Answer

He breaks it. Judah couldn't throw off Assyria, but God said, “I will.” Christ does that for us with sin: He sets us free. Then Paul says, “Stand fast,” don't put the old yoke back on. Staying free means staying close to Him.

Question 6 of 6

Good news

What are the good tidings on the mountains?

What verses tell us about this?

“How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace; that bringeth good tidings of good, that publisheth salvation; that saith unto Zion, Thy God reigneth!”

Isaiah 52:7

“And how shall they preach, except they be sent? as it is written, How beautiful are the feet of them that preach the gospel of peace, and bring glad tidings of good things!”

Romans 10:15

Answer

For Judah, it was news that Nineveh was finished and the enemy would never pass through again. Paul uses the same words for the gospel: Christ has won, and peace with God is offered to anyone who believes. Every man who has heard it can carry it to the next man.
