Part one

Deep waters

Verses 1–9

What comes out of a man's mouth shows what's down inside him.

Alone, and loud

Verse 1 is one of the hardest verses in Proverbs to translate. The KJV reads it as a man setting himself apart to dig into wisdom. Many others read it as a warning: the man who cuts himself off follows his own desires and fights against all good sense.

Either way, the warning fits with verse 2. A fool doesn't want to understand. He only wants to “discover” (show off) what's in his own heart. He's talking, not learning.

Isolation and a closed mind make a dangerous pair. A man on his own, listening only to himself, is easy prey. We need each other.

“Deep waters”

A man's words come up from deep inside him. You can't always see the bottom, and what's down there takes time to draw out.

“A flowing brook”

Wisdom isn't a stagnant pond. It runs fresh and clear and keeps giving life to whoever drinks.

Verse 5 adds a warning for anyone who judges: don't play favorites with a guilty man (“accept the person”) so that an innocent man loses.

the ears the mind the memory the heart “the innermost parts”

Gossip goes down deep

A “talebearer” is a gossip, a man who carries stories. The word for “wounds” can also mean tasty bites. Gossip goes down easy, like a good snack, and then it sits deep inside and does damage.

Verses 6–7: a fool's own mouth picks his fights and sets his traps. Many of us got our worst beatings, inside and out, because of our mouths.

Brothers in the same family

The slacker

Lets it rot

Never does the work. What he has slowly falls apart.

The waster

Burns it up

Blows the paycheck the day it comes. What he has is gone fast.

Different style, same result: nothing left. Doing your work faithfully is part of following the Lord, not something separate from it.

Part two

A strong tower

Verses 10–17

One safe place is real. The other only exists in a rich man's head.

Run to the tower

When enemies came, people ran into the city's tower. God's “name” means who He really is. Running to Him means calling on Him and trusting Him.

The righteous man runs into it. He doesn't stand outside arguing with the enemy. He goes straight to God, and he's safe.

The rich man's wall exists only “in his own conceit,” in his head. A bottle, a stash, a bank account: all promise safety they can't deliver.

The name to call on

“For thou hast been a shelter for me, and a strong tower from the enemy.”

Psalm 61:3

“Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved.”

Acts 4:12

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

The New Testament tells us the name: Jesus. Whosoever means anyone. When the craving hits at 2 a.m., when the old life calls, you don't have to fight it out in the open. Call on His name, and call a brother. Run to the tower.

What comes first

↓A haughty heartcomes right before destruction
↑Humilitycomes right before honour

Pride is often the last thing a man sees before he crashes. “Haughty” means high, lifted up. Humility goes low first, and God is the one who lifts.

“Likewise, ye younger, submit yourselves unto the elder. Yea, all of you be subject one to another, and be clothed with humility: for God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble.”

1 Peter 5:5

Who can bear a wounded spirit?

A man's spirit can carry him through a lot of pain. Men have gotten through sickness, prison, and loss on grit alone. But when the spirit itself is wounded, what holds it up?

Shame, grief, abuse, the things we did and the things done to us: a lot of drinking and using is really an attempt to carry a wounded spirit. It never works.

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

The answer to verse 14's question is the Lord. He can bear it, and He invites you to bring it to Him. Verse 13 helps too: don't answer before you hear. Listen to a hurting brother all the way through.

There's always another side

The first man talks

His story sounds airtight. You're already on his side.

Then his neighbour speaks

Questions get asked. Details come out. Suddenly it looks different.

Before you take sides in a fight, hear the whole story. Verse 13 called answering too early “folly and shame.” And verse 15: a wise man's ear goes looking for knowledge.

Verse 16 is an observation, not advice: gifts open doors with powerful people. Proverbs tells it like it is, then (as in 17:23) warns where it leads.

Part three

Death and life

Verses 18–24

The tongue can kill or give life, and one Friend never leaves.

Bars on the gate

A brother you've hurt can be harder to win back than a walled city. The hurt drops like iron bars across the gate.

Some of us have family behind bars like that. Make it right where you can, be patient, and keep your side open: “forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you” (Ephesians 4:32).

Verse 18: casting “the lot” (like drawing straws) settled disputes, leaving the decision to God (16:33).

Death and life on the tongue

Death

  • “You'll never change.”
  • “You're worthless.”
  • a lie
  • a threat
  • gossip

Life

  • “I'm sorry.”
  • “I forgive you.”
  • “I'm proud of you.”
  • “Call me, day or night.”
  • “Jesus loves you.”

Your words can tear a man down or help save his life. Verse 20 says you'll “eat” the fruit of your own mouth: what you say comes back around to you. “Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying” (Ephesians 4:29).

A good thing, and a rough answer

Verse 22: a good wife is a gift, “favour of the LORD.” If you're married, she's not the enemy. Treat her like a gift from God's hand.

Verse 23: a poor man has to ask nicely; a rich man can afford to answer rudely. Proverbs isn't praising it. It's showing us how money can harden a man, and warning us not to become him.

Closer than a brother

“Sticketh” is the word used of a husband who cleaves to his wife (Genesis 2:24). Glued on. Not going anywhere.

A man who wants friends has to be friendly: show up, call, be there. Some friends come and go. But there is a Friend who sticks closer than family ever could.

Jesus said, “I have called you friends” (John 15:15). And He promised, “I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee” (Hebrews 13:5).

  3. Hurt“A wounded spirit who can bear?” 18:14You can't carry it alone, and no bottle can. Bring it to the Lord.

Question 1 of 6

Who God is

Where can a man run when trouble comes?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

To the Lord. His name, who He really is, is a strong tower, and whoever runs into it is safe. Money, pride, and anything else we hide behind is a wall that only exists in our heads. Call on the name of Jesus, and run to Him first.

Question 2 of 6

Pride

What comes before a fall, and what comes before honour?

What verses tell us about this?

“Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.”

Proverbs 16:18

“Likewise, ye younger, submit yourselves unto the elder. Yea, all of you be subject one to another, and be clothed with humility: for God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble.”

1 Peter 5:5

Answer

A proud, lifted-up heart comes right before destruction. Humility comes before honour. God resists the proud, but He gives grace to the humble. The way up starts by going low.

Question 3 of 6

Hurt

Who can carry a wounded spirit?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

Not us, not on our own. Grit can get a man through a lot, but not a wounded spirit. Jesus invites the heavy-laden to come to Him, and He gives rest. And He gives us brothers to help carry the load, and to win back the ones we've hurt.

Question 4 of 6

Listening

Why should we hear the whole story before we answer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the first story always sounds right until the other side is heard. Answering before you've listened is “folly and shame.” A wise man goes looking for the facts before he speaks or takes a side.

Question 5 of 6

Our words

How much power do our words really have?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The power of death and life. Gossip and cruel words go deep and wound. Kind, true words build a man up and can help save his life. And what we say comes back around: we eat the fruit of our own mouths.

Question 6 of 6

Friendship

What kind of friend sticks closer than a brother?

What verses tell us about this?

“Henceforth I call you not servants; for the servant knoweth not what his lord doeth: but I have called you friends; for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you.”

John 15:15

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Answer

A friend who holds on and won't let go. Jesus calls us His friends, and He promised never to leave or forsake us. Because He sticks with us, we can learn to be that kind of friend: friendly, faithful, and there when it counts.
