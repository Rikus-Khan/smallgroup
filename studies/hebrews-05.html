Hebrews 5

A priest who knows

Jesus is a high priest who prayed with strong crying and tears, and He can be gentle with men who have wandered off the road. Then the writer asks a hard question: why are you still on milk?

Chapter 4 ended with “we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities.” Now the writer slows down and explains what a high priest is, and why Jesus is the one we need.

Halfway through, he stops to warn his readers. They have been believers long enough to teach others, and they are still babies. That warning runs into chapter 6.

Part one

Taken from among men

Verses 1–6

A priest stands between God and men. The best one knows what it is to be a man.

A man in the middle

A high priest was “taken from among men” to carry their sacrifices to God and to deal gently with them: “the ignorant,” who sin without fully knowing it, and men “out of the way,” wandered off the road. He was weak too, and offered for his own sins first (v. 3).

Jesus had no sin of His own (4:15), but He was a real man, tired, tempted and hurting. He isn't harsh with the man who wandered off yesterday. He comes down the road to get him.

Called, not self-made

The old priesthood

Aaron

Chosen by God, not by a vote (Exodus 28:1). A sinner like the men he served, offering for himself first. He died, and his sons took over.

The new priesthood

Jesus

Called by the Father: “Thou art my Son” (Psalm 2:7) and “Thou art a priest for ever” (Psalm 110:4). Tempted, yet without sin. He never dies, so He never hands the job to anyone else.

Nobody could grab the high priest's job. Even Jesus “glorified not himself.” He didn't promote Himself; the Father put Him there. That means the man standing up for you before God is there by God's own appointment. The Father wants Him to help you.

Who was Melchisedec?

  1. about 2000 BCMelchizedek, king of Salem, blesses AbramGenesis 14:18–20
  2. about 1440 BCAaron made high priest at SinaiExodus 28
  3. about 1000 BCDavid: “a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek”Psalm 110:4
  4. about AD 30 to 33Jesus dies, rises, and enters heavenHebrews 4:14
  5. about AD 64 to 68Hebrews writtenHebrews 5:6

“And Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine: and he was the priest of the most high God.”

Genesis 14:18

Melchisedec (the Old Testament spells it Melchizedek) was a king and a priest in Salem, the old name of Jerusalem, centuries before Aaron. Jesus wasn't from the priestly tribe of Levi; He was from Judah. So God made Him a priest of an older, higher order. Chapter 7 tells the whole story.

Part two

In the days of his flesh

Verses 7–10

Our high priest knows the worst night of a man's life from the inside.

Gethsemane

This is Gethsemane, the night before the cross. Jesus fell on His face. Luke says His sweat fell “as it were great drops of blood” (Luke 22:44). His closest friends slept through it.

Heard, and obedient

“And he went a little further, and fell on his face, and prayed, saying, O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt.”

Matthew 26:39

“Heard in that he feared” (v. 7): feared means holy reverence, a Son's trust in His Father. God heard Him. He didn't skip the cross, but He was brought out of death: three days later the tomb was empty.

“Learned he obedience” doesn't mean He was ever disobedient. He learned, by doing it, what obeying costs when everything in a man wants out.

When you are on your face at night with nothing left but crying, you are praying to Someone who has been there. Strong crying and tears are a real prayer.

The author of eternal salvation

αἴτιος said aitios the cause, the source: the one it all comes from

“Being made perfect” means made complete for the job, not fixed from a flaw. Through the cross He became the source of a salvation that is eternal: it doesn't run out, and it doesn't wear off when you have a bad week.

“Unto all them that obey him.” The first thing He asks is to trust Him: “This is the work of God, that ye believe on him whom he hath sent” (John 6:29). Saving faith then shows itself in following Him, one day at a time.

Part three

Milk and strong meat

Verses 11–14

A baby on milk is fine. A grown man still on the bottle has a problem.

Dull of hearing

Not slow to learn. Slow to listen.

The writer wants to say “many things” about Melchisedec, but he can't yet. The problem isn't the subject; it's the hearers. Dull means sluggish, lazy in the ears. They weren't new: “for the time,” as long as they had been believers, they should have been teaching others. Instead they needed the ABCs again.

Nobody here should feel bad for being new. But a man can sit in church for years and stay a baby, because he hears and never does anything with it. The cure starts with your ears: open the Book, listen, and act on one thing.

A growth chart

Milk is “the first principles,” the basics of the gospel; strong meat is the deeper teaching of chapter 7. A babe needs milk. But a man who never moves past it stays a babe, however many years go by.

See where the bright line starts: at the bottom, today. It isn't too late. The red line turns the day you start using what you hear.

Senses exercised

γυμνάζω said gumnazō to train, like an athlete. Our word gymnasium comes from it.

Discernment is a muscle. Nobody gets strong by reading about push-ups. A bank teller learns to spot a fake bill by handling real ones all day. A man learns to spot a lie, a setup, or a bad friend by handling God's word and then using it in real decisions, again and again: “by reason of use.”

Many of these men have had their senses trained the wrong way: to find the next drink, the next hustle. The same training can run the other way. Today, take one verse and use it in one real choice. That is a rep.

Points to remember

  1. A priest who understands“Who can have compassion on the ignorant, and on them that are out of the way” 5:2He is gentle with the man who wandered off. He knows what it is to be a man.
  2. Called of God“Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec” 5:6The Father put Jesus there, for ever, to stand for you.
  3. Strong crying and tears“He had offered up prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears” 5:7Jesus has been on His face at night. Your hardest prayers are real prayers.
  4. Eternal salvation“He became the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey him” 5:9He is the source, and what He gives doesn't wear off.

Question 1 of 6

A priest who understands

Why can a high priest have compassion on men who have gone astray?

What verses tell us about this?

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.”

Hebrews 4:15

Answer

Because he is taken from among men and knows weakness himself. Jesus never sinned, but He was a real man and was tempted in every way. He is gentle with the ignorant and with men who wander off the road.

Question 2 of 6

Called of God

Who made Jesus a high priest, and after what order?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God the Father called Him, as He called Aaron; Jesus didn't promote Himself. He is a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec, the king-priest of Salem who came before Aaron.

Question 3 of 6

Strong crying and tears

What did Jesus do in the days of His flesh, when death was in front of Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“And being in an agony he prayed more earnestly: and his sweat was as it were great drops of blood falling down to the ground.”

Luke 22:44

Answer

He prayed, with strong crying and tears, to the Father who could save Him from death, and He was heard. Then He obeyed all the way to the cross. He knows the worst night of a man's life.

Question 4 of 6

Eternal salvation

What did Jesus become for all them that obey Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus answered and said unto them, This is the work of God, that ye believe on him whom he hath sent.”

John 6:29

Answer

The author, the source, of eternal salvation. It comes from Him, not from us, and it is eternal. The first obedience He asks is to believe on Him; then faith follows Him day by day.

Question 5 of 6

Milk and meat

Why were these believers still on milk when they ought to have been teachers?

What verses tell us about this?

“As newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that ye may grow thereby:”

1 Peter 2:2

Answer

They had become dull of hearing: lazy in listening. Time had passed, but they hadn't grown, because they heard and didn't use it. Milk is good for a babe; it's meant to grow him.

Question 6 of 6

Growing up

How does a man get his senses exercised to discern good and evil?

What verses tell us about this?

“But grow in grace, and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ. To him be glory both now and for ever. Amen.”

2 Peter 3:18

Answer

By reason of use: training, like an athlete. He takes in the word and uses it in real choices, again and again, until he can tell good from evil quickly. Anyone can start today, from wherever he is.
