1 Peter 1

A living hope for strangers

The man who denied Jesus three times writes to believers who are suffering for Him. His first word to them is not try harder. It is “Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.”

The first letter of Peter

“Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, to the strangers scattered throughout Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia”

Written from
“Babylon”: “The church that is at Babylon… saluteth you” (5:13). Most think this is a code name for Rome
When
about AD 62 to 64, just before Nero turned on the Christians
To whom
believers scattered across five regions of what is now Turkey, some far from any other believer
Sent with
Silvanus, “a faithful brother unto you” (5:12), who carried the letter
Why
to stand beside people who were suffering, and to remind them what they really are: God's own, with a hope that cannot die

Where 1 Peter fits

  1. about AD 30 to 33Peter denies Jesusthree times, then is put back (John 21)
  2. about AD 30 to 33PentecostPeter preaches; three thousand believe
  3. about AD 49The Jerusalem councilsaved by grace, Peter says (Acts 15)
  4. about AD 62 to 641 Peterfrom “Babylon,” to scattered believers
  5. about AD 64Rome burnsNero turns on the Christians
  6. about AD 67 to 68Peter diesin Rome, by tradition

Peter is writing near the end of his life. He is an old man, and he knows what is coming for the church. He writes to people who are not yet being put to death, but who are already being laughed at, shut out and slandered.

Notice who is comforting them: a man who knows exactly what it is to fail, and exactly what it is to be forgiven.

The man writing this letter

“And Peter went out, and wept bitterly.”

Luke 22:62

Peter said he would die for Jesus, and a few hours later he swore he had never met Him. Jesus did not drop him. A man who has been through that is the right man to write to people who are struggling. And he starts with grace.

Part one

A hope that lives

Verses 1–12

Peter writes to strangers, tired and far from home. Before he asks them for anything, he tells them what God has already given.

Strangers, and chosen

strangers: foreigners, scattered, far from home → “Elect”: known and chosen by God The word strangers means people living as outsiders in a place that is not theirs. The Christians of those five regions felt it: the neighbours who stopped talking to them, the trade guild that would not hire them, the family that thought they had lost their minds.

If you've ever walked into the room as the new man, the one with the record or the past everybody heard about, you know the feeling. Peter's answer is not fit in. It is: you belong to Someone.

All three Persons of God in one verse

  1. The Father foreknew“foreknowledge” means He knew beforehand. He is never surprised by you, and He has not been fooling Himself about who you are
  2. The Spirit sets apart“sanctification of the Spirit”: from the day you believed, the Holy Spirit began to make you His own
  3. The Son's blood covers“sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ”: the price of every sin you will ever confess

Bible teachers explain election in more than one way. What is plain here is the order: the Father knew who would trust His Son, the Spirit sets that man apart, and the blood covers him. And the offer itself is open to everyone: “whosoever believeth in him” (John 3:16).

“A lively hope”

Lively is the old word for living. This is not wishing (I hope it doesn't rain). It is a hope with a pulse, because it rests on one fact: Jesus came out of the grave.

“But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.”

1 Corinthians 15:20

Notice the words “begotten us again.” Peter says God gave us a second beginning, by mercy and not by merit. Whatever happened yesterday, today is not the end of your story.

An inheritance nobody can wreck

  1. Incorruptibleit cannot rot, break or be used up
  2. Undefiledno stain on it, and nobody's sin can dirty it
  3. Fadeth not awayit never loses its colour, like a flower that never wilts

Some men here have been left things by their families, and the best of it was a fight over who got what. Peter says there is an inheritance kept in heaven “for you,” and it is already in the safe. Nobody can steal it, and you cannot gamble or drink it away.

The inheritance is kept. So are you.

The inheritance is reserved for you (v. 4), and you are kept for it (v. 5). Kept is a soldier's word: to stand guard. God posts a guard at both ends of the road.

“By the power of God”, not the power of your grip. A man can hold on and still wear out. God doesn't.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Gold goes through the fire

A goldsmith melts the metal so the dross, the rubbish mixed in, floats to the top and gets skimmed off. He does not put gold in the fire to destroy it, but to burn out what is not gold.

Peter says your faith is “much more precious than of gold that perisheth.” Gold ends up in a drawer. Faith ends up “unto praise and honour and glory.”

He does not say the heat is nothing: “Ye are in heaviness.” You are allowed to hurt. But the fire is not God giving up on you. His eye is on the metal the whole time.

Loving a Man you have never seen.

Peter saw Jesus with his own eyes, ate with Him, failed Him, and was restored by Him. The people he writes to never did. And he says they love Him anyway, and have “joy unspeakable and full of glory.”

That is for you too. You did not see Him with your eyes, but something in you answered. That is real, and it is the Spirit's work, not a feeling you made up.

“Receiving the end of your faith, even the salvation of your souls.” Present tense. You are already receiving it.

Even the angels lean over the rail

  1. The prophets searchedthey wrote about a coming Saviour and wanted to know when
  2. The preachers announcedthey told it, “with the Holy Ghost sent down from heaven”
  3. The angels lean inthey have never needed saving, and still they want a look

Prophets longed to see this day. Angels have seen everything else and cannot take their eyes off it. And you are the man it was written for. Don't treat as ordinary what heaven watches with wonder.

Part two

Be ye holy

Verses 13–21

Because of what God has done, here is how a man lives. And here is the price that was paid to set him free from the old way.

Get your mind ready for work

A man in a long robe could not run or work until he tucked the hem into his belt. That is “gird up the loins.” Peter says to do that with your mind.

  1. Gird uptuck in the loose ends of your thinking. Get ready to move
  2. Be soberclear-headed. Not a bad word for a man in recovery
  3. Hope to the endfix your hope on the grace coming when Jesus is revealed

Most falls start in the mind a long time before they reach the hands. Today: decide ahead of time what you will do when the call comes or the old crowd shows up. Do it while you are sober, not when you are in the middle of it.

Holy means set apart

What Peter says to leave behind

“The former lusts in your ignorance”

The old appetites you followed before you knew any better: the drink, the pills, the anger, the woman who wasn't yours. You were following them because you did not know a better way.

What God calls you into

“Holy, for I am holy”

Holy means set apart for God. It is not first of all perfect, it is His. Peter is quoting what God said to Israel (Leviticus 11:44), and now says it to the church.

If you fell yesterday, you have not stopped being His. Run to the Father, not away from Him. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9). Then get back up and keep walking in the new direction.

A different kind of fear

“fear” = cringing, hiding from God → “fear” = awe, taking Him seriously The believer who calls God Father has no reason to dread Him (Romans 8:15). But a father who loves you is still a father worth respecting. “Sojourning” means living somewhere you are only passing through: this world is the road, not the home.

God “judgeth according to every man's work.” For the believer that is not a trial to see if he is saved: that was settled at the cross. It is God caring about how we live, because He loves us.

Redeemed from the empty way

“Conversation” in the King James means way of life. “Vain” means empty. Handed down, like an heirloom nobody wanted.

Some of us inherited the bottle, the fists, or a name the police already knew. You did not choose that. But Peter says the chain is broken, and it has been: “redeemed.” Bought back. Not by your best effort, but by what was paid. Your sons can start on the free side.

What it cost, and what it did not

  • silver and goldMoney cannot buy a man out of what is wrong with him. All of it perishes, and none of it gets into heaven.
  • your own promisesYou have made them. So have I. A promise made in the morning breaks by night, and we both know it.
  • the precious blood of ChristA lamb “without blemish and without spot”: the Passover lamb, whose blood on the door saved the house. Jesus gave His own life, with nothing wrong in it, in your place.

Peter does not say precious by accident. A price was paid, and God does not hand over what He paid that much for. You are worth that to Him.

It was never Plan B

  1. before the worldThe Lamb planned“foreordained before the foundation of the world”
  2. these last timesHe appeared“manifest in these last times for you”
  3. the third dayGod raised Him“and gave him glory”
  4. todayYou believe“your faith and hope might be in God”

God did not see your life go wrong and hurry to think of something. Before He made the world, He had already chosen the cost of getting you back. And He raised His Son, so you can put your whole weight on Him.

Part three

Born again of the word

Verses 22–25

A new birth, a seed that doesn't die, and a family to love.

Two kinds of seed

Your first birth

“Corruptible seed”

“All flesh is as grass, and all the glory of man as the flower of grass.” It's good-looking in May and dry by August. Every life that stops at the first birth ends in the withering.

Your second birth

“Incorruptible”: the word of God

God's own word, planted in you when you believed. “The word of the Lord endureth for ever.” It's the one thing in your life that nothing can wither, kill or take.

“Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.”

John 3:3

You cannot clean up the old seed. You need a new one. That is why this letter calls it being born again: a second beginning, with God's word at the root. That also means you grow by feeding on that word, which Peter comes to in chapter 2.

A new family to love hard.

“Fervently” is a runner's word: stretched out, straining. Peter says love one another like you mean it, not like you're being polite. Some of you lost your old friends when you got sober. That is a loss, and this is the answer to it.

Today: text or call one brother from this group before the end of the day. Not to talk about anything deep. Just: how are you holding up? That is what purified in obeying the truth looks like when it gets to a phone.

Points to remember

  1. Hope“Hath begotten us again unto a lively hope by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead” 1:3Jesus is risen, so your hope is alive. God gave you a second beginning, and it was not earned.
  2. Security“Who are kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation” 1:5God keeps you, not your grip. The inheritance is kept for you and you are kept for the inheritance.
  3. Trials“The trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire” 1:7The fire is to prove and refine the faith, not to destroy it. It is allowed to hurt, and it will end in praise.
  4. Holiness“Be ye holy; for I am holy” 1:16Holy means set apart for God. Because He is, and because you are His, leave the old lusts behind.
  5. Redeemed“Not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold… But with the precious blood of Christ” 1:18–19The empty way of life handed down by your fathers was broken by the blood of the spotless Lamb. You do not have to pass it on.
  6. New birth“Born again, not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible, by the word of God” 1:23A seed that cannot die. God's word planted in you at the new birth lasts for ever.

Question 1 of 6

Hope

What has God begotten us again unto, and by what?

What verses tell us about this?

“But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.”

1 Corinthians 15:20

Answer

A lively, living hope, by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead. Because He is alive, your story is not over.

Question 2 of 6

Security

Who keeps the believer, and what is he kept for?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Answer

God keeps him, by His own power, through faith, for a salvation ready to be revealed. A man who has put his trust in Jesus is guarded.

Question 3 of 6

Trials

Why does God let our faith be tried with fire?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So that the faith is proven real, more precious than gold, and ends in praise and honour and glory when Jesus appears. The fire is allowed to hurt, but God never leaves the crucible.

Question 4 of 6

Holiness

Why are we to be holy?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Because the One who called us is holy, and we belong to Him. Holy means set apart for God. When we fall, we go back to Him and confess, and then we keep going.

Question 5 of 6

Redeemed

What were we not redeemed with, and what were we redeemed with?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not with silver and gold, but with the precious blood of Christ, the spotless Lamb. It was planned before the world was made, and it set us free from the empty way of life handed down to us.

Question 6 of 6

New birth

What kind of seed are we born again of?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Incorruptible seed, the word of God, which lives and abides for ever. Flesh withers like grass, but what God plants in a believer never does.
