Leviticus 2

Flour, oil and frankincense

The second offering has no animal and no blood. It is bread: the work of a man's hands, given back to the LORD, with oil, frankincense and salt, and never any leaven.

Part one

Fine flour, oil and frankincense

Verses 1–3

A gift from the table, not from the herd.

Hard words in verses 1 to 3

  1. meat offeringa food offering: grain, not flesh. “Meat” used to mean food, and nothing in this chapter is flesh
  2. oblationa gift offered to God
  3. fine flourwheat ground fine and sifted, without lumps
  4. frankincensea white resin that gives a sweet smoke when it burns
  5. memorialthe handful that is burned, to stand for the whole offering
  6. remnantwhat is left over (verse 3)

Bread, not blood

The word “blood” is not in this chapter at all. Chapter 1 brought a life to the altar. Chapter 2 brings the work of a man's hands: grain he grew, ground and baked. “Meat offering” is used fifteen times, and every time it is bread.

Everything the man earned came from God. Here he gives some of it back. Solomon would later write:

“Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase:”

Proverbs 3:9

The LORD asks for the first and the best of what we make. And he is pleased with it: “a sweet savour” (verses 2 and 9).

Flour, oil, frankincense: three pictures

  1. Fine flourbread. Jesus says, “I am the bread of life” (John 6:35)
  2. Oilanointing. “The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me” (Luke 4:18)
  3. Frankincensea sweet smell going up. Christ “hath given himself for us… for a sweetsmelling savour” (Ephesians 5:2)

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

These are pictures of Christ, tied to what the New Testament says of him. The chapter itself says only what to do. A man who brought the flour, the oil and the frankincense was handing the LORD his daily bread, and the LORD was pleased with it.

Part two

The oven, the pan and the fryingpan

Verses 4–10

Whatever a man could make in his own kitchen.

Three ways to bake it

  1. bakenbaked: an old form of the word
  2. in the ovenunleavened cakes, or wafers (thin cakes) brushed with oil (verse 4)
  3. in a panon a flat plate over the fire; he breaks it in pieces and pours oil on it (verses 5 and 6)
  4. in the fryingpana deeper pan, made with oil (verse 7)

Whatever kitchen he had, whatever he could make, there was a way for him to bring it. As in chapter 1, the LORD did not leave out the man with little.

A handful for the LORD, the rest for the priests

The priest burns only a handful, “the memorial”, on the altar. What is left belongs to Aaron and his sons, and the LORD calls it “most holy”. The same words are used in verse 3 and verse 10.

The men who served at the altar ate from what was brought to it. God took care of them that way, and Paul says it still stands:

“Let him that is taught in the word communicate unto him that teacheth in all good things.”

Galatians 6:6

Your pastor and the men who teach you are looked after by the people they serve. Sharing with them is part of the offering.

Part three

No leaven, salt, and the firstfruits

Verses 11–16

What must be left out, and what must be put in.

No leaven

Leaven is what makes dough rise: yeast, or a lump of old sour dough. A little works through the whole lump. The offering was unleavened (verses 4 and 5), and no honey was allowed either. Moses does not tell us why the honey was left out.

But the New Testament tells us what leaven stands for:

“Your glorying is not good. Know ye not that a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump? Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us: Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.”

1 Corinthians 5:6–8

Leaven is a picture of sin that spreads from a little. Nothing that works through the dough could go to the altar. If one small thing is left in, ask a brother to help you see it, before it goes through the whole lump.

The salt of the covenant

What had to be left out was leaven. What had to be put in was salt. The word is in verse 13 three times: season with salt; “the salt of the covenant of thy God”; “with all thine offerings thou shalt offer salt”.

Salt keeps food from going bad. The LORD calls it the salt of the covenant: his promise to his people, which does not spoil. The offering is not complete without it.

“Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man.”

Colossians 4:6

Salt in the New Testament is how a Christian talks: kind and clear, with grace. Jesus told his disciples, “Ye are the salt of the earth” (Matthew 5:13).

The firstfruits

Firstfruits are the first of the harvest, given to God before the man takes any for himself. Verse 12 says they were not burned on the altar for a sweet savour, but verses 14 to 16 give the offering made from them: green ears of corn (grain), dried by the fire, beaten out, with oil and frankincense, and a handful for the memorial.

The first handful of the harvest showed that all of it was the LORD's. The New Testament calls Christ the firstfruits:

“But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.”

1 Corinthians 15:20

The first ears are a promise of the rest of the harvest. Because he rose, those who sleep in him will rise too. For a man who thinks his story is over, that changes everything.

Points to remember

  1. Bread, not blood“his offering shall be of fine flour” 2:1The work of a man's hands, given back to God.
  2. Oil and frankincense“he shall pour oil upon it, and put frankincense thereon” 2:1The anointing, and a sweet smell. Both point to Christ.
  3. The memorial“the remnant of the meat offering shall be Aaron’s and his sons’” 2:3A handful for God, the rest for the men who serve.
  4. No leaven“No meat offering, which ye shall bring unto the LORD, shall be made with leaven” 2:11Leaven is “malice and wickedness” (1 Corinthians 5:8).
  5. Salt“neither shalt thou suffer the salt of the covenant of thy God to be lacking” 2:13The promise that does not spoil.
  6. Firstfruits“if thou offer a meat offering of thy firstfruits unto the LORD” 2:14Christ is risen, the firstfruits (1 Corinthians 15:20).

Question 1 of 6

Bread, not blood

What was this offering made of?

What verses tell us about this?

“Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase:”

Proverbs 3:9

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

Answer

Fine flour, with oil and frankincense: bread, the work of a man's hands. No animal, no blood. It points to the one who says, “I am the bread of life.”

Question 2 of 6

Oil and frankincense

What did the man put on the flour?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor; he hath sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to preach deliverance to the captives, and recovering of sight to the blind, to set at liberty them that are bruised,”

Luke 4:18

“And walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour.”

Ephesians 5:2

Answer

Oil poured on it and frankincense laid on it: the anointing, and a sweet smell going up. Both are pictures of Christ.

Question 3 of 6

The memorial

How much of the offering was burned, and who got the rest?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A handful, the memorial, was burned. The rest belonged to Aaron and his sons, “most holy”: God fed the men who served at his altar.

Question 4 of 6

No leaven

What was never allowed in this offering, and what does Paul say leaven stands for?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and wickedness; but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth.”

1 Corinthians 5:8

Answer

Leaven, and honey. Paul says leaven is “malice and wickedness”. The answer is the unleavened bread “of sincerity and truth”.

Question 5 of 6

Salt

What did every offering need to have in it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let your speech be alway with grace, seasoned with salt, that ye may know how ye ought to answer every man.”

Colossians 4:6

Answer

Salt: “with all thine offerings thou shalt offer salt”, the salt of the covenant of God. It keeps things from going bad, and Paul says our speech should be seasoned with it, in grace.

Question 6 of 6

Firstfruits

Who is called the firstfruits in the New Testament?

What verses tell us about this?

“But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept.”

1 Corinthians 15:20

Answer

Christ. He is “become the firstfruits of them that slept”. The first handful of the harvest promises the rest, and his rising promises that all who are his will rise too.
