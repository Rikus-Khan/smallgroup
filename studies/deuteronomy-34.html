Deuteronomy 34

Moses sees the land, and dies on the mountain

From the top of Pisgah the LORD shows Moses all the land. Moses dies there, “according to the word of the LORD”, and the LORD buries him where no man knows. Israel weeps thirty days, Joshua leads on, and the five books of Moses come to their end.

Part one

“The LORD shewed him all the land”

Verses 1–4

The last climb, and everything God promised, in one look.

What Moses saw

“The top of Pisgah, that is over against Jericho”. The verses walk round the land the way a man's eye would:

North: “all the land of Gilead, unto Dan”, and all Naphtali.

West: Ephraim, Manasseh, “all the land of Judah, unto the utmost sea”, the far sea on the west.

“The south”: the dry country at the bottom of the land.

And below him, “the plain of the valley of Jericho, the city of palm trees, unto Zoar”, the little city Lot fled to (Genesis 19:22).

“This is the land which I sware”

“And the LORD appeared unto Abram, and said, Unto thy seed will I give this land: and there builded he an altar unto the LORD, who appeared unto him.”

Genesis 12:7

The promise was made to Abram in this very land. Generations later the LORD points to it and says to Moses: this is it. Every word God said to the fathers was still standing.

“I have caused thee to see it with thine eyes, but thou shalt not go over thither.” Both halves are true at once. The consequence of Meribah stood to the end (32:51). And God himself held the land up before his servant's eyes. A man can live with a door that stays shut and still be shown God's goodness.

Part two

“So Moses the servant of the LORD died there”

Verses 5–8

A death, a burial no man saw, and thirty days of tears.

“According to the word of the LORD”

Moses is given his title at his death: “the servant of the LORD”. Not the great leader, not the lawgiver. A servant. The next book opens the same way: “after the death of Moses the servant of the LORD” (Joshua 1:1).

He did not die of sickness or of age, as verse 7 will show. He died “according to the word of the LORD”: when God said, where God said. A believer's death is never an accident that slipped past God.

“Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of his saints.”

Psalm 116:15

“No man knoweth of his sepulchre”

“And he buried him”

Who buried Moses? “He”: the LORD. No priest, no son, no crowd. God himself laid his servant to rest, “in a valley in the land of Moab, over against Beth-peor”. A sepulchre is a tomb, and nobody knew where his was, “unto this day”. The Bible does not say why, and it marks no grave, so neither do we.

The New Testament adds one thing, and we say only what it says:

“Yet Michael the archangel, when contending with the devil he disputed about the body of Moses, durst not bring against him a railing accusation, but said, The Lord rebuke thee.”

Jude 1:9

An hundred and twenty years

  1. Bornin Egypthid in the ark of bulrushes (Exodus 2)
  2. 40“full forty years old”went out to his brethren, then fled to Midian (Acts 7:23)
  3. 80“fourscore years old”when he spake unto Pharaoh (Exodus 7:7)
  4. 120on mount Nebo“his eye was not dim” (34:7)

Forty years in Pharaoh's house, forty in Midian (“when forty years were expired”, Acts 7:30), forty in the wilderness. His eye was not dim, his natural force not abated: his strength had not gone down. He had told the people, “I can no more go out and come in” (31:2), and yet the LORD kept his eyes and his strength to the very end.

Some of us think our best years are spent. Moses' last forty began at eighty.

Thirty days of weeping

Israel wept for Moses thirty days, as they had wept for Aaron: “they mourned for Aaron thirty days, even all the house of Israel” (Numbers 20:29). Then “the days of weeping and mourning for Moses were ended.” Grief is right, and it is given its time. Then the people get up and go on.

“But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope.”

1 Thessalonians 4:13

Moses himself prayed it: “So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom” (Psalm 90:12, “A Prayer of Moses the man of God”). If you have lost someone, weep. Your brothers will weep with you. Then, in God's time, get up.

Part three

“There arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses”

Verses 9–12

The work goes on under Joshua, and the book points past Moses.

The work did not die with the man

We saw it done in Numbers 27: “he laid his hands upon him, and gave him a charge, as the LORD commanded by the hand of Moses” (Numbers 27:23). Now the fruit shows: Joshua is “full of the spirit of wisdom”, and the people hearken to him.

God's work does not depend on one man, not even Moses. Leaders die; the LORD does not. And the best thing an older man can do is lay his hands on a younger one and send him on.

“Whom the LORD knew face to face”

“And the LORD spake unto Moses face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend. And he turned again into the camp: but his servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man, departed not out of the tabernacle.”

Exodus 33:11

That is what set Moses apart: not the miracles first, but that the LORD knew him “face to face”, as a friend. The LORD said of him, “My servant Moses is not so, who is faithful in all mine house. With him will I speak mouth to mouth” (Numbers 12:7–8). The signs and wonders in Egypt, the mighty hand and the great terror, come second in the verse (11–12).

And notice who was in the tent when God talked with Moses: young Joshua, who “departed not out of the tabernacle”. He learned near the man who was near to God.

Not yet: the Prophet like unto me

Moses promised

“a Prophet… like unto me”

“The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken” (18:15).

The last chapter says

“there arose not a prophet since”

Not one like Moses, “since”. So the Prophet of 18:15 was still to come.

“For Moses truly said unto the fathers, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear in all things whatsoever he shall say unto you.”

Acts 3:22

Peter says it plainly: that Prophet is Jesus. Philip said, “We have found him, of whom Moses in the law, and the prophets, did write” (John 1:45).

A servant in the house, and the Son over it

“And Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant, for a testimony of those things which were to be spoken after; But Christ as a son over his own house; whose house are we, if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end.”

Hebrews 3:5–6

Moses knew God face to face. Jesus is the one whose face shows us God. Moses was a faithful servant in the house; Christ is the Son who owns it, and “whose house are we”.

“For the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ.”

John 1:17

In the land at last

The book ends with Moses buried in Moab, outside the land. But that is not the end of Moses. Centuries later, on a high mountain in the land, he stood beside the Prophet he had promised:

“And, behold, there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him.”

Matthew 17:3

“And, behold, there talked with him two men, which were Moses and Elias: Who appeared in glory, and spake of his decease which he should accomplish at Jerusalem.”

Luke 9:30–31

The man shut out for his sin at Meribah stood in the land in glory, talking with Christ about the cross. That is how far God's grace goes. What your sin cost you here is real. It is not the last word.

Looking back: Genesis to Deuteronomy

  1. GenesisCreation, the fall, the flood, the fathers“unto the land which he sware to Abraham” (50:24)
  2. ExodusOut of Egypt, the law at Sinai, the tabernacle“I bare you on eagles’ wings” (19:4)
  3. LeviticusThe offerings, the priests, holy living“Ye shall be holy” (19:2)
  4. NumbersKadesh, forty years, the march to Moabthe old generation falls, the new is counted
  5. DeuteronomyThe law again, the song, the blessing“This is the land which I sware” (34:4)

One promise runs through all five. Genesis ends with Joseph dying in Egypt: “God will surely visit you, and bring you out of this land unto the land which he sware to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob” (Genesis 50:24). Deuteronomy ends with Moses looking at that land. Moses wrote this law (31:9, 24); the account of his death was, by old Jewish and Christian tradition, added by Joshua.

“And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.”

Luke 24:27

Looking forward: Joshua

“Now after the death of Moses the servant of the LORD it came to pass, that the LORD spake unto Joshua the son of Nun, Moses’ minister, saying, Moses my servant is dead; now therefore arise, go over this Jordan, thou, and all this people, unto the land which I do give to them, even to the children of Israel.”

Joshua 1:1–2

The next book does not stay at the grave. “The people came up out of Jordan on the tenth day of the first month, and encamped in Gilgal” (Joshua 4:19).

Real world: arise, go over

  • SinOne sin at Meribah kept the greatest prophet out of the land. Sin costs, even forgiven.
  • RegretMoses did not die bitter. He blessed, he sang, he laid his hands on Joshua, and he climbed the mountain when God called.
  • GraceGod showed him the land, buried him himself, and brought him into the land with Christ.
  • HopeThe Prophet like unto Moses has come. The law came by Moses; grace and truth came by Jesus Christ.
  • Today“Arise, go over.” Take the next step God has put in front of you, and if you are an older man in the faith, put your hand on a younger one.

Question 1 of 6

The land

What did the LORD show Moses from the top of Pisgah?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All the land: Gilead to Dan, Naphtali, Ephraim, Manasseh, Judah to the utmost sea, the south, and the plain of Jericho to Zoar. It was the land God sware to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob.

Question 2 of 6

His burial

Who buried Moses, and who knows where?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD buried him in a valley in the land of Moab, over against Beth-peor, and no man knows where his grave is.

Question 3 of 6

His strength

How old was Moses, and what had not failed him?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto them, I am an hundred and twenty years old this day; I can no more go out and come in: also the LORD hath said unto me, Thou shalt not go over this Jordan.”

Deuteronomy 31:2

Answer

He was an hundred and twenty. His eye was not dim and his natural strength had not gone down. He did not go over because the LORD had said so.

Question 4 of 6

Joshua

Why was Joshua full of the spirit of wisdom?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD said unto Moses, Take thee Joshua the son of Nun, a man in whom is the spirit, and lay thine hand upon him;”

Numbers 27:18

Answer

Because Moses had laid his hands upon him, as the LORD commanded, and the LORD was with him as he had been with Moses.

Question 5 of 6

Face to face

What made Moses unlike every prophet after him?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD spake unto Moses face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend. And he turned again into the camp: but his servant Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man, departed not out of the tabernacle.”

Exodus 33:11

Answer

The LORD knew him face to face, as a man speaks to his friend, and sent him to do the signs and wonders in Egypt.

Question 6 of 6

The Prophet

Who is the Prophet like unto Moses?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken;”

Deuteronomy 18:15

“For Moses truly said unto the fathers, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear in all things whatsoever he shall say unto you.”

Acts 3:22

Answer

Jesus Christ. None arose in Israel like Moses until he came, and Peter says Moses spoke of him.
