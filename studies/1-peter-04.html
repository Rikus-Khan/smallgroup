1 Peter 4

Enough is enough

Your old friends think you have lost your mind. Peter says the years you spent on that life were enough, the end is near, and the fire you are walking through has not caught God by surprise.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Where we are in Peter's story

  1. About AD 62–64Peter writesfrom “Babylon,” which most Bible teachers take to be Rome
  2. About AD 64The fireRome burns; Nero blames the Christians
  3. About AD 67–68Peter's own endput to death in Rome, by tradition

Many think the storm was already gathering when Peter wrote: people talking, neighbours turning, trouble coming. So when he says “the fiery trial,” he is not being poetic. He knows what is coming, and a few years later it comes for him too. He is not telling the men a story from a safe place. Today's chapter is where the letter turns to face it.

Part one

Enough

Verses 1–6

Peter looks at the old life, and the friends still in it, and says what a man needs to hear: that was enough.

Arm yourself with the same mind

Christsuffered in the flesh, and would not sin to avoid it
→
so youtake up the same mind, the same decision.

“Arm yourselves” is a soldier's word: put the gear on before the fight, not during it. The mind of Christ is that when it costs something to do right, you pay it. A man who has once taken the hit rather than go back to the old thing has “ceased from sin”: sin no longer runs him.

That doesn't mean he never fails again. It means sin is no longer the master, and when he stumbles he gets up and says so to God (1 John 1:9). The goal of the rest of your life is verse 2: “the will of God,” not “the lusts of men.”

“May suffice us”

  • lasciviousness, lustsappetite with no brakes
  • excess of winedrinking to be drunk
  • revellings, banquetingsthe wild nights, the drinking parties
  • abominable idolatriesputting something else in God's place
  • enoughyou have already spent your share of years on it

Peter is a little dry about it: you have “wrought the will of the Gentiles” long enough, and you know what it was like. No lecture, and no shame. He just says you do not owe it one more year. Paul once told the Corinthians the same list, and then said, “And such were some of you.” Were.

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

They think it strange

The word “strange” is from the same family as stranger, the big word of this whole letter. Once you stop, they treat you like a foreigner. They are not mainly angry. They are surprised, and often a little hurt, because your stopping shows them something. So they talk: “speaking evil of you.”

What do I do about the old crowd?

Old friendYou don't come out anymore? One drink won't kill you.

YouI'm good. I'll catch up with you over coffee.

Old friend, laterYou think you're better than us now?

YouNo. I just can't do that anymore. I'm not mad at you.

Peter does not say hate them, and he does not say run with them to prove you're not stuck-up. He says expect the talk, and do not let it move you. Be honest that some of them may drop away, and that it hurts. Today: change one route, one phone contact or one Friday night, and tell one believer you are doing it.

Two courts

Their court

“Judged according to men in the flesh”

  • they call you strange
  • they say evil of you
  • for some, it goes as far as the sword

His court

“Live according to God in the spirit”

  • He is ready to judge, and He is fair
  • everyone gives account to Him
  • the believer lives, even after men have done their worst

Verse 6 is one of the hard ones. Most Bible teachers read it plainly: the gospel was preached to people who heard it while they were alive, and who have since died. Men judged them harshly in the body, but they live with God in the spirit. It does not teach a second chance after death (Hebrews 9:27 closes that door). It teaches that the verdict that counts is God's, not the crowd's.

Part two

Ready, and useful

Verses 7–11

The end is near. So Peter gives four jobs: stay clear-headed, love, open the door, and use what God gave you.

Near, so be ready

“At hand” means near, close enough that it could be today. Nobody knows the day, and Peter has already been told that (Acts 1:7). He doesn't use it to scare anybody. He uses it to wake people up.

  1. Soberclear in the head
  2. Watcheyes open
  3. Praytalking to Him

Sober means clear-headed and self-controlled in every way. For a man who has lost years to the bottle or the pipe, it also means what it says: Baptists have long urged believers to leave alcohol alone, and many men in recovery would say that is where their freedom started. Today: put prayer first on the list, before the first thing that can go wrong.

Love covers

Spread it

Gossip uncovers

Every failure is a story to tell. A man's worst night follows him around the room before he gets there.

Cover it

Love covers

“Fervent” means stretched, like a muscle under load: love that keeps going when it's hard. It forgives, and doesn't broadcast.

“Hatred stirreth up strifes: but love covereth all sins.”

Proverbs 10:12

Covering is not pretending nothing happened, and it does not mean hiding abuse. It means that when a brother falls, you go to him, not around him. The same love will be needed for you. Guys in this room will fall; the ones who stay are the ones who got covered.

An open door, without muttering

In Peter's day there were no hotels you'd want to stay in, and a traveling believer needed a bed. Hospitality was how the church got fed and housed. And “without grudging” means without muttering: no sighing at the door.

Many of these men came out of a life with no table at all. Now you can be the table. It doesn't take a house or a good cook. A coffee on the porch, a ride to a meeting and a seat at the cheap burger place all count. Today: ask one man who's alone to sit down with you.

Good stewards

Every man has receiveda gift he didn't earn
→
so he is a stewardmanaging it for Someone Else.
  1. Speak“as the oracles of God”: say what God said, not just what sounds good
  2. Serve“as of the ability which God giveth”: with His strength, not a show
  3. Why“that God in all things may be glorified”

A steward runs somebody else's house. The gift isn't yours to be proud of, and it isn't yours to bury. You don't have to be gifted at speaking. Someone who can fix a car, listen well, drive, cook or just show up on time is already ministering. Today: tell one man one thing he is good at, and put your own to use for him.

Part three

The fiery trial

Verses 12–19

The honest part: some suffering is for Christ and some is our own doing. God meets us in both.

Not strange, and not wasted

The word “try” is the word for testing metal. Peter already wrote about it in chapter 1, and now he comes back to it:

“That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ:”

1 Peter 1:7

Fire doesn't destroy gold. It lifts off the junk. You are not being punished for being a Christian, and you didn't take a wrong turn. This is the road Jesus walked first.

Reproach, company, glory

Verse 14Reproached

Mocked, written off, talked about, “for the name of Christ.”

Verses 13, 14Not alone

You are a “partaker” of Christ's sufferings, and the Spirit of glory and of God “resteth upon you.”

Verse 13Glory revealed

“When his glory shall be revealed, ye may be glad also with exceeding joy.”

“For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory;”

2 Corinthians 4:17

The Spirit already lives in every believer from the day he trusts Christ. “Resteth upon you” says He doesn't leave when it gets hot: He is there, and especially there, when you are reproached for Jesus.

Two kinds of trouble

“As an evildoer”

Because of what I did

  • the court date and the fine
  • the job I can't get with that record
  • the family that doesn't pick up
  • a busybody's trouble: meddling and talking

“As a Christian”

Because of whose I am

  • mocked for not drinking
  • left out by the old crowd
  • passed over for saying no to the shady job
  • “let him not be ashamed”

Peter is honest, and so should we be. A lot of what hurts a man like you comes from the old days, and that is not persecution. Don't dress it up as suffering for Jesus. Own it, take the consequence, and go the long way through it. But it doesn't mean God has gone. Forgiven men still live with the results (1 John 1:9), and God still meets them there.

It begins at the house of God

If a Father is working on His own sons firstjudgment begins at the house of God
→
then think what it meansfor those who won't obey the gospel.

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

This is not a threat to a believer. The word is closer to God's refining and correcting of His own family, as a father does with a son. There is “no condemnation” for those in Christ (Romans 8:1). “Scarcely saved” doesn't mean we barely squeak in: it means salvation was hard and costly, and it was Jesus who paid. Those He has, He keeps (John 10:28).

Put your soul in His hands

Commithand it over, like a deposit left for safekeeping
→
to a faithful Creatorwho made you and won't walk off from His work.

“And when Jesus had cried with a loud voice, he said, Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit: and having said thus, he gave up the ghost.”

Luke 23:46

“Commit” is the same word Jesus used on the cross: “into thy hands I commend my spirit.” You can say it too: with your record, with your shame, with the phone call you're dreading. And Peter adds two words that carry the weight: “in well doing.” Keep doing the next right thing while you wait. God keeps the soul, and you keep walking. Today: say it out loud once.

Points to remember

  1. Enough“the time past of our life may suffice us” 4:3You have already spent your share of years on the old life. You don't owe it one more.
  2. Strange“Wherein they think it strange that ye run not with them” 4:4When you stop, old friends are surprised and may say evil of you. Expect it, and don't let it move you.
  3. Love“charity shall cover the multitude of sins” 4:8Love forgives and doesn't broadcast. You will need it, and so will your brother.
  4. Gifts“good stewards of the manifold grace of God” 4:10What you have been given is on loan for other people. Use it.
  5. Fire“think it not strange concerning the fiery trial” 4:12The fire is a test, not an accident. Own the trouble you caused, and don't be ashamed of the trouble you get for Christ.
  6. Keeping“commit the keeping of their souls to him in well doing, as unto a faithful Creator” 4:19Hand your soul to Him, and keep doing the next right thing.

Question 1 of 6

Enough

What does Peter say about the old life, and how much of it is enough?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

The old life was lust, drunkenness, wild parties and idols, and you have already spent enough years on it. From here on you live for the will of God, not the lusts of men. It is not a lecture: it is a door that's open, and Paul says of the same list, “such were some of you.”

Question 2 of 6

Strange

Why do old friends think it strange when you stop?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

You used to run with them, and now you don't. Your stopping shows them something, so they are surprised, sometimes hurt, and they talk. Peter says expect it, and remember that everybody, them included, gives account to God, not to the crowd.

Question 3 of 6

Love

What does love do about other people's sins?

What verses tell us about this?

“Hatred stirreth up strifes: but love covereth all sins.”

Proverbs 10:12

Answer

It covers them: it forgives and doesn't broadcast. Gossip uncovers and hatred stirs up trouble, but love goes to the brother who fell and helps him up. It doesn't pretend nothing happened, and it doesn't hide abuse.

Question 4 of 6

Gifts

What are we supposed to do with what God has given us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Use it for others, as stewards of grace that belongs to God. Open the door without muttering, speak what God said, serve with the strength He gives, and let all of it point back to Him.

Question 5 of 6

Fire

Why shouldn't we be surprised by the fiery trial, and which suffering is not for Christ?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The fire is a test, and Jesus walked it first, so it is not strange. But the trouble you earned as a murderer, a thief, an evildoer or a busybody isn't suffering for Christ. Own that, and don't call it persecution. Suffering as a Christian is something to glorify God in, not hide.

Question 6 of 6

Keeping

Where do we put our souls when everything hurts?

What verses tell us about this?

“And when Jesus had cried with a loud voice, he said, Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit: and having said thus, he gave up the ghost.”

Luke 23:46

Answer

In God's hands. He is a faithful Creator: He made you and He won't walk off from His work. Hand your soul to Him the way Jesus did on the cross, and keep doing the next right thing.
