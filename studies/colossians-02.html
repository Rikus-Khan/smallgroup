Colossians 2

Complete in him

Christ is enough: the debt is paid, and no list of rules can add to Him. Paul warns the church not to be taken in by men who say otherwise.

Where we are in Colossians

Paul, a prisoner in Rome, is writing to a small church he has never visited. In chapter 1 he said who Jesus is: over everything and living in every believer. Now he warns them about teachers who say Jesus isn't enough.

Part one

Rooted in Christ

Verses 1–10

A prisoner fights in prayer for strangers, then tells them what to hold on to.

Where the treasure is

Paul has never met these people, yet he calls his prayer a “great conflict”: a struggle, like an athlete's. He fights for them and for the church at Laodicea, a few miles away.

What he wants them to know: “In whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge.” The real wisdom isn't in a secret teaching a clever man sells you. It's in Christ, and every believer already has Him.

Rooted and built up

Paul uses two pictures at once: a tree and a building. Rooted: the life goes down into Christ. Built up: the man grows up on top of it. Stablished means made firm.

Notice the one rule: “As ye have therefore received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk ye in him.” You started by receiving Him, as a gift, by faith. You keep going the same way. Nobody starts on grace and finishes on his own effort.

Roots are mostly out of sight. The daily habits that feed them are small: a few verses, a prayer, one honest word to a brother.

Don't be carried off

Spoil means to carry off as plunder, the way an army carries off captives. Philosophy here means human ideas about God that leave Christ out.

  • after the tradition of menrules and ideas handed down by people
  • after the rudiments of the worldthe ABCs: the world's basic way of thinking
  • but after Christthe only test: does it keep me near Him?

Smooth talk and a good-sounding system have carried off plenty of men who meant well. Check anything you hear against Christ and what He has already done.

Christ, plus anything

Christ+
  • more rules
  • special days
  • secret knowledge
  • worshipping angels
  • a better record
…adds nothing. He is alreadycomplete.

All the fulness of God lives in Jesus, bodily: in a real man's body. So when you have Him, you are filled full in Him. There is no second level to climb.

Part two

Dead, raised and forgiven

Verses 11–15

What Christ has already done for every man who believes, and the debt that was nailed to a cross.

A picture, not the means

The picture

Baptism: going under, and coming up

A believer is lowered under the water, like a burial, and raised up again. It shows what happened to him: the old life is buried with Christ, and a new life has begun.

What does the saving

“Through the faith of the operation of God”

God does the work, and a man receives it by faith. The water doesn't save anyone. The faith in Christ does, and baptism is the first step of obedience that says so out loud.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Paul calls it “the circumcision made without hands”: God cutting away the old life, not a ceremony. If you've been baptized, it was a good step. If you haven't yet, talk to your pastor, and know that your forgiveness never depended on it.

Dead. Alive. Forgiven.

BeforeDead in your sins

Not sick, not struggling: dead to God. A man can't fix that himself.

ThenQuickened together with him

Quickened is an old word for made alive. He raised us with Christ.

And nowAll trespasses forgiven

Not some of them. All. The ones from yesterday and the ones you're still ashamed of.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

If you fall after this, don't hide. Confess it to God, and He's faithful to forgive. Your standing doesn't change, and the way back is open.

The debt nailed to the cross

“Handwriting of ordinances” was a certificate of debt: a signed record of what a man owes. Every one of us signed one by how we lived. The law could only list what we owed. It couldn't pay it.

God “took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross.” When Jesus was crucified, the charge against Him was posted above His head. Paul says our charge went up there too.

Paid. “Blotting out” means wiped clean, like ink washed off a page. There is nothing left for anyone to collect.

The accuser has nothing left

He made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them.

Verse 15 pictures a Roman victory parade: the defeated enemies led through the streets in front of the crowds. Principalities and powers are the unseen forces that opposed God.

Their hold on us was the debt: you owe, so we have a claim on you. With the debt cancelled, their claim is gone. The voice that says “you're still guilty” is lying. Don't argue with it. Point to the cross.

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Part three

No man-made rules

Verses 16–23

Why a list of dos and don'ts can look wise and still never make a man new.

The shadow and the real thing

Paul lists the old rules: food and drink laws, festival days, the new moon, sabbath days. They pointed ahead, the way a shadow shows something is coming.

Once the person arrives, you don't keep staring at the shadow. “The body is of Christ.” Nobody gets to hold a checklist over your standing with God.

“For the law having a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with those sacrifices which they offered year by year continually make the comers thereunto perfect.”

Hebrews 10:1

What this does and doesn't say

It does say

No one's rule list decides where you stand

In Paul's day, this was the old Jewish food laws and feast days. Don't let anyone tell you that keeping them, or any list like them, makes you right with God. Christ already did.

It does not say

Anything goes

It isn't a green light for the bottle. Baptists have long urged believers to leave alcohol alone, and for a man in recovery that is simply wisdom. The point is that a fence is a tool. It never earns you anything with God.

Keep the boundaries you need. Don't let anyone, you included, treat them as what saves you. And don't judge another man by his list. Paul says “Let no man judge you,” and the same goes for how you treat the next man.

Holding the Head

Some teachers in Colosse worshipped angels and boasted about their visions. Paul says they were “not holding the Head.” A body gets its food and life through the Head, along “joints and bands”: the other believers.

Cut a hand off from the body and it withers, however religious it looks. The fix is not more rules. It's to stay joined to Christ, and joined to His people, so His life keeps reaching you.

Rules that look wise

Touch not
Taste not
Handle not

These are the sort of rules men make: “after the commandments and doctrines of men.” Paul says they have “a shew of wisdom”, and a lot of effort goes into them (“neglecting of the body”), but they are “not in any honour to the satisfying of the flesh.” In other words, they do nothing to cure what the flesh wants.

“But in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.”

Matthew 15:9

A fence is not a new heart

Rules alone

Fence the door. Nothing changes inside.

You can stay out of the bar and still be full of anger, pride and craving. Plenty of men have kept every rule on the outside and been miserable and cold inside.

Christ in you

A new man, and a new want

Christ cancels the debt, gives you His Spirit, and starts changing what you love. Then the rules (and you will still need some) become help, not a cage.

“Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage.”

Galatians 5:1

That's exactly where chapter 3 goes next: “If ye then be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above.” Not a longer list of don'ts, but a Person.

What to do with this today

  1. SinName the list you've been trying to keep. It can't save you, and it never could.
  2. RegretThe debt was nailed to the cross. Thank Him for that, out loud.
  3. GraceYou are complete in him. Nothing to add, nothing to earn.
  4. HopeDead, then raised. Today isn't the end of your story.

For the real world: pick one thing that has been making you feel you have to earn your place with God: a rule, a record, a guilty voice. Say to Jesus, “You paid for this.” Then keep the one boundary you need, and keep it as a man who is already forgiven.

Points to remember

  1. Rooted“Rooted and built up in him” 2:7Your life goes down into Christ and grows up from there. Keep walking as you began: by faith.
  2. Beware“Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit” 2:8Human ideas that leave Christ out can carry a man off. Test everything against Him.
  3. Enough“Ye are complete in him” 2:10All the fulness of God is in Christ, and you have Him. Nothing to add.
  4. Baptism“Buried with him in baptism” 2:12A picture of the old life buried and a new one raised. Faith in Christ saves, not the water.
  5. The debt“Nailing it to his cross” 2:14The record of debt against us was wiped out and nailed to the cross. Paid, and cancelled.
  6. Rules“Touch not; taste not; handle not” 2:21Rules look wise but can't change a heart. Christ cancels the debt and makes a man new.

Question 1 of 6

Rooted

What does it mean to be “rooted and built up in him”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Your life draws from Christ, like a tree from its roots, and you are built up on Him, like a house on a foundation. You began by receiving Him by faith, and you go on the same way. The treasure you need is already in Him.

Question 2 of 6

Beware

What did Paul tell the Colossians to beware of?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Smooth talk that carries a man off: human ideas, rules and traditions of men, and proud claims of visions and secret knowledge. They all leave Christ out. The test is simple: does it keep me near Christ, or move me off Him?

Question 3 of 6

Enough

Why are we “complete in him”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because all the fulness of God lives in Christ, and you are in Him. He made us alive and forgave all our trespasses. There's no second level and nothing to add, so you can stop trying to earn what He has already given.

Question 4 of 6

Baptism

What does being “buried with him in baptism” picture?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.”

Romans 6:4

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

It pictures death and new life: the old man buried with Christ, and a new man raised with Him. It is the believer's first step of obedience, and it says out loud what God has done. It isn't what saves him. Faith in Christ does.

Question 5 of 6

The debt

What happened to the record of debt that was against us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God wiped it out and nailed it to the cross of Christ. The debt is cancelled, and everything that gave the accuser a claim on you is gone. When your conscience says you still owe, point to the cross.

Question 6 of 6

Rules

Why can't rules alone change a man's heart?

What verses tell us about this?

“Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage.”

Galatians 5:1

Answer

Rules work on the outside. They look wise, but they do nothing about what the heart wants. A fence can help, and a man in recovery may need some. But only Christ cancels the debt, gives the Spirit, and makes a man new from the inside.
