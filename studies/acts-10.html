Acts 10

The door opens wide

A Roman officer prays, a Jewish fisherman has a dream he doesn't like, and God brings them into one room to show that the good news is for everybody.

Part one

Two visions

Verses 1–23

God speaks to a Roman officer in Caesarea and to a hungry fisherman on a roof in Joppa, about thirty miles apart.

A good man who still needed the gospel

  1. A centurionan officer over about a hundred Roman soldiers, in the army that occupied the land.
  2. Devoutand he feared God: not a Jew, but he worshipped the one true God.
  3. Gave much almshe gave generously to the poor, even though they weren't his own people.
  4. Prayed alwaya man who kept talking to God.

God noticed. “Thy prayers and thine alms are come up for a memorial before God.” A man reaching for God doesn't go unseen.

But a good life didn't save Cornelius. He still needed to hear about Jesus, and God sent a man to tell him:

“Who shall tell thee words, whereby thou and all thy house shall be saved.”

Acts 11:14

“Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;”

Titus 3:5

Thirty miles down the coast

Caesarea was the Roman capital of the province, a harbor town where the governor lived. Joppa was the old Jewish seaport, about thirty miles south.

The angel gave Cornelius an address: a house by the sea side, with a tanner named Simon. Cornelius didn't argue. He sent two servants and a soldier, a devout soldier, at once.

He didn't know what Peter would say. He only knew where to send. God arranges the meeting before either man understands why.

A sheet full of everything

It was about noon. Peter was hungry, lunch was still being cooked, and he fell into a trance. He saw a great sheet, like a big sail, let down by its four corners. Inside were all manner of fourfooted beasts, wild animals, reptiles, and birds.

The law of Moses had sorted animals into clean and unclean (Leviticus 11), and Peter had kept it all his life. “Rise, Peter; kill, and eat.” And Peter said, “Not so, Lord.”

Notice the two words can't go together. Not so and Lord don't fit in the same sentence. If He is your Lord, the answer is yes. And it happened thrice, three times, until it stuck.

It was never about lunch

“What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common.”

Acts 10:15

It wasn't really about lunch. Jesus had already said it: food can't make a man unclean from the inside.

“And he saith unto them, Are ye so without understanding also? Do ye not perceive, that whatsoever thing from without entereth into the man, it cannot defile him; Because it entereth not into his heart, but into the belly, and goeth out into the draught, purging all meats?”

Mark 7:18–19

The sheet was about people. In a minute, Peter will say so himself (verse 28). If God has cleansed a man, you don't get to call him dirty.

That goes for the man in your own mirror too. If you've come to Jesus, God has cleansed you. Don't call yourself common or unclean when He hasn't.

Doubting nothing

The SpiritBehold, three men seek thee. Arise therefore, and get thee down, and go with them, doubting nothing: for I have sent them.

PeterBehold, I am he whom ye seek: what is the cause wherefore ye are come? no argument

Peter was still doubting in himself what the vision meant. He didn't have the full answer. But the next step was clear: three men at the gate. So he went down to them.

He even called them in, and lodged them, Gentile strangers, as his guests overnight. He had crossed the first line before he understood it. Act on what's clear today, and God will make the rest clear.

Part two

Cornelius's house

Verses 24–33

A Jewish preacher walks into a Roman officer's house, and a full room waits to hear.

Not a man to bow to

“Stand up; I myself also am a man.”

Cornelius was an important man, and he knew an important thing was happening. He fell down at his feet, and worshipped him. Peter pulled him up at once.

This is the same Peter who once swore he didn't even know Jesus. Now he says: I'm just a man too. Good preachers and good friends point away from themselves, to Jesus.

The wall Peter walked through

a Galilean fishermana Roman officerA man from an occupied people, inside the house of a soldier from the occupying army.

Notice “without gainsaying.” He came without arguing. A vision, a Spirit's word and a knock at the gate had finished the argument.

Four days

  1. Day oneThe angel comes to Corneliusthe ninth hour, about 3 pm
  2. Day twoPeter's vision on the roofthe sixth hour, about noon
  3. Day threePeter leaves with the menand some brothers from Joppa
  4. Day fourThey enter CaesareaCornelius is waiting

Count it out: the angel on the first afternoon, the vision at noon the second day, the walk the third, and the arrival the fourth, just as Cornelius says. Nobody planned these days. God lined them up.

A room ready to listen

Cornelius had called together his kinsmen and near friends (verse 24)

He didn't keep this to himself. He invited everybody he loved. And look how they came: to hear all things that are commanded thee of God. Not just the parts they'd like.

When did you last open the Bible, or sit in a room like this, ready to hear all of it? That's a good place to start today.

Part three

Lord of all

Verses 34–43

Peter starts to preach, and learns out loud what God has been teaching him: He has no favorites, and Jesus is Lord of all.

No favorites

“God is no respecter of persons.”

A respecter of persons plays favorites: this one gets in because of who he is, that one doesn't. God isn't like that. The same Lord is rich to the Jew and the Roman, the man with a clean record and the man with a long one.

“For there is no difference between the Jew and the Greek: for the same Lord over all is rich unto all that call upon him. For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:12–13

“Accepted with him” doesn't mean good deeds earn salvation. Cornelius's prayers and gifts showed a heart reaching toward God, and God answered that reach by sending him the gospel. Peter will tell him in a minute how a man is saved.

“He is Lord of all”

“(he is Lord of all:)”

Peter slips it in as a quick aside, and it's the whole point. Jesus isn't only the Lord of the Jews. He's Lord of the Romans, of the poor, of every man in this room.

And what did the Lord of all do? He went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil. Oppressed means pressed down, held under by something stronger than they were. Some of us know what that feels like. Jesus came to lift that weight off, and God was with him.

What the witnesses saw

  1. ✓whom they slew and hanged on a treea real death, in front of many10:39
  2. ✓Him God raised up the third day, and shewed him openlyGod's own answer to the cross10:40
  3. ✓who did eat and drink with him after he rose from the deada body, a table, a meal, not a ghost story10:41
  4. ✓the Judge of quick and deadquick means living: He'll judge them all10:42

Peter was one of the witnesses, and he'd been the one who failed hardest. He talks about Jesus' death and resurrection without flinching, because he'd seen them. The man who denied Him was now vouching for Him in front of a Roman officer.

“Whosoever believeth”

Ifwhosoever believeth in him,
→
thenhe shall receive remission of sins.

Remission means forgiveness, a debt sent away. It comes through his name, and it's for whosoever. Not Jews only, and not good men only. Any man who believes in Jesus.

That's the sin and the regret. Everything you've done, however long ago and however recently, is forgiven the moment you trust Him. Not after you've cleaned up. Not after a streak of good days. Believing comes first, and the changed life follows.

Peter doesn't say earn, and he doesn't say pay. He says receive.

Peter's sermon, in six steps

  1. 34–35God has no favorites
  2. 36–38Jesus, Lord of all, did good
  3. 39He was killed on a tree
  4. 40–41God raised Him up
  5. 42He is the Judge
  6. 43Whosoever believes is forgiven

It's the gospel in a few lines: who He is, what He did, what God did about it, and what you do with it. Every point is about Jesus. Not one is about what Cornelius had to do first.

Notice where it ends: not in a rule, in a promise.

Part four

The Spirit falls

Verses 44–48

Peter doesn't get to finish. God shows who belongs.

The door opens in steps

Acts is a history in transition, and each time a new group came in, God made it plain that they truly belonged.

Jerusalem (Acts 2): the Jews, and the languages heard at Pentecost. Then Samaria (Acts 8): the Samaritans.

Caesarea (Acts 10): the first Gentiles, “on the Gentiles also.” Their tongues were the same gift of real languages as at Pentecost. Later, at Ephesus (Acts 19), came a small group who had only known John's baptism.

The Jewish believers who came with Peter were astonished. They never saw it coming. God did it so they couldn't say no.

No formula to follow

Samaria, Acts 8:15–17

Baptized, then hands, then the Spirit

“For as yet he was fallen upon none of them: only they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus.” The apostles came and laid their hands on them.

Caesarea, Acts 10:44–48

The Spirit, then baptized

While Peter yet spake. No hands, no waiting. The water came after.

Two different orders, because God wasn't following a pattern. He was showing the church, step by step, that Samaritans and Gentiles were fully in. After that, there was nothing left to prove.

“And as I began to speak, the Holy Ghost fell on them, as on us at the beginning.”

Acts 11:15

So these scenes aren't a checklist every believer has to repeat. Today the Spirit comes to live in a man the moment he believes. If you've trusted Jesus, you have Him. Nothing is missing.

Water, after

  1. 10:44They heard the word
  2. 10:44–45They believed, and the Spirit came
  3. 10:47–48They were baptized
  4. 10:48Peter stayed some days

Baptism came after. These people had already believed and received the Spirit, so the water didn't save them. It showed what had already happened. That's what baptism is: the believer's first step of obedience, a picture of a new life.

Then “prayed they him to tarry certain days”: they asked him to stay and teach them more. A new believer needs some time with people who know the Lord.

No difference

“And put no difference between us and them, purifying their hearts by faith.”

Acts 15:9

“But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.”

Ephesians 2:13

Maybe you've felt far off: too far gone, too dirty, a man with a record. A Roman officer who'd never been inside a synagogue was welcomed that day, with no extra steps. Far off and made nigh: that's the whole story of Acts 10.

Today, two things. Don't write anyone off, because God hasn't. And do one concrete thing: invite a man to hear the gospel with you, the way Cornelius did.

Points to remember

  1. Cornelius“Thy prayers and thine alms are come up for a memorial before God” 10:4God notices a man who reaches for Him, and sends him the gospel.
  2. The vision“What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common” 10:15It was never about animals. If God cleansed a man, nobody gets to call him dirty, you included.
  3. No favorites“God is no respecter of persons” 10:34The same Lord is rich to everyone who calls on Him.
  4. Jesus“He is Lord of all” 10:36He did good, died, rose, and will judge. He is Lord of every man.
  5. Forgiveness“Whosoever believeth in him shall receive remission of sins” 10:43Forgiveness is received by believing, not earned.
  6. The Spirit“The Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word” 10:44God showed the Gentiles were fully in. Now every believer has the Spirit.

Question 1 of 6

Cornelius

Who was Cornelius, and why did God take notice of him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A Roman centurion in Caesarea: a devout man who feared God, gave generously to the poor and prayed all the time. God saw his heart, sent an angel, and then sent Peter to tell him how to be saved. His good life didn't save him, but it led him to hear the gospel.

Question 2 of 6

The vision

What was Peter taught by the sheet full of animals?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That God had cleansed what Peter thought was unclean, and that applied to people too. Peter shouldn't call any man common or unclean. That's why he could go into a Gentile's house and preach the gospel to him.

Question 3 of 6

No favorites

What did Peter learn about how God treats people?

What verses tell us about this?

“For there is no difference between the Jew and the Greek: for the same Lord over all is rich unto all that call upon him.”

Romans 10:12

Answer

That God has no favorites. He isn't partial to one nation, one background or one kind of record. Anyone in any nation who fears Him and comes to Him is welcome, and the same Lord is rich to everyone who calls on Him.

Question 4 of 6

Jesus

What did Peter preach about Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That He is Lord of all. He went about doing good and healing. He was killed on a tree, and God raised Him up on the third day. The apostles saw Him alive, and one day He'll be the Judge of the living and the dead.

Question 5 of 6

Forgiveness

Who can be forgiven, and how?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

“Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;”

Titus 3:5

Answer

Whosoever believes in Jesus. Forgiveness is received through His name, by faith, and not earned by good works. It's for any man, of any past.

Question 6 of 6

The Spirit

What happened while Peter was still speaking?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The Holy Ghost fell on everyone who heard, Gentiles included. They spoke in tongues and praised God, just as the Jewish believers had. That settled it: these people belonged, so Peter had them baptized. The Spirit comes to every believer who trusts Christ.
