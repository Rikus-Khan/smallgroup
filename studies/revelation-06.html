In chapter 5 nobody was worthy to open God's sealed book, until the Lamb that was slain stepped up and took it. All heaven sang: “Worthy is the Lamb.”

Now He breaks the seals, one at a time. Before you look at the horses, look at whose hand is on the book.

How do we read the seals?

Good Bible teachers read them in different ways:

Already pastWhat the first Christians faced under Rome: war, hunger, plague, and persecution.
All through historyPatterns that repeat between Christ's first and second coming, as Jesus said: wars, famines, earthquakes (Matthew 24:6–7).
Still to comeA coming time of tribulation before Christ returns. Many Baptists read it this way, and believe the church is caught up to Christ before it begins.

We won't draw charts or set dates. What is plain is enough: the Lamb opens every seal. Nothing in this chapter happens outside His hand.

“And ye shall hear of wars and rumours of wars: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet.”

Matthew 24:6

The white horse: conquest

A rider with a bow, given a crown (the Greek word is a victor's wreath), out to win. Many teachers see conquest: an empire on the march. Others see a false christ who looks like the real one on His white horse in 19:11.

But notice who says “Come”: one of the beasts from around the throne. And the crown was “given unto him.” Even the conqueror rides on permission.

The red horse: war

Red like blood. He takes peace from the earth, and men “kill one another.” Not an army from outside: neighbor against neighbor.

Every man here has seen what this horse leaves behind: on a street, in a cell, in a house. Sin always ends in somebody bleeding. That's why the world needs more than a better plan. It needs the Lamb.

The black horse: hunger

War is followed by famine. The rider holds balances, scales for weighing out food a little at a time, the way you do when there isn't enough.

Then a voice from among the beasts sets the price, and a limit: “hurt not the oil and the wine.” Many teachers read that as God saying this far, and no farther. The famine has a fence around it.

The pale horse: death

Pale is the Greek chloros: a sick yellow-green, the color of a corpse. His name is Death, and Hell, the grave, follows behind to collect.

Even he gets a fourth part, not the whole earth. And the Man who broke the seal holds the keys:

“I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death.”

Revelation 1:18

Four riders, one hand

  • “a crown was given unto him”6:2, the white horse
  • “power was given to him… there was given unto him a great sword”6:4, the red horse
  • “see thou hurt not the oil and the wine”6:6, the black horse is fenced in
  • “power was given unto them over the fourth part”6:8, the pale horse is limited

Sword, famine, beasts, and death are the same four judgments God named through Ezekiel long before (Ezekiel 14:21). Nothing in this chapter is new to God, and nothing gets loose on its own. When the news is bad, and when your own life feels like the pale horse came through, the Lamb still holds the book.

Under the altar

At the temple, the blood of a sacrifice was poured out at the bottom of the altar. These men and women poured out their lives the same way, killed “for the word of God, and for the testimony which they held.”

John is writing to churches where this was already happening: Antipas had been killed at Pergamos (2:13). Christians still die for Jesus in many countries. Their lives aren't wasted. They are under the altar, close to God, and He knows every name.

“How long, O Lord?”

It isn't unbelief to ask. These are saints in heaven, and they ask it out loud. David asked it too: “How long wilt thou forget me, O LORD?” (Psalm 13:1).

They don't take revenge. They hand it to the Judge who is “holy and true.” If someone did you wrong, that's your way out of the anger too:

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

A robe and a rest

What they're given

White robes

Not earned by dying. Given, “unto every one of them.” White is the color of a clean record and a victory.

What they're told

Rest a little longer

God hasn't forgotten. He has a number and a time, and He's keeping track. Their wait is a rest, not a punishment.

Where does a white robe come from? The next chapter says:

“These are they which came out of great tribulation, and have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb.”

Revelation 7:14

The sky comes apart

The prophets used these same words for the day of the LORD: “The sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood” (Joel 2:31).

The stars fall “as a fig tree casteth her untimely figs”, like the late, unripe figs a storm knocks off a tree.

And the sky rolls up “as a scroll” (Isaiah 34:4). Real signs in the sky, or pictures of the world's powers falling? Teachers differ. Either way, everything men lean on comes loose.

The wrath of the Lamb

An angry Lamb: the Jesus who was slain for sinners will judge those who refuse Him.

Men beg the rocks to hide them, but the mountains were just “moved out of their places” (6:14). No rock can hide a man from God. The only shelter from the wrath of the Lamb is the Lamb Himself.

Question 1 of 6

The seals

Who opens the seals, and why does that matter?

What verses tell us about this?

“Are not two sparrows sold for a farthing? and one of them shall not fall on the ground without your Father.”

Matthew 10:29

Answer

The Lamb, Jesus, who was slain for us. That matters because nothing in this chapter breaks loose on its own. The One who loves us most holds the book and decides when each seal opens.

Question 2 of 6

Horsemen

What do the four horsemen bring on the earth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Conquest (the white horse), war (the red), famine (the black), and death with the grave behind it (the pale). Each rider is "given" his power, and each is limited: a fence around the famine, a fourth part for death.

Question 3 of 6

Martyrs

What do the souls under the altar ask for, and what are they given?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They ask how long until God judges and avenges their blood. They are given white robes and told to rest a little longer. God sees them, comforts them, and has a set time for justice.

Question 4 of 6

The sixth seal

What happens when the sixth seal is opened?

What verses tell us about this?

“The sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, before the great and the terrible day of the LORD come.”

Joel 2:31

Answer

A great earthquake; the sun goes black and the moon turns to blood; the stars fall like figs; the sky rolls up like a scroll; every mountain and island moves. It is the great day the prophets warned of.

Question 5 of 6

Hiding

Where do men try to hide from the wrath of the Lamb?

What verses tell us about this?

“Kiss the Son, lest he be angry, and ye perish from the way, when his wrath is kindled but a little. Blessed are all they that put their trust in him.”

Psalm 2:12

Answer

In the dens and the rocks of the mountains, begging them to fall on them. But no rock can hide a man from God. The only safe place is in the Son Himself: blessed are all who trust in Him.

Question 6 of 6

Standing

Who shall be able to stand in the great day of His wrath?

What verses tell us about this?

“Who art thou that judgest another man’s servant? to his own master he standeth or falleth. Yea, he shall be holden up: for God is able to make him stand.”

Romans 14:4

Answer

Not the strong, the rich, or the good enough. Those washed in the blood of the Lamb will stand, because God holds them up. Chapter 7 shows them: a great multitude standing before the throne in white robes.
