Nahum 2

The lion's den left empty

Nahum watches Nineveh's fall as if it were already happening: red shields, racing chariots, the river gates thrown open, and the richest city on earth carried off.

Part one

Keep the munition

Verses 1–2

Nahum, about 650 BC, sees Nineveh's last days as if he were standing on the wall.

Get ready all you like

God speaks to Nineveh like a sergeant shouting orders on the wall. It's a taunt. Every order is good advice, and none of it will help.

  • keep the munitionguard the fortress
  • watch the waypost lookouts on the roads
  • make thy loins strongbrace yourselves
  • fortify thy power mightilycall up every man you have
  • “I am against thee, saith the LORD of hosts”verse 13: the only thing that matters

“He that dasheth in pieces” is the army God is sending, the Babylonians and the Medes. Nineveh had smashed nations for two hundred years. Now the hammer comes for it.

The emptiers emptied

Jacob and Israel are two names for the same man, and here for all his people, south and north. Assyria had stripped them like thieves cutting down a vineyard.

Assyria→emptied→God's people“The emptiers have emptied them out, and marred their vine branches.” (verse 2)
God→empties→Nineveh“She is empty, and void, and waste.” (verse 10)

God noticed every vine they cut. The reason Nineveh falls is “for the LORD”: He is taking up His people's cause. Nobody who belongs to Him is forgotten when they've been robbed.

Part two

The gates of the rivers

Verses 3–8

The siege, told like a man who can already see it.

Red shields at the gate

  1. Red shieldsarmies of that day often dyed their leather shields red
  2. Scarletthe soldiers' cloaks, the color of blood
  3. Like torchesbronze and iron on the chariots flashing in the sun
  4. Like lightningracing through the streets, crashing into each other

In 612 BC the Babylonians, the Medes and their allies came against Nineveh together. An old Babylonian record says the siege lasted about three months, and then the city fell.

Notice the tense. Nahum wrote this decades before it happened, but he writes as if he's watching. When God says a thing, it's as good as done.

The river gates

Nineveh's walls ran about seven and a half miles around, with fifteen gates. The Tigris guarded one side, and a smaller river ran right through the city, through gates in the wall built to let the water in.

The enemy came. The defenders ran for the wall, stumbling as they went (verse 5).

Then “the gates of the rivers shall be opened.” A Greek historian, writing later, said a flooded river broke down part of the wall. The palace was built of mud brick, and water “dissolved” it.

The thing that made the city strong was the thing that brought it down.

Draining like a pool

Nineveh had been full of people for centuries, like a pool full of water. Now the dam is broken and they pour out. The officers shout “Stand! Stand!” and nobody even turns around.

Verse 7: Huzzab is probably the queen, or a name for the city itself; no one is sure. Her maids moan like doves and beat their chests like drums (“tabering”).

Strength that depends on people standing with you is only as good as the moment they run. The LORD is the one who doesn't run.

Part three

Where is the dwelling of the lions?

Verses 9–13

The plunderer is plundered, and the lion's den is empty.

No end, then empty

Verse 9

No end

“None end of the store and glory.” Two hundred years of plunder from every nation around.

Verse 10

Empty

Carried off by the enemy. The men who guarded it can't stand for fear.

בּוּקָה וּמְבוּקָה וּמְבֻלָּקָה said buqah u-mevuqah u-mevullaqah “empty, and void, and waste”

In Hebrew the three words sound alike, one hollow echo after another. That's what was left of all that gold.

“Riches profit not in the day of wrath: but righteousness delivereth from death.”

Proverbs 11:4

The lion's den

Assyria's kings liked to compare themselves to lions. They hunted lions for sport and carved the hunts on their palace walls. Those carvings were dug up at Nineveh and can be seen in a museum in London today.

“None made them afraid.” For two hundred years nobody could touch them. Now God asks, Where is it? The den is empty. Pride always thinks it's the one thing that will last.

Behold, I am against thee.

“The LORD of hosts” means the LORD of armies, the commander of every power in heaven and earth.

Judah hears a messenger

“Behold upon the mountains the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace!”

Nahum 1:15

Nineveh's messengers go silent

“…and the voice of thy messengers shall no more be heard.”

Nahum 2:13

Assyria's messengers carried threats to every nation, like the man who mocked the LORD outside Jerusalem. Those voices stop. The voice that keeps going is the one with good news.

If God against you is the worst thing that can happen, God for you is the best. And through Christ, He is for every man who trusts Him.

Points to remember

  1. God's power“Behold, I am against thee, saith the LORD of hosts” 2:13No wall, river or army holds when God says that.
  2. Restoration“The emptiers have emptied them out” 2:2God sees what was taken from His people, and He takes up their cause.
  3. Riches“She is empty, and void, and waste” 2:10Endless treasure, and then nothing.
  4. Pride“Where is the dwelling of the lions?” 2:11The proud lion's den ends up empty.
  5. Courage“If God be for us, who can be against us?” Romans 8:31The God who was against Nineveh is for those who trust Him.

Question 1 of 5

God's power

Can any wall stand when God says, “I am against thee”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it: except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain.”

Psalm 127:1

Answer

No. Nineveh had huge walls, a river for a moat, and the best army in the world. God opened the river gates and it was over. Whatever a man builds his life on, if God isn't keeping it, the watchman is staying up for nothing.

Question 2 of 5

Restoration

What is God doing for His own people while He judges their enemy?

What verses tell us about this?

“For now will I break his yoke from off thee, and will burst thy bonds in sunder.”

Nahum 1:13

Answer

He is taking up their cause. Nineveh falls “for the LORD” and for His people, who had been stripped like a vineyard. God noticed every branch that was cut. He broke the yoke off Judah, and He is still a God who sees what was taken from His people and makes it right.

Question 3 of 5

Riches

Can treasure keep a man safe?

What verses tell us about this?

“Riches profit not in the day of wrath: but righteousness delivereth from death.”

Proverbs 11:4

“Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy;”

1 Timothy 6:17

Answer

No. Nineveh had treasure with “none end,” and in one day it was “empty, and void, and waste.” Money can be stolen, spent, or left behind. Paul says trust the living God instead. He's the only treasure nobody can carry off.

Question 4 of 5

Pride

What happens to the lion that preys on everyone?

What verses tell us about this?

“Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.”

Proverbs 16:18

Answer

God brings him down. Nineveh lived like a lion: take what you want, and nobody can touch you. Some of us lived that way too, in a smaller way. God doesn't let pride have the last word. It's far better to humble ourselves before Him than to wait for Him to empty the den.

Question 5 of 5

Courage

Who can stand against the man God is for?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is good, a strong hold in the day of trouble; and he knoweth them that trust in him.”

Nahum 1:7

“What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us?”

Romans 8:31

Answer

Nobody. The same God who said “I am against thee” to Nineveh says to every believer, through Christ, “I am for you.” That doesn't mean no trouble. It means no trouble, no enemy and no past can pull you out of His hand.
