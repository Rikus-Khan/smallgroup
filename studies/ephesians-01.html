Ephesians 1

Every spiritual blessing

A prisoner in Rome writes to a church he loves, and starts by counting what they already have in Christ. Then he prays they'd see it.

The letter to the Ephesians

“Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, to the saints which are at Ephesus, and to the faithful in Christ Jesus”

Written from
Rome, where Paul was a prisoner under guard, waiting for his trial: “the prisoner of Jesus Christ” (3:1)
When
about AD 60 to 62
To whom
the believers at Ephesus, the great port city of Asia, where Paul had lived and taught for about three years
Why
to show them how rich they are in Christ, and then how to live like it. Tychicus carried it to them (6:21).

Paul's three years at Ephesus

“And this continued by the space of two years; so that all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, both Jews and Greeks.”

Acts 19:10

A city of idols and magic. When men believed, they “brought their books together, and burned them before all men” (Acts 19:19): the tools of the old life, gone.

Later Paul called the elders to Miletus to say goodbye: “three years I ceased not to warn every one night and day with tears” (Acts 20:31). These were his people.

  3. about 57Arrested in JerusalemActs 21

Paul wrote four letters from that house arrest in Rome. Many call them the prison letters. He was chained to a soldier, but people could come and go (Acts 28:30).

Notice what a chained man writes about: not his rights, not his bad luck. He writes about how rich these believers are. Your circumstances don't get the last word on what you have in Christ.

Part one

Chosen in him

Verses 1–6

Before Paul asks anything of them, he starts praising God for what they already have.

Saints, in Ephesus

men who burned their books of magic → saints Saints means holy ones, set apart for God. Not the best behaved: the ones who belong to Him.

Paul doesn't write to “the ones who've got it together at Ephesus.” He writes to former idol-makers, magicians and drunks, and calls them saints and “the faithful”, the believing ones. That's what God calls you too, on your worst morning.

Watch for two little words

1×12  3×14×15  6×17×18 9  10×211×112×1 13×214 

In Christ, in him, in whom, in the beloved: eleven times in fourteen verses. Every blessing Paul names is found in one place: in Christ. Not in your record, not in your feelings.

“Hath blessed” is already done. “Heavenly places” means the unseen world where Christ reigns now. Your bank account may be empty today; your account in Christ isn't.

Chosen “in him”

Read it slowly: “chosen us in him.” Before the world began, God chose a people in Christ, and decided what they would be: “holy and without blame,” adopted as sons.

How does a man get in? “In whom ye also trusted… after that ye believed” (v. 13). The door is faith, and it's open to “whosoever” (John 3:16). Whoever is in Christ is in the Chosen One, and all of this is his.

Predestinated: to what?

Bible teachers have argued over these verses for centuries. Here is what's plain. Predestinate means to set the destination ahead of time. Look at what God set:

  • some men picked for heaven, the rest made for hellPaul never says it. God “will have all men to be saved” (1 Timothy 2:4).
  • so it doesn't matter whether I believeIt's “after that ye believed” that a man is sealed (v. 13).
  • where every man in Christ is headedholy, blameless, adopted as God's own sons (vv. 4–5), made “conformed to the image of his Son” (Romans 8:29)

Think of a bus whose last stop was fixed before anyone got on. The door is open, and whoever gets on goes where the bus goes. Christ is God's Chosen One, “mine elect, in whom my soul delighteth” (Isaiah 42:1). Get in Him, and you go where He goes.

Adopted, and accepted

a slave to sin, with no family claim → a son, with the Father's name In Rome an adopted son took his new father's name and became his full heir. His old family had no more claim on him.

“For ye have not received the spirit of bondage again to fear; but ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father.”

Romans 8:15

“The beloved” is Jesus. God accepts you the way He accepts His own Son. Not tolerated, not on probation: accepted. If your own father never said he was proud of you, read verse 6 again.

Part two

Redeemed and sealed

Verses 7–14

What the Son paid for, and what the Spirit guarantees.

Bought back

Redemption is buying a slave's freedom by paying the price. The price here: “his blood.” And what it buys: “the forgiveness of sins.” Not some of them. Sins.

How much forgiveness? “According to the riches of his grace.” Not measured by how big your pile is, but by how rich He is. A billionaire giving out of his riches might write a small check. According to his riches, it's a big one.

Where history is going

A mystery in the Bible is a plan God kept secret for ages and has now told. Here it is: when the time is full, everything broken and scattered, in heaven and on earth, comes back together under one Head, Christ.

The world isn't spinning out of control. And if your life feels like pieces on the floor, it's the same God with the same plan. He gathers.

Sealed

Follow the order: heard, trusted, sealed. The moment a man believes, God puts His own Holy Spirit in him as a seal. “Who first trusted” (v. 12) were Jews like Paul; “ye also”, Gentiles. No second blessing to chase: every believer has Him.

“And grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption.”

Ephesians 4:30

The earnest

ἀρραβών said arrabōn a down payment that promises the rest
Paid nowthe Holy Spirit in you
The rest is coming“until the redemption of the purchased possession”: the day He comes for what He bought

Nobody puts money down on a house and then walks away from it. God has already put His own Spirit down on you. You are “the purchased possession”, and He will come back for what He paid for. That's why a believer is secure.

In Paul's Greek, verses 3 to 14 are one long sentence. He can't stop. It moves through the Father, the Son and the Spirit, and each part ends the same way:

Part three

That ye may know

Verses 15–23

They already have it all. Paul prays they'd see it.

A prisoner praying for free men

Up

“Your faith in the Lord Jesus”

Trusting Him is where it starts.

Out

“Love unto all the saints”

Real faith shows up as love for other believers, all of them, the hard ones too.

Paul is chained to a guard, and he spends his time thanking God for other people and naming them in prayer. Try it today: name one man in this group to God, and thank God for him.

Eyes opened

Paul doesn't pray they'd get more. He prays they'd see what they've got. Three things:

  1. The hope of His callingwhere He is taking you
  2. His inheritance in the saintsyou are His treasure
  3. His power to us-wardwhat He's doing in you now

Read the middle one again: “his inheritance in the saints.” Not just what you get from God; you are what God gets. He counts you His riches.

The power that emptied a grave

  1. “raised him from the dead”v. 20
  2. “set him at his own right hand”v. 20
  3. “far above all principality, and power”v. 21
  4. “head over all things to the church”v. 22

Paul uses four different Greek words for power in verse 19, because one isn't enough. And it's pointed “to us-ward”: toward us “who believe.”

“But if the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you, he that raised up Christ from the dead shall also quicken your mortal bodies by his Spirit that dwelleth in you.”

Romans 8:11

Head and body

Christ is“the head over all things”
→
and the church is“his body”

Principality, power, might, dominion: every ruler there is, seen and unseen, including whatever has had a hold on you. All of it is under His feet.

And the One who rules it all is joined to His people like a head to a body. A head doesn't forget its own hand. You are not a spare part. The body needs you, and you need the body: don't try to follow Jesus alone.

Where Paul is going next

Ephesians 1:20He raised Christ

“when he raised him from the dead, and set him at his own right hand.”

Ephesians 2:1Dead men

“And you hath he quickened, who were dead in trespasses and sins.”

Ephesians 2:6Raised with Him

“And hath raised us up together, and made us sit together in heavenly places.”

The power Paul prays they'd see is the very power that comes for dead men in chapter 2. Chapter 1 is the riches. Chapter 2 is how men like us ever got them.

Points to remember

  1. Every blessing“All spiritual blessings in heavenly places in Christ” 1:3Every blessing God gives is found in Christ, and the man in Christ already has them.
  2. Chosen“He hath chosen us in him” 1:4God chose a people in Christ and set where they're headed: holy, adopted sons. Faith puts a man in Him.
  3. Forgiven“The forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of his grace” 1:7Christ's blood bought us back, and forgiveness is measured by His riches, not by our record.
  4. Sealed“After that ye believed, ye were sealed” 1:13The Spirit seals every believer the moment he believes, a down payment on all that's coming.
  5. Eyes opened“The eyes of your understanding being enlightened” 1:18We need to see our hope, God's treasure in His people, and His power.
  6. Power“The exceeding greatness of his power to us-ward” 1:19The power that raised Jesus from the dead is at work for every believer.

Question 1 of 6

Every blessing

Where does a believer find every spiritual blessing?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In Christ. Paul says it eleven times in fourteen verses. Chosen, adopted, accepted, forgiven, sealed: all of it is found in Him, and God has already given it. A believer isn't working toward these blessings. He has them.

Question 2 of 6

Chosen

What did God choose and plan before the world began?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

God chose a people in Christ and settled what they would become: holy, without blame, His adopted sons. He didn't pick some men for hell. Faith is the door into Christ, and it's open to whosoever.

Question 3 of 6

Forgiven

What do we have through Christ's blood, and how much of it?

What verses tell us about this?

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

Answer

Redemption, the forgiveness of sins. Jesus paid for our freedom with His own blood. And it's measured by the riches of God's grace, not by the size of what we did. Nothing you carry outweighs it.

Question 4 of 6

Sealed

When is a believer sealed with the Holy Spirit, and what does the seal promise?

What verses tell us about this?

“Who hath also sealed us, and given the earnest of the Spirit in our hearts.”

2 Corinthians 1:22

Answer

The moment he believes; there's no second blessing to wait for. The seal says he belongs to God and is kept safe. The Spirit is the down payment, God's promise that the rest is coming.

Question 5 of 6

Eyes opened

What did Paul pray these believers would see?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Elisha prayed, and said, LORD, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see. And the LORD opened the eyes of the young man; and he saw: and, behold, the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha.”

2 Kings 6:17

Answer

The hope of God's calling, the riches of His inheritance in the saints, and the greatness of His power toward believers. They already had it all; they needed eyes to see it. Pray that for the man next to you.

Question 6 of 6

Power

What power is at work for those who believe?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The power that raised Jesus from the dead and set Him above every ruler there is. It's pointed toward us who believe. Whatever has a hold on you is under His feet.
