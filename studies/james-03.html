James 3

The little fire

Part one

Be not many masters

Verses 1–2

Teachers get judged more strictly, and James puts himself in the same boat: “we offend all.”

Handle with care

A man who teachesspeaks for God, and people trust him
→
so he facesthe greater condemnation

A master here is a teacher. Anyone who tells others what God says, a pastor, a Bible-study leader, a man giving a friend advice, will answer for it more strictly, because people will build on what he said. James says it about himself: “we shall receive.”

For in many things we offend all.

The honest part: all. James puts himself in with everyone else. He is not a clean man talking down to dirty ones. Then he says what a mature man looks like: the one who does not “offend in word.” That is how hard words are to control. If the speaker is not perfect, and the listener is not perfect, we need the same mercy.

Part two

Small thing, great fire

Verses 3–12

A bit, a rudder and a spark. James picks three small things to show how much weight the tongue carries.

Small steering

a bitin a horse's mouth
the whole horse
a helma very small rudder
the whole ship, in fierce winds

A horse weighs hundreds of pounds, and a little piece of metal in its mouth turns all of it. A ship is driven by “fierce winds,” and a rudder, small next to the ship, turns it where the pilot wants.

“Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things.” Small, and it steers the whole man. What you say to yourself and to others at 7 a.m. points the day. A man who talks himself down all morning ends up where he is talking to.

There is good news in the picture: whoever holds the bit decides. Put the words under His hand, and a very small thing can turn a very big life the right way.

The tongue is a fire

James does not soften it: “a fire, a world of iniquity,” “an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.” One lie, one cutting remark, one rumour, and a whole forest goes up.

Every man here has set a fire with his mouth. “Set on fire of hell” uses the word Gehenna, the burning rubbish dump outside Jerusalem.

“The tongue can no man tame.” You cannot fix your mouth by willpower alone. But notice: no man. What no man can do, God can.

The tongue is only the tap

“O generation of vipers, how can ye, being evil, speak good things? for out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.”

Matthew 12:34
What is in the heartanger, envy, pride, hurt
→
comes out of the mouththe mouth speaks it

The mouth is where it comes out, not where it starts. That is why the cure is not a stricter lip, but a new heart, and a Helper. A man who has been forgiven, and who is being changed from the inside by the Holy Spirit, begins to talk differently over months, not days.

“Set a watch, O LORD, before my mouth; keep the door of my lips.”

Psalm 141:3

Pray that one. Out loud, in the morning. And if your mouth has already wrecked something lately, the way back is a short, plain sentence: “I was wrong. I'm sorry.”

Sweet water and bitter

“Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God.” Singing on Sunday and swearing at the driver on Monday. Praising God and then tearing into a man He made in His own likeness.

“These things ought not so to be.” A spring does not send out sweet water and bitter, a fig tree does not grow olives, a salt spring does not give fresh water. What is in the well comes up in the bucket.

Notice who gets the cursing: men, made after the similitude of God. Even the one who hurt you.

Three questions at the gate

Between the thought and the mouth, put a gate. Before you speak, one of these three:

  1. Is it true?No lies, and no half-truths that do the same job.
  2. Is it kind?You can say a hard thing without cruelty. “A soft answer turneth away wrath.”
  3. Does it build up?“That which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.”

“Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.”

Ephesians 4:29

You will fail some days, as everybody does. Say sorry, and start again with the next sentence. Today, pick one person and say one good thing to them, and mean it.

Part three

Two kinds of wisdom

Verses 13–18

James ends with a test for any man who calls himself wise. Where does it come from, and what does it grow?

Where it comes from

Verses 14–16

Earthly, sensual, devilish

Earthly: it runs on what people think of you. Sensual: it runs on feelings and cravings. Devilish: it copies the devil's own moves. It shows up as “bitter envying and strife.” And where those are, “there is confusion and every evil work.”

Verse 17

From above

It “descendeth” from the Father of lights, the same Father as in chapter 1. It is not clever. It is clean, calm and kind, and it grows peace.

Envy and strife are old companions of men in recovery: the guy who got the better job, the one who got out sooner, the one whose family came back. James says that kind of cleverness looks like wisdom but is not. Look at what it grows: confusion, and every evil work.

So “glory not, and lie not against the truth.” Do not boast about being wise while you are eaten up with envy. Admit it, to God, and to one man.

What wisdom from above looks like

  1. Purefirst of all: clean motives.
  2. Peaceableit quiets a room.
  3. Gentlekind, even when it is right.
  4. Easy to be intreatedyou can talk to it. It listens.
  5. Full of mercyit forgives, because it has been forgiven.
  6. Good fruitsit does things for people.
  7. Without partialityno favourites: chapter 2 again.
  8. Without hypocrisythe same in private as in public.

That is a picture of Jesus. And “easy to be intreated” may be the hardest for a stubborn man, so ask yourself it today: can anyone tell me when I am wrong, and live?

The fruit of righteousness is sown in peace of them that make peace.

A peacemaker is not a man who avoids every fight. He is a man who plants peace on purpose and waits for the harvest. It is slow, like a seed, and you will not see it in a day.

“If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.”

James 1:5

And remember where wisdom comes from: it is given. You do not have to be clever. Ask. Then, today, plant one small piece of peace: a text to a man you have been cold to, a word to the one you argued with. That is how wisdom from above gets started.

Points to remember

  1. Teachers“My brethren, be not many masters, knowing that we shall receive the greater condemnation.” 3:1Whoever teaches answers for it more strictly. James puts himself in the same boat: in many things we offend all.
  2. Small things“Behold also the ships, which though they be so great, and are driven of fierce winds, yet are they turned about with a very small helm, whithersoever the governor listeth.” 3:4A bit turns a horse and a rudder turns a ship. Small things steer big ones, and the tongue steers a life.
  3. The tongue“But the tongue can no man tame; it is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.” 3:8No man can tame it by willpower, but God can. Ask Him to set a watch before your mouth.
  4. One mouth“Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.” 3:10Praise and cursing do not belong in the same mouth. What is in the well comes up in the bucket.
  5. Earthly wisdom“For where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work.” 3:16Envy and strife look clever and grow only confusion and every evil work.
  6. Wisdom from above“But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy.” 3:17Pure, peaceable, gentle, merciful, and the same in private as in public. It is given to anyone who asks.

Question 1 of 6

Teachers

Why does James say, “be not many masters”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because teachers will be judged more strictly. And he adds himself to the list: in many things we all offend, so no teacher stands above his hearers.

Question 2 of 6

Small things

What little things steer great things?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A bit turns a horse, a very small helm turns a great ship, and the tongue, a little member, steers a whole life.

Question 3 of 6

The tongue

What is the tongue like, and who can tame it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Set a watch, O LORD, before my mouth; keep the door of my lips.”

Psalm 141:3

Answer

A fire, a world of iniquity, an unruly evil full of deadly poison. No man can tame it, so ask the Lord to set a watch before your mouth.

Question 4 of 6

One mouth

What ought not to come out of the same mouth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Blessing and cursing. A spring does not give sweet water and bitter, and the mouth speaks out of what fills the heart.

Question 5 of 6

Earthly wisdom

Where envying and strife is, what follows?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Confusion and every evil work. That kind of wisdom is earthly, sensual and devilish, however clever it sounds.
