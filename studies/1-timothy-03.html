Part one

The overseer

Verses 1–7

What a pastor is like, and so, in a way, what every man in Christ is growing into.

ἐπίσκοπος said episkopos an overseer, one who watches over

Bishop is the old English word for overseer. In the New Testament it's the same office as elder and pastor (shepherd). Paul told the Ephesian elders: “the Holy Ghost hath made you overseers, to feed the church of God” (Acts 20:28).

Notice how Paul begins: “a good work.” Not a position of honor or power, but work. Baptists hold that the office of pastor is for men, and verse 2 is part of why. Whether a particular man should fill it is a decision for his church, after he has grown and been tested.

The portrait, in four rooms

His own self

23

vigilant (clear-headed), sober (steady), patient. Not given to wine, no striker (not violent), not a brawler.

His money

3

Not greedy of filthy lucre, not covetous. He's not in it for what he can get.

His home

245

The husband of one wife: faithful to her. He rules well his own house, and shows hospitality.

His name

27

Blameless, of good behaviour, with a good report from outsiders. And apt to teach.

Blameless doesn't mean sinless. It means nothing anyone can fairly pin on him: no hole in his name. And look at the list: drink, temper, money, women, home. These are the very areas where we fall, and the Bible says plainly that they're where a man is meant to grow.

It starts at home

A man who runs his house well“ruleth well his own house”
→
can be trusted with“the church of God”

“Ruleth” here means to lead and care for, not to boss. “Husband of one wife” is a one-woman man: faithful. Good teachers differ about exactly how it applies to a man who was divorced or remarried before he knew Christ; his church decides that in prayer.

Some of you have left a house in ruins. Nobody can undo it. But a man can start tomorrow: show up, pay what's owed, say “I was wrong” to his kids, stay faithful from today. That's how growth in this chapter begins.

Roots take time

Novice is the Greek neophytos: newly planted. A plant needs roots before it can carry weight. A new believer put up front, praised and trusted too soon, is in danger of the pride that took down the devil himself.

That's not an insult. It's protection, for the man and for the church. If you're new, you're not behind: you're a sapling. Put roots down: Bible, a church, one brother who'll ask you hard questions.

A picture, not a trapdoor

It would be easy to read this list and decide you're out. Read it again, a different way. What is it?

  • a test that decides whether God loves youHe loved you before you changed a single thing (Romans 5:8), and that has not changed.
  • a wall that your past locks you behindPaul had been a persecutor, and God grew him.
  • a picture of the man every believer is growing intoCalm, faithful, honest, steady, humble, kind. Read it as a direction, not a bar to the door.

“Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before, I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 3:13–14

Who may be a pastor or a deacon is a church's decision. Who may grow into this man is every believer. Pick one line from verses 2 and 3 you most need, and ask God for that one this month.

Part two

The servants

Verses 8–13

The second office: men who serve. Proved first, then trusted.

Deacon means servant

Who they were

Chosen to serve

The first of them looked after food for widows: “seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom” (Acts 6:3). Practical, trusted men.

What they're like

Grave, straight, steady

Grave means serious, dignified. Not doubletongued: says the same thing to everyone. Not given to much wine, not greedy. And holding the faith “in a pure conscience.”

There are two ways to read verse 11, “their wives”: the wives of deacons, or women who serve as deaconesses. Bible teachers differ. Either way the Bible has a woman who served: “Phebe our sister, which is a servant of the church” (Romans 16:1).

Proved, then trusted

  1. “first be proved”v. 10: shown over time, not on the first day
  2. “use the office of a deacon”v. 10: serve, being found blameless
  3. “a good degree”v. 13: standing and respect
  4. “great boldness in the faith”v. 13: confidence in Christ

Service comes first, and it builds a man. A man who sets up chairs, makes the coffee, drives a brother to work, gains a steadier faith and a name that means something. You don't need an office. Start with one small job nobody sees.

Part three

The house of God

Verses 14–16

Why any of this matters, and the One it's all about.

Not a building. People.

Ephesus was famous for its huge temple of Diana, with over a hundred columns. Paul says the real house of God has no stone in it: “the church of the living God.” A pillar holds something up for everyone to see. A ground is the base it rests on.

The church doesn't make the truth. It holds it up and stands on it. Jesus said, “upon this rock I will build my church” (Matthew 16:18). That's you, this group, your church. Don't try to be a pillar alone.

A mystery is a secret God has now told. This one is a Person: the whole list of verses 2 to 7 describes a man no one of us can be on his own. Jesus is the one Man who was all of it, and Christ living in you is how any man grows toward it.

Points to remember

  1. A good work“If a man desire the office of a bishop, he desireth a good work” 3:1Leading a church is work, not status. The desire is a good one.
  2. A grown man“A bishop then must be blameless, the husband of one wife, vigilant, sober” 3:2It's a picture of mature manhood: faithful, steady, self-controlled, honest. Every believer can grow toward it.
  3. Not a novice“Not a novice, lest being lifted up with pride he fall” 3:6Roots take time. A new believer isn't behind; he's a sapling that needs time and good soil.
  4. Deacons“Let these also first be proved; then let them use the office of a deacon” 3:10Servants are proved over time, and those who serve well gain a good standing and boldness in the faith.
  5. God's house“The church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth” 3:15The church is God's people, who hold the truth up for the world to see.
  6. The mystery“Great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifest in the flesh” 3:16Godliness isn't a program. It's a Person: Jesus, who came, was raised, and is received up into glory.

What does Paul call the desire to serve as a pastor?

A good work. It's about feeding and watching over God's people, not about being important.

Question 2 of 6

A grown man

What kind of man is a bishop to be, at home and in public?

What verses tell us about this?

“Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before, I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.”

Answer

Blameless, faithful, clear-headed, self-controlled, not violent, not greedy, leading his home well, with a good name outside. It's a picture of mature manhood every believer can grow toward, from where he is.

Question 3 of 6

Not a novice

Why must a bishop not be a novice?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because a new believer set up too soon can be lifted up with pride and fall, as the devil did. Roots take time. It protects the man and the church.
