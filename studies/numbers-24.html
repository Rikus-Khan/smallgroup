Numbers 24

A Star out of Jacob

No more enchantments. Balaam looks down on Israel's tents and the spirit of God comes upon him. The third blessing, and then, unasked, a fourth: a King far off, a Star and a Sceptre, whom Balaam will see, “but not now.”

Part one

“How goodly are thy tents”

Verses 1–9

The third blessing, from the top of Peor.

The camp, laid out by God

NorthDan, Asher, Naphtali2:25
WestEphraim, Manasseh, Benjamin2:18
The tabernaclethe Levites round about“in the midst of the camp” (2:17)
EastJudah, Issachar, Zebuluntoward the sunrise, 2:3
SouthReuben, Simeon, Gad2:10

This time Balaam doesn't go off looking for omens. He “set his face toward the wilderness”, looks down, and sees what Numbers 2 laid out: every tribe in its place, and the LORD's tent in the middle. Forty years of wandering, and the camp is still in order around God.

“The spirit of God came upon him.” In the Old Testament the Spirit came upon men for a work, even on Balaam, to speak. Since Christ, he lives in every believer, for good (John 14:16–17).

“The man whose eyes are open”

  • trancea vision that takes hold of a man, so he sees what God shows him
  • the AlmightyGod, who has all power
  • the most HighGod, above every god of the nations (verse 16)

Balaam calls himself “the man whose eyes are open.” Two chapters ago his ass saw the angel and he did not; the LORD had to open his eyes (22:31). Now he sees visions of the Almighty.

And yet his heart stayed shut. A man can see a great deal of truth, and even say it out loud, and still not give himself to God. Open eyes are a gift. What a man does with what he sees is the question.

A desert camp, seen as a garden

  • goodlyfine, handsome
  • valleys spread forthwide and green
  • gardens by the river's sidealways watered
  • lign aloesaloe trees: lign means wood
  • cedar trees beside the waterstall, strong, deep-rooted
  • Agagthe name of Amalek's king (Saul spared one called Agag, 1 Samuel 15:8)

What Balaam actually saw was rows of tents in dry country. What God showed him was a garden the LORD had planted, by the water. That is how God sees the people he has planted.

“And he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth his fruit in his season; his leaf also shall not wither; and whatsoever he doeth shall prosper.”

Psalm 1:3

The promise to Abraham, from Balaam's mouth

The LORD to Abram, Genesis 12:3

“And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.”

Balaam, Numbers 24:9

“He couched, he lay down as a lion, and as a great lion: who shall stir him up? Blessed is he that blesseth thee, and cursed is he that curseth thee.”

Balak brought Balaam to curse Israel, and Balaam ends his third blessing by reading Balak his own sentence: whoever curses this people is cursed. The lion couching is Jacob's word over Judah (Genesis 49:9), and the Lion of the tribe of Juda is Christ (Revelation 5:5). The unicorn is the strong horned beast no man can tame (23:22).

Part two

“I shall see him, but not now”

Verses 10–19

Balak sends him home, and Balaam has one more word: about a King.

Three times, and three times

The ass turned aside123“these three times” (22:28, 32, 33)
Balaam blessed Israel123“these three times” (24:10)

Balaam beat his ass for turning aside three times. Now Balak smites his hands together (claps them in fury) because Balaam turned aside from cursing three times. Balaam got from Balak something like what he gave his donkey: anger, for turning aside three times. “These three times” comes four times in the story, three on the road and once here.

“The LORD hath kept thee back from honour,” Balak sneers. He was more right than he knew. The honour Balaam wanted was the kind men hand out, and God kept it from him.

“How can ye believe, which receive honour one of another, and seek not the honour that cometh from God only?”

John 5:44

One more word, unasked

  • advertisetell beforehand, give warning
  • the latter daysdays still far off, in the future
  • my peopleBalaam is going home

Balak didn't ask for this one. He has already told Balaam to flee. But before he goes, Balaam tells him what Israel will one day do to Moab. The fourth prophecy looks past the camp below, past Joshua, past David, to someone coming.

A Star, and a Sceptre

There shall come a Star out of Jacob.

A sceptre is a king's rod. Christians have always read this verse of Christ, and the New Testament has every piece of it:

  1. ✓I am the root and the offspring of David, and the bright and morning star.Revelation 22:16, Jesus speakinga Star
  2. ✓a sceptre of righteousness is the sceptre of thy kingdomHebrews 1:8, of the Sona Sceptre
  3. ✓the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, hath prevailedRevelation 5:5out of Jacob
  4. ✓we have seen his star in the east, and are come to worship himMatthew 2:2, the wise menhis star

“I shall see him, but not now”

Balaam knew he would see this King. Every man will:

“Behold, he cometh with clouds; and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him: and all kindreds of the earth shall wail because of him. Even so, Amen.”

Revelation 1:7

But Balaam saw him “not nigh”: far off, from the wrong side, as one of the nations the Sceptre would smite. There is another way to see him:

“Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.”

1 John 3:2

Balaam stood a long way off. The same King is near you, today:

“But what saith it? The word is nigh thee, even in thy mouth, and in thy heart: that is, the word of faith, which we preach; That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.”

Romans 10:8–9

He that shall have dominion

  • the corners of MoabMoab, from edge to edge
  • the children of Shethnamed beside Moab; the verse says no more about them
  • Edom, SeirEsau's land and its mountains
  • valiantlybravely, with strength
  • dominionrule

It began to come true in David, the king from Jacob's line who beat Moab and put garrisons in Edom:

“And he put garrisons in Edom; throughout all Edom put he garrisons, and all they of Edom became David’s servants. And the LORD preserved David whithersoever he went.”

2 Samuel 8:14

But David died, and his kingdom shrank. The One who “shall have dominion” for good is David's Son, the King whose kingdom has no end.

Part three

“Who shall live when God doeth this!”

Verses 20–25

Three short sayings over the nations, and Balaam goes home.

Amalek and the Kenites

  • Amalek“the first of the nations” to make war on Israel, at Rephidim (Exodus 17:8)
  • the Kenitesa people living in the rocky heights; Moses' father in law's people were Kenites (Judges 1:16)
  • AsshurAssyria, the empire that would one day carry peoples away captive

“And the LORD said unto Moses, Write this for a memorial in a book, and rehearse it in the ears of Joshua: for I will utterly put out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven.”

Exodus 17:14

Amalek came first against God's people, and God said its latter end would be to perish. The Kenites felt safe: “thou puttest thy nest in a rock.” But a nest in a rock is only as safe as the rock. There is one Rock that never gives way (1 Corinthians 10:4).

Empires come and go

The last saying looks furthest of all. Ships “from the coast of Chittim”, a people across the sea, will afflict Asshur, the great empire, and Eber, and “he also shall perish for ever.” The ships of Chittim come up again in Daniel 11:30. Balaam doesn't say more, and neither will we.

The point is plain enough: “Alas, who shall live when God doeth this!” Amalek, the Kenites, Assyria, the ships of Chittim: every power that looks permanent has its end written. Only the Star out of Jacob rises and stays.

“Returned to his place”, but not for long

  1. Hired“the rewards of divination”22:7
  2. Blessedfour prophecies, no curse23–24
  3. CounselledIsrael's sin at Peor31:16
  4. Slainwith the sword, among Midian31:8

“Behold, these caused the children of Israel, through the counsel of Balaam, to commit trespass against the LORD in the matter of Peor, and there was a plague among the congregation of the LORD.”

Numbers 31:16

“Balaam rose up, and went and returned to his place.” But he didn't stay there. He couldn't curse Israel, so he taught Balak how to trap them, and the next chapter tells how they fell. The New Testament names him three times: “the way of Balaam” (2 Peter 2:15), “the doctrine of Balaam” (Revelation 2:14), and here:

“Woe unto them! for they have gone in the way of Cain, and ran greedily after the error of Balaam for reward, and perished in the gainsaying of Core.”

Jude 11

Looking back: the curse turned to a blessing

“Because they met you not with bread and with water in the way, when ye came forth out of Egypt; and because they hired against thee Balaam the son of Beor of Pethor of Mesopotamia, to curse thee. Nevertheless the LORD thy God would not hearken unto Balaam; but the LORD thy God turned the curse into a blessing unto thee, because the LORD thy God loved thee.”

Deuteronomy 23:4–5

That is the whole of Numbers 22 to 24 in one line: because the LORD thy God loved thee. Israel was down in the plains, not even knowing a king and a prophet were bargaining over them on the hills. God fought for them before they knew there was a fight.

He still does. Some of the battles over your life were won on hilltops you never saw.

Real world: near, not far off

Balaam had open eyes, a gift from God, true words in his mouth, and a vision of Christ. He still went home and sold out God's people for a fee. Knowing the right words is not the same as belonging to the Lord.

  • SinBalaam's: love of money and honour, wrapped in religious talk. Often ours too.
  • RegretSeeing the truth and walking away from it. Many of us did, for years.
  • GraceThe curse was turned to a blessing “because the LORD thy God loved thee.”
  • HopeThe Star out of Jacob is risen. You don't see him “not nigh”: “The word is nigh thee.”
  • TodayDon't stand off at a distance like Balaam. Tell God, and a brother, that Jesus is your Lord, and live under his Sceptre today.

Points to remember

  1. No enchantments“he went not, as at other times, to seek for enchantments … and the spirit of God came upon him” 24:1–2Balaam gave up the omens, and God spoke by his Spirit.
  2. Goodly tents“How goodly are thy tents, O Jacob, and thy tabernacles, O Israel!” 24:5God sees the camp he planted as a garden by the water.
  3. Bless and curse“Blessed is he that blesseth thee, and cursed is he that curseth thee” 24:9God's promise to Abraham, spoken by the man hired to curse.
  4. The Star“there shall come a Star out of Jacob, and a Sceptre shall rise out of Israel” 24:17Christ, the bright and morning star. Every eye will see him; see him now, near.
  5. The nations“Amalek was the first of the nations; but his latter end shall be that he perish for ever” 24:20Every power that looks permanent has its end written.
  6. Balaam's end“Balaam rose up, and went and returned to his place” 24:25He went home, counselled Israel into sin, and died by the sword. Knowing the words isn't belonging to the Lord.
