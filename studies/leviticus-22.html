Leviticus 22

The holy things, and what may be offered

Who may eat the portions of the offerings, what a man must do when he is unclean, and what an offering to the LORD must be: without blemish. Nothing is offered that costs nothing, and nothing is eaten carelessly.

Part one

“That they separate themselves from the holy things”

Verses 1–9

Coming near the holy things unclean, and the way back.

What are “the holy things”?

  • the holy thingsthe portions of the offerings given to the priests to eat (chapters 6 and 7)
  • hallowmake holy; set apart for the LORD
  • profanetreat a holy thing as common
  • cut off from my presenceshut out from standing before him

The priests lived from the altar. Their daily food was a share of what Israel gave to God. So the very thing that fed them was holy, and they could not take it as a snack from the cupboard. Verse 3 is blunt: whoever “goeth unto the holy things… having his uncleanness upon him, that soul shall be cut off from my presence.”

Verses 2, 3 and 8 each end on the same words, and they will come again and again: “I am the LORD.” This is not about the priests keeping rules for the sake of rules. It is about whose bread it is.

What could shut a priest out

  • a lepera man with the skin disease of chapters 13 and 14
  • a running issuean unclean bodily discharge (chapter 15)
  • touching the deadanything made unclean by a death
  • a creeping thingan unclean crawling creature (chapter 11)
  • “a man of whom he may take uncleanness”touching someone who was himself unclean

Notice what is on that list and what is not. These are conditions, things that happened to a man or that he touched. Most were not a choice and not a sin. They were still a real barrier. A man could be a good priest and an unclean one on the same afternoon.

The point of all of them is the same one: the LORD is holy, and a man must be clean to eat what is his. And with every one of them comes the same door, which is verse 7.

The way back, in four steps

  1. Unclean“shall be unclean until even”verse 6
  2. Washed“wash his flesh with water”verse 6
  3. The sun goes down“when the sun is down”verse 7
  4. Clean, and eating“he shall be clean, and shall afterward eat of the holy things; because it is his food”verse 7

Even means evening. A man was not shut out for ever. He was shut out until he washed and the day ended. “Because it is his food”: when he was clean again, it was his own bread, and he sat down to it without apology.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

That is the same door for us: not a man who never needs washing, but a man who knows where the washing is. Fall in the morning, confess in the afternoon, and by evening you are sitting at his table again, eating your own food.

Dead and torn

Verse 8 adds: “That which dieth of itself, or is torn with beasts, he shall not eat to defile himself therewith.” The priest was not to eat an animal that died of itself or was killed by wild beasts. It was not slaughtered, its blood was not drained, and it was not offered. Chapter 17 says more on this.

Verse 9 gives the whole reason: “They shall therefore keep mine ordinance, lest they bear sin for it, and die therefore, if they profane it: I the LORD do sanctify them.” An ordinance is something he ordered. To bear sin is to carry the guilt of it. Verses 9 and 16 both end “I the LORD do sanctify them”: the same LORD who gave the rule is the one who made them holy.

Part two

“There shall no stranger eat of the holy thing”

Verses 10–16

Who may eat: the household of the priest, and no one else.

At the table, and outside it

May not eat

“a stranger”

“a sojourner of the priest, or an hired servant”: a guest or a man hired by the day. And the priest's daughter who “be married unto a stranger.”

May eat

“of his house”

A soul the priest buys “with his money,” a man “born in his house,” and a daughter who “returned unto her father's house, as in her youth.”

Here stranger means someone outside the priest's household, and a sojourner is a guest who lives with it for a while. The rule is about the house: those inside it eat, those outside do not. A bought servant belonged to the family, and a widowed daughter came home. Home is where the food is.

A household, not a hired hand

There is the New Testament's answer to the old rule:

“Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God;”

Ephesians 2:19

In Christ, men who were strangers are of the household of God, and a man who has come back home sits down at the table. “Ye are bought with a price” (1 Corinthians 6:20): not a hired man at the door, but a son whose Father paid for him.

If he ate it by mistake

A man eats a holy thing unwittinglyverse 14
+ he adds the fifth partone part in five, on top
= and gives it to the priest with the holy thingput right with the one who lost it

Unwittingly means without knowing it. Notice the law does not say, “It was an accident, forget it.” It says put it right. The mistake is real, the loss is real, and the repair is real, with a fifth added. God asks for a mended thing, not only a sorry feeling. (The same measure comes in 5:16, and again for wrongs done to a neighbour in Numbers 5:7.)

For the men in this room that is a word of hope, not weight. Where you took what wasn't yours, a clear path is given: give it back, and add. It is an exact and doable thing, and it ends at a priest, not at a shouting match.

Eating the holy things lightly

The priests were told to “not profane the holy things of the children of Israel” (verse 15), and not to “suffer them to bear the iniquity of trespass, when they eat their holy things” (verse 16). The New Testament says something like it about the Lord's supper:

“Wherefore whosoever shall eat this bread, and drink this cup of the Lord, unworthily, shall be guilty of the body and blood of the Lord. But let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of that bread, and drink of that cup. For he that eateth and drinketh unworthily, eateth and drinketh damnation to himself, not discerning the Lord’s body.”

1 Corinthians 11:27-29

Paul is not telling anyone to stay away from the table. He is telling them not to come to it carelessly, as if it were any other meal. “Let a man examine himself”: look at yourself honestly, confess what you find, and come. The priest who washed came in.

Part three

“It shall be perfect to be accepted”

Verses 17–33

What may be offered: without blemish, and not a day late.

Words in the offering

  • oblationan offering, a gift brought to God
  • vowspromises made to God (“if you do this, I will give that”)
  • freewill offeringsgifts brought because a man wanted to, and nobody asked
  • at your own willof your own free will
  • beevesoxen, cattle
  • without blemishwith nothing wrong with it, nothing spoiled
  • a wena lump or growth on the skin
  • scurvy, scabbeda scaly, itching skin; a skin crusted over

“Blind, or broken, or maimed.” The animal had to be whole. And it was a male of the beeves, of the sheep or of the goats, because what came to the altar was the best the man had.

The best, or a cheat

Long afterward the LORD put it to the people who had forgotten it. They were bringing the blind and the lame, the sick and the torn, and calling it worship:

“And if ye offer the blind for sacrifice, is it not evil? and if ye offer the lame and sick, is it not evil? offer it now unto thy governor; will he be pleased with thee, or accept thy person? saith the LORD of hosts.”

Malachi 1:8

No man would hand his governor a limping lamb. The LORD asks a man to give him at least what he would give his governor. A leftover is an insult. This is one reason the rule was so strict: the animal stood for the one who would come, and he could not be a second-best.

The Lamb without blemish

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18-19

Peter says the Lamb is Christ: “without blemish and without spot.” And now look what that means for us. The blemish list was never about our offering. We bring nothing to the altar that is good enough, and that is exactly the point. God provided the unblemished One in our place.

A gift, and a promise

A freewill offering

“superfluous or lacking in his parts”

An animal with an extra part, or one short a part, “mayest thou offer for a freewill offering.” A gift given from the heart could be less than perfect, and the LORD received it.

A vow

“it shall not be accepted”

But for a vow, the promise made to God, it had to be whole. A promise has to be kept in full.

Verse 24 names what could not be offered at all: “bruised, or crushed, or broken, or cut,” an animal injured in the body. Verse 25 adds that such animals were not to be offered from a stranger's hand either, “because their corruption is in them,” meaning the defect.

Look at the line between the two. A man who gives what he has, honestly, is not turned away. A man who makes a promise to God has to keep it. Many of you have promised God something in a bad hour. The vow is the reason to keep it. The grace is that a Saviour who was himself without blemish keeps you while you keep it.

Seven days under the dam

A dam is the mother of an animal. A calf, a lamb or a kid stayed with its mother seven days before it could be offered.

1dam2dam3dam4dam5dam6dam7dam8accepted

“From the eighth day and thenceforth it shall be accepted.” A week with its mother first. And verse 28 is plain mercy: “ye shall not kill it and her young both in one day.” God thinks about the cow and the calf. If he makes a law for them, think what he has for you.

Thanks the same day

The thanksgiving offering was “eaten up” the same day. “Ye shall leave none of it until the morrow.” Gratitude is not a thing to put on the shelf. Say it today, while it is warm.

It is the same lesson as the manna: the bread of yesterday spoils, and God gives it fresh. For men who are tempted to say, “I'll be grateful later, when things are better,” this is the answer. Be thankful today, with what you were given this morning:

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22-23

“I am the LORD”

Seven times in this chapter, in verses 2, 3, 8, 30, 31, 32 and 33:

The chapter closes on who is speaking, and what he did: “I am the LORD which hallow you, That brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God: I am the LORD.” He brought them out to be their God. They did not make themselves holy by keeping the rules; he made them holy and then gave the rules.

Twice more, in verses 9 and 16, he says “I the LORD do sanctify them,” and the end of verse 32 says the same of everyone: “I am the LORD which hallow you.”

Real world: whole, washed and fed

  • SinSome things shut a man out, and some of them were not his fault. Others were. God names both, and gives a door back from each.
  • Regret“Until even.” The shutting out was never for ever, and there was a washing at the end of it.
  • GraceThe Lamb “without blemish” stands in the place where we could never bring an offering good enough.
  • HopeA man bought with a price is not a hired hand at the door. He eats at the table in his Father's house.
  • TodayIf you took something that was not yours, give it back and add a fifth. If you made a promise to God, keep it. And say your thanks before nightfall.

  1. Uncleanness“that soul shall be cut off from my presence: I am the LORD” 22:3A man could not eat what is holy with his uncleanness upon him.
  2. The way back“unless he wash his flesh with water. And when the sun is down, he shall be clean” 22:6–7Washed, and clean by sundown. The door back is open, and then it is his own food.

Question 1 of 6

Uncleanness

What happened to the man who came to the holy things with his uncleanness upon him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“That soul shall be cut off from my presence.” To come near the holy things unclean was to profane them, and to bear sin for it.

Question 2 of 6

The way back

What did an unclean man have to do before he could eat the holy things again?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

He had to wash his flesh with water and wait until the sun was down. Then he was clean and could eat, “because it is his food.” There is a way back for the unclean.

Question 3 of 6

Who may eat

Who could not eat the holy thing, and who could?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No stranger, no guest, no hired servant. But a soul bought with the priest's money, and one born in his house, could eat. The household eats.

Question 4 of 6

The mistake

What did a man do if he ate a holy thing without knowing it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He added the fifth part and gave it to the priest with the holy thing. A mistake is put right, and put right with something extra.

Question 5 of 6

Without blemish

What kind of animal could be offered, and what could not?

What verses tell us about this?

“But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:19

Answer

A male without blemish. Nothing blind, broken, maimed, or with a wen, scurvy or scab. It had to be whole, and Christ is the Lamb “without blemish and without spot.”

Question 6 of 6

The LORD

What does the LORD say about himself at the end of the chapter?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“I am the LORD which hallow you,” the one who brought them out of Egypt to be their God. He makes holy, and he rescues, and then he gives the rules.
