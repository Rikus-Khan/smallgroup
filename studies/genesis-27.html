Genesis 27

The stolen blessing

A blind father, a scheming mother, a son who lies, and a brother who weeps and then plans a murder. Everyone sins in this chapter, and God's word still stands. A hard chapter for any man who has wronged his family, or been wronged by it.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. SinWho sinned in this chapter?

Part one

A blind man's plan

Verses 1–5

Isaac decides to bless Esau, and tells nobody but Esau.

What Isaac already knew

  1. Genesis 25:23The LORD speaks“the elder shall serve the younger”
  2. Genesis 25:33Esau sells ithis birthright, for a meal
  3. Genesis 26:34–35Two Hittite wives“a grief of mind”
  4. Genesis 27:1–4Isaac blesses Esau anyway“my son… that my soul may bless thee”

Isaac's plan runs against what God had said (25:23). And he plans it in private, sending Esau off to cook, with no word to Rebekah or Jacob. Maybe it is the favourite son in him: “Isaac loved Esau, because he did eat of his venison” (25:28). A man's appetite can put a hand on the scale when he blesses.

So this chapter does not start with a lie. It starts with a father who knew the LORD's word, and meant to do otherwise.

  2. venisonmeat from a wild animal that has been hunted: deer or antelope, in those days. It does not only mean deer.
  3. savoury meattasty, well-seasoned food, cooked the way he liked it
  4. quiverthe case that holds a hunter's arrows
  5. my soul may bless thee"I myself, with all that I am, will bless thee"

The blessing was a father's spoken word over the one who would carry the family's future. It was not magic, but in this family it carried the promise God had made to Abraham (28:4 calls it "the blessing of Abraham"). That is why this fight is so ugly: both brothers knew exactly what was at stake.

Part two

Rebekah's plan

Verses 5–17

She heard it, she was right about what God said, and she did it in the worst way.

Rebekah: right about God, wrong about how

What she knew

The LORD's word

Years before, she had asked the LORD about the twins, and he answered her: the elder shall serve the younger (25:22–23). She was not making that up.

What she did

Took it into her own hands

She sent her favourite son to trick his blind husband. This time she did not go to the LORD. She went to the goat pen.

It is the same mistake as Sarai's in Genesis 16: a true promise, a long wait, and a plan to help God along. God does not need help to keep his word.

Notice, too, her words in verse 8: “obey my voice.” And Jacob, who is grown, does. Any man can find himself doing something wrong because someone he loves or fears said "do it."

Jacob's only worry

JacobMy father will feel me, and I will seem to him a deceiver, and I shall bring a curse on myself, not a blessing.

RebekahUpon me be thy curse, my son. Only obey my voice.

What worries Jacob? Being caught. He never says "this is wrong." The fear of the consequence is louder than the fear of the sin.

“Upon me be thy curse”

It is a promise no mother can keep. A person cannot take another person's curse. Only one Man has taken ours:

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

Rebekah would carry the cost of her scheme anyway. Christ carries the cost of yours, and everything else, once and for all.

Four props for a lie

  1. 1The foodSavoury meat, made "such as his father loved"14
  2. 2Esau's clothesHis "goodly raiment" (fine clothing), with the smell of the field on it (v. 27)15
  3. 3Goat skinsOn the hands and the smooth of the neck, so a smooth man would feel hairy16

A lie often needs props: a made-up story, a borrowed look, a clean-smelling cover. The more of them it needs, the heavier it gets. Notice, too, whose clothes Jacob is wearing: his brother's. He is pretending to be someone else.

Part three

“Who art thou, my son?”

Verses 18–29

Three lies at the bedside, one of them in God's name.

Three lies, and the worst one last

IsaacWho art thou, my son?

JacobI am Esau thy firstborn.

IsaacHow is it that thou hast found it so quickly, my son?

JacobBecause the LORD thy God brought it to me.

He lies about who he is, then about what he did, then he puts the lie on the LORD. Notice: not "my God." "Thy God."

When sin starts to sound religious

“Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain; for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain.”

Exodus 20:7

Men who have lived a long time in a lie know this move well: when sin is cornered, it starts to sound religious. "God has shown me," "it's a blessing," "the Lord is in this."

Anything that has to borrow God's name to get through a door is probably not from him. If you ever catch yourself saying "God told me" about something you already wanted, stop, and ask a brother in Christ or your pastor.

The voice and the hands

  1. Hearing“The voice is Jacob's voice”: his ears told him the truth (v. 22)
  2. Touch“the hands are the hands of Esau”: his hands told him a lie (v. 22–23)
  3. Tastethe savoury meat tasted right (v. 25)
  4. Smellthe field on the clothes (v. 27)
  5. Prayerthe one thing he did not do: ask the LORD

Isaac's ears said Jacob, and every other sense said Esau. He trusted the senses that agreed with what he wanted. Anyone who wants a thing badly enough, a drink, a relationship, a way out, can find "evidence" for it.

He asked "Art thou my very son Esau?" a second time (v. 24). He had one more chance to stop, and he did not take it, because he wanted to believe it.

A kiss, and a lie

In a Bible home, a father's kiss was a sign of love and welcome. Jacob accepts it with the smell of Esau on his back. Judas would later come to Jesus with a kiss, too (Luke 22:47–48).

It is not that Jacob is exactly like Judas. It is that a loving gesture can carry a lie. A man can say "I love you" while he is deceiving you, and a man who has been deceived by a kiss knows how deep it cuts.

Don't miss what Jacob was missing: he was willing to take what was always going to be his by a road God did not ask him to take. It is the oldest temptation: to get a good thing by a bad way.

Isaac's blessing: the land

Therefore God give thee of the dew of heaven,

and the fatness of the earth,

and plenty of corn and wine:

Heaven first, then earth. Dew was the night moisture that kept crops alive in a dry summer; fatness means rich soil; corn and wine are grain and the grape harvest. A full table, from top to bottom.

The blessing opens with God, not Isaac: “God give thee.” Even here, a father only passes on what comes from him. (Wine in the Bible is a harvest gift, and also a danger; the Bible says both. A man who is done with drink does not need to read himself into this verse.)

Isaac's blessing: the rule

Let people serve thee,

and nations bow down to thee:

be lord over thy brethren,

and let thy mother’s sons bow down to thee:

Each line says it again, a little bigger. The heir of the promise would lead the family: "thy brethren" and "thy mother's sons" are Esau and the rest.

Isaac's blessing: the last pair

cursed be every one that curseth thee,

and blessed be he that blesseth thee.

This pair is lifted from what the LORD had said to Abraham: “I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee” (12:3). Isaac is handing on the blessing of Abraham, and that is why it could not be undone.

  2. discernedrecognised, could tell who it was
  3. very sonreal son, truly my son
  4. raimentclothing
  5. fatness of the earthrich, fertile land
  6. brethrenbrothers, family

Part four

Esau comes in

Verses 30–40

The tent door barely closes behind Jacob when Esau walks in.

“Isaac trembled very exceedingly”

Isaac plannedto bless Esau
→
Isaac discoversthe blessing has been given
→
Isaac says“yea, and he shall be blessed”

He shakes. And then he does not take it back. At this moment Isaac seems to see, at last, that he had been fighting what God had said all along, and that the LORD's word had been done in spite of him.

God's plan stood. The sin still cost.

“By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau concerning things to come.”

Hebrews 11:20

The Bible lists Isaac among the people of faith, in spite of the whole mess. It is not a reward for a perfect man.

And here is the careful truth: the lie did not make God's plan work. The plan was going to stand anyway. The lie only added pain.

Jacob: “heel-catcher”

יַעֲקֹב said Ya‘aqov “he takes by the heel”: to supplant, to trip up and take another's place

Esau says, “Is not he rightly named Jacob? for he hath supplanted me these two times.” Supplant means to push someone out of their place and take it. Jacob had been born holding his brother's heel (25:26).

Esau is right about what Jacob did. But listen to what he leaves out: he sold the birthright himself, for a bowl of stew (25:33). "Two times" is what Jacob did to him, and what he did to himself is not mentioned. Regret that only points a finger is not repentance.

Esau's tears

The cry is real. “A great and exceeding bitter cry.” It is one of the saddest sounds in Genesis. A man has lost something that cannot be handed back, and he knows it.

“For ye know how that afterward, when he would have inherited the blessing, he was rejected: for he found no place of repentance, though he sought it carefully with tears.”

Hebrews 12:17

Hebrews says he sought the blessing with tears, and found no way to undo what had been done. The warning is plain: do not trade what lasts for one morsel, and do not think it can always be bought back later.

But look at what the tears were for: the lost blessing. There is no word here of sorrow toward God.

Two kinds of sorrow

Sorrow about the loss

“Bless me, even me also”

Sorry for what it cost me. It weeps, it blames, and it wants the thing back.

Sorrow toward God

“I have sinned”

Sorry for what I did, and turning to the LORD about it. It leads to being forgiven and made new.

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

Every man who has woken up with regret knows the first kind. It is real pain, and it is not sin. But it is only a start. The next step is to turn that sorrow toward God.

Your tears are not too late

“All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.”

John 6:37

Whoever comes to Christ, he does not cast out. A blessing Isaac could not give twice is not the same as a salvation Christ will not refuse.

If you are sitting in the wreck of something you did, bring that sorrow to him, and tell a brother in Christ and your pastor. That is where regret turns into hope.

Esau's blessing

thy dwelling shall be the fatness of the earth,

and of the dew of heaven from above;

And by thy sword shalt thou live,

and shalt serve thy brother;

It is not nothing. Esau is not cursed. He gets a country, a living, and a day when he will break free. What he does not get is the promise: that went to Jacob.

“Break his yoke”

“Yet Edom revolted from under the hand of Judah unto this day. Then Libnah revolted at the same time.”

2 Kings 8:22

Hundreds of years later, Edom did break away from Judah. God kept his word to Esau too.

Esau was not the chosen line, but he was not forgotten. The LORD kept his word to the one who was passed over.

Part five

Fury and flight

Verses 41–46

What a grudge does when it is fed.

How a grudge grows

  1. A real wrong“thy brother came with subtilty”
  2. Hatred“Esau hated Jacob”
  3. Said in his heart“Esau said in his heart”
  4. A plan“then will I slay my brother Jacob”

“Looking diligently lest any man fail of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you, and thereby many be defiled;”

Hebrews 12:15

Esau had been wronged, and he had a real reason. That is what makes a grudge so dangerous. It starts with something true, and gets fed in secret until it is a plan. “Said in his heart”: the plan was formed before anyone heard it.

Do not let it grow in the dark

“Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer: and ye know that no murderer hath eternal life abiding in him.”

1 John 3:15

Say it out loud, today, to a brother in Christ or to your pastor: "I am angry at this man, and I am thinking things." A grudge that is spoken to a friend loses its grip.

Flee to Haran

Rebekah's plan: send Jacob to Laban my brother, her family in Haran, far to the north. More than five hundred miles by the road.

She says "a few days." It was twenty years (31:41). She would pay the cost of her own scheme: the Bible does not show her seeing her favourite son again.

Jacob will leave a home, a father, and a brother. He sets out with nothing but a staff. And in the next chapter, at a place called Bethel, the LORD will meet him.

Everyone in the chapter sinned, and everyone lost something. The "win" came at a high price, and one family was broken into pieces.

Look at Rebekah's closing words: “I am weary of my life.” She says it as a way of getting her husband to send Jacob away, but a worn-out, frightened person says those words for real, too. If you ever feel that way, call or text a brother in Christ, and your pastor. Do it today. You are not meant to carry it alone.

Where was God?

Read the chapter again: God does not speak once. The people say his name, "the LORD thy God," "a field which the LORD hath blessed," but he says nothing. His silence is not absence.

He had spoken decades earlier, to Rebekah (25:23). Everything he said is carried out, in the end, through blind, scared, proud, sinful people.

“And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.”

Romans 8:28

That verse is not a reason to sin ("let us do evil that good may come"). It is a comfort for the man who has already sinned or been sinned against: the LORD can take a family's worst day and still bring his purpose through. Hold on to that, but do not use the lie to prove it.

Question 1 of 6

Sin

Who sinned in this chapter?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All four. Isaac meant to bless the son God had passed over. Rebekah schemed. Jacob lied to his father's face. Esau planned to kill his brother. Nobody is the good guy here, and the honest thing is to look for your own part rather than for the worst person.

Question 2 of 6

Lies

What did Jacob say to his blind father that made the lie worse?

What verses tell us about this?

“Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain; for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain.”

Exodus 20:7

Answer

"The LORD thy God brought it to me." He put his lie in God's mouth. Sin often starts to sound religious when it is cornered. If you ever have to say "God told me" to cover your own wish, stop and ask your brothers in Christ and your pastor.

Question 3 of 6

God's plan

Did God need the lie to keep his word?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD said unto her, Two nations are in thy womb, and two manner of people shall be separated from thy bowels; and the one people shall be stronger than the other people; and the elder shall serve the younger.”

Genesis 25:23

Answer

No. God had said it years before, and he does not need help. The lie did not make his plan happen. It only added twenty years of pain to a family. Wait on his word, and do it his way.

Question 4 of 6

Regret

What did Esau cry, and what was missing from his tears?

What verses tell us about this?

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

“All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.”

John 6:37

Answer

"Bless me, even me also." He wept for what he had lost, and the text gives no word of sorrow toward God. Regret over the cost is where it begins. Turn it toward the LORD, and Jesus says he will in no wise cast you out.

Question 5 of 6

Bitterness

What did Esau plan to do with his anger, and what would it have cost?

What verses tell us about this?

“Looking diligently lest any man fail of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you, and thereby many be defiled;”

Hebrews 12:15

Answer

He planned to wait for his father to die and then kill his brother. It would have cost him a brother and left a whole family in pieces. A grudge fed in secret becomes a plan. Say it out loud, to a brother in Christ and to your pastor, before it grows.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

Can God still work through a family this broken?

What verses tell us about this?

“And, behold, I am with thee, and will keep thee in all places whither thou goest, and will bring thee again into this land; for I will not leave thee, until I have done that which I have spoken to thee of.”

Genesis 28:15

“And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.”

Romans 8:28

Answer

Yes. The very next chapter shows it: God meets Jacob, running from home, and says "I am with thee." It does not excuse the sin, and the family paid for it. But God does not drop a family because it fell. Whatever you have broken, he has not stopped being with you.
