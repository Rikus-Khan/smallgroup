Genesis 29

The deceiver gets deceived, and Leah gets a name for praise

Jacob reaches a well, finds Rachel, and works seven years for her. Then he wakes up next to the wrong sister. The man who tricked his own father learns what it feels like.

Part one

At the well

Verses 1–14

Jacob comes to the end of his road, and the first person he meets is family.

The people of the east

“The land of the people of the east” is the country around Haran. It is the same Haran where Abraham's family stopped on the way to Canaan, and where Abraham's servant found Rebekah (chapter 24).

The Bible calls this country Padan-aram. Jacob had walked five hundred miles, and the first thing he sees is a well with three flocks waiting beside it.

Old words in chapter 29

  2. abodestayed
  3. baregave birth to (bare a son)
  4. beguiledtricked, deceived
  5. tender eyedunclear: soft, weak or dull eyes
  6. fulfil her weekfinish the seven-day wedding feast

“Went in unto her” (21, 23) is the Bible's modest way of saying a man slept with his wife.

At the well

JacobKnow ye Laban? Is he well?

The shepherdsHe is well: and, behold, Rachel his daughter cometh with the sheep. just then

JacobIt is yet high day. Water ye the sheep.

The shepherdsWe cannot, until all the flocks be gathered. the rule

Jacob asks about Laban, and before they finish answering, the daughter is walking up: “While he yet spake with them, Rachel came.” Genesis doesn't say “the LORD led him” here, but God had said I am with thee only weeks before, and the timing is hard to call luck.

He rolled the stone

The shepherds said they had to wait until everyone was there to roll the stone away. Jacob walked up and did it by himself. After five hundred miles, with a girl in front of him, he found a strength he didn't know he had.

Then he kissed her, and “lifted up his voice, and wept.” It was relief: a man who left home as a runaway, with his brother's threats in his ears, who spent nights on stones.

He had just been told, in a dream, I will bring thee again to this land, I will not leave thee. Now a family was in front of him. Some of the weight was lifting, and he let it show. Grown men do weep, and the Bible does not blush about it.

“My bone and my flesh”

Laban runs out to meet him, hugs him and takes him home. It's a warm welcome from a relative, and Jacob has been looking for exactly that.

For a month Jacob lives with Laban, and he works. Laban is a shrewd man who can see what a hard worker is worth. His next words are about wages, not about family.

Keep an eye on Laban for the rest of the chapter. A man can be warm and use you at the same time. The Bible lets us see this before Jacob does.

Part two

Seven years, and a wedding

Verses 15–30

Jacob pays a bride-price in years, and gets a surprise in the morning.

Two sisters

The elder

Leah

“Tender eyed.” Teachers differ on the Hebrew: weak or dull, or gentle. Either way, the verse sets her beside her sister and she loses.

The younger

Rachel

“Beautiful and well favoured.” Jacob loved her and offered seven years of work, because he had no money to pay a bride-price.

Jacob did love her, and that is a good thing. But notice who is described by a comparison. Leah has been measured against her sister all her life. Her pain is going to run through the next two chapters.

“They seemed unto him but a few days, for the love he had to her.”

Seven years of herding sheep in the heat and the cold, and it felt like a few days. That is what love does to hard work.

Think about what you would walk through for the people you love: your kids, your wife, your own good name. A man in recovery is doing a daily kind of seven years. It is slow, and it is worth it, and one day you look back and it felt shorter than you expected.

The morning after

The Bible's account is short, and it leaves a lot unsaid: Laban throws a feast, and gathers “all the men of the place.” “In the evening” he brings Leah to Jacob. “In the morning, behold, it was Leah.”

JacobWhat is this thou hast done unto me? Did not I serve with thee for Rachel? Wherefore then hast thou beguiled me?

Jacob uses a very strong word. Beguiled, deceived. It's the same sort of word used when the serpent deceived Eve. He knows exactly what it feels like to be fooled, and the man who is shouting it is the man who fooled his father.

The deceiver, deceived

What Jacob did (chapter 27)

To his father, Isaac

Took the place of the firstborn. Used a disguise and a blind old man's weak eyes. Said “I am Esau.”

What was done to him (chapter 29)

By his uncle, Laban

The wrong sister at night. Laban's excuse: it isn't done to give the younger before the firstborn.

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Don't miss the point of the pattern. A man who sows deceit eventually gets some back. And yet look at the grace: God is not abandoning him. He is teaching him, and God stays with him in it. Many of us have felt the other end of our own sin.

Fourteen years

  1. Seven yearswork for Rachel“but a few days”
  2. The weddingLeah, not Rachelthe morning surprise
  3. A week laterRachel too“Fulfil her week”
  4. Seven more yearswork for Rachelpaid after the wedding

Rachel was given at the end of the wedding week, and Jacob worked off the second seven years afterward. Fourteen years in all. We will see in 31:41 that he counts it that way himself.

Laban gave each daughter a maid: Zilpah to Leah, Bilhah to Rachel. Remember those two names.

Not how God made marriage

“Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.”

Genesis 2:24

One man, one wife: that was the beginning. Jacob ends up with two wives, and the rest of these chapters show what it brings: jealousy, bitter rivalry, a house at war with itself.

Genesis tells it without ever saying “this was a fine idea.” It is honest, and it lets the story speak. If you have made a mess of your own relationships, you aren't looking at people who had it all figured out. These are people God loved and stayed with while they made a mess.

Part three

The LORD saw Leah

Verses 31–35

The wife nobody picked gets four sons, and four names.

“Hated”

The word isn't as strong as it sounds. Moses uses it in the law for the wife who is loved less:

“If a man have two wives, one beloved, and another hated, and they have born him children, both the beloved and the hated; and if the firstborn son be hers that was hated:”

Deuteronomy 21:15

Leah was loved less: second choice, the one Jacob woke up next to and didn't want. She knew it every day.

And then, “when the LORD saw that Leah was hated.” Rachel was the one Jacob loved. Leah was the one God saw. If you have ever felt overlooked, the one who wasn't picked, the one who isn't talked about, notice this verse. The LORD sees.

Four names, four prayers

  1. Reuben“see, a son”“now therefore my husband will love me”
  2. Simeon“hearing”“the LORD hath heard that I was hated”
  3. Levi“joined”“Now this time will my husband be joined unto me”
  4. Judah“praise”“Now will I praise the LORD”

Watch what happens to the prayer. With Reuben, Leah is looking at her husband: now he will love me. With Levi, again: now he will be joined to me. It didn't work. He still loved Rachel.

By the fourth son she lets it go. Nothing about her situation had changed. But “Now will I praise the LORD.” She stopped waiting for a man to fill the hole and gave her praise to God.

The sons nobody expected

Look at who these two sons turn out to be, the ones Leah named when she felt the least loved:

Levi

The priests

His family will serve in the tabernacle and the temple, and carry the things of God.

Judah

The kings, and the Saviour

David came from Judah, and so did Jesus.

“Abraham begat Isaac; and Isaac begat Jacob; and Jacob begat Judas and his brethren;”

Matthew 1:2

The line of Christ does not run through Rachel, the loved one. It runs through Leah, the one nobody wanted. (Judas is the Greek form of Judah.) God does His best work through people who thought they were second choice.

Question 1 of 5

Guidance

Was it chance that Rachel came to the well just as Jacob arrived?

What verses tell us about this?

“Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.”

Matthew 28:20

Answer

Genesis doesn't say in so many words that God led him here. But God had just said “I am with thee”, and Rachel arrived in the middle of Jacob's question. God goes ahead of those who belong to Him. Jesus promises His own presence to every believer: “I am with you alway.”

Question 2 of 5

Love

What did Jacob's love for Rachel cost him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Fourteen years of hard work for a wife, with a trick in the middle. Love made it feel like “a few days.” Whatever it costs to do right by the people you love (time, work, patience, staying sober one more day) it is worth paying.

Question 3 of 5

Reaping

Why did Laban do to Jacob what Jacob had done to his father?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Answer

Genesis doesn't say “this is your punishment.” But it fits what Paul says: whatever a man sows, he reaps. Jacob tricked a father with a disguise; he was tricked by an uncle in the dark, about the firstborn. Sin has a way of coming back round. The grace is that God did not leave him there. Own it, and keep walking with Him.

Question 4 of 5

The unloved

Did God see Leah when her husband did not love her?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Yes. “The LORD saw.” He looked on her affliction and He heard her. If you have felt like the one nobody picks, God has seen you. Out of Leah came Levi's priests and Judah's kings, and in the end Jesus.

Question 5 of 5

Praise

What changed in Leah by her fourth son?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Her aim changed. The first three names ask for a husband's love. The fourth is praise: Judah. Nothing in her marriage had been fixed, but she turned to the LORD. No person can fill the hole a man carries; God can. Praise Him today, before things get better.
