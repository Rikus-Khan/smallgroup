Paul wrote this letter from a house in Rome, where he was chained to a soldier, about AD 60 to 62. Tychicus carried it to the church at Colosse. Two chapters told the Colossians who Christ is and warned them not to trade Him for rules and clever ideas.

Now comes the “if ye then.” If you've been raised with Christ, how does a man live on Monday morning? Chapter 3 is the answer, and it's very practical: your thoughts, your temper, your mouth, your marriage, your boss.

Part one

Look up

Verses 1–4

You've been raised with Christ. Start looking like it.

Where you point your mind

“If ye then be risen” means since you have been. When Christ rose, everyone who trusts Him rose with Him, into a life that can't be buried again.

“Affection” is an old word for mind. Set your mind on Christ, where He is. That isn't daydreaming past your real life (the rest of this chapter is about work and temper). It's where your thinking aims.

The bills and the old crowd are still there. They just lose the vote.

Dead, and hidden

“Ye are dead.” The old you, who ran your life and did all that damage, died with Christ.

“Hid” means kept safe, locked away where no thief can reach it. Your life is in Christ, and Christ is in God.

And it won't stay hidden: “then shall ye also appear with him in glory.” Where you are now is not where your story ends. If you fell yesterday, your hidden life didn't move.

Part two

Off with the old

Verses 5–11

Some things have to go. Paul names them plainly.

This is what a man in his old life lived on. Nobody here needs it explained twice, so Paul's list stands plain.

“Mortify” means put to death. Not hate yourself: that never worked. Stop feeding it, cut it down, and keep cutting. Roots dry up when nothing waters them.

“For if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die: but if ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live.”

Romans 8:13

You don't cut it down alone.

Notice who does the killing: “through the Spirit.” The Spirit already lives in every believer. You're not doing this to earn anything, and you're not doing it with willpower alone.

And notice “shall live.” The goal isn't a man who is only holding himself back. It's a man who is alive.

One thing for today: name the one on that list that gets you most, and tell it to one brother today.

That was then

The old road · v. 6

“the wrath of God”

God's settled anger at what wrecks people and the people made in His image. It “cometh on the children of disobedience.” Paul isn't hiding it, and he isn't pointing it at you.

The new road · v. 7

“ye also walked some time”

“Some time” means once, in the past. “When ye lived in them.” Paul says lived, not live. That was your address. It isn't any more.

“Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him.”

Romans 5:9

The wrath Paul warns about is the wrath Christ took on the cross. Any man who trusts Him is saved from it. So we put these things to death out of thanks, not out of fear.

A change of clothes

Paul's picture is plain: put off, like taking off a filthy work shirt, and put on, like a clean one. The list on the left is the old wardrobe: temper, rage, hate, curses, dirty talk, lies.

Off it comes. “Ye have put off the old man with his deeds” is already done in Christ. So put off in practice what is already off in fact.

Then dress. “Have put on the new man, … renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him.” You don't have to build a new man. You put on the one God made.

The old life ran on lies.

Every man here knows it. You lie to cover the using, the money, where you were, how you feel. A lie is the first thing an old life needs to survive, and honesty is the first thing a new one needs.

Notice “one to another.” Paul is talking about the people in the church, the men in this group. A group where nobody tells the truth is just a room.

One thing for today: tell one true thing you've been hiding to one man you trust. It will be smaller out loud than it is in your head.

Part three

On with the new

Verses 12–17

Mercy, patience, forgiveness, and love to hold it together.

The bond of perfectness

First, who you are: “the elect of God, holy and beloved.” You put these on because you already belong to Him. “Bowels of mercies” is an old way of saying gut-level compassion.

Left alone, these scatter. A man can be patient on Tuesday and cruel on Wednesday.

“Above all these things put on charity.” Love is the band that ties the rest together so they hold. “Bond” is the word for a ligament, what keeps a body in one piece.

Forgiving, as you were

Paul expects a quarrel: “if any man have a quarrel against any.” Somebody has done you wrong. Forgiving doesn't say it didn't hurt, and it doesn't hand him your trust back. It means you let go of collecting.

Why? “Even as Christ forgave you.” He cancelled a bigger bill than anyone owes you.

“And you, being dead in your sins and the uncircumcision of your flesh, hath he quickened together with him, having forgiven you all trespasses;”

Colossians 2:13

What forgiving is not

  • “It didn't hurt.”it did, and Paul never pretends otherwise
  • “I trust him again.”trust is earned back slowly; forgiveness is a gift
  • “I'll stay where I'm being hurt.”if you're in danger, get out and get help
  • “I'm done collecting.”I hand the debt to God, the way Christ handed mine to the cross

And if you're the man who owes someone an apology, the same grace that covers you tells you to go and make it right.

Let peace be the umpire

“Rule” was the word for an umpire at the games, the man who makes the call. Let Christ's peace make yours.

An umpire works from the rulebook, and Paul's next line is “let the word of Christ dwell in you.” Peace never overrules Scripture. And fear isn't a foul: the call is the peace that settles in after the right choice.

Let it move in

“Dwell” means live here, not visit on Sunday. A man can't take a week's worth of Word on Sunday and live on it until Saturday. Give it a room in the day:

  1. MorningOne verse before the phone. Say it out loud.
  2. At workThe same verse on a card in your pocket.
  3. EveningSing it, or text it to one of the guys.
  4. NightSay thank you for three things, out loud.

“Teaching and admonishing one another.” The Word moves in faster when brothers help. “Admonish” means warn and set straight, in love. It's what this group is for.

Whatever you do, sign it with His name.

A man's name is his word and his reputation. “In the name of the Lord Jesus” means: the way He would, and with His name attached. “Word or deed” covers the mouth and the hands: the shift, the parking lot, the group text.

That's why Paul now moves straight to the kitchen and the job site. He doesn't ask for church-only faith.

Part four

Home and work

Verses 18–25

Christ goes where you go: the kitchen table and the job site.

In a Roman house, the head of the home held nearly all the power. Paul gives the one with the power the heavier orders: love, don't be harsh, don't wind them up, be fair. Nobody gets a free pass.

On wives: “as it is fit in the Lord” makes it a willing yielding, not a doormat and never an excuse for harshness. Good Bible teachers differ on how far it reaches, but this is plain: the man's side is the harder one, and it is measured by Christ.

thenthey lose heart.

“Discouraged” means they give up trying: nothing they do will ever be good enough. And “not bitter” for husbands means sharp and sour: the harsh word, the cold shoulder.

Some of you grew up on the wrong end of that, and some of you were that father. Both are in this room. If you blew it, the road back is short: “I was wrong, and I'm sorry,” said to the child, without an excuse after it.

If you're not married, or you've lost the marriage, these verses still tell you what a man is for, and what God can teach you for whoever He puts in front of you next. It's never too late to start being that man today.

Whoever's watching

“Eyeservice” is work done only while the boss's eye is on you. “Menpleasers” work for the look of it.

So what do you do when the boss walks out? “Whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord.” Same speed, same care, same honesty with the clock. The One who is really your boss never leaves the site.

For us: the first honest job after a bad stretch is how a man gets a name back. Be early, do what you said, don't take what isn't yours, and don't quit on the hard Tuesday.

A slave who inherits.

Slavery was part of the Roman world, and a slave owned nothing, least of all an inheritance. Paul never says the system was good. But look at what he does: he tells the slave that he will be an heir, that his real Master is Christ, and (4:1) that every earthly master has a Master in heaven. Within a few years he would send a runaway slave back with a letter that said “a brother beloved.” We'll meet him in chapter 4.

Then v. 25: “he that doeth wrong shall receive for the wrong which he hath done: and there is no respect of persons.” God plays no favorites. Not for the boss, not for the worker, not for the friend, not for the stranger. Wrong has real results here, even for forgiven men: records, debts, trust that has to be earned back. But the cross already covers the judgment.

Points to remember

  1. Look up“Set your affection on things above” 3:2You've been raised with Christ. Point your mind at Him, and the earthly stuff loses its vote.
  2. Put to death“Mortify therefore your members which are upon the earth” 3:5Name the old sins plainly, starve them, and let the Spirit do the killing.
  3. New clothes“Ye have put off the old man … and have put on the new man” 3:9–10A change of clothes: it's already done in Christ, so wear it.
  4. Forgiveness“Even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye” 3:13Cancel the debt the way yours was cancelled, and let love tie the rest together.
  5. Peace and the Word“Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly” 3:16Give the Word a room in your day and let Christ's peace make the calls.
  6. Home and work“Whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord” 3:23The same man at home and at work: honest, fair, steady, whoever is watching.

Question 1 of 6

Look up

What does Paul mean by “set your affection on things above”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Point your mind at Christ, where He is, and not at the things on the earth. It isn't ignoring real life. It's choosing what steers it. You've been raised with Christ, and your life is safe with Him.

Question 2 of 6

Put to death

What does a believer do with the sins of his old life?

What verses tell us about this?

“For if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die: but if ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live.”

Romans 8:13

Answer

He puts them to death: names them honestly, stops feeding them, and cuts them down each time they grow back. He does it through the Spirit who lives in him. A fall is not the end: get up and keep cutting.

Question 3 of 6

New clothes

How does a man take off the old life and put on the new?

What verses tell us about this?

“But the father said to his servants, Bring forth the best robe, and put it on him; and put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet:”

Luke 15:22

Answer

Like a change of clothes. In Christ the old man is already off and the new man already on, so he puts off the temper, the lies and the filth, and puts on mercy, kindness and patience. The Father even brings out the best robe for a man coming home.

Question 4 of 6

Forgiveness

Why should a believer forgive, and how far?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then came Peter to him, and said, Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times? Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven.”

Matthew 18:21–22

Answer

Because Christ forgave us a bigger debt than anyone owes us, and He sets the pattern: “even as Christ forgave you.” It goes as far as His forgiveness went. It doesn't mean trusting him again, or staying where you're being hurt.

Question 5 of 6

Peace and the Word

What keeps a believer steady from day to day?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Christ's peace to make the calls, His Word living in him richly, brothers who teach and warn each other, and a thankful heart, with everything done in the Lord Jesus' name.

Question 6 of 6

Home and work

How does Christ change the way a man treats his family and his boss?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He loves his wife and isn't harsh, he doesn't wind his kids up until they quit, and he works as if the Lord is his boss, because He is. If he's failed at any of it, today is a good day to start.
