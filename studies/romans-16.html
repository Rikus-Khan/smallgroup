Romans 16

Every name

The greatest letter Paul ever wrote ends with a list of friends: men and women, slaves and free, each one known and loved. Then a warning, a promise, and a song of praise.

She carried the letter

Cenchrea was Corinth's eastern harbor. Phebe was heading to Rome, and she almost certainly carried this letter: the most important letter ever written, in one woman's travel bag.

“A servant of the church”: the Greek word is diakonos, the same word Paul uses for himself as a minister. Whatever her work was, she did it for the church.

“A succourer” is a helper, someone who stands behind people and looks after them. So Rome is to look after her. Nobody is too ordinary to carry something big for God.

A couple who kept moving

Rome to Corinth, about AD 49, when “Claudius had commanded all Jews to depart from Rome” (Acts 18:2). There they met Paul.

Corinth to Ephesus, about AD 52: they sailed with Paul and stayed on. A church met in their house (1 Corinthians 16:19).

Back to Rome by about AD 57, with a church in their house again.

Somewhere they “laid down their own necks”: risked their lives for Paul. Wherever they landed, their door was open.

Most of these people are never mentioned again. No sermons, no miracles. Just faithful, ordinary believers that Paul remembered by name, and what each one meant to him. If God's Word stops to name them, you're not a number to Him either.

“And they compel one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country, the father of Alexander and Rufus, to bear his cross.”

Mark 15:21

Mark names Simon's sons as if his readers knew them, and Mark most likely wrote for the church at Rome. So this Rufus may be the son of the man who carried Jesus' cross. We can't be sure, but it would be just like God.

And she had mothered Paul too. Some of you know that: the people who took you in when your own family couldn't are family. Thank one of them today.

One family

Look who's on the wall. Jews (“my kinsmen”) and Gentiles. Men and women. “Them which are of Aristobulus' household” and “the household of Narcissus” were the servants and slaves of big Roman houses. Several of the other names were ones Romans often gave to slaves.

In Rome those people would never sit at the same table. In the church, they greeted each other with “an holy kiss”: the warm, family greeting of that day. For us it's a handshake, a hug, a hand on the shoulder.

Some of the men you sit with today came from very different places than you: different jobs, different records, different neighborhoods. Greet them like family anyway. That's what the church is.

Part three

Watch out, and look up

Verses 17–20

Smooth talkers, a wisdom worth having, and a promise about the enemy.

Mark them, and avoid them

What you hear

“Good words and fair speeches”

Smooth, friendly, convincing. They sound like they care about you.

What they serve

“Their own belly”

Their appetite, their money, their following. Not the Lord Jesus Christ.

“Mark” means keep an eye on, notice. “Avoid” means turn away. This isn't about brothers who disagree over food and holy days (that was chapter 14). It's about people who split the church by pulling against the gospel you were taught.

You've met the type: the guy with the smooth pitch who wants your money, your loyalty, or you back in the old life. “One won't hurt.” “Those church people are fakes.” Notice where he's leading. Then walk the other way.

Wise unto good. Simple concerning evil.

Get wise here

That which is good

Study it. Practice it. Get good at being faithful, honest, kind and steady. Become an expert in the things of God.

Stay simple here

Evil

“Simple” means unmixed, innocent. You don't need to know more about it. You've had enough education there already.

A lot of us were experts in evil. We knew every angle. That knowledge doesn't need to be kept sharp. Let it go dull. Don't go back to “just look,” to check what the old crowd is doing, or to see how close you can get. Spend your curiosity on good.

The first promise, kept

“And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.”

Genesis 3:15

Jesus crushed the serpent's head at the cross. Now it's under your feet. The God of peace does the crushing; you get to stand on the victory.

“Shortly.” The fight you're in won't last forever. The enemy who whispers that you're finished is the one who's finished.

Paul spoke the letter, and Tertius wrote it down, then slipped in a line of his own. Gaius opened his home to Paul and to the whole church. Erastus was the city's treasurer. A stone found at Corinth names an Erastus who paid for a paved street. It may be the same man.

The letter that has changed more lives than almost any other was written at somebody's table, by a team: a city official, a scribe, a host, a young helper. Your part may be small. It still counts.

He is able

“Stablish” means make you stand firm. Paul doesn't end by telling the Romans to hold themselves up. He ends by pointing to the One who is “of power” to do it.

Romans 1:5, the first page

“…for obedience to the faith among all nations, for his name:”

Romans 16:26, the last page

“…made known to all nations for the obedience of faith:”

The letter ends where it began. The “mystery” kept quiet for ages is now out in the open: God saves people from every nation, Jew and Gentile alike, the same way, by faith in Jesus Christ.

And the last word isn't about Paul or the Romans. “To God only wise, be glory through Jesus Christ for ever. Amen.”

If you fell yesterday, chapter 3 says you're not the only one: all have sinned. Chapter 5 says peace with God isn't earned; it's received by faith. Chapter 8 says there's no condemnation for the man in Christ, and chapters 6 and 7 say the fight with the old life is real and normal.

Chapter 10 says whosoever, and that includes you. And chapters 12 to 16 hand you today: your body, your mouth, your money, your friendships, given to God. That's Romans. Start it again any time you need it.

Points to remember

  1. Serving“Phebe our sister, which is a servant of the church which is at Cenchrea” 16:1The greatest letter traveled with a servant. No one is too ordinary to carry something big for God.
  2. Friends“Who have for my life laid down their own necks” 16:4Real friends risk something for you, and keep their door open wherever they go.
  3. Known by name“Salute Rufus chosen in the Lord, and his mother and mine” 16:13God's Word stops to name ordinary believers. You're known by name too.
  4. Divisions“mark them which cause divisions and offences contrary to the doctrine which ye have learned; and avoid them” 16:17Notice where the smooth talker is leading, and walk the other way.
  5. Wisdom“wise unto that which is good, and simple concerning evil” 16:19Become an expert in good. Let your knowledge of evil go dull.
  6. Victory“the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly” 16:20God crushes the enemy and lets you stand on the victory. The fight won't last forever.

Question 1 of 6

Serving

Who carried this letter to Rome?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Phebe, a servant of the church at Cenchrea, Corinth's harbor. Paul commends her and asks the Romans to receive her and help her, because she had been a helper of many, and of Paul himself. Paul trusted his greatest letter to a faithful servant.

Question 2 of 6

Friends

What did Priscilla and Aquila risk for Paul?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Their lives. They “laid down their own necks” to save Paul's life, and all the Gentile churches were thankful. They also opened their home: in Corinth, in Ephesus and now in Rome, a church met in their house.

Question 3 of 6

Known by name

Why does Paul fill a whole chapter with names?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the gospel isn't just ideas. It's people. Paul knew these believers, what they'd suffered, how hard they'd worked, and what they meant to him. God's Word stops to name ordinary men and women, slaves and free. Every one of them was known and loved, and so are you.

Question 4 of 6

Divisions

Who are we told to mark and avoid?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

People who cause divisions and stumbling blocks against the doctrine we've been taught. They talk smooth and sound kind, but they serve their own appetites, not Christ, and they fool people who don't see it coming. Notice them, and turn away.

Question 5 of 6

Wisdom

How much do we need to know about evil?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Very little. Paul wants us “wise unto that which is good, and simple concerning evil”: experts in what's good, and innocent about evil. We don't need to keep our knowledge of the old life sharp. We need to grow in the things of God.

Question 6 of 6

Victory

What will the God of peace do to Satan?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Bruise him under our feet, and shortly. It's the promise of Genesis 3:15, kept at the cross. God does the crushing, and He's the One “of power to stablish you,” so you stand firm until the fight is over.
