Joel 3

The valley of decision

God calls the nations to account for how they treated His people, and the book ends with a fountain, and the LORD at home in Zion.

Things to watch for

Joel spoke to Judah, the southern kingdom, after a locust plague. Chapter 2 promised restoration. Now he looks further ahead. Keep these questions in mind as we read.

Part one

For my people

Verses 1–8

The nations thought nobody was watching what they did to Judah. Somebody was.

The valley of Jehoshaphat

יְהוֹשָׁפָט said Yehoshaphat “the LORD judges”

Nobody knows for sure where this valley is. Later tradition put it in the Kidron valley, right beside Jerusalem. Joel may have picked the name for what it means: this is the place where the LORD judges. (Jehoshaphat was also a good king of Judah who once watched God fight for him in a valley, 2 Chronicles 20.)

“Bring again the captivity” means to restore, to bring the captives home. God gathers the nations to answer for His people: “my people,” “my heritage,” “my land.” He takes it personally.

A boy for a harlot. A girl for a drink.

The enemies who raided Judah divided the captives by drawing lots, like loot. Then they traded a boy for a night with a prostitute and sold a girl for the price of some wine.

That's what sin does to people. It makes a human being worth one night's lust or one drink. Some men here have seen people used like that, or have been used like that. God saw every bit of it, and He calls it what it is.

“And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.”

Genesis 12:3, God's promise to Abraham

Sold far away

Tyre and Zidon were rich port cities up the coast, traders by sea. “Palestine” here means the Philistines, on the southern coast. They plundered Judah's silver and gold, even the treasures of God's house, and sold Judah's children to the Greeks, far across the sea, where they'd never find their way home.

God's answer comes in verse 8: their own children will be sold the other way, to the Sabeans, traders in faraway Arabia.

It comes back around

Tyre, Zidon, Philistia→sold→Judah's children, to the GreciansVerse 6: they thought it was just business.
The LORD→will sell→their sons and daughters, to the SabeansVerse 8: “for the LORD hath spoken it.”

Notice who does the paying back: God, not Judah's own revenge. And first He promises to raise His scattered people out of the places they were sold to.

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

Some of us carry a list of people who owe us. Resentment keeps a man drinking at that list. God says: hand it to Me. I see it, and I will settle it.

Part two

The valley of decision

Verses 9–17

God calls the nations out to fight. It isn't a fight. It's a verdict.

Swords and plowshares

Joel 3:10, the nations

“Beat your plowshares into swords, and your pruninghooks into spears…”

Isaiah 2:4, when the Lord reigns

“And he shall judge among the nations, and shall rebuke many people: and they shall beat their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruninghooks: nation shall not lift up sword against nation, neither shall they learn war any more.”

Here God is almost daring the nations: Bring everything. Melt down your farm tools for weapons. Let the weakling call himself a soldier. It won't be enough. (“Let the weak say, I am strong” is often quoted as comfort, but here it's the nations' empty boast.)

Isaiah saw the other side. When the Lord has judged, the swords go back to being plowshares. War isn't the last word. Peace is.

When the harvest is ripe

Joel pictures judgment as harvest time: the grain is ripe for the sickle, the winepress is full, the vats run over. The book of Revelation picks up the same picture for the last judgment (Revelation 14:15).

Grain isn't cut green. God waits a long time, giving room to turn. But when wickedness is full, the harvest comes.

Multitudes, multitudes

The word for “decision” means something cut sharp: a verdict. This isn't the crowd making up its mind. It's the valley where God hands down His decision on the nations. Joel says it twice, because the crowd is past counting.

Preachers sometimes use this verse to call people to decide for Christ. Joel's point is sterner: one day the time to decide is over. That's exactly why today matters.

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

Hope when everything shakes

The LORD roars like a lion from Zion, and heaven and earth shake. The same voice that terrifies His enemies is the safest place in the world for His people.

“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea;”

Psalm 46:1–2

“No strangers pass through her any more” means no more invaders tramping through Jerusalem. God's city is finally safe.

Part three

The LORD dwelleth in Zion

Verses 18–21

The book that began with bare vines ends with a fountain.

A fountain for the dry valley

Shittim means acacia trees, the tough trees that grow in dry, rocky ground. Water from God's house runs down into the driest place there is. Ezekiel saw the same river (Ezekiel 47), and so did John:

“And he shewed me a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb.”

Revelation 22:1

Chapter 1 had the new wine “cut off.” Now the hills drip with it. It's a picture of God's plenty after the famine, not a call back to the cup. The man who drank to fill a dry place can drink from this river instead.

Egypt, Edom, and Judah

Egypt and Edom

Desolate

Israel's oldest enemies: Egypt once enslaved them, and Edom, Esau's people, turned on their own brother Judah. They shed innocent blood.

Judah and Jerusalem

For ever

“From generation to generation.” The people who were sold and scattered end up the ones who stay.

Verse 21 is hard to translate, and Bible teachers read it two ways: either God will no longer leave His people's innocent blood unavenged, or He will cleanse His people of guilt He hadn't yet cleansed. Either way, the last word is about God: “for the LORD dwelleth in Zion.”

The whole book

Joel 1Ruin

“joy is withered away”

Locusts, drought, empty barns, and a prophet crying out.

Joel 2Return

“I will restore”

Rend your heart. God answers with rain, harvest, and His Spirit.

Joel 3Home

“the LORD dwelleth in Zion”

The wrongs set right, and God living among His people.

Joel ends where the whole Bible ends: not just with the land fixed, but with God at home among His people.

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.”

Revelation 21:3

Points to remember

  1. God's justice“For my people and for my heritage” 3:2God sees every wrong done to His people, and He takes it personally.
  2. Payback“I will return your recompence upon your own head” 3:7Settling scores is God's job. Hand Him the list.
  3. God's patience“The harvest is ripe” 3:13God waits long so people can turn, but the harvest does come.
  4. The day of the LORD“Multitudes in the valley of decision” 3:14There God gives His verdict. Today is the day to call on Him.
  5. Hope“The LORD will be the hope of his people” 3:16When heaven and earth shake, His people are safe in Him.
  6. God's presence“For the LORD dwelleth in Zion” 3:21The story ends with God at home among His people.

Question 1 of 6

God's justice

Does God see how His people are mistreated?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.”

Genesis 12:3

Answer

Yes. He saw the captives divided by lot and the children sold for a harlot and a drink, and He calls them “my people” and “my heritage.” God takes what is done to His people personally. Nobody who has been used and thrown away is invisible to Him.

Question 2 of 6

Payback

Who gets to settle the score?

What verses tell us about this?

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

Answer

God does. In Joel, He is the one who pays back Tyre, Zidon, and the Philistines, and He is the one who raises His people out of the places they were sold. Vengeance belongs to Him. That frees us to drop the resentment we carry, because the score will be settled by Someone who never gets it wrong.

Question 3 of 6

God's patience

Why does God wait so long to judge?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

“And another angel came out of the temple, crying with a loud voice to him that sat on the cloud, Thrust in thy sickle, and reap: for the time is come for thee to reap; for the harvest of the earth is ripe.”

Revelation 14:15

Answer

Because He is longsuffering and wants men to repent, not perish. Like a farmer who doesn't cut the grain green, He gives time. But the waiting has an end. When wickedness is full, the harvest comes. God's patience is an invitation, not a sign that He doesn't care.

Question 4 of 6

The day of the LORD

What is decided in the valley of decision?

What verses tell us about this?

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

Answer

God's verdict. The valley of decision is where the LORD sits as Judge over the nations. The multitudes don't decide there. He does. That's why the decision to call on Him has to be made now, while it's still the day of salvation.

Question 5 of 6

Hope

Where can a man find hope when everything shakes?

What verses tell us about this?

“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea;”

Psalm 46:1–2

“Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Answer

In the Lord Himself. When heaven and earth shake, “the LORD will be the hope of his people, and the strength” of His children. Life can shake a man hard: lost jobs, broken families, cravings that won't quit. God is a very present help in trouble, and He has promised never to leave His own.

Question 6 of 6

God's presence

What is the best promise at the end of Joel?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.”

Revelation 21:3

Answer

God with His people. Joel's last words aren't about harvests or enemies. They're “the LORD dwelleth in Zion.” The best thing God gives is Himself. Revelation ends the same way: God dwelling with men. And today every believer already has God's Spirit living in him.
