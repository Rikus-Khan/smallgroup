Zechariah 3

A brand plucked out of the fire

Joshua the high priest stands before God in filthy clothes while Satan accuses him. God rebukes the accuser, takes the filth away, and dresses him clean.

Part one

Filthy garments

Verses 1–5

A courtroom in heaven. The high priest is on trial, and he has nothing clean to wear.

A courtroom in heaven

Quick reminder: Zechariah was a priest and prophet in Jerusalem about 520 BC, after the exile. The Jews were home from Babylon under Persian rule, rebuilding a temple that was only half done. In one night God showed him eight visions. This is the fourth.

  1. The Judgethe angel of the LORD
  2. On trialJoshua the high priest
  3. The accuserSatan, at his right hand

Joshua was the real high priest of that day (Haggai 1:1). He stands for the whole nation: when he goes on trial, they all do. And in verse 2 the angel of the LORD speaks as the LORD Himself. Many Bible teachers believe this is the Son of God, long before He was born at Bethlehem.

The accuser

שָׂטָן said satan the adversary, the one who accuses

“To resist him” is the same Hebrew word: to accuse him. In a court of that day, the accuser stood at the right hand of the man on trial. Satan is doing his old job.

“And I heard a loud voice saying in heaven, Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night.”

Revelation 12:10

Here's what hurts: most of what he says is true. Joshua is dirty (v. 3). That's how the accuser works on us too: “Look what you did. Look what you are.”

A brand plucked out of the fire.

A “brand” is a stick that's already burning, snatched out before it turns to ashes. Israel had just come out of the fire of the exile, scorched and smoking.

Notice how God answers Satan. He doesn't say, “Joshua isn't that bad.” He says, I chose Jerusalem, and I pulled this man out of the fire. The defense rests on God, not on Joshua's record.

“I have overthrown some of you, as God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah, and ye were as a firebrand plucked out of the burning: yet have ye not returned unto me, saith the LORD.”

Amos 4:11

Some of us came out of the fire with the smoke still on us. The scorch marks don't mean God didn't want you. They mean He reached into the fire to get you.

Dressed in filth

The Hebrew word for “filthy” here is the strongest the Old Testament has. It's used for human waste. This isn't a little dust on his robe.

And this is the high priest, the one man whose clothes had to be spotless to stand before God (Exodus 28). If even he is filthy, what about everybody else?

“But we are all as an unclean thing, and all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags; and we all do fade as a leaf; and our iniquities, like the wind, have taken us away.”

Isaiah 64:6

Nobody in this room is pretending. Joshua can't hide it, and God doesn't pretend not to see it. That's actually good news: grace starts where pretending stops.

Who did the washing?

  • Joshua scrubbed himself cleanhe couldn't
  • Joshua argued his casehe never says a word
  • Joshua promised to do betterthere's no promise in the chapter
  • “Take away the filthy garments from him”God did it, with a word

And God says what it means: “I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee.” Not just the dirty clothes, but the guilt itself, gone.

Taken off, and put on

filthy garments → change of raiment fine, clean clothes, the kind a man wore on a feast day

God doesn't strip a man and leave him standing there bare. He takes the filth off and puts something clean on.

“I will greatly rejoice in the LORD, my soul shall be joyful in my God; for he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decketh himself with ornaments, and as a bride adorneth herself with her jewels.”

Isaiah 61:10

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Zechariah speaks up

Zechariah is only watching, but he gets so caught up that he calls out: put the turban on him too! The “mitre” is the high priest's turban.

“And thou shalt make a plate of pure gold, and grave upon it, like the engravings of a signet, HOLINESS TO THE LORD.”

Exodus 28:36

Joshua doesn't just get clean clothes. He gets his calling back. The man who was on trial is wearing “Holiness to the LORD” on his forehead again, and the angel of the LORD stands right there beside him.

Part two

Walk in my ways

Verses 6–7

Joshua is clean. Now God gives him something to do.

First clean, then the charge

“Protested” means solemnly charged. Now look at the order. In verse 4 Joshua was made clean with no “if” at all. The “if” comes after, and it's about serving in God's house, not about getting clean.

ifwalk in my ways, keep my charge
→
thenrun my house, keep my courts, walk among those who stand by

“These that stand by” are the angels in the vision. A man who was on trial a minute ago is promised a place to walk in God's own presence.

“For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.”

Ephesians 2:10

Part three

My servant the BRANCH

Verses 8–10

Joshua and the priests are a picture of Someone still to come.

A shoot from a cut-down tree

“Men wondered at” means men who are a sign. What just happened to Joshua is a preview. Something bigger is coming, and its name is the BRANCH.

  1. about 1000 BCDavid is king
  2. 586the tree cut down: no king left
  3. about 520Zechariah: still a stump
  4. to comethe BRANCH grows

“Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper, and shall execute judgment and justice in the earth.”

Jeremiah 23:5

The BRANCH is a new shoot out of David's cut-down family tree: the promised King. The New Testament opens by calling Jesus “the son of David” (Matthew 1:1). Chapter 6 will say more.

Sin removed in one day

The stone is most likely a stone for the temple they were rebuilding. God Himself will carve it, and it has “seven eyes”: seven is the number of completeness, and chapter 4 says these are “the eyes of the LORD.” He sees all of it.

The high priest's sacrifices

every year

over and over, and never finished

What God promises here

one day

sin removed, once

Zechariah doesn't say which day. The New Testament tells us how God dealt with sin once for all:

“But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;”

Hebrews 10:12

Under the vine and under the fig tree.

This is an old picture of peace: every man sitting in the shade of his own yard, safe, and calling his neighbor over to sit with him. No enemy at the gate, nobody to fear.

“But they shall sit every man under his vine and under his fig tree; and none shall make them afraid: for the mouth of the LORD of hosts hath spoken it.”

Micah 4:4

This promise is made to Israel, and God will keep it to Israel. But the order holds for us too: once God deals with sin, peace follows, with Him first, and then with the people around us.

Points to remember

  1. Accused“The LORD rebuke thee, O Satan” 3:2God Himself answers the accuser. He doesn't deny the dirt. He defends the man.
  2. Rescued“A brand plucked out of the fire” 3:2God reached into the fire because He chose to save, not because we weren't scorched.
  3. Cleansed“Take away the filthy garments from him” 3:4God does the washing. Joshua brought nothing but his filth.
  4. New clothes“I will clothe thee with change of raiment” 3:4God doesn't leave a forgiven man bare. He dresses him in clean clothes.
  5. Walking“If thou wilt walk in my ways” 3:7Cleansing came first, and free. Walking with God is how a clean man lives and serves.
  6. The BRANCH“My servant the BRANCH” 3:8The King from David's line, who would take sin away in one day: Jesus.

Question 1 of 6

Accused

What does God say to the one who accuses us?

What verses tell us about this?

“Who shall lay any thing to the charge of God’s elect? It is God that justifieth. Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died, yea rather, that is risen again, who is even at the right hand of God, who also maketh intercession for us.”

Romans 8:33–34

“And I heard a loud voice saying in heaven, Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night.”

Revelation 12:10

Answer

“The LORD rebuke thee.” God doesn't argue that we're clean. He shuts the accuser down because He has chosen to save. For a believer, the case is closed: God is the One who justifies, and Jesus speaks up for us. When the old voice says “look what you are,” the answer is “look whose I am.”

Question 2 of 6

Rescued

Why would God pull a man out of the fire?

What verses tell us about this?

“I have overthrown some of you, as God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah, and ye were as a firebrand plucked out of the burning: yet have ye not returned unto me, saith the LORD.”

Amos 4:11

“And the Lord said, Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat: But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Luke 22:31–32

Answer

Because He wants him. A burning stick isn't worth much, but God reached in anyway. He didn't wait for Joshua to cool off and clean up. He pulled him out, still smoking, because of His own mercy. And like Peter, a man who's been pulled out can turn around and help pull others out.

Question 3 of 6

Cleansed

Who takes our filthy garments away?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come now, and let us reason together, saith the LORD: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool.”

Isaiah 1:18

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

God does. Joshua didn't scrub, argue, or promise. God gave the word, and the filth and the guilt were gone. That's how salvation works: we can't clean ourselves up enough for God. We come as we are, trust Jesus, and He makes us clean.

Question 4 of 6

New clothes

What does God put on a forgiven man?

What verses tell us about this?

“I will greatly rejoice in the LORD, my soul shall be joyful in my God; for he hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decketh himself with ornaments, and as a bride adorneth herself with her jewels.”

Isaiah 61:10

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Answer

Clean clothes: “the robe of righteousness.” Jesus took our sin, and God puts Christ's righteousness on us. A forgiven man isn't just a man with the dirt gone. He's dressed for the feast, and he gets his calling back, like Joshua with his mitre.

Question 5 of 6

Walking

Once God has made us clean, how do we walk?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

“For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.”

Ephesians 2:10

Answer

In His ways. The cleansing came first, as a gift, with no “if.” A believer stays clean in God's sight; he doesn't go back into the filthy clothes when he stumbles. But walking in God's ways is how a clean man lives, how he enjoys God's company, and how he gets to serve in God's house.

Question 6 of 6

The BRANCH

Who is the BRANCH?

What verses tell us about this?

“Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper, and shall execute judgment and justice in the earth.”

Jeremiah 23:5

“But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;”

Hebrews 10:12

Answer

The promised King from David's family, a new shoot from a tree that had been cut down. God calls Him “my servant,” and ties His coming to sin being removed in one day. Jesus is the son of David, and by one sacrifice He dealt with sin for ever.
