Micah 7

When I fall, I shall arise

Micah looks around and finds no one to trust. So he looks up, and his book ends with the best question in the Bible: “Who is a God like unto thee?”

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. DisappointmentWhat do you do when everyone around you lets you down?
  2. FallingIs a fall the end of the road?
  3. Owning itHow should a man carry the cost of his own sin?
  4. Who God isWho is a God like ours?

  6. God's promisesWill God keep His promises?

Part one

Woe is me

Verses 1–6

Micah walks through Judah like a hungry man in a vineyard after the harvest.

Nothing left on the vine

Micah, who preached to Judah about 740 to 700 BC, pictures himself in a vineyard after the pickers are done. “Grapegleanings” were the few grapes left behind for the poor. He's hungry for one good cluster, and there isn't one.

That's what he finds when he goes looking for a good man: none upright among men. They lie in wait like hunters, and the prey is their own brother.

“As it is written, There is none righteous, no, not one:”

Romans 3:10

Everybody's in on it

They do evil “with both hands earnestly”: not by accident, but working hard at it. And the men at the top run the deal together:

the princeasksthe judgeasks for a bribethe great mannames what he wants“so they wrap it up”

A “reward” here is a bribe. To “wrap it up” is to twist the strands together like a rope: one man's greed tied to the next, until nobody can pull out.

Even the best of them is a brier, a thorn bush: get close and you get cut. “The day of thy watchmen” is the day the prophets, God's lookouts on the wall, had warned them about. It was coming.

Enemies at home

It's so bad a man can't tell his secrets even to his own wife. Sons mock their fathers. Families turn on each other. Jesus quoted these very words when He sent out the twelve:

Micah, about 700 BC

“…the daughter in law against her mother in law; a man’s enemies are the men of his own house.”

Micah 7:6

Jesus, sending out the twelve

“For I am come to set a man at variance against his father, and the daughter against her mother, and the daughter in law against her mother in law. And a man’s foes shall be they of his own household.”

Matthew 10:35–36

Some of us know this one from the inside: the people who should have stood by us didn't. And sometimes following Jesus costs you at home. But you're not left alone:

“When my father and my mother forsake me, then the LORD will take me up.”

Psalm 27:10

Part two

I shall arise

Verses 7–10

One word turns the whole chapter around: “Therefore.”

Look up

Around me (verses 1–6)

No one to trust

No good man left. No friend to confide in. Enemies in my own house.

Above me (verse 7)

My God will hear me

I will look to the LORD. I will wait for Him. He will hear me.

“Therefore”: because everyone around him has failed, Micah turns to the One who doesn't. People let you down. That's the time to look up, not to give up.

And notice the little word my: “the God of my salvation … my God will hear me.” This isn't a far-off God. He's Micah's own.

Down, but not out

Here the prophet speaks for God's people as one man, and the “enemy” is the nation gloating over Jerusalem's fall. He doesn't make excuses: “because I have sinned against him.” And he doesn't stay down: “I shall arise.”

A fall, even a relapse, is not the end of a believer's road. Get up. Not by gritting your teeth in the dark, but because the LORD is your light.

“For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.”

Proverbs 24:16

Bearing it, then brought out

Micah 7:8–9In the dark

“I will bear the indignation of the LORD.” He owns it and carries the cost without blaming anybody.

Micah 7:9Until

“Until he plead my cause.” The God he sinned against becomes the One who takes up his case.

Micah 7:9Into the light

“He will bring me forth to the light, and I shall behold his righteousness.”

Some consequences of sin stay with a man even after God forgives him. Carry them humbly. They don't pay for your sin (Jesus did that), but God can use them to shape you. And they have an “until.”

“My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:”

1 John 2:1

And the voice that sneered, “Where is the LORD thy God?” (verse 10) will go quiet.

Part three

A building day

Verses 11–17

After the ruins, God promises walls going up and people coming home.

From sea to sea

Jerusalem's walls would be torn down by Babylon. Micah sees the day they go back up. It began after the exile, when Nehemiah rebuilt them, about 445 BC. But Micah sees further than that.

People come from everywhere: from Assyria, from the fortress cities (many think of Egypt), from “the river” (the Euphrates), from sea to sea. And “the decree” against them is “far removed.”

But verse 13 comes first: “Notwithstanding the land shall be desolate.” The ruin is real, the fruit of their own doings. God's building day comes after it, not instead of it.

A prayer, and an answer

Micah prays (verse 14)Feed thy people with thy rod, the flock of thine heritage … let them feed in Bashan and Gilead, as in the days of old.

The LORD answers (verse 15)According to the days of thy coming out of the land of Egypt will I shew unto him marvellous things.

The “rod” is a shepherd's staff, for guiding and guarding sheep. Carmel, Bashan, and Gilead were the richest grazing land Israel had. Gilead, east of the Jordan, had already been lost to Assyria about 733 BC (2 Kings 15:29). Micah asks God to give it back.

And God answers: I'll do it again, like when I brought you out of Egypt. The God who did wonders then still does them.

“Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me.”

Psalm 23:4

Speechless

The nations that bragged about their strength will be put to shame by what God does. Micah piles up three pictures:

  • Hand upon their mouthNothing left to say. Job did the same before God (Job 40:4).
  • Lick the dustBeaten flat, face in the dirt, like the serpent in the garden.
  • Out of their holesCrawling out of hiding, trembling.

Every proud power on earth will one day bow to the LORD. Better to bow now, willingly, than later:

“That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.”

Philippians 2:10–11

Part four

Who is like Thee?

Verses 18–20

Micah's own name is a question. He ends his book by answering it.

Micah's name

מִיכָה said Mikah “Who is like the LORD?”

Every time someone called his name, they asked the question: “Who is a God like unto thee?” Now Micah answers it.

What the remnant owed

  • iniquity
  • transgression
  • His anger, earned

Balance duemore than we could pay

Pardonedhe delighteth in mercy

Other gods had to be bribed and calmed down. The LORD's anger is real, but He doesn't hold on to it forever. He delights in mercy. How can a holy God pardon? Because Jesus paid the bill on the cross.

Into the deep

“Subdue” means to tread down, to put under foot. God doesn't just forgive sin's guilt. He breaks its grip.

And the guilt itself? He throws it into the depths of the sea. Not the shallows, where you can wade out and fish it back. The deep, where nobody goes looking.

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

If God has sunk it, stop diving for it. Don't let anyone, yourself included, keep hauling it back up.

Sworn from the days of old

  1. about 2000 BCGod's oath to AbrahamGenesis 22
  2. Jacobthe promise passed onGenesis 28
  3. about 700 BCMicah: “thou wilt perform”
  4. about 5 BCZacharias: “the oath which he sware”Luke 1

Micah ends his book resting on a promise made over a thousand years before him. When John the Baptist was born, his father Zacharias praised God in almost the same words:

“To perform the mercy promised to our fathers, and to remember his holy covenant; The oath which he sware to our father Abraham,”

Luke 1:72–73

Those promises belong to Abraham's children, and God will keep every one of them. What they show us is His character: He has never broken His word to anyone.

“For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen, unto the glory of God by us.”

2 Corinthians 1:20

The whole book of Micah

Micah's book has three parts. Each one opens with “Hear,” and each one moves from judgment to hope:

“Hear, all ye people”

12

Samaria and Jerusalem judged for idols and greed. Then the Breaker leads His flock out through the gate (2:13).

“Hear, O heads of Jacob”

345

Crooked leaders, and Zion plowed like a field. Then swords into plowshares, and a Ruler from Bethlehem.

“Hear ye now”

67

The LORD's lawsuit. Then “I shall arise,” and “Who is a God like unto thee?”

And it worked. A hundred years later, men remembered that King Hezekiah heard Micah, feared the LORD and prayed, and God held back the judgment (Jeremiah 26:18–19). God's warnings are an open door. The last word belongs to His mercy.

Points to remember

  1. Disappointment“Therefore I will look unto the LORD” 7:7When people let you down, look up and wait on Him. He will hear you.
  2. Falling“When I fall, I shall arise” 7:8A fall isn't the end. Get up, with the LORD as your light.
  3. Owning it“Because I have sinned against him” 7:9Own it, carry it humbly, and trust Him to bring you out.
  4. Who God is“Who is a God like unto thee” 7:18No one pardons like He does. He delights in mercy.
  5. Forgiveness“Into the depths of the sea” 7:19Forgiven sin is gone for good. Stop diving for it.
  6. God's promises“Which thou hast sworn unto our fathers” 7:20He has never broken His word, and He never will.

Question 1 of 6

Disappointment

What do you do when everyone around you lets you down?

What verses tell us about this?

“When my father and my mother forsake me, then the LORD will take me up.”

Psalm 27:10

Answer

Look up. Micah couldn't find a good man, a trustworthy friend, or peace at home. So he said, “Therefore I will look unto the LORD.” People will fail you, sometimes the very ones who should have been there. God won't. Look to Him, wait for Him, and count on this: He will hear you.

Question 2 of 6

Falling

Is a fall the end of the road?

What verses tell us about this?

“For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief.”

Proverbs 24:16

“The steps of a good man are ordered by the LORD: and he delighteth in his way. Though he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down: for the LORD upholdeth him with his hand.”

Psalm 37:23–24

Answer

No. A believer can fall, and falling hurts and costs. But God doesn't throw away His own. “When I fall, I shall arise.” That isn't a license to fall. It's a reason not to stay down. Confess it, get up, and walk on with the LORD holding your hand.

Question 3 of 6

Owning it

How should a man carry the cost of his own sin?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

“My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:”

1 John 2:1

Answer

Honestly and humbly. Micah didn't blame anyone: “because I have sinned against him.” He carried the consequences, trusting that they had an “until.” They don't pay for sin; Jesus did that. But God uses them to train us. And we don't stand alone: Jesus pleads our cause.

Question 4 of 6

Who God is

Who is a God like ours?

What verses tell us about this?

“For his anger endureth but a moment; in his favour is life: weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning.”

Psalm 30:5

Answer

Nobody. That's the point of Micah's name. Other gods had to be bribed and calmed down. Our God pardons iniquity, passes by transgression, doesn't hold on to His anger, and delights in mercy. He isn't looking for a reason to punish you. He's glad to forgive.

Question 5 of 6

Forgiveness

What does God do with the sins He forgives?

What verses tell us about this?

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

“For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and their sins and their iniquities will I remember no more.”

Hebrews 8:12

“For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace.”

Romans 6:14

Answer

He gets rid of them for good. He casts them into the depths of the sea, removes them as far as east from west, and remembers them no more. And He subdues them: sin's guilt is gone and its grip is broken. If God won't bring it up again, you don't have to either.

Question 6 of 6

God's promises

Will God keep His promises?

What verses tell us about this?

“To perform the mercy promised to our fathers, and to remember his holy covenant; The oath which he sware to our father Abraham,”

Luke 1:72–73

“For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen, unto the glory of God by us.”

2 Corinthians 1:20

Answer

Every one. He swore an oath to Abraham, and over a thousand years later Micah still counted on it. Then Zacharias saw God keep it in sending the Saviour. God's promises to Israel stand, and every promise He makes to us in Christ is “yea” and “Amen.” You can stake your life on His word.
