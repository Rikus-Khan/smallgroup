Leviticus 16

The day of atonement

Once a year, one man goes in behind the vail with blood, and comes out alive. Two goats: one dies for the people, and one carries all their sins away into the wilderness, never to come back. This is the heart of Leviticus.

We are still at the foot of mount Sinai, in the first weeks after the tabernacle went up. Moses dates this chapter by a funeral: “after the death of the two sons of Aaron.” The day itself was kept every year after, “in the seventh month, on the tenth day of the month” (verse 29).

Part one

“That he die not”

Verses 1–10

A warning to a grieving father, and then the way in: the clothes, the animals, and the two goats.

After the death of the two sons

“And Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, took either of them his censer, and put fire therein, and put incense thereon, and offered strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not. And there went out fire from the LORD, and devoured them, and they died before the LORD.”

Leviticus 10:1–2

Aaron had lost two sons who came near God their own way. Now the LORD speaks to that grieving house, and the first word is a warning: not at all times, “that he die not.” God is holy, and that has not changed since chapter 10.

But listen to the rest of it. He does not say “never.” He says “not at all times,” and then he spends a whole chapter telling Aaron how. The warning comes with a way in. “I will appear in the cloud upon the mercy seat”: he wants to be met.

“But into the second went the high priest alone once every year, not without blood, which he offered for himself, and for the errors of the people:”

Hebrews 9:7

Linen, not gold

Every other day

garments for glory

The ephod, the breastplate with its twelve stones, the robe of blue, the gold plate on the mitre: “for glory and for beauty” (Exodus 28:2).

On this day

plain white linen

A linen coat, linen breeches, a linen girdle, a linen mitre. “Linen” comes four times in verse 4. Nothing gold.

Breeches are short linen drawers worn under the coat. Girded means belted. Attired means dressed. And first he washes: “wash his flesh in water, and so put them on.”

The greatest man in Israel goes in to God dressed like the plainest servant. It is a picture of the one who “made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant” (Philippians 2:7). Nobody walks in there in his own glory.

For himself first

  • For Aaron and his housea young bullock for a sin offering, a ram for a burnt offering
  • For the peopletwo kids of the goats for a sin offering, one ram for a burnt offering

Before Aaron can carry anybody's sin, his own has to be dealt with. “For himself” comes seven times in this chapter. The high priest was a sinner too, and he knew it. A kid is a young goat. His house is his household, his family.

“For such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and made higher than the heavens; Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people’s: for this he did once, when he offered up himself.”

Hebrews 7:26–27

Here is the difference. Aaron needed a bullock for himself. Jesus needed nothing for himself, so all of it could be for you.

Two goats, one sin offering

Verse 5 calls the two goats together “a sin offering”: it takes both to show what God does with sin. Aaron casts lots, and the LORD decides which is which: “The lot is cast into the lap; but the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD” (Proverbs 16:33).

The goat “upon which the LORD's lot fell” dies. Its blood goes within the vail. Sin is paid for.

The other is the scapegoat, the goat that escapes. It is “presented alive,” then walks away into the wilderness carrying the people's sins. Sin is taken away.

Part two

“Within the vail”

Verses 11–19

Incense, a cloud, and blood on the mercy seat, by one man alone.

Behind the vail

First the incense, so “the cloud of the incense may cover the mercy seat… that he die not.” A censer is a pan for live coals; beaten small means ground fine.

Then the blood, “upon the mercy seat eastward,” and seven times before it. Under it lay the tables of the law Israel had broken. The blood went between the broken law and the holy God.

The mercy seat

Paul takes this very place, the lid with blood on it, and says what it was pointing at. Propitiation is the sacrifice that turns away God's wrath; Hebrews 9:5 uses the same word for the mercy seat.

“Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God;”

Romans 3:25

You do not have to hide the law you broke, or pretend you kept it. God set Christ forth as the mercy seat, and his blood is on it. The law is not thrown out. It is covered.

No man with him

Not a priest, not a son, not Moses. The whole camp stood outside and waited, and nobody could help. Aaron went in alone, and atonement was made “for all the congregation of Israel.”

“Who being the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person, and upholding all things by the word of his power, when he had by himself purged our sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high;”

Hebrews 1:3

“By himself.” Nobody helped Jesus pay for sin, and nobody needs to. Your part is not to add to it. Your part is to stand where Israel stood and trust the one who went in.

And look at verse 16: the tabernacle “remaineth among them in the midst of their uncleanness.” God did not move his tent away from a dirty camp. He stayed in the middle of it, and made a way to cleanse it.

Even the altar needed cleansing

  • the horns of the altarthe four corner points of the brasen altar
  • hallowmake holy, set apart again for God
  • reconciling(verse 20) making peace, bringing back together
  • seven timestwice in this chapter: before the mercy seat (14), and upon the altar (19)

A year of a sinful people living around God's house left its mark, even on the holy things. So the blood went everywhere: the most holy place, the tent, the altar. Sin gets into everything. So does the blood.

“How much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?”

Hebrews 9:14

Part three

“The goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities”

Verses 20–28

Both hands on its head, every sin said out loud, and the goat led away. Then the clothes, the washing, and the fire outside the camp.

Both hands, all their sins

Aaron does not wave at the goat. He lays both hands on its head and confesses, out loud. Count the word “all” in verses 21 and 22: four times. Three words for wrong, so nothing slips through:

  • iniquitiescrooked things, what is bent in a man
  • transgressionslines crossed, the rebellion
  • sinsmissing the mark, falling short

God knew the sins already. The confessing was for the people: their sins named, then put on another, then carried out of sight. That is still how it works.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Unto a land not inhabited

A fit man, a man made ready for the job, leads the goat out past the last tent, out where nobody lives, and lets it go. Israel watched their sins walk away, and the goat did not come back.

Psalm 103:12

As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.

John 1:29

The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.

John did not say “behold the man who will teach you to manage your sin.” He said, this is the one who takes it away. Some of you carry something you did years ago as if it were still on your back. It was laid on another, and he carried it off.

Without the camp

Everything that touched the day got washed: the high priest, the man who led the goat, the man who burned the bodies. And the bodies of the bullock and the goat, whose blood had gone in to the mercy seat, were not eaten. They were carried outside the camp and burned to ashes. Hebrews takes us right there:

“For the bodies of those beasts, whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin, are burned without the camp. Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate. Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach.”

Hebrews 13:11–13

Jesus died outside the walls, with the outcasts, in the place for things thrown away. If you have spent years on the outside of decent company, that is where he went to find you. “Let us go forth therefore unto him.” Being with him may cost you some reproach, some scorn. He bore it first.

Follow the high priest

  1. The whole day, step by step. Click to follow Aaron in and out.
  2. 1In at the gate. At the altar he kills the bullock for himself and his house (11).
  3. 2Coals from the altar and incense in his hands, past the vail: the cloud covers the mercy seat (12–13).
  4. 3The bullock's blood: on the mercy seat, and before it seven times (14).
  5. 4He kills the people's goat and brings its blood within the vail too (15).
  6. 5Out to the altar: blood on its horns, and upon it seven times (18–19).
  7. 6The live goat: both hands on its head, the sins confessed, led away into the wilderness (20–22).
  8. 7Into the tent to put off the linen; washed, in his own garments, he comes forth to the burnt offerings (23–24).
  9. 8The bullock and the goat carried forth and burned without the camp (27).

He came out

Think of the people that day, standing outside. Their high priest has gone in behind the vail, nearer than Nadab and Abihu ever came. Nobody can see him. Then, verse 24, he puts on his garments “and come forth.” He is alive, and the sins are gone. Hebrews says our high priest has gone in, and will come out again:

“For Christ is not entered into the holy places made with hands, which are the figures of the true; but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us:”

Hebrews 9:24

“So Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many; and unto them that look for him shall he appear the second time without sin unto salvation.”

Hebrews 9:28

We are the people outside, waiting for him to come forth. He has done the work; he has not forgotten the ones who are watching for him.

Part four

“Once a year”

Verses 29–34

The day on the calendar, what the people did, and a statute for ever.

Afflict your souls, and rest

The people's part

afflict your souls

Humble yourselves before God, grieve over the sin. In Paul's day it was known simply as “the fast” (Acts 27:9). Do no work at all.

The priest's part

make an atonement for you

“To cleanse you, that ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD.” The whole work was his.

Notice what the people did not do. They did not work their way clean. They were sorry, they stopped, and they rested while another did it. “A sabbath of rest.” Being sorry is right. Trying to pay for it yourself is not your job; it never was.

Verse 32: the day outlived Aaron. The priest anointed “in his father's stead,” in his father's place, would do it after him. The chapter ends the way so much of Exodus ended: “he did as the LORD commanded Moses.”

Once a year, every year

“Atonement” comes fifteen times in this one chapter. And verse 34 says it had to be done “once a year,” every year, as long as the law stood. The next year, the same goats, the same blood, the same sins remembered.

“But in those sacrifices there is a remembrance again made of sins every year. For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins.”

Hebrews 10:3–4

“And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins: But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;”

Hebrews 10:11–12

“Standeth… sat down.” There was no seat for a priest in the tabernacle; his work was never done. Jesus offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, and sat down, because the work was finished.

The way in is open

For Aaron: one day a year, “that he die not.” Then Jesus died:

“And, behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom; and the earth did quake, and the rocks rent;”

Matthew 27:51

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;”

Hebrews 10:19–20

Not once a year and trembling: “boldness to enter,” any day, for any man who comes by the blood of Jesus, the man who fell yesterday too.

Real world: the day of atonement is done

Israel waited a year for this day. You do not have to wait for anything.

“And their sins and iniquities will I remember no more. Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin.”

Hebrews 10:17–18
  • SinName it, all of it, the way Aaron did over the goat. Iniquities, transgressions, sins. Vague confession keeps a man stuck.
  • RegretThe goat did not come back. Neither do the sins God has carried off. Stop digging them up in the wilderness.
  • GraceOne man went in alone and did the whole work. You add nothing to it.
  • HopeOur high priest is alive, and he will come forth again.
  • TodayLay your hands on it: tell God plainly what you did, then tell a brother in Christ. Then rest. Don't work at being forgiven.

Points to remember

  1. The warning“that he come not at all times into the holy place within the vail… that he die not” 16:2God is holy, and after Nadab and Abihu he warns Aaron. But he says “not at all times,” not “never,” and then shows him the way in.
  2. The linen“these are holy garments; therefore shall he wash his flesh in water, and so put them on” 16:4Washed, then plain white linen, no gold. Nobody goes in to God in his own glory.
  3. For himself“make an atonement for himself, and for his house” 16:6Aaron needed atonement first. Jesus needed none for himself, so all of it is for us.
  4. The blood“sprinkle it with his finger upon the mercy seat eastward; and before the mercy seat… seven times” 16:14Blood on the mercy seat, over the broken law. Christ is our propitiation, our mercy seat.
  5. The two goats“the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited” 16:22One goat died, and its blood went in. One carried the sins away and did not come back.
  6. Once a year“that ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD” 16:30The people humbled themselves and rested, while the priest made them clean. Jesus did it once for all.

Question 1 of 6

The warning

Why could Aaron not come within the vail at all times?

What verses tell us about this?

“But into the second went the high priest alone once every year, not without blood, which he offered for himself, and for the errors of the people:”

Hebrews 9:7

Answer

God is holy, and he appeared there in the cloud upon the mercy seat; Nadab and Abihu had just died. But he did not shut the door. He showed Aaron how to come: once a year, not without blood.

Question 2 of 6

The linen

What did Aaron wear to go in, and what did he do before he put it on?

What verses tell us about this?

“But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men:”

Philippians 2:7

Answer

Plain white linen: coat, breeches, girdle and mitre, with none of his gold. He washed first. The highest man in Israel went in dressed as a servant, a picture of Christ, who took the form of a servant.

Question 3 of 6

For himself

Whose sin was atoned for first, and why does that matter?

What verses tell us about this?

“Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people’s: for this he did once, when he offered up himself.”

Hebrews 7:27

Answer

Aaron's own, and his house's. The high priest was a sinner too. Jesus is holy and undefiled; he needed no sacrifice for himself, so he offered himself, once, for us.

Question 4 of 6

The blood

Where did the high priest put the blood, and how many times?

What verses tell us about this?

“Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God;”

Romans 3:25

Answer

On the mercy seat, and before it seven times; then on the horns of the altar, and upon it seven times (19). Christ is our propitiation, our mercy seat, through faith in his blood.

Question 5 of 6

The two goats

What happened to the goat for the LORD, and to the scapegoat?

What verses tell us about this?

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

Answer

The LORD's goat died, and its blood went within the vail. The scapegoat carried the confessed sins away into the wilderness. Jesus is both: he died for our sins, and he takes them away.

Question 6 of 6

Once a year

What were the people to do on that day, and what was done for them?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.”

Hebrews 10:14

Answer

They humbled themselves and did no work at all, a sabbath of rest. The priest made the atonement that made them clean, once a year. Jesus has done it once, for ever.
