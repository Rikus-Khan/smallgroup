Part one

Take heed how ye hear

Verses 1–21

One seed, four kinds of ground, and a family anyone can join.

Jesus walked town to town through Galilee with the twelve, and these women came too. They had been healed, and they paid for the work out of their own money. One of them came out of Herod's palace: the good news had already reached the court of the man who killed John.

Mary Magdalene had been held by seven devils, a life completely taken over. She became the first person to see Jesus risen from the dead:

“Mary Magdalene came and told the disciples that she had seen the Lord, and that he had spoken these things unto her.”

John 20:18

A sower went out

A farmer scattered seed by hand as he walked. Some landed on the footpath packed hard through the field, some on a thin skin of soil over rock, some where thorn roots were waiting, and some on good, deep soil.

Same sower. Same seed. The only difference was the ground.

Why tell it in parables?

A parable is an everyday story laid alongside a truth about God. A mystery in the Bible isn't a puzzle. It's something God kept hidden for a long time and now makes known.

Jesus is quoting Isaiah. A story opens the truth to a man who wants it, and leaves it closed to the man who has already made up his mind against God:

“And he said, Go, and tell this people, Hear ye indeed, but understand not; and see ye indeed, but perceive not.”

Isaiah 6:9

Look at what the disciples did. They didn't understand either, so they asked, and He explained it. That door is open to anyone who asks.

Good ground

15

“The seed is the word of God.” The problem is never the seed. Jesus isn't telling us which kind of man we're stuck being. He's telling us how to hear. Hard ground can be broken up, rocks dug out, and thorns pulled.

Roots, and patience

Most of us know the rock: a big, emotional start, at the altar or in treatment, then the first hard week, and it's gone. The trouble wasn't the joy. It was the missing root.

Jesus isn't saying a child of God can lose his salvation. He keeps His own (John 10:28). He's warning about a faith that stays on the surface and never goes down into the heart.

And the thorns: cares, riches, pleasures. The old life grows back if nobody pulls it. Good ground keeps the word and waits: “with patience.” Fruit takes seasons, not days.

A lamp isn't for hiding

The candle was a small clay lamp that burned olive oil. Nobody lights one and then puts a jar over it or shoves it under the bed. You set it up high where it can light the room.

What God shows you is meant to be lived and seen. Use it, and you'll be given more. Sit on it, and you'll lose even what you thought you had.

A family anyone can join

Family by birth

“stand without”

His mother and His brothers, outside, unable to get in for the crowd.

Family by the word

“hear the word of God, and do it”

Anyone, from any family, with any past.

Jesus wasn't turning His back on His mother. He cared for her even from the cross (John 19:26–27). He was opening the door wider. Hearing and doing makes you His.

Some of us have burned every bridge we had. Jesus gives a man a new family, brothers who hear the word and do it.

“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.”

James 1:22

Part two

What manner of man is this!

Verses 22–39

The wind obeys Him, and so does an army of devils.

The other side

The lake lies about 700 feet below sea level, in a bowl of hills. Cool air can pour down the valleys and stir up a violent storm in minutes.

Jesus said, “Let us go over,” and on the way the storm hit. Some of these men had fished that water all their lives, and they thought they were going to drown.

The east shore was Gentile country, the Decapolis, with steep hills dropping to the water. That's where they were headed, and where a man was waiting among the tombs.

Asleep in the storm

He was asleep: a real man, worn out after a long day. Then He stood up and rebuked the wind and the water, the way a master gives an order: a real God.

Storms die down slowly. This one stopped at once. The craving, the panic, the 2 a.m. fear: the same voice speaks to those too.

They did right to wake Him. But He asked, “Where is your faith?” He was in the boat. He'd said they were going to the other side.

Nothing could hold him

  • Chainshe broke them
  • Fettersleg irons; he broke those too
  • The tombs, the wildernessdriven out, naked and alone
  • Jesus“he fell down before him”

The town tried to control him from the outside. Chains can hold a man for a while. They can't set him free. Some of us know that: courts, jail, rules, and our own promises held us for a while, and then we broke them.

The devils knew exactly who Jesus was, “Son of God most high,” and they were afraid of Him.

“Legion”

Legion a Roman army unit about 6,000 soldiers

Jesus asked his name, and an army answered. The man had lost even his own name. That's what bondage does: after a while, the thing that owns you does the talking.

“The deep” is the abyss, the bottomless pit, where the devils feared being locked away (Revelation 20:3). Notice that they asked. An army of devils couldn't move an inch without His leave. Whatever they got into, they wrecked. One man mattered more to Jesus than a whole herd of swine.

The whole town came out and found him sitting still, dressed, and talking sense. That's what Jesus does with a man: He covers his shame, sits him down at His feet, and gives him his mind back.

Go home and tell

The town

“besought him to depart”

Afraid, and short a herd of swine. They'd rather have their pigs than their neighbor whole.

The man

“besought him that he might be with him”

He wanted to get in the boat and go wherever Jesus went.

Jesus said no, and gave him a harder job: “Return to thine own house.” Go back to the people who knew you at your worst. Your story starts there.

Jesus said, “how great things God hath done.” The man told “how great things Jesus had done.” Luke sets them side by side. What Jesus did, God did.

Part three

Fear not: believe only

Verses 40–56

A desperate father, a woman nobody would touch, and a little girl who wakes up.

Two twelves

A ruler of the synagogue ran the town's Sabbath meetings: he chose who read and who spoke, and kept things in order. Jairus was a somebody. Today he's on his face in the street, because his only girl is dying.

Jairus's daughter

Twelve years of life

Loved, at home, and now dying.

A woman in the crowd

Twelve years of suffering

Sick, broke, and shut out for as long as the girl had been alive.

Desperation doesn't care about dignity. Some of us only came to Jesus when we ran out of every other option. He didn't turn Jairus away for that, and He won't turn you away.

Twelve years down, then one touch

Luke was a doctor, and he says it straight: she spent everything on physicians and nobody could help her. Under the Law her bleeding made her unclean, and anyone she touched (Leviticus 15:25). No worship, no hugs, no hand on her shoulder for twelve years.

So she came from behind and touched the border of His garment, the tassel the Law told every Jewish man to wear on the corners of his cloak (Numbers 15:38). Twelve years, then one touch.

Thronged, and touched

Peter was right: the whole crowd was pushing against Him. But only one person reached out to Him in faith, and He felt it. Virtue here means power: healing power went out from Him.

You can be pressed up against church, Bible study, and religious people your whole life and never touch Him. And in a crowd of hundreds, He knows the one who does.

“a woman” → “Daughter” She came in as an outcast nobody would touch. She went home as family.

She meant to slip away healed and unseen. Jesus wouldn't let her. Not to shame her: she came trembling, and He answered with comfort. He called her out so the whole town would know she was clean, and so she'd hear what He thought of her.

He doesn't want you sneaking a blessing and hiding. He wants to give you a new name and send you off in peace.

When the news is the worst

From the ruler's houseThy daughter is dead; trouble not the Master.

JesusFear not: believe only, and she shall be made whole.
before Jairus could say a word

Think of Jairus while Jesus stopped for the woman. Every minute counted, and then the messenger came. It looked like Jesus had been too slow.

Jesus spoke before fear could settle in. Believe only: not believe and also fix it, or believe and also earn it. Just trust Him, even when the news says it's too late.

Not dead, but sleeping

The mourners were already wailing; families even hired them. They knew a dead body when they saw one, and they laughed at Him.

Jesus wasn't saying she hadn't died. Verse 55 says her spirit came again. He meant that for Him, death is like sleep: He wakes people up from it. That's how the New Testament speaks of believers who have died:

“For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.”

1 Thessalonians 4:14

Maid, arise.

He took a dead girl by the hand. Touching the dead made a man unclean, but once again the uncleanness didn't spread to Jesus. Life spread to her.

Then: “give her meat,” something to eat. After the biggest moment of their lives, Jesus thought about her supper.

The Lord who raises the dead also cares that you eat, sleep, and get through an ordinary day. Recovery is lived in those plain things, and He's in them too.

Question 1 of 6

Hearing

What kind of hearer bears fruit?

What verses tell us about this?

“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.”

James 1:22

Answer

The good ground: a man with an honest and good heart who hears the word, keeps it, and bears fruit with patience. He doesn't let the devil snatch it, doesn't stay shallow, and doesn't let worry, money, or pleasure choke it out.

Question 2 of 6

Family

Who did Jesus call His mother and His brethren?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever shall do the will of God, the same is my brother, and my sister, and mother.”

Mark 3:35

Answer

Those who hear the word of God and do it. Jesus loved His own family, but He opened the door to everyone. Whosoever hears and obeys His word belongs to His family, whatever family he came from.

Question 3 of 6

The storm

What did Jesus ask His disciples after He calmed the storm?

What verses tell us about this?

“He maketh the storm a calm, so that the waves thereof are still.”

Psalm 107:29

Answer

“Where is your faith?” They were right to run to Him, but they'd forgotten who was in the boat. He had said they were going to the other side, and even the wind and the water obey Him.

Question 4 of 6

Set free

What did Jesus do for the man full of devils, and where did He send him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Chains couldn't hold him, but Jesus set him free: clothed, and in his right mind, sitting at His feet. Then He sent him home to tell his own people how great things God had done for him.

Question 5 of 6

The touch

What did Jesus say to the woman who touched Him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Daughter, be of good comfort: thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace.” She came trembling, expecting trouble. He gave her comfort, told everyone she was healed, called her family, and sent her home in peace.

Question 6 of 6

Fear not

What did Jesus tell Jairus when the news was the worst it could be?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Fear not: believe only, and she shall be made whole.” He spoke before fear could take hold. Then He took the girl by the hand and raised her up. Too late for us is never too late for Him.
