Exodus 31

Filled with the Spirit to work

God calls two craftsmen by name and fills them with His Spirit for the work of their hands. Then He gives the sabbath as a sign between Himself and His people, and ends forty days on the mount by giving Moses two tables of stone, written with the finger of God.

Things to watch for

Part one

“I have called by name Bezaleel”

Verses 1–11

The first man God fills with His Spirit in this book is a craftsman.

Two families, one work

  • JudahLeah’s son
    • Hur
      • Uri
        • Bezaleel
  • DanBilhah’s son
    • Ahisamach
      • Aholiab

Bezaleel is of Judah, the tribe of kings, and his grandfather Hur is very likely the Hur who held up Moses' arm at Rephidim (chapter 17) and was left with Aaron in charge (chapter 24), though Exodus does not say so. Aholiab is of Dan, a son of Jacob by Bilhah, his wife Rachel's servant. The LORD names them, so He knows them by name.

Filled for the work of his hands

God gave him

wisdom, understanding, knowledge

Wisdom to know what to do, understanding to see how it fits, knowledge to know the trade.

He worked in

gold, silver, brass, stone, timber

Metalwork, setting stones, carving wood: “all manner of workmanship.” A trade, in other words.

  • deviseplan out, design
  • cunning worksskilled designs (here “cunning” is praise, not trickery)
  • workmanshipcraftwork, work done by hand
  • wise heartedskilled, with a gift for it

Moses was given the pattern on the mount. Bezaleel was given the hands to build it. Both came from the same God.

The Spirit and a working man

“I have filled him with the spirit of God.” Not a priest, not a prophet, but a man whose work was in metal, stone and wood. God did not stop being God when the work was done with hands.

“Now there are diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit. And there are differences of administrations, but the same Lord. And there are diversities of operations, but it is the same God which worketh all in all. But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal.”

1 Corinthians 12:4–7

“For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.”

Ephesians 2:10

Paul says it of every believer: the Spirit gives, “to every man to profit withal.” And Ephesians says we are God's workmanship, His handiwork, made for good works He prepared for us. The man who frames houses, lays pipe, drives a truck or washes dishes is not on the outside of this. Your work is not second-class to God.

All the list, one by one

  • the tent and the ark with the mercy seat
  • the table and the pure candlestick
  • the two altars and the basin
  • the cloths of service and the holy garments
  • the anointing oil and the sweet incense

Every item from chapters 25 to 30 is named again: nothing is left out, and “according to all that I have commanded thee shall they do.” Cloths of service are the woven coverings used for the tent's service.

Real world: your hands

Some of you have been told you are only good for one thing, and a few of you have been told you are good for nothing. Look at who God picks to build His house.

  • Called by nameGod knows yours, and He knew it before the worst year.
  • Filled, not fixed upHe gives the skill and the Spirit, and He does not wait until you are good first.
  • Wise hearted, every oneBezaleel was not alone: all the wise hearted had wisdom put in them. Find your place in the work beside the others.

One thing today: do your job as work that matters to God. Show up on time, do it well, and treat the man beside you as a fellow worker. The Spirit of God lives in you, and He is not shut out of the shop, the site or the cab.

Part two

“A sign between me and you”

Verses 12–18

The sabbath, and the two tables written by God.

A sign, and who does the sanctifying

  • a signa mark that says something: the rainbow after the flood (Genesis 9), circumcision for Abraham's house (Genesis 17), and here, the sabbath for Israel
  • sanctifyset apart as holy: “that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you”

Read the end of verse 13 again. The sabbath is a sign that He sanctifies them. Israel does not make itself holy by resting. God has already set them apart, and the day is a weekly reminder of it.

“Moreover also I gave them my sabbaths, to be a sign between me and them, that they might know that I am the LORD that sanctify them.”

Ezekiel 20:12

A hard verse, plainly

“Every one that defileth it shall surely be put to death.” That is hard to read. Here is what to keep in mind.

  • Civil law for IsraelThese laws governed Israel as a nation under God, and were carried out in its courts. They are not given as law to believers today.
  • The sabbath was the covenant signTo treat it as nothing was to throw off the covenant itself, and the penalty is the one for rebellion, “cut off from among his people.”
  • Six days of workLook at verse 15: “Six days may work be done.” Work is good, and God gives it.

None of us could have kept it. That is the point of the law: to show a man what he cannot do, and where to go. Jesus carried the penalty we earned and gave us the rest.

Six days, then rest

  1. day 1work
  2. day 2work
  3. day 3work
  4. day 4work
  5. day 5work
  6. day 6work
  7. day 7restthe sabbath

The pattern is God's own: “in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed.” Refreshed is a plain, human word: He drew breath. God did not need the rest. He gave us the pattern, and it is for our good.

How the New Testament speaks of it

“And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath: Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.”

Mark 2:27–28

“Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days: Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.”

Colossians 2:16–17

“There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his.”

Hebrews 4:9–10

Jesus: the sabbath was made for man, a gift, and He is Lord of it. Paul: it was “a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.” Hebrews: there is a rest for God's people, and the man who has entered it has stopped working to earn his place. The day pointed to the rest the Son gives.

Real world: rest is a gift

The old way

Earn it, and then rest

Work, prove, push, never stop, and crash.

His way

Rest, because it's finished

He has already done the work that makes you His.

Many of you know what tired does. Hungry, angry, lonely and tired are the doors a relapse walks through. God made rest for you before you ever needed it. Sleep, eat, sit down with the brothers, and take real rest.

And before the first day of work, and after the last, rest in this: the work that settles your place with God is done, and it is Christ's.

Written with the finger of God

  • communingtalking together
  • tables of testimonythe tablets that bear witness: the ten commandments
  • the finger of GodGod's own hand wrote them: not Moses, and not a scribe

“Forasmuch as ye are manifestly declared to be the epistle of Christ ministered by us, written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart.”

2 Corinthians 3:3

The first tables were written on stone, outside a man. Paul says God now writes on the heart, with the Spirit. The same finger. And notice the place in the story: Moses holds tables written by God's own finger, and down below, the people are about to turn away from it (chapter 32). It is the last thing in the forty days, and what comes next is a fall, and then mercy.

Points to remember

  1. Called by name“See, I have called by name Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah” 31:2God knows you by name, and He calls working men for His work.
  2. The Spirit“I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge” 31:3The Spirit fills a man for skilled work with his hands.
  3. Not alone“I have given with him Aholiab… and in the hearts of all that are wise hearted I have put wisdom” 31:6He gave a partner, and wisdom to all the wise hearted. Nobody builds alone.
  4. The sabbath“it is a sign between me and you… that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you” 31:13The sign says it is God who sets us apart, not our effort.
  5. The reason“in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed” 31:17Rest is God's pattern and His gift, and in Christ we have the real rest.
  6. The tables“two tables of testimony, tables of stone, written with the finger of God” 31:18God wrote His own law. Now His Spirit writes it on the heart.

Question 1 of 6

Called by name

Who did the LORD call by name to build the tabernacle, and from which tribe?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Moses called Bezaleel and Aholiab, and every wise hearted man, in whose heart the LORD had put wisdom, even every one whose heart stirred him up to come unto the work to do it:”

Exodus 36:2

Answer

Bezaleel, the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah. God knows men by name, and He picks a working man for the work.

Question 2 of 6

The Spirit

What did God fill Bezaleel with, and for what kind of work?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now there are diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit. And there are differences of administrations, but the same Lord. And there are diversities of operations, but it is the same God which worketh all in all. But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal.”

1 Corinthians 12:4–7

Answer

The Spirit of God, with wisdom, understanding and knowledge, for all manner of workmanship: gold, silver, brass, stone and timber. God fills ordinary working men, and your work matters to Him.

Question 3 of 6

Not alone

Who was given to work with him, and who else received wisdom?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then wrought Bezaleel and Aholiab, and every wise hearted man, in whom the LORD put wisdom and understanding to know how to work all manner of work for the service of the sanctuary, according to all that the LORD had commanded.”

Exodus 36:1

Answer

Aholiab, the son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan, and all that are wise hearted. Nobody builds alone: find the brothers you work beside.

Question 4 of 6

The sabbath

What did the LORD call the sabbath, and what did He say it would help Israel know?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath: Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.”

Mark 2:27–28

Answer

A sign between Him and Israel, so that they would know He is the LORD that sanctifies them. It was God setting them apart, not their effort. Jesus said the sabbath was made for man, and that He is Lord of it.

Question 5 of 6

The reason

Why did the LORD say the seventh day is a day of rest?

What verses tell us about this?

“There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his.”

Hebrews 4:9–10

Answer

Because in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh He rested. Rest is His pattern and His gift to us, and in Christ there remains a rest for the people of God.

Question 6 of 6

The tables

What did God give Moses when He had made an end of speaking with him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Forasmuch as ye are manifestly declared to be the epistle of Christ ministered by us, written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart.”

2 Corinthians 3:3

Answer

Two tables of testimony, tables of stone, written with the finger of God. God wrote His own law, and now His Spirit writes it on the hearts of those who trust Him.
