Leviticus 4

The offering for the sin nobody meant

Whether a priest, a whole people, a ruler or one of the common people sins through ignorance, the LORD has a way back: a life given, blood on the horns of the altar, and at the end of each case, “it shall be forgiven him.”

Part one

“If the priest that is anointed do sin”

Verses 1–12

The man who stands nearest to God falls, and what it costs to put it right.

A new kind of offering

Chapters 1 to 3

Gifts

The burnt offering, the meat offering and the peace offering: a man brings what he wants to give. Not one of them begins with a sin.

Chapter 4

The sin offering

The first offering that begins with a wrong done: “If a soul shall sin.” This one is not a gift from a happy man. It is what a guilty man needs.

The tabernacle was raised, and Moses is hearing the LORD “out of the tabernacle” (1:1) at the foot of Mount Sinai, in the second year after Egypt and about a month before the numbering of the people (Exodus 40:17; Numbers 1:1). The first thing he gives is a way for sinners to come near.

Ought not means should not. Through ignorance means without knowing, or without meaning to.

“Through ignorance”

The LORD uses this phrase four times in this chapter: verses 2, 13, 22 and 27. Four different people, and every one of them sinned without knowing it.

Ignorance did not make the thing nothing. It was still a sin, and it still needed blood. But notice the mercy: the LORD made an offering for the very sins a man did not know he had done.

“Who can understand his errors? cleanse thou me from secret faults.”

Psalm 19:12

“Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do.”

Luke 23:34

For a man in recovery this lands hard. Some of what you did, you did before you knew better, or before you knew it was wrong. It still left damage. But God made a way for exactly that.

When the priest falls

“According to the sin of the people.” When the anointed priest sinned, he brought the same offering as the whole congregation would (verse 14): a young bullock, the biggest animal in the chapter. His sin was never a private matter. The people stood where he stood, so his fall touched them all.

“For every high priest taken from among men is ordained for men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins: Who can have compassion on the ignorant, and on them that are out of the way; for that he himself also is compassed with infirmity. And by reason hereof he ought, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins.”

Hebrews 5:1–3

Even the priest had to bring a bullock for himself. The Bible does not pretend its leaders are above sin. Pray for your pastor and your brothers; they carry weight, and they fall as men do.

And the other way: if you ever have to stand in front of others, or are the one the younger men watch, your fall costs more than yours. It does not shut you out. It calls for the biggest offering, and the LORD has one.

The hand on the head

The sinner lays his hand on the animal's head, and then it is killed “before the LORD.” The chapter has this action five times: verses 4, 15, 24, 29 and 33.

The man

Guilty

His sin, and his hand leaning on the animal as hard as he can.

The animal

“Without blemish”

It stands in his place. The word comes four times: verses 3, 23, 28 and 32.

He did it himself, with his own hand, and watched. Nobody dealt with his sin for him while he looked the other way. He saw what sin costs.

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Where the blood went

For the anointed priest, the blood goes inside the tent: sprinkled “seven times before the LORD, before the vail of the sanctuary,” put on the horns of the altar of sweet incense, and the rest poured at the foot of the altar of the burnt offering at the door (verses 6–7).

The vail hangs between the holy place and the most holy place, where the ark is. The priest's sin reached as far as the LORD's own door, and so the blood must go that far.

The chapter says “seven times” twice: here in verse 6 and again in verse 17 for the whole congregation.

The fat, and the old words

  • inwardsthe inner parts, what is inside the animal
  • flanksthe loins, the sides of the body below the ribs
  • caulthe fatty covering over the liver
  • kidneysthe two kidneys and the fat on them
  • hornsthe four corner-pieces of the altar, standing up like horns
  • dungthe waste matter of the animal (verse 11)

The fat, the best part, was taken off and burned on the altar “as it was taken off from the bullock of the sacrifice of peace offerings” (verse 10), the same way as in chapter 3. The best of it went up to the LORD.

Carried outside

The skin, the flesh, the head, the legs, all that was left of the bullock was carried “forth without the camp unto a clean place, where the ashes are poured out,” and burned on the wood. The sin offering for the priest and for the whole people ended outside the camp (verses 12 and 21). Look at the place: a clean place. The sin was carried out, and the place it was burned was kept clean.

“For the bodies of those beasts, whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin, are burned without the camp. Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate. Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach.”

Hebrews 13:11–13

Paul says it plainly in Hebrews: the bodies of the sin offerings were burned without the camp, and Jesus “suffered without the gate.” Outside the city, outside where the respectable people were.

If you have ever felt like a man outside the camp, put out of the family, the job, the church, the room, then hear Hebrews 13:13. Jesus is out there. “Let us go forth therefore unto him.” You are not shut out; you have been called to where he is.

Part two

“If the whole congregation of Israel sin”

Verses 13–21

A whole people can be wrong and not know it, until it is made known.

When the sin is known

  1. hidfrom the eyes of the assemblyverse 13
  2. known“the sin… is known”verse 14
  3. the elders’ handson the bullock’s headverse 15
  4. forgiven“it shall be forgiven them”verse 20

Notice the order. The offering did not start until the sin was known. Something had to open their eyes. The LORD does that through his word, through a brother who tells the truth, through a conscience that will not leave a man alone.

The elders lay their hands for the whole people. The men who lead lean their weight on the animal for everyone. The group answers for its own, and no man is left to carry it alone.

Assembly and congregation mean the same thing here: all the people together.

The higher a man stood, the more it cost, and the closer the blood had to go. The common man's offering was smaller. But there was a place for the common man too: “any one of the common people.” Nobody was too small or too low to be forgiven.

Brasen means made of brass: the altar of the burnt offering, at the door of the tabernacle (verses 7, 18).

“And it shall be forgiven him”

verse 20“it shall be forgiven them”
verse 26“it shall be forgiven him”
verse 31“it shall be forgiven him”
verse 35“it shall be forgiven him”

Four times, at the end of each case, the LORD says the same thing: “and the priest shall make an atonement… and it shall be forgiven.” Not “it may be.” Not “if he behaves afterward.” The chapter ends on the word forgiven.

“For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins.”

Hebrews 10:4

The Bible is honest about it: the blood of bulls and goats could never itself take sin away. The offerings pointed to the one who would. Paul says God set forth Christ “to be a propitiation through faith in his blood… for the remission of sins that are past, through the forbearance of God” (Romans 3:25).

“How much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?”

Hebrews 9:14

Real world: when it comes to your knowledge

Verses 23 and 28 say it: “if his sin… come to his knowledge.” Some day you will see something you did, or something you did not see before: a wrong done to your wife, your kids, a boss, a friend, years ago, in a haze. This chapter tells you what to do with it.

  • Don't bury itThe offering started when the sin was known. Nothing started until he faced it.
  • Bring itNot to a bullock now. To the LORD, with your own mouth: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” (1 John 1:9).
  • Say it to a brotherCall or text one of the men, and your pastor. You were not meant to carry it by yourself.

The sin you did not know about, and the sin you carry with regret: both have a name in this chapter, and both end with the same word. Forgiven.

Points to remember

  1. Ignorance“If a soul shall sin through ignorance against any of the commandments of the LORD” 4:2The LORD made an offering even for the sins a man did not know he had done.
  2. The priest“If the priest that is anointed do sin according to the sin of the people” 4:3A leader's fall costs the most, and there is still a bullock for him.
  3. The hand“shall lay his hand upon the bullock’s head, and kill the bullock before the LORD” 4:4The animal stood in his place, and he saw what sin costs.
  4. The blood“sprinkle of the blood seven times before the LORD, before the vail of the sanctuary” 4:6The blood went as near to God as the sin had reached.
  5. Outside the camp“Even the whole bullock shall he carry forth without the camp unto a clean place” 4:12Jesus suffered without the gate, so a man outside can be brought in.
  6. Forgiven“the priest shall make an atonement for him… and it shall be forgiven him” 4:26Four cases, four times: forgiven. The chapter ends on that word.

Question 1 of 6

Ignorance

Does the LORD count a sin as nothing because the man did not know he was doing it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Who can understand his errors? cleanse thou me from secret faults.”

Psalm 19:12

Answer

No. A sin through ignorance is still a sin, and it needs an offering. But the LORD made an offering for exactly those sins, four times in this chapter, so that a man who did not know can still be forgiven.

Question 2 of 6

The priest

What did the anointed priest have to bring if he sinned, and what does that tell us about his sin?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A young bullock without blemish, the same offering as for the whole congregation. His sin was “according to the sin of the people”: when the man nearest to God falls, it touches everyone. And there was still an offering for him.

Question 3 of 6

The hand

What did the man bringing the offering do to the animal's head before it was killed?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Answer

He laid his hand on its head: five times in the chapter. The animal stood in his place, and he watched what sin costs. “He hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin.”

Question 4 of 6

The blood

Where was the blood put, and what happened to the rest of it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For the priest and the whole congregation: sprinkled seven times before the vail, put on the horns of the altar of sweet incense, and the rest poured at the foot of the altar of burnt offering. For a ruler or one of the common people (verses 25, 30): on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and the rest poured at its foot.

Question 5 of 6

Outside the camp

Where was the whole bullock burned, and who “suffered without the gate”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate.”

Hebrews 13:12

Answer

Outside the camp, “unto a clean place, where the ashes are poured out.” Jesus “suffered without the gate.” He went outside so that we could be brought in.

Question 6 of 6

Forgiven

What does the LORD say at the end of each case, and how many times does he say it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“And it shall be forgiven him”: four times, in verses 20, 26, 31 and 35. “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” (1 John 1:9).
