Deuteronomy 17

No blemish, two or three witnesses, and a king who must read the law

Only the best for the LORD. No man put to death on one witness. The hard cases go up to the priests and the judge. And when Israel asks for a king, he is not to multiply horses, wives or silver, and he is to write himself a copy of the law and read it all the days of his life.

Part one

“Wherein is blemish”

Verses 1–7

The best for the LORD, and a court that must be certain.

The best, not the leftovers

Evilfavouredness is an old word for anything ugly or faulty about an animal. We met the same law at the end of chapter 15 (15:21): nothing lame, or blind, or with “any ill blemish” on the LORD's altar. Here Moses says what God thinks of the other way: “an abomination”, something he hates.

“But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:19

The animal without blemish pointed to the Lamb who was without blemish: Christ, whose blood bought us. We cannot give God a perfect life. But God does not ask for it: he has given it for us. What we do bring, our time and our hearts, is to be our best and not what is left over.

Before anyone is punished

The sin was serving other gods, even “the host of heaven”, the stars: treason against the LORD whose covenant Israel had entered into. This was the law of Israel's courts under God, and five things had to be sure first. It shows how seriously God takes worship, and his care that no one is wrongly condemned. We saw the same care in 13:14, before a city was judged: not on a rumour.

Two or three witnesses

  • One is not enough“At the mouth of one witness he shall not be put to death.” One accuser can lie, or be mistaken, or hate the man.
  • Two or three are enoughWhen two or three say the same thing, the matter is settled, and it is more than one man's word.
  • The witnesses go first“The hands of the witnesses shall be first upon him.” The man who accuses must be ready to stand behind it, to the end.

“But if he will not hear thee, then take with thee one or two more, that in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may be established.”

Matthew 18:16

“This is the third time I am coming to you. In the mouth of two or three witnesses shall every word be established.”

2 Corinthians 13:1

Jesus and Paul both took this rule into the church: no charge on one man's say-so, when a brother has sinned. Gossip is one witness with a big mouth. We will meet the rule again in chapter 19.

Part two

“A matter too hard for thee”

Verses 8–13

Go up to the place, ask the priests and the judge, and do as they say.

When the local judge cannot decide

  1. 1Too hardfor the gate
  2. 2Go upto the place
  3. 3Askthe priests and the judge
  4. 4Doas they say

Three kinds of hard case: “between blood and blood” is life and death, like the killer who meant it and the one who did not (we saw this in Numbers 35); “plea and plea” is a dispute between two men who each claim to be right; and “stroke and stroke” is a fight and its injury.

The answer was not to shrug, or fix it his own way: a man was to go to those who knew the law. If you are stuck on something too hard for you, it is wisdom to go to your pastor and to brothers who know the Book, and not to carry it alone.

Presumptuously

Presumptuously means to act with a proud boldness, knowing the ruling and doing as you please. Decline means to turn aside, “to the right hand, nor to the left”. The ruling was from the law and from the priest who stood “to minister there before the LORD”, so to refuse it was to refuse the LORD himself. We read of such a sin in Numbers 15:30.

It is the sin of the man who hears God's answer and does as he pleases. The verse says its purpose: “all the people shall hear, and fear, and do no more presumptuously.”

There is grace for the man who has gone his own way. God does not wait for him to be good first; he forgives the one who comes back and owns it: “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” (1 John 1:9).

Part three

“I will set a king over me”

Verses 14–20

The law of the king, and how it went.

“Like all the nations”

Moses says Israel will one day say it: “like as all the nations that are about me”. He does not forbid it. He says the king must be the one “whom the LORD thy God shall choose”, “one from among thy brethren”, not a stranger. In any wise is old English for “by all means”.

“And said unto him, Behold, thou art old, and thy sons walk not in thy ways: now make us a king to judge us like all the nations.”

1 Samuel 8:5

“And the LORD said unto Samuel, Hearken unto the voice of the people in all that they say unto thee: for they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them.”

1 Samuel 8:7

When the day came, it was just as Moses said, and in the same words: “like all the nations”. God allowed it, but he told Samuel what it really was. Samuel's sons were a real trouble, but that was not the heart of it. They wanted to be like everyone else. A man can ask for what everyone has, and lose what God had for him.

What Samuel said it would cost

“He will take your sons, and appoint them for himself, for his chariots, and to be his horsemen; and some shall run before his chariots.”

1 Samuel 8:11

“He will take the tenth of your sheep: and ye shall be his servants. And ye shall cry out in that day because of your king which ye shall have chosen you; and the LORD will not hear you in that day.”

1 Samuel 8:17–18

“Nevertheless the people refused to obey the voice of Samuel; and they said, Nay; but we will have a king over us;”

1 Samuel 8:19

Notice the first thing the king would take: sons for “his chariots” and “his horsemen”. Moses had said not to multiply horses. The people heard the warning, and said, Nay. That is how it goes with us too: we hear it plainly, and want it anyway.

Three things not to multiply

  • HorsesStrength you can count, bought from Egypt: “nor cause the people to return to Egypt… Ye shall henceforth return no more that way.”
  • Wives“that his heart turn not away.” The reason is given: it is the heart.
  • Silver and goldWealth piled up, “greatly multiply to himself”.

The word multiply comes four times in these two verses. It is not the having of a horse, a wife or a purse that is forbidden, but the piling up. Each one is a thing a man can trust in instead of the LORD. Horses are strength, a wife is comfort, and silver is security.

“Woe to them that go down to Egypt for help; and stay on horses, and trust in chariots, because they are many; and in horsemen, because they are very strong; but they look not unto the Holy One of Israel, neither seek the LORD!”

Isaiah 31:1

Look at the last row: the law said “that his heart turn not away”, and the book says “his wives turned away his heart”. Almost the same words. And the horses came out of Egypt, the very place the law named.

The heart goes first

“For it came to pass, when Solomon was old, that his wives turned away his heart after other gods: and his heart was not perfect with the LORD his God, as was the heart of David his father.”

1 Kings 11:4

“Did not Solomon king of Israel sin by these things? yet among many nations was there no king like him, who was beloved of his God, and God made him king over all Israel: nevertheless even him did outlandish women cause to sin.”

Nehemiah 13:26

No king was greater, “beloved of his God”, and he fell the way the law said he would. It did not happen in a day. It was “when Solomon was old”, after years of one more horse and one more wife. A man does not go back to the old life in a leap. He goes a little at a time, and the heart turns first.

If this is you, the way back is the same way as the heart went: a little at a time, by the Word, with the brothers, and by confessing it today. The LORD is “faithful and just to forgive”.

A king with his own copy

The king was to write the copy himself, by hand, from the book “before the priests the Levites”, and read it “all the days of his life”. A king who has copied the law out knows it. It was Moses' own book: “Moses wrote this law, and delivered it unto the priests the sons of Levi” (31:9).

  • Write it“a copy of this law in a book”
  • Read it“all the days of his life”
  • Learn from it“to fear the LORD his God, to keep all the words of this law… to do them”

The point is stated in verse 20: “That his heart be not lifted up above his brethren”, and not turn aside “to the right hand, or to the left”. The most powerful man in the land was to sit under the Word like everyone else. The Bible does not say whether Solomon kept this, and we should not guess. It does tell us how his heart ended.

The Word in the heart

“Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee.”

Psalm 119:11

“Wherefore in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren, that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God, to make reconciliation for the sins of the people.”

Hebrews 2:17

The king was to be “from among thy brethren”, and not lifted above them (verse 20). There is a King who came “meek, and sitting upon an ass” (Matthew 21:5), made “like unto his brethren” (Hebrews 2:17), who did not multiply horses or wives or silver, and whose heart never turned. He humbled himself and was obedient (Philippians 2:8). He is the King we should follow, and the one who reconciles us.

And for the day-to-day: the king read his copy every day of his life. That is the daily reading we do here. A little of the Word each day does what a horse, a wife or a purse never could: it keeps the heart.

Real world: keep the heart

  • SinWanting to be “like all the nations”, piling up the horse, the woman and the money to trust in, and the slow turn of the heart.
  • RegretMost of us can say exactly when the heart began to turn, and what it cost. Solomon's end is a warning, and not the last word on a man.
  • GraceThe best was given for us: the Lamb without blemish (1 Peter 1:19), the King who is like his brethren (Hebrews 2:17).
  • HopeThe way back is the same as the way away: a little at a time, with the Word in the heart (Psalm 119:11).
  • TodayRead something of the Word today, as the king did. Don't pass on a rumour on one man's word. And if something is too hard for you, take it to your pastor and your brothers in Christ: call or text them today.

Points to remember

  1. The blemish“Thou shalt not sacrifice unto the LORD thy God any bullock, or sheep, wherein is blemish” 17:1The best belongs to the LORD, and he has given his own best, the Lamb without blemish.
  2. The witnesses“At the mouth of two witnesses, or three witnesses, shall he that is worthy of death be put to death” 17:6Not on one man's word, and the witnesses go first.
  3. The hard case“thou shalt come unto the priests the Levites, and unto the judge that shall be in those days, and enquire” 17:9What is too hard for you, take to those who know the law.
  4. The king's limits“he shall not multiply horses to himself… Neither shall he multiply wives to himself… neither shall he greatly multiply to himself silver and gold” 17:16–17Horses, wives, silver: Solomon did all three, and his wives turned away his heart.
  5. The copy“he shall write him a copy of this law in a book” 17:18He wrote it with his own hand, and read it all the days of his life.
  6. The heart“That his heart be not lifted up above his brethren” 17:20The Word keeps the heart low and turned toward the LORD.

Question 1 of 6

The blemish

What was not to be sacrificed to the LORD, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Any bullock or sheep with a blemish, or any evilfavouredness, “for that is an abomination unto the LORD thy God”.

Question 2 of 6

The witnesses

How many witnesses were needed to put a man to death?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Two or three. At the mouth of one witness he was not to be put to death, and the hands of the witnesses were to be first upon him.

Question 3 of 6

The hard case

Where was a matter too hard for the local judges taken?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Up to the place the LORD should choose, to the priests the Levites and the judge that should be in those days; and what they decided was to be done.

Question 4 of 6

The king's limits

What three things was the king not to multiply?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Horses, wives, and silver and gold.

Question 5 of 6

The copy

What was the king to write, and what was he to do with it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A copy of this law in a book, out of that which was before the priests the Levites. It was to be with him, and he was to read in it all the days of his life.

Question 6 of 6

The heart

Why was the king to read the law all his days?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That he might learn to fear the LORD and keep his words, and that his heart not be lifted up above his brethren, nor turn aside from the commandment.
