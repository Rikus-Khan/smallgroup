Part one

Two brothers, two offerings

Verses 1–5

The first family outside Eden, and the first time anybody comes to worship.

Two sons, two names

The firstborn · a tiller of the ground

Cain: Qayin

Sounds like the Hebrew for “gotten.” Eve said, I have gotten a man. Some think she hoped he was the promised seed of 3:15.

The second son · a keeper of sheep

Abel: Hevel

Means breath, a vapor: the same word Ecclesiastes uses for vanity. His life was short as a breath.

“Adam knew Eve” is the Bible's modest way of saying a husband and wife lay together. A tiller of the ground is a farmer. Both jobs were honest work. Farming wasn't the problem in this story, and herding wasn't the answer.

Why Abel's and not Cain's?

  • because sheep beat vegetableslater, God's law asked for grain offerings too
  • because God has favoritesHe talks to Cain next, and pleads with him
  • Abel came by faith, and gave his best“the firstlings… and the fat thereof”

“By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain, by which he obtained witness that he was righteous, God testifying of his gifts: and by it he being dead yet speaketh.”

Hebrews 11:4

“In process of time” means after a while. Firstlings are the firstborn lambs; the fat was counted the best part. Had respect unto means looked on with favour. Notice the order: God looked at Abel first, then at his offering. God weighs the man's heart before the gift. Many teachers also see the blood: Abel's offering cost a life, like the coats of skins in 3:21.

Part two

Sin lieth at the door

Verses 6–8

God talks to an angry man before he does something he can't take back.

Crouching at the door

God pictures sin as an animal crouched by the door, waiting for it to open. Lieth means lies, crouches.

“Unto thee shall be his desire”: it wants you. Every man who has fought an addiction knows that hunger is real, and it's patient.

But God doesn't say, you're doomed. He says, “thou shalt rule over him.” Don't open the door. Anger is the warning light, not the verdict.

God talks to angry men

Cain sulked: “his countenance fell” means his face dropped. And God came and talked to him, gently, with questions. Why are you angry? Why the long face? You can still do well, and be accepted. The door wasn't shut on Cain. It was open the other way.

“Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath: Neither give place to the devil.”

Ephesians 4:26–27

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

From a long face to a dead brother

  1. turned downverse 5
  2. “very wroth”burning anger
  3. “countenance fell”sulking, brooding
  4. won't listenGod's warning ignored
  5. “in the field”alone, away from help
  6. “slew him”can't be undone

Cain didn't wake up a murderer. He got there one step at a time, and every step had an exit. Most of us know this staircase: a slight, a grudge, a long night of stewing, and then the thing we swore we'd never do.

“Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer: and ye know that no murderer hath eternal life abiding in him.”

1 John 3:15

Part three

Am I my brother's keeper?

Verses 9–16

God comes with a question again, and a killer gets more mercy than he asked for.

God's two questions

To Adam, Genesis 3:9

“Where art thou?”

To Cain, Genesis 4:9

“Where is Abel thy brother?”

The first question asks where a man stands with God. The second asks where he stands with his brother. God asks them both, and he still does.

Cain answered with a lie and a sneer: I don't know. Is it my job to look after him? The answer God's whole Book gives is yes. In Christ, the man next to you is your brother, and you are his keeper.

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Blood that speaks

Abel's blood · Genesis 4:10

It cries for justice

God hears the blood of the murdered. No killing is hidden from him, and no victim is forgotten.

Jesus' blood · Hebrews 12:24

It speaks better things

Abel's blood says, make him pay. Christ's blood says, paid in full. It pleads for mercy for the guilty.

“And to Jesus the mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling, that speaketh better things than that of Abel.”

Hebrews 12:24

Some men carry blood on their hands, or damage they can't undo. Saul stood by and approved when Stephen was killed (Acts 8:1), and God made him Paul. Christ's blood speaks louder than anything you've done.

Greater than I can bear

Old words: a fugitive is a runaway; a vagabond is a wanderer with no home; henceforth means from now on. The ground Cain worked would fight him, and he'd never settle.

The sorrow of the world

Sorry it hurts

Cain grieves his punishment, his losses, his fear. Not one word about Abel.

Godly sorrow

Sorry I did it

It turns a man back to God, and it leads somewhere: to forgiveness and a new start.

“For godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

2 Corinthians 7:10

A mark of mercy

Cain was afraid every man he met would kill him. God didn't take that fear lightly. He put a mark on Cain, not to shame him but to protect him. The Bible doesn't say what the mark was; it says what it was for.

Cain deserved to die. God let him live, gave him time, and guarded his life. If God was that patient with a murderer who never said sorry, he hasn't given up on you.

Maybe you know Cain's words: it's more than I can bear. Don't carry it alone, and don't wander off with it. Call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor, today. That's what brothers are for.

“Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.”

1 Peter 5:7

Further east

  1. EdenGod walks with manGenesis 2
  2. East of the gardenAdam sent out, the gate guarded3:24
  3. Nod, east of EdenCain goes out from the presence of the LORD4:16
נוֹד said Nod “wandering”

Nobody knows where Nod was. Its name tells you what it was: the land of wandering, the same root as vagabond in verse 12. A man who walks away from God doesn't find freedom. He finds Nod.

Part four

Two family lines

Verses 17–26

Cain's family builds cities, makes music and forges iron. Seth's family calls on the LORD.

Two Enochs, two Lamechs

Builded is the old form of built. Where did Cain's wife come from? Adam “begat sons and daughters” (5:4). In the first generations brothers and sisters married; God forbade it later, in the law of Moses.

Cain's line · Genesis 4

Enoch: a city

A wanderer builds walls and names them after his son. Settled down, but not with God.

Lamech: a killer's song

Brags of a killing (4:23–24).

Seth's line · Genesis 5

Enoch: walked with God

“And Enoch walked with God: and he was not; for God took him.” (5:24)

Lamech: a father's hope

Names his son Noah, “This same shall comfort us” (5:29).

Same names, opposite roads. Your family name doesn't decide where you end up. Who you walk with does.

Lamech's family

  • CainEnoch, Irad, Mehujael, Methusael
    • Lamechtwo wives
      • Adah
        • Jabaltents and cattle
        • Jubalharp and organ
      • Zillah
        • Tubal-cainbrass and iron
        • Naamahhis sister

Old words: the organ was a pipe or flute; an artificer is a craftsman; an instructer, a master who trains others; brass here is bronze. Lamech is the first man in the Bible with two wives, a break from God's pattern of one man and one wife (2:24).

Herding, music, metalwork: all good gifts, and they came from Cain's line. A man can be gifted and still far from God. Talent is not the same as walking with him.

Lamech's song

for I have slain a man to my wounding,

and a young man to my hurt.

If Cain shall be avenged sevenfold,

truly Lamech seventy and sevenfold.

A poem, and a boast. To my wounding means for wounding me: a man hurt him, so he killed him. Five generations after Cain, murder has become a song. Sin doesn't stay the same size in a family. It grows.

Seventy and seven

Lamech: “seventy and sevenfold”→revenge without limitGenesis 4:24
Jesus: “seventy times seven”→forgiveness without limitMatthew 18:22

“Then came Peter to him, and said, Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times? Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven.”

Matthew 18:21–22

Jesus takes Lamech's number and turns it inside out. Lamech kept score so he could pay back. Christ's people stop keeping score, because Christ paid what we owed. A man who has been forgiven that much can forgive the man who wronged him, one day at a time.

Another seed

שֵׁת said Sheth, our “Seth” “appointed,” “put in place”
  1. Abelkilled: the promise looks dead
  2. Caingone east, out of God's presence
  3. Seth“another seed”: God keeps his word from 3:15

Seth's son is Enos, which means man: frail, mortal man. And then: “began men to call upon the name of the LORD.” The chapter that began with a murder ends with men praying. That door is still open:

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Your brother's keeper, today

  1. Watch the doorKnow what makes you angry, and what crouches behind it. Name it before it moves.
  2. Settle it before sundownDon't take a grudge to bed. Talk to God about it, and to the man, if you can.
  3. Keep your brotherCall or text one man from this group today. Ask him how he's really doing.

Points to remember

  1. Worship“The LORD had respect unto Abel and to his offering” 4:4God looks at the man first. Abel came by faith and gave his best.
  2. Anger“Thou shalt rule over him” 4:7Anger is a warning. Sin crouches at the door, but you don't have to open it.
  3. The road down“Cain rose up against Abel his brother” 4:8A grudge left to stew became murder, one step at a time.
  4. Brothers“Where is Abel thy brother?” 4:9God asks every man about his brother. Yes, you are his keeper.
  5. Mercy“The LORD set a mark upon Cain” 4:15God warned Cain first, and protected him after. His patience is bigger than our sin.
  6. Hope“Then began men to call upon the name of the LORD” 4:26God kept his promise through Seth, and the chapter ends in prayer.

Question 1 of 6

Worship

Why did God accept Abel and his offering, but not Cain and his?

What verses tell us about this?

“By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain, by which he obtained witness that he was righteous, God testifying of his gifts: and by it he being dead yet speaketh.”

Hebrews 11:4

“Not as Cain, who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother. And wherefore slew he him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother’s righteous.”

1 John 3:12

Answer

Abel came by faith, and he gave the first and best of what he had. God looked at the man before the gift, and Cain's heart wasn't right: his works were evil. Worship starts with trusting God, not with what's in your hands.

Question 2 of 6

Anger

What did God tell Cain to do with his anger?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath: Neither give place to the devil.”

Ephesians 4:26–27

Answer

Look at it: why are you angry? Then do well, and you'll be accepted. Sin is crouching at the door and wants you, but you must rule over it. Anger isn't the sin; opening the door to it is.

Question 3 of 6

The road down

How did Cain get from a fallen face to a dead brother?

What verses tell us about this?

“Whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer: and ye know that no murderer hath eternal life abiding in him.”

1 John 3:15

Answer

One step at a time: anger, sulking, refusing God's warning, getting his brother alone, and then the blow. John says hatred is murder already started in the heart. Every step had an exit. The time to stop is at the top of the stairs, when it's still only a feeling.

Question 4 of 6

Brothers

Is a man his brother's keeper?

What verses tell us about this?

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

Answer

Yes. Cain sneered at the idea, but God asked him about his brother, and God hears a brother's blood. In Christ we carry each other's burdens. The man next to you is yours to look after.

Question 5 of 6

Mercy

How did God show mercy, even to Cain?

What verses tell us about this?

“And to Jesus the mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling, that speaketh better things than that of Abel.”

Hebrews 12:24

Answer

He warned Cain before the murder and offered him a way to be accepted. After it, he spared his life and put a mark on him so no one would kill him. And for us there is better still: Christ's blood speaks mercy where Abel's cried for justice.

Question 6 of 6

Hope

After all this, where does the chapter end?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

With a new son, Seth, “another seed” in Abel's place, so God's promise lives on. And with men calling on the name of the LORD. However dark a family's story gets, God keeps a line of hope going, and any man can call on him.
