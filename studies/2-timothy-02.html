2 Timothy 2

Pass it on

A dying apostle tells a young man how to last: hand the truth to faithful men, keep working like a soldier, an athlete and a farmer, and stay clean for the Master's use.

Where we are in 2 Timothy

Paul is in a Roman prison, about AD 66 to 67, near the end of his life. In chapter 1 he told Timothy to stop being afraid. Now he says what Timothy should do with his strength: give it away.

Everything in this chapter is a picture of lasting: a soldier, an athlete, a farmer, a workman, a clean vessel. Paul wants Timothy to still be standing, and still useful, long after Paul is gone.

Part one

Hand it on, and last

Verses 1–7

Be strong, give it away, and keep going like a man with a job to finish.

Four generations

Count the generations in one verse: Paul, Timothy, “faithful men,” and “others also.” Paul isn't asking Timothy to be a star. He's asking him to be a link. “Faithful” means reliable: not the most gifted, but the ones who show up and can be trusted with the truth.

Somebody taught you. Somebody got the gospel to the man who got it to you. And the chain doesn't stop at you. This group is a link: the man sitting next to you, a younger man, your own son. Who will you tell what you heard?

Three pictures of staying power

The soldier

VERSES 3–4

One mission. A soldier doesn't tangle himself in “the affairs of this life”: the debts, old crowds and old habits that pull a man off duty. He wants to please his commander.

The athlete

VERSE 5

Play by the rules. A runner who cuts the course loses the crown. “Strive lawfully” means no shortcuts and no cheating, including cheating yourself about how you're doing.

The farmer

VERSE 6

Work first, harvest later. The one who labours eats first from his own crop, but the crop takes months. Real change is slow, and one day at a time is how a farm works.

Three hard jobs, and each one rewards patience: the soldier is rewarded by his commander, the athlete by the judge, the farmer by the harvest. Notice Paul doesn't say “try harder.” He says “Consider what I say; and the Lord give thee understanding in all things” (v. 7). Think it over, and ask for help.

For today: what is the one thing from your old life still tangled around your ankle? A debt, a number in your phone, a place on your drive home? The soldier cuts it loose.

Part two

He abideth faithful

Verses 8–13

Paul is chained, but he is sure of two things: the word is free, and Christ doesn't let go.

Paul is bound. The word is not.

Paul is in chains. The word is not.

Paul is “bound” in a cell, treated “as an evil doer,” and still he writes to a friend across the sea. The letter you're reading is the proof: a prisoner's words are still reaching men nearly two thousand years later.

He starts the whole thing with “Remember that Jesus Christ… was raised from the dead” (v. 8). Look at the order: first the risen Christ, then the chains. Your circumstances may be bad. Your Saviour is alive, and no cell, record, or past can bind Him.

A faithful saying

Ifwe be dead with him,
→
thenwe shall also live with him.
Ifwe suffer,
→
thenwe shall also reign with him.
Ifwe deny him,
→
thenhe also will deny us.

Most of this is good news: dying with Christ means living with Him, and suffering for Him means reigning with Him. The third if is hard, and good Bible teachers explain it a little differently. Here is what's plain: it's about a man who flatly turns his back on Christ and stays that way. It's not about the man who stumbles. Peter denied Jesus three times in one night, and Jesus went and found him (John 21).

He cannot deny himself

Our faith wobbles. It runs high one morning and sinks the next. Verse 13 says that when we “believe not,” Christ “abideth faithful.” He stays the same.

Why? “He cannot deny himself.” Everyone who trusts Him belongs to Him, and He can't go against His own word or His own body. That's why Baptists say a believer is secure: your hold on Him slips, and His hold on you doesn't.

That isn't a license to drift. It's a safety net under a man who just fell. Doubting yesterday didn't change who He is. Get up and walk with Him today.

Part three

A workman not ashamed

Verses 14–19

Handle God's word with care. Some men play with it, and it ruins them.

Rightly dividing the word

Study in the KJV means be diligent, make every effort. Rightly dividing is a picture of cutting straight, the way a workman cuts a board or lays a straight road. Treat the Bible with care, in its context, so the line comes out straight.

“Approved” means tested and passed, and “a workman that needeth not to be ashamed” is the opposite of what Paul feared for Timothy in chapter 1. A man who knows his Bible has less to be ashamed of, and more to say to a brother who's struggling. A little time in this book each day is how it's done, and you're doing it right now.

A word that spreads

“just a clever idea” → canker Canker is the Greek word for gangrene, a rot that creeps from one place into the whole body.

Hymenaeus and Philetus said “the resurrection is past already.” They treated Jesus' rising as a symbol only, and it “overthrew the faith of some.” Paul calls it a gangrene because a small lie in the truth keeps spreading.

Notice verse 14 and verse 16: don't “strive about words,” and “shun profane and vain babblings.” Not every argument deserves your day. A man new in the faith can waste a month arguing online. Stay with the plain gospel.

A foundation stone with a seal

One side of the seal

God's side

“The Lord knoweth them that are his.” Even when false teaching shakes some men, God isn't confused about who is His. The foundation “standeth sure.”

The other side

Our side

“Let every one that nameth the name of Christ depart from iniquity.” Whoever calls himself Christ's turns away from wrong. A seal marks ownership, and the owner expects his name to be on a clean life.

Both sides are on the same stone. God holds you, and you turn from sin because you are held, not so that you will be held. A man who's afraid he's lost it all can lean on the first side and live by the second.

Part four

A vessel for the Master's use

Verses 20–26

Any man can be cleaned up and put to use, if he will clear the old things out and run toward the right ones.

A vessel, cleaned

A great house holds gold and silver for the table, and wood and earth for daily work. What matters isn't the material. It's whether the vessel is clean. “Purge himself from these” means the dishonourable things of verses 16 to 18.

This is about usefulness, not about getting saved: the vessels are already in the house. God sets a man apart (“sanctified”), and the man clears out the dirt. A clean wooden bucket is used every day. No man here is too far gone to be put to work.

Flee. Follow. With them.

Paul says flee, not fight. Joseph, with a woman grabbing his coat, didn't stand and argue:

“And she caught him by his garment, saying, Lie with me: and he left his garment in her hand, and fled, and got him out.”

Genesis 39:12

“Youthful lusts” are the hot, fast wants of any man: sex, anger, drink, pride, the thrill. Don't test your strength. Run, and run toward something: righteousness, faith, charity (love), peace, “with them that call on the Lord.” That's what this group is for. Call one of these men the next time you feel the pull.

How to help a man who's stuck

  • win the argument“foolish and unlearned questions avoid”
  • shout, shame, or lecture him“the servant of the Lord must not strive”
  • write him off“if God peradventure will give them repentance”
  • gentle, patient, teaching in meekness“gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient”

Peradventure means perhaps. Only God can give a man repentance, so a helper's job is to be kind and be there. The goal is that they “recover themselves out of the snare of the devil, who are taken captive by him at his will.” The word recover carries the idea of coming back to your senses, like a man sobering up.

You know that snare from the inside, and you know who was gentle with you. Be that man for the next one. Say less than you want to, listen more, and keep showing up.

Points to remember

  1. Passed on“The same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also” 2:2You are a link in a chain. Hand on what you were given.
  2. Staying power“Endure hardness, as a good soldier of Jesus Christ” 2:3Soldier, athlete, farmer: one mission, honest rules, and patience for the harvest.
  3. He stays faithful“If we believe not, yet he abideth faithful: he cannot deny himself” 2:13Your faith wobbles. His doesn't, and He won't let go of His own.
  4. Approved“Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed” 2:15Handle the Bible with care and cut it straight, a little every day.
  5. A vessel for use“He shall be a vessel unto honour, sanctified, and meet for the master’s use” 2:21Clear out the dirt and God will use you. No man is too far gone.
  6. Flee and follow“Flee also youthful lusts: but follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace, with them that call on the Lord” 2:22Run from the old pull, toward the right things, and with other men.

Question 1 of 6

Passed on

Who is Timothy to teach, and who are they to teach after that?

What verses tell us about this?

“Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost: Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.”

Matthew 28:19–20

Answer

Faithful, reliable men, who will teach others also. Four generations in one verse: Paul, Timothy, faithful men, others. The gospel is meant to be handed on, and you are a link.

Question 2 of 6

Staying power

What three workers does Paul say a believer should be like?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A soldier (one mission, no entanglements), an athlete (play by the rules), and a farmer (work first, harvest later). Each one asks for patience.

Question 3 of 6

He stays faithful

When we believe not, what does the Lord do, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

Romans 8:38–39

Answer

He abides faithful. He can't deny Himself, His own word, or the people who belong to Him. A believer's hold on Christ slips, but Christ's hold doesn't. So get up and keep walking.

Question 4 of 6

Approved

How does a workman handle God's word so he need not be ashamed?

What verses tell us about this?

“All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness:”

2 Timothy 3:16

Answer

By being diligent and by rightly dividing it: cutting it straight, in its context. Make every effort, a little every day, and you won't be ashamed.

Question 5 of 6

A vessel for use

What makes a man a vessel unto honour, fit for the Master's use?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

Being clean: purging the dishonourable things and letting God set him apart. It isn't about gold or wood. A clean vessel of any kind is used. No man here is too far gone to be put to work.

Question 6 of 6

Flee and follow

What should a man flee, what should he follow, and who should he do it with?

What verses tell us about this?

“And she caught him by his garment, saying, Lie with me: and he left his garment in her hand, and fled, and got him out.”

Genesis 39:12

Answer

Flee youthful lusts. Follow righteousness, faith, love and peace. Do it with other believers, with a pure heart. Don't stand and argue with temptation: run, and run toward something. And don't run alone.
