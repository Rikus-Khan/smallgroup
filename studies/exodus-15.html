Exodus 15

The song of Moses, and the bitter water

On the far shore of the sea, a whole nation sings to the LORD. Three days later they are thirsty and angry, at a place called Marah. The LORD heals the water, and leads them on to Elim.

Part one

“I will sing unto the LORD”

Verses 1–12

The first half of the song: what the LORD did to the enemy at the sea.

The song begins with him

Hebrew poetry does not rhyme. It says a thing, and then says it again another way. Watch how the pieces line up.

I will sing unto the LORD, for he hath triumphed gloriously:

the horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea.

The LORD is my strength and song,

and he is become my salvation:

“My strength and song… my salvation”

“The LORD is my strength and song, and is become my salvation.”

Psalm 118:14

The line was so loved that it is sung again in Psalm 118 and in Isaiah 12:2. A man in trouble can borrow it: strength when he has none, song when he has nothing to sing about, salvation because he cannot rescue himself.

  • habitationa home, a dwelling place
  • exaltlift high, praise
  • my father’s Godthe God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob: not a new god, the same one who made the promise

“The LORD is a man of war”

Pharaoh’s chariots and his host hath he cast into the sea:

his chosen captains also are drowned in the Red sea.

The depths have covered them:

they sank into the bottom as a stone.

“A man of war” is the language of poetry: the LORD fights, and Israel did not. Israel had no army that day. And “the LORD is his name” means this is who he is: the one who wins and the one who saves.

The right hand, the blast and the wall

Thy right hand, O LORD, is become glorious in power:

thy right hand, O LORD, hath dashed in pieces the enemy.

  • excellencygreatness, majesty
  • stubbledry stalks left in a field after harvest, which a fire eats at once
  • congealedmade solid, set firm: the water stood like a wall
  • the blast of thy nostrilsa picture of the wind God sent: God does not have a nose, but poetry says it as a man would feel it

The song puts into words what verse 21 of chapter 14 put plainly: it was the LORD's wind. The right hand is the hand of strength. It is the same hand that “brought thee out” (13:9). Now it breaks the enemy.

What the enemy said, and what God did

The enemy

“I will… I will… I will…”

“I will pursue, I will overtake, I will divide the spoil; my lust shall be satisfied upon them; I will draw my sword, my hand shall destroy them.” Six boasts, every one about himself.

The LORD

“Thou didst blow”

“Thou didst blow with thy wind, the sea covered them: they sank as lead in the mighty waters.” One breath answered them all.

Spoil means plunder, and lust here is greedy desire to have and to do. The enemy was sure he would take what he wanted, and the sea closed over every word he said.

Sin talks like this to a man: I will overtake you. I will have what I want. It is a boast. When the LORD blows, it sinks like lead.

“Who is like unto thee?”

Who is like unto thee, O LORD, among the gods?

Who is like unto thee, O LORD, among the gods?

who is like thee, glorious in holiness, fearful in praises, doing wonders?

Fearful in praises means worthy of awe when he is praised. Egypt had many gods, for the river, the sun, the cattle, and the king himself. None of them could do this.

“Against all the gods of Egypt”

Before the Passover, God had said what he would do:

“For I will pass through the land of Egypt this night, and will smite all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast; and against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment: I am the LORD.”

Exodus 12:12

Now the answer sings out: who is like unto thee? The men in this room know what it is to put their trust in something that could not save them. There is no one like him.

The shape of the song

  1. verses 1–5The LORD has savedhorse and rider in the sea
  2. verses 6–12The enemy overthrown“who is like unto thee?”
  3. verses 13–18The people led hometo his holy habitation

The first half looks back at what God has done. The second half looks forward at what he will do. A song that begins with the sea ends with the mountain, and with “The LORD shall reign for ever and ever.”

Part two

“Thou in thy mercy hast led forth the people”

Verses 13–21

The song looks ahead, and Miriam takes up a timbrel.

The nations are listening

The song names the peoples ahead: Palestina (the KJV's word for Philistia, the coast by Gaza), the dukes (clan chiefs) of Edom, the mighty men of Moab, and the inhabitants of Canaan. They will hear, and be afraid.

They did. Forty years later a woman in Jericho said it herself:

“For we have heard how the LORD dried up the water of the Red sea for you, when ye came out of Egypt;”

Joshua 2:10

“In thy mercy… which thou hast redeemed”

Verse 13 is the center of the song. Not “in thy power”, but “in thy mercy hast led forth the people which thou hast redeemed.” They were redeemed: bought back. And the LORD does not stop at the sea. He guides them in his strength “unto thy holy habitation.”

Look at what they were not: they were not a strong people, or a good one. Just before this they had said they would rather be in Egypt. It was mercy.

If you are tempted to think God is finished with you because you were afraid, or because you wanted to go back, read verse 13 again. The LORD redeemed them first, and then he led them. He does it in that order with us.

“The LORD shall reign for ever and ever”

The song ends with a mountain and a throne. Purchased means bought, the people the LORD paid for. And a place “which thou hast made for thee to dwell in… the Sanctuary”. God does not only free his people. He wants to live with them. He would say so himself:

“And let them make me a sanctuary; that I may dwell among them.”

Exodus 25:8

That is where Exodus is going: the tabernacle, God's house in the middle of the camp. And past that, the same song is sung in heaven:

“And they sing the song of Moses the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying, Great and marvellous are thy works, Lord God Almighty; just and true are thy ways, thou King of saints.”

Revelation 15:3

Miriam and the timbrel

  • timbrela hand drum, like a tambourine
  • prophetessa woman who spoke for God
  • answeredsang back: the women took up the men's song

Verse 19 says it again: Pharaoh's horse went into the sea, and the LORD brought the waters back, “but the children of Israel went on dry land in the midst of the sea.” Then Miriam sings the first line of the song back.

Think who she was. Eighty or so years earlier she had been the girl who stood far off by the river, watching a baby in a basket (2:4). She had waited all her life to see this.

“Is any among you afflicted? let him pray. Is any merry? let him sing psalms.”

James 5:13

Singing is how a rescued man says thank you. Tell your brothers what the LORD has done. You do not need a good voice.

Part three

“The name of it was called Marah”

Verses 22–27

The song has barely died away. Then comes the first test of the road: three days without water.

After the sea, the desert

  1. the seaSong and danceExodus 15:1–21
  2. three daysThe wilderness of Shurno water
  3. MarahThe water is bitter15:23–26
  4. ElimTwelve wells15:27
  5. Mount SinaiThe mount of Godchapter 19

All of this is in Arabia, on the far side of the sea, on the way to Mount Sinai. The exact sites of Marah and Elim are not certain, so no map pins them here. What the text gives us is the order: the sea, the dry stretch, the bitter water, then the oasis.

Three days with no water is a long time. The people had sung about the LORD on the first day. By the third they were wondering if he had brought them out here to die.

Marah

Marah = bitter The name tells you what they found. At last, water, and they “could not drink of the waters of Marah, for they were bitter.”

There is a kind of bitterness that comes the very day after the best day. A man gets sober, or comes to Christ, and he sings. Then the first dry stretch arrives, and the first thing that was meant to satisfy him turns bitter in his mouth. That is not the failure of the rescue. It is the first test on the road, and it comes to every man.

Murmured, and cried

The people

They murmured

“What shall we drink?” Murmured means grumbled, complained under the breath, at Moses. It was the first complaint, and not the last.

Moses

He cried unto the LORD

He did not answer them or lecture them. He took it to God, and “the LORD shewed him a tree.”

The cure came from God, not from Moses' wisdom. He had to be shown it. And Moses had to throw the tree in. Prayer first, then the step.

“Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come.”

1 Corinthians 10:11

The tree

The text says only that the LORD showed Moses a tree, and that when he cast it into the waters the waters were made sweet. It does not say what the tree was.

“Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed.”

1 Peter 2:24

Peter does not tell us that Marah was a picture of the cross. But he does say that Jesus bore our sins “on the tree”, and that by his stripes we were healed. Many have seen the same pattern: something bitter, and something thrown into it that makes it sweet. What is certain is this: God can turn a bitter place sweet.

What has gone bitter for you? A marriage, a job, a friendship, a memory? The LORD is not surprised by it. Take it to him as Moses did, and ask him to show you what to do.

“I am the LORD that healeth thee”

Here, for the first time, the LORD makes a statute and an ordinance (a standing rule) for his people, and tests them: proved them means tested, put to the proof. If they hearken to his voice and keep his commandments, he will put none of “these diseases” on them, the plagues he brought on Egypt.

That was said to Israel, as a nation under his covenant. It is not a promise that no believer will ever be sick, and nothing in the Bible says that. What it shows is who God is: the one who heals. The Hebrew word is rapha, to heal, to mend.

“Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits: Who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases;”

Psalm 103:2–3

Notice the order in the psalm: first he forgives, and then he heals. He is the healer of the whole man: the body, and the sin and the sorrow that sicken a man inside. Come to him for it.

Elim

12wells of water
70palm trees

Threescore and ten is sixty plus ten: seventy. After the bitter water there was an oasis: twelve springs and seventy palms, shade and drink for the whole camp. “They encamped there by the waters.”

Marah was not the end of the road. Elim was just ahead. God did not bring them to the bitter place to leave them there, and he does not do that to us. If you are in a Marah today, keep walking. There may be an Elim on the other side of it.

“For the Lamb which is in the midst of the throne shall feed them, and shall lead them unto living fountains of waters: and God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes.”

Revelation 7:17

Question 1 of 6

Praise

Who did Moses and Israel sing to, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To the LORD, because he had triumphed over the army that was chasing them. He led out the people he had redeemed, in his mercy. The credit is his, and a rescued man is meant to sing.

Question 2 of 6

Salvation

What did Moses call the LORD in his song?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD is my strength and song, and is become my salvation.”

Psalm 118:14

Answer

His strength, his song and his salvation, and a man of war. When you have no strength, no song and no way out, he is all three.

Question 3 of 6

The enemy

What did the enemy say he would do, and what did the LORD do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He boasted: I will pursue, overtake, divide the spoil, and destroy them. The LORD blew with his wind, and the sea covered them. Sin makes big promises. One breath of God ends it.

Question 4 of 6

Who God is

Who is like unto the LORD?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No one. He is glorious in holiness, worthy of awe, and he does wonders. The gods of Egypt could not save, and he reigns for ever and ever. Put your trust in him.

Question 5 of 6

The bitter place

What happened at Marah, and how was the water made sweet?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

After three days without water, theirs was bitter, and the people murmured. Moses cried to the LORD, and he showed him a tree that made it sweet. Take bitter things to him.

Question 6 of 6

Rest

Where did Israel camp after the bitter water?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

At Elim, where there were twelve wells and seventy palm trees. The LORD is the one who heals, and he did not leave them at Marah. After the bitter place, he led them to rest.
