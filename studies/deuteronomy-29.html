Deuteronomy 29

The covenant in Moab, the root of bitterness, and the secret things

Moses reminds the new generation what the LORD did for forty years, and brings them, with their wives, little ones and strangers, into covenant with him. He warns of the man who hears the curse and says “I shall have peace”, and of the land that would be left like Sodom. “The secret things belong unto the LORD our God.”

Things to watch for

Part one

“Ye have seen all that the LORD did”

Verses 1–9

Moses tells the children what their own eyes have seen, and calls them to keep the covenant.

One covenant at Horeb, and now this one

At Horeb, Exodus 24:7

And he took the book of the covenant, and read in the audience of the people: and they said, All that the LORD hath said will we do, and be obedient.

In Moab, Deuteronomy 29:12

That thou shouldest enter into covenant with the LORD thy God, and into his oath, which the LORD thy God maketh with thee this day:

“Beside” here means in addition to. The LORD does not set the first covenant aside; he has Moses make this one with the children who stand on the plains of Moab. Their fathers said “All that the LORD hath said will we do” at Horeb, and the golden calf came soon after (we saw it in Exodus 32). Now it is their turn to hear it for themselves.

God never lets a man live on his father's promise or his own old promise. Every man has to stand before him this day.

Seen it all, and still needing a heart

Their eyes had seen“the signs, and those great miracles” (2–3)
→
And yet“not given you an heart to perceive, and eyes to see, and ears to hear” (4)
→
The answer, next chapter“the LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart” (30:6)

“Temptations” here means proofs, trials. Their fathers saw the same signs and still said “let us return into Egypt” (Numbers 14:4): seeing a miracle is not perceiving who is behind it.

Moses says it not to excuse them but to show where a seeing heart comes from: the LORD gives it. A man rescued out of the worst of it can still ask him for that heart, and he promises it next chapter.

Forty years, and nothing wore out

  • Your clothesnot waxen old upon you
  • Thy shoenot waxen old upon thy foot
  • Bread, wine, strong drinknone, and not a want

Each bar is the forty years (one tick for each year). “Waxen old” means grown old, worn out. A man who walked out of Egypt had walked forty years since, and the shoes on his feet were still holding. Moses says it as plain history, and it is: the LORD kept them, for “forty years in the wilderness”.

Nothing about it was clever. Their clothes lasted because the LORD took care of them. What a man could not do for himself, God did every day for forty years, and he is the same God to the man who has nothing today but a bed and a job to get to.

“Neither have ye drunk wine or strong drink”

What the verse says

Forty years without it

They did not eat the bread of a sown field, and they drank no wine or strong drink. They lived on manna (8:3) and on water the LORD gave them, “that ye might know that I am the LORD your God”.

What it was for

So they would know him

The point is not the cup. It is that they did not need it: the LORD fed them, kept them, and was enough.

Elsewhere in this book wine is part of a feast before the LORD (14:26), and a rebellious son is called “a glutton, and a drunkard” (21:20). We take each verse as it stands. But for a man whom God has set free from the cup, this verse is no door back in. It is proof that a man can go forty years without it and lack nothing.

The answer to a thirst

“But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life.”

John 4:14

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

The answer to a thirst is never more of the cup. It is to be filled with the Spirit, and to have brothers beside you. If today is hard, call or text your brothers in Christ.

Their own eyes had seen this too

We read of Sihon and Og in Numbers 21, and Moses told it again in Deuteronomy 2 and 3. Their land east of the Jordan was given to Reuben, Gad and the half tribe of Manasseh.

What God has already done for a man is the best ground for trusting him with what is next.

“That ye may prosper”

Prosper here means go well, come through, succeed in what you do. It was a promise to Israel, in their covenant, in their land: if they kept the words of the covenant, the LORD would go with them in everything they set their hand to.

It is not a promise to us that obedience pays in money or health. Those blessings were Israel's, in their land, and the book of Job and the cross show that a righteous man may suffer. The same word comes to Joshua with the book of the law in his hand: “then thou shalt make thy way prosperous, and then thou shalt have good success” (Joshua 1:8).

Ours is a blessing already given, “all spiritual blessings… in Christ” (Ephesians 1:3), and it does not rise and fall with how we did yesterday.

Part two

“Ye stand this day all of you”

Verses 10–15

From the captains to the one who chops the wood, and then those who are not even born.

Who stood in the covenant

The leadersyour captains, elders and officers
All the men of Israelevery man of the camp
Your little ones and your wivesnamed in the covenant, not left out
The stranger in thy camp“from the hewer of thy wood unto the drawer of thy water”

A hewer cuts wood, and a drawer of water carries it from the well: the lowest jobs in the camp, in the same covenant as the captains. “This day” (or “to day”) comes eight times in this chapter: the covenant is for the people standing there.

An oath is a sworn promise, and here God swears it: “that he may be unto thee a God, as he hath said unto thee, and as he hath sworn unto thy fathers” (13).

“Him that is not here with us this day”

Him that standeth here with us this dayeveryone in front of Moses on the plain
And also him that is not herethe children not yet born, and every generation after

Moses reaches past the plain to every son and grandson of Israel who would ever hear this read. The covenant belongs to them as surely as to the men who heard it.

“For the promise is unto you, and to your children, and to all that are afar off, even as many as the Lord our God shall call.”

Acts 2:39

Peter says it on the day of Pentecost, and the words are very like Moses'. God's promises are made to people he has not yet met: the man at the back who was not there, the man who comes in late, the man who has been a long way off. The Lord calls, and the call goes out past the room.

Part three

“A root that beareth gall and wormwood”

Verses 16–21

The warning against a heart that turns away quietly, and tells itself it is safe.

How the root grows

  1. a heart turns away“this day from the LORD” (18)
  2. a root“that beareth gall and wormwood” (18)
  3. he blesses himself“I shall have peace” (19)
  4. the anger of the LORD“shall smoke against that man” (20)

Gall and wormwood are two bitter plants, and gall is poison. A root is hidden, and you do not see it grow. It looks like nothing until the day it bears. Moses says it can be “man, or woman, or family, or tribe”: anyone in the camp, in any house.

The turning away does not begin with an idol. It begins in the heart, “this day”, quietly, and the bitter fruit comes later.

He heard it, and blessed himself

The man hears the curse“all the curses that are written in this book”

In his heartI shall have peace, though I walk in the imagination of mine heart.
blessing himself

Imagination here means what the heart devises and follows: its own way. He has heard God's warning and tells himself it does not mean him. He is going his own way and calling it peace.

“To add drunkenness to thirst”

More of the cup, and the thirst only grows

The verse says one plain thing: he adds drunkenness to thirst. Many of us know that word from the inside. The cup never satisfied the thirst; it made it worse, and a man who answers an old craving with the old thing is adding drunkenness to thirst.

The verse is wider than the bottle. It is about any man who hears the warning, shrugs, and keeps feeding what is killing him, saying in his heart, “I shall have peace.”

Pull the root up early

“Looking diligently lest any man fail of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you, and thereby many be defiled;”

Hebrews 12:15

“Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God. But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

Hebrews 3:12–13

Hebrews takes Moses' root and gives it to the church: it troubles you, and “many be defiled”. The answer is not to watch yourself alone. It is “exhort one another daily”: the men beside you, while it is still “To day”.

A root named out loud

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

If you feel a root in yourself, a resentment, a secret, a plan you are telling yourself is fine, say it to a brother in Christ today, before it bears. A root named out loud is a root half pulled.

He is faithful and just to forgive: the door that was shut to the man who blessed himself is open to the man who confesses.

Why so severe?

The curse falls on this man because of what he did with the warning. He heard the curses (“all the curses that are written in this book”), and he blessed himself. The sin that has no fear left in it is the one nothing can reach. “The anger of the LORD and his jealousy shall smoke against that man”: the LORD is jealous for his people the way a husband is for his wife.

It is a warning to the whole camp, written before anyone has done it. The LORD says it ahead of time so that no one has to find out the hard way.

For a man in Christ, the answer is not to be afraid of a curse but to be quick to turn, and to confess.

Part four

“Wherefore hath the LORD done thus unto this land?”

Verses 22–29

The land left like Sodom, and what we are to do with what we do not know.

The question, and the answer

All nationsWherefore hath the LORD done thus unto this land? what meaneth the heat of this great anger?

Men shall sayBecause they have forsaken the covenant of the LORD God of their fathers… and served other gods.

Brimstone is burning sulphur. Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah and Zeboim were the cities “which the LORD overthrew in his anger, and in his wrath”. Moses speaks of a day still ahead, when strangers look at a ruined land and ask what happened, and he gives the answer in advance: they forsook the LORD, and served gods “whom they knew not, and whom he had not given unto them”.

Said before, and so it was

“For the children of Israel walked in all the sins of Jeroboam which he did; they departed not from them; Until the LORD removed Israel out of his sight, as he had said by all his servants the prophets. So was Israel carried away out of their own land to Assyria unto this day.”

2 Kings 17:22–23

Moses said it on the plains of Moab. The history says it again, in the same terms: they “departed not”, and the LORD “had said” it by his servants the prophets. The word did not fail. It was the people who did not hear.

That cuts both ways. If his warnings come true, so do his promises, and the next chapter is full of them: the same God who removed them says “will return and gather thee”.

Two kinds of things

Belong to the LORD

“The secret things”

What he has not told us: why a thing happened as it did, what he will do with someone we love, the days and times kept in his hand.

Belong to us

“Those things which are revealed”

What he has told us, in this book: “that we may do all the words of this law”. They belong to us “and to our children for ever”.

Many of the questions that keep men awake belong in the first column: Why did it happen to me? Why was I the one who lived? Why did he go so soon? The chapter does not say they are wrong to ask. It says some answers are kept with the LORD, and that he is to be trusted with them.

And a man is never left with nothing to do. What he has told us is plain, and it is ours to do: pray, read, go to work, tell a brother the truth, confess, come back. Do the thing that is revealed today, and leave the secret thing with him.

Real world: this day

  • SinThe root that grows quietly: a heart turning away this day, and a man telling himself “I shall have peace” while he feeds the old craving.
  • RegretWe have seen great things and still stumbled. Moses told these people that too: seeing is not perceiving, and a heart that perceives has to be given.
  • GraceThe same Lord who kept their clothes for forty years keeps us, and is “faithful and just to forgive us our sins” when we confess (1 John 1:9).
  • HopeThe covenant reaches “him that is not here”. Nobody is too late, too far, or too low in the camp to be brought in.
  • TodayName one root to a brother in Christ before it bears. And one thing you cannot understand: leave it with the LORD, and go do what is revealed. If it is heavy, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor today.

Question 1 of 6

The covenant

Where was this covenant made, and beside which other covenant?

What verses tell us about this?

“That thou shouldest enter into covenant with the LORD thy God, and into his oath, which the LORD thy God maketh with thee this day:”

Deuteronomy 29:12

Answer

In the land of Moab, beside the covenant he made with them in Horeb. The children of those who stood at Horeb now stand in Moab, and the LORD makes the covenant with them.

Question 2 of 6

Forty years

What did not wear out in the wilderness, and what had Israel not eaten or drunk?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Their clothes and their shoes did not wear out in forty years. They had not eaten bread, nor drunk wine or strong drink, “that ye might know that I am the LORD your God”.

Question 3 of 6

Who stood there

Who stood in the covenant, and who else was included?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All of them: the captains, elders and officers, all the men, the little ones, the wives, and the stranger, from the hewer of wood to the drawer of water. And also “him that is not here with us this day”, the generations to come.

Question 4 of 6

The root

What is the root that beareth gall and wormwood, and what does the man say in his heart?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A man, woman, family or tribe whose heart turns away from the LORD to serve other gods. When he hears the curse he blesses himself in his heart: “I shall have peace, though I walk in the imagination of mine heart.”

Question 5 of 6

The land

What will the nations say when they see the land, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

All nations will ask, “Wherefore hath the LORD done thus unto this land?” and men will answer: because they forsook the covenant of the LORD God of their fathers and served other gods.

Question 6 of 6

The secret things

What belongs to the LORD, and what belongs to us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The secret things belong to the LORD our God. The things that are revealed belong to us and to our children for ever, “that we may do all the words of this law”.
