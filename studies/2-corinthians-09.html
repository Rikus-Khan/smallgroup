2 Corinthians 9

A cheerful giver

Sowing, reaping, and the gift that is bigger than anything we can give. Giving is a response to grace. It isn't a way to get rich.

Where we are in 2 Corinthians

About AD 56, from Macedonia. Paul has just spent chapter 8 on why a believer gives. Now he gets practical: the gift for Jerusalem is nearly ready, and he wants it done properly.

1–2Comfort in trouble
3–4The new covenant
5Reconciled to God
6Open hearts
7Titus brings news
8–9The collection
10–13Paul answers his critics

Chapter 8 was why, and chapter 9 is how: finish what you promised, give from the heart, and say thank you to God. Read alone, it still makes sense: a church is about to send help to people in need, and Paul tells them how to do it well.

Part one

Be ready

Verses 1–5

A promise made in public, and a friend who helps you keep it.

A gift a year in the making

  1. about AD 55Paul tells Corinth to set money aside“lay by him in store” (1 Cor. 16:2)
  2. about AD 55–56Corinth starts, then stalls“ready a year ago” (9:2)
  3. about AD 56Paul writes, and sends Titus and two brothers ahead“go before unto you” (9:5)
  4. about AD 57Paul carries the gift to Jerusalem“to bring alms to my nation” (Acts 24:17)

The brothers aren't checking up on Corinth. They're there so the gift is ready when Paul arrives. The word he uses, “bounty,” means a blessing, and he wants it a real blessing, “and not as of covetousness”: not squeezed out of people.

A promise out loud

Paul, to the Macedonians“Achaia was ready a year ago.” (v. 2)

Paul, to Corinth“Lest haply if they of Macedonia come with me, and find you unprepared, we (that we say not, ye) should be ashamed in this same confident boasting.” (v. 4)

The fix“I thought it necessary to exhort the brethren, that they would go before unto you.” (v. 5)

Paul had bragged about Corinth in public. Now he needs them to finish, and he does it gently: a friend goes ahead to help, and nobody is humiliated.

Everyone has said “I'll do it” and then let it slide. Pick one thing you told somebody you'd do, and finish it today. A kept promise is a gift too.

Part two

Sowing and reaping

Verses 6–11

What a farmer knows about giving, and what Paul does not promise.

A farmer's arithmetic

Any farmer knows it: you can't eat what you didn't plant. Paul uses a plain picture. A man who plants a few seeds gets a small harvest, and a man who plants freely gets a big one.

So what's the harvest?

  • a fatter bank accountPaul never says the harvest is money
  • a deal: “give to get”that's a trade, not a gift, and not grace
  • God owing youHe owes no man a thing
  • more grace, and more to give“the fruits of your righteousness,” and “thanksgiving to God” (vv. 10–11)

Some preachers read this chapter as “send money and God will make you rich.” That's not what's here. Look at who's giving: Macedonians in deep poverty, for poor believers far away. Paul's harvest is righteous living and thanks to God.

Paul even says what you get back: “all sufficiency in all things,” enough, so that you may “abound to every good work.” God supplies the seed so you can sow more. You aren't paid back in cash. You're made able to give again.

Three ways to give

Grudgingly

“Fine, here.”

The gift comes with a sour face. It may be right, but the heart isn't in it.

Of necessity

“I have to.”

Pushed by guilt, pressure, or somebody watching. Paul says no to this as well.

Cheerfully

“I decided, and I'm glad.”

Decided ahead, “as he purposeth in his heart,” and given with joy. The Greek word is where we get hilarious.

“God loveth a cheerful giver” doesn't mean put on a smile. It means decide ahead what you can honestly give, in a quiet moment, before anybody asks. A small gift decided on in advance beats a big one squeezed out.

That works for time and help, not just money. If you can only give twenty minutes, give twenty good ones, and be glad you did.

Where the seed comes from

  1. Godsupplies the seed

    “ministereth seed to the sower”

  2. Yousow it

    to “every good work”

  3. Godbread, and more seed

    “bread for your food, and multiply your seed sown”

  4. The harvestrighteousness, thanks

    “the fruits of your righteousness”

“He hath dispersed, he hath given to the poor; his righteousness endureth for ever; his horn shall be exalted with honour.”

Psalm 112:9

Notice who owns the seed. It was never yours: God supplies it. All you do is decide whether it sits in a jar or goes in the ground. A man with nothing in his hands this morning may still have time, strength and a story to sow.

Part three

Thanks to God

Verses 12–15

What one gift sets in motion, and the gift behind it.

One gift, four ripples

  1. 1The need is met. The gift “supplieth the want of the saints.”
  2. 2Thanks to God. “Many thanksgivings unto God.”
  3. 3God is glorified. They see the gospel at work in Gentiles.
  4. 4They pray for you, and long for you. Jews and Gentiles, one family.

Thanks be to God for his unspeakable gift.

Two chapters on money end here, with the gift no money can pay for: Jesus. Everything we give is a thank-you for that one. “Unspeakable” means beyond words, too big to describe.

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

It's a gift: you couldn't earn it, and you can't pay it back. That's good news if you're carrying regret. You can't make up for last week by being generous. You also don't have to. You're already forgiven, and you give out of thanks, not out of guilt.

Today: decide one thing to give, before anyone asks. A little money, an hour, a ride, a kind word, a call to a man who's struggling. Decide it now, give it gladly, and say “thank You” for the gift you can't repay.

Points to remember

  1. Ready“I thought it necessary to exhort the brethren, that they would go before unto you” 9:5Finish what you promised. Let a friend help you do it.
  2. Sowing“He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly” 9:6Plant freely. The harvest is righteousness and thanks to God, and not a promise of cash.
  3. The heart“Not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver” 9:7Decide ahead, give gladly. Time and help count too.
  4. The seed“Now he that ministereth seed to the sower” 9:10God supplies the seed. You decide whether it sits in a jar or goes in the ground.
  5. Thanks“Abundant also by many thanksgivings unto God” 9:12A gift meets a need, and also sets off thanks, prayer, and love between believers.
  6. The gift“Thanks be unto God for his unspeakable gift” 9:15Jesus. You can't earn it or repay it, so you give out of thanks.

Question 1 of 6

Ready

Why did Paul send the brothers ahead to Corinth?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So the gift would be ready when Paul came, and nobody would be embarrassed. He had bragged about Corinth. A friend going ahead helped them finish what they'd promised.

Question 2 of 6

Sowing

What happens to the man who sows sparingly, and the man who sows bountifully?

What verses tell us about this?

“They that sow in tears shall reap in joy. He that goeth forth and weepeth, bearing precious seed, shall doubtless come again with rejoicing, bringing his sheaves with him.”

Psalm 126:5–6

Answer

A small sower gets a small harvest, and a generous one gets a big one. The harvest Paul names is righteousness and thanks to God, and not a bigger bank balance. Giving is not a way to get rich.

Question 3 of 6

The heart

How does God want a man to give?

What verses tell us about this?

“For if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath, and not according to that he hath not.”

2 Corinthians 8:12

Answer

Gladly, and by his own decision: not grudgingly, and not out of pressure. Decide ahead what you honestly can, and give it cheerfully. God never asks for what you don't have.

Question 4 of 6

The seed

Who supplies the seed, and what is it for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God supplies it, and it's for sowing: for every good work, and so there's bread for you and seed to give again. It was never ours to hoard.

Question 5 of 6

Thanks

What does a gift do besides meet a need?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It sets off thanks to God, glory to Him, and prayer and love between believers who've never met. One gift ties the family together.

Question 6 of 6

The gift

What is God's “unspeakable gift”?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

Romans 6:23

“He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?”

Romans 8:32

Answer

Jesus Himself, and the eternal life He gives. Nobody can earn it or repay it. We give because we've been given so much.
