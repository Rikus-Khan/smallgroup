Galatians 2

Crucified with Christ

Paul stands up for the gospel in Jerusalem, then stands up to Peter at Antioch. Then he says what it all comes down to: Christ liveth in me.

So far: teachers had come to the young churches of Galatia saying Gentile believers must keep the law of Moses to be saved. Paul answered, “Which is not another” gospel (1:7). Then he told how the gospel came to him: straight from Jesus, to a man who had tried to destroy the church.

Chapter 2 finishes his story with two showdowns, one in Jerusalem and one in Antioch. Then comes the verse many men have taped to their mirror: “I am crucified with Christ.”

Part one

Not for an hour

Verses 1–5

Paul brings an uncircumcised Greek to Jerusalem, and won't give an inch.

Up to Jerusalem

  1. Paulthe former persecutor
  2. Barnabasa Jew from Cyprus, the man who first stood up for Paul (Acts 9:27)
  3. Titusa Greek believer, never circumcised: the test case

Fourteen years after Paul came to Christ. Many think this was the trip of Acts 11:30, carrying famine help; others think it was the council of Acts 15. Either way, Paul laid his gospel before the leaders to make sure his work wasn't “in vain.”

Later, Peter came north to Antioch (v. 11). Hold on to that.

Titus, the test case

“And certain men which came down from Judaea taught the brethren, and said, Except ye be circumcised after the manner of Moses, ye cannot be saved.”

Acts 15:1

What the false brethren wanted

Bondage

Titus has to become a Jew first. Faith in Jesus isn't enough; add the knife and the rule book.

What Paul defended

Liberty

Titus belongs to Jesus as he is. “No, not for an hour.” Not one inch, “that the truth of the gospel might continue with you.”

Privily means sneakily. These men spied. Paul fought for Titus so he could fight for the Galatians, and for you. You don't have to become a “church type” before you belong to Christ.

Part two

The right hands of fellowship

Verses 6–10

The famous leaders add nothing to Paul's gospel. They shake his hand.

God plays no favorites

Three times Paul calls the leaders men who “seemed” to be somebody (vv. 6, 9). He isn't mocking them. He's saying a big name doesn't impress God. “Accepteth no man's person” means God doesn't judge by the face: rank, fame, money, the right family.

That cuts both ways. The man with the reputation gets no extra credit. And the man with a record gets no less grace. God looks at Christ, and at whether a man has trusted Him.

Notice too: the same God “wrought effectually”, worked powerfully, in Peter among the Jews and in Paul among the Gentiles (v. 8). One God, one gospel, two very different men.

Partners, not rivals

Pillars: the men who held up the church in Jerusalem. Cephas is Peter's Aramaic name. A right handshake was how men sealed a partnership.

They split the work, not the gospel: Peter to the Jews, Paul to the Gentiles.

One thing they asked: “remember the poor.” Paul was already eager to. Grace that's real reaches for a wallet. Who could you help today?

Part three

To the face

Verses 11–14

Even Peter can slip back into fear. Somebody has to say so.

The table at Antioch

Peter ate with Gentile believers, one family at one table.

“Certain came from James”: strict Jewish believers from Jerusalem.

Peter got up and moved, “fearing them which were of the circumcision.”

The other Jewish believers followed him.

Then even Barnabas. Nobody said a word against the Gentiles. They just changed seats, and the message was loud: you're second class.

Fear of men

ὑπόκρισις said hupokrisis playing a part, like an actor behind a mask

Dissembled and dissimulation are this word: our word hypocrisy. Peter knew better. God had shown him years before “that I should not call any man common or unclean” (Acts 10:28). This was the same Peter who once denied Jesus to a servant girl by a fire. Fear of what people think still had a hook in him.

“The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.”

Proverbs 29:25

How Paul did it

  1. To the facenot behind his back, not in gossip
  2. Before them allthe harm was public, so the fix was too
  3. About the gospelnot his pride: “the truth of the gospel”

“Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.”

Proverbs 27:6

Every man in recovery needs a brother who'll tell him the truth to his face, and needs to be that brother. And it didn't end Peter. Years later he wrote of “our beloved brother Paul” (2 Peter 3:15). A fall, a hard word, and the friendship held.

Part four

Christ liveth in me

Verses 15–21

What Paul said to Peter, and what it means for every one of us.

Three times in one verse

Justified is a courtroom word: the Judge declares a man in the right. How does a guilty man get that verdict? Verse 16 answers three times:

  • by the works of the law“a man is not justified” that way
  • by the works of the law“and not” that way
  • by the works of the law“shall no flesh be justified”: no one, ever
  • by the faith of Jesus Christ“even we have believed in Jesus Christ”

Paul is talking to Peter, a Jew, raised on the law: even we had to come by faith. If the law couldn't justify Peter, your best week won't justify you. And your worst week can't undo what faith receives.

Don't rebuild what God tore down

IfI build again the things which I destroyed,
→
thenI make myself a transgressor.

Some said, “If you're right with God without the law, men will just keep sinning, and Christ gets the blame.” “God forbid!” Grace isn't a license.

“What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid. How shall we, that are dead to sin, live any longer therein?”

Romans 6:1–2

Peter tore down the wall at the table, then built it back. Going back to earning God's favor is building again. So is going back to the old life. Either way, don't pick up the bricks.

I am crucified with Christ:

nevertheless I live;

yet not I,

but Christ liveth in me.

When Jesus died, the old you died with Him in God's eyes: the man who tried to earn it, and the man who gave up trying. “Nevertheless I live”: same name, same job, same body. “Yet not I”: the life now runs on a different source. Christ lives in every believer by His Spirit, from the day he believes.

“who loved me, and gave himself for me.”

Not “loved the world” this time. Me. Paul, the man who voted for believers to die, says it about himself. You can say it about yourself.

“Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin.”

Romans 6:6

“The life which I now live in the flesh” is today: the shift, the commute, the craving at four o'clock. You live it by faith, one hour at a time. The old man is dead; you don't have to take his orders. Say this verse out loud before you walk out the door.

Dead in vain?

If righteousness came by the law

The cross was for nothing

If a man could keep enough rules to be right with God, Jesus didn't need to die.

But it doesn't

The cross did what nothing else could

He died because there was no other way. Nothing needs adding to it.

Frustrate means set aside, treat as nothing. Every time we try to pay God back for our own sins, we push His grace aside. The man who fell yesterday doesn't need to earn his way back. He needs to come back to the cross, confess, and take the grace again.

Points to remember

  1. Free“To whom we gave place by subjection, no, not for an hour” 2:5A Gentile belongs to Christ by faith alone. Don't let anyone put the chains back on.
  2. The pillars“They gave to me and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship” 2:9One gospel, different fields. God plays no favorites.
  3. Fear of men“Fearing them which were of the circumcision” 2:12Even a leader can slip back into fear of men. Watch for it in yourself.
  4. To the face“I withstood him to the face” 2:11Speak the truth to a brother's face, for the gospel's sake, and keep the friendship.
  5. Justified“A man is not justified by the works of the law, but by the faith of Jesus Christ” 2:16Declared right with God by faith in Christ, never by rule-keeping.
  6. Christ in me“I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me” 2:20The old man died with Christ. Christ lives in you. Live today by faith in Him who loved you.

Question 1 of 6

Free

Why would Paul not let Titus be circumcised?

What verses tell us about this?

“And certain men which came down from Judaea taught the brethren, and said, Except ye be circumcised after the manner of Moses, ye cannot be saved.”

Acts 15:1

Answer

Because false brethren were saying it was needed to be saved. Giving in would have taught that faith in Christ isn't enough, and brought believers into bondage. Paul held firm so that the truth of the gospel would continue.

Question 2 of 6

The pillars

What did James, Peter and John do when they saw the grace given to Paul?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They added nothing to his gospel. They gave Paul and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship, as partners: Paul to the Gentiles, they to the Jews. They only asked them to remember the poor.

Question 3 of 6

Fear of men

Why did Peter pull away from the Gentile believers at Antioch?

What verses tell us about this?

“The fear of man bringeth a snare: but whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe.”

Proverbs 29:25

Answer

Fear. When strict Jewish believers came from Jerusalem, he was afraid of what they'd think, so he stopped eating with Gentiles. Others followed, even Barnabas. Fear of men is a snare, even for a great leader.

Question 4 of 6

To the face

How did Paul deal with Peter's hypocrisy?

What verses tell us about this?

“Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.”

Proverbs 27:6

Answer

He went to him directly, to his face, in front of everyone who had been affected, and he made it about the truth of the gospel, not about himself. A true friend tells you the truth.

Question 5 of 6

Justified

How is a man justified before God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law.”

Romans 3:28

“Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;”

Titus 3:5

Answer

By faith in Jesus Christ, not by the works of the law. No one, Jew or Gentile, is declared right with God by keeping rules. God declares right the man who trusts His Son.

Question 6 of 6

Christ in me

What does it mean to be crucified with Christ?

What verses tell us about this?

“Knowing this, that our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin.”

Romans 6:6

Answer

When Christ died, the old man died with Him. The believer still lives, but Christ lives in him, and he lives each day by faith in the Son of God who loved him and gave Himself for him. Sin is no longer his master.
