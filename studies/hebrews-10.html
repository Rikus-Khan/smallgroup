Once for all

The priests stood every day and never finished. Jesus offered one sacrifice and sat down. The work is done, the way is open, and God has decided not to bring your sins up again.

This chapter is a hinge. Verses 1–18 close the writer's long argument. From verse 19 on he tells us what to do about it. The writer of Hebrews never puts doctrine and life in separate boxes, and neither should we.

Part one

One sacrifice for ever

Verses 1–18

A shadow, a body prepared, a Priest who sat down, and sins God will not remember.

A shadow, and the real thing

A shadow is real, and it tells you something is coming: its shape, its size. But you can't shake hands with a shadow. The sacrifices of the law were the shadow of what Christ would do.

“Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.”

Colossians 2:17

Don't go back to the shadow when the real thing is standing in front of you. That was the danger for these Hebrew believers, and it's ours when we try to fix ourselves with rules instead of Christ.

A reminder, not a cure

If those sacrifices had really cleansedthey would have stopped
→
but they came round every yeara yearly reminder of sin

Every Day of Atonement said again: you are still guilty. The sacrifice kept the guilt on the books instead of ending it.

It is not possible that the blood of bulls and goats should take away sins.

Some of us live like that: the same confession, the same shame, round and round, as if Christ's work needed topping up. It doesn't. Keep reading.

“Lo, I come”

These are the words of Psalm 40, and the writer hears Jesus saying them as He came into the world. Watch what God sets aside, and what He takes up.

  • sacrifice and offering“thou wouldest not”
  • burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin“thou hast had no pleasure”
  • a body hast thou prepared methe Son comes in a real body, to do God's will all the way to the cross

“I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart.”

Psalm 40:8

God never wanted dead animals for their own sake. He wanted a man who would obey Him perfectly. No man ever did, until Jesus. His obedience is counted for everyone who trusts Him.

Set apart, once for all

The first → The second He taketh away the old way of animal sacrifice, to establish the new: one offering, His own body.

Sanctified means set apart for God, made His. Notice the tense: we are sanctified. It isn't a prize at the end of a long clean streak. It happened when Christ's body was offered, and it reached you the day you trusted Him.

Once for all: one time, for all time. You will grow (verse 14 says we are being sanctified), but the setting apart was done at the cross.

The priests stood. Christ sat down.

Remember the tabernacle from chapter 9: no chair. A priest's work was never done, so he never sat. Jesus offered one sacrifice and sat down. A man sits down when the job is finished.

“When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost.”

John 19:30

Perfected for ever

Perfected doesn't mean you'll never fail again. It means complete before God: nothing missing from your account, nothing left to pay. And it says for ever.

So when you stumble, the cross doesn't need to be done again. You don't get re-saved every time you repent. You come back to a Father who already calls you His, confess it, and get up.

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

And He sits expecting: waiting until every enemy is under His feet. The fight is still going on in you, but the outcome isn't in doubt.

REMEMBEREDno more

“I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins.”

Isaiah 43:25

God knows everything, so this isn't forgetfulness. It's a promise: He will never bring them up against you again. If the Judge has closed the file, don't keep opening it.

Nothing left to pay

The Holy Ghost Himself witnesses to it, from Jeremiah 31: the new covenant ends with sins forgiven for good. And where sins are forgiven, nobody needs to bring another offering.

“As far as the east is from the west, so far hath he removed our transgressions from us.”

Psalm 103:12

So stop trying to pay. Not with misery, not with overtime at church, not by punishing yourself. Regret can make you humble and careful. It can't pay a bill that's already been paid. Thank Him instead.

Part two

Let us draw near

Verses 19–25

The veil is open. Come in, hold on, and don't come alone.

A new and living way

For the high priest it was one day a year, and he went in trembling. For you, the writer says, it's boldness, any day, any hour, by the blood of Jesus.

The veil was a picture of his flesh. When His body was torn on the cross, the curtain in the temple tore from the top down. The way in was opened from God's side.

New: never there before. Living: not a dead animal's blood, but a living Saviour who walked the way first.

Three “let us”

  1. Let us draw nearTo God, with a true heart, fully sure He'll have us (10:22).
  2. Let us hold fastWhat we confess, without wavering, because He is faithful (10:23).
  3. Let us consider one anotherThink about each other, and stir each other up to love and good works (10:24).

The priests of old were sprinkled with blood and washed with water before they served. Every believer is now a man made clean inside, an evil conscience sprinkled clean. Many Bible teachers hear baptism in bodies washed: not what saves, but the outward picture of the washing Christ already did.

“Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you. Cleanse your hands, ye sinners; and purify your hearts, ye double minded.”

James 4:8

Why this group meets

Pull one coal out of the fire and set it on the hearth. It doesn't go out because it's a bad coal. It goes out because it's alone. Put it back, and it catches again.

Some had already got into the habit of staying away: as the manner of some is. The writer says the opposite: so much the more, the closer the day of Christ's return gets.

Provoke each other, the good way

Most of us know how to provoke a man the bad way. Here it means stir him up: to love, to good works. Exhorting means coming alongside with a word that puts courage back in him.

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted. Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:1–2

The man who skips is usually the man who's struggling, and the enemy likes him best alone. If a brother has gone quiet, call him today. And if you're the one who wants to hide, that's exactly the day to show up.

Part three

Don't draw back

Verses 26–39

A hard warning for the man who walks away from the only sacrifice, and a strong word for the man who means to keep going.

No other sacrifice

These were Hebrews tempted to go back to the temple. The writer says: if you leave Christ, where will you go?

  • the old animal sacrificestaken away (10:9); they never took sin away anyway
  • your own good worksdead works (9:14)
  • another saviourthere isn't one
  • Christ's one sacrificethe only one there is, and enough for any sinner who comes

That's what no more sacrifice means. Not that God's patience has a limit for a believer who keeps falling, but that a man who rejects the one sacrifice has nothing left to stand on. Many Bible teachers see here men who sat close to the faith, even went through the motions, but never truly trusted Christ.

Who is this warning about?

Read it slowly. Look at what this man does: he has trodden under foot the Son of God, counted Christ's blood an unholy thing, and done despite (shown contempt) the Spirit of grace.

The man it describes

Turns his back on Christ

He has heard the truth plainly, then settles it: he wants nothing to do with Jesus' sacrifice. It isn't one sin. It's walking away from the only Saviour, with contempt.

The man it doesn't describe

A believer who stumbled

A man who fell, hates it, and comes back to the cross. He isn't trampling the blood. He's running to it. Every believer sins (1 John 1:8). That isn't what this is.

“They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would no doubt have continued with us: but they went out, that they might be made manifest that they were not all of us.”

1 John 2:19

If you're afraid you've done this

The man afraid he has done it is not the man described.

The man in verse 29 doesn't lie awake worried about Christ's blood. He despises it. If your heart sinks when you read this, if you want Jesus and hate your sin, that is the Spirit of grace at work in you, not gone from you.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

A fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God: for the man who tramples His Son. For the man who has fled to Christ, those same hands are the ones holding him.

Remember how far you've come

  1. Made a gazingstockA public spectacle, mocked for following Jesus.
  2. Stood with the prisonersThey visited and helped believers in chains, and were treated the same.
  3. Lost their goodsTheir homes were plundered, and they took it joyfully.
  4. Why?“A better and an enduring substance” in heaven, that no one can take.

When you're tired, look back. Remember the man you were, the first days after you believed, the things God has already carried you through. The God who brought you this far didn't bring you here to drop you.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Which kind of men we are

Draw back→unto perditionthe man who turns away from Christ for good: ruin
Believe→to the saving of the soulthe man who keeps trusting Christ: kept, all the way home

Look at the writer's last word: we are not of them. After the hardest warning in the book, he doesn't leave his readers in fear. He puts them, and every true believer, on the side of faith.

“But, beloved, we are persuaded better things of you, and things that accompany salvation, though we thus speak.”

Hebrews 6:9

Yet a little while

Don't throw your confidence away like a soldier tossing his shield. You need patience, staying power, because the promise comes at the end of the road, not the start.

“For the vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end it shall speak, and not lie: though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry.”

Habakkuk 2:3

The just shall live by faith: not by feelings, not by a perfect week. By trusting Him one day at a time. Yet a little while, and He will come.

For today: draw near (pray before work), hold fast (say out loud what you believe), and consider one brother (call or text him). Then be back here tomorrow.

Points to remember

  1. A shadow“It is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins” 10:4The law was a shadow. Its sacrifices only reminded men of sin, year after year. Christ is the real thing.
  2. He sat down“After he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down” 10:12The priests stood because their work was never done. Christ sat down: it is finished.
  3. Forgotten“Their sins and iniquities will I remember no more” 10:17God has promised never to bring them up again. Nothing is left to pay.
  4. Draw near“Boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus” 10:19The veil is open. Come to God any day, sure He'll have you, because of the blood.
  5. Together“Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together” 10:25A coal alone goes cold. We meet to stir each other up, more and more as the day comes.
  6. Hold on“We are not of them who draw back unto perdition” 10:39The warning is for the man who tramples Christ's blood. Believers cast not away their confidence: they believe to the saving of the soul.

Question 1 of 6

A shadow

Why could the old sacrifices never take away sins?

What verses tell us about this?

“Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.”

Colossians 2:17

Answer

Because the law was only a shadow of what was coming, not the real thing. Animal blood could never take away sins, so the sacrifices had to be repeated, and every year they reminded the people of their sins all over again.

Question 2 of 6

He sat down

What did Christ do after He offered one sacrifice for sins, and why does it matter?

What verses tell us about this?

“When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost.”

John 19:30

Answer

He sat down on the right hand of God. The priests always stood, because their work was never done. Christ sat because His one offering finished it: it has perfected for ever those who are sanctified.

Question 3 of 6

Forgotten

What does God promise to do with our sins and iniquities?

What verses tell us about this?

“I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake, and will not remember thy sins.”

Isaiah 43:25

Answer

To remember them no more: never to bring them up against us again. Where sins are forgiven like that, no more offering is needed. There is nothing left to pay.

Question 4 of 6

Draw near

How can a man come near to God with boldness?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By the blood of Jesus, through the new and living way He opened when His flesh was torn like the veil. We have a great High Priest, so we can come with a true heart, in full assurance of faith, with a conscience sprinkled clean.

Question 5 of 6

Together

Why must believers not forsake the assembling of themselves together?

What verses tell us about this?

“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up.”

Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

Answer

Because we need each other to keep going. Together we stir one another up to love and good works and put courage back into each other, and the closer the day of Christ's return, the more we need it. Alone, a man goes cold.

Question 6 of 6

Hold on

Who is the warning of verses 26–31 about, and what are believers told to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A man who knew the truth and then turned his back on Christ for good, trampling His Son and despising His blood. For him no other sacrifice remains. Believers are told to keep their confidence and keep believing: we are not of them who draw back.
