Mark 4

Seed, soil, and a storm

Jesus teaches from a boat about a sower and his seed, then sleeps through a storm that only He can stop.

Part one

There went out a sower

Verses 1–9

A boat for a pulpit, a hillside for a church, and a story every farmer knew.

A boat for a pulpit

The crowd was so big that Jesus pushed off in a boat and sat down to teach, the way teachers did then. The water carried His voice up to the people on the slope.

Tradition puts this at a cove near Capernaum, where the hillside curves around the water like a natural theater.

A parable is a story from everyday life that carries a spiritual truth. It's simple enough for anyone to remember, and it rewards the man who keeps thinking about it.

One seed, four kinds of ground

A farmer walked his field throwing seed by the handful. Some always landed where it couldn't grow. Watch what happens to each:

By the way side

packed hard, the birds take it (v. 4)

Stony ground

quick to sprout, no root, scorched (vv. 5–6)

Among thorns

it grows, but gets choked (v. 7)

Good ground

it grows and bears fruit (v. 8)

Same sower, same seed, every time. The only thing that changes is the ground. A thirtyfold harvest was a good year. A hundredfold was more than any farmer dared hope for.

Part two

He that hath ears to hear

Verses 10–25

The men who stayed and asked got the meaning. Jesus explains the seed, the soil, and the light.

Inside and outside

“about him”

stayed and asked

Not only the twelve. Anyone who came close and wanted to know got the meaning.

“them that are without”

heard a story and left

The same words, but no hunger to understand them.

A mystery in the Bible isn't a puzzle. It's something God had kept hidden and now makes known. Verse 12 quotes Isaiah 6:9–10, words about people who had already hardened their hearts against God. Parables open the truth to the hungry and hide it from the man who has made up his mind not to hear.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Fast up, fast down

The way side: the word bounces off a hard heart, and Satan cometh immediately to snatch it before it can sink in. He knows what the word can do.

Many of us have seen this man, or been him: fired up after a meeting, a sermon, a scare, and gone the first time trouble comes. Offended here means tripped up. Roots grow underground where nobody sees, in time in the Word, in prayer, with other believers. That's what holds a man when the sun gets hot.

What chokes the word

  1. “the cares of this world”worry: bills, court dates, the job, the family
  2. “the deceitfulness of riches”money promises it will fix things, and lies
  3. “the lusts of other things”any craving that wants first place: a drink, a drug, a woman, a screen

Thorns don't kill a plant in a day. They grow up slowly beside it, taking the light and the water, until there's nothing left for the good seed. Notice they come “entering in.” They have to be let in, and they're easiest to pull while they're small.

This man heard the word. It just never got room to bear fruit. Ask yourself what's growing up next to it in your life.

Good ground does three things: it hears, it receives, and it brings forth fruit. The amount differs, thirty, sixty, a hundred, and Jesus is pleased with all three. You don't have to be the hundredfold man. Just let the word in, and let it stay.

Nobody hides a light

The candle was a small clay oil lamp, the bushel a basket for measuring grain, and the candlestick a stand that held the lamp up high.

Who lights a lamp and then puts a basket over it? It would be pointless.

God doesn't light up a man's life so he can hide it. What He's done in you is meant to be seen, for “there is nothing hid, which shall not be manifested.”

The measure you use

“Mete” means measure out. Picture a man buying grain with his own basket. Bring a thimble, and you go home with a thimble. Bring a barrel, and it gets filled.

Recovery works the same way. The tools you use grow stronger. The ones you leave on the shelf slip away. Keep coming, keep listening, keep doing what you hear.

Part three

He knoweth not how

Verses 26–34

Two more seeds: one that grows while the farmer sleeps, and one so small you can barely see it.

Growing while he sleeps

The farmer's part is small: put the seed in the ground. Then he goes to bed, gets up, goes to bed again.

Down in the dark, where he can't see, the seed is already at work.

One day there's a field of grain, and “he knoweth not how.” God's word works like that in a man. You can't force it, and you can't always see it. But it's growing.

First the blade

  1. the bladea green shootnew faith
  2. the earthe head formsgrowing
  3. the full corngrain fills the headfruit
  4. the sickle“the harvest is come”v. 29

“Corn” in the King James means grain, like wheat or barley. Growth comes in order, one stage after another, and it takes a season. No farmer yells at a green blade for not being a full head of wheat yet.

Be patient with yourself, and with the new man next to you. If you've trusted Christ, God isn't finished with you:

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

A speck to a tree

The mustard seed was the smallest seed a Galilean farmer sowed.

Yet it can grow ten feet tall, with birds in its branches. That's the kingdom: twelve men in a boat, and now believers in every nation.

It works that way in a man too. One day sober. One honest prayer. “For who hath despised the day of small things?” (Zechariah 4:10).

As they were able

He teaches at the pace you can take.

Jesus didn't dump everything on the crowd at once. He gave them what they could carry. Then, alone with His disciples, He “expounded,” explained, all of it.

You don't need to understand the whole Bible all at once. Come close, keep asking, and He'll keep opening it up to you, one day at a time.

Part four

Peace, be still

Verses 35–41

Evening on the lake, a storm out of nowhere, and the Teacher sound asleep.

Crossing at evening

After a whole day of teaching: “Let us pass over unto the other side.” They took Him “even as he was,” worn out, in the same boat.

On the east shore, in Gentile country, a man full of devils was waiting (5:1–20). Jesus crossed the lake for him.

These fishermen knew this water, and Jesus was the one who said go. Obeying Him doesn't keep the storm away.

Asleep in the storm

The lake sits about 700 feet below sea level, ringed by hills. Wind pours down the valleys and can whip it up in minutes.

Jesus was asleep in the hinder part, the stern, on a cushion: tired as any man, and at perfect peace with His Father.

“Carest thou not?” Every man has prayed that one at 3 a.m. But Jesus was in the same boat, in the same storm. He never left it.

Three words

He rebuked the wind the way He rebuked unclean spirits (1:25). “Be still” is literally “be muzzled.”

Storms die down slowly. This one stopped at once, and even the waves lay flat. The same voice can speak to the storm in you: the craving, the panic, the rage.

What manner of man is this?

A real man

tired, asleep on a cushion

He got worn out like any of us.

God with us

the wind and the sea obey Him

Only the One who made the sea can tell it to sit down.

“He maketh the storm a calm, so that the waves thereof are still.”

Psalm 107:29

“Why are ye so fearful?” They were afraid of the storm. After the calm they “feared exceedingly,” but now it was the right kind of fear: awe at who was in their boat. When you know who He is, the storm gets smaller.

Points to remember

  1. Hearing“The sower soweth the word” 4:14The seed is always good. What changes is the ground. Let the word in, and let it stay.
  2. Thorns“Choke the word, and it becometh unfruitful” 4:19Worry, money, and cravings crowd out the word. Pull them while they're small.
  3. Light“To be set on a candlestick” 4:21What God has done in you is meant to be seen, not hidden.
  4. Growth“He knoweth not how” 4:27God makes the seed grow, in its season. Be patient with yourself and others.
  5. Small beginnings“Less than all the seeds that be in the earth” 4:31God starts big things small. Don't despise one day, one prayer, one step.
  6. Storms“Peace, be still” 4:39He is in the boat with you, and the wind and the sea obey Him.

Question 1 of 6

Hearing

What happens to the word in four kinds of hearts?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On a hard heart, Satan snatches it away. On a shallow heart, it springs up and dies when trouble comes. In a crowded heart, worry, money and cravings choke it. In good ground, it is heard, received, and bears fruit.

Question 2 of 6

Thorns

What chokes the word in a man's life?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The cares of this world, the deceitfulness of riches, and the lusts of other things. They grow up slowly beside the word until there's no room for fruit. They come in when we let them, and they're easiest to pull up early.

Question 3 of 6

Light

What is a candle for?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.”

Matthew 5:16

Answer

To be set up where it gives light. Nobody lights a lamp to hide it. What God has done in your life is meant to be seen, so that others see it and give glory to God.

Question 4 of 6

Growth

Who makes the seed grow?

What verses tell us about this?

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Answer

God does. The farmer sows and sleeps, and the seed grows, he knows not how. Growth comes in stages, the blade, the ear, the full corn. God finishes what He starts in a believer.

Question 5 of 6

Small beginnings

What does the mustard seed teach us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God's kingdom starts small and grows big. Twelve men became believers in every nation. In a man's life, too, God can take a tiny start and grow it into something others find shelter in.

Question 6 of 6

Storms

Does Jesus care when we feel like we're going under?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Yes. He was in the same boat, in the same storm. One word from Him and there was a great calm. He doesn't promise we'll never face storms, but He never leaves the boat, and the wind and the sea obey Him.
