Chapter 5 gave the message: “be ye reconciled to God.” Chapter 6 shows how Paul lived it out, and what a reconciled man does about his company.

Paul wrote this letter from Macedonia in about AD 56, a year after 1 Corinthians. In between came a sharp letter, a painful visit, and a long wait for news. Titus brought good news at last (7:6–7), and this letter is Paul pouring out his heart.

Part one

Now is the day

Verses 1–10

An urgent appeal, and a man who has paid for it with everything.

Behold, now

Paul quotes an old promise God made to His Servant (Isaiah 49:8): “I have heard thee in a time accepted.” Then he points at the clock. That time is here. It is now.

Accepted means welcome: a time when the door is open and God is glad to see you. Not a day He puts up with you, but a day He has been waiting for.

“Receive not the grace of God in vain” means don't take it and leave it empty. Grace you've been given is meant to be lived in, starting today. Not after you get cleaned up first, and not when the weekend is over.

Yesterday is gone, however bad it was. Tomorrow isn't yours yet. If there's a hard call to make, a step to take, a man to ask for help: today.

Three kinds: what the world did to him, what the job cost him, and what he went without. Some of you know a few of these from the inside. Prison, the street, a long hungry stretch.

Notice the first line: “in much patience.” He did not hold up by being tough. And verse 3 is why: “giving no offence in any thing, that the ministry be not blamed.” He wanted his life to match his message, so nobody could say it was a lie.

Having nothing, and yet possessing all things.

Look at how Paul describes himself: sneered at, broke, beaten, and he calls himself rich. How? Because the things that matter most, a clean conscience, God as his Father, a place in heaven, nobody could take.

“He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?”

Romans 8:32

Many of us have lost the house, the truck, the family, the years. That's real, and it hurts. But a man who has Christ is not poor. Today, name one thing nobody can take from you.

Part two

Yoked, and free

Verses 11–18

A father opens his heart, then gives his sons some hard advice about the company they keep.

An open heart

Paul's heart: enlarged

Wide open

“Our mouth is open unto you.” Nothing held back, room for every one of them. He is talking to them like a father to his children.

Their hearts: straitened

Cramped

“Straitened” means squeezed into a tight space. The Corinthians were holding back, keeping him at arm's length.

Paul isn't asking for money or praise. He asks them to open up. “Bowels” is the old word for the deep feelings in the gut. Men like us learned to close up to survive. In God's family, you can open up again.

Today, let one brother in on how you're really doing. Don't just say fine.

One yoke, two directions

A yoke is the wooden beam that locks two animals together so they pull one plow. Moses' law said not to yoke an ox with a donkey (Deuteronomy 22:10): different strength, different pace, different will.

Put a believer and an unbeliever in the same yoke and one wants to go one way, the other the opposite. The furrow goes crooked. Or round and round.

“Can two walk together, except they be agreed?”

Amos 3:3

Five things that don't mix

  1. righteousness✕unrighteousnessfellowship?
  2. light✕darknesscommunion?
  3. Christ✕Belialconcord?
  4. he that believeth✕an infidela share?
  5. the temple of God✕idolsagreement?

Belial is a Hebrew word for worthlessness and lawlessness, a name Jews used for the evil one. An infidel here is simply an unbeliever. And look at the last pair. “Ye are the temple of the living God.” You are God's house, and what you partner with is what you're letting into the house.

Where the yoke reaches

  1. Marriagethe biggest yoke of all. For a believer: “only in the Lord” (1 Corinthians 7:39)
  2. Datinga close tie that pulls you back toward the old life is a bad road, however much you like her
  3. Business partnersyour money and your name tied to a man who isn't straight
  4. The old crowdthe man you used with is not a casual friend. He's a yoke

A yoke is a deep tie where you have to pull together. It isn't your neighbor, your coworker, or the man you're trying to reach for Christ.

“Yet not altogether with the fornicators of this world, or with the covetous, or extortioners, or with idolaters; for then must ye needs go out of the world.”

1 Corinthians 5:10

So Paul isn't telling you to stop talking to unbelievers. He tells you not to hitch your life to them. Be the friend, the good worker, the one who shows up. Just don't let them hold the lines.

Already married to an unbeliever?

If that's you, this verse is not a reason to leave. Paul wrote the Corinthians about this exact case:

“But to the rest speak I, not the Lord: If any brother hath a wife that believeth not, and she be pleased to dwell with him, let him not put her away.”

1 Corinthians 7:12

“For what knowest thou, O wife, whether thou shalt save thy husband? or how knowest thou, O man, whether thou shalt save thy wife?”

1 Corinthians 7:16

Stay. Be the light in that house: patient, honest, sober, kind. Pray for her by name. No guilt about the marriage you're in.

And if you've already partnered with the wrong man in business or friendship? Don't make a mess leaving, but pray, and take the first honest step toward getting loose.

Come out, and be received

You doCome out. Be separate. Let go of the unclean thing.
→
God does“I will receive you,” and be a Father to you.

Separate doesn't mean hide in a cabin. It means a clean break from what is dragging you down: the people, the places, the habit. Every man in recovery knows that one.

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

That's the promise: “I will receive you.” A Father who runs to meet you. The first step is yours, but He is already coming. Read what follows in the next verse: “having therefore these promises.” The promises come first, and the clean-up follows from them.

  2. Hard times“In much patience, in afflictions, in necessities, in distresses” 6:4Paul held up with God's Spirit, word, and power, not his own grit.
  3. Nothing, yet all“As having nothing, and yet possessing all things” 6:10A man in Christ has what nobody can take.
  4. Yoked“Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers” 6:14Don't hitch your life to someone pulling the other way.
  5. Come out“Come out from among them, and be ye separate” 6:17Make a clean break from what pulls you down.

Question 1 of 6

Today

What does “now is the accepted time” mean for a man today?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Answer

That God's door is open today, and He is glad to receive whosoever calls. Whether it's the first time or the hundredth time he's come back, today is the day to take the step. Yesterday is behind him, and tomorrow isn't promised.

Question 2 of 6

Hard times

How did Paul hold up under beatings, prison, and hunger?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ's sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong.”

2 Corinthians 12:10

Answer

Not by being tough. He leaned on the Holy Spirit, the word of truth, and the power of God, and kept his life clean so the message wouldn't be blamed. When he was weak, God was strong.

Question 3 of 6

Nothing, yet all

Can a man have nothing and still possess everything?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that spared not his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?”

Romans 8:32

Answer

Yes, if he has Christ. A man can be broke and still own what matters most: forgiveness, God as his Father, and a place in heaven. Nobody can repossess those.

Question 4 of 6

Yoked

What does it mean to be unequally yoked?

What verses tell us about this?

“Can two walk together, except they be agreed?”

Amos 3:3

Answer

It means hitching your life, in marriage, partnerships, or the closest friendships, to someone pulling the opposite way. Two animals in one yoke can't go in two directions. A believer should choose close partners who walk with the Lord.

Question 5 of 6

Come out

What is a man supposed to come out of?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed.”

Proverbs 13:20

Answer

Out of the partnerships and company that pull him back toward the old life. Not out of the world, but out of the yoke. The wise path is to walk with people who are going where God is going.

Question 6 of 6

A Father

What does God promise the man who comes out?

What verses tell us about this?

“But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his name:”

John 1:12

Answer

That He will receive him, and be a Father to him. He doesn't take a man on probation. He runs to meet him, like the father in Jesus' story, and makes him His own son.
