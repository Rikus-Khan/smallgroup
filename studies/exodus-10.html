Exodus 10

Locusts, and a darkness that could be felt

The LORD asks Pharaoh, “How long wilt thou refuse to humble thyself before me?” The locusts strip Egypt bare, Pharaoh says “I have sinned,” and then goes right back. Three days of darkness fall, and Israel has light.

Part one

“How long wilt thou refuse to humble thyself?”

Verses 1–11

Seven plagues are behind him. God asks the question again, and Pharaoh starts bargaining.

Who hardened Pharaoh's heart?

This is the verse people trip on. The text gives the story in order, so let's read it in order.

  1. 4:21God says it will happen“I will harden his heart”
  2. 8:15Pharaoh hardens itwhen the frogs stopped
  3. 8:32Pharaoh hardens itwhen the flies stopped
  4. 9:12the LORD hardens itthe sixth plague
  5. 9:34Pharaoh again“he sinned yet more”
  6. 10:1, 20, 27the LORD confirms itnow he is set

Before the sixth plague, it is Pharaoh who hardens his heart: when the frogs stop (8:15), when the flies stop (8:32). The text says again and again that his heart “was hardened,” and twice that he hardened it himself. The words “the LORD hardened” first come at the sixth plague (9:12).

What this does, and does not, say

God saw where Pharaoh was headed, and said so ahead of time. When Pharaoh went on saying no, God let him be what he had chosen to be. The apostle Paul reads it that way:

“Therefore hath he mercy on whom he will have mercy, and whom he will he hardeneth.”

Romans 9:18

It does not say God shuts a man out who wants to turn. Look at verse 3: God asks him, “How long wilt thou refuse to humble thyself?” A God who asks is a God who would take a yes. The door was open the whole time. Pharaoh is the warning, not the rule.

“But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin.”

Hebrews 3:13

That is how a heart hardens: not in one big decision, but one “not yet” at a time. Notice the cure is daily, and it is done together: “exhort one another.” That is what this group is for.

A story for your son's son

Verse 2 says why God does these signs: “that thou mayest tell in the ears of thy son, and of thy son’s son, what things I have wrought in Egypt.”

  • wroughtdone, worked (an old past tense of “work”)
  • signswonders that point to who God is
  • thy son's sonyour grandchildren, and theirs

Some of you are fathers, or wish you had been better ones. Notice what God wants handed down: not a rulebook, a story. “This is what the LORD did for us.” The stories of how God got you out are worth telling your kids. In chapter 12 we will see Israel do exactly that.

“How long?”

  • humble thyselfbring yourself down, stop standing on your own pride
  • to morrowtomorrow (two words in the old spelling)
  • coasthere: territory, the whole country, not just the seashore

God has been after Pharaoh's pride from the start: “Who is the LORD, that I should obey his voice to let Israel go?” was his answer in chapter 5. Now the question is not “who is God?” but “how long?” Pharaoh knows by now. The only thing left is whether he will bend.

Pride is a hard thing for men who have lost a lot. It says: I can handle this. I don't need anybody. God's question to you is the same one: how long?

Residue means the rest, what was left. The hail (chapter 9) had killed what stood in the field, but the wheat and the rye “were not grown up,” so they were spared (9:32). The locusts come for what the hail left. Whatever Egypt had saved, Egypt is about to lose.

Verse 6 ends with a small line: “he turned himself, and went out from Pharaoh.” Moses says what he has to say and walks out. He does not argue or beg. He delivers it, and leaves it with God.

His own servants can see it

Pharaoh's servantsHow long shall this man be a snare unto us? let the men go, that they may serve the LORD their God: knowest thou not yet that Egypt is destroyed?

PharaohGo, serve the LORD your God: but who are they that shall go? 10:8

A snare is a trap. The men around Pharaoh have run out of patience. The fields are bare, the animals are dead, and he is the only one who cannot see it. A proud man is the last to know.

Real world: the people closest to you often see where this is going before you do. When a wife, a boss or a brother in Christ says, “do you not see it yet?” take it as a gift. Pharaoh's servants were right, and he overruled them.

“Go now, ye that are men”

Moses says everyone goes: young and old, sons and daughters, flocks and herds. Pharaoh answers: only the men.

  • “We will go with our young and with our old”Moses: the whole family, because the whole family is to worship
  • “Let the LORD be so with you, as I will let you go, and your little ones”Pharaoh, sneering: “may the LORD be with you the day I ever let your children go”
  • “Go now ye that are men”keep the children, and the men have to come back

Think about what Pharaoh wants. A man will come back for his children. Take the children, and you hold the man. It was a clever offer, and a trap.

Part two

“There remained not any green thing”

Verses 12–20

The east wind blows all night, and in the morning the locusts have come.

A plague you could not fight

The text keeps the plague plain. The LORD sent a wind, the wind carried the locusts, and they covered the ground until it was dark with them. They are a real insect, and they ate everything, down to the bark. Grievous means heavy and hard to bear.

Nothing a man could do would stop it. No army, no wall, no wealth. For a people who thought their land safe, this was the moment they could count on nothing they had stored up.

Some of you have seen a year, or ten, eaten down to nothing, and the green things gone: job, marriage, money, trust. That is the next slide.

“I will restore the years”

Centuries later the prophet Joel wrote about another swarm of locusts that stripped a land, and God made a promise to the people who had suffered it:

“And I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten, the cankerworm, and the caterpiller, and the palmerworm, my great army which I sent among you.”

Joel 2:25

God does not say the years did not happen. He says I will restore them. That is regret handled the way God handles it: not by pretending the loss was nothing, and not by letting it be the last word.

Real world: you cannot get back the time. But you can hand the empty fields to God and begin planting. He is the One who gives the harvest.

“I have sinned”: but what did he want?

Pharaoh

Sorry that it hurts

“Forgive my sin only this once, and take away from me this death only.” He wants the locusts gone. He says nothing about changing.

A man who turns

Sorry that it's wrong

He asks to be forgiven, and then does something different. The sorrow reaches past the pain to the thing he did.

Intreat means to plead with someone. Pharaoh said the right words. He had said “I have sinned” before (9:27). Words said under pressure, to make it stop, are not the same as a changed heart. Every man here knows both kinds: the “never again” said in a hospital bed, and the one that held.

When the pain stops, so does the promise

Moses prayed, and the LORD turned the wind. It carried every last locust into the Red sea, and “there remained not one locust in all the coasts of Egypt.” God answered the prayer completely. And Pharaoh's answer was no.

This is the pattern in the chapters before: the plague, the “I have sinned,” the relief, and the hardening (8:15, 8:32, 9:34). Relief is not repentance. A man can be sorry in the storm, and forget it all in the calm.

“He, that being often reproved hardeneth his neck, shall suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy.”

Proverbs 29:1

Real world: when the trouble is over, that is the day to act on what you promised in the middle of it. Call your sponsor or your pastor the morning after, not the night of. Say it again to your brothers in Christ: “I meant what I said.”

Part three

“Darkness which may be felt”

Verses 21–29

This time there is no warning. The darkness just comes, for three days.

Darkness for Egypt, light for Israel

The Egyptians

A thick darkness

“Darkness which may be felt.” For three days they could not see each other, and no one stood up from his place.

The children of Israel

Light in their dwellings

In the same land, under the same sky, “all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings.”

Egypt worshipped the sun. Here the LORD turns it off, over Egypt only. He is not one god among others. The One who made the light decides where it shines.

Three days alone in the dark

Look at what the darkness did: “They saw not one another, neither rose any from his place.” Nobody could see anybody, so nobody moved. The darkness cut every man off.

That is how it goes for a man in his sin. He sits alone, where nobody can see him, and he does not get up. Shame stays in the dark. You know that room.

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

The cure is the opposite of the darkness: light, and “fellowship one with another.” Today, tell one brother in Christ the thing you have been keeping in the dark. The darkness only keeps its grip while nobody sees.

Out of the darkness

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

“Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son:”

Colossians 1:13

Translated here means moved, carried across. A people who sat in a felt darkness had light in their houses because they belonged to the LORD. A believer has been moved out of the dark by Jesus himself, not because he was better. He was brought out. You are not still stuck in there.

Pharaoh's four deals

  • 1Go ye, sacrifice to your God in the land.Worship, but stay right here in Egypt.8:25
  • 2only ye shall not go very far awayLeave, but not far. Stay in reach.8:28
  • 3Go now ye that are menGo, but the children stay.10:11
  • 4only let your flocks and your herds be stayedGo, but leave your wealth behind with me.10:24

Stayed means kept back. Every one of Pharaoh's offers sounds like a yes, and every one keeps a hand on Israel. A man who half leaves is still half in.

“There shall not an hoof be left behind”

Moses will not take the deal. He gives the reason: the cattle are needed for the sacrifices, “and we know not with what we must serve the LORD, until we come thither.” They did not yet know exactly what God would ask. So they would not leave anything behind that God might ask for.

Moses said no to the compromise. Everything went with them, because everything was the LORD's.

Real world: the deals still get offered. Keep the number in the phone, just in case. Stay in touch with the old crowd, but only the nice ones. Go to the place, but just for the food. Each one sounds reasonable, and each leaves a hand on you. You do not have to win this by willpower. Ask a brother in Christ to look at your phone and your week with you. Take everything with you.

Grace: this is a God who wants the whole man, not because he is greedy, but because he is the only one who can keep him.

The last meeting

Pharaoh runs out of bargains and threatens: “see my face no more; for in that day thou seest my face thou shalt die.” That is a king who has chosen to shut his own door. The LORD has now confirmed him in it (verse 27).

Moses answers: “Thou hast spoken well.” Many readers hear the opening of the next chapter (11:4–8) as Moses' parting words, spoken as he walks out of that same room. Pharaoh has sent away the one man who could have told him the way. Nine times the door was open.

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Longsuffering means patient, slow to anger. God waited through nine plagues. For you he is still waiting, and this is the day to turn.

Real world: what are you holding back?

  • The “not yet”One thing you keep putting off telling someone, or God
  • The dealThe “almost” you have been bargaining with: leave, but not all the way
  • The morning afterAct on what you said last night, before the relief makes you forget it
  • The dark roomTell one brother in Christ what you have been hiding

The first nine plagues did not move Pharaoh. The tenth will. Next chapters: the night God spares every house with blood on the door.

Points to remember

  1. A hard heart“But the LORD hardened Pharaoh’s heart” 10:20Pharaoh hardened his own heart again and again first. A heart hardens one “not yet” at a time.
  2. Pride“How long wilt thou refuse to humble thyself before me?” 10:3God asks, and the door is open. Humbling yourself is the way through.
  3. Bargaining“only let your flocks and your herds be stayed” 10:24Pharaoh's deals all sounded like yes and kept a hand on Israel. A half-leave is still half in.
  4. Sorry for the wrong reason“I have sinned against the LORD your God” 10:16Sorry the pain hurts is not sorry it's wrong. Act on it the morning after.
  5. Light“all the children of Israel had light in their dwellings” 10:23The LORD decides where the light shines, and Jesus brings us out of the dark. Don't stay in it alone.
  6. No half-measures“there shall not an hoof be left behind” 10:26Moses took everything with him, because it all belonged to the LORD.

Question 1 of 6

A hard heart

Who hardened Pharaoh's heart?

What verses tell us about this?

“But when Pharaoh saw that there was respite, he hardened his heart, and hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had said.”

Exodus 8:15

Answer

Both, in order: Pharaoh hardened his own heart again and again, then the LORD confirmed him in his road. God still asked, “How long?” Tell your brothers daily, so you do not harden a “not yet” at a time.

Question 2 of 6

Pride

What did the LORD ask Pharaoh to do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

To humble himself, and let the people go. God asked him “how long,” which means the answer could still be yes. Pride is the last to see the ruin, even when the people around us can. Listen to them, and humble yourself before God today.

Question 3 of 6

Bargaining

What deals did Pharaoh offer instead of letting Israel go?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Worship here, go not far, only the men, leave the flocks. Each sounded like a yes and kept a hand on Israel. A man who half leaves is still half in. Take your “almost” to a brother in Christ.

Question 4 of 6

Sorry for the wrong reason

Why did Pharaoh's “I have sinned” change nothing?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

He wanted the locusts gone, “only this once,” and went right back. Sorry that it hurts is not sorry that it's wrong. Real confession changes a life.

Question 5 of 6

Light

What was different about Israel's houses in the darkness?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

Answer

They had light, while Egypt sat in a darkness that could be felt. Jesus is the light of the world. Don't sit in the dark alone: tell a brother in Christ.

Question 6 of 6

No half-measures

What would Moses not leave behind in Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not a single hoof. Everything went with them, since everything was the LORD's, and they did not yet know what he would ask for. Don't keep a foot in Egypt. If you need a brother in Christ to help you cut a tie, ask him today.
