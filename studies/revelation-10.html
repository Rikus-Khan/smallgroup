Revelation 10

Sweet in the mouth, bitter in the belly

Between the sixth trumpet and the seventh, heaven stops to promise that God will not delay, and hands John a little book to eat. It is sweet, and it is hard to carry. So is the word God gives us.

Revelation does this more than once. Between the sixth seal and the seventh there was a pause (chapter 7), when God sealed His servants. Now, between the sixth trumpet and the seventh, there is another. The storm stops, and God speaks to His own.

After all the darkness of chapter 9, the pause is a mercy. Before the last trumpet, God makes sure His people know two things: He is not late, and they still have work to do.

Part one

The mighty angel

Verses 1–4

A messenger big enough to stand on land and sea, with a promise over his head, and a voice like a lion.

One foot on the sea, one on the land

Everything about him is big: clothed in cloud, face like the sun, legs like pillars of fire. Standing on sea and land says this message is for the whole world. Nothing is outside it.

Some teachers say

It is Christ

The face like the sun and feet of fire match Jesus in 1:15–16.

Most say

A great angel

John calls him “another” angel, like the strong angel of 5:2, and he swears by God, as a servant would.

A promise worn on his head

Genesis 9:13

I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth.

Revelation 4:3

… and there was a rainbow round about the throne, in sight like unto an emerald.

After the flood God hung His bow in the cloud as a promise. Then John saw a rainbow around God's own throne. Now, in the middle of the trumpets, the messenger comes wrapped in a cloud with a rainbow on his head.

The message: even in judgment, God keeps His word. He has not forgotten His promises to you, even on a day when everything around you is shaking.

Some things are not ours to know

John had his pen ready, and heaven said no. Nobody knows what the thunders said; anyone who claims to is guessing.

“The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law.”

Deuteronomy 29:29

God hasn't told us everything: why that happened to you, or when He will come. He has told us more than enough to live on today.

When the lion roars

“The lion hath roared, who will not fear? the Lord GOD hath spoken, who can but prophesy?”

Amos 3:8

In the Bible a lion's roar is the sound of God speaking, and it gets a man's attention. Amos says that when God has spoken, His servant can't keep quiet. Keep that verse in mind: this chapter ends with John being told to prophesy again.

And remember who the Lion is. In chapter 5, the only one worthy to open God's book was “the Lion of the tribe of Juda,” who turned out to be a Lamb that had been slain (5:5–6).

Part two

No more delay

Verses 5–7

An oath sworn by the Maker of everything: the waiting is almost over.

Daniel heard the first half of this oath

Daniel 12:7

And I heard the man clothed in linen, which was upon the waters of the river, when he held up his right hand and his left hand unto heaven, and sware by him that liveth for ever that it shall be for a time, times, and an half; and when he shall have accomplished to scatter the power of the holy people, all these things shall be finished.

About six hundred years earlier, Daniel heard an oath that the end would come after a set time. Now the angel swears: “time no longer.” The clocks don't stop; it means no more delay. What Daniel waited for is about to happen.

The mystery of God, finished

A mystery in the Bible is God's plan, once hidden and now told to “his servants the prophets.” At the seventh trumpet, heaven shouts where it ends:

“And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.”

Revelation 11:15

God finishes what He starts, in the world and in you (Philippians 1:6).

Part three

Sweet and bitter

Verses 8–11

John has to take the book himself, eat it, and then go tell it.

Sweet, then bitter

Nobody hands John the book. He has to go and ask for it, and then take it all in: “eat it up.” God's word isn't only for looking at. It's for taking inside.

Sweet, because it is God speaking: the Lamb wins, evil is judged, His people are kept. Bitter, because the same book tells of judgment on men who will not turn, and John will have to carry that and say it out loud.

Ezekiel ate a scroll too

Ezekiel 3:3

And he said unto me, Son of man, cause thy belly to eat, and fill thy bowels with this roll that I give thee. Then did I eat it; and it was in my mouth as honey for sweetness.

Ezekiel 2:10

And he spread it before me; and it was written within and without: and there was written therein lamentations, and mourning, and woe.

Ezekiel's scroll was full of “lamentations, and mourning, and woe,” and it still tasted like honey. That is the secret of this picture. Even a hard word from God is good, because it is His, and it is true. David said the same:

“How sweet are thy words unto my taste! yea, sweeter than honey to my mouth!”

Psalm 119:103

The same Book, both ways

Sweet

When it lands on me as grace

“Thy sins are forgiven.” “I will never leave thee.” My name is written down. The first weeks with a Bible can taste like that.

Bitter

When it asks something hard

Make it right with the man I robbed. Let go of the thing. Tell the truth in court. Watch someone I love keep refusing God.

If the Bible has gotten hard to swallow lately, that doesn't mean something is wrong with you. John and Ezekiel felt it too. Keep eating. Today: read one chapter slowly, and do the one hard thing it asks.

“Thou must prophesy again”

John is an old man, a prisoner on a rocky island, about AD 95. Most of his friends are dead. You might think his work was done. God says it isn't: “before many peoples, and nations, and tongues, and kings.” From that little island, this book went out to the whole world, and it is still going.

Maybe you think your past disqualified you, or that you're too far gone or too late. God isn't finished with you either. Tell one person today what He has done for you. That's prophesying, in plain words.

Points to remember

  1. God's promise“A rainbow was upon his head.” 10:1Even in judgment, God keeps His word, to the world and to you.
  2. Secrets“Seal up those things which the seven thunders uttered, and write them not.” 10:4Some things belong to God alone. He has told us enough to live on today.
  3. No more delay“That there should be time no longer.” 10:6The Maker of everything swore it: He will not be late.
  4. Finished“The mystery of God should be finished.” 10:7God's plan ends with Christ reigning. He finishes what He starts, in you too.
  5. Sweet and bitter“It was in my mouth sweet as honey: and as soon as I had eaten it, my belly was bitter.” 10:10God's word is sweet, and hard to carry. Take it all in anyway.
  6. Again“Thou must prophesy again.” 10:11God wasn't done with an old prisoner, and He isn't done with you.

Question 1 of 6

God's promise

What does the rainbow on the angel's head remind us of?

What verses tell us about this?

“I do set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be for a token of a covenant between me and the earth.”

Genesis 9:13

Answer

God's promise to Noah, and the rainbow around His throne in chapter 4. Even in the middle of judgment, God remembers His covenant and keeps His word.

Question 2 of 6

Secrets

Why was John told not to write what the seven thunders said?

What verses tell us about this?

“The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law.”

Deuteronomy 29:29

Answer

God didn't say why, and that is the point: some things belong to Him alone. What He has revealed is ours to believe and do, and it is more than enough for today.

Question 3 of 6

No more delay

What did the angel swear?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

By the living God, the Maker of heaven, earth and sea, he swore that there would be no more delay. The end Daniel was promised is coming, right on God's time.

Question 4 of 6

Finished

What will be finished when the seventh angel sounds?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.”

Revelation 11:15

Answer

The mystery of God: His plan, told to the prophets, that ends with the kingdoms of this world becoming Christ's. God finishes what He starts.

Question 5 of 6

Sweet and bitter

Why was the little book sweet in the mouth but bitter in the belly?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto me, Son of man, cause thy belly to eat, and fill thy bowels with this roll that I give thee. Then did I eat it; and it was in my mouth as honey for sweetness.”

Ezekiel 3:3

Answer

God's word is sweet because it is His, and it tells us the Lamb wins. It is bitter because it also tells of judgment, and it asks hard things of the man who carries it. We take in both.

Question 6 of 6

Again
