In chapter 4 John, an old man exiled on Patmos, went up through an open door into heaven and saw the throne. Everything there sang to God: “Thou art worthy, O Lord… for thou hast created all things” (4:11).

Now the same room, the same throne. But the song is about to change: worthy, because thou wast slain.

Part one

The sealed book

Verses 1–4

God holds a book sealed seven times. Heaven asks who can open it, and nobody answers.

A book sealed seven times

A book then was a scroll. This one is so full it's written on both sides, and it's tied shut with seven seals. In the Bible, seven is the number of completeness: this book is completely shut.

Many teachers point out that a Roman will was sealed by seven witnesses, and only the rightful heir could open it. Most see in it God's plan to judge the world and set it right. It's in God's own hand, and chapter 6 shows what happens as it opens.

Who is worthy?

“In heaven”?No manNot one of the angels, not one of the saints already home.
“In earth”?No manNot a king, not a prophet, not the best man you ever knew.
“Under the earth”?No manNot the great men of the past in their graves.

Nobody could even look at it. Being worthy isn't about trying hard. It means having the right, the clean record, the standing before God. Every man who ever lived comes up short.

“This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief.”

1 Timothy 1:15

A grown man crying

John is an old apostle, in exile, who has seen it all. And he cries hard. If nobody can open the book, nobody can put the world right: the wrong stays wrong for ever.

Maybe you know those tears. You look at what you've done, at what was done to you, at the mess, and nobody you know can fix it. You can't either. John wasn't wrong to weep. He was honest.

But he isn't left there. Watch what heaven does with a man's tears:

“And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.”

Revelation 21:4

Part two

The Lion is a Lamb

Verses 5–7

“Weep not.” Somebody has won. John turns to look at Him, and sees the last thing he expected.

The Lion and the Root

Genesis 49:9

Judah is a lion’s whelp: from the prey, my son, thou art gone up: he stooped down, he couched as a lion, and as an old lion; who shall rouse him up?

Isaiah 11:10

And in that day there shall be a root of Jesse, which shall stand for an ensign of the people; to it shall the Gentiles seek: and his rest shall be glorious.

Jacob, dying, called his son Judah a lion, and said “the sceptre shall not depart from Judah” (Genesis 49:10). King David came from Judah, and Jesse was David's father. The elder is saying: here is the King God promised, from the tribe of kings, David's own Son and David's root.

Prevailed is the Greek word nikao: to conquer, to overcome. It's the same word as “to him that overcometh” in the letters to the seven churches. He has overcome, so His people can.

He heard “Lion.” He saw a Lamb.

John is told to look at a conquering Lion. He turns, and there in the middle of the throne stands a Lamb, still marked “as it had been slain.”

That's how He won. Not by tearing His enemies apart, but by being torn for them. The Lion conquered by dying as a Lamb.

And notice: slain, yet standing. He died, and He is alive. He still carries the wounds, even on the throne (John 20:27). That is the center of this whole book.

Seven horns, seven eyes

Horns

All the power

In the Bible a horn is a picture of strength, like a bull's. Seven horns: complete power. This Lamb isn't weak.

Eyes

Sees everything

The prophet Zechariah saw seven lamps: “they are the eyes of the LORD, which run to and fro through the whole earth” (Zechariah 4:10). Seven eyes: He misses nothing. Many teachers read “the seven Spirits” as the Holy Spirit in His fullness.

Then the Lamb walks up and takes the book out of God's right hand (5:7). Nobody hands it to Him reluctantly. It's His by right. The Man who died for sinners now holds the future of the world.

The Lamb through the whole Bible

  1. Genesis 22:8“God will provide himself a lamb”Abraham on the mountain
  2. Exodus 12:13“when I see the blood, I will pass over you”Passover in Egypt
  3. Isaiah 53:7“he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter”the suffering servant
  4. John 1:29“Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world”John the Baptist
  5. Revelation 5:6“a Lamb as it had been slain”on the throne

From the first book to the last, God was telling one story: a lamb dies, and the guilty go free. In Revelation He is called the Lamb nearly thirty times. He's the hero of the whole book.

Part three

A new song

Verses 8–10

Heaven falls on its face, and sings about what the Lamb bought with His blood.

Bowls of prayer

Vials are wide bowls, and odours is incense. In the temple, incense burned every morning and evening as the prayers went up. David prayed:

“Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense; and the lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice.”

Psalm 141:2

The prayer you prayed on a bunk at 2 a.m., the one you weren't sure got past the ceiling: it's kept in gold, before the throne. Not one is lost. Saints means every believer, not just the famous ones.

Kings and priests

What the world called you

A number

An inmate number. A case file. A drunk. A disappointment. A man nobody would trust with a key.

What the Lamb made you

Kings and priests

A priest goes straight in to God, with nobody in between. A king shares in the reign of the King. That's not a someday promise only: “hast made us.”

“But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light:”

1 Peter 2:9

Part four

Worthy is the Lamb

Verses 11–14

The song spreads out ring after ring, until every creature everywhere is singing it.

Ring after ring

Closest to the throne, the four beasts and the twenty-four elders sing first (5:8–10).

Then the angels: “ten thousand times ten thousand.” That's a hundred million, and John adds “thousands of thousands”: more than he could count. Daniel saw the same crowd (Daniel 7:10).

Then every creature, everywhere (5:13). The praise spreads from the Lamb until it fills everything God made.

Seven things He deserves

  1. powerthe right to rule
  2. richeseverything of worth
  3. wisdomthe last word on how to live
  4. strengththe might to do it
  5. honourthe respect of all
  6. glorythe shining credit
  7. blessingevery good word

Look at that list: power, money, respect. It's what men lie, steal and fight for. The Lamb got all seven by being slain. The way up in God's kingdom runs through the cross.

Every knee

The same praise goes to the One on the throne and to the Lamb. Heaven never worships a creature. It worships Jesus, because He is God.

“That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth; And that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.”

Philippians 2:10–11

One day every man will bow, some gladly and some too late. You can bow today, by choice. And the four beasts say the last word: “Amen.” So be it.

Take it to work today

5:5Weep not

Bring God the thing you can't fix. You're not worthy, and you don't have to be. The Lamb is.

5:9You were bought

When the old name comes back at you, answer with the new one: redeemed by His blood.

5:8Pray anyway

Say one honest prayer at work or on the road. It goes up like incense, and it's kept.

Points to remember

  1. Worthy“No man was found worthy” 5:4No one in heaven, earth, or under the earth had the standing to open God's book.
  2. Tears“Weep not” 5:5Stop crying: the Lion of Juda has overcome, and He can open it.
  3. The Lamb“A Lamb as it had been slain” 5:6The Lion conquered by dying as a Lamb, and He stands alive, still bearing the wounds.
  4. Redeemed“Hast redeemed us to God by thy blood” 5:9Bought back by His blood, out of every kindred, tongue, people, and nation.
  5. Prayer“Golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints” 5:8Believers' prayers rise like incense and are kept before the throne.
  6. Worship“Worthy is the Lamb that was slain” 5:12Elders, angels without number, and every creature give Him the glory.

Question 1 of 6

Worthy

Why was no man found worthy to open the book?

What verses tell us about this?

“For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God;”

Romans 3:23

Answer

Because to open it you need the right and a clean record before God, and every man has sinned. No angel, no saint, no king, no dead hero was worthy. Only the Lamb, who was slain for sinners, has earned that right.

Question 2 of 6

Tears

What did the elder tell John when he wept?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Weep not.” The Lion of the tribe of Juda, the Root of David, has prevailed, has overcome, and can open the book. John's tears were honest, but they weren't the end: someone had already won.

Question 3 of 6

The Lamb

Why is the Lion of Juda seen as a Lamb that was slain?

What verses tell us about this?

“The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.”

John 1:29

Answer

Because that's how He won. Jesus is the promised King from Judah, but He conquered by dying as the Lamb of God for our sins. He stands alive, still marked by the wounds, and that is why He is worthy.

Question 4 of 6

Redeemed

Whom has the Lamb redeemed, and how?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

Men and women out of every kindred, tongue, people, and nation, and every kind of past. He bought them back to God with His own blood, and made them kings and priests.

Question 5 of 6

Prayer

What happens to the prayers of saints?

What verses tell us about this?

“Let my prayer be set forth before thee as incense; and the lifting up of my hands as the evening sacrifice.”

Psalm 141:2

Answer

They go up before God like incense and are kept in golden bowls before the throne. None of them is lost or forgotten, even the ones prayed in the dark.

Question 6 of 6

Worship

Who joins in worshipping the Lamb?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

First the four beasts and the twenty-four elders, then angels past counting, then every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth, and in the sea. All of them give the same praise to God on the throne and to the Lamb.
