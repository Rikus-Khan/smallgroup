Deuteronomy 20

Be not afraid: the LORD goes with you to fight for you

Before the battle the priest speaks: fear not, for the LORD goes with you. The officers send home the man with a new house, a new vineyard, a new bride, and the man who is afraid. Peace is offered before a siege. The nations of the land are judged. And the fruit trees are spared, for the tree of the field is man's life.

Part one

“Be not afraid of them”

Verses 1–4

A bigger army, and a God who goes with you.

Horses, chariots, and more men

The enemy would come with horses and chariots, and more men than Israel had. Moses does not say the other army will be small. He says, “be not afraid of them”, and gives the reason:

“the LORD thy God is with thee”

And he points back: “which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt”. Pharaoh had come after them with horses and chariots too, and “the waters returned, and covered the chariots, and the horsemen” (Exodus 14:28).

“Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.”

Psalm 20:7

Four fears, and one reason

Before a sword was drawn, the priest stood up and spoke. “Hear, O Israel” are the words Moses used in chapter 6; here the priest uses them on the edge of battle.

  • hearts that faintgiving up inside before it starts
  • fear“fear not”
  • tremblingthe body shaking
  • terror“neither be ye terrified because of them”
  • “the LORD your God is he that goeth with you”to fight for you, to save you

“…Be not afraid nor dismayed by reason of this great multitude; for the battle is not yours, but God’s.”

2 Chronicles 20:15

The fight you're in

“For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.”

Ephesians 6:12

Israel's wars were real wars, in that land, at that time. Ours is a different fight. The New Testament tells us who the enemy is, and he is not the man across the street. He is the one who wants you back in the old life.

“Ye are of God, little children, and have overcome them: because greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world.”

1 John 4:4

That pull may look like horses and chariots: bigger than you, and more of them. Be not afraid of it. The Lord goes with you, to fight for you.

Part two

“Let him go and return unto his house”

Verses 5–9

The men who were sent home before the battle.

Three men sent home

“built a new house, and hath not dedicated it”go homeA house built and never lived in. Dedicated means set apart for its use: he has not yet begun his life in it.
“planted a vineyard, and hath not yet eaten of it”go homeYears of work, and not yet one grape for himself.
“betrothed a wife, and hath not taken her”go homeBetrothed means promised in marriage. The wedding has not yet come.

Each time the same words: “lest he die in the battle, and another man” take what he worked for. God cared about a man's home, his work and his bride, even in a war.

A good excuse, and a bad one

“When a man hath taken a new wife, he shall not go out to war, neither shall he be charged with any business: but he shall be free at home one year, and shall cheer up his wife which he hath taken.”

Deuteronomy 24:5

God gave a new husband a whole year at home. A man's marriage is not something to put off until the work is done.

“And they all with one consent began to make excuse. The first said unto him, I have bought a piece of ground, and I must needs go and see it: I pray thee have me excused. And another said, I have bought five yoke of oxen, and I go to prove them: I pray thee have me excused. And another said, I have married a wife, and therefore I cannot come.”

Luke 14:18–20

Land, work, a wife: God let those things send a man home from war. But in Jesus' story, men used the same things to stay away from the “great supper” (Luke 14:16), and missed it. Your house, your job and your wife are gifts from God. Don't let them be the reason you stay away from him.

“Lest his brethren's heart faint”

Fainthearted means a man whose heart has gone weak with fear. He was not beaten or shamed. He was sent home, and the reason is given: fear spreads down a line of men. One man running can turn a whole company.

“Now therefore go to, proclaim in the ears of the people, saying, Whosoever is fearful and afraid, let him return and depart early from mount Gilead. And there returned of the people twenty and two thousand; and there remained ten thousand.”

Judges 7:3

At the LORD's word Gideon made this very proclamation, and more than two men in three went home. God won with the few who stayed. The other side is true too: courage spreads like fear does. In a group like this, your faith on a hard day helps the man next to you hold.

Part three

“Then proclaim peace unto it”

Verses 10–18

Peace offered first, and the judgment on the nations of the land.

Peace first

If“it make thee answer of peace, and open unto thee”
→
then“they shall serve thee”

Tributaries are people who pay tribute, a yearly tax, to another nation and serve it. A city that opened its gate lived. Only a city that would “make no peace” was besieged.

“Now then we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God did beseech you by us: we pray you in Christ’s stead, be ye reconciled to God.”

2 Corinthians 5:20

God offers peace before he judges. That is still the order. Today the gate can still open.

Two kinds of city

Cities “very far off” (verses 10–15)

peace offered

If the city opened, it lived and served. If it fought, every male was put to the sword, and the women and little ones were spared.

“The cities of these people” (verses 16–18)

utterly destroyed

The six nations in the land itself: the Hittites, Amorites, Canaanites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites. Chapter 7 named seven, with the Girgashites.

This is hard, and we looked at it in chapter 7: God's own judgment on those nations, after he had waited until their iniquity was full (Genesis 15:16). Verse 18 gives the reason here: “That they teach you not to do after all their abominations”. Chapter 18 named them: children burned in the fire, sorcery, calling up the dead. “Shall not the Judge of all the earth do right?” (Genesis 18:25).

“That they teach you not”

“But were mingled among the heathen, and learned their works. And they served their idols: which were a snare unto them.”

Psalm 106:35–36

God knew what would happen if Israel lived side by side with those altars: they would learn them. And they did (Judges 2:11–13); the psalm says it in four words, “and learned their works”.

God does not send us to war with anyone. But he is just as plain about what will teach us the old ways again. The old crowd, the old corner, the numbers still in your phone: those teach. What in your life is teaching you, and what is it teaching?

Part four

“The tree of the field is man's life”

Verses 19–20

Even in a war, don't cut down what feeds you.

Spare the fruit tree

A siege could last a long time. The quick way to get timber was to cut down every tree around the city. God said no: not the trees for meat, that is, for food. Bulwarks were siege works built up against the walls; only a tree that bore no food could be cut for them.

“For the tree of the field is man's life.” The war would end, and people would still need to eat. Don't destroy, in a fight, what you will need when it is over.

Don't burn it all down

An angry man in a fight cuts down everything in reach: the marriage, the job, the friend who tried to help, the church. He wins the argument, and there is nothing left to eat when it is over. God's law said to leave the fruit trees standing.

“And he answering said unto him, Lord, let it alone this year also, till I shall dig about it, and dung it: And if it bear fruit, well: and if not, then after that thou shalt cut it down.”

Luke 13:8–9

In Jesus' story, the tree had no fruit at all, and it was still given another year, and someone to dig about it. That is how the Lord has dealt with many of us. Today is part of that year. Let him dig.

Real world: go out with him

  • SinTrusting what you can count: horses, chariots, money, your own strength. Learning the ways of the people around you. Cutting down everything in a fight.
  • RegretMaybe you ran when the battle came, and others ran with you. God sent the fearful man home; he did not throw him away.
  • GraceGod offers peace before judgment, and goes with his people “to fight for you… to save you”.
  • HopeThe battle is not yours, but God's. Greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world.
  • TodayName the fight you're in, and say out loud: the LORD goes with me. Tell one brother in Christ about it today, so his courage can help yours. And leave the fruit trees standing.

Question 1 of 6

Fear

Why was Israel not to be afraid of horses, chariots and a bigger army?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because the LORD their God was with them, the same God who brought them up out of the land of Egypt.

Question 2 of 6

The priest

What did the priest say to the people before the battle?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Hear, O Israel: don't let your hearts faint, don't fear, tremble or be terrified, for the LORD your God goes with you to fight for you and to save you.

Question 3 of 6

Sent home

Which men were sent home from the battle?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The man with a new house not yet dedicated, the man with a vineyard not yet eaten of, the man betrothed and not yet married, and the man who was fearful and fainthearted.

Question 4 of 6

The fearful

Why was the fearful man sent home?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now therefore go to, proclaim in the ears of the people, saying, Whosoever is fearful and afraid, let him return and depart early from mount Gilead. And there returned of the people twenty and two thousand; and there remained ten thousand.”

Judges 7:3

Answer

Lest his brethren's heart faint as well as his: fear spreads from one man to the rest.

Question 5 of 6

Peace first

What was Israel to do first when they came near a city to fight against it?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Proclaim peace to it. If it answered with peace and opened its gates, its people lived and served Israel as tributaries.

Question 6 of 6

The trees

Which trees were they not to cut down, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The trees that gave food. They could eat of them, for the tree of the field is man's life. Only trees not for meat could be cut down for bulwarks.
