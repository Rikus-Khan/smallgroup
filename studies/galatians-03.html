Paul started these churches on his first journey, about AD 46 to 48. After he left, teachers came telling the Gentile believers they weren't really saved until they were circumcised and kept the law of Moses:

“And certain men which came down from Judaea taught the brethren, and said, Except ye be circumcised after the manner of Moses, ye cannot be saved.”

Acts 15:1

That's Jesus plus: Jesus, plus your own works. In chapter 3 Paul answers it two ways: from what the Galatians lived through, and from the Scriptures.

Part one

Who hath bewitched you?

Verses 1–5

Paul asks one question: how did you get the Spirit? Their own story is the answer.

Set forth before your eyes

Evidently set forth is the word for a public notice pasted up in the town square. Paul preached the cross so plainly it was as if they'd watched it.

Bewitched means put under a spell. Paul isn't talking about magic. He means: you saw Christ crucified for you. Who talked you out of that?

Then one question. When the Spirit came into you, was it because you had kept the law? Or because you heard about Jesus and believed?

Begun in the Spirit, finished by the flesh?

Made perfect means finished, brought to the end. The Galatians started with grace, then tried to finish with rules. Circumcision, holy days, a list.

But a man can't finish by his own sweat what God began. The same Spirit who started it carries it home.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

The trap after a fall

Earning my way back

“I'll fix this first”

Stay away from God and the brothers until you've cleaned up. Make big promises. Punish yourself a while. Do extra to make up for it. It feels holy. It's the flesh trying to finish the job.

Coming back the way you came in

By faith, today

You didn't get in by being good, and you don't get back on track by being good. Tell God the truth, trust what Christ did, and take the next step with the Spirit's help.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Verse 4: “Have ye suffered so many things in vain?” Don't throw away what God has already done in you because of one bad day.

Part two

The children of Abraham

Verses 6–14

The false teachers loved to talk about Abraham. Paul goes back to him too, and to the curse that Christ carried.

Abraham's real family

What the teachers said

By the knife

To be Abraham's son, a Gentile had to be circumcised like Abraham and keep the law.

What the Scripture says

By faith

Abraham believed God and was counted righteous, centuries before Moses. His real children are the ones who believe like he did.

“And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.”

Genesis 12:3

God told Abraham from the start that the blessing was meant for all families of the earth. Paul calls it the gospel “preached before.” Nobody's family, background or record keeps him out.

All things, or nothing

The law is like a chain holding a man over a drop. Break one link and the whole chain fails. “Cursed is every one that continueth not in all things.” Not most things. All.

The law's way (3:12)

Do them, and live

Every command, every day, never once failing. No man but Jesus has ever done it.

God's way (3:11)

Believe, and live

“The just shall live by faith.” Paul is quoting the prophet Habakkuk.

He took the curse

Underthe curse

Giventhe blessing

Uslaw-breakers: “cursed is every one”
Christ“who knew no sin”

Redeemed is a slave-market word: bought out, paid for, set free. Christ hung on the tree, the death the law called cursed (Deuteronomy 21:23), so the curse could fall on Him and not on you.

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

If you feel cursed by your past, by what you did and what was done to you, hear it: the curse was carried. It's not yours to carry any more.

Part three

The promise came first

Verses 15–22

A signed agreement can't be rewritten later. So why did God give the law at all?

Four hundred and thirty years after

Once a man's covenant is signed and sealed, nobody adds to it or cancels it (3:15). God's promise to Abraham was settled first. The law came 430 years later and can't cancel it. The promise runs straight over the law to Christ.

Paul also notices one word in Genesis: seed, not seeds. The promise pointed to one Man, Christ. Everyone who is in Him gets what was promised.

Then why the law?

The law was “added because of transgressions”: to show sin for what it is, until Christ came. It could show a man his sin. It could never give him life.

Concluded means shut up, locked in together. The law put every man in the same cell, the religious man and the criminal alike. Nobody gets out by his own record.

Then the one key: “the promise by faith of Jesus Christ… given to them that believe.” Everyone was in the same cell, so the same door is open to every one of them.

Part four

Our schoolmaster to Christ

Verses 23–29

The law had a job: to get us to Christ. Once we're there, we're sons, and we all sit at the same table.

The man who walked the boy to school

The paidagogos wasn't the teacher. He was a household slave who walked the boy to school, kept him in line, and used the rod when he had to.

His whole job was to get the boy to the teacher's door. That's the law: it kept us in line and showed us we needed Christ, then delivered us to Him. Once you're through the door, you don't need the man with the rod walking behind you.

Three walls down

JewGreekYour race and your background don't make you more or less His.
bondfreeWhether you've done time or never had a ticket, the same Christ.
malefemaleMen are still men and women still women. Before God, they stand on the same ground.

Many Bible teachers point to an old Jewish prayer that thanked God for not making a man a Gentile, a slave or a woman. Paul takes those same three lines and says: in Christ, the ranking is gone. There's no first-class seat at this table.

Put on Christ

the clothes you came in → Christ On release day a man changes out of the jumpsuit. Put on is a clothes word: in Christ, God sees you dressed in His Son.

Baptism pictures this. It doesn't save; faith in Christ does (3:26). But going under the water and coming up shows everyone whose you are now. And verse 29 says you're Abraham's seed and an heir. Not a hired hand. A son.

Question 1 of 6

Begun

Can a man finish by his own effort what the Spirit began?

What verses tell us about this?

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Answer

No. We received the Spirit by believing, not by keeping the law, and the Christian life goes on the same way it began. After a fall, don't try to earn your way back. Come back by faith, and let God finish what He started.

Question 2 of 6

Abraham

Who are the real children of Abraham?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Those who believe God the way Abraham did. He was counted righteous by faith, and God promised that all nations would be blessed in him. Anyone who belongs to Christ is Abraham's seed and an heir of the promise.

Question 3 of 6

The curse

How did Christ redeem us from the curse of the law?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Answer

The law curses everyone who doesn't keep all of it, and that's all of us. Christ bought us out by being made a curse in our place. He hung on the tree, so the curse fell on Him, and the blessing comes to us through faith.

Question 4 of 6

The promise

Can the law cancel a promise God made first?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No. God's promise to Abraham was confirmed first, and the law came 430 years later. A later law can't cancel it or add conditions to it. The inheritance comes by promise, not by keeping the law.

Question 5 of 6

Schoolmaster

What was the law for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It was added because of sin, to show it for what it is, until Christ came. It shut everyone up under sin so the promise could be given to all who believe. Like the slave who walked a boy to school, it brought us to Christ.

Question 6 of 6

One

What do all believers share in Christ?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Every believer is a child of God by faith and has put on Christ. Race, rank and record don't make anyone more or less His: we are all one in Christ Jesus, and all heirs of the promise.
