Romans 12

A living sacrifice

Eleven chapters on what God has done. Now Paul turns to Monday morning: your body, your mind, your place among God's people, and the man who did you wrong.

Chapters 1 to 11 are what God has done: every man guilty, Christ dying for the ungodly, justified by faith, no condemnation, mercy upon all. Chapter 11 ended in worship.

Now the turn. Chapters 12 to 16 are how a forgiven man lives: at work, at home, in the church, and with people who hurt him. The hinge is one word in the first verse: “therefore.”

Part one

Body and mind

Verses 1–2

Two verses that turn the whole letter into a way of life.

Mercy first, then the ask

BecauseGod has shown you mercy (chapters 1–11),
→
thereforegive Him yourself.

Watch the order. Paul doesn't say, clean yourself up so God will love you. He says God already has, so now give Him your life. Obedience is the answer to grace, never the price of it.

I beseech you: I beg you. Paul is pleading, not threatening, the way a brother would.

Romans 12:1

A living sacrifice

You hand yourself over to God every morning, then get up and live for Him all day.

Your bodies. The same body some of us poisoned, fought with, and sold for a high. God wants it. Not a cleaned-up version first: this one, today, the hands, the eyes, the feet, the mouth.

“For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God's.”

1 Corinthians 6:20

Reasonable service: after all He's done, it's the only thing that makes sense.

Conformed, or transformed

Conformed means squeezed into a mould. The world presses from the outside: the old crowd, the old habits, the screen in your pocket.

Transformed is the Greek metamorphoō, where we get metamorphosis: a caterpillar becoming a butterfly. The same word is used of Jesus shining on the mountain (Matthew 17:2). It's a change from the inside out, and God does it.

The renewing of your mind

Every man in recovery knows this loop. It runs through a thought: a lie you tell yourself just before you fall.

God breaks it where it starts. Renewing is making new, again and again: swapping the lie for what God says is true.

“…whatsoever things are true, whatsoever things are honest, whatsoever things are just, whatsoever things are pure… think on these things.”

Philippians 4:8

Part two

One body, many members

Verses 3–8

Think straight about yourself, then find your place among God's people.

Think soberly

Pride says, I'm better than these guys. Shame says, I'm worthless; God could never use me. Plenty of men swing between the two in one day. Both keep your eyes on yourself.

Soberly: clear-headed, level, the way a sober man sees. Not puffed up, not crushed. You're a sinner saved by grace, and God has given you a measure of faith to use.

Every member has a job

  1. Prophecyspeaking God's word plainly, as far as your faith goes
  2. Ministryserving: the coffee, the chairs, the rides
  3. Teachingexplaining the Bible so it makes sense
  4. Exhortingcoming alongside a man to keep him going
  5. Givingwith simplicity: freely, no strings
  6. Rulingleading, with diligence
  7. Mercywith cheerfulness, not a long face
  8. Yours?what has God given you to serve with?

A hand isn't an eye, and the body needs both. Every gift is “according to the grace that is given”: a gift, not a reward. No believer is a spare part.

Your past can become your gift. The man who has been through the fire is often the best encourager for the next man walking in. Somebody in this room needs what God gave you.

Part three

Love without a mask

Verses 9–16

A string of short commands, every one of them something to do today.

No mask

ἀνυπόκριτος said anypokritos without a mask: un-hypocritical. A hypokrites was an actor on a stage.

Dissimulation is faking it. Plenty of us learned to wear a mask to survive: on the street, inside, at home. Love doesn't put on a show. It's the same in the room as out of it.

Abhor evil, recoil from it like a hot stove. Cleave to good, stick to it like glue. Not slothful in business: show up for work today and do it well. That's serving the Lord too.

About seven years after this letter, many of the believers who first heard it were hunted down. Bless, and curse not was not theory for them.

Weep with them that weep: show up at the funeral, sit with the man whose wife left. Condescend to men of low estate: go and sit with the guy fresh out with nothing. Nobody is beneath you.

Part four

Overcome evil with good

Verses 17–21

For men who have been wronged, and men who have done wrong. Most of us are both.

How it goes down

  1. he disrespects me
  2. I mouth off
  3. he shoves
  4. I swing
  5. somebody gets hurt
  6. cuffs

Evil for evil only goes one direction. Recompense to no man evil for evil stops it on the first step. Somebody has to be the one who doesn't hit back. Let it be you.

As much as lieth in you: you can't control the other man, only your half. And if you're the one who did the wrong, that half may mean going back to make it right, where you can do it without making it worse.

What they owe me

  • The friend who set me upowed
  • The one who stole from meowed
  • The family who wrote me offowed
  • The man who hurt someone I loveowed
Balance dueI want them to pay
MINEI will repay, saith the Lord

Hand Him the bill

Give place unto wrath: step aside and leave room for God's anger. He doesn't say the wrong didn't happen. He says it's His to settle, and He will.

You stop carrying the bill. Revenge keeps a man chained to the person who hurt him.

“Who, when he was reviled, reviled not again; when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously:”

1 Peter 2:23

Coals of fire

Paul is quoting Proverbs 25:21–22. Kindness to an enemy is like live coals on his head: a burning shame he can't shake, that may bring him to his senses. You can't control whether it does. You can control whether you feed him.

When you've been wronged

Feed him

Do him good on purpose. Pray for him by name. It won't feel fair. It isn't meant to be fair; it's meant to be like Jesus.

When you did the wrong

Remember

You were God's enemy once, and He fed you anyway. That's how every one of us got saved (5:8–10).

“Then said Jesus, Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do.”

Luke 23:34

“Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.”

Evil wins two ways: when it hurts you, and when it turns you into the same kind of man. Paying it back means it beat you twice.

Good is stronger. It isn't weakness to hold your fist; it takes more strength than swinging. Today, think of one person you'd rather get even with, and do one good thing for him instead. Then hand the rest to God.

Points to remember

  1. Your body“Present your bodies a living sacrifice” 12:1Because God has shown you mercy, hand Him your whole self, every day.
  2. Your mind“Be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind” 12:2Change starts on the inside, one true thought at a time.
  3. Humility“To think soberly” 12:3Not puffed up, not crushed: a sinner saved by grace.
  4. One body“We, being many, are one body in Christ” 12:5Every believer has a gift, and the body needs it.
  5. Real love“Let love be without dissimulation” 12:9No mask. Real love shows up, works, prays, and opens the door.
  6. Enemies“Overcome evil with good” 12:21Don't pay it back. Hand the bill to God, and feed your enemy.

Question 1 of 6

Your body

What does it mean to present your body a living sacrifice?

What verses tell us about this?

“What? know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you, which ye have of God, and ye are not your own?”

1 Corinthians 6:19

Answer

It means giving God your whole self, body included, not once but every day, and then living for Him. We don't do it to earn mercy; we do it because of the mercies He has already shown. After all He's done, it only makes sense.

Question 2 of 6

Your mind

How does a man stop being conformed to this world?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

Not by squeezing harder from the outside, but by being changed from the inside. God renews the mind: we trade the old lies for what He says is true, one thought at a time. Then we prove, by living it, that God's will is good.

Question 3 of 6

Humility

How should a man think of himself?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Soberly: clear-headed and level. Not more highly than he ought, and not as worthless either. He is a sinner saved by grace, given a measure of faith by God, and no one is beneath him.

Question 4 of 6

One body

Why does every believer matter in the church?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because we are one body in Christ, and every member has a job the others can't do. Each gift comes by grace, so nobody gets to boast and nobody is useless. The body needs what God gave you.

Question 5 of 6

Real love

What does love without dissimulation look like day to day?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

No mask, no act. It hates evil and holds on to good, puts other people first, works hard, prays all day, shares, opens the door, and shows up to laugh or cry with a brother.

Question 6 of 6

Enemies

What do you do with a man who has wronged you?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Don't pay him back. Leave the settling to God, who will repay. Then do him good on purpose: feed him, give him drink, pray for him. That's how evil is overcome.
