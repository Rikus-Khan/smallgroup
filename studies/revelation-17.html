Revelation 17

The woman with the golden cup

John is shown a woman dressed like a queen, riding a beast, holding a golden cup. She has fooled the whole world. Then the angel tells him what she really is, and how it ends: the Lamb wins.

The seven bowls of chapter 16 ended with a voice from the throne: “It is done.” And “great Babylon came in remembrance before God” (16:19).

Now one of those same seven angels takes John aside to show him Babylon up close: chapter 17 shows what she is, and chapter 18 shows her fall. Then comes the bride, and the King (chapters 19 to 22).

Who is Babylon?

Good Bible teachers read her in different ways, and they hold them without heat:

  1. Ancient RomeThe great city of John's day, famous for its seven hills (verse 9), rich, proud, and killing Christians.
  2. A world system still to comeA last great power of trade, religion and rule that stands against God before Christ returns.
  3. The world, in every ageThe way of life that says “you don't need God”: in every city, in every century.

These can all be true at once: Rome was the first picture, and the pattern runs to the end. We won't pin a modern country, city, church or person on her. The angel himself says it takes “the mind which hath wisdom” (verse 9).

What is plain is enough: she looks rich, her cup is poison, and the Lamb overcomes.

Part one

The woman and her cup

Verses 1–6

Dressed like a queen, and full of poison.

An old name, an old picture

“O thou that dwellest upon many waters, abundant in treasures, thine end is come, and the measure of thy covetousness.”

Jeremiah 51:13, to Babylon

“The waters which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues.”

Revelation 17:15

Old Babylon sat on the Euphrates and its canals. John's Babylon sits on people: the whole world under her. And the prophets called it whoredom when people gave their love and loyalty to anything but God. It's a word about unfaithfulness, not just about sex.

Look who goes to her. The kings “committed fornication” with her: they made deals and sold out. Everyone else was “made drunk”. That's how she rules: not by force, but by making people want her.

A golden cup

Purple and scarlet were the colors of kings and the rich. Gold, jewels, pearls: she looks like everything a man ever wanted. And she holds out a golden cup.

Look inside: “full of abominations and filthiness.” The cup is gold. What's in it is poison.

“Look not thou upon the wine when it is red, when it giveth his colour in the cup, when it moveth itself aright. At the last it biteth like a serpent, and stingeth like an adder.”

Proverbs 23:31–32

You've seen this cup

Most of the men in this room have held it. It never looks like poison. It looks like gold.

The cup says

“Just this once.”

The bottle, the pipe, the pills, the screen at 2 a.m., the easy money: every one comes dressed in gold. It promises relief, freedom, a good time.

What it gives

“At the last it biteth.”

Lost jobs, lost families, a cell, a hospital bed. “There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death” (Proverbs 14:12).

“Babylon hath been a golden cup in the LORD’S hand, that made all the earth drunken: the nations have drunken of her wine; therefore the nations are mad.”

Jeremiah 51:7

Revelation doesn't call the cup ugly. It tells you what's inside before you drink. Today, you don't have to drink it. Set it down.

Babylon, all through the Bible

  1. Babel“let us make us a name”Genesis 11:4
  2. Nebuchadnezzar“great Babylon, that I have built”Daniel 4:30
  3. Jeremiaha golden cup; “thine end is come”Jeremiah 51
  4. Peter's day“the church that is at Babylon”1 Peter 5:13 (most think Rome)
  5. Revelation“Babylon the great is fallen”17–18

A mystery in the Bible is a secret God makes known. This Babylon is more than one town on the Euphrates. It's man's proud city, built without God, from the tower of Babel on: “let us make us a name.”

And she's the “mother of harlots”: the source of every lesser idol. Every time a man trades God for something that promised more, he's drinking from her cup.

Drunk on the blood of the saints

She doesn't only make others drunk. She is drunk too, on the blood of Jesus' people. The world that offers you the cup has never been a friend to those who follow Jesus.

“If ye were of the world, the world would love his own: but because ye are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hateth you.”

John 15:19

“Great admiration” means great amazement. Even John stood there staring. If an old apostle could be dazzled, don't be ashamed that you were. Just don't stay dazzled: the angel is about to tell him what she really is.

Part two

The beast, explained

Verses 7–14

“Wherefore didst thou marvel?”

A cheap copy of God

The beast

“was, and is not, and yet is”

It comes up out of the pit, and it ends in perdition: ruin, lost for ever.

The Lord

“which is, and which was, and which is to come”

“I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the ending … the Almighty” (Revelation 1:8).

The beast copies God's own name, and gets it wrong: there is a hole in the middle, “is not”. It looks like it died and came back, and the world wonders at it. But its story has only one ending.

Who isn't fooled? The ones whose names are in the book of life. God knew His own from the start, and the door is still open: “whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely” (Revelation 22:17). Your name can be written there today.

Seven heads are seven mountains, and also seven kings. Rome was famous as the city on seven hills, and many count seven emperors; others see seven empires in turn. Either way: kings rise, and kings fall. The eighth “goeth into perdition.”

The angel says this takes wisdom, so be slow to trust anyone who has every detail figured out. The plain thing: no beast lasts.

The Lamb shall overcome

Ten horns come from Daniel 7, where “the ten horns out of this kingdom are ten kings” (Daniel 7:24). Here they have power for “one hour”: a short time. They all think alike, and they hand it all to the beast.

Then they go to war with a Lamb. It isn't close. “The Lamb shall overcome them: for he is Lord of lords, and King of kings.”

“And he hath on his vesture and on his thigh a name written, KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS.”

Revelation 19:16

Who stands with the Lamb

  1. CalledThe invitation went out to everyone: “whosoever will” (22:17).
  2. ChosenThose who answered it, and belong to Him now: “many are called, but few are chosen” (Matthew 22:14).
  3. FaithfulThey keep trusting Him, because He keeps them.

Look where the battle is won: not by the men with Him, but by the Lamb. They are with Him. Your part is to stand where He stands. He does the overcoming.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

If you fell yesterday, you're still called and still His. Faithful starts again this morning.

Part three

The beast turns on her

Verses 15–18

The thing she rode eats her alive.

What she rode, ate her

She sat on the beast (verse 3) → The beast devoured her Evil has no loyalty. It uses you, then it turns on you.

“While they promise them liberty, they themselves are the servants of corruption: for of whom a man is overcome, of the same is he brought in bondage.”

2 Peter 2:19

Every addict knows this. At first you ride it: you're in control. Then one day it's riding you, and it wants all of you. But that isn't the end of the story: “If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed” (John 8:36).

The same crowd, a different song

Babylon sat on peoples, multitudes, nations and tongues, and she sold them. Now listen to the song sung to the Lamb in heaven, about that same crowd:

“And they sung a new song, saying, Thou art worthy to take the book, and to open the seals thereof: for thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation;”

Revelation 5:9

Babylon uses people from every nation. The Lamb buys them back with His own blood. Every man in this room has been under one or the other. You get to choose which song is yours.

“Until the words of God shall be fulfilled.”

The beast and the ten kings think they're running things. They aren't. God steers even their plans, so that evil eats itself and His word comes true.

God doesn't make men sin; they choose their way. But even their worst choices end up serving His will, and only for as long as He allows: “until.”

That's for the man whose life feels out of control today. It isn't out of God's control. No beast, no boss, no habit, no court date has the last word.

Two women, two cities

Babylon (chapters 17–18)

The harlot

Purple and scarlet, gold and pearls. A cup full of filth. Drunk on the blood of the saints. Made “desolate and naked”, and burned.

New Jerusalem (chapters 19–21)

The bride

“I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband” (21:2).

Revelation tells the end of two cities. One is built by men to make a name for themselves, and it falls. The other comes down from God, and it lasts. Babylon sells herself to everyone. The bride belongs to one Husband, who loved her and gave Himself for her.

Today, every choice is a vote for one city or the other. And if you've lived in Babylon a long time, you can still move.

Points to remember

  1. Babylon“MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT” verse 5Man's proud city without God, from Babel to Rome to the end. Teachers differ on the details; what she does is plain.
  2. The cup“a golden cup in her hand full of abominations” verse 4The cup looks like gold. What's in it is poison. You don't have to drink.
  3. The saints“drunken with the blood of the saints” verse 6The world has never loved the people of Jesus. They overcome by the blood of the Lamb.
  4. The beast“and goeth into perdition” verse 11Kings rise and fall, and the beast's story has only one ending. Evil even turns on its own.
  5. The Lamb“the Lamb shall overcome them: for he is Lord of lords, and King of kings” verse 14The Lamb wins, and those with Him are called, chosen and faithful.
  6. In control“until the words of God shall be fulfilled” verse 17God steers even the plans of His enemies. Nothing is out of His control.

Question 1 of 6

Babylon

Who is the woman, and why is she called Babylon?

What verses tell us about this?

“And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.”

Genesis 11:4

Answer

She is “that great city”: man's proud city, built without God, as at Babel. John's first readers saw Rome in her; teachers also see a last world system, or the world in every age. She seduces the world, and she falls.

Question 2 of 6

The cup

What does her golden cup promise, and what is really in it?

What verses tell us about this?

“Look not thou upon the wine when it is red, when it giveth his colour in the cup, when it moveth itself aright. At the last it biteth like a serpent, and stingeth like an adder.”

Proverbs 23:31–32

“There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death.”

Proverbs 14:12

Answer

Riches, pleasure and freedom: it's gold outside. Inside it is full of abominations and filthiness, and at the last it bites. Knowing what's inside helps you set it down.

Question 3 of 6

The saints

How does the world treat the people of Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“If ye were of the world, the world would love his own: but because ye are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hateth you.”

John 15:19

“And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death.”

Revelation 12:11

Answer

Babylon is drunk on their blood. The world hates those Jesus called out of it. But the saints overcome by the blood of the Lamb.

Question 4 of 6

The beast

How does it end for the beast and the kings who follow him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In perdition. The kings get “one hour”, and the beast goes into ruin. Along the way evil turns on its own: the beast devours the woman who rode it.

Question 5 of 6

The Lamb

Who wins, and who stands with Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he hath on his vesture and on his thigh a name written, KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS.”

Revelation 19:16

“For whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith.”

1 John 5:4

Answer

The Lamb, because He is Lord of lords and King of kings. With Him are the called, chosen and faithful: all who answered His invitation, which is open to whosoever will.

Question 6 of 6

In control

Who is really in charge, even of the beast?

What verses tell us about this?

“These things I have spoken unto you, that in me ye might have peace. In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.”

John 16:33

“I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the ending, saith the Lord, which is, and which was, and which is to come, the Almighty.”

Revelation 1:8

Answer

God. Even the kings fulfil His will, and only “until the words of God shall be fulfilled.” Nothing in your life is out of His hand either.
