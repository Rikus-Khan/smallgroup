Proverbs 29

Stiff necks and safe places

Solomon's last collection ends with a warning about hard hearts, and a promise of safety for every man who trusts the LORD.

Things to watch for

Part one

Who's in charge

Verses 1–8

A man who won't bend, and the people who live under good and bad leaders.

A neck that won't bend

A farmer put a yoke on an ox's neck to steer it. A stiff-necked ox fought the yoke and wouldn't turn. The Bible uses that picture for a man who won't take correction.

Look at the word “often.” God doesn't trap anybody. He warns, and warns, and warns again: through His Word, a friend, a wife, a judge, a hospital bed.

But warnings don't last forever. The man who keeps stiffening his neck finally runs out of road, “suddenly,” with no fix left.

The door is still open today

Verse 1 is a warning, not a closed door. If you can still hear the warning, you still have time to turn.

“While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation.”

Hebrews 3:15

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Every man in this room has been “often reproved.” The difference isn't who got warned. It's who bent his neck and turned.

Everybody feels who's in charge

The righteous in authority

People rejoice

A king who rules “by judgment” (fair, honest decisions) makes the whole land steady (v. 4).

The wicked in charge

People mourn

“He that receiveth gifts” means a man who takes bribes. He sells out the ones he's supposed to protect, and the land comes apart.

You may not run a country, but somebody lives under you: a wife, kids, a crew at work, a younger man in recovery. Do they rejoice or mourn when you walk in?

Where the money went

Proverbs 29:3

he that keepeth company with harlots spendeth his substance.

Luke 15:13

the younger son … took his journey into a far country, and there wasted his substance with riotous living.

“Substance” is everything a man has: his money, his health, his time, his name. Sin always sends a bill, and it always costs more than it said it would.

But remember how Jesus' story ends. The son who wasted it all came home, and his father ran to him. It's not too late to make your Father glad.

Nets and snares

A hunter hid a net or a snare on the path and baited it. This chapter is full of them:

  1. Flatterya net for your feet (29:5)
  2. Sinhas a snare in it (29:6)
  3. Scofferssnare a whole city (29:8)
  4. Fear of manbrings a snare (29:25)

A flatterer tells you what you want to hear so he can get something from you. Watch the man who only ever praises you, especially when he wants a favor, a ride, or for you to “come out just this once.”

Meanwhile, the righteous man “doth sing and rejoice” (v. 6). He isn't walking around waiting for the trap to spring.

Part two

Fools, rulers, and sons

Verses 9–17

Hold your tongue, listen to the truth, and don't leave a child to himself.

Say it all, or say it later

The fool→uttereth all his mind→right nowWhatever he feels comes out of his mouth, all of it, on the spot.
The wise man→keepeth it in→till afterwardsHe has the same feelings. He just waits until he can say it right.

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath:”

James 1:19

And verse 9: arguing with a fool goes nowhere. Whether he blows up or laughs it off, “there is no rest.” Sometimes the wise move is to stop arguing.

It rolls downhill

If the man at the top rewards liars, everyone under him figures out fast that lying is how you get ahead.

Same in a home. Kids learn what Dad listens to. If gossip, excuses, and “just don't tell your mother” work in your house, they'll learn that.

Verse 14 shows the other way: a king who “faithfully judgeth the poor” gets a throne that lasts.

The same God made them both

“The deceitful man” here is the man who uses people, the oppressor. He and the poor man live side by side. And the LORD gives the light of life to both of them. Every breath either one takes comes from God.

“That ye may be the children of your Father which is in heaven: for he maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good, and sendeth rain on the just and on the unjust.”

Matthew 5:45

So the poor man isn't beneath you, and the powerful man isn't above God. “The righteous considereth the cause of the poor” (v. 7): he takes the time to know what they're up against.

Not left to himself

The “rod” stands for loving discipline, with reproof, words that explain what was wrong. It's never permission for rage or abuse. The same Bible says:

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

The real danger in verse 15 is a child “left to himself”: nobody around to say no, nobody who cares enough to correct him. A lot of us grew up that way, or raised kids that way while we were using.

Good news: it's not too late to be the father who shows up. God is that kind of Father to us: “no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous… nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness” (Hebrews 12:11).

Part three

Where a man is safe

Verses 18–27

God's Word, a humble spirit, and trust in the LORD instead of the crowd.

What “vision” means here

חָזוֹן said chazon (khah-ZONE) a message from God, what a prophet “saw”

It's the word for God's revealed Word. When Samuel was a boy, “the word of the LORD was precious in those days; there was no open vision” (1 Samuel 3:1). That's what a land with “no vision” is like: God isn't being heard.

And “perish” is a word for throwing off restraint, running wild. It's the same word used when Israel danced around the golden calf: “the people were naked” (Exodus 32:25). Without God's Word, people don't just get lost. They come loose.

Not a business plan

You'll see this verse on office walls and motivational posters. But that isn't what it means.

  • a five-year plangood to have, but not this verse
  • a big dream for your lifenot what Solomon meant
  • a leader with big ideasideas don't hold people together
  • God speaking, and men keeping itthe two halves of the verse match

Read both halves together: no vision (God's Word not heard) on one side, keepeth the law (God's Word obeyed) on the other. One man comes apart. The other is “happy.”

“Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.”

Psalm 119:105

What goes up

Pride lifts a man up in his own eyes. Then it drops him. The humble man keeps himself low, and God lifts him up.

“God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble” (James 4:6). Pride is the one thing that puts a man on the other side from God.

Anger and pride usually ride together. “An angry man stirreth up strife” (v. 22): he walks into a calm room and stirs it until it boils.

Covering for a thief

“Bewray” is an old word for reveal, give away. In Israel, when a theft was reported, a public oath (the “cursing”) was spoken, calling every witness to come forward:

“And if a soul sin, and hear the voice of swearing, and is a witness, whether he hath seen or known of it; if he do not utter it, then he shall bear his iniquity.”

Leviticus 5:1

The thief's partner hears it and stays quiet. He thinks he's being loyal. Solomon says he hates his own soul. The code of the street says “don't snitch.” God's way says don't partner with it in the first place.

The trap and the high place

The fear of man is living for what people think: “Come on, one won't hurt.” “You think you're better than us now?”

You do what you know is wrong to keep people happy, and the trap snaps shut.

The Hebrew word for “safe” means set up high, out of reach. The snare can't get to the man who trusts the LORD.

Whose opinion counts

Everybody's chasing the approval of the man in charge. But the final word on your life doesn't come from him. It comes from the LORD.

“For do I now persuade men, or God? or do I seek to please men? for if I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ.”

Galatians 1:10

“So that we may boldly say, The Lord is my helper, and I will not fear what man shall do unto me.”

Hebrews 13:6

And don't be surprised by verse 27. When you start walking upright, some people won't like it. “He that is upright in the way is abomination to the wicked.” That's not a sign you're doing it wrong.

Points to remember

  1. Hearing correction“Being often reproved hardeneth his neck” 29:1God warns again and again. Bend your neck while you can still hear Him.
  2. Leading well“When the righteous are in authority, the people rejoice” 29:2Lead honestly, listen to the truth, and don't leave your kids to themselves.
  3. The poor“The LORD lighteneth both their eyes” 29:13God gives life to rich and poor alike. The righteous take up the poor man's cause.
  4. God's Word“He that keepeth the law, happy is he” 29:18“Vision” is God's revealed Word. Without it, people run wild. Keep it, and be happy.
  5. Anger and pride“Honour shall uphold the humble in spirit” 29:23Anger stirs up trouble and pride brings a man low. God lifts the humble.
  6. The fear of man“Whoso putteth his trust in the LORD shall be safe” 29:25Living for people's approval is a trap. Trusting the LORD sets you up high, out of reach.

Question 1 of 6

Hearing correction

What happens to a man who keeps hardening his neck?

What verses tell us about this?

“While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation.”

Hebrews 3:15

“The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance.”

2 Peter 3:9

Answer

God warns him “often,” because He is patient and doesn't want anyone to perish. But the man who keeps refusing correction will one day be broken suddenly, with no remedy left. The time to bend is today, while you can still hear His voice.

Question 2 of 6

Leading well

What kind of leader and father does God want a man to be?

What verses tell us about this?

“And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.”

Ephesians 6:4

Answer

A righteous one: honest, fair, and not for sale. He doesn't listen to lies, because whatever he tolerates spreads to everyone under him. As a father he shows up and corrects with love, without provoking his kids to anger, and his home gets rest instead of shame.

Question 3 of 6

The poor

Why does God care how we treat the poor?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because He made the poor man and gives him life, just like He does the powerful man. The righteous take the time to understand what the poor are up against, and God says a ruler who judges the poor faithfully will have a throne that lasts.

Question 4 of 6

God's Word

What does “vision” really mean in verse 18?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the child Samuel ministered unto the LORD before Eli. And the word of the LORD was precious in those days; there was no open vision.”

1 Samuel 3:1

“Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.”

Psalm 119:105

Answer

It means revelation, God's Word made known, not a goal or a dream. Where God's Word isn't heard, people throw off restraint and run wild. The second half says the same thing from the other side: the man who keeps God's law is happy.

Question 5 of 6

Anger and pride

What do anger and pride do to a man?

What verses tell us about this?

“But he giveth more grace. Wherefore he saith, God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble.”

James 4:6

Answer

Anger stirs up fights and piles up sin. Pride lifts a man up in his own eyes and then brings him low, because God resists the proud. But God gives grace to the humble and holds them up with honor.

Question 6 of 6

The fear of man

Why is the fear of man a trap, and where is a man safe?

What verses tell us about this?

“So that we may boldly say, The Lord is my helper, and I will not fear what man shall do unto me.”

Hebrews 13:6

Answer

Living for people's approval pulls a man into things he knows are wrong, and it snaps shut on him. But the final word on our lives comes from the LORD, not people. The man who trusts Him is “safe,” set up high where the snare can't reach.
