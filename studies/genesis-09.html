Genesis 9

The bow in the cloud

God starts the world over with a promise, and hangs a sign of it in the sky. Then the man who came through the flood falls in his own tent, and his sons show what they are made of.

Part one

Be fruitful, and multiply

Verses 1–7

Eight people step off the ark into an empty world. The first thing God gives them is a blessing.

The same words as the first day

To Adam, Genesis 1:28

“And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it…”

To Noah, Genesis 9:1

“And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth.”

Replenish here simply means fill. God speaks to Noah the way he spoke to Adam. After all the judgment, his first word is a blessing. The flood was not the end of his plan for men; it was a new start.

One thing is new: “Every moving thing that liveth shall be meat for you.” Meat means food. In Eden God gave the plants (1:29); now animals may be eaten too. And the fear of man falls on every beast and fowl (bird) (v. 2): the world is still under the curse, but God keeps it from turning on man.

The life is in the blood

Men may eat meat, but not with the blood still in it. Blood stands for life, and life belongs to God. A man was to pour it out, not treat it as nothing. Later the law explains why blood mattered so much:

“For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul.”

Leviticus 17:11

From here on, the Bible teaches that sin costs a life, and that God accepts a life given in the sinner's place. Every animal on every altar pointed ahead to the one sacrifice that would be enough:

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

What a man's life is worth

“for in the image of God made he man”

Require means call to account; whoso means whoever. Killing a man is an attack on the One whose image he carries. Here is the root of a government's duty to punish murder (Romans 13:4).

That image is on every man: the guy who cut you off, the man you fought, the one you hurt, and the man in your mirror. You are not trash, whatever you've done or had done to you.

“Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God.”

James 3:9 (similitude = likeness)

Part two

My bow in the cloud

Verses 8–17

The first covenant in the Bible, and God does all the promising.

Who signs this covenant?

A covenant is a binding promise, sealed and kept. Spake is spoke; establish means set up and make firm. Now look at who does the promising in verses 8 to 17:

What God says he will do

  • “I establish my covenant”v. 9
  • “I will establish my covenant”v. 11
  • “the covenant which I make”v. 12
  • “I do set my bow in the cloud”v. 13
  • “I will remember my covenant”v. 15
  • “I will look upon it”v. 16
  • “which I have established”v. 17

What Noah has to do

0Nothing. No conditions, no fine print. It covers his sons, his grandsons, every animal and the earth itself.

That's grace: a promise that rests on the one who made it. Every sunrise since has been God keeping his word to men who did nothing to earn it.

The token

A token is a sign, something you can see that stands for a promise, the way a wedding ring does. Perpetual means lasting for ever.

The Hebrew word for bow is qesheth, the same word used for a warrior's bow. Many have seen a picture in that: after the storm of judgment, God hangs up his bow in the sky.

Every time the clouds come back, so does the sign. The storm doesn't cancel the promise. The storm is when you see it.

“I will remember”

God doesn't forget things. In the Bible, when God remembers, he acts on what he promised. And notice who looks at the rainbow in verse 16: God does. We look up and see a promise. He looks at the same sign and keeps it.

Hundreds of years later, God pointed back to this very day to tell his people how sure his kindness is:

“For this is as the waters of Noah unto me: for as I have sworn that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth; so have I sworn that I would not be wroth with thee, nor rebuke thee. For the mountains shall depart, and the hills be removed; but my kindness shall not depart from thee, neither shall the covenant of my peace be removed, saith the LORD that hath mercy on thee.”

Isaiah 54:9–10 (wroth = very angry)

The rainbow shows up again at the end of the Bible, around the throne of God (Revelation 4:3). The promise-keeping God is still on his throne. If he kept his word to Noah, he will keep his word to you.

Part three

In his tent

Verses 18–29

The Bible doesn't hide its heroes' failures. A great man falls, and his sons choose what to do about it.

A good man falls

This is the first time wine appears in the Bible, and the first time a man is drunk. It isn't a stranger or a villain. It's Noah, the man God called just, right after the greatest rescue in history. Husbandman means farmer.

The Bible doesn't excuse him and doesn't dwell on it. It just tells the truth: drink took a godly man down, and it can take any man down. The danger is often greatest right after a victory, when a man feels he's earned a rest from watching himself.

“Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging: and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise.”

Proverbs 20:1

Straight talk about drink

A mocker laughs at you: it promises rest and leaves you exposed. Noah lay “uncovered within his tent”: not out in the street, but at home, where he thought it was safe and nobody was looking.

Most of us in this room know exactly how that goes. The answer isn't just “try harder.” God offers something better to fill a man than the bottle:

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

And Noah's fall isn't the end of his story. Centuries later God still names him among the men of faith (Hebrews 11:7). God records the fall honestly, and he remembers the faith too.

Two ways to handle a man's shame

“saw … and told his two brethren without”

Ham

He looked, and then he went and spread it. Without means outside; brethren means brothers. His father's shame became his story to tell.

“took a garment … and covered”

Shem and Japheth

They walked in backward so they wouldn't stare, and covered him. They didn't lie about what happened or pretend it was fine. They just refused to make a show of it.

Covering a brother isn't hiding his sin or covering for it. It's protecting him from being shamed while he gets back up. That is how the church is told to treat a man who falls:

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

“Hatred stirreth up strifes: but love covereth all sins.”

Proverbs 10:12

When it's your brother

When a man in this group slips, you get to choose which son you'll be. The group text is Ham's way. A phone call, a ride, a coat over his shoulders: that's Shem's.

And if you're the one who fell, call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor. Don't wait to be found. Men who love you will walk in backward with a garment.

Every man here has been covered by Someone. Jesus didn't spread our shame; he bore it on the cross (Hebrews 12:2). Now we get to do for each other what he did for us:

“And above all things have fervent charity among yourselves: for charity shall cover the multitude of sins.”

1 Peter 4:8 (charity = self-giving love)

Noah's words, as Hebrew poetry

Cursed be Canaan; a servant of servants shall he be unto his brethren.

Blessed be the LORD God of Shem; and Canaan shall be his servant.

God shall enlarge Japheth, and he shall dwell in the tents of Shem; and Canaan shall be his servant.

These words are a prophecy: they look ahead to whole peoples, not just three brothers. Enlarge means make room for, spread out wide. Japheth's families would spread far, and one day come in under Shem's tent to share his blessing. Most of us probably come from Japheth's or Ham's families, and that's how we get in:

“That the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ; that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.”

Galatians 3:14

Why Canaan?

Ham sinned, but the words fall on his son Canaan. The text doesn't tell us all of why. What it does show is where this was headed: Canaan's descendants were the peoples of the land of Canaan, and their sins later grew so great that God gave their land to Israel (15:16). Some of them, the Gibeonites, ended up as servants just as Noah said:

“Now therefore ye are cursed, and there shall none of you be freed from being bondmen, and hewers of wood and drawers of water for the house of my God.”

Joshua 9:23 (bondmen = slaves; hewers = cutters)

One thing this verse does not say: it says nothing about Africa or the colour of anyone's skin. Men who used it to defend slavery twisted it.

And no family line is beyond God's reach. Rahab, a woman of Canaan, trusted the LORD and became great-great-grandmother of King David, in the family line of Jesus (Matthew 1:5). Your family's past doesn't have to be your future.

The LORD God of Shem

  • Noah
    • Shem“the LORD God of Shem”
      • Abrahamthen Israel, David …
        • Jesus“the son of Sem”, Luke 3:36
    • Ham
      • Canaan
    • Japheth“God shall enlarge”

Noah doesn't say “Blessed be Shem.” He blesses Shem's God. The LORD ties his name to Shem's family, and the promise runs down that line: to Abraham, to David, and to Jesus.

Noah's long life

Noah lived 350 more years after the flood, and died just before Abraham was born. Shem lived on through most of Abraham's life. The men who saw the flood could tell their great-grandsons about it face to face.

Notice how the chapter ends: not with the vineyard, but with a whole life. One night doesn't sum up a man. God wrote down Noah's fall, but he wrote down his faith too.

Points to remember

  1. A man's worth“In the image of God made he man” 9:6Every man bears God's image, including you, and God holds every life precious.
  2. The promise“I will establish my covenant with you” 9:11God made the promise and God keeps it. Noah signed nothing.
  3. The sign“I do set my bow in the cloud” 9:13God keeps his word, and he gives us something to look at when the storms come back.
  4. A good man falls“He drank of the wine, and was drunken” 9:21Drink can take down even a godly man, often right after a victory. Keep watch.
  5. Brothers“Took a garment … and covered” 9:23Don't spread a brother's shame. Cover him and help him up.
  6. The line of promise“Blessed be the LORD God of Shem” 9:26God's promise runs through Shem to Jesus, and through him to every nation.

Question 1 of 6

A man's worth

Why is a man's life so precious to God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God.”

James 3:9

Answer

Because man is made in the image of God. To take a man's life, or to curse him, is to strike at the God he resembles, and God will call it to account. That image is on every man, the one you can't stand and the one in your mirror.

Question 2 of 6

The promise

What did God promise, and who has to keep it?

What verses tell us about this?

“While the earth remaineth, seedtime and harvest, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease.”

Genesis 8:22

Answer

That he will never again destroy all flesh with a flood, a promise to Noah, his descendants, every animal and the earth. God makes it and God keeps it. Noah isn't given a single condition. That's what grace looks like.

Question 3 of 6

The sign

What does the rainbow tell us about God?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the mountains shall depart, and the hills be removed; but my kindness shall not depart from thee, neither shall the covenant of my peace be removed, saith the LORD that hath mercy on thee.”

Isaiah 54:10

Answer

That God keeps his word. He puts the sign in the clouds, right where the storm is, and he himself looks at it and remembers. His kindness to his people is as sure as his promise to Noah.

Question 4 of 6

A good man falls

How did Noah fall, and what does it warn us about?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

Answer

He got drunk and lay exposed in his own tent, soon after the greatest deliverance in history. No man is too godly to fall, and the time after a victory is a dangerous time. Don't lean on willpower alone: be filled with the Spirit, and keep watch.

Question 5 of 6

Brothers

What should a man do when he sees his brother's shame?

What verses tell us about this?

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Answer

Not what Ham did: look, then spread it. Do what Shem and Japheth did: cover him, without lying about it, and help him get restored, gently, knowing you could fall too.

Question 6 of 6

The line of promise

Why does Noah bless “the LORD God of Shem”?

What verses tell us about this?

“That the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ; that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.”

Galatians 3:14

Answer

Because the LORD would make himself known through Shem's family: Abraham, Israel, David, and at last Jesus. The blessing doesn't stop there. Japheth comes to live in Shem's tents: the nations, men like us, share the blessing through Christ.
