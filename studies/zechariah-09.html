Zechariah 9

Behold, thy King cometh

Proud cities fall, but Jerusalem's King comes riding on a donkey, bringing peace. And God calls to men stuck in a dry pit: “ye prisoners of hope.”

Part one

The burden on the neighbors

Verses 1–8

From the far north down to the Philistine coast, the proud cities around Israel are going to fall. Jerusalem will be guarded.

A heavy word, going south

Quick reminder: Zechariah preached to the Jews back from exile, about 520–518 BC. Chapters 9–14 carry no date, and they look much further ahead.

A “burden” is a heavy message of judgment. It starts in the far north, in Syria.

Then the rich ports, Tyre and Zidon.

Then the Philistine cities down the coast.

But God guards Jerusalem (verse 8). Many Bible teachers see this fulfilled about 333–332 BC, when Alexander the Great of Greece came down this very road.

Tyre's walls

Tyre was a trading city, partly built on an island just off the coast, with walls rising out of the sea. It had held off sieges for years. It was so rich, Zechariah says, that silver lay around like dust and gold like mud in the street.

  • “a strong hold”“the Lord will cast her out”
  • “silver as the dust”“he will smite her power in the sea”
  • “fine gold as the mire”“she shall be devoured with fire”

In 332 BC Alexander built a causeway out to the island, broke through after about seven months, and burned the city. Walls and money are the two things men trust most. Neither one can hold back God.

“Riches profit not in the day of wrath: but righteousness delivereth from death.”

Proverbs 11:4

Even a Philistine

The Philistines were Israel's oldest enemies, Goliath's people. Their kings and their pride would be cut off, and a “bastard,” a mixed, foreign people, would live in Ashdod. Then God says something surprising:

the Philistines → “for our God” God takes the blood and the idol meat out of their mouths: no more pagan sacrifices. Some will be left, and they'll belong to God, counted in Judah like one of its own leaders.

“Ekron as a Jebusite”: the Jebusites held Jerusalem until David took it. Afterward they lived on among God's people. So will these Philistines. If God has room for Goliath's people, He has room for you.

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

God on guard

While armies march up and down the coast, God says He'll camp around His own house like a soldier on watch. “For now have I seen with mine eyes”: He's paying attention.

Jewish history records that when Alexander came through, he left Jerusalem standing. The same God watches over His people today.

“The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them.”

Psalm 34:7

Part two

Thy King cometh

Verses 9–10

After the conqueror on his war horse, a different King. Watch what He rides.

Two kinds of king

The conqueror (verses 1–8)

On a war horse

Comes to take. Burns cities, makes widows, and is gone in a few years.

Zion's King (verse 9)

On a donkey's colt

Comes to give. Just: He's right with God. Having salvation: He brings rescue. Lowly: humble, gentle.

A horse meant war. A donkey meant peace. This King comes in peace, not to crush His people, but to save them. “Daughter of Zion” is a tender name for Jerusalem and her people.

“Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.”

Matthew 11:29

About five hundred years later

A few days before He was crucified, Jesus sent two disciples to fetch a donkey and her colt. He rode into Jerusalem while the crowds spread their coats on the road and shouted. Two Gospels tell us plainly what was happening:

  1. ✓Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zionthe crowds shouting “Hosanna”Matthew 21:9
  2. ✓thy King cometh unto theeriding into JerusalemJohn 12:13
  3. ✓riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an asssent for and riddenMatthew 21:2–7

“All this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophet, saying, Tell ye the daughter of Sion, Behold, thy King cometh unto thee, meek, and sitting upon an ass, and a colt the foal of an ass.”

Matthew 21:4–5

“And Jesus, when he had found a young ass, sat thereon; as it is written, Fear not, daughter of Sion: behold, thy King cometh, sitting on an ass’s colt.”

John 12:14–15

The peace He speaks

This King doesn't win by chariots and war horses. He takes them away. Ephraim (the north) and Jerusalem (the south) won't need them. He wins by speaking peace, and His rule will reach “from sea even to sea.”

  1. He camelowly, on a donkey, to die for sinners (9:9)
  2. He speaks peaceto the nations, today, through the gospel (9:10)
  3. He will reign“to the ends of the earth” when He comes again (9:10)

The first part is done. The last part is still ahead. We live in the middle, where peace is being offered to anyone, anywhere, who will take it:

“And came and preached peace to you which were afar off, and to them that were nigh.”

Ephesians 2:17

Part three

Prisoners of hope

Verses 11–17

A word for men at the bottom of a dry pit. God hasn't forgotten them.

Out of the dry pit

A “pit wherein is no water” was a dry cistern, a hole cut in the rock to hold rain. Empty, it made a prison. That's where Joseph's brothers threw him: “the pit was empty, there was no water in it” (Genesis 37:24).

Where they wereIn the pit

“the pit wherein is no water”

Stuck. Can't climb out. No water, no way up.

What they arePrisoners of hope

“ye prisoners of hope”

Still locked up, but held by a promise.

Where to goThe strong hold

“Turn you to the strong hold”

Run to God. He's the fortress.

Why would God free them? Not because they earned it, but “by the blood of thy covenant”: the covenant God made with Israel and sealed with blood. He keeps His word.

Ye prisoners of hope.

Some of us know the dry pit: addiction, a cell, a hole we dug ourselves and can't climb out of. God doesn't call these men “prisoners of the pit.” He calls them prisoners of hope. Hope has a grip on them that the pit doesn't.

And He promises “double”: back twice over for what was lost. That promise was to Israel, and the principle holds: God doesn't just get a man out. He restores.

For us, the blood that opens the pit is Jesus' own. On the night before the cross He said:

“Likewise also the cup after supper, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you.”

Luke 22:20

“He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings.”

Psalm 40:2

Against Greece

Judah is God's bow, Ephraim His arrow, Zion's sons His sword, and He names the enemy: Greece. In Zechariah's day, Greece was a small, far-off land. God saw a long way ahead:

  1. about 520 BCZechariahPersia rules
  2. 332Alexander of Greeceverses 1–8
  3. about 167Jews fight the Greek kingsverses 13–15
  4. about AD 30the King on a donkeyverse 9

About 167–164 BC, the Greek king Antiochus IV tried to stamp out the Jewish faith. Many Bible teachers see these verses fulfilled when the Maccabees, a small band of Jews, fought back and won.

In verse 15 they shout “as through wine”: a picture of the roar of a victory God gave, not a nod to drinking. Real fullness comes another way:

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

Jewels in His crown

The chapter ends with a shepherd and a crown. God saves His people “as the flock of his people,” and then lifts them up like jewels set in a crown, shining over His land like a banner (“ensign”).

Think of how these men saw themselves: a few poor families in a ruined town. God saw a crown full of jewels. That's how He sees the people He saves. Not as rejects, but as His treasure.

“And they shall be mine, saith the LORD of hosts, in that day when I make up my jewels; and I will spare them, as a man spareth his own son that serveth him.”

Malachi 3:17

Verse 17 is a harvest: “corn” is grain, and “new wine” is the fresh juice of the grape harvest. Plenty of food for everyone, young men and young women. But Zechariah's eyes are on the Giver: “how great is his goodness, and how great is his beauty!”

Points to remember

  1. Riches“Tyrus did build herself a strong hold” 9:3Walls and money can't hold back God. Only He is a safe place.
  2. Mercy“He that remaineth, even he, shall be for our God” 9:7Even Israel's worst enemies could belong to God. Nobody is too far gone.
  3. The King“Lowly, and riding upon an ass” 9:9Jesus is the just, saving, humble King who rode into Jerusalem, just as written.
  4. Peace“He shall speak peace unto the heathen” 9:10He wins by peace, not war, and offers peace with God to anyone, anywhere.
  5. Hope“Turn you to the strong hold, ye prisoners of hope” 9:12God hasn't forgotten the man in the pit. Run to Him; He restores.
  6. Worth“As the stones of a crown” 9:16God sees the people He saves as jewels, His treasure.

Question 1 of 6

Riches

Can money or walls keep a man safe?

What verses tell us about this?

“Riches profit not in the day of wrath: but righteousness delivereth from death.”

Proverbs 11:4

Answer

No. Tyre had island walls and silver like dust, and it burned. Jerusalem had neither, and God guarded it. Money, muscle, and the walls we put up around ourselves won't hold when trouble comes. The LORD is the only safe place.

Question 2 of 6

Mercy

Is anyone too far gone for God?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

“And came and preached peace to you which were afar off, and to them that were nigh.”

Ephesians 2:17

Answer

No. The Philistines were Goliath's people, Israel's enemies for centuries, and God said some of them would belong to Him, counted like family. God cleans up mouths that ate idol sacrifices. He can clean up any man who comes to Him through Jesus.

Question 3 of 6

The King

What kind of King is Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“All this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the prophet, saying, Tell ye the daughter of Sion, Behold, thy King cometh unto thee, meek, and sitting upon an ass, and a colt the foal of an ass.”

Matthew 21:4–5

“Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.”

Matthew 11:29

Answer

Just, saving, and lowly. He's right with God, He brings salvation, and He came humble, on a donkey, not a war horse. About five hundred years after Zechariah wrote it, Jesus rode into Jerusalem exactly that way. He's a King you can come to without fear.

Question 4 of 6

Peace

How does Jesus bring peace?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ:”

Romans 5:1

“He shall have dominion also from sea to sea, and from the river unto the ends of the earth.”

Psalm 72:8

Answer

Not by force, but by speaking peace. He made peace between sinners and God at the cross, and anyone who trusts Him has it now. One day His peaceful rule will cover the whole earth. Until then, He's still offering it to the nations.

Question 5 of 6

Hope

What hope is there for a man stuck in a pit?

What verses tell us about this?

“He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock, and established my goings.”

Psalm 40:2

“Which hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast, and which entereth into that within the veil;”

Hebrews 6:19

Answer

God Himself. He calls men in the dry pit “prisoners of hope,” and tells them to run to Him, the strong hold. He brings them out because of the covenant blood, not because they climbed out. For us, that's the blood of Jesus. And God doesn't just rescue. He restores.

Question 6 of 6

Worth

How does God see His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“And they shall be mine, saith the LORD of hosts, in that day when I make up my jewels; and I will spare them, as a man spareth his own son that serveth him.”

Malachi 3:17

Answer

As a flock He saves and as jewels in His crown. Men may have written us off. God lifts up the people He saves and shows them off as His treasure. That says more about His goodness than about us, and it's why we can hold our heads up.
