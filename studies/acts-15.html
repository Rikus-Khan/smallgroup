Acts 15

Saved by grace, and nothing added

The church holds its first big meeting to settle one question: is Jesus enough? Then two friends part ways over a young man who quit.

Part one

Jesus, plus what?

Verses 1–5

Some men arrive at Antioch with one more thing to add.

The toll booth on the door

The men from Judaea

Jesus + circumcision

Believe, yes. But also be circumcised and keep the law of Moses, or “ye cannot be saved.”

The gospel

Jesus, and that's all

He has already done everything. God asks us to trust Him, not to bring a payment.

Circumcision was the sign of God's promise to Abraham, and it was in the law of Moses. These men weren't villains. Verse 5 calls them believers. They honestly thought they were protecting the faith. But once you add a “plus,” the gospel is gone.

We add our own pluses. Jesus, plus ninety days sober. Jesus, plus never slipping again. Jesus, plus being a better man first. Clean living is good fruit, but it is never the price of the door.

A long walk, with good news

Paul and Barnabas went up from Antioch to Jerusalem to settle the question, a walk of more than 250 miles, “through Phenice and Samaria.”

On the road every church heard the news that Gentiles were turning to God, and “they caused great joy.” Then in Jerusalem, believing Pharisees said the Gentiles must keep the law. Same news, two reactions: joy on the road, a fight at the table.

Part two

The council

Verses 6–21

Peter speaks, then Barnabas and Paul, then James.

Peter's evidence

the Gentilesheard the gospel, and believedGodgave them the Holy Ghost, as He did usno difference

“While Peter yet spake these words, the Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word.”

Acts 10:44

Peter was there in Cornelius' house. He saw it. Nobody had been circumcised and nobody had kept a single law, yet God gave His Spirit the moment they believed. “God, which knoweth the hearts” saw real faith, and He put His stamp on it.

And think who is talking. This is Peter, who swore three times he never knew Jesus. He stands in front of the whole church and says it straight. A man God has restored doesn't stay quiet about grace.

A yoke nobody could carry

A yoke is the heavy wooden frame an ox wears to pull a load. As a way of earning heaven, Peter says, the law was a load no one ever carried: “neither our fathers nor we.”

The law is holy. It shows us where we fall short, but it can't lift anyone out. Only grace does.

Notice “even as they.” Peter, a Jew, says we are saved like them. Everybody comes to Jesus empty-handed.

What the door really costs

  • keep the law of Moses“a yoke… which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear”
  • get cleaned up firstyou can't, and God never asked you to
  • make up for what you didno payment of yours is big enough
  • “the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ”He paid it. You trust Him.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

If you fell yesterday, that doesn't put you back outside the door. You are saved by His grace, not by your record. Come back to Him today and tell Him the truth.

James opens the Scriptures

James doesn't argue from feelings. He goes to the prophets: God would set David's fallen house up again, so that “the residue of men might seek after the Lord,” Gentiles included. It was no surprise to God (v. 18).

Amos wrote it hundreds of years earlier. The Messiah is David's son, He rebuilds, and the nations come in.

Four things to stay away from

  1. pollutions of idolsmeat from pagan temples, and the feasts that went with it
  2. fornicationsexual sin, which was part of much pagan worship
  3. things strangledmeat with the blood still in it
  4. bloodforbidden since Noah's day, and still a sore point for Jewish believers

These four were not the price of being saved. Verse 11 had already settled that. James says “we trouble not” the Gentiles, then names a few things that would wound their Jewish brothers (verse 21: Moses is read in every synagogue every sabbath), and one that is plain sin.

Grace forgives sexual sin. It doesn't make it harmless. “Turned to God” also means turned from something. A man who has been forgiven doesn't have to keep a list of rules to earn it, but he does stay away from the places that nearly killed him.

Part three

The letter

Verses 22–35

The church writes it down, and sends two men to carry it.

The letter from Jerusalem

“The apostles and elders and brethren send greeting unto the brethren which are of the Gentiles in Antioch and Syria and Cilicia”

Written from
Jerusalem, after the council
When
about AD 49
Carried by
Judas Barsabas and Silas, with Paul and Barnabas
Why
“no greater burden than these necessary things” (v. 28)

“It seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us”

Nobody won the argument by shouting. They listened to Peter's testimony, to Barnabas and Paul's report, and to the Scriptures, and they agreed together. The Spirit who lives in every believer led them, and He never contradicts His own Word.

Look at what the letter does. It is short, kind, and clear, and it takes a burden off men. Compare that to the men from Judaea, who had troubled them “with words, subverting your souls.” Words that pile on a load aren't from God. Words that lift it are.

Then they signed it with their lives: “Men that have hazarded their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.” It's easy to preach grace when it costs nothing. These two men had already been stoned and left for dead.

Where this sits in the story

  1. about AD 34–35Saul meets Jesus on the Damascus road (Acts 9)
  2. about AD 46–48First journey: Cyprus and Galatia (Acts 13–14)
  3. about AD 49The council at Jerusalem, and the letter (Acts 15)
  4. about AD 49–52Second journey: Philippi, Athens, Corinth (Acts 15:36–18:22)
  5. about AD 57Romans: Paul explains this same gospel in full

The church could have split in two that year, a Jewish church and a Gentile church. Instead the letter was read aloud in Antioch, and “they rejoiced for the consolation.” Consolation means comfort. Hearing that you owe nothing extra is exactly that.

Part four

A sharp disagreement

Verses 36–41

Two good men, one young quitter, and a split.

The story of John Mark

Mark turned back at Perga on the first journey, and Paul took it as quitting (Acts 13:13). Years later:

“Only Luke is with me. Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry.”

2 Timothy 4:11

Paul asks for him by name. (Church tradition says he wrote the Gospel of Mark.) One failure didn't finish him, and it won't finish you.

Two teams, two roads

“Contention” is paroxysmos, where we get “paroxysm”: a hot, sharp quarrel.

Barnabas took Mark to Cyprus, his home island. He once vouched for Paul when the church feared him (Acts 9:27), so he gave Mark another chance.

Paul chose Silas and went overland, “confirming the churches.” Scripture doesn't say who was right. God used both. Is there a man you've walked away from? Pray for him today.

Points to remember

  1. Salvation“Through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved” 15:11Jesus plus nothing. You can't add to it, and you can't lose it by failing.
  2. Proof“Giving them the Holy Ghost, even as he did unto us” 15:8God gave His Spirit to men who believed, before they had cleaned up one thing.
  3. God's Word“To this agree the words of the prophets” 15:15When we disagree, we go to the Scriptures, not to our feelings.
  4. Freedom and love“We trouble not them, which… are turned to God” 15:19Grace takes burdens off. Out of love we still stay away from what harms us and trips our brothers.
  5. Disagreement“The contention was so sharp between them” 15:39Good men disagree. God used both teams, so don't let a quarrel end the work.
  6. Second chances“Take Mark… he is profitable to me” 2 Tim. 4:11One failure isn't the end of the story. Mark went from quitter to useful.

Question 1 of 6

Salvation

What does Peter say saves us?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, received by faith. Nothing is added: no circumcision, no keeping of the law, no cleaning up first. Jew and Gentile alike are saved the same way, and it is a gift.

Question 2 of 6

Proof

How did God show He had accepted the Gentiles?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He gave them the Holy Ghost, just as He had given Him to the Jewish believers, when they heard and believed. And He worked miracles and wonders among them through Paul and Barnabas. God saw their hearts, and He put His seal on them.

Question 3 of 6

God's Word

How did James settle the argument?

What verses tell us about this?

“In that day will I raise up the tabernacle of David that is fallen, and close up the breaches thereof; and I will raise up his ruins, and I will build it as in the days of old:”

Amos 9:11

Answer

He pointed to the prophets. God had said through Amos that He would rebuild David's fallen house so that all the Gentiles would seek the Lord. What God was doing at Antioch was what He had promised all along. The Word settles what feelings can't.

Question 4 of 6

Freedom and love

Why did the council ask for only a few things?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because salvation was already settled by grace, so they wouldn't “trouble” the Gentiles with more. The few things they asked were about love and a clean break from the old life: don't offend your Jewish brothers, and stay away from idols and sexual sin. They were not conditions for being saved.

Question 5 of 6

Disagreement

What happened when Paul and Barnabas disagreed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The quarrel was so sharp that they went separate ways. Barnabas took Mark to Cyprus, and Paul took Silas through Syria and Cilicia. It was a painful split, but God used both teams, and the churches were strengthened. Disagreement didn't end the work.

Question 6 of 6

Second chances

Was John Mark finished after he walked out on the work?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now when Paul and his company loosed from Paphos, they came to Perga in Pamphylia: and John departing from them returned to Jerusalem.”

Acts 13:13

“Only Luke is with me. Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry.”

2 Timothy 4:11

Answer

No. Mark quit once, and even Paul wrote him off for a time. But Barnabas gave him another chance, and years later Paul asked for him by name. A failure is not the end of the story. If you fell yesterday, get back up today.
