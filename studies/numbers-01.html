Numbers 1

Take ye the sum of all the congregation

A year after the Red sea, at the foot of Mount Sinai, the LORD tells Moses to count the men of Israel, every man by his name. Twelve tribes, one camp, and a God who knows who is in it.

The book of Numbers

Where: it opens “in the wilderness of Sinai” (1:1), moves on to the wilderness of Paran (10:12), and ends “in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho” (36:13).

Who wrote it: Moses. “Moses wrote their goings out according to their journeys by the commandment of the LORD” (33:2). Jesus speaks of Moses in the wilderness: “as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness” (John 3:14).

To whom: Israel, redeemed out of Egypt, now to be counted and set in order for the march.

It is called Numbers for the two numberings, here and in chapter 26.

Three places, forty years

  1. The wilderness of Sinaichapters 1 to 10: the people counted, set in order, made ready
  2. The wilderness of Paran10:12 on: the march, and the spies sent out (13:3)
  3. The plains of Moab“by Jordan near Jericho” (36:13): the second numbering, and the land in sight

Mount Hor stands “in the edge of the land of Edom” (33:37), and Aaron died there. Mount Nebo is “over against Jericho” (Deuteronomy 34:1), where Moses saw the land. The map shows only the places the text itself fixes.

When: from the second year to the fortieth

  1. 15th, 1st monthOut of EgyptNumbers 33:3
  2. 3rd monthIsrael comes to SinaiExodus 19:1
  3. 1st day, 1st month, 2nd yearThe tabernacle reared upExodus 40:17
  4. 1st day, 2nd month, 2nd yearNumbers begins: the people numberedNumbers 1:1
  5. 20th day, 2nd month, 2nd yearThe cloud lifts; they marchNumbers 10:11
  6. KadeshThe spies; the people refuse to go inNumbers 13–14
  7. 1st day, 5th month, 40th yearAaron dies on Mount HorNumbers 33:38
  8. 40th year, 11th monthMoses speaks in the plains of MoabDeuteronomy 1:3

The tabernacle was reared up on the first day of the first month; Numbers opens a month later, on the first day of the second month. The sentence of forty years falls at Kadesh: “each day for a year” (14:34). From Kadesh-barnea until they crossed the brook Zered was “thirty and eight years” (Deuteronomy 2:14).

The shape of the book

  1. 1 to 10:10: at Sinaicounted, camped, cleansed and made ready to march
  2. 10:11 to 14: the march, and Kadeshthe murmuring, the spies, and a people that would not go in
  3. 15 to 20: the wandering yearsKorah, Aaron's rod, the red heifer, the rock
  4. 21 to 36: on to the plains of Moabthe serpent of brass, Balaam, Baal-peor, the second numbering, the land divided

Two numberings frame the book: the first in this chapter, a generation that would fall in the wilderness, and the second in chapter 26, a new one. Paul reads that whole story as a warning and a lesson for us:

“Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come.”

1 Corinthians 10:11

The big idea is that God leads, feeds and keeps a murmuring people, and that unbelief keeps a man out of the rest God has promised. But God keeps his word to the children.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

  1. Counted by nameWho did the LORD tell Moses to count, and how were they to be counted?
  2. A man with youWho was to stand with Moses and Aaron when they counted?
  3. The totalHow many men were numbered in all, and which tribe was the largest?
  4. Left outWhich tribe was not numbered, and what were they given to do?
  5. No wrathWhy were the Levites to camp round about the tabernacle?
  6. As commandedWhat did the children of Israel do with all that the LORD commanded?

Part one

Take ye the sum

Verses 1–19

Every man by name, and a man from every tribe to stand beside Moses.

Hard words in verses 2 to 4

  1. sumthe total, the whole count
  2. congregationthe whole gathered people
  3. pollsheads: “every male by their polls” is every male, one head at a time
  4. the house of their fathersa man's father's family
  5. armiesthe divisions of the host; Israel is counted as an army

The chapter says “by their polls” four times, and “the number of their names” again and again. This is not a crowd counted in lumps. It is one man after another, each with his name.

Counted, and ransomed

This was not Israel's first count. A year before, on the mount, when he showed Moses the pattern of the tabernacle, the LORD had told him what to do whenever the people were numbered:

“When thou takest the sum of the children of Israel after their number, then shall they give every man a ransom for his soul unto the LORD, when thou numberest them; that there be no plague among them, when thou numberest them.”

Exodus 30:12

The price was half a shekel, and it was the same for everyone: “The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less” (Exodus 30:15). Every man bought his own soul back with the same coin as the man beside him.

The same number, a year before

“A bekah for every man, that is, half a shekel, after the shekel of the sanctuary, for every one that went to be numbered, from twenty years old and upward, for six hundred thousand and three thousand and five hundred and fifty men.”

Exodus 38:26

The very same number: 603,550 (Numbers 1:46). Every man on this list had a ransom paid for his soul. Rich or poor, nobody was counted without the same ransom paid for him. Peter says what ours was: “the precious blood of Christ” (1 Peter 1:19).

One man from every tribe, “head of the house of his fathers” (verse 4), twelve in all (verse 44). Moses and Aaron did not do the counting alone. Look at Judah's man: Nahshon the son of Amminadab. The New Testament spells the names the Greek way, Naasson the son of Aminadab, and he stands in the line of Jesus (Matthew 1:4).

Hard words in verses 16 to 19

  1. renownedwell known, men of name and standing
  2. princesthe heads, the chiefs of their tribes
  3. heads of thousandsthe men over the thousands of their tribe
  4. expressed by their namescalled out by name, one by one
  5. pedigreesfamily lines: each man told who his father was, and his father's father

They “declared their pedigrees” (verse 18): each man stood up and said whose son he was and to which family he belonged. No man was counted by guess.

Who stands with you?

Moses and Aaron are the two greatest men in the camp, and the LORD still gives them twelve men to stand with them. Nobody was meant to do this alone.

“Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.”

Proverbs 27:17

“Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.”

Galatians 6:2

A man in recovery learns this the hard way. The man who tries to stand alone is the first one to fall. If you have a pastor and a few brothers in Christ you could call at midnight, thank God for them. If you do not, make that your first job today: pick one man and tell him the truth.

Part two

The twelve tribes

Verses 20–46

Every man from twenty years old and upward, able to go to war.

The same words, twelve times

Every tribe is counted in nearly the same words. The chapter says “from twenty years old and upward” fifteen times, and “able to go forth to war” fourteen times. Read them slowly:

  1. by their generationsby their births: each man in his own line
  2. after their familiesby his family
  3. by the house of their fathersby his father's house
  4. according to the number of the namesone name at a time
  5. twenty years old and upwarda grown man, old enough to fight

Verse 20 says “Israel’s eldest son”: Reuben was the firstborn, and he is counted first. No tribe is left off.

Twelve tribes, tribe by tribe

An old number: threescore is sixty, so “threescore and fourteen thousand” is 74,000. Each bar is the men of war of one tribe, in the order of the chapter.

Judah is the largest, Manasseh the smallest. Jacob had taken Joseph's two sons as his own, Ephraim and Manasseh (Genesis 48:5), so there are still twelve tribes, with Levi left out.

603,550 men

603,550men of war, twenty years old and upward

The twelve numbers, added together, make 603,550, exactly what verse 46 says. A year before, as they left Egypt, the Bible said “about six hundred thousand on foot that were men, beside children” (Exodus 12:37). With the women, the children and the Levites, the whole camp was far larger. That is the host the LORD led, fed and kept in a dry and empty land.

Keep this number in mind. Of all these men who were numbered here, only two would go into the land (Numbers 26:64–65). The book is about how that happened, and about the children who went in after them.

Counted at Sinai, counted again in Moab

603,550numbered at Sinai (1:46)
2of them went in: Caleb and Joshua (26:65)
601,730numbered in Moab (26:51)

“But with whom was he grieved forty years? was it not with them that had sinned, whose carcases fell in the wilderness? And to whom sware he that they should not enter into his rest, but to them that believed not? So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief.”

Hebrews 3:17–19

That is what this chapter's list is for. The men who stand on it will fall. Yet the LORD said of their children, “them will I bring in” (Numbers 14:31). If you have thrown away years, this is for you: God did not throw away his people. He kept his word to the next generation, and he keeps it to a man who turns back to him.

An army that is counted

Israel is numbered as an army, and the chapter says so fourteen times: “able to go forth to war.” They are ransomed men, and now they are soldiers. The New Testament gives the same word to every believer.

“Thou therefore endure hardness, as a good soldier of Jesus Christ.”

2 Timothy 2:3

“Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand.”

Ephesians 6:13

The new believer is not only a man who was forgiven. He has been enlisted. The enemy that wants him back is real, and he does not fight alone: there is a camp, there are standards, and there is a man standing next to him. Chapter 2 will show how the camp was laid out.

Part three

The Levites

Verses 47–54

The tribe that was not counted among the soldiers, but set round the holy place.

Hard words in verses 50 to 54

  1. tabernacle of testimonythe tent where the ark of the testimony, with the two tables of the law, was kept
  2. ministerserve; the Levites serve in the tabernacle
  3. encamp, pitchto set up tents, to camp
  4. the stranger that cometh nighany man outside the priests' and Levites' family who came near the holy things
  5. standarda banner or flag each tribe camped under (chapter 2 has them all)
  6. hostsarmies, the great divisions of the camp
  7. wraththe anger of the LORD against sin

A ring round the holy place

The Levites are left out of the army count so that they can be set round the tabernacle: they carry it, take it down and set it up, and camp close about it, “that there be no wrath upon the congregation”. Between a holy God and a sinful people stood a living wall of men appointed for the work. Verse 51 puts it plainly: the stranger who came near was put to death. Nobody walked into the holy place on his own.

That was a real danger, and it shows why the road to God had to be opened for us. Now look at what the New Testament says about that road:

“But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.”

Ephesians 2:13

The Levites kept the guard so that no man was struck dead. The blood of Christ makes a man nigh. The holiness has not changed. The way has.

A good beginning

The chapter ends with obedience: “the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so did they.” They counted, they named, they stood with their princes.

It does not last. In the middle of the book the same people will be crying for the food of Egypt, and asking for a captain to take them back there (11:5; 14:4). We should not be shocked. A good beginning is a gift from God, but it is not a guarantee. Paul warns the man who thinks he is safe: “Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall” (1 Corinthians 10:12).

If you have fallen since your good beginning, you are not the first. The next thirty-five chapters are full of men who did, and of a God who did not leave them.

Be counted, today

The LORD knew every man in that camp by his name, and he knows yours. You are not a number to him, even in a chapter that is full of them.

  1. Sinname it plainly to God. He has already seen it.
  2. Regretdo not let it drag you out of the camp. A man can lose the first place and keep his name.
  3. Graceevery man here was counted with a ransom paid. Yours was paid with the blood of Christ.
  4. Hopethe children went in. Today is not the end of your story.
  5. Todaychoose one man to stand with you, and tell him one true thing you are carrying

“To him the porter openeth; and the sheep hear his voice: and he calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out.”

John 10:3

“Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.”

Luke 10:20

Points to remember

  1. Counted by name“with the number of their names, every male by their polls” 1:2God counts men one by one, by name.
  2. A man with you“these are the names of the men that shall stand with you” 1:5Nobody was meant to stand alone. Find your man.
  3. The total“six hundred thousand and three thousand and five hundred and fifty” 1:46603,550 men, every one ransomed. Judah the largest.
  4. Left out“thou shalt appoint the Levites over the tabernacle of testimony” 1:50Not soldiers but servants of the holy place.

Question 1 of 6

Counted by name

Who did the LORD tell Moses to count, and how were they to be counted?

What verses tell us about this?

“To him the porter openeth; and the sheep hear his voice: and he calleth his own sheep by name, and leadeth them out.”

John 10:3

Answer

All the congregation of Israel: every male from twenty years old and upward, able to go to war, counted by his family, his father's house and his name, one head at a time. God does not count men in lumps.

Question 2 of 6

A man with you

Who was to stand with Moses and Aaron when they counted?

What verses tell us about this?

“Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.”

Proverbs 27:17

Answer

A man of every tribe, the head of his father's house: twelve princes, each named, with his father's name. Moses and Aaron were not left to do it alone, and neither are we.

Question 3 of 6

The total

How many men were numbered in all, and which tribe was the largest?

What verses tell us about this?

“A bekah for every man, that is, half a shekel, after the shekel of the sanctuary, for every one that went to be numbered, from twenty years old and upward, for six hundred thousand and three thousand and five hundred and fifty men.”

Exodus 38:26

Answer

603,550 men of war, and Judah was the largest with 74,600. Every one of them had a ransom paid for his soul.

Question 4 of 6

Left out

Which tribe was not numbered, and what were they given to do?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come.”

1 Corinthians 10:11

Answer

The tribe of Levi. They were not counted among the soldiers but appointed over the tabernacle: to bear it, to take it down, to set it up, and to camp round about it.

Question 5 of 6

No wrath

Why were the Levites to camp round about the tabernacle?

What verses tell us about this?

“But now in Christ Jesus ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh by the blood of Christ.”

Ephesians 2:13

Answer

“That there be no wrath upon the congregation.” The LORD is holy, and a sinful people could not walk up to his tent. The Levites kept the guard. Now the blood of Christ makes a man nigh.

Question 6 of 6

As commanded

What did the children of Israel do with all that the LORD commanded?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand.”

Ephesians 6:13

Answer

They did it: “according to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so did they.” It was a good beginning, and the book will show how easily a good beginning is lost. The answer is not to stop beginning. It is to keep standing, with the armour of God and a man at your side.
