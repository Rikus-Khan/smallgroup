Exodus 14

Stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD

The sea in front, Pharaoh's army behind, and nowhere to go. God has led them here on purpose. Then he divides the sea, and the people who were slaves walk across on dry ground.

Part one

“Encamp before Pi-hahiroth”

Verses 1–9

God turns his people toward a dead end, and tells them why before they get there.

Down to the sea

At the end of a long wadi the road comes out on the beach at Nuweiba, on the Gulf of Aqaba. The map follows the route as Ron Wyatt draws it. Pi-hahiroth, Migdol and Baal-zephon are the names of the places at the shore. Hills behind, water in front.

Across the water is Arabia, the land of Midian, and Mount Sinai: the mountain where Moses met the burning bush. God was bringing them to the place he had promised (3:12).

On purpose

What Pharaoh will think

“Entangled”

“They are entangled in the land, the wilderness hath shut them in.” Entangled means hemmed in, trapped, like a net. A people with the sea in front and hills on the sides.

What the LORD is doing

“I will be honoured”

Honoured here means glorified: he will show who he is. “That the Egyptians may know that I am the LORD.”

Israel is not lost. They are exactly where God put them. If you have ever found yourself at a dead end, nowhere to turn, with the old trouble coming up behind you, remember that a dead end is where a man learns whether God is able. This one was a trap that God had set for Pharaoh, not for them.

“I will harden Pharaoh’s heart”

Read these in the order the book gives them. God said it beforehand, but Pharaoh's own choices come first, again and again, and then God confirms him in what he chose.

  1. 4:21God says it will come“I will harden his heart”
  2. 8:15Pharaoh hardens itwhen he saw there was respite
  3. 8:32Pharaoh again“at this time also”
  4. 9:12The LORD hardens itafter Pharaoh's choice
  5. 9:34Pharaoh, once more“he sinned yet more”
  6. 10:20The LORD hardens itand again in 14:8

“But when Pharaoh saw that there was respite, he hardened his heart, and hearkened not unto them; as the LORD had said.”

Exodus 8:15

A heart does not harden all at once. It hardens one “not yet” at a time: the pressure lifts, and the man goes back on what he said. Each time it is easier. That is the warning for every man in this room. When God is speaking, do not put it off: today is the day to listen.

The old master gives chase

  • hostan army
  • chosenpicked: the best chariots and captains
  • an high handboldly, openly, with nothing to hide
  • overtookcaught up with

Pharaoh had let them go, and then he changed his mind: why have we done this? He wanted his slaves back. He took six hundred of his best chariots and all the rest.

If you have been set free from a habit, you know this. The old master does not take it well. It gets its men out and comes after you, usually when you are tired, and usually when you have your back to the sea. But look: Israel went out with an high hand. They were not sneaking out. God had freed them in front of everybody.

“Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son: In whom we have redemption through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins:”

Colossians 1:13–14

Part two

“Fear ye not, stand still”

Verses 10–18

They cry out to God, and then turn on Moses. Moses gives them four words, and God gives them one more.

“Sore afraid”

Sore here means very. The cry in verse 10 was right: “the children of Israel cried out unto the LORD.” That was the right thing to do with fear. Then the cry turned sour.

the peopleBecause there were no graves in Egypt, hast thou taken us away to die in the wilderness?

the people, a moment laterIt had been better for us to serve the Egyptians, than that we should die in the wilderness.
the old chains, remembered kindly

Egypt had whips and burdens and murdered children (1:22). But when the army appeared, slavery began to look like home. Every man who has quit something knows that voice. Do not trust a memory that comes only when you are scared. Call a brother in Christ instead.

They would say it again

“And they said one to another, Let us make a captain, and let us return into Egypt.”

Numbers 14:4

“Neither murmur ye, as some of them also murmured, and were destroyed of the destroyer.”

1 Corinthians 10:10

Here at the sea the people were only afraid. About a year later, at Kadesh, they would say it again, this time as a decision, and it cost a whole generation the land. A wish to go back is a thing to take to God and to your brothers, while it is still only a wish.

“There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it.”

1 Corinthians 10:13

Four words from Moses

Fear not. Stand still.

  • Fear ye notthey were right to be afraid, and told not to be ruled by it
  • Stand stilldo not run, do not turn back, do not fight it yourself
  • See“the salvation of the LORD”: watch him do it
  • Hold your peacebe quiet: stop the complaining

“The LORD shall fight for you.” The one thing the people could not do was save themselves, and they were told so. This is what grace looks like: the work belongs to him.

“Be still, and know that I am God: I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth.”

Psalm 46:10

Stand still, and then go forward

FirstStand still. See the LORD act.
→
thenGo forward. Lift the rod.

Moses had just told the people to stand still. Now God says, wherefore criest thou unto me? speak unto the children of Israel, that they go forward. Praying is right, and the time comes to move. They were to walk toward the water before it opened.

The rod was only a shepherd's staff, the one from the burning bush (4:2–4). God would use what Moses already held. The sea was divided by God, not by wood, but Moses had to lift it. A man who has prayed about the call he needs to make, the meeting he must attend or the apology he owes, may have to lift the rod. Do the next thing, and let God divide the sea.

Part three

“The waters were a wall unto them”

Verses 19–31

A night between two armies, a road through the sea, and a morning with nothing behind them.

Between the camps

The pillar that had led them from in front now moved behind them and stood between. To the Egyptians it was cloud and darkness. To Israel it gave light. “So that the one came not near the other all the night.” Pharaoh's army could not take one step closer.

The same LORD stands between the believer and what is chasing him. He is both the light and the wall. Nothing gets to you that has not first got past him.

Across the sea

All that night the LORD sent a strong east wind and the water went back. By morning Israel was on the other side, in Arabia. This is a deep sea, and it was crossed on a road God made. Nobody on that beach did it by their own strength.

Notice the time: “all that night.” The sea did not open in a flash. They had hours of waiting, with the wind in their faces and the wall of water on both sides, and they had to keep walking.

On dry ground, in the midst of the sea

“The waters were a wall unto them on their right hand, and on their left.” A wall on each side, and between them a floor of dry ground. Israel walked the whole way.

“By faith they passed through the Red sea as by dry land: which the Egyptians assaying to do were drowned.”

Hebrews 11:29

“The LORD fighteth for them”

  • morning watchthe last part of the night, before dawn
  • troubledthrew them into confusion
  • dravedrove: “they drave them heavily” means the chariots dragged and jammed
  • fightethfights

Only now, with the sea open and the host in the middle of it, does the LORD look out through the pillar. The chariots that were their pride jammed in the soft ground. The Egyptians did not need a prophet to tell them what was happening. They said it themselves: Let us flee from the face of Israel; for the LORD fighteth for them.

Even Pharaoh's men could see it. The people who had hardened themselves against every sign finally spoke the truth, and too late. Do not wait for the water to come back before you admit who is fighting for you.

The waters return

At Moses' word the sea “returned to his strength when the morning appeared.” The walls came together, and the host of Pharaoh that had come in after Israel was covered.

Hard to read, and just

This is hard to read, and it should be. These were men, and each one had a family. But it is also justice. This was the army of Egypt, the nation that had thrown Israel's baby boys into the river, at a king's order:

“And Pharaoh charged all his people, saying, Every son that is born ye shall cast into the river, and every daughter ye shall save alive.”

Exodus 1:22

God had been patient through every plague, and Pharaoh had refused every time. The sea closed on the army that was chasing a people it meant to bring back in chains.

“Thus the LORD saved Israel that day”

Israel saw the Egyptians dead on the shore. The enemy that had been behind them for four hundred years was not there anymore. They had not fought. The LORD had.

“And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage.”

Hebrews 2:15

That is what the cross does for a man. The one who held us in bondage is beaten, and the chains do not belong to us anymore. The old master may still shout from the shore, but he cannot cross the water.

And then: “the people feared the LORD, and believed the LORD, and his servant Moses.” Feared means respected, stood in awe. It took the sea to make them believe, and a man's belief often starts with what he has seen God do.

Baptized in the cloud and in the sea

“Moreover, brethren, I would not that ye should be ignorant, how that all our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea; And were all baptized unto Moses in the cloud and in the sea;”

1 Corinthians 10:1–2

Paul reads the crossing as a baptism. Under the cloud, through the sea, and the old life stayed on the other side. It joined the people to Moses, their rescuer and leader.

“Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.”

Romans 6:4

A believer goes down into the water and comes up on the other side. The old life is buried, and a new walk begins. If you have been baptized, that is what it meant. If you have not, talk to your pastor.

Real world: after the sea

“When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow thee: when thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned; neither shall the flame kindle upon thee.”

Isaiah 43:2

Israel's story today is yours. You will have a day when the sea is in front and the old trouble is behind, and your back is to the wall. Then:

  • Cry outto the LORD, and call your brothers in Christ and your pastor
  • Do not go backthe old life only looks kind when you are afraid
  • Stand stilland let him do what only he can do
  • Go forwardand take the next step he shows you

“What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us?”

Romans 8:31

One warning, for the shore

“Then believed they his words; they sang his praise. They soon forgat his works; they waited not for his counsel:”

Psalm 106:12–13

The very next chapter, Israel sings. And within three days they are complaining about the water. A man can stand on the far shore and swear he will never doubt God again, and the next week he is back to murmuring. That is not the end of the story, and God does not walk away from them. But it is a warning to keep remembering, just as chapter 13 said.

If you have forgotten, and you have fallen since the day you were set free, the sea does not close on you. The one who parted the water is the one who forgives (1 John 1:9). Come back.

Points to remember

  1. God's plan“I will be honoured upon Pharaoh, and upon all his host; that the Egyptians may know that I am the LORD” 14:4The dead end was God's doing. He leads us there to show who he is.
  2. Fear“It had been better for us to serve the Egyptians, than that we should die in the wilderness” 14:12Fear makes the old chains look kind. Do not trust it.
  3. Grace“Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD” 14:13The LORD fights for you. Stand still, then go forward.
  4. The way through“the children of Israel went into the midst of the sea upon the dry ground” 14:22God makes a road where there was none, and we walk it by faith.
  5. Justice“there remained not so much as one of them” 14:28The enemy that held Israel was gone, and God's patience with Pharaoh had a limit.
  6. Faith“the people feared the LORD, and believed the LORD, and his servant Moses” 14:31They saw, and they believed. Keep remembering what you have seen him do.

Question 1 of 6

God's plan

Why did the LORD lead Israel to camp by the sea?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So that Pharaoh would follow, and the LORD would be honoured: “that the Egyptians may know that I am the LORD.” Israel was not lost. They were exactly where God put them. If you feel stuck at a dead end, he knows where you are.

Question 2 of 6

Fear

What did the people say when they saw Pharaoh coming?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They cried to the LORD, which was right, then blamed Moses and wished they were back in slavery. Fear can make old chains look like home. When it does, call your brothers in Christ instead of going back.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

What did Moses tell them to do, and what would the LORD do?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Fear not, stand still, and see: the LORD would fight for them. Then, when God said so, they went forward. We cannot save ourselves. The work is his, and then he asks us to take the next step.

Question 4 of 6

The way through

How did Israel cross the sea?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On dry ground, through the midst of the sea, with a wall of water on each side. The LORD opened it with a strong east wind, and Israel walked through by faith. God can make a road where there is no road.

Question 5 of 6

Justice

What happened to the Egyptians who followed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God troubled their host, and the sea came back and covered them. It is hard to read, but it is justice on the army of the nation that had drowned Israel's sons. The enemy that held Israel was gone.

Question 6 of 6

Faith

What did the people do when they saw the great work?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They feared the LORD and believed him and his servant Moses. They saw what he did, and they trusted him. Keep what you have seen him do in front of you for the next hard day.
