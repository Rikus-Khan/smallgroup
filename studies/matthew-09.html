Matthew 9

The Physician

Part one

Power to forgive

Verses 1–8

A man who can't move, the friends who carry him, and a word nobody expected.

Back home to Capernaum

“His own city” is Capernaum, a fishing town on the north shore of the Sea of Galilee. Jesus moved there from Nazareth when His ministry began (4:13). Peter's house was there (8:14).

He had just crossed back from the east shore, where He set two men free from devils (8:28–34). Mark says the crowd was so thick the friends opened the roof (Mark 2:4).

Carried by his friends

four friendscarrieda man who couldn't movetoJesus

“And they come unto him, bringing one sick of the palsy, which was borne of four.”

Mark 2:3

“Palsy” means paralysis. He could not walk to Jesus. Jesus saw their faith, the men who carried him. Some of us got to Jesus because somebody carried us: a friend, a sponsor, a mother who never quit praying. And some days you're the one carrying.

He came hoping to walk. Jesus dealt first with the deeper thing: “Son… thy sins be forgiven thee.”

Which is easier to say?

Anyone can say

“Thy sins be forgiven thee”

Nobody in the room can see whether it happened. Easy to say, and impossible to check.

Everyone can check

“Arise, and walk”

Every eye in the house would know in a second whether it worked.

The scribes called it blasphemy, and they had one thing right: “who can forgive sins but God only?” (Mark 2:7). So Jesus did the thing they could see to prove the thing they couldn't. If the man walks, the forgiveness is real, and so is the One who gave it.

Take up thy bed

carried on his bed → carrying it home The mat that had carried him for years, he now carried out the door, in front of everyone who knew him.

“Power” here means authority, the right to do it. Jesus has the authority, here “on earth,” to forgive sins. Not someday. Today, to a man lying in front of Him.

The thing that used to hold you down can become the thing you carry home as your story. The crowd saw it and “glorified God.”

Part two

Follow me

Verses 9–17

A tax collector walks away from his booth and throws a dinner for his friends.

The man at the tax booth

The “receipt of custom” was a toll booth. Capernaum sat on a trade road near the edge of Herod Antipas' land, and goods passing through paid duty. Publicans bought the right to collect taxes, then charged whatever they could get. Their own people counted them as traitors and thieves.

Levi the publican → Matthew Mark and Luke call him Levi (Mark 2:14, Luke 5:27). He wrote this book, and in his own list of the twelve he still calls himself “Matthew the publican” (10:3). He never forgot where Jesus found him.

Two words, “Follow me,” and he got up. A fisherman could always go back to his boat. A publican who walked off his booth probably couldn't get it back. Luke says he “left all” (Luke 5:28).

Dinner with the wrong crowd

Luke tells us this was a big dinner Levi threw “in his own house” (Luke 5:29). The first thing Matthew did was bring his old crowd to meet Jesus. “Sat at meat” means sat down to a meal.

Jesuspublicans and sinners“came and sat down with him and his disciples”

The Pharisees were strict keepers of the law and of their own purity rules. To share a table with someone was to accept him. A holy man, they reasoned, keeps away from people like that.

Notice who they asked: not Jesus, but His disciples. Talking behind His back.

Doctors go to the sick.

Jesus didn't eat with sinners because He approved of the sin. He went to them the way a physician goes to a patient: to make them well.

Hosea 6:6

For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice; and the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings.

Matthew 9:13

But go ye and learn what that meaneth, I will have mercy, and not sacrifice…

“Go ye and learn” was a teacher's phrase. Jesus sends the Bible experts back to their Bible. And you don't have to get well before you come to Him. Coming sick is the only way anybody comes.

You don't fast at a wedding

To fast is to go without food to pray and mourn. Strict Pharisees fasted twice a week (Luke 18:12). The “children of the bridechamber” are the groom's friends. A Jewish wedding feast could last a week, and nobody went hungry at it.

  1. nowthe bridegroom is with themfeast, not fast
  2. the crosstaken from themchapters 26–27
  3. after“then shall they fast”Acts 13:2–3
  4. one daythe Bridegroom returns25:1–13

Jesus is the Bridegroom. “Taken from them” is the first quiet hint in Matthew that He is going to die. Fasting still has its place for believers. Jesus said “when ye fast,” not “if” (6:16).

New wine, new skins

“Bottles” were goatskin bags. Fresh grape juice is still working, and it swells. A new skin stretches with it. An old, dried-out one splits, and you lose both.

It's a picture from everyday life, not a word about drinking. His new life can't be stitched onto the old one like a patch. We tried patching: a new rule, a new job, a new town. He makes a new man.

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Part three

Made whole

Verses 18–26

A father with a dead girl, a woman nobody would touch, and a crowd that laughed.

A ruler on his knees

Mark and Luke give his name: Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue (Mark 5:22), one of the leading men who ran the services in town. Plenty of synagogue men were against Jesus. This one “worshipped him,” down on his knees in front of everybody.

if You will“lay thy hand upon her”
→
then“she shall live”

Grief strips the pride off a man. He didn't care any more who saw him begging. And Jesus “arose, and followed him.” He never turns down a desperate father.

Twelve years

the ruler's daughter: her whole life, about twelve years (Luke 8:42)

the woman: twelve years bleeding, and broke

The same twelve years. While one family watched a daughter grow up, this woman had been sick, alone, and paying doctors who couldn't help.

“And had suffered many things of many physicians, and had spent all that she had, and was nothing bettered, but rather grew worse,”

Mark 5:26

She touched Him, and she was clean

Under the law, a woman with that kind of bleeding was unclean, and so was anyone she touched (Leviticus 15:25–27). For twelve years she had been kept out of worship and away from people. She wasn't supposed to be in that crowd at all.

the law said→her touch makes others unclean
with Jesus→His touch made her whole

The “hem” was the tassel on the corner of a Jewish man's cloak (Numbers 15:38). The power wasn't in the cloth. It was in Him, and her faith reached for Him. Then He called her “Daughter.” A woman shut out for twelve years was family.

Not dead, but sleeping

The “minstrels” were flute players hired for a funeral. Mourning was loud and public. By the time Jesus got there, the funeral had already started.

The mourners

“dead”

The end. Nothing left to do but weep. So when Jesus said otherwise, they laughed at Him.

Jesus

“sleepeth”

For Him, death is like sleep: you wake up from it. He took her by the hand, and she got up.

“For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him.”

1 Thessalonians 4:14

Part four

Sheep having no shepherd

Verses 27–38

Blind eyes open, a silent man speaks, and Jesus looks at the crowds and hurts for them.

Blind men who saw who He was

Who did they say He was?Son of DavidThe promised King from David's family (2 Samuel 7:12–13). Matthew opens his book with it (1:1). Two blind men saw what the scholars missed.
“Believe ye that I am able?”Yea, LordThat's all the faith He asked for. Not a speech, not a clean record. Just: You can do this.

“Straitly charged” means strictly ordered. Why keep it quiet? Crowds wanted a Son of David to drive out Rome, and loose talk could start a riot before His time. They told everybody anyway.

Signs Isaiah said to watch for

About seven hundred years earlier, Isaiah described the day God Himself would come to save His people: “he will come and save you” (Isaiah 35:4). Then he said what it would look like.

  1. ✓the lame man leap as an hartIsaiah 35:69:7
  2. ✓the eyes of the blind shall be openedIsaiah 35:59:30
  3. ✓the tongue of the dumb singIsaiah 35:69:33

The same miracle, two verdicts. The crowd: “never so seen.” The Pharisees: it's the devil. A heart that has made up its mind can always find another explanation.

All the cities and villages

Josephus, a Jewish historian of that time, counted about two hundred towns and villages in Galilee. Jesus walked to them, on foot, one after another.

Three things everywhere He went: teaching in the synagogues, preaching the good news that God's kingdom had come near, and healing every kind of sickness. Matthew said the same about the start of His work (4:23).

Moved with compassion

σπλαγχνίζομαι said splanchnizomai moved deep in the gut

The Greek word comes from the word for a man's insides, his heart and gut. It's what you feel when you see your own child hurt. That is how Jesus looked at the crowds.

He didn't look at them with disgust, or with a list of what they'd done. He hurt for them. That includes you on your worst day.

Worn out and scattered

“Fainted” means worn out, harassed. “Scattered abroad” means thrown down, like sheep dropped in a field with nobody to lead them to water or keep the wolves off.

Moses once prayed God would give Israel a leader, so they would not be “as sheep which have no shepherd” (Numbers 27:17). Jesus is the Shepherd that prayer was waiting for.

Pray for workers

the harvest“plenteous… the labourers are few”
→
therefore“Pray ye… the Lord of the harvest”

A ripe field won't wait. Leave it too long, and the crop is lost. Jesus looks at worn-out people and sees a harvest ready to bring in. It's His harvest, and He is “the Lord of the harvest.”

Watch what happens next. In the very next verses, Jesus sends out the same men He told to pray (10:1, 5). Often the answer to that prayer is the man praying it. There are men still sitting where you used to sit. Your story is a tool in His harvest.

Points to remember

  1. Forgiveness“The Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins” 9:6He proved it by making a paralyzed man walk. Every sin can be forgiven.
  2. Mercy“They that be whole need not a physician” 9:12Jesus came for sick people. You don't get well first. You come sick.
  3. New life“They put new wine into new bottles” 9:17He doesn't patch the old life. He makes a new man.
  4. Faith“Thy faith hath made thee whole” 9:22Faith reaches for Him: carried by friends, through a crowd, on your knees.
  5. Compassion“He was moved with compassion on them” 9:36He looks at worn-out, scattered people and hurts for them.
  6. Prayer“Pray ye therefore the Lord of the harvest” 9:38Pray for workers, and be ready to be the answer.

Question 1 of 6

Forgiveness

How did Jesus show He has power to forgive sins?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Anyone can say “thy sins be forgiven,” and nobody can see it. So Jesus did what everyone could see: He told a paralyzed man to walk, and he did. That proved the forgiveness was real, and that Jesus has God's own authority to forgive sins.

Question 2 of 6

Mercy

Why did Jesus eat with publicans and sinners?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

For the same reason a doctor goes to sick people: to make them well. He didn't approve of their sin. He came to call sinners to repentance, and God wants mercy more than religious show. Nobody has to clean up before coming to Him.

Question 3 of 6

New life

Why doesn't Jesus just patch up the old life?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

A patch doesn't hold. New cloth tears an old coat, and new wine splits an old skin. Jesus doesn't improve the old man. He makes a new one.

Question 4 of 6

Faith

What did faith look like in the people Jesus healed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Friends carrying a man, a ruler on his knees, a sick woman reaching through a crowd, two blind men. Each came to Jesus trusting He was able. Faith isn't feeling strong. It's reaching for Him.

Question 5 of 6

Compassion

How does Jesus feel when He looks at hurting people?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He is moved deep inside, the way a father hurts for his own child. He sees people worn out and scattered like sheep with no shepherd, and He goes to them, town after town. He doesn't look at us with disgust. He looks at us with compassion.

Question 6 of 6

Prayer

What does Jesus tell us to pray for?

What verses tell us about this?

“And when he had called unto him his twelve disciples, he gave them power against unclean spirits, to cast them out, and to heal all manner of sickness and all manner of disease.”

Matthew 10:1

Answer

For workers. The harvest is huge and the workers few, so we ask the Lord of the harvest to send more. Then He sent the very men who prayed. Be ready to be the one He sends.
