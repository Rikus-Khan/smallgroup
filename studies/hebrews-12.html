Chapter 11 walked through the hall of faith, from Abel to the martyrs, and ended: they “received not the promise… that they without us should not be made perfect.”

Now the writer turns to us. They ran their race. Here's how to run yours.

Part one

The race

Verses 1–4

The stands are full of people who finished. Drop what slows you down, and keep your eyes on the One at the finish line.

A stadium of witnesses

The stands hold chapter 11's people. They aren't booing your mistakes; their lives testify that God got them home.

So drop the weights: things that may not be sins, but slow you down.

And shake off “the sin which doth so easily beset”, the one that wraps around your legs.

Then run, eyes on Jesus. He's at both ends of the track.

Weights and the sin

“every weight”

What slows you down

Not always sin. A friend who still uses. The bar where you watch the game. Your phone at 2 a.m. The grudge you rehearse. A runner doesn't ask “Is it wrong?” He asks “Does it slow me down?”

“the sin which doth so easily beset us”

The one that trips you

Beset means to wrap around, like a long robe tangling a runner's legs. Every man knows his. It's the one you don't have to go looking for; it finds you.

Notice who lays it aside: “let us.” You do it on purpose, and you don't do it alone. Name your besetting sin to God, and to a brother you trust.

At both ends of the track

the authorHe started your faith
→
the finisherHe will finish it

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

He ran His own race to a cross, and He thought nothing of the shame because of the joy ahead: bringing you home. You don't look at the stands, at the man in the next lane, or at your feet. You look at Him.

When you're tired of fighting

The fight against sin wears a man out. Some days you're sick of resisting. The writer says: consider Him. Jesus was opposed, mocked and beaten by sinners, and He didn't quit.

“Ye have not yet resisted unto blood”: none of them had died for it yet. Jesus had. The fight is hard, but it isn't bigger than Him, and you don't fight it alone.

“For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.”

Hebrews 4:15

Part two

A Father's discipline

Verses 5–13

Hard days aren't proof God has given up on you. Sometimes they're proof He hasn't.

What chastening is

  • not punishment for your sinsJesus already took that: “the chastisement of our peace was upon him” (Isaiah 53:5)
  • not God giving up on youit's the opposite: He deals with you because He received you
  • not a father losing his temperGod isn't the man who hurt you
  • a Father training a sonChasten means to train a child: correcting, teaching, shaping

“For whom the LORD loveth he correcteth; even as a father the son in whom he delighteth.”

Proverbs 3:12

Proof you belong

In those days a son born outside the family might be fed, but nobody bothered to raise him. A real father trains his own boys. The writer's point: if God is working on you, that's because you're His.

Some of you grew up with a father who never showed up, or never cared what you did. That wasn't love. God is not absent, and He isn't indifferent to you.

“When my father and my mother forsake me, then the LORD will take me up.”

Psalm 27:10

Two kinds of fathers

“fathers of our flesh” (12:9–10)

For a few days

They corrected us “after their own pleasure”: as it seemed best to them. Some did their best. Some did it in anger, or drunk, or not at all.

“the Father of spirits”

For our profit

Never in a rage, never for His own sake. Always for your good: “that we might be partakers of his holiness.”

“Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him.”

Psalm 103:13

If “father” is a hard word for you, let God define it, not the man who raised you, or didn't.

Afterward

The Bible is honest: discipline doesn't feel good. Losing what you lost, facing what you did, the long days of getting clean. Grievous.

“Exercised” is a gym word: trained by it. Pain you run from teaches nothing. Pain you let God use grows peace in you, a little at a time.

Hands up, road straight

Hands hanging down, knees giving out: a worn-out runner. The writer is quoting Isaiah 35:3: “Strengthen ye the weak hands, and confirm the feeble knees.”

Then straighten the road. If your way home passes the liquor store or the old house, take another street. Make the path so plain that a limping man can't wander off it.

And men behind you are limping too. A straight path helps “that which is lame” be healed, not just you.

Part three

One morsel

Verses 14–17

A root nobody sees, and a man who traded his whole future for one meal.

A root of bitterness

Bitterness grows underground. Nobody sees it for years. Then it comes up: in your temper, your marriage, your kids, the men around you.

A man who nurses a grudge against his father, his ex, or the judge is drinking poison and calling it justice. And resentment has sent many men back to the bottle.

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Sold for one meal

Esau came in from the field starving and traded his rights as the firstborn son for a bowl of stew: “what profit shall this birthright do to me?” (Genesis 25:32). In that moment the bowl felt heavier.

It never was. Genesis sums it up: “thus Esau despised his birthright” (25:34). You know that moment: one drink, one hit, one night. Your whole future on one side, and one morsel on the other.

“No place of repentance”

It doesn't mean

God refused him

Esau wasn't begging God for forgiveness. He was begging his father to take back the blessing. God turns away no one who comes to Him.

It means

He couldn't undo it

The blessing had gone to Jacob, and Isaac couldn't change his mind: “yea, and he shall be blessed” (Genesis 27:33). Esau wept, but tears couldn't reverse the deal.

Some consequences stay: a record, a lost marriage, years. That's the warning, before the next morsel. But the forgiveness is never closed:

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Part four

Two mountains

Verses 18–29

Israel stood trembling at Sinai. You have come to a different mountain, and a kingdom nothing can shake.

Not that mountain

At Sinai God gave the law. The mountain smoked, the ground shook, a trumpet blasted, and anyone who touched the mountain was to be put to death. The people begged God to stop speaking. Even Moses shook.

“there were thunders and lightnings, and a thick cloud upon the mount, and the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud; so that all the people that was in the camp trembled.”

Exodus 19:16

That's what the law does to a sinner: keeps him at a distance, afraid. “But ye are come unto mount Sion.”

Where you have already come

  1. Mount Sionthe city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem
  2. Angelsan innumerable company of them
  3. The churchof the firstborn, “written in heaven”
  4. God the Judgeof all, and you're welcome before Him
  5. Just men“made perfect”: the hall of faith, home
  6. Jesusthe mediator, standing between you and God

Notice the tense: “ye are come.” Not “you might get there if you behave.” If you belong to Christ, your name is “written in heaven” already, and you stand there by Him, not by yourself.

Two kinds of blood

Genesis 4:10

Abel's blood

Cried out from the ground against his murderer: “the voice of thy brother’s blood crieth unto me from the ground.” It asked for justice.

12:24

Jesus' blood

Speaks better things. It doesn't cry out against you. It says: paid, forgiven, welcome.

Maybe there's blood, real or not, that cries out against you: someone you hurt, something you can't take back. The blood of Jesus speaks louder.

What can be shaken, will be

moneymy reputationmy healththe old lifemy plansthis world a kingdom which cannot be moved

God shook the ground at Sinai. He has promised one more shaking, of heaven and earth, and everything that can fall will fall (Haggai 2:6). Only His kingdom stays standing.

The warning, “refuse not him that speaketh,” is for the man who has heard the gospel and keeps turning away. It isn't meant to make a believer afraid he's lost his salvation. A man who belongs to Christ is held: “they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand” (John 10:28). The warning drives you to hold on to Him.

Our God is a consuming fire.

“For the LORD thy God is a consuming fire, even a jealous God.”

Deuteronomy 4:24

Moses said it to Israel, warning them against idols. God is not a buddy, not a vending machine. He is holy, and He burns up what is false.

So serve Him with “grace” (many teachers read it as thankfulness) and with “reverence and godly fear.” Not the terror of Sinai: the respect of a son for a holy Father who will never let him go.

“I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.”

Hebrews 13:5

Question 1 of 6

Witnesses

Who is the great cloud of witnesses?

What verses tell us about this?

“And these all, having obtained a good report through faith, received not the promise:”

Hebrews 11:39

Answer

The men and women of faith in chapter 11, from Abel to the martyrs. They ran their race and finished. Their lives testify that God is faithful, and they surround us like a crowd in the stands.

Question 2 of 6

The race

How are we to run the race set before us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Lay aside every weight and the sin that so easily wraps around us. Run with patience, steadily, for the long haul. And keep looking to Jesus, who started our faith and will finish it.

Question 3 of 6

Chastening

Why does God chasten His children?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because He loves us and we are His sons. He trains us for our good, so that we share His holiness. It hurts at the time, but afterward it grows the peaceable fruit of righteousness. It isn't punishment; Jesus took that.

Question 4 of 6

Esau

What did Esau lose, and why couldn't he get it back?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He sold his birthright, and then lost the blessing that went with it, all for one meal. Afterward he wept, but the blessing had been given to Jacob and Isaac couldn't take it back. Some consequences can't be undone, though God's forgiveness is open to anyone who comes.

Question 5 of 6

Mountains

What mountain have believers come to?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not Sinai, with its fire, darkness, and fear. Believers have come to mount Sion, the heavenly Jerusalem, the city of the living God, with our names written in heaven, through Jesus the mediator and His blood that speaks better things than Abel's.

Question 6 of 6

Fire

How should we serve a God who is a consuming fire?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

With grace, that is, thankfulness for a kingdom that can never be shaken, and with reverence and godly fear. Not terror, because we are His sons, but deep respect for a holy God.
