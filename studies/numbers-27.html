Numbers 27

Five sisters, a mountain, and a man to follow Moses

Five daughters ask for their father's share of the land, and the LORD says they speak right. Then Moses is told to go up and see the land he will not enter, and he asks for a shepherd for the people he loves.

Part one

“Give unto us a possession”

Verses 1–11

Five sisters at the door of the tabernacle.

Five sisters at the door

Last chapter ended with their names (26:33). Now they stand up. They go to the door of the tabernacle, where the whole leadership is: Moses, Eleazar the priest, the princes and “all the congregation”. Five women, no father, no brothers, no husbands named, and a question nobody else had asked.

Nothing in the law yet covered them. The land was to be divided to the sons, family by family (26:53–55). A man who died with no son left his name, and his share, with nobody. They do not wait to see what happens. They bring it to the people who stand before the LORD.

They have a right to be there. Zelophehad was of the tribe of Manasseh, named on this very list (26:33), and his daughters were part of Israel. God cares about the people the system forgot to plan for.

An honest case, and a humble answer

  • “in his own sin”They don't make their father out to be a hero. He died in the wilderness for his own sin, like his generation (26:64–65).
  • “not in the company of Korah”But he was not one of those who joined Korah's rebellion. They say what is true, no more.
  • “done away”taken out, wiped from the family. They ask that his name not vanish.

Their request is humble and plain. They don't demand. They ask a question, “Why should the name of our father be done away?”, and they tell the whole truth, the bad part too. Then Moses does the best thing a leader can do when he doesn't know: “Moses brought their cause before the LORD.”

“And Moses said unto them, Stand still, and I will hear what the LORD will command concerning you.”

Numbers 9:8

He did the same in chapter 9. The man who wrote the law does not make up an answer when the law is silent. Neither should we. When you don't know, take it to the LORD first, and to a brother who will pray with you.

“They speak right”, and a new law

  • speak rightsay what is right and just
  • statute of judgmenta standing law for the courts, a rule to judge by
  • kinsmana relative: here the nearest one in the family
  1. His sonthe way it was
  2. His daughterverse 8
  3. His brethrenverse 9
  4. His father's brethrenverse 10
  5. His next kinsmanverse 11

The LORD's answer: “The daughters of Zelophehad speak right.” He didn't say they were out of line. He gave their cause a permanent place in the law, and he made it for every family that would ever be in their place, “as the LORD commanded Moses” (verse 11). A bold, honest, respectful request was heard, and others after them benefited.

And when they came into the land

“And they came near before Eleazar the priest, and before Joshua the son of Nun, and before the princes, saying, The LORD commanded Moses to give us an inheritance among our brethren. Therefore according to the commandment of the LORD he gave them an inheritance among the brethren of their father.”

Joshua 17:4

They remembered what the LORD had said, and when the day came they stood up again and reminded the leaders. It was done “according to the commandment of the LORD”. Numbers 36 adds one more thing: the five married within their own tribe, so that their inheritance stayed in the tribe of their father (36:11–12). They asked boldly, and then they obeyed gladly.

There is a lesson for a man who has been told, all his life, that he has no claim on anything. In the family of God there is an inheritance for every believer (Romans 8:17), and nobody is overlooked.

Part two

“Get thee up into this mount”

Verses 12–14

Moses is shown the land, and told he will not go in.

Abarim, over against Jericho

The LORD says “this mount Abarim”. Deuteronomy 32 says more, on the day Moses spoke his song (32:48):

“Get thee up into this mountain Abarim, unto mount Nebo, which is in the land of Moab, that is over against Jericho; and behold the land of Canaan, which I give unto the children of Israel for a possession:”

Deuteronomy 32:49

The mountain looks across the Jordan to Jericho and the land. Moses stands on the wrong side of the river, and sees everything.

Even the best man answers for his anger

“And the LORD spake unto Moses and Aaron, Because ye believed me not, to sanctify me in the eyes of the children of Israel, therefore ye shall not bring this congregation into the land which I have given them.”

Numbers 20:12

Verse 14 reminds Moses of it: at the water of Meribah, in the wilderness of Zin, he and Aaron did not sanctify the LORD before the people. He struck the rock when he was told to speak to it. One angry moment, after forty years of faithfulness, and he is shut out of the land. Sin has a price even for the meekest man on earth (12:3). No one is so important that he is spared the consequences.

But look at what the LORD does next: he takes Moses up the mountain and shows him the whole land.

Not in the land, and yet in it

Moses never walked in the land, but he was “the servant of the LORD” to the end, and the psalm says of Moses and Aaron:

“Thou answeredst them, O LORD our God: thou wast a God that forgavest them, though thou tookest vengeance of their inventions.”

Psalm 99:8

That is the grace in this verse. The consequence of a sin may stay with a man to his grave. The man himself is not cast off. If you are living with the results of something you did, and some doors will not open again, remember Moses on Abarim: forgiven, kept, and seen by God to the end. Call or text a brother in Christ and tell him what you carry.

Part three

“A man set over the congregation”

Verses 15–23

Moses' first thought is not himself.

“As sheep which have no shepherd”

He has just been told he will die. And Moses' answer is not “why me?” or “what about me?” It is about the people. He asks for “a man” to go out before them and come in before them, and to lead them out and bring them in: a man who goes ahead.

“But when he saw the multitudes, he was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd.”

Matthew 9:36

That is the picture Jesus took up when he looked at the crowds. A people without a shepherd faint and scatter. Moses cared about this more than his own ending. He addresses the LORD as “the God of the spirits of all flesh”: he knows that God knows the heart of every man, and so he can name the kind of man they need.

Joshua, set before them

  • a man in whom is the spiritThe LORD himself chooses him, and says what he is.
  • lay thine hand upon hima sign, in front of everyone, that this man is set apart
  • a chargea command, a duty given to him; the congregation hears it (verse 19)
  • some of thine honourMoses shares some of his own standing, not all of it
  • the judgment of UrimJoshua takes counsel from the LORD through the priest. He leads, and he is led.

“And Joshua the son of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom; for Moses had laid his hands upon him: and the children of Israel hearkened unto him, and did as the LORD commanded Moses.”

Deuteronomy 34:9

Moses did not cling to the work. He handed it on in the open, before the priest and the whole congregation. He put his own honour on the man who would replace him, so that the people would be obedient. A good leader is glad to hand the work on.

Joshua, and Jesus

The two names are one name. When Stephen tells this story, the KJV gives it as Jesus:

“Which also our fathers that came after brought in with Jesus into the possession of the Gentiles, whom God drave out before the face of our fathers, unto the days of David;”

Acts 7:45

Moses led the people to the edge of the land and could take them no further. It was Joshua who led them in. We may say, as Christians have always said, that this is a picture: the law shows us the land and the sin that keeps us out, and another, with the same name, leads us in. Hebrews says as much: “For if Jesus had given them rest, then would he not afterward have spoken of another day” (Hebrews 4:8). We hold it as a picture, and we stop where the text stops: Stephen simply gives Joshua the name Jesus.

There is one Shepherd, not a succession of them. “I am the good shepherd” (John 10:11), who laid down his life for the sheep.

Real world: ask, and hand on

  • SinEven Moses sinned in anger, and paid for it. We are no better, and we shouldn't pretend.
  • RegretA man may stand on a mountain and look at what he can't have any more. Say it to the LORD, as Moses did.
  • GraceThe daughters were heard. Moses was shown the land and kept to the end. God deals with the people nobody planned for.
  • HopeHe gave Israel a shepherd, and he has given you the Good Shepherd, and brothers who are part of his flock.
  • TodayBring one thing to the LORD plainly, as the daughters did. And think of one younger man you could help along: a new believer who needs somebody to go out before him.

Question 1 of 6

The daughters' case

What did the daughters of Zelophehad ask for, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore according to the commandment of the LORD he gave them an inheritance among the brethren of their father.”

Joshua 17:4

Answer

A possession among their father's brethren, so his name would not be done away because he had no son. They told the truth about his sin, and asked humbly.

Question 2 of 6

The LORD's answer

What did Moses do with their case, and what did the LORD say?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Moses said unto them, Stand still, and I will hear what the LORD will command concerning you.”

Numbers 9:8

Answer

Moses brought their cause before the LORD. The LORD said the daughters speak right, and told Moses to give them an inheritance among their father's brethren.

Question 3 of 6

The heirs

If a man died without a son, who was to inherit?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

His daughter; if none, his brethren; if none, his father's brethren; if none, his next kinsman. It became a statute of judgment for Israel.

Question 4 of 6

Mount Abarim

What was Moses told to do on the mountain, and why could he not go in?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD said unto him, This is the land which I sware unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, saying, I will give it unto thy seed: I have caused thee to see it with thine eyes, but thou shalt not go over thither.”

Deuteronomy 34:4

Answer

To go up and see the land, and then be gathered to his people as Aaron was. He could not enter because he and Aaron rebelled at the water of Meribah, and did not sanctify the LORD.

Question 5 of 6

A shepherd

What did Moses ask the LORD for the congregation?

What verses tell us about this?

“But when he saw the multitudes, he was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd.”

Matthew 9:36

Answer

A man to go out and come in before them and lead them, so that the congregation of the LORD would not be as sheep which have no shepherd.

Question 6 of 6

Joshua

How was Joshua set apart to lead?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD chose him, a man in whom is the spirit. Moses laid his hands on him, set him before Eleazar and all the congregation, and gave him a charge.
