Part one

At ease

Verses 1–7

The party at the top, while the house burns.

Go take a look

Zion (Jerusalem) and Samaria were the two capitals. Amos hits the comfortable leaders of both kingdoms, the men “named chief.”

Then he sends them on a trip to three proud cities. Uzziah of Judah broke down Gath's walls in Amos's day (2 Chronicles 26:6), and within about twenty years Assyria took Calneh and Hamath.

Are you better than they were? Nobody is too big to fall.

The danger of “at ease”

“At ease” means careless, too comfortable to pay attention. Not resting after hard work. Coasting, sure that nothing bad could happen.

That's a dangerous place for a man in recovery. The worst time is often not the first hard month. It's the season when things are going well and you stop doing what got you well.

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

Everything but a broken heart

  1. Beds of ivorycouches inlaid with carved ivory
  2. Lambs and calvesthe best meat, picked from the stall
  3. Musicnew instruments, songs to show off
  4. Wine in bowlsnot cups: drinking by the bowlful
  5. Chief ointmentsthe finest perfumed oils
  6. Not grievedfor the ruin of their own people

Archaeologists have dug up carved ivory pieces at Samaria from about this time. Amos wasn't exaggerating.

The sin God names isn't having a nice couch. It's being numb. Their nation, “Joseph,” was falling apart, and they drank by the bowlful and felt nothing. Any of us can use something to stop feeling: a bottle, a pill, a screen, a party. God wants a man who can still feel, and still care.

Pushing the day away

Theyput far away the evil day
→
andcause the seat of violence to come near

That's denial. “It won't happen to me. Not yet. Not today.” But the more they pushed the day of reckoning out of their minds, the closer they pulled the trouble. The judges' seat in Samaria had become a seat of violence.

Denial never moves the day back. It only keeps you from getting ready. Facing the truth, out loud, with God and with a brother, is where help starts.

“Rejoice with them that do rejoice, and weep with them that weep.”

Romans 12:15

First in line

They were named→“chief of the nations”The first families, first seat at every table (v. 1).
They would go→“captive with the first”At the front of the line again, this time in chains.

The banquet here is a word for a loud, sprawled-out party. God says it will simply be “removed.” The music stops. The men who had stretched out on their couches would walk to Assyria first.

Part two

The LORD hath sworn

Verses 8–14

Pride that God hates, and a strength that was never theirs.

Sworn by Himself

God swears “by himself,” because there's no one greater to swear by. When God puts His own name behind a word, it's settled.

The “excellency of Jacob” is Israel's pride: their palaces, their wealth, their sense of being untouchable. God says He abhors it, and He hates the palaces they built. Pride is the one thing that makes a man think he doesn't need God, and that's why God opposes it.

When the city falls

A relative, come to carry out the deadIs there yet any with thee?

The last one hiding in the houseNo.

The relativeHold thy tongue: for we may not make mention of the name of the LORD.

A hard scene, told plainly. After siege and sickness, a relative comes to burn the bodies, and he's afraid even to say God's name. People who ignored His name for years now can't bear to hear it.

Questions with obvious answers

“Shall horses run upon the rock?”NoHorses gallop on open ground, not across a cliff face. They'd break their legs.
“Will one plow there with oxen?”NoNobody hitches a team to plow bare rock. It's foolish, and everybody knows it.
“Ye have turned judgment into gall”But you didSomething just as foolish: they took justice, meant to heal, and made it poison. Gall and hemlock were bitter, poisonous plants.

And verse 11: “the great house … and the little house.” Rich or poor, nobody would be skipped when the walls came down.

Bragging about nothing

King Jeroboam II had won back a lot of land (2 Kings 14:25). Many Bible teachers think Amos is punning on two towns Israel captured: Lo-debar and Karnaim.

Lo-debar → “a thing of nought” Lo-debar sounds like “no thing” in Hebrew. Karnaim means “horns,” a picture of strength. “We took Horns by our own strength!”

God answers: I'll raise up a nation (Assyria) that will press you “from the entering in of Hemath unto the river of the wilderness,” the very borders you were bragging about. What a man wins by his own strength, he can lose the same way.

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.”

John 15:5

Points to remember

  1. Comfort“Woe to them that are at ease in Zion” 6:1The most dangerous time is when things go well and we stop paying attention.
  2. Warning“Be they better than these kingdoms?” 6:2Others fell before us. Nobody is too big to fall.
  3. Compassion“They are not grieved for the affliction of Joseph” 6:6God wants men who still feel and still care, not men numbed by comfort.
  4. Pride“I abhor the excellency of Jacob” 6:8Pride makes us think we're untouchable. God opposes it.
  5. Strength“Have we not taken to us horns by our own strength?” 6:13What we win by our own strength, we lose. Real strength comes from abiding in Christ.

Question 1 of 5

Comfort

What is dangerous about being “at ease”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

Answer

A man at ease stops paying attention. He pushes the evil day out of his mind and pulls the trouble closer. The time things are going well is exactly when we need to keep doing what got us well.

Question 2 of 5

Warning

What can we learn from others who fell?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

Answer

That we aren't the exception. Calneh, Hamath, and Gath were proud places too, and they fell. Israel's leaders thought they were different, and they went captive first. When we see a man fall, the wise response isn't “not me.” It's “take heed.”

Question 3 of 5

Compassion

What did these men fail to grieve over?

What verses tell us about this?

“Rejoice with them that do rejoice, and weep with them that weep.”

Romans 12:15

Answer

The ruin of their own people, “the affliction of Joseph.” They had the best food, music, oils, and wine by the bowlful, and they felt nothing for the people suffering around them. God wants us to feel, to care, and to weep with those who weep.

Question 4 of 5

Pride

Why does God hate pride?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because pride takes credit for what God gave, and it makes a man think he's untouchable. Israel trusted its palaces, its walls, and its victories. God swore by Himself that He abhorred it. Pride is the first step toward the fall.

Question 5 of 5

Strength

Where does a man's real strength come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing.”

John 15:5

“Then he answered and spake unto me, saying, This is the word of the LORD unto Zerubbabel, saying, Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts.”

Zechariah 4:6

Answer

Not from ourselves. Israel bragged, “we took Horns by our own strength,” and God handed the same land to Assyria. What a man wins on his own, he can lose on his own. Lasting strength comes from abiding in Christ, by His Spirit.
