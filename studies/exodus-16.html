Exodus 16

Bread for today

A month out of Egypt, the people are hungry and they grumble. God hears them, and rains bread from heaven: enough for today, every day, for forty years.

Part one

“Would to God we had died”

Verses 1–12

The sea is a month behind them, the food is running out, and the people look back at Egypt with soft eyes.

A month on the road

They left Egypt on the fifteenth day of the first month (Numbers 33:3). Now it is the fifteenth of the second: a month to the day.

Since the sea they have had bitter water made sweet at Marah, and the springs and palm trees of Elim. Now they are in the wilderness of Sin, “between Elim and Sinai”. (This “Sin” is a place name. It has nothing to do with the word we use for wrongdoing.)

Six hundred thousand men, besides women and children, and their flocks, in a dry land. The food they carried out of Egypt has run out.

Egypt, remembered kindly

What they remembered

“The flesh pots”

“We sat by the flesh pots, and we did eat bread to the full.” Flesh here is meat, and a flesh pot is the pot it was cooked in. In memory, Egypt is a table and a full belly.

What Egypt was

“The bondage”

“And they made their lives bitter with hard bondage, in morter, and in brick, and in all manner of service in the field: all their service, wherein they made them serve, was with rigour.”

Exodus 1:14

Murmured means grumbled, quietly and bitterly. Notice what they ask for: to have died in Egypt. A month ago they cried to God for rescue. Now hunger has rewritten the story.

Every man in this room knows the trick. The old life, remembered, has the good meals and no chains. The pot is in the memory, and the whip is not. When you start to miss it, tell the whole story, out loud, to a brother in Christ. What did it cost? Who got hurt? Where did it end up?

God's answer to the grumbling is bread

  • rain breadit comes down on them from the sky, like rain, free
  • a certain ratea set daily amount: so much for each day
  • provetest, to find out and show what is really in them
  • prepareon the sixth day, get ready the double portion

God does not answer the complaint with a lecture. He answers with food. The people had murmured and He fed them. That is who He is: “merciful and gracious, longsuffering” is His own description of Himself (34:6), and this is what it looks like.

But there is a test inside the gift: whether they will walk in my law, or no. Bread every day means they must trust Him every day. A man with a full pantry does not have to.

“What are we?”

Moses and Aaron are not offended for themselves. They are two men in the middle: what are we, that ye murmur against us? The people were pointing at the leaders, but it was God they were calling a murderer: “ye have brought us forth to kill this whole assembly.”

At even means in the evening. By evening they would have meat, and in the morning bread to the full. The LORD would hear the complaint and answer it by sundown.

“Do all things without murmurings and disputings:”

Philippians 2:14

That is not a rule that we may never be tired or hungry or low. The Psalms are full of honest cries. It is a different thing to say, God has brought me here to ruin me. Complaining to God, in prayer, brings the answer. Complaining about God, in the dark, to each other, only spreads.

“I have heard”

MosesCome near before the LORD: for he hath heard your murmurings.

the people look toward the wildernessAnd, behold, the glory of the LORD appeared in the cloud.

the LORDI have heard the murmurings of the children of Israel… ye shall know that I am the LORD your God.

The glory of the LORD appeared in the same cloud that had led them since Egypt, and what He says is: I have heard. Not “I am sick of you”. If you have failed, and you are afraid to come near, keep this picture: the same God who knows every grumble says, come near.

Part two

“It is manna”

Verses 13–21

Quails in the evening, a small round thing in the morning, and one rule: only what you need for today.

“What is it?”

“What is it?” → Manna In Hebrew, man hu: “What is it?” They wist (knew) not what it was, so the name they gave it was a question.

At evening quails (a small, plump bird) came up and covered the camp, and in the morning the dew lay round about the host (the whole company). When the dew went up, there lay a fine, flaky thing, as small as the hoar frost (a thick white frost) on the ground.

Moses does not explain it. He says, This is the bread which the LORD hath given you to eat. They did not earn it, plant it, or grind it. It came down. All they had to do was go out and pick it up.

An omer for every man

1 omerabout two litres (two quarts) of grain
1/10of an ephah (verse 36)
every manby the number of his household

Mete means measure. Some gathered more and some less, and when they measured it out the strong and fast man who brought in a heap had nothing over, and the weak one who brought in a little had no lack.

“As it is written, He that had gathered much had nothing over; and he that had gathered little had no lack.”

2 Corinthians 8:15

Paul quotes this about sharing: God's supply is set to the need, not to how hard a man grabbed. Nobody could get ahead of the others, and nobody could be left behind. A man who could not work that day still ate.

Yesterday's manna stinks

  • notwithstandingeven so, in spite of what he said
  • bred wormsit crawled with maggots
  • stankit rotted and smelled
  • wrothvery angry

Some of them hearkened not (did not listen), and saved some for the morning. It was disobedience, born of fear: what if there is none tomorrow? Their fear spoiled it. And when the sun rose hot, what was left on the ground melted. The manna was meant to be gathered early, and eaten the same day.

God was teaching them to trust Him daily, with nothing in the bank. Our stockpiling instinct is strong: we want enough grace for a month, enough strength for the week. But yesterday's grace is for yesterday's trouble.

Today's bread

“Give us this day our daily bread.”

Matthew 6:11

“It is of the LORD’S mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Lamentations 3:22–23

“Take therefore no thought for the morrow: for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof.”

Matthew 6:34

Jesus teaches us to pray for this day our daily bread, the manna prayer. This room meets daily for the same reason. A man cannot store up a month of sobriety, or a month of the Lord's mercy. He gets today's, early, and then he goes to work on it.

Part three

“To morrow is the rest”

Verses 22–30

Twice as much on the sixth day, none on the seventh, and God gives it as a gift.

Six days, then rest

  1. days 1–5one omer eachgathered fresh
  2. day 6two omersand this time it keeps
  3. day 7the sabbathnone in the field

Seethe means boil. They could bake and boil on the sixth day and lay by the rest. On any other day that would rot. On this one, “it did not stink, neither was there any worm therein.” The same God who made manna spoil made it keep.

This is the first time the sabbath appears as something Israel is asked to keep, before the commandment on Sinai. God does it by a miracle every week for forty years: a double portion, then a day with nothing to pick up.

“The LORD hath given you the sabbath”

Some went out anyway, and found none. The LORD's question is sad, not angry: How long refuse ye to keep my commandments and my laws? And then He says what the rest is: “the LORD hath given you the sabbath.” A gift, not a trap.

“And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:”

Mark 2:27

“There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his.”

Hebrews 4:9–10

What rest teaches

The New Testament says the sabbath was made for man, and that the rest God promises is finished in Christ: we stop trying to earn our place. Colossians 2:16–17 calls the old days “a shadow of things to come”, and the body, the real thing, “is of Christ”.

For the man in this room, the plain lesson is about stopping. We are not machines, and the world will not end if we stop. Tiredness is one of the oldest doors back into old habits. Build in rest, and call it obedience.

Part four

“Lay it up before the LORD”

Verses 31–36

What manna was like, the pot that kept it, and the forty years it lasted.

What it was like

  • round and smalllike frost (verse 14)
  • coriander seed, whitea small, pale seed, still used in cooking
  • wafers made with honeya thin, sweet cake

Every morning, for forty years, the same sweet bread, free. And later, in the wilderness, the people said, “our soul loatheth this light bread” (Numbers 21:5). A gift can become so ordinary that we despise it.

Moses tells them to keep an omer, “that they may see the bread wherewith I have fed you.” A memorial for the grandchildren: this is what God did.

The pot of manna

A single jar, kept “before the Testimony” (the tables of the law, which the ark of the covenant would hold, as the next chapters will show). Hebrews 9:4 says that in the ark were “the golden pot that had manna, and Aaron’s rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant.”

The one omer that did not spoil was the one laid up before God. It was a reminder, not a stockpile: not to gather for tomorrow, but to remember who fed them yesterday.

This is what a man in recovery does with a story: not live in it, not hide it, but keep it where he can see it. Tell it. This is what God brought me out of, and this is how He fed me.

Forty years of bread

  1. about 1446 BCthe manna beginswilderness of Sin
  2. 40 yearsevery morningexcept the sabbath
  3. about 1406 BCit stopsJoshua 5:12

“They did eat manna, until they came unto the borders of the land of Canaan.” God supplied exactly as long as they needed it. When they ate the grain of the land, the manna ceased, the day after (Joshua 5:12).

Forty years is a long time to depend on one miracle. It means a man who started out grumbling could be fed, every morning, for decades, and still be loved. The omer is “the tenth part of an ephah” (verse 36), so that anyone who reads it later will know the measure.

The bread that came down

The manna

Bread from heaven

Given free. Needed every day. Enough for each one. Could not be stored. “Your fathers did eat manna in the wilderness, and are dead.”

Jesus

The true bread

“I am the living bread which came down from heaven: if any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever.”

“Then said they unto him, Lord, evermore give us this bread. And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:34–35

The crowd wanted another miracle like the manna. Jesus told them it was He who had come down from the Father. The manna fed the body for a day. He feeds the whole man for ever. A man who comes to Him will not be sent away hungry.

Real world: gather today's

  • EarlyThe manna was gathered in the morning, before the sun was hot. Start the day with the Lord, not after it has already gone wrong.
  • Only todayDon't carry yesterday's failure or tomorrow's worry. Both spoil.
  • RestBuild real rest into your week. Rest is part of the plan.
  • RememberKeep your own omer: write down what God has brought you out of.

“And did all eat the same spiritual meat;”

1 Corinthians 10:3

Today: read your chapter and pray before you pick up the phone or the news. Then text one brother in Christ and tell him what you are asking God for today. Daily bread works best when it is shared.

Points to remember

  1. Grumbling“your murmurings are not against us, but against the LORD” 16:8Hunger made them rewrite Egypt. Tell the whole story, and take the complaint to God.
  2. Daily bread“Gather of it every man according to his eating, an omer for every man” 16:16Enough for today, for each man. What was kept for tomorrow spoiled.
  3. Trust“that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no” 16:4Bread a day at a time taught them to trust Him a day at a time.
  4. Rest“the LORD hath given you the sabbath” 16:29Rest is a gift, and God supplied it for them. Stop; He is still God.
  5. Remembering“lay it up before the LORD, to be kept for your generations” 16:33Keep the story of what He has done where you can see it.
  6. Christ“I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger” John 6:35The manna was a picture. Jesus is the true bread, for ever.

Question 1 of 6

Grumbling

What did Israel say when the food ran short, and who were they really complaining against?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“Would to God we had died … in the land of Egypt”, by the flesh pots (verse 3). But it was against the LORD, and He answered with bread. When Egypt starts to look good again, tell your brothers the whole truth about it.

Question 2 of 6

Daily bread

How much manna was a man to gather, and what happened to what he stored up?

What verses tell us about this?

“Give us this day our daily bread.”

Matthew 6:11

Answer

An omer, enough for each man, and what was kept over until the morning bred worms and stank. Grace is for today. Ask for today's, and do not live on yesterday's or fret over tomorrow's.

Question 3 of 6

Trust

What did the LORD say He was proving by giving bread a day at a time?

What verses tell us about this?

“But he answered and said, It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.”

Matthew 4:4

Answer

Whether they would walk in His law, or no: whether they would trust and obey Him one day at a time. A man with a full barn has little to ask for. A man with an omer asks every morning, and that is where trust grows.

Question 4 of 6

Rest

What did the sixth and the seventh day teach Israel?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:”

Mark 2:27

Answer

That God provides for the rest too: a double portion on the sixth day, and the sabbath as His gift. Rest is made for man. Stop, and trust Him to be God meanwhile.

Question 5 of 6

Remembering

Why did Aaron lay up a pot of manna?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

So their children would see the bread God fed them with, for generations. A man in recovery needs the same: a story kept before the Lord of what He has done.

Question 6 of 6

Christ

What did Jesus say about the manna in John 6?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Jesus said unto them, I am the bread of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger; and he that believeth on me shall never thirst.”

John 6:35

“I am that bread of life. Your fathers did eat manna in the wilderness, and are dead. This is the bread which cometh down from heaven, that a man may eat thereof, and not die.”

John 6:48–50

Answer

That the manna was only a picture. The fathers ate it and died, but He is the living bread from heaven, and whoever comes to Him will never hunger, and will live for ever. Come to Him today, hungry.
