Exodus 20

The ten commandments

God speaks from the mountain. He begins with “I brought thee out,” then gives the ten commandments. Every man in this room has broken them, and they show us our need, and why Christ came.

Part one

“I am the LORD thy God”

Verses 1–11

God speaks, and the first thing he says is who he is and what he has done.

Before the first command

I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.

Read the words in order. God does not begin with “Thou shalt not.” He begins with who he is, and what he has done: I brought you out. The commandments are given to people who are already rescued.

The law is not a ladder that slaves climb to earn their freedom. It is how a free people live with the one who set them free. (“Bondage” means slavery.)

The first four are about God, the last six about people. They are God's holy standard, not a list to try harder at, and every man in this room has broken them. Jesus sums them up in two sentences:

“Jesus said unto him, Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind. This is the first and great commandment. And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.”

Matthew 22:37–40

“No other gods before me”

A god is whatever you run to first, trust most, and cannot do without. Most men in this room never bowed to a statue. But they know what it is to have a god.

  • The bottle, the pipe, the needlewhat you ran to when life hurt
  • Money and what it buysthe thing you would give anything to have
  • A personwhose approval ran your whole life
  • The screen, the game, the next thrillthe place you go to feel nothing

God says: before me. The word means in front of my face, in my presence. There is no room in his house for a second god. And that is not because he is selfish. It is because everything else you run to lets you down, and he does not.

“No man can serve two masters: for either he will hate the one, and love the other; or else he will hold to the one, and despise the other. Ye cannot serve God and mammon.”

Matthew 6:24

Images, and a jealous God

  • graven imagea god carved or cast by men's hands
  • likeness of any thinga shape of anything in heaven, earth or sea
  • jealousnot envy: the fierce, protective love of a husband for his wife
  • visitingbringing the results of sin down on

No image, because no image can show him. And a made god is a god you can handle, move, and keep in your pocket. The God who spoke from the fire cannot be managed.

“Jealous” means he will not share you with what is killing you. A man who loves his wife is jealous in the right way when another man comes for her.

Three or four, and thousands

The fathers' sin

3–4

Generations. Sin does not stay put. A father's drinking, anger or lies run on into his children and grandchildren. Many of you know it from the inside.

The LORD's mercy

1000s

Thousands. “Shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments.” His mercy outlasts the damage by far.

It says of them that hate me: it is not saying an innocent child is guilty for his father's sin. It says sin has consequences that run down a family, and that God's mercy runs further. Where the pattern ends is with you, and Jesus, and a prayer that starts today.

His name, in vain

In vain means for nothing, emptily, falsely. It is more than cursing. To carry the name of the LORD is to call yourself his, and so you take it in vain when you say it lightly, swear by it falsely, or claim to be his while living as though he were not.

“The LORD will not hold him guiltless.” It is a serious thing to bear his name.

Real world: for a man new in the faith, the answer is not to go silent. It is to be honest about who you are, and say his name with respect, in the plain words of prayer, at work, and at home.

The word sabbath means to stop, to cease. It was given to people who had been slaves for four hundred years, who had never had a day off. Notice who rests: the son, the daughter, the servants, the cattle, and “thy stranger that is within thy gates”, the foreigner. Everyone, even the animals.

The reason is in verse 11: God made the world in six days and rested the seventh, and so “hallowed” the day: made it holy. Rest is not laziness. It is trust, and remembering that the world does not fall apart when you stop.

“And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath: Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.”

Mark 2:27–28

What the New Testament says

A shadow

The day

“Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days: which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.” Colossians 2:16–17

The body

The rest itself

“There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his.” Hebrews 4:9–10

The sabbath was a picture, and Christ is what it pointed to: rest from our own works, because he finished the work. A man who is trying to earn God's favour never rests. A man who has been carried can sit down.

Real world: do you ever stop? A man who never stops, never prays, never sits, is a man heading for the next crash. Take real rest, and remember who you belong to. Ask your pastor how he does it.

Part two

“Thy neighbour”

Verses 12–17

Six commandments about the people next to you: parents, life, marriage, property, truth and wanting.

Father and mother

The first commandment about people is about the people who raised you. Honour means give weight to: treat them as persons who matter. It does not mean pretending they were good to you.

“Honour thy father and mother; (which is the first commandment with promise;) That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth.”

Ephesians 6:2–3

Paul calls it “the first commandment with promise.” For men whose fathers hurt them, or were never there, this one is hard. You can honour a person without excusing what he did: speak to him respectfully, forgive what you can, and leave the judging to God. If it is a wound, tell your pastor or your brothers in Christ.

And if you are a father, you are the one the commandment is leaning on. Somebody is learning from you what honour looks like.

The commandments go all the way down

Do not killExodus 20:13→Do not hate: “whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment”Matthew 5:22

Do not commit adulteryExodus 20:14→Do not lust: “whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery with her already in his heart”Matthew 5:28

Jesus did not make the law easier. He showed that it reaches the heart. (The word for kill here is the one for murder: taking a life that was not yours to take. The same law allows a court to put a murderer to death, 21:12.) You may never have killed a man. Have you hated one?

Steal, lie, covet

  • stealtaking what is not yours: a wallet, an hour on the clock, a name
  • false witnesslying about a man, in court or in the street
  • covetwanting what is your neighbour's: his house, his wife, his servants, his ox, his ass, anything

The first nine can be seen from outside. The tenth cannot. Nobody knows you are coveting. Only you, and God.

“What shall we say then? Is the law sin? God forbid. Nay, I had not known sin, but by the law: for I had not known lust, except the law had said, Thou shalt not covet.”

Romans 7:7

Paul was a strict Pharisee, a man who thought he had kept the law, and it was the tenth that caught him. I had not known lust, except the law had said, Thou shalt not covet. The law showed him what was inside.

Not one man in this room can answer all ten “no.” Neither can the man teaching it.

Romans 3:19–20

That is exactly what the law is for. “By the law is the knowledge of sin.” Every mouth is stopped, and nobody can say he never needed a Saviour. A schoolmaster was a guardian who walked a child to school: the law leads us to the One who teaches.

What the law could not do

The law

weak through the flesh

It could show us the sin. It could not take it away, or change a man's heart.

God sent his Son

kept it, and paid it

He lived without sin, and then bore the curse for those who broke it.

“For what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh: That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:3–4

“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree:”

Galatians 3:13

Jesus is the one man who kept all ten (Hebrews 4:15: “yet without sin”). And he took the curse of the broken law on himself: “Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us.” If you are in him, the debt is paid and the Spirit is now at work in you.

So what do we do with the ten?

We do not ignore them, and we do not use them to earn anything. We read them as a description of love, and ask the Spirit to make them true in us.

“If ye love me, keep my commandments.”

John 14:15

“Love worketh no ill to his neighbour: therefore love is the fulfilling of the law.”

Romans 13:10

If you fell yesterday, hear it twice: the law convicts, and the gospel forgives. Confess it, as John says, and turn: he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins. Then pick one commandment today that you will take to a brother in Christ, and ask him to pray for you.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Part three

“Speak thou with us”

Verses 18–26

The people back away from the fire, and God tells them how to come to him.

“Let not God speak with us”

They had heard the voice of God out of the fire. They saw the thunders, the lightning, the trumpet and the smoking mountain, and “they removed”: they moved back. They stood far away, and said to Moses: you speak to us, and we will hear. But not God, lest we die.

They are right about what they are: they cannot stand before this God as they are. God does not rebuke them for it.

They needed a mediator, and Moses stood in the gap. But what they needed was a better one. John says it:

“For the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ.”

John 1:17

“Fear not”, and “his fear”

Fear not

do not run away

“God is come to prove you.” Prove means test. He has not come to destroy them. He has come near.

His fear

keep it before your face

“That his fear may be before your faces, that ye sin not.” A right fear of the LORD is a fence that keeps a man from the next sin.

Both are true. There is a fear that makes you run away from God, and there is a fear that keeps you close to him. Notice that Moses, afraid as well, “drew near unto the thick darkness where God was.” He went toward the fire.

That is what a man does who knows what God is like: he does not hide. He comes, with a mediator, and with his eyes open.

An altar of earth

  • record my namecause my name to be remembered: where I am worshipped
  • burnt offeringsgiven wholly to God on the altar
  • peace offeringsa shared meal in God's presence

He has just said: no gods of silver and gold. Now: an altar of plain earth. No gold, no show. A man needs a place to bring a sacrifice, and God says: in all places where I record my name I will come unto thee, and I will bless thee.

The point is the sacrifice, and God coming to meet the man who brings it, not the building. A sacrifice stands in the place of the man who offers it. That points forward to the Lamb of God, whose blood is the way in (1 Peter 1:18–19).

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

No tool on the altar

  • hewncut and shaped with a tool
  • pollutedmade unclean, spoiled
  • nakedness discovereda man's body uncovered as he climbed steps

If a man made an altar of stone, it was to be whole stones as God made them. No chisel. Man's art is not what makes an altar holy. A sacrifice is not improved by our skill, and nothing we add helps. The way to God is something he gives.

And no steps up to it: a matter of modesty before God. Even the way a man comes to worship is to be done with reverence.

That is the end of the chapter. The ten words were given, the people backed away, and God gave a plain way to come near him. Next come the rules for how a free people live together, in chapter 21.

Points to remember

  1. Grace first“I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage” 20:2God rescued them, and then he gave the law. The law is for people already carried.
  2. God“Thou shalt have no other gods before me” 20:3He comes first, he cannot be made into an image, his name is weighty, and he is to be remembered and trusted.
  3. Neighbour“Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour’s house” 20:17Honour your parents. Do not kill, commit adultery, steal, lie, or covet. Jesus says it all comes down to love.
  4. Our need“by the law is the knowledge of sin” Romans 3:20The law is a mirror and a schoolmaster. It shows our need and leads us to Christ.
  5. Grace“Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us” Galatians 3:13Jesus kept them all, took our curse, and paid in full. The debt is gone.
  6. Fear“Fear not: for God is come to prove you, and that his fear may be before your faces, that ye sin not” 20:20Do not run from God. Keep a right fear of him, and draw near with a mediator.

Question 1 of 6

Grace first

Who is speaking, and what had he already done?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the law was given by Moses, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ.”

John 1:17

Answer

The LORD himself, who had just brought them out of slavery. He did not begin with a rule. He began with who he is and what he did. The law comes after the rescue, not before it.

Question 2 of 6

God

What do the first four commandments say about God?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He is to come first: no other gods, no carved images, his name held in respect, and a day to stop and remember him. A man who puts anything ahead of him, a bottle, a person, money, has made a god of it.

Question 3 of 6

Neighbour

What do the last six commandments say about how we treat people?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Honour your parents; do not murder, commit adultery, steal, lie or covet. Jesus sums up the ten: love God, and love your neighbour as yourself.

Question 6 of 6

Fear

Why did the people stand afar off, and what did Moses say about fear?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They saw the fire and the smoke and were afraid to die. Moses said: do not run, and keep his fear before you so that you do not sin. And Moses himself drew near. We come near through the one mediator.
