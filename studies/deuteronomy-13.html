Deuteronomy 13

Though the sign come to pass

A prophet whose wonder comes true, a brother or a friend who whispers in secret, a whole city gone astray: all of them say “Let us go after other gods.” Moses tells Israel not to hearken, for “the LORD your God proveth you, to know whether ye love the LORD your God with all your heart.”

One voice, three faces

Verses 1–5A prophet, or a dreamer of dreamswith a sign that comes true“Let us go after other gods”
Verses 6–11Thy brother, thy son, thy wife, thy friend“secretly”“Let us go and serve other gods”
Verses 12–18Certain men, the children of Belialand a whole city with them“Let us go and serve other gods”

Chapter 12 told Israel where to worship: “the place which the LORD your God shall choose”. Chapter 13 tells them whom they must never follow, whoever asks. Three times in eighteen verses the same words come: “other gods” (verses 2, 6 and 13). The face changes; the invitation doesn't.

Part one

“A prophet, or a dreamer of dreams”

Verses 1–5

What if the wonder is real?

then“Thou shalt not hearken.”

A dreamer of dreams is a man who claims God speaks to him in dreams. The hard part is that the sign comes to pass. Moses doesn't say it was a trick. A true wonder still doesn't make a true prophet. The word judges the wonder; the wonder never judges the word.

“For there shall arise false Christs, and false prophets, and shall shew great signs and wonders; insomuch that, if it were possible, they shall deceive the very elect.”

Matthew 24:24

“The LORD your God proveth you”

To prove is to test, to try something to show what it really is. God allows the false prophet's day for one reason: “to know whether ye love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul.” That is the great commandment of 6:5, and here it is put to the test.

“And thou shalt remember all the way which the LORD thy God led thee these forty years in the wilderness, to humble thee, and to prove thee, to know what was in thine heart, whether thou wouldest keep his commandments, or no.”

Deuteronomy 8:2

God isn't finding out something he didn't know. The test brings into the open what is in a man's heart, so that he knows it. A man is not tested by what he can't have; he is tested by what seems to work. The feeling, the “sign”, the thing that went well for somebody else: test it by the word.

“Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world.”

1 John 4:1

The other way: six things

  1. 1walk after
  2. 2fear him
  3. 3keep his commandments
  4. 4obey his voice
  5. 5serve him
  6. 6cleave unto him

The false prophet says “Let us go after”; Moses answers, “Ye shall walk after the LORD your God.” It ends with cleave: hold on, stick fast, the word used of a man and his wife (Genesis 2:24). The best guard against the wrong voice is to be holding on tight to the right one.

And look at what the false prophet really did: he tried to turn men from the God “which brought you out of the land of Egypt, and redeemed you out of the house of bondage”. Whoever calls a set-free man back to his old master is no friend, however many signs he has.

“Put the evil away”

The law of Israel put such a prophet to death. This was civil law for Israel's courts under God, and it shows how seriously God takes the leading of his people away from him. “Though we, or an angel from heaven” is how Paul puts it:

“But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed.”

Galatians 1:8

Paul takes Moses' own words, “put the evil away”, for the church. The church carries no stones. It puts an unrepentant man out of its fellowship, and it takes him back when he turns:

“But them that are without God judgeth. Therefore put away from among yourselves that wicked person.”

1 Corinthians 5:13

“So that contrariwise ye ought rather to forgive him, and comfort him, lest perhaps such a one should be swallowed up with overmuch sorrow. Wherefore I beseech you that ye would confirm your love toward him.”

2 Corinthians 2:7–8

Part two

“Entice thee secretly”

Verses 6–11

No sign this time. Just someone you love.

The closer the voice

The list keeps coming closer: “the son of thy mother”, “the wife of thy bosom”, “thy friend, which is as thine own soul”. And it comes “secretly”. To entice is to draw a man on with something pleasant. To consent is to agree to it.

For a man coming out of addiction, the voice that calls him back is almost never a stranger. It is the old friend, the cousin, the one he was closest to: “just once, for old times' sake.” Nobody in Moses' list is a villain. That is why it is so hard.

Hard words, and why

These are the hardest verses in the chapter. In Israel, the man who heard the secret was to be the first witness: “thine hand shall be first upon him”. Elsewhere Moses adds the safeguard: “At the mouth of two witnesses, or three witnesses, shall he that is worthy of death be put to death” (17:6). It was a court's sentence, not a family's revenge.

The reason is given twice: he “sought to thrust thee away from the LORD thy God” (to push, to shove out), and “all Israel shall hear, and fear”. The sentence falls on the one who tried to lead others away.

We are not Israel's courts, and no one in the church takes up a stone. Jesus says it our way:

“He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me: and he that loveth son or daughter more than me is not worthy of me.”

Matthew 10:37

Love them; don't follow them. Some of the people who called you back may come home themselves one day, and the father in Luke 15 still runs to meet that kind of son (Luke 15:20).

Part three

“The children of Belial”

Verses 12–18

A rumour about a city, and the mercy at the end.

Not on a rumour

“If thou shalt hear say…”→ enquire→ make search→ ask diligently→ “if it be truth, and the thing certain”

Belial means worthlessness: “children of Belial” are worthless, lawless men. Paul sets the name against Christ: “what concord hath Christ with Belial?” (2 Corinthians 6:15).

But even about such men, Israel was not to act on talk. Three ways of checking, and only then. A man who has been the subject of rumours knows why this matters.

“He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him.”

Proverbs 18:13

The same judgment for his own

This is the judgment God laid on the nations of Canaan for their wickedness (9:5), and here he lays it on a city of Israel that goes their way. God has no favourites in sin. Being one of his people gave no city leave to serve other gods.

Nothing was to be kept: all the spoil burned “every whit”, the city left “an heap for ever”, never built again. An heap is a mound of ruins. It stood as a warning to everyone who passed.

We are not called to destroy any city. The lesson for us is the seriousness of it: God does not treat turning from him as a small thing, not even in his own people.

Keep nothing back

Keep a piece of it

“the cursed thing”

Nought means nothing. Not one thing from that city was to stick to a man's hand. The cursed thing is what was given over to destruction.

Let it all go

“shew thee mercy”

“That the LORD may turn from the fierceness of his anger, and shew thee mercy, and have compassion upon thee, and multiply thee.”

The chapter that began with a false prophet ends with mercy and compassion. The men at Ephesus who turned to Christ understood keeping nothing back:

“And many that believed came, and confessed, and shewed their deeds. Many of them also which used curious arts brought their books together, and burned them before all men: and they counted the price of them, and found it fifty thousand pieces of silver.”

Acts 19:18–19

Real world: whose voice?

  • SinThe pull back to the old gods rarely comes with horns. It comes with a sign that worked, or in the voice of someone you love, “secretly”.
  • RegretMaybe you once enticed somebody else. God's word calls that serious. Name it to him, and to the man if you can.
  • GraceHe is the God “which brought you out… and redeemed you out of the house of bondage”. The chapter ends with his mercy and compassion.
  • HopeEach test is a chance to find out that you do love him with all your heart (13:3), and a chance to grow in it.
  • TodayGet rid of the one thing you kept back: the number, the stash, the app. If the pull is strong, call or text your brothers in Christ and your pastor before you answer it.

Points to remember

  1. The sign“Thou shalt not hearken unto the words of that prophet” 13:3A true wonder doesn't make a true prophet: the word judges the sign.
  2. The test“the LORD your God proveth you, to know whether ye love the LORD your God with all your heart” 13:3The test brings into the open what is in the heart.
  3. The way“Ye shall walk after the LORD your God… and cleave unto him” 13:4Walk after, fear, keep, obey, serve, cleave: hold on to the right voice.
  4. The closest voice“thy friend, which is as thine own soul, entice thee secretly” 13:6Love them, but don't consent or hearken; the nearest voice is the hardest to refuse.
  5. The report“Then shalt thou enquire, and make search, and ask diligently” 13:14Not on a rumour: only “if it be truth, and the thing certain”.
  6. The mercy“that the LORD may… shew thee mercy, and have compassion upon thee” 13:17Keep nothing of the cursed thing, and mercy follows.

Question 1 of 6

The sign

What if a prophet's sign comes to pass, but he says, “Let us go after other gods”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world.”

1 John 4:1

Answer

Israel was not to hearken to him. A sign that comes true does not make a man a true prophet; whoever calls men away from the LORD is judged by the word, whatever wonders he shows.

Question 2 of 6

The test

Why does the LORD let such a prophet come?

What verses tell us about this?

Deuteronomy 8:2

Answer

To prove them: to bring out whether they love the LORD their God with all their heart and with all their soul.

Question 3 of 6

The way

What six things does verse 4 tell Israel to do?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then saith Jesus unto him, Get thee hence, Satan: for it is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve.”

Matthew 4:10

Answer

Walk after the LORD, fear him, keep his commandments, obey his voice, serve him, and cleave unto him. Jesus answered the tempter the same way: him only shalt thou serve.

Question 4 of 6

The closest voice

Who might entice a man secretly, and what must he not do?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me: and he that loveth son or daughter more than me is not worthy of me.”

Matthew 10:37

Answer

His brother, his son or daughter, his wife, or the friend who is as his own soul. He must not consent, nor hearken, nor hide it. No one comes before the Lord.

Question 5 of 6

The report

What must Israel do before acting on a report about a city?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him.”

Proverbs 18:13

Answer

Enquire, make search, and ask diligently, and act only if it be truth and the thing certain. Hearsay was never enough.

Question 6 of 6

The mercy

What does the LORD promise to a people that lets go of the cursed thing?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That he would turn from the fierceness of his anger, shew them mercy, have compassion on them and multiply them, as he swore to their fathers, when they hearken to his voice.
