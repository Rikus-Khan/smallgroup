1 Thessalonians 1

Turned to God

Paul writes one of his very first letters to a church only weeks old. He opens with thanks, and a picture of what a changed life looks like: they turned from idols to serve the living God, and now they wait for His Son.

The letter to the Thessalonians

“Paul, and Silvanus, and Timotheus, unto the church of the Thessalonians which is in God the Father and in the Lord Jesus Christ”

Written from
Corinth, in Greece, a few months after Paul was run out of Thessalonica (Acts 17–18)
When
about AD 50 to 51. One of the very first letters Paul wrote, and so one of the earliest books of the New Testament.
To whom
a young church in Thessalonica, the capital of Macedonia, started on Paul's second journey (Acts 17)
Why
Paul had to leave in a hurry and worried about them. Timothy brought good news, so Paul writes to thank them, to answer the whispers against him, and to settle their questions about Jesus coming back.

Driven out after three sabbaths

Beaten and jailed at Philippi, Paul and Silas went along the coast road to Thessalonica (Acts 17:1).

Three sabbaths in the synagogue, then a mob at the door of Jason, who had taken them in (Acts 17:2, 5–9).

That night the brothers sent Paul and Silas off to Berea.

Trouble followed, so Paul went by sea to Athens, alone.

Then Corinth, where he writes this letter.

The newest believers in town

“And Paul, as his manner was, went in unto them, and three sabbath days reasoned with them out of the scriptures, Opening and alleging, that Christ must needs have suffered, and risen again from the dead; and that this Jesus, whom I preach unto you, is Christ.”

Acts 17:2–3

“But the Jews which believed not, moved with envy, took unto them certain lewd fellows of the baser sort, and gathered a company, and set all the city on an uproar, and assaulted the house of Jason, and sought to bring them out to the people. And when they found them not, they drew Jason and certain brethren unto the rulers of the city, crying, These that have turned the world upside down are come hither also;”

Acts 17:5–6

Acts names three sabbaths in the synagogue; we can't tell how long Paul stayed in all. Either way, these people had known Jesus for weeks or months. The teachers were gone overnight, and their host Jason had been dragged before the rulers. Nobody would be surprised if they gave up. This letter is for people in exactly that spot: new, shaky, and under pressure.

Only about twenty years after the cross, and long before the Gospels were written down, Paul was already writing letters like this one. Galatians is the only letter that might be earlier.

Notice how fast it all moves: planted, driven out, sent a messenger, a report back, a letter. Paul is writing while the news is still warm.

Five short chapters. The first three are one long, warm conversation: what Paul saw in them, how he lived among them, and what he did when he couldn't stop worrying. Then come the practical chapters. Today is chapter 1.

Part one

Remembering you

Verses 1–4

Paul's first words are thanks. And he starts with what he has seen in their lives.

Three things you can see

faithshows up aswork

Faith that stays inside a man's head isn't much. Real faith gets up and does something.

loveshows up aslabour

The word means hard, tiring toil. Love that costs you sleep and time.

hopeshows up aspatience

Staying power. You keep going when it's hard because you know how the story ends.

Faith, love and hope can't be seen, but what they do can. And look at the very end of verse 3: “in our Lord Jesus Christ.” This isn't self-help. It's all in Him. These believers were weeks old, and Paul could already point to a record.

Try it today: what is one small work of faith for you? A call you've been putting off, a meeting you'll go to, one honest sentence to someone.

How Paul knew God had chosen them

  1. ✓our gospel came not unto you in word only, but also in powerGod made it count1:5
  2. ✓ye became followers of us, and of the Lordthey followed Jesus1:6
  3. ✓received the word in much affliction, with joyit cost them1:6
  4. ✓ye turned to God from idolsa changed direction1:9

Election means God's choosing. Good Bible teachers have long argued over how God's choosing and ours fit together. What is plain: the invitation goes to everyone, and Paul doesn't read a secret list. He points to what he can see: they heard, believed, and turned.

Romans 10:13

Part two

How it came, how it spread

Verses 5–8

The message came with power, landed in hard times, and rang out from there across Greece.

Not words only

  • in word onlyTalk is cheap. Any con man has words.
  • but also in power, and in the Holy Ghost, and in much assuranceGod Himself was at work, and lives changed.

Paul adds: “as ye know what manner of men we were among you.” The message came with messengers whose lives matched it. The men around you may read your life long before they hear your words.

And look at verse 6: “much affliction, with joy of the Holy Ghost.” That isn't pretend happiness. This is joy that shows up in the middle of trouble, because the Holy Spirit lives in every believer from the day he believes. There is no extra step to wait for.

From Thessalonica, all of Greece

Macedonia is northern Greece: Thessalonica, Philippi, Berea. Achaia is the south: Athens and Corinth.

“Ensamples” means examples, patterns. Weeks-old believers had become the pattern for older ones.

Paul is writing from Corinth, in Achaia. He says he hardly has to speak: people there already tell him how the Thessalonians turned to God. A changed life is the best advertisement there is.

“Sounded out”

ἐξήχηται said exēchētai “has rung out”: like a trumpet blast or a roll of thunder

Our word echo comes from the same root. The sound of their changed lives kept bouncing from town to town.

Nobody planned it. They didn't have a program or a budget. They just turned to God out loud, in front of everybody, and people noticed. You don't have to be a preacher. Just be a changed man where people already know you.

Part three

Turned

Verses 9–10

Two verses that sum up what happens when a man becomes a Christian.

Turned from, turned to

First, turned from: whatever a man hands his life to in place of God.

Then turned to. Quitting alone leaves a hole. The new life faces the living and true God, to serve Him.

And wait. A man with his eyes on Jesus' return keeps going.

What's an idol?

In Thessalonica the idols were carved gods of stone and gold. Here is what the Bible says about them:

“Their idols are silver and gold, the work of men’s hands. They have mouths, but they speak not: eyes have they, but they see not: They have ears, but they hear not: noses have they, but they smell not: They have hands, but they handle not: feet have they, but they walk not: neither speak they through their throat. They that make them are like unto them; so is every one that trusteth in them.”

Psalm 115:4–8

Today's idols: the bottle, the needle, the bet, the screen, the money, your own reputation. They promise everything and answer nothing, and men become “like unto them.”

“Ye turned” is a direction, not a perfect record. A man who steps back toward the old thing doesn't start over from nothing. He turns again, and God is waiting (1 John 1:9).

Rescued from what's coming

What was coming

“The wrath to come”

Sin has a cost, and God is just. This isn't a threat to scare you. It's a fact, like a flood warning.

Who stepped in

“Jesus, which delivered us”

He took the whole cost Himself on the cross. And God “raised him from the dead”, proof that the price was paid.

“Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him.”

Romans 5:9

If you're carrying yesterday's failure, hear this: the wrath is the one thing you don't have to wait for. He has already delivered us.

Every chapter ends with Jesus coming back

  1. Chapter 1to wait for his Son from heaven1:10
  2. Chapter 2in the presence of our Lord Jesus Christ at his coming2:19
  3. Chapter 3at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ with all his saints3:13
  4. Chapter 4the Lord himself shall descend from heaven4:16
  5. Chapter 5unto the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ5:23

Paul can't mention their faith, his work, or his prayers without looking up at the end. For him, Jesus' return isn't a puzzle for later; it's why a man keeps going today.

Chapter 1 is the first of five. By the last one you'll see why: the men who wait for Him are the men who stay awake and clean.

“Ye turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God.”

Here is the whole new life in one line: turned from, turned to, waiting. Your regret belongs on the "from" side of that line, with the old things. It doesn't come with you.

One thing to do today: say out loud, to yourself or one other man, what you turned from and what you are turning toward. Then do one small work that goes with it.

Question 1 of 6

Faith, love, hope

What did Paul remember about them “without ceasing”?

What verses tell us about this?

“And now abideth faith, hope, charity, these three; but the greatest of these is charity.”

1 Corinthians 13:13

Answer

Their work of faith, labour of love, and patience of hope: faith that acts, love that works hard, hope that holds on. All three were in Jesus, and Paul could see them already.

Question 2 of 6

The gospel's power

How did the gospel come to them?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek.”

Romans 1:16

Answer

Not in words only, but in power, in the Holy Ghost, and in much assurance, with messengers who lived what they preached. It is the power of God to everyone who believes.

Question 3 of 6

Joy in hard times

What did they receive the word with?

What verses tell us about this?

“These things I have spoken unto you, that in me ye might have peace. In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world.”

John 16:33

Answer

Joy of the Holy Ghost, in the middle of much affliction. Jesus said we'd have trouble, and He said to be of good cheer, because He has overcome the world.

Question 4 of 6

A changed life

What did they turn from, and what did they turn to?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

They turned from idols to the living and true God, to serve Him. Old things passed away. It started when they turned, and it keeps going every day.

Question 5 of 6

Word gets around

How far did the news of their faith travel?

What verses tell us about this?

“First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for you all, that your faith is spoken of throughout the whole world.”

Romans 1:8

Answer

All through Macedonia and Achaia, and in every place. A few weeks of real change rang out across Greece.

Question 6 of 6

Waiting

Who are they waiting for, and what has He done for them?

What verses tell us about this?

“Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him.”

Romans 5:9

They wait for God's Son from heaven, Jesus, whom God raised from the dead. He delivered us from the wrath to come. It's finished, and He's coming back.
