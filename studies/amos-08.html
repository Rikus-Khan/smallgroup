Part one

The end is come

Verses 1–3

A basket of fruit, and a word that sounds just like it.

Ripe, and about to rot

Quick reminder: Amos was a herdman from Judah, preaching to the northern kingdom of Israel about 760 BC, in good times, about forty years before Assyria carried them off. This is his fourth vision. God uses a pun:

qayits · summer fruit → qets · the end In Hebrew the two words sound almost the same.

Late-summer fruit is the last harvest of the year, fully ripe. Leave it a few days and it spoils. Israel was ripe for judgment. And for the second time God says, “I will not again pass by them any more” (7:8).

The temple songs here are at the king's shrine. The singing turns to howling, and there are so many dead that people carry them out in stunned silence.

Part two

Thumb on the scale

Verses 4–10

Religious on the holiday, crooked by the hour.

Pay more, get less

The ephah was the basket used to measure grain. The shekel was the stone weight used to weigh the buyer's silver. Shrink the basket, enlarge the weight, and the poor man pays more and takes home less. Then sell him the sweepings off the floor (“the refuse of the wheat”).

And these men kept the holy days. They just sat through the new moon feast and the sabbath wishing they'd end, so they could get back to the scale.

“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.”

Proverbs 11:1

“A pair of shoes”: a man could be sold into slavery over a debt the size of a pair of sandals. God swears He won't forget.

Unconfessed sin stays on the books. But for the man who comes to God through Christ:

“For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and their sins and their iniquities will I remember no more.”

Hebrews 8:12

Everything turned around

noon→darkness“I will darken the earth in the clear day.”
feasts→mourning“Sackcloth upon all loins, and baldness upon every head”: rough grief clothes, and heads shaved in sorrow.
songs→lamentation“As the mourning of an only son”: the deepest grief anyone knew.

Assyrian records mention a total eclipse of the sun in 763 BC, right around Amos's time. Whether or not Amos had it in mind, his hearers knew what noon darkness felt like: the world gone wrong.

The things they loved, the feasts and the songs, would become the very places their grief showed up. Sin always promises a party and leaves a funeral.

Part three

A famine of the word

Verses 11–14

They told the prophet to be quiet. God would grant their wish.

The worst kind of hunger

  • a famine of breadbad, but not the worst
  • a thirst for waterbad, but not the worst
  • “of hearing the words of the LORD”God goes silent

Remember chapter 7? Amaziah told Amos, “prophesy not again any more at Bethel.” The day would come when they'd want a word from God, and run everywhere looking, and find nothing.

“But he answered and said, It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.”

Matthew 4:4

Most of us have the whole Bible on our phones. The danger for us isn't that God goes silent. It's that we stop opening it. Feed on it today, while it's in your hand.

From Dan to Beer-sheba

Dan was the far north, Beer-sheba the far south. Dan and Bethel had golden calves (1 Kings 12:29). People swore by those gods, and even by the road to Beer-sheba.

They'd wander the whole land for a word, and even the strong young men and women would faint. Idols can't speak.

“Fall, and never rise up again.” Hold on to that. In chapter 9 God says, “I will raise up.”

Points to remember

  1. God's timing“The end is come upon my people of Israel” 8:2Ripe fruit spoils fast. Israel was ripe for judgment. Don't put God off.
  2. Honesty“Falsifying the balances by deceit” 8:5A crooked scale is sin against God, no matter how religious the merchant looks.
  3. The poor“The needy for a pair of shoes” 8:6God sees people treated as cheap, and He takes their side.
  4. God's memory“I will never forget any of their works” 8:7Unconfessed sin stays on the books. Forgiven sin, God remembers no more.
  5. God's Word“A famine … of hearing the words of the LORD” 8:11The worst hunger is a silent heaven. Feed on His Word while it's in your hand.
  6. False gods“They shall fall, and never rise up again” 8:14Idols can't speak, and they can't lift a man up.

Question 1 of 6

God's timing

What did the basket of summer fruit mean?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That the end had come. “Summer fruit” and “end” sound almost alike in Hebrew. Late-summer fruit is fully ripe and spoils fast, and Israel was ripe for judgment. God had passed them by many times. Now the time was up.

Question 2 of 6

Honesty

How does God see cheating in business?

What verses tell us about this?

“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.”

Proverbs 11:1

Answer

As sin against Him, an abomination. These men shrank the measure, enlarged the weight, and rigged the scale, and they did it between holy days. God doesn't separate a man's worship from his business. An honest deal delights Him.

Question 3 of 6

The poor

Why does God take it personally when the poor are used?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because people are not merchandise. These men bought the poor for silver and sold a man into slavery over a pair of sandals. They thought nobody would stand up for the needy. God did. Many of us know what it's like to be treated as cheap. God sees, and He takes our side.

Question 4 of 6

God's memory

Does God forget sin?

What verses tell us about this?

“For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and their sins and their iniquities will I remember no more.”

Hebrews 8:12

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

Not unconfessed, unrepented sin. He swore He wouldn't forget what those merchants did. But for the man who comes to Him through Jesus, God makes a different promise: “their sins and their iniquities will I remember no more.” Forgiven sin is off the books for good.

Question 5 of 6

God's Word

What is worse than a famine of bread?

What verses tell us about this?

“But he answered and said, It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.”

Matthew 4:4

“Then Simon Peter answered him, Lord, to whom shall we go? thou hast the words of eternal life.”

John 6:68

Answer

A famine of hearing God's word. Israel silenced the prophet, and God warned that a day would come when they'd search everywhere for a word and find none. We live by His word more than by bread. Jesus has the words of eternal life, so open them today.

Question 6 of 6

False gods

What happens to those who trust in other gods?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

They wear out, and they fall. Israel swore by the calves at Dan and Samaria and by the road to Beer-sheba, and those gods couldn't speak a word or lift anyone up. Every idol, a bottle, a pill, money, a person, leaves a man fainting for thirst. Jesus says, “Come unto me … and I will give you rest.”
