They went out depending on God and on the people who took them in. Where a town turned them away, they were to shake off the dust. Jews did that coming home from Gentile lands. Doing it to a Jewish town said, you've chosen to stand outside. It was a warning, not a curse.

He sent men with nothing but Him. You don't have to have your life together to carry His message. You just have to go.

A king with a guilty conscience

Herod the tetrarch is Herod Antipas, a son of Herod the Great. Rome let him rule Galilee and Perea, part of his father's kingdom, from about 4 BC to AD 39. He had locked up John the Baptist, and then had him beheaded.

Herod got his wish at the trial. He wanted a show, not a Savior, and Jesus wouldn't say a word to him. Curiosity about Jesus isn't the same as coming to Him.

Part two

But whom say ye that I am?

Verses 18–27

The most important question, and what it costs to follow the answer.

“The Christ of God”

  • John the Baptistback from the dead
  • EliasElijah, come again
  • One of the old prophetsrisen again
  • The Christ of GodPeter, v. 20

Christ is the Greek word for the Hebrew Messiah: “the Anointed One,” the King God had promised for centuries. The crowds thought well of Jesus. A prophet is a compliment. Peter said more: He's the One.

Everybody has an opinion about Jesus. The question that counts is the second one: “But whom say ye?”

The Christ must suffer

The elders, chief priests and scribes were the men of the council in Jerusalem that ruled the Jews under Rome. People expected the Christ to throw Rome out. That's why Jesus told them to keep quiet for now: they had to learn what kind of Christ He was.

He must suffer. Not an accident, not a defeat: the plan. And the line doesn't end at the bottom. It ends at the third day.

Take up his cross daily

SunMonTueWedThuFriSat

deny himself · take up his cross · follow me

A man carrying a cross in those days wasn't carrying a hard life. He was walking to his death; Rome made the condemned carry it. To take up the cross is to let the old self, the one that runs the show, die.

And daily. Yesterday's surrender doesn't cover today. One day at a time is how a man follows Jesus.

This isn't how a man gets saved. Salvation is a gift, received by faith (Ephesians 2:8). This is how a saved man walks.

What is a man advantaged?

Grab your life and hold on, and you lose it. Hand it over to Jesus, and you find it. Put the whole world on one side and your own soul on the other, and the world doesn't weigh enough.

Many of us chased the world: the high, the money, the approval. We didn't gain the world. We lost ourselves.

Verse 27: some standing there would see the kingdom of God before they died. About eight days later, three of them did.

Part three

This is my beloved Son: hear him

Verses 28–45

Glory on the mountain, and a father's need at the bottom of it.

Glory, and an exodus

Luke doesn't name the mountain. He tells us Jesus went up to pray, and while He prayed, the glory He'd always had shone through: His face changed, His clothes went bright white. Moses, who gave the Law, and Elias, the great prophet, stood and talked with Him.

ἔξοδος said exodos “decease”: a going out, a departure

They talked about His death in Jerusalem, and Luke's word is exodus. Moses led Israel out of slavery in Egypt. Jesus would lead a greater exodus, out of sin and death, through the cross. Peter never forgot that night:

“For we have not followed cunningly devised fables, when we made known unto you the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, but were eyewitnesses of his majesty.”

2 Peter 1:16

Jesus alone

Tabernacles are shelters, booths of branches. Peter wanted to keep the moment going, and he put Jesus on the same shelf as Moses and Elias: “not knowing what he said.”

The Father answered out of the cloud: “This is my beloved Son: hear him.” When it lifted, Moses and Elias were gone. The Law and the Prophets point to Him, and then step aside.

“The LORD thy God will raise up unto thee a Prophet from the midst of thee, of thy brethren, like unto me; unto him ye shall hearken;”

Deuteronomy 18:15

Bring thy son hither

Down from the glory, straight into a father's nightmare. His only boy, thrown down and torn by a spirit. The disciples had cast out devils before (v. 6), but not this time.

The disciples“they could not”
→
Jesus“Bring thy son hither.”

“O faithless and perverse generation”: Jesus grieves over the unbelief all around Him, His own men included.

The father didn't quit when the disciples failed. Some of us have been let down by a church, a counselor, a program. Don't stop there. Bring it to Jesus. He gave the boy back to his father, and He still gives sons back to their fathers.

Right when everybody was amazed, Jesus told them the hard part again. They didn't get it, and they were afraid to ask.

It finally sank in after He rose: “And they remembered his words” (24:8). Some things in God's word only make sense later. Keep listening anyway, and don't be afraid to ask.

Part four

He stedfastly set his face

Verses 46–62

Arguments about rank, fire from heaven, and the road to Jerusalem.

Us first

The twelve, v. 46

“which of them should be greatest”

Jesus set a child beside Him: “he that is least among you all, the same shall be great.”

John, v. 49

“he followeth not with us”

Jesus: “Forbid him not: for he that is not against us is for us.”

He'd just told them He was going to be handed over to die, and they argued about rank. A child had no standing at all in that world. Jesus says: welcome the least, and you welcome Me.

Then John tried to shut down a man doing real good in Jesus' name, because he wasn't in their group. Same root both times: pride.

“Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.”

Proverbs 16:18

He set His face

Received up means taken up into heaven (24:51). From here to 19:28, about ten chapters, Luke tells one long journey: Jesus walking toward Jerusalem, and the cross, on purpose.

The road south ran through Samaria. Jews and Samaritans had despised each other for centuries, and the Samaritans worshipped on their own mountain, not in Jerusalem (John 4:20). A Jew headed for the temple got no room.

“For the Lord GOD will help me; therefore shall I not be confounded: therefore have I set my face like a flint, and I know that I shall not be ashamed.”

Isaiah 50:7

Fire, or the gospel

Elijah once called down fire on soldiers sent to seize him (2 Kings 1:10). James and John, the sons of thunder (Mark 3:17), wanted to do the same to a village that snubbed Jesus. He turned and rebuked them.

“command fire to come down” → “preached the gospel” “in many villages of the Samaritans”: John, a few years later, with Peter (Acts 8:25)

Jesus changed John's temper into a heart for the very people he wanted to burn. He can do that with our anger too.

The second man may not have meant his father had died that day. He may have meant, “After Dad's gone, someday.” Each of the three put something in front of Jesus: comfort, family duty, the old life at home.

Notice the word first, twice. Jesus doesn't take second place.

Hand to the plough

A ploughman fixed his eyes on a mark at the far end of the field and walked toward it. Look back over your shoulder, and the furrow goes crooked.

Looking back with longing at the old life, the old crowd, the old high, is how a man starts to drift. Jesus isn't saying a believer gets thrown out. He's saying you can't plough straight while you look behind you. “Remember Lot’s wife” (17:32).

Question 1 of 6

Sent

What did Jesus give the twelve, and what did He tell them to leave behind?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He gave them power and authority over all devils and to cure diseases, and sent them to preach. They were to take nothing: no staff, no bag, no bread, no money, no extra coat. They went depending on Him, and the work got done.

Question 2 of 6

Enough

What did Jesus do with five loaves and two fishes?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He looked up to heaven, blessed them, broke them, and gave them to the disciples to set before the crowd. About five thousand men ate until they were full, and twelve baskets were left over. What looked like nothing was more than enough in His hands.

Question 3 of 6

Who He is

Who did Peter say Jesus is?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

“The Christ of God”: the promised, anointed King. The crowds said a prophet; Peter said the One. Then Jesus showed what kind of Christ He is: one who must suffer, be slain, and be raised the third day.

Question 4 of 6

Daily

What does Jesus ask of any man who will come after Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me.”

Galatians 2:20

Answer

To deny himself, take up his cross daily, and follow Him. Not to earn salvation, which is a free gift, but to walk with Him: saying no to self every day, and finding real life by giving it to Him.

Question 5 of 6

Hear Him

What did the voice out of the cloud say?

What verses tell us about this?

“For he received from God the Father honour and glory, when there came such a voice to him from the excellent glory, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

2 Peter 1:17

Answer

“This is my beloved Son: hear him.” The Father Himself named Jesus as His Son and told us to listen to Him. When the cloud lifted, Moses and Elias were gone, and Jesus was found alone.

Question 6 of 6

No looking back

What did Jesus say about the man who puts his hand to the plough and looks back?

What verses tell us about this?

“Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before,”

Philippians 3:13

Answer

He is not fit for the kingdom of God. You can't plough a straight furrow while you look over your shoulder. Following Jesus means keeping your eyes ahead, not on the old life.
