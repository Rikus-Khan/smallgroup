Proverbs 21

The LORD weighs the heart

Kings, liars, lazy men, and horses ready for battle: every road a man takes is weighed by the LORD, and safety comes from Him alone.

Part one

In the hand of the LORD

Verses 1–8

Even a king's heart is a stream God can turn.

A stream in God's hand

Farmers in dry lands watered their crops through small ditches. Move a little dirt, open a little gate, and the whole stream runs to a different field.

That is how easily the LORD turns the most powerful heart on earth. “he removeth kings, and setteth up kings” (Daniel 2:21).

The judge, the boss, the people who hold your future in their hands: none of them is out of God's reach. Pray for them, and rest.

I'm always right, to me

We all grade our own papers, and we always pass.

“Pondereth” means weighs. God doesn't grade on our excuses. He weighs the real reasons underneath.

“The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?”

Jeremiah 17:9

So we need His word, and brothers who'll tell us the truth.

Doing right beats putting on a show

1 Samuel 15:22

Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.

Micah 6:8

…and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?

Sacrifices were commanded by God. But a man could bring an animal to the altar on Saturday and cheat his neighbor on Monday. God wants the whole life, not just the religious part. Church attendance never makes up for how we treat people.

Slow and steady, or fast and gone

The hasty way

quick money“only to want”

The diligent way

small, steady“plenteousness”

The hustle, the scheme, the quick score: it comes fast and leaves faster. Verse 6 adds that money gotten by lying is “a vanity tossed to and fro”, a puff of smoke in the wind. Steady, honest work, one day at a time, is how God usually builds.

Even his plowing?

Plowing a field isn't sin. So what does this mean? The Hebrew word here can also mean lamp: the thing a proud man lives by and steers by.

Either way, the point is the same. When a man lives with “an high look, and a proud heart,” everything he does is done for himself, even the ordinary work of his hands. Pride doesn't stay in one corner of a life. It runs the whole thing.

Part two

Strife, mercy, and wandering

Verses 9–19

A big house full of fighting, a closed ear, and a man who wandered off.

Better up on the roof

Houses in Israel had flat roofs that people used like a porch. Sleeping out in one corner up there, in the rain and the cold, was rough living.

Rough, but better than a mansion full of fighting. Peace at home is worth more than square footage.

This isn't a put-down of women. The same book honors the virtuous woman (31:10), and it warns men just as hard about their own anger. Ask yourself: am I the one who makes my home hard to live in?

The closed ear

A man who plugs his ears when the poor cry out will one day cry out himself, and find no one listening. What we hold back from others comes back around.

“Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy.”

Matthew 5:7

Many of us know what it's like to be the one crying out. Somebody heard us. Now it's our turn to keep our ears open.

Verse 11 shows another way we learn: when the scorner gets what he has coming, “the simple is made wise.” Sometimes we learn by watching where another man's road ended.

He didn't plan to end up there

Notice the word: “wandereth.” Nobody sets out to destroy his life. He just drifts off the path a step at a time, and one day he's sitting with the dead.

Verse 14 is also an honest look at the world: a quiet gift, a bribe, does calm people down. Proverbs reports that it works. It doesn't approve of it (see 17:23).

The good news: a man who wandered off can be found. Jesus came “to give his life a ransom for many” (Mark 10:45). That brings us to verse 18.

Living for the feeling

What it promises

Pleasure

Relief, a good time, a break from the pain. “Wine and oil” were the party supplies of the day.

What it costs

Everything

Money, time, family, health. A man who lives for the next good feeling always ends up poorer than he started.

Paul warned of people who are “lovers of pleasures more than lovers of God” (2 Timothy 3:4). Pleasure is a gift from God. It makes a terrible god.

The ransom turned around

A ransom is a price paid to set someone free. Here the trouble that the wicked stirred up falls back on them, and the upright walk free.

At the cross, God turned this proverb upside down. The righteous One became the ransom for the wicked:

“For Christ also hath once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God.”

1 Peter 3:18

We deserved to pay. He paid instead. That's grace.

Part three

Safety is of the LORD

Verses 20–31

Get the horse ready. Then trust God with the battle.

Spent up

The wise man keeps something put back. The fool spends every bit of it the day it comes in. “Spendeth it up” is the picture of a man who swallows it all at once.

Then verse 22: a wise man can take a whole fortified city. Brains beat walls. And verse 23 says the battle we lose most often is with our own mouth.

“Wherefore, my beloved brethren, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, slow to wrath.”

James 1:19

How many of your worst days started with something you said? Keep your mouth, and you keep yourself out of a lot of trouble.

Wanting without working

The slothful→wants everything→does nothingHis wanting never stops, “all the day long,” but his hands won't move. The craving eats him alive.
The righteous→works and gives→has enough to shareHe doesn't just get; he “giveth and spareth not.” Giving breaks the grip of greed.

Desire with no work turns into misery. If you're stuck, don't wait to feel like it. Move your hands at one small thing today.

Using God

Verse 3 said doing right matters more than sacrifice. Verse 27 goes further: a gift to God brought “with a wicked mind” is worse than none. That's a man trying to buy God off so he can keep on sinning.

Verse 29: “A wicked man hardeneth his face.” He sets his jaw and won't be told. The upright man is different: he directs his way. He thinks about where he's going and changes course when he needs to.

Nothing beats Him

  • no wisdomthe smartest plan
  • nor understandingthe best experts
  • nor counselthe strongest team
  • safety is of the LORDHe has the last word

But notice: the horse still gets prepared. Trusting God isn't sitting around. Do your part: show up, work, make the call, go to the meeting. Then leave the outcome to Him.

“Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.”

Psalm 20:7

Wisdom has a name

All through this chapter the LORD is the one who turns kings, weighs hearts, and gives the victory. The New Testament tells us where that wisdom shows up in person:

“But unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God.”

1 Corinthians 1:24

You don't get wise by memorizing a list of sayings. You get wise by walking with Jesus, the wisdom of God. He's the safety verse 31 is talking about.

Points to remember

  1. Who God is“The king’s heart is in the hand of the LORD” 21:1No ruler is out of God's reach. He turns hearts like a farmer turns water.
  2. The heart“But the LORD pondereth the hearts” 21:2We always look right to ourselves. God weighs what's really underneath.
  3. Worship“More acceptable to the LORD than sacrifice” 21:3He wants a life of doing right, not a religious show.
  4. Mercy“He also shall cry himself, but shall not be heard” 21:13Keep your ears open to people in need. Mercy comes back around.
  5. Desire“The desire of the slothful killeth him” 21:25Craving without working, and living for pleasure, eat a man alive.
  6. Trust“But safety is of the LORD” 21:31Prepare the horse, then trust God with the battle.

Question 1 of 6

Who God is

Who really holds the power in this world?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he changeth the times and the seasons: he removeth kings, and setteth up kings: he giveth wisdom unto the wise, and knowledge to them that know understanding.”

Daniel 2:21

Answer

The LORD does. Kings, judges, and bosses have real power, but their hearts are like a stream in God's hand, and He turns them wherever He wants. No plan or counsel can stand against Him. So we can pray for the people over us, and trust Him with the outcome.

Question 2 of 6

The heart

Why can't we trust our own sense that we're right?

What verses tell us about this?

“The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it? I the LORD search the heart, I try the reins, even to give every man according to his ways, and according to the fruit of his doings.”

Jeremiah 17:9–10

Answer

Because every way of a man looks right in his own eyes. Our hearts are great at making excuses. Only the LORD weighs the real motives. That's why we need His word, His Spirit, and honest brothers around us, not just our own gut.

Question 3 of 6

Worship

What does God want more than religious show?

What verses tell us about this?

“He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”

Micah 6:8

Answer

He wants us to do what is right and fair, every day of the week. Religious acts can't cover for a crooked life, and a gift brought to keep on sinning is an insult to Him. He wants our obedience, our mercy, and our humble walk with Him.

Question 4 of 6

Mercy

What happens to the man who shuts his ears to the poor?

What verses tell us about this?

“Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy.”

Matthew 5:7

Answer

One day he'll cry out himself and no one will listen. The righteous man gives freely instead. Most of us have been the one crying out, and somebody heard us. Mercy we show comes back to us.

Question 5 of 6

Desire

How can a man's own desires end up killing him?

What verses tell us about this?

“Traitors, heady, highminded, lovers of pleasures more than lovers of God;”

2 Timothy 3:4

Answer

When wanting runs his life. The lazy man craves all day but won't lift a hand, and the craving eats him up. The man who lives for pleasure ends up poor. Desire makes a terrible master. Love God first, put your hands to work, and learn to give.

Question 6 of 6

Trust

Where does real safety come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.”

Psalm 20:7

Answer

From the LORD. We still prepare the horse: we do the work, keep the plan, make the call. But the outcome is His. Chasing righteousness and mercy leads to life, and our safety rests in Jesus, the wisdom and power of God.
