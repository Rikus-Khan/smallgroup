Exodus 39

As the LORD commanded Moses

The priests' garments are made, the ephod and the breastplate with their twelve names, the bells, and the plate of gold that says HOLINESS TO THE LORD. Then everything is brought to Moses, and he looks at all of it, and blesses them.

Part one

“The holy garments for Aaron”

Verses 1–7

Gold beaten into wire, woven into blue, purple and scarlet, and twelve names carried on the shoulders.

Gold you can sew

  • ephoda priest's sleeveless garment, worn over the robe, like a short apron-vest
  • cloths of servicecloths used in the service of the holy place, probably the coverings for the furniture
  • cunning workskilled work, done by a master. Curious in “curious girdle” means the same: skilfully made
  • blue, purple, scarletdyed thread, the most costly colours of that day, with fine linen and gold

Gold does not bend like thread, so the craftsmen hammered it flat, cut the thin sheet into wires, and wove the wire in beside the dyed thread. Hours of work in every handbreadth. Nothing about these garments was cheap or hurried.

Look at verse 1: “as the LORD commanded Moses.” Keep count. The chapter will say it again, and again.

Names on his shoulders

  • shoulderpiecesthe straps that joined the front of the ephod to the back
  • onyxa banded stone. Two of them, one on each shoulder
  • ouchessmall settings of gold that held a stone in place
  • signetsseals, cut with a name so they could stamp it in wax or clay
  • memoriala reminder: something to bring a person to mind

The names of the twelve tribes were cut in the stones, six on each, as the LORD had said: “Aaron shall bear their names before the LORD upon his two shoulders for a memorial.”

“And thou shalt put the two stones upon the shoulders of the ephod for stones of memorial unto the children of Israel: and Aaron shall bear their names before the LORD upon his two shoulders for a memorial.”

Exodus 28:12

The priest carried the people. When he walked into the presence of God, every tribe went in on his shoulders, the big ones and the small ones, the faithful and the ones who had gone wrong.

Part two

“The breastplate”

Verses 8–21

Twelve stones, twelve names, set over his heart and tied on so that it could not come loose.

Twelve stones, four rows

sardiustopazcarbuncle
emeraldsapphirediamond
ligureagateamethyst
berylonyxjasper

A span is the width of a spread hand, about nine inches. “Doubled” means folded over to make a pocket. Inclosed in ouches means each stone sat in its own gold setting. Sardius and carbuncle are red stones. Some of these names are very old, and we are not sure every one is the stone we mean today (nobody is sure what a ligure was). What matters: twelve different stones, one for each tribe, and each with its own name cut in it.

On his heart, and tied on

The shoulders

carried

Two onyx stones, the names of the tribes. Strength: the weight of all of them rests on him.

The breastplate

loved

Twelve stones over his heart. The names are not only carried, they are held close.

Wreathen chains are twisted, like a rope. The breastplate was fastened by gold chains and rings, and a blue lace, “that the breastplate might not be loosed from the ephod.” It could not slip off, and it could not fall.

“And Aaron shall bear the names of the children of Israel in the breastplate of judgment upon his heart, when he goeth in unto the holy place, for a memorial before the LORD continually.”

Exodus 28:29

Carried, held, and not let go

The priest did not carry the tribes because they were good. Some of them were already tired of the wilderness, and in chapter 32 the whole camp had been dancing around a calf. His job was to bring every one of them into the presence of God, tied on.

“And when he hath found it, he layeth it on his shoulders, rejoicing.”

Luke 15:5

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

If you belong to Christ, your name is on his shoulders and on his heart. You are not holding on to him with your grip, and you do not drop off when you stumble. He holds on to you, and that was Aaron's whole picture: a name cut in stone and fastened on with gold.

Part three

“A bell and a pomegranate”

Verses 22–31

The robe with its bells, the coats of linen for his sons, and a gold plate on his forehead.

Bells you can hear

  • habergeona coat of mail. The neck-hole was bound round so it could never tear, as a soldier's mail is
  • pomegranatesa round red fruit packed with seeds, worked in blue, purple and scarlet thread
  • bellssmall bells of pure gold, one between each pomegranate round the hem

“And it shall be upon Aaron to minister: and his sound shall be heard when he goeth in unto the holy place before the LORD, and when he cometh out, that he die not.”

Exodus 28:35

The text says his sound “shall be heard… that he die not.” Many picture the people outside listening for it: the priest was in there, alive, working for them. He did not go in once and leave them wondering.

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.”

Hebrews 7:25

When you cannot feel him, and cannot see him, that is the sound: he is in there, and he lives to speak for you.

What the priests wore

  • coatslong tunics of woven fine linen
  • mitrethe high priest's turban of linen
  • goodly bonnetsfine caps for Aaron's sons. Goodly means fine, handsome
  • breecheslinen undergarments, to cover them decently as they served at the altar (28:42)
  • girdlea sash. Aaron's was embroidered in blue, purple and scarlet

Aaron's sons are clothed too. The high priest wore the glory, and his sons wore plain white linen. They did not earn their place by their own record. They were dressed for it, all of them, by the same command.

On his forehead

HOLINESS TO THE LORD

A gold plate, bound to the front of the mitre with a blue lace, so that it was the first thing anyone saw when they looked at the high priest. It said that this man, and everything he touched, was set apart for God.

“And it shall be upon Aaron’s forehead, that Aaron may bear the iniquity of the holy things, which the children of Israel shall hallow in all their holy gifts; and it shall be always upon his forehead, that they may be accepted before the LORD.”

Exodus 28:38

Read that again. Even the holy gifts the people brought carried some sin, and Aaron wore the plate to bear it. The priest did not stand there as a man with no sin. He stood there with the sin of the offering on him, covered by the gold, so that they might be accepted.

That is a high priest who knows you cannot do it perfectly, and brings you in anyway.

Clothed by Another

Joshua the high priest once stood before the LORD in filthy garments. He did not wash them himself. Listen to what was done:

“Now Joshua was clothed with filthy garments, and stood before the angel. And he answered and spake unto those that stood before him, saying, Take away the filthy garments from him. And unto him he said, Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment. And I said, Let them set a fair mitre upon his head. So they set a fair mitre upon his head, and clothed him with garments. And the angel of the LORD stood by.”

Zechariah 3:3–5

“For ye are all the children of God by faith in Christ Jesus. For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ.”

Galatians 3:26–27

This is the gospel in clothes. You do not make yourself fit to stand before God. The old filthy garment is taken off you, and God puts a new one on. The priest's holy garments were a picture of that, and every believer wears the real thing.

Part four

“Moses did look upon all the work”

Verses 32–43

Every piece is brought to Moses, he looks at it, and he blesses the people.

All of it, brought to Moses

Taches are the clasps that held the curtains together, badgers' skins a tough leather outer covering, and shewbread the loaves set before the LORD. They carried the tent to Moses in pieces, and the pieces were all there: the boards, the bars, the pillars, the sockets, the furniture, the garments. Nothing missing.

Seven times in a chapter

Count the verses where the garments are finished with the same words, “as the LORD commanded Moses”:

  • verse 1the holy garments
  • verse 5the curious girdle
  • verse 7the onyx stones
  • verse 21the breastplate
  • verse 26the robe and its bells
  • verse 29the coats, the mitre, the girdle
  • verse 31the plate of gold

Then “according to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so did they” (verses 32 and 42). Nobody improved it, and nobody left a corner out.

Moses looked, and blessed

Genesis 1:31

And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.

Exodus 39:43

And Moses did look upon all the work, and, behold, they had done it as the LORD had commanded, even so had they done it: and Moses blessed them.

The words echo Genesis 1:31. At creation God looked at all he had made, and “behold, it was very good.” Here Moses looks at all they had made, “behold,” and blesses them. A people rescued from slavery had built a house for God, and it was good.

Remember where this people began: slaves, in chapter 1. And remember where they were seven chapters ago, around a golden calf. The same people. God took them from there to here, and Moses blessed them.

Real world: do it as commanded, and let someone look

  • SinThe priest bore “the iniquity of the holy things”: even our best offerings are not clean. Don't wait to be perfect.
  • RegretThe people who made the calf made this tent. What you did yesterday is not the last word on what you build tomorrow.
  • GraceYou do not dress yourself. He takes off the filthy garment and puts on a clean one, and he carries your name.
  • TodayPick one thing God has plainly told you to do, and do exactly that. Then let a brother look at it.

Moses looked at the work and blessed it. A good brother or pastor will do the same. Do not hide your work from the men who would bless it.

Points to remember

  1. The ephod“they did beat the gold into thin plates, and cut it into wires” 39:3Gold hammered, cut and woven: no corner was cut for God's house.
  2. The names“stones for a memorial to the children of Israel” 39:7Twelve names on his shoulders and on his heart. Christ carries yours.
  3. The robe“A bell and a pomegranate… round about the hem of the robe to minister in” 39:26The bells told the people he was alive inside, serving for them. He lives to make intercession.
  4. The plate“HOLINESS TO THE LORD” 39:30The high priest bore the sin of the holy things, so that they would be accepted.
  5. As commanded“as the LORD commanded Moses” 39:1, 5, 7, 21, 26, 29, 31Seven times: they did it exactly as they were told.

Question 1 of 6

The ephod

How did they work the gold into the cloth of the ephod?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They beat the gold into thin plates, cut the plates into wires, and wove the wires in among the blue, purple and scarlet thread and the fine linen. It was costly, careful work, all of it done for God.

Question 2 of 6

The names

Whose names were on the stones, and where did Aaron wear them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The names of the twelve tribes, on two onyx stones on his shoulders and on twelve stones over his heart. Christ carries every man who is his.

Question 3 of 6

The robe

What hung on the hem of the robe, and what was it for?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Golden bells and pomegranates round the hem, so the people could hear the priest serving inside the holy place. And our high priest “ever liveth to make intercession” for us.

Question 4 of 6

The plate

What was written on the plate of pure gold?

What verses tell us about this?

“And it shall be upon Aaron’s forehead, that Aaron may bear the iniquity of the holy things, which the children of Israel shall hallow in all their holy gifts; and it shall be always upon his forehead, that they may be accepted before the LORD.”

Exodus 28:38

Answer

HOLINESS TO THE LORD, on a gold plate on the front of the mitre. It said the priest was set apart for God, and that he bore the iniquity of the holy things so that the people would be accepted.

Question 5 of 6

As commanded

How often does the chapter say “as the LORD commanded Moses”?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Seven times in the garments (verses 1, 5, 7, 21, 26, 29 and 31), and then twice more as the whole work is summed up (32 and 42). They did exactly what they were told. The people who made a calf in chapter 32 now obey in every detail.

Question 6 of 6

The inspection

What did Moses do when they brought him all the work?

What verses tell us about this?

“And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day.”

Genesis 1:31

Answer

He looked at all of it, found it done as the LORD commanded, and blessed the people. The same people who had made the calf had built the tent, and Moses blessed them. Let a brother look at your work.
