Deuteronomy 18

No fortune-tellers: a Prophet like unto Moses

The Levites have no land, for the LORD is their inheritance. Israel must not learn the dark arts of the nations: no divination, no witch, no medium, no talking to the dead. Instead, God will raise up a Prophet like unto Moses, and put his words in his mouth. And here is how to know a false one.

Part one

“The LORD is their inheritance”

Verses 1–8

The tribe with no land, and the God who was enough.

No land, and the LORD himself

Every tribe would get its fields and towns. Levi would get none. We have seen the Levite at the table in chapters 12, 14 and 16, and the cities of the Levites in Numbers 35. Here Moses says why he has no field: he has something better. “As he hath said unto them” points back to what God told Aaron in Numbers 18:20: “I am thy part and thine inheritance”. Jeremiah said it for himself in the worst days Jerusalem ever saw:

“The LORD is my portion, saith my soul; therefore will I hope in him.”

Lamentations 3:24

Most men measure a life by what they own. The Levite owned no field, and he had the LORD. A man who has lost the house, the truck and the job can still say it: the LORD is my portion. That is not a smaller life. It may be the first time it was the real one.

The priest's due

  • The shouldera choice cut of the ox or sheep that was offered
  • The two cheeksthe meat of the jaw
  • The mawan old word for the stomach
  • The firstfruitthe first of the corn, the wine and the oil
  • The first of the fleecewool for clothing

A due is what is owed. The people fed the men who served at the altar, so that those men could give their days to it. Paul draws the same line for the church:

“Do ye not know that they which minister about holy things live of the things of the temple? and they which wait at the altar are partakers with the altar? Even so hath the Lord ordained that they which preach the gospel should live of the gospel.”

1 Corinthians 9:13–14

The man who comes late, with all his heart

A Levite might live far off in some town, and one day come up to the place of the LORD “with all the desire of his mind”. He was not to be sent to the back. He would serve “as all his brethren the Levites do”, and eat “like portions”, the same share as the men who had been there all along.

Patrimony is what came down to a man from his father's house. Whatever he got for that was his own, besides his share at the altar.

Some of us came to the Lord late, from a long way off, with years behind us we would like back. Here is the LORD's way with such a man: he comes in with all his heart, and he sits at the same table.

Part two

“Thou shalt not learn to do after the abominations”

Verses 9–14

Fortune-tellers, spells and the dead: every door God shuts.

Nine things, all shut

  • Pass through the fireburning a son or daughter as an offering to a false god
  • Divinationreading signs to tell what is coming
  • An observer of timesone who keeps lucky and unlucky days
  • An enchanterone who works charms and spells
  • A witchone who deals in sorcery
  • A charmerone who binds with spells
  • A consulter with familiar spiritsone who calls on a spirit for answers
  • A wizarda man of secret magic arts
  • A necromancerone who seeks to talk with the dead

Every one is a way to get answers, power or a future from somewhere other than God. The men in this room know their modern names: the palm reader, the tarot cards, the horoscope, the spirit board, the man who says he can reach your dead mother.

“Should not a people seek unto their God?”

Verse 12 gives the reason the nations were driven out: “because of these abominations”. We met that hard judgment in chapter 7. Verse 13 gives Israel's side: “Thou shalt be perfect with the LORD thy God.” Perfect here means whole, all of you, not half with God and half with the fortune-teller.

“And when they shall say unto you, Seek unto them that have familiar spirits, and unto wizards that peep, and that mutter: should not a people seek unto their God? for the living to the dead? To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them.”

Isaiah 8:19–20

Why go to the dead for the living? The God who made you has spoken, and his Word is in your hand.

Two kings, two endings

“So Saul died for his transgression which he committed against the LORD, even against the word of the LORD, which he kept not, and also for asking counsel of one that had a familiar spirit, to enquire of it; And enquired not of the LORD…”

1 Chronicles 10:13–14

“And he made his son pass through the fire, and observed times, and used enchantments, and dealt with familiar spirits and wizards: he wrought much wickedness in the sight of the LORD, to provoke him to anger.”

2 Kings 21:6

“And when he was in affliction, he besought the LORD his God, and humbled himself greatly before the God of his fathers, And prayed unto him: and he was intreated of him, and heard his supplication, and brought him again to Jerusalem into his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that the LORD he was God.”

2 Chronicles 33:12–13

King Manasseh did one thing after another on Moses' list. Then he humbled himself, and the LORD heard him. No man has gone too deep into the dark for that.

Burn the books

We saw in chapter 7 how the believers at Ephesus brought their books of curious arts (magic) and burned them before all men, at a cost of fifty thousand pieces of silver (Acts 19:19).

If you still have a deck of tarot cards, a charm you wear for luck, a number you call, or an app that reads your stars, this is the day to be rid of it. It is not harmless fun. God calls it an abomination, and he has something far better for you, in the next verse.

Part three

“A Prophet… like unto me”

Verses 15–19

The nations had their diviners. Israel would have God's own Prophet.

The answer to Horeb

We read that day in chapter 5: the fire on the mount, the people afraid, and their plea for a man to stand between them and God. God said, “They have well spoken.” Now Moses tells what God promised in answer: not another sight of the fire, but a Prophet, one of their own brethren, who would bring God's words to them.

“For ye are not come unto the mount that might be touched, and that burned with fire, nor unto blackness, and darkness, and tempest, And the sound of a trumpet, and the voice of words; which voice they that heard intreated that the word should not be spoken to them any more:”

Hebrews 12:18–19

“And to Jesus the mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling, that speaketh better things than that of Abel.”

Hebrews 12:24

Who is that Prophet?

At the end of this book, Israel was still waiting: “And there arose not a prophet since in Israel like unto Moses, whom the LORD knew face to face” (34:10). The New Testament says who came.

Peter and Stephen both name Moses as the man who said it. Peter says plainly that the Prophet is Jesus (Acts 3:20–26), and Stephen's sermon closes on “the Just One”, whom his hearers had betrayed and murdered (Acts 7:52).

Like Moses, and greater

Like unto Moses

one of their brethren

He stood between God and the people, and he spoke only what God gave him: “For I have not spoken of myself; but the Father which sent me, he gave me a commandment, what I should say, and what I should speak” (John 12:49).

Greater than Moses

a son, not a servant

“And Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant… But Christ as a son over his own house; whose house are we” (Hebrews 3:5–6). God spoke by the prophets; now he “hath in these last days spoken unto us by his Son” (Hebrews 1:2).

“Hear ye him”

“While he yet spake, behold, a bright cloud overshadowed them: and behold a voice out of the cloud, which said, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him.”

Matthew 17:5

Moses himself stood on that mountain with Jesus (Matthew 17:3), and the Father said what Moses had said: “unto him ye shall hearken”.

“And it shall come to pass, that every soul, which will not hear that prophet, shall be destroyed from among the people.”

Acts 3:23

God says, “I will require it of him.” Every man will answer to God for what he did with Jesus' words. The nations went to the diviner to hear about tomorrow. You have the Son of God, who holds tomorrow, speaking in a Book you can open today.

Part four

“How shall we know?”

Verses 20–22

The man who speaks for God, and God never sent him.

The test of a prophet

If“the thing follow not, nor come to pass”
→
then“the LORD hath not spoken… thou shalt not be afraid of him”

Chapter 13 gave the other half of the test: even if a prophet's sign does come to pass, if he says “Let us go after other gods”, he is false. So a true prophet passes both: what he says comes true, and it agrees with what God has already said.

“Then said the prophet Jeremiah unto Hananiah the prophet, Hear now, Hananiah; The LORD hath not sent thee; but thou makest this people to trust in a lie… So Hananiah the prophet died the same year in the seventh month.”

Jeremiah 28:15, 17

“Thou shalt not be afraid of him”

A false prophet runs on fear: do what I say, or something terrible will happen to you. God says the man who speaks presumptuously, boldly, in a name that never sent him, is nothing to be afraid of.

“Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep’s clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves. Ye shall know them by their fruits. Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles?”

Matthew 7:15–16

“These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.”

Acts 17:11

There are still men who say “God told me” about your money, your marriage or the end of the world. Hold what they say up to the Book. If it doesn't match, you can let it go, and sleep at night.

Real world: whom will you hear?

  • SinGoing around God for answers: the fortune-teller, the charm, the voice of the dead. God calls it an abomination.
  • RegretIf you have dabbled in it, or gone deep, you are not the first. Manasseh did it all, and he came home.
  • GraceGod did not leave his people with no voice. He sent a Prophet, his own Son, and put his words in his mouth.
  • HopeYou don't need anyone to tell your future. The One who holds it speaks to you in his Word, and he is for you.
  • TodayGet rid of anything of the dark arts you still keep. Then open the Gospels and read the words of the Prophet. If you need help to let something go, call or text a brother in Christ today.

Question 1 of 6

The Levites' portion

What did the Levites have instead of land?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD himself was their inheritance, and they ate of the offerings of the LORD made by fire, with the priest's due and the firstfruits.

Question 2 of 6

The abominations

What did the nations do that Israel was not to learn?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Burning their children in the fire, divination, keeping lucky times, enchantments, witchcraft, charms, familiar spirits, wizardry and calling on the dead.

Question 3 of 6

Perfect with the LORD

What did God want from Israel in place of the nations' ways?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That they be perfect, whole, with the LORD their God, and not listen to observers of times and diviners as the nations did.

Question 4 of 6

The Prophet

Whom did the LORD promise to raise up, and who is he?

What verses tell us about this?

“For Moses truly said unto the fathers, A prophet shall the Lord your God raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me; him shall ye hear in all things whatsoever he shall say unto you.”

Acts 3:22

Answer

A Prophet like unto Moses, from among their brethren, with God's words in his mouth. The New Testament says he is Jesus (John 1:45; 6:14; Acts 3:22).

Question 5 of 6

Hearken

What would God do to the man who would not hearken to that Prophet?

What verses tell us about this?

“And it shall come to pass, that every soul, which will not hear that prophet, shall be destroyed from among the people.”

Acts 3:23

Answer

“I will require it of him.” God would hold him to account; Peter says that every soul which will not hear that Prophet shall be destroyed from among the people.

Question 6 of 6

The test

How was Israel to know a word the LORD had not spoken?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

If what the prophet said did not come to pass, the LORD had not spoken it. He spoke presumptuously, and they were not to be afraid of him.
