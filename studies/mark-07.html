Part one

Washed hands, far-off hearts

Verses 1–13

Inspectors arrive from Jerusalem. They find dirty hands. Jesus finds something worse.

Inspectors from headquarters

These scribes didn't live in Galilee. They walked up from Jerusalem, home of the temple and the top teachers, to look this new rabbi over. They had come before, and said His power came from Beelzebub (3:22).

Notice how Mark explains Jewish customs as he goes: “that is to say, with unwashen, hands,” “the Pharisees, and all the Jews…” He was writing for Gentile readers, by tradition Christians in Rome, who didn't know these ways.

Not about germs

This washing wasn't soap and hygiene. It was a ceremony: water poured over the hands in a set way, to be “clean” before God.

What God's law said

Priests washed to serve

Aaron and his sons washed their hands and feet before they went into the tabernacle or served at the altar (Exodus 30:19–21).

What the elders added

Every man, every meal

Wash before you eat. Wash after the market. Wash the cups, the pots, the brasen (bronze) pans, even the tables, the couches they lay on to eat.

“The tradition of the elders” was a huge body of rules passed down by word of mouth, meant as a fence around God's law. About two hundred years later it was written down. By Jesus' day, many treated it like God's own Word.

An actor behind a mask

ὑποκριτής said hupokritēs a stage actor, speaking from behind a mask

Isaiah 29:13, about 700 years earlier

Wherefore the Lord said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men:

Mark 7:6–7

This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. Howbeit in vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.

Clean hands, right words, a heart somewhere else. Most of us have worn that mask: in a courtroom, at a family dinner, in church. God isn't after the performance. He wants the man behind it.

Laid aside, then buried

Nobody tore up God's law. They just kept stacking their own rules on top of it, one at a time.

The commandment of God“Honour thy father and thy mother” (Exodus 20:12)
Wash your hands before you eat· the elders
Wash again after the market· the elders
Wash the cups, the pots, the couches· the elders
Say “Corban,” and you owe your parents nothing· “your own tradition”

Rules aren't the problem. The problem is when a man's rules start to matter more than God's Word, and then get used to get around it.

A holy-sounding loophole

קָרְבָּן said qorban an offering, something given to God

An old fatherSon, I can't work anymore. I need help.

His sonSorry. That money is Corban. It's promised to God.
a loophole

Once he said the word, the rule said his parents could have none of it, though he could often go on using it himself. God's command to honour them was set aside by a religious word. Addiction teaches us loopholes too. Jesus closes them, and He still cares how we treat our families.

Part two

From within

Verses 14–23

What goes in can't make a man unclean. What comes out can.

In one end, out the other

Jesus speaks bluntly. Food goes to the stomach, not the heart, and then out into “the draught,” the latrine. It can't touch the real man.

Mark adds that by saying this Jesus was “purging all meats”: declaring all foods clean. Peter learned it years later on a rooftop: “What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common” (Acts 10:15).

The real trouble runs the other direction. It doesn't get in. It comes out.

Thirteen things, one source

  1. evil thoughtswhere every one starts
  2. adulteriesbreaking a marriage
  3. fornicationssex outside marriage
  4. murderstaking a life
  5. theftstaking what isn't yours
  6. covetousnessalways wanting more
  7. wickednesswanting to do harm
  8. deceitlies, scams, cons
  9. lasciviousnesslust with no brakes
  10. an evil eyeenvy, begrudging

  12. pridelooking down on others
  13. foolishnessliving as if God didn't matter

Every deed on the list starts as a thought, and every one comes from the same place: “out of the heart.”

“Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life.”

Proverbs 4:23

Not a wash. A new heart.

The bottle, the pipe, the bad crowd: they were never the whole problem. Take them all away, and the heart that reached for them is still there. So what fixes a heart?

  • wash more, keep more rulesthe Pharisees' answer; it only cleans the outside
  • try harder, be a better manyou can't scrub your own heart
  • a new heart from GodHe does it when a man turns and trusts His Son

“A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh.”

Ezekiel 36:26

Part three

Crumbs from the table

Verses 24–30

Jesus goes where no rabbi would go, and a Gentile mother won't let Him go.

Out of the land

Tyre and Sidon were rich Gentile port cities on the coast, outside Israel. Old enemy country: Jezebel, the queen who led Israel into Baal worship, was a princess of the Zidonians (1 Kings 16:31).

“A Greek, a Syrophenician”: Greek-speaking, a Gentile, born in the Phoenician part of Roman Syria. Everything a strict Jew kept away from.

Jesus came back the long way, around the north and down into the Decapolis, the Ten Cities, the Gentile side of the lake (v. 31).

“Yes, Lord: yet…”

JesusLet the children first be filled: for it is not meet to take the children’s bread, and to cast it unto the dogs.

The motherYes, Lord: yet the dogs under the table eat of the children’s crumbs.
she agrees, and asks anyway

“The children” are Israel. God's plan was to bring the gospel to them first, and then to the whole world. The word for “dogs” here means little dogs, the pets under the family table, not the wild street dogs.

She doesn't argue or get offended. She takes the low place and holds on: a crumb from You is enough. That's faith.

“For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek.”

Romans 1:16

She went home on His word

  1. She heard“heard of him” (v. 25)
  2. She came“fell at his feet”
  3. She held on“Yes, Lord: yet…”
  4. She wentwith nothing but His word
  5. She foundher daughter at rest, free

Jesus never went to the house or saw the girl. He spoke, and it was done. Matthew records what He said to her: “O woman, great is thy faith” (Matthew 15:28).

Some of us are here because a mother or grandmother wouldn't stop praying for us. Some of us are the ones praying now, for a son or daughter in the grip of something. Bring them to Jesus. He is not too far away to help.

Part four

Be opened

Verses 31–37

A man who can't hear and can hardly speak, and a Lord who talks to him in a way he understands.

Spoken in his own language

The man couldn't hear a word. So Jesus took him away from the noise and showed him, one sign at a time:

  1. Asideaway from the crowd
  2. His earsfingers in them: “I know what's wrong”
  3. His tonguespit, and a touch
  4. Heavena look up: “the help comes from God”
  5. A sigh, a word“Ephphatha”

The crowd brought him, like the four friends with the roof (2:3). This is the Decapolis, where the man Jesus freed from Legion went home and told everyone what Jesus had done (5:20). Now they bring their deaf.

Jesus doesn't run men through a line. He deals with each one personally, in a way that man can understand. He sighed, too: He feels what sickness and brokenness do to us.

“Ephphatha”

Ephphatha said ef-fa-THAH “Be opened”

This is Aramaic, the everyday language Jesus spoke at home. Mark keeps a handful of His Aramaic words, like “Talitha cumi” (5:41), probably just as Peter remembered hearing them.

Some of us have had ears shut a long time: to our wives, our mothers, our sponsors, to God. Some of us couldn't say a straight word without a lie in it. Jesus still says it: Be opened.

He hath done all things well

Isaiah 35:5–6, the promise

Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped. Then shall the lame man leap as an hart, and the tongue of the dumb sing: for in the wilderness shall waters break out, and streams in the desert.

Mark 7:37, the crowd

He hath done all things well: he maketh both the deaf to hear, and the dumb to speak.

Isaiah said that when God came to save His people, the deaf would hear and the dumb would sing. Mark even uses a rare word for the man's speech trouble, the same one the Greek Old Testament uses in Isaiah 35:6.

He told them to keep quiet, and they couldn't. He wanted followers, not a crowd chasing wonders. But a man who has been set free usually can't stop talking about it.

Points to remember

  1. Worship“Their heart is far from me” 7:6God isn't after a show. He wants the heart.
  2. God's Word“Making the word of God of none effect” 7:13When man's rules come first, God's Word gets pushed aside. Keep His Word on top.
  3. The heart“From within, out of the heart of men” 7:21The trouble is inside us. Only God can give a new heart.
  4. Faith“Yes, Lord: yet the dogs under the table eat” 7:28Humble, stubborn faith takes the low place and holds on to Jesus.
  5. Personal care“He took him aside from the multitude” 7:33Jesus deals with each man personally, in a way he can understand.
  6. Who Jesus is“He hath done all things well” 7:37He is the God Isaiah promised, who opens deaf ears and loosens tongues.

Question 1 of 6

Worship

What does God want from us more than words from our lips?

What verses tell us about this?

“…for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart.”

1 Samuel 16:7

Answer

Our hearts. The Pharisees said the right words and kept the right washings, but their hearts were far from God, so their worship was “in vain.” God looks past the outside. He wants a man who is honest with Him and gives Him his heart.

Question 2 of 6

God's Word

What happens when man's rules take the place of God's Word?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God's commandment gets laid aside, then rejected, then made “of none effect.” With “Corban,” a religious rule became a way to dodge caring for your own parents. Any rule, even a good one, has to stay under God's Word, never on top of it.

Question 3 of 6

The heart

Where does Jesus say the real filth comes from?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

From within, out of the heart. Food and dirty hands can't make a man unclean before God. Evil thoughts, lust, theft, lies and pride all come out of the heart. That's why we need more than a cleaner outside. We need the new heart God gives to every man who trusts His Son.

Question 4 of 6

Faith

What kind of faith did the Greek mother bring to Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Humble, persistent faith. She was a Gentile with no claim on Him. She fell at His feet, didn't argue when He tested her, agreed with Him, and still asked: even a crumb from You is enough. Then she went home on nothing but His word, and found her daughter free.

Question 5 of 6

Personal care

How did Jesus treat the man who could not hear?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Personally and gently. He took him away from the crowd and spoke to him in signs he could understand: fingers in his ears, a touch on his tongue, a look to heaven. He sighed over his trouble, then said, “Be opened.” Jesus never treats a man like a number.

Question 6 of 6

Who Jesus is

What did the people say about the way Jesus does things?

What verses tell us about this?

“Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped.”

Isaiah 35:5

Answer

“He hath done all things well.” They were astonished beyond measure. He did what Isaiah said God would do when He came to save His people: He opened deaf ears and loosed tongues. Everything He does, He does right, including what He is doing in your life.
