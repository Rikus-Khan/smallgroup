Part one

Seven loaves

Verses 1–9

Three days in the wilderness with Jesus, and the food has run out.

Where this chapter goes

We're still in the Decapolis, the Gentile side of the lake, where Jesus came at the end of chapter 7. Many in this crowd were likely Gentiles, and “divers of them came from far.”

By ship west to Dalmanutha, near Magdala (Matthew 15:39), where Pharisees want a sign (vv. 10–13).

Back across to Bethsaida, where a blind man sees (v. 22).

Then north, up toward Mount Hermon, to Caesarea Philippi (v. 27).

He thought about the walk home

3 dayswith Him, nothing left to eat
≈ 4,000men, some from far away
7 loavesand a few small fishes

Nobody asked Jesus for food. He noticed. “Compassion” means His heart went out to them. He knew they “will faint by the way” if He sent them off empty. He cares about your body, your hunger, and the long road home.

The disciples had watched Him feed five thousand not long before (6:35–44). Still they asked, “From whence?” We do the same. God gets us through one hard week, and the next one we panic like it never happened.

Four thousand filled

each dot is 100 peopleabout 4,000, “beside women and children” (Matthew 15:38)

Same pattern as before: He gave thanks, broke the bread, and gave it to His disciples to hand out. And it kept coming until everyone was filled.

The baskets here are a different word from chapter 6: big hampers, the kind of basket Paul was later let down over a city wall in (Acts 9:25). Seven of those, left over. Jesus never runs short.

Part two

Eyes that don't see

Verses 10–26

The Pharisees want a sign. The disciples want bread. A blind man just wants to see.

They weren't asking because they wanted to believe. They were “tempting him,” testing Him, hoping He'd fail. No sign is enough for a heart that has already decided. Jesus sighed deeply, got back in the boat, and left them.

“And he said unto him, If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead.”

Luke 16:31

A little leaven

Leaven is yeast, or a bit of old sour dough. Work a pinch into the lump, and in time it spreads through all of it (Galatians 5:9).

The Pharisees' leaven is “hypocrisy” (Luke 12:1): religion for show. Herod's leaven is the life Herod lived: power, pleasure, and silencing the man who told him the truth (6:17–28).

Neither one moves in all at once. One small compromise, one old friend, one “just this once,” and it spreads. Beware of the little things.

They were worried about lunch, with one loaf in the boat. Jesus had them count the baskets out loud: “Twelve… Seven.” The One who fed thousands was in the boat.

Fear forgets. Keep a record of what God has done for you, and read it when worry comes: “forget not all his benefits” (Psalm 103:2).

A second touch

Jesus took the man by the hand and walked him out of town, the blind man holding on to Him.

After the first touch he could see something: “men as trees, walking.” Better, not finished. He told the truth about it, and Jesus didn't send him off half-healed.

A second touch, and “he saw every man clearly.” This is the only healing in the Gospels that comes in stages. Recovery often does too. Don't quit after the first touch. He finishes what He starts (Philippians 1:6).

Part three

Thou art the Christ

Verses 27–33

The most important question anyone will ever be asked.

Among the gods of men

Caesarea Philippi sat at the foot of Mount Hermon, where one of the springs of the Jordan comes out of a cave. Pagans worshipped the god Pan at that cave. Herod the Great built a temple there to Caesar.

His son Philip rebuilt the town and named it for Caesar, and himself. A town full of other gods and a man the empire called divine. Here Jesus asks who He is.

Men had plenty of opinions: John come back to life, Elijah, a prophet. Good things to say, but all too small.

“But whom say ye?”

  • John the BaptistHerod had already killed John (6:16)
  • Eliasa great prophet, but only a man
  • one of the prophetsa messenger, not the Message
  • “Thou art the Christ”the Anointed One, the King God promised

Christ is Greek for the Hebrew Messiah: anointed. Israel's kings were anointed with oil. For centuries God had promised one final King to save His people. Peter says: You're Him.

What others say about Jesus can't save you. Your mother's faith can't either. He asks every man for himself: “But whom say ye that I am?”

The turning point

  1. Baptizedabout AD 27Mark 1
  2. Galileecrowds, healingsMark 1–6
  3. 5,000 feda Passover, about AD 29Mark 6
  4. Caesarea Philippi“Thou art the Christ”Mark 8
  5. To Jerusalemthe cross foretold againMark 9–10
  6. The crossand the empty tomb, about AD 30Mark 15–16

As soon as they know who He is, He tells them what He came to do: suffer, be rejected, be killed, and rise again. The elders, chief priests and scribes were the three groups of the Jews' ruling council. He would say it twice more (9:31, 10:33–34).

“Must.” Not bad luck, not a plan gone wrong. The cross was why He came: “while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us” (Romans 5:8).

Right, then wrong

Peter, v. 29→“Thou art the Christ”Right. Matthew says the Father showed him that (Matthew 16:17).
Peter, v. 32→took Jesus aside to rebuke HimWrong. He wanted a Christ with a crown and no cross.

“Savourest” means set your mind on. A crown without a cross was the same offer Satan made in the wilderness. Jesus turned and looked at the others as He said it. They all needed to hear it.

A man can be dead right one minute and dead wrong the next. Jesus rebuked Peter, but He never dropped him. Peter stayed, and grew.

Part four

Take up the cross

Verses 34–38

Jesus calls the crowd in close and tells them plainly what following Him means.

What following looks like

Ifany man will come after me,
→
thendeny himself, take up his cross, follow me.

Deny himself: stop letting me run the show. Take up his cross: in Jesus' day a man carrying a cross was a dead man walking, on his way out of his old life for good. Follow me: go where He goes.

This isn't the price of salvation. Salvation is a free gift, received by faith (Ephesians 2:8–9). This is what life looks like for the man who has come to Him. And it's daily:

“And he said to them all, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me.”

Luke 9:23

A bad trade

The word for “soul” here is the same word as “life” in verse 35. Jesus is asking: what is your life worth?

Put the whole world on one side: every dollar, every high, every woman, every win. Your soul still outweighs it. Most of us traded our lives for far less than the world, and came up empty.

“In exchange”: once a soul is lost, there's nothing a man can pay to buy it back. But Jesus paid that price Himself, on the cross, for any man who will trust Him.

Not ashamed

Now

“this adulterous and sinful generation”

A world that laughs at faith. It's easy to keep quiet about Jesus around the old crowd.

Then

“in the glory of his Father”

He is coming back, with the holy angels. What matters then is whose side you stood on.

Peter, who heard this, was ashamed of Jesus one night by a fire and denied Him three times. Jesus met him again after He rose and gave him work to do (John 21). Failing Him isn't the end. Coming back to Him is always open.

Points to remember

  1. Compassion“I have compassion on the multitude” 8:2Jesus sees your need before you ask, and cares about the road home.
  2. A warning“Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, and of the leaven of Herod” 8:15Hypocrisy and worldliness spread from a little. Watch the small things.
  3. Remembering“And do ye not remember?” 8:18Fear forgets. Remember what He has already done.
  4. Growing“He put his hands again upon his eyes” 8:25He sometimes works in stages, and He always finishes.
  5. Who Jesus is“Thou art the Christ” 8:29He is the promised King who came to die and rise again.
  6. Following“Take up his cross, and follow me” 8:34Say no to self, leave the old life behind, and go where He goes, daily.

Question 1 of 6

Compassion

How does Jesus feel about hungry, worn-out people?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He has compassion on them. Nobody asked Him; He noticed they had been with Him three days with nothing to eat, and He worried they would faint on the road home. Then He filled every one of them. He cares about the whole man, body and soul.

Question 2 of 6

A warning

What leaven did Jesus warn His disciples about?

What verses tell us about this?

“Beware ye of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy.”

Luke 12:1

“A little leaven leaveneth the whole lump.”

Galatians 5:9

Answer

The Pharisees' hypocrisy, religion for show, and Herod's worldliness, living for power and pleasure. Like yeast, both start small and spread through a whole life. So watch the little compromises before they take over.

Question 3 of 6

Remembering

Why did Jesus ask the disciples to remember the baskets?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because they were worried about one loaf of bread when He had already fed thousands twice, with basketfuls left over. Remembering what He has done is the cure for fear about what we lack. The Bread-maker was in the boat with them.

Question 4 of 6

Growing

What does the healing in two stages teach us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That Jesus sometimes works in stages, and He doesn't stop halfway. The man was honest about what he still couldn't see, and Jesus touched him again until he saw every man clearly. In recovery and in faith, keep coming back to Him. He finishes what He starts.

Question 5 of 6

Who Jesus is

Who did Peter say Jesus is?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Simon Peter answered and said, Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God.”

Matthew 16:16

Answer

“Thou art the Christ”: the Messiah, the anointed King God had promised for centuries, the Son of the living God. Not just a prophet or a good teacher. And right away Jesus told them that the Christ must suffer, die, and rise again on the third day.

Question 6 of 6

Following

What does it mean to take up your cross?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said to them all, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me.”

Luke 9:23

Answer

It means saying no to self running the show, leaving the old life behind like a man who isn't going back, and going where Jesus goes, every day. It doesn't earn salvation; that's a free gift. It's how a saved man lives. And the life you lose for Him, you find.
