Proverbs 24

Seven times down, seven times up

A house built by wisdom, a just man who keeps getting up, and a field gone to thorns.

Things to watch for

Keep these questions in the back of your mind as we read. We'll come back to every one of them at the end.

Part one

Built by wisdom

Verses 1–12

Evil men tear down. Wise men build.

Don't miss the old life

Envy isn't just wanting what they have. It's wanting to be with them. Some days the old crowd looks like freedom.

But listen to what's really going on with them: their hearts “study destruction.” They spend their energy planning the next hustle, the next hit, the next way to hurt somebody. That's not freedom. That's a job working for death.

“Fret not thyself because of evildoers, neither be thou envious against the workers of iniquity.”

Psalm 37:1

A house that lasts

A house here means more than a building. It's a home, a family, a life.

Wisdom lays the foundation. Understanding makes it stand. Knowledge fills the rooms with things worth having.

Jesus said the wise man “built his house upon a rock” by hearing His sayings and doing them (Matthew 7:24). You're building something every day. Build on Him.

Don't go to war alone

Real strength isn't muscle. Verse 5 says knowledge “increaseth strength.” And a wise man doesn't fight his battles off the top of his head. He gets counsel first.

  1. The WordGod's counsel first
  2. A pastorsomeone who watches over your soul
  3. A sponsor or older brothersomeone who's walked it
  4. This groupmen who'll tell you the truth

Verse 7: the fool never opens his mouth “in the gate,” where the town elders met to decide things. Wisdom is out of his reach, so he has nothing to add.

The day of adversity

Trouble doesn't create weakness. It shows how much strength was there. Anybody looks strong on an easy day.

So where does strength come from? Not from gritting your teeth.

“But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint.”

Isaiah 40:31

Build your strength on the good days, in His word and in prayer, so it's there on the bad ones.

“We knew it not”

Some people are being dragged toward death right now. Some of them we know by name: the buddy still using, the brother who stopped coming around.

The easy way out is to say, “I didn't know.” But God “pondereth the heart” (the same word as 21:2). He knows whether we knew.

“And others save with fear, pulling them out of the fire; hating even the garment spotted by the flesh.”

Jude 1:23

You can't save anyone. Only Jesus can. But you can make the call, tell your story, and bring a man to the meeting. Just be careful: don't go back into the fire alone to get him.

Part two

Down, and up again

Verses 13–22

What makes a man just isn't that he never falls.

Wisdom tastes good

Honey was the sweetest thing they had, and it was good for you too. Wisdom is like that. It isn't medicine you choke down. Once you've found it, it's sweet to the soul.

And it comes with a promise you've heard before (23:18): “thy expectation shall not be cut off.” A life lived God's way has a future.

Seven times down, seven times up

In the Bible, seven often stands for full, complete. The just man doesn't fall once. He falls again and again. What makes him just isn't a clean record. It's that he's right with God, and every time, he gets back up.

The wicked man is different: he falls into mischief and stays there. The difference isn't the fall. It's what happens next.

Getting up isn't planning to fall

Proverbs 23:35

…when shall I awake? I will seek it yet again.

Proverbs 24:16

For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again.

One man is planning his next fall. The other is getting up from his last one. This verse is great hope after a relapse, but it is never a hall pass to go use.

“My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:”

1 John 2:1

If you've fallen, you're not finished. Get up today: tell God the truth (1 John 1:9), tell a brother, and go back. “Though he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down: for the LORD upholdeth him with his hand” (Psalm 37:24).

No gloating

Right after a verse about our own falls comes this: don't celebrate when your enemy falls. The man who knows he's been picked up by grace has no business cheering when someone else hits the ground.

Gloating displeases God so much that He may ease up on your enemy just to deal with your heart. Jesus took it further:

“But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you;”

Matthew 5:44

A candle that goes out

Verses 19–20 repeat verse 1 because we need it twice: don't fret over evil men. Their light looks bright now, but it's a candle, and it “shall be put out.” They have no future reward.

Verse 21: “fear thou the LORD and the king.” Respect God first, and respect lawful authority. “Them that are given to change” are rebels, men always looking to overthrow whoever's in charge. Don't ride with them. Their crash comes suddenly (v. 22).

Part three

Also for the wise

Verses 23–34

A few more sayings, and a walk past a ruined field.

Straight answers

“These things also belong to the wise” starts a short add-on section (24:23–34). “Respect of persons” means playing favorites: going easy on the rich man or your buddy, and hard on the stranger. A man who calls the guilty “righteous” ends up cursed by everybody (v. 24).

Verse 26 is a great picture: an honest answer is like a kiss on the lips. In that culture a kiss was the greeting of a true friend. Telling a man the truth, even when it's hard, is a way of loving him.

First things first

First

The field

Get your work going. Plant, plow, make it able to feed you.

Then

The house

Build the home and the family on something that can hold them up.

A lot of us tried to build the house first: the relationship, the big purchase, the fresh start, before there was steady work and a steady walk with God underneath. Get the field ready. The house can come.

Settling the score

  • “I will do so to him as he hath done to me”payback
  • a witness against thy neighbour without causegetting even with lies (v. 28)
  • “Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord”Romans 12:19

Payback feels like justice, but it chains you to the man who hurt you. Handing him to God doesn't mean what he did was okay. It means you trust God to judge it better than you can, and you get to walk free.

A walk past a ruined field

Nobody burned this field. The owner just didn't show up, and the thorns did the rest.

The stone wall kept animals and thieves out. Neglect it, and the stones fall one at a time until anything can walk in.

The teacher looked, considered, and learned. You don't have to wreck your own field to learn this. Learn from the ones you've seen.

Just a little

a little sleep a little slumber a little folding of the hands → poverty, like an armed man

Nobody plans to ruin his life. He just says “a little” one more time. Poverty doesn't knock. It comes like a traveler who keeps walking toward you, and then like an armed robber you can't stop.

The same words show up in 6:10–11. God says it twice because we need it twice. The cure is just as small: one faithful step today, then another tomorrow. “let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not” (Galatians 6:9).

Points to remember

  1. Envy“For their heart studieth destruction” 24:2The old life isn't freedom. Their candle goes out.
  2. Building a life“Through wisdom is an house builded” 24:3Build on God's wisdom, with good counsel, one day at a time.
  3. Rescue“Behold, we knew it not” 24:12God sees the heart. Reach for the man being dragged toward death.
  4. Falling and rising“A just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again” 24:16If you fall, get up today. It's hope, never a hall pass.
  5. Revenge“Rejoice not when thine enemy falleth” 24:17No gloating, no payback. Leave the judging to God.
  6. Laziness“I looked upon it, and received instruction” 24:32“A little” at a time ruins a field. Faithful steps rebuild it.

Question 1 of 6

Envy

Why shouldn't we envy evil men?

What verses tell us about this?

“Fret not thyself because of evildoers, neither be thou envious against the workers of iniquity.”

Psalm 37:1

Answer

Because their hearts are busy planning destruction, and their future is a candle about to go out. The old life can look like freedom, but it's working for death. Don't fret, don't envy, and don't go back to sit with them.

Question 2 of 6

Building a life

How is a life built to last?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock:”

Matthew 7:24

Answer

With wisdom for the foundation, understanding to make it stand, and knowledge to fill it. Get counsel instead of going it alone. Do first things first. And build on Jesus by hearing His words and doing them.

Question 3 of 6

Rescue

What does God expect us to do for people headed for destruction?

What verses tell us about this?

“And others save with fear, pulling them out of the fire; hating even the garment spotted by the flesh.”

Jude 1:23

Answer

To reach out and not look away. “We didn't know” doesn't work with the God who weighs hearts. Only Jesus saves, but we can call, tell our story, pray, and bring a man along, while being careful not to walk back into the fire ourselves.

Question 4 of 6

Falling and rising

What does a righteous man do when he falls?

What verses tell us about this?

“The steps of a good man are ordered by the LORD: and he delighteth in his way. Though he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down: for the LORD upholdeth him with his hand.”

Psalm 37:23–24

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

He gets back up. Again and again. He confesses it to God, who forgives and cleanses, tells a brother, and goes on. The LORD holds his hand so he isn't utterly cast down. But getting up from the last fall is not the same as planning the next one.

Question 5 of 6

Revenge

How should we treat people who have hurt us?

What verses tell us about this?

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

Answer

No gloating when they fall, and no payback. That doesn't mean what they did was okay. It means we hand them over to God, who judges rightly, and we pray for them. Revenge chains you to the man who hurt you. Trusting God sets you free.

Question 6 of 6

Laziness

What can we learn from the field of the slothful?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A life is ruined “a little” at a time, not usually all at once. Thorns grow and walls fall when a man just doesn't show up. Learn from what you've seen in others instead of repeating it. And the fix is small too: faithful steps, every day.
