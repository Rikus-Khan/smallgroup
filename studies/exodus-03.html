Part one

A bush that burns

Verses 1–6

A shepherd turns aside to look, and finds the ground holy.

The mountain of God

Moses is eighty (7:7), and he has kept Jethro's flock for forty years. He led them “to the backside of the desert”: the far side, where the grazing was. And he came to “the mountain of God, even to Horeb”.

Horeb is the mountain also called Sinai. Paul puts it “in Arabia” (Galatians 4:25), which is where Midian was. It is called the mountain of God before Israel ever reaches it.

Hold on to this place. In a few chapters a whole nation will stand here.

Burning, and not burned up

A dry desert bush, the kind that goes up like paper. It burned, and it stayed green.

“I will now turn aside, and see this great sight.” The whole story turns on that step: he was out for the sheep, and he stopped for God.

Many have seen a picture of Israel: burning in Egypt, and not consumed (1:12). God was in it with them.

Called by name, twice

  1. Abraham, Abrahamon the mountain, the knife raised (Genesis 22:11)
  2. Jacob, Jacobon the way down to Egypt (Genesis 46:2)
  3. Moses, Mosesat the bush, on the way out

When God says a man's name twice, it is for someone He has marked out. And it is not for his record. Moses had killed a man and hidden it. The LORD said Moses, Moses anyway.

“Here am I” is all he has to say. Not an excuse yet. Not a résumé. Just present, and listening. Isaiah 43:1 says it for every man who is the LORD's:

“But now thus saith the LORD that created thee, O Jacob, and he that formed thee, O Israel, Fear not: for I have redeemed thee, I have called thee by thy name; thou art mine.”

Isaiah 43:1

Holy ground

קֹדֶשׁ said qodesh “holy”: set apart, belonging wholly to God

It wasn't holy soil. It was holy because God was there. Shoes off says: I am not walking in on my own terms.

In verse 6 Moses hides his face, afraid. A man who has killed and hidden should be. But God draws near, and says He is the God of his fathers.

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;”

Hebrews 10:19–20

Not “was”, but “am”

Abraham, Isaac and Jacob had been dead for hundreds of years. Yet God says, “I am the God of Abraham.” Jesus used exactly this verse to answer men who said there was no life after death:

“And as touching the dead, that they rise: have ye not read in the book of Moses, how in the bush God spake unto him, saying, I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob? He is not the God of the dead, but the God of the living: ye therefore do greatly err.”

Mark 12:26–27

Jesus saw it in the bush. If God is still their God, they are still alive to Him. He is not the God of old graves. Every believer who has died is alive with Him now.

Part two

I have seen, I have heard, I know

Verses 7–10

God has been watching all along.

Seen, heard, known, come down

  1. seenthe affliction of my people
  2. heardtheir cry because of their taskmasters
  3. knowtheir sorrows
  4. come downto deliver them

“My people.” Four hundred years in, God still says my.

And He came down. God does not wait for people to climb up to Him.

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

John 1:14

A good land and a large

“Milk and honey” is a farmer's picture: goats and cattle for milk, bees and fruit for honey. A land of plenty, good and wide, after a life of mud and bricks. And it was not an empty land. Six peoples lived in it:

  1. Canaanites
  2. Hittites
  3. Amorites
  4. Perizzites
  5. Hivites
  6. Jebusites

God doesn't promise Moses a safe, easy road, only a good place at the end of it. It was promised to Abraham centuries earlier, and God had not lost the paper.

“I will send thee”

Three words land on Moses: I will send thee. Forty years ago he walked out of Pharaoh's palace as a fugitive, and God now sends him back into it. Notice the order: God has seen, God has come down, and then God sends a man. The rescue is God's. Moses is the one who carries the message.

The same Moses who killed an Egyptian and fled is the one God picks to stand in front of Pharaoh. Not because of his record. Because God had decided.

“I have seen, I have seen the affliction of my people which is in Egypt, and I have heard their groaning, and am come down to deliver them. And now come, I will send thee into Egypt.”

Acts 7:34

Part three

Who am I? I AM

Verses 11–15

Moses has questions. God has a name.

“Who am I?”

MosesWho am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh?

GodCertainly I will be with thee.

Forty years ago Moses thought he was somebody. Now he is sure he is nobody, and he is right: he is not enough. God doesn't say “you are great”. He says I will be with thee. The answer to “who am I?” is “who is with me?” (“My grace is sufficient for thee”, 2 Corinthians 12:9.)

The sign comes after

“Token” is an old word for a sign, a proof. And here is a strange one: the sign that God sent him will be what happens after. “When thou hast brought forth the people out of Egypt, ye shall serve God upon this mountain.”

Moses will have to go first, with only the word, and see the proof later. Faith often works like that: you don't get the sign first. You get the next step. And the place where he stands (the mountain, v. 1) is the place where the road is going to end.

Notice too what it is for: not just out of Egypt, but out to serve God. They are freed for something. A man freed from a bottle or a habit is freed to belong to God.

I AM THAT I AM

אֶהְיֶה אֲשֶׁר אֶהְיֶה said Ehyeh asher ehyeh from the Hebrew verb “to be”: “I am who I am”, or “I will be what I will be”

Moses asked for a name because in Egypt every god had one, and a name told you what a god did. The God of Abraham answers with the plainest word there is: I am. No beginning, no one who made Him, no one who can make Him go away. He just is.

He needs nothing and nobody. And He is whatever His people need at that moment. Not “I was”, not “I will be” someday: I AM, present tense, here, today.

Every “LORD” in capitals

In verse 15 God gives the name Israel will use: the LORD. Whenever you see LORD in capital letters in your King James Bible, the Hebrew behind it is this name (it is related to “I am”, YHWH). The translators put it in capitals to show it is God's own name, and not just “Lord”, a title.

It stands in the Old Testament thousands of times. “The LORD is my shepherd.” “The LORD heareth.” Every time, God is saying His own name over His people. He said it would be “my memorial unto all generations”, a name for them to remember, and it is.

“Memorial” means a thing set up to be remembered by: His name is the reminder.

Part four

The plan

Verses 16–22

God tells Moses how it will go, including the parts that will be hard.

The plan, in order

  1. Gatherthe elders16–17
  2. They hearand will hearken18
  3. Askthree days to worship18
  4. Nothe king refuses19
  5. God's handwonders in Egypt20
  6. Let goPharaoh sends them20
  7. Leavenot empty-handed21–22

“Visited” means God has come to look after them. “Hearken” means listen and obey. “I am sure” means I know: God tells Moses the “no” is coming, so it won't throw him. Knowing what Pharaoh will choose is not making him choose it; we'll watch him choose.

“Borrow” and “spoil”

  1. borrowthe Hebrew simply means ask
  2. spoiltake as plunder, as the winner of a battle takes it
  3. jewelsarticles of silver and gold

“And also that nation, whom they shall serve, will I judge: and afterward shall they come out with great substance.”

Genesis 15:14

God promised Abraham they would come out “with great substance”. It was wages for centuries of unpaid work, and God turned the Egyptians' hearts to give it (v. 21). It was not stealing.

That gold will be used well, for God's house (25:3), and badly (32:2–4). What a man does with wealth is what matters.

What to take with you today

  1. You are not the answer.Moses learned it in forty years. He still said “Who am I?”
  2. You are known.“Moses, Moses.” Your name, not your record.
  3. He is with you.“I will be with thee.” Today, in the car, at the job, at the kitchen table.
  4. He is I AM.Whatever you need today, He is it.

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Moses did not leave the bush a different man. He left it with a promise and a name. That is enough to start with. Call a brother in Christ today, and tell him where you are.

Question 1 of 6

God's holiness

Why was Moses told to take off his shoes?

What verses tell us about this?

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;”

Hebrews 10:19–20

Answer

Because the ground was holy: God was there. Shoes off says, I am not coming in on my own terms. Moses was afraid, but God called him by name, and Jesus has opened a new and living way for us.

Question 2 of 6

God sees

What did God say He had seen, heard and known about His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“I have seen, I have seen the affliction of my people which is in Egypt, and I have heard their groaning, and am come down to deliver them. And now come, I will send thee into Egypt.”

Acts 7:34

Answer

That He has seen their affliction, heard their cry and known their sorrows, and has come down to deliver them. Nobody suffers out of God's sight.

Question 3 of 6

The call

What kind of man did God choose, and what did he say about himself?

What verses tell us about this?

“And seeing one of them suffer wrong, he defended him, and avenged him that was oppressed, and smote the Egyptian:”

Acts 7:24

Answer

A man who had killed in anger and run, now eighty and a shepherd, who said, “Who am I?” God chose him anyway. A past doesn't disqualify a man. What matters is that God is with him.

Question 4 of 6

God with us

How did God answer Moses' question, “Who am I?”

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

“Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.”

Matthew 28:20

Answer

“Certainly I will be with thee.” God didn't tell Moses he was enough. He told him He was. And the risen Jesus says the same to every believer: “I am with you alway.”

Question 5 of 6

His name

What name did God give Himself, and what does it mean?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death.”

Revelation 1:18

Answer

The God who is: no beginning, no end, needing no one, and whatever His people need today. In your Bible it is “the LORD” in capitals. Jesus takes it as His own.

Question 6 of 6

Jesus

What was Jesus claiming when He said, “Before Abraham was, I am”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus said unto them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Before Abraham was, I am.”

John 8:58

“I said therefore unto you, that ye shall die in your sins: for if ye believe not that I am he, ye shall die in your sins.”

John 8:24

“And as touching the dead, that they rise: have ye not read in the book of Moses, how in the bush God spake unto him, saying, I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob?”

Mark 12:26

Answer

That He is the I AM of the bush: God. The men who heard it picked up stones. He says, “if ye believe not that I am he, ye shall die in your sins.” We trust Him with our sins and our past, because He is the One of the bush, and He is with us now.
