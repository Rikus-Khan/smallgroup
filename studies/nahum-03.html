Woe to the bloody city

Nahum reads out the charges against Nineveh, points to a great city it had already destroyed, and ends with a question that answers the question at the end of Jonah.

Part one

Woe to the bloody city

Verses 1–7

Nahum, about 650 BC, reads out the charges before the sentence.

The charges

Nineveh: what is owed

  • “the bloody city”3:1
  • “full of lies and robbery”3:1
  • “the prey departeth not”3:1
  • “selleth nations”3:4
  • “thy wickedness passed continually”3:19

Duewoe

“Bloody” is not an exaggeration. Assyria's kings had their own deeds written down and carved in stone: captives impaled on stakes, men skinned alive, piles of heads at a conquered city's gate. They were proud of it.

Verses 2–3 come in short bursts, like the noise of battle. What Nineveh did to others is about to be done to it.

Every one of us has a bill too. The difference is that Jesus offers to pay ours. Nineveh never asked.

Good-looking, and for sale

God pictures Nineveh as a beautiful prostitute (“wellfavoured” means good-looking) and a sorceress casting spells. She drew nations in with promises of protection and trade, and then owned them.

What she offered

Come to me

Safety, money, a strong friend. Judah's King Ahaz once paid Assyria to rescue him from his enemies (2 Kings 16).

What she did

Now you're mine

Heavy tribute every year, and an army at the door if you were late. She “selleth nations” and “families.”

That's how sin works too. It shows up good-looking and promises to help. Then it sends the bill, and it takes your family with it. The only One who offers help and doesn't come to own you is the LORD.

Uncovered

A city taken in war was treated like a captive woman: stripped and paraded in shame. God says Nineveh's hidden filth will be shown to the whole world, and no one will be sorry. “Who will bemoan her?”

Hidden sin doesn't stay hidden. Either God uncovers it, or we do.

When we bring it out, it ends differently: “whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy” (Proverbs 28:13).

Part two

Art thou better than No?

Verses 8–13

Nineveh had already seen what happens to a great city that thinks it can't fall. It did it.

Remember Thebes

“No”, or No-Amon, is Thebes, the great temple city far up the Nile in Egypt. The river and its canals wrapped around it like a moat. Ethiopia ruled Egypt then, and Put and Lubim, peoples of Libya, fought alongside.

In 663 BC, Assyria's army marched all the way there and sacked it. Nineveh did that. Its own soldiers carried Thebes' treasures home.

So God asks, “Art thou better?” You saw a great city fall. Why do you think you can't?

“It won't happen to me”

Verse 10 is hard to read. Children killed in the streets, leaders sold by lottery and led off in chains. That's what Assyria did to Thebes, and now the same is coming back on Nineveh.

Nineveh's sin was thinking it was the exception. Every man in recovery has heard someone's story and thought, that won't be me. Then it was.

“Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come. Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:11–12

Other men's falls are a gift if we learn from them. That's part of why we sit in this room and tell the truth to each other.

Ripe figs

The first figs of the season are so ripe that a light shake drops them off the branch. Nineveh's forts look solid, but they're like that.

If“they be shaken,”
→
then“they shall even fall into the mouth of the eater.”

“Thou also shalt be drunken” (verse 11): in the prophets, being drunk often pictures a nation staggering under God's judgment, like a man who can't stand or think straight. Many of us know that feeling too well. Nineveh would reel and try to hide.

“Thy people… are women” (verse 13) was an old way of saying the soldiers would be too terrified to fight. The gates would stand open, and fire would eat the bars that locked them.

Part three

No healing of thy bruise

Verses 14–19

The last word of the book is a question.

Patch the walls all you want

“Draw thee waters for the siege”the fireFill the cisterns so you can hold out for months.
“Fortify thy strong holds”the swordDouble the guard on every tower.
“Tread the morter, make strong the brickkiln”eaten upStomp clay and straw with your feet, and bake bricks to patch the walls.

God says, go ahead. “There shall the fire devour thee.” Those who dug up Nineveh found signs of fire in its palaces. Hard work is good, but no amount of it saves a man from God. Only God does.

Here today, gone at sunrise

Nineveh had more merchants than stars, and princes and officers everywhere. Nahum compares them to locusts. On a cold morning locusts sit stiff in the hedges. As soon as the sun warms them, they're gone.

verse 17In the cold day

The princes and captains camp in the hedges, thick as locusts.

verse 17The sun ariseth

Trouble comes. The heat is on.

verse 17Place not known

They flee away. Nobody can even find them.

Every man who has hit bottom knows this. The friends who were always around when the money and the party were there were gone when the sun came up. The LORD is not that kind of friend.

Sleeping shepherds

“Shepherds” here means Assyria's rulers. They lie in the dust, and the people scatter on the hills with no one to bring them home. Assyria never rose again.

Assyria's shepherds

They slumber

The people are scattered, and “no man gathereth them.”

The LORD

He never sleeps

“Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep” (Psalm 121:4). And Jesus said, “I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep” (John 10:11).

Every man follows some shepherd. The ones this world offers fall asleep, or fall. The Good Shepherd gave His life to gather His sheep, and He doesn't lose them.

Two books, two questions

Jonah and Nahum both end with a question, and both questions are about Nineveh.

Jonah, about 760 BC: God's mercy

“And should not I spare Nineveh, that great city, wherein are more than sixscore thousand persons that cannot discern between their right hand and their left hand; and also much cattle?”

Jonah 4:11

Nahum, about 650 BC: God's justice

“…all that hear the bruit of thee shall clap the hands over thee: for upon whom hath not thy wickedness passed continually?”

Nahum 3:19

God spared Nineveh when it turned. When it went back to its cruelty for a hundred years, He judged it, and the whole world clapped. (“Bruit” means the news.)

Nineveh's wound had no healing because it refused the Healer. Ours can be healed: “with his stripes we are healed” (Isaiah 53:5).

Points to remember

  1. Sin“Woe to the bloody city!” 3:1God judged Nineveh for real blood, real lies, and real robbery, after long patience.
  2. Hidden sin“I will discover thy skirts upon thy face” 3:5Hidden sin comes out. Confessed sin finds mercy.
  3. Lessons“Art thou better than populous No?” 3:8If it happened to them, it can happen to me. Learn from other men's falls.
  4. Strength“There shall the fire devour thee” 3:15No amount of patching the walls saves a man from God. Only God does.
  5. Shepherds“Thy shepherds slumber” 3:18The world's shepherds sleep. The Good Shepherd never does.
  6. Mercy“Should not I spare Nineveh?” Jonah 4:11Nineveh had its chance and took it once. Mercy refused leaves no healing.

Question 1 of 6

Sin

Why was God's judgment on Nineveh just?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.”

Galatians 6:7

Answer

Because Nineveh had shed blood, lied, robbed and enslaved nations for generations, and nobody on earth could stop it. Everyone it touched was hurt. God gave it a hundred years after Jonah. What it sowed, it reaped. Judgment like that isn't cruelty. It's a just God refusing to let evil win.

Question 2 of 6

Hidden sin

What happens to sin that is covered up?

What verses tell us about this?

“But if ye will not do so, behold, ye have sinned against the LORD: and be sure your sin will find you out.”

Numbers 32:23

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

Answer

It comes out. God uncovered Nineveh in front of the whole world. But there's a better way: bring it out ourselves. Confess it to God, tell a trusted brother, and walk away from it. Covered sin rots. Confessed sin finds mercy.

Question 3 of 6

Lessons

What should Nineveh have learned from Thebes?

What verses tell us about this?

“Wherefore let him that thinketh he standeth take heed lest he fall.”

1 Corinthians 10:12

Answer

That no city is too strong to fall. Thebes had rivers, allies, and fame, and Nineveh itself tore it down. Nineveh should have said, “If them, then us.” The same goes for us: when we see another man fall, the right response isn't “not me,” but “Lord, keep me.”

Question 4 of 6

Strength

Can a man's own preparations save him from God's judgment?

What verses tell us about this?

“Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.”

Psalm 20:7

Answer

No. Nineveh filled its cisterns, patched its walls and baked new bricks, and the fire came anyway. A man can't work his way out from under God's judgment, and he doesn't have to. God offers what we can't build: forgiveness in Christ, received by faith.

Question 5 of 6

Shepherds

What happens to people when their shepherds sleep?

What verses tell us about this?

“Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep.”

Psalm 121:4

“I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep.”

John 10:11

Answer

They scatter, and nobody gathers them. That's what happened to Assyria. The LORD is a different Shepherd. He never sleeps, and Jesus laid down His life for His sheep. Whoever else has let you down, He won't.

Question 6 of 6

Mercy

Did Nineveh ever have a chance to turn?

What verses tell us about this?

“And God saw their works, that they turned from their evil way; and God repented of the evil, that he had said that he would do unto them; and he did it not.”

Jonah 3:10

“And should not I spare Nineveh, that great city, wherein are more than sixscore thousand persons that cannot discern between their right hand and their left hand; and also much cattle?”

Jonah 4:11

“Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?”

Ezekiel 33:11

Answer

Yes. God sent Jonah, and when Nineveh turned, He spared it. Then He waited another hundred years. God takes no pleasure in anyone's death. He would rather a man turn and live. The door Nineveh walked away from is still open to anyone who will come today.
