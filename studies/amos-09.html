Amos 9

I will raise up

Nobody can hide from God's judgment, but He will not utterly destroy. The book that opened with a lion's roar ends with a fallen house rebuilt and a harvest too big to bring in.

Part one

Nowhere to hide

Verses 1–6

The fifth and last vision: God at the altar.

No place to run

Quick reminder: Amos, a herdman from Judah, preached to Israel in the north about 760 BC. In his last vision he sees the LORD standing by the altar, likely at Bethel, the false shrine. God orders the doorframe struck so the whole building comes down on the worshippers. Judgment starts at the place of false worship.

  • dig into helldown to the grave, the deepest place
  • climb up to heaventhe highest place
  • the top of Carmela forested mountain full of caves
  • the bottom of the sea“thence will I command the serpent”
  • into captivityeven a far country

For a man running from God, that's terrifying. For a man who has come home to God, the same truth is the best news there is: you can never get anywhere He isn't.

“Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence? If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there.”

Psalm 139:7–8

The LORD is his name.

Three times in this book Amos stops in the middle of judgment and sings a hymn about who God is. Each one ends with the same line:

  1. Amos 4:13“formeth the mountains, and createth the wind”
  2. Amos 5:8“maketh the seven stars and Orion”
  3. Amos 9:6“buildeth his stories in the heaven”

He touches the land and it melts. His “stories” are His upper rooms, built in the sky. His “troop” is the vault of the heavens, set on the earth like a dome. He calls the sea and pours it out like a bucket. This isn't a local god of one shrine. He's God of everything, and no idol comes close.

Part two

Sifted, not lost

Verses 7–10

Judgment, with one great “saving that.”

Chosen isn't a free pass

Israel's favorite argument was, “God brought us out of Egypt. We're His people. We're safe.” God answers: I've moved other nations too.

  1. Israelout of Egypt
  2. The Philistinesout of Caphtor, an island in the Mediterranean, likely Crete
  3. The Syriansout of Kir, and Amos 1:5 said they'd be sent back there

The Ethiopians lived far away up the Nile. To God, Israel was no more automatically safe than they were. Being chosen was a calling to live like God's people, not a license to sin.

But then come two of the most merciful words in the book: “saving that.” The sinful kingdom would fall. The house of Jacob would not be utterly destroyed.

Shaken, but kept

A farmer shakes grain in a sieve. The dirt falls through. The grain stays.

God would shake Israel among the nations. The proud who said “The evil shall not overtake … us” (v. 10) would fall. Not one true grain would be lost.

Jesus used the same picture the night Peter denied Him:

“And the Lord said, Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat: But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Luke 22:31–32

Part three

I will raise up

Verses 11–15

The last word in Amos isn't judgment.

The apostles read this verse

The “tabernacle of David” is a booth, a hut: David's royal house fallen down to a shack. God promises to raise it up.

Amos 9:11–12, about 760 BC

In that day will I raise up the tabernacle of David that is fallen … That they may possess the remnant of Edom, and of all the heathen, which are called by my name, saith the LORD that doeth this.

Acts 15:16–17, about AD 49

After this I will return, and will build again the tabernacle of David, which is fallen down; and I will build again the ruins thereof, and I will set it up: That the residue of men might seek after the Lord, and all the Gentiles, upon whom my name is called, saith the Lord, who doeth all these things.

Must Gentiles become Jews to be saved? At the council in Jerusalem, James answered by quoting Amos. Gentiles coming to God by faith was what the prophets had said all along.

From a palace, to a ruin, to a risen King

Peter said it at Pentecost: God swore to David that one of his sons would sit on his throne, and David “spake of the resurrection of Christ” (Acts 2:30–31). Jesus is the Son of David whom God raised up. And He is building a house out of Jews and Gentiles together.

That's us. Men from every background, “called by my name.”

The curse turned around

Remember Amos 5:11? Here God turns those lines inside out.

Amos 5:11, judgment

Built, but never lived in

“Ye have built houses of hewn stone, but ye shall not dwell in them; ye have planted pleasant vineyards, but ye shall not drink wine of them.”

Amos 9:14, restoration

Built, and lived in

“They shall build the waste cities, and inhabit them; and they shall plant vineyards, and drink the wine thereof.”

The harvest will be so big the plowman catches up with the reaper: the next season's work starts before the last one is done. “The mountains shall drop sweet wine” is a farmer's picture of hills overflowing with crops. It's about God's blessing on the land, not about drinking.

These promises were made to Israel, and God will keep them to Israel. We don't claim them as our own guarantee. But they show us who He is: God keeps His word, and He rebuilds what sin tore down.

“And I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten, the cankerworm, and the caterpiller, and the palmerworm, my great army which I sent among you.”

Joel 2:25

The whole book in three lines

Amos 1:2The roar

“The LORD will roar from Zion, and utter his voice from Jerusalem”

Amos 5:4The call

“Seek ye me, and ye shall live”

Amos 9:15The promise

“I will plant them upon their land”

Amos is a hard book. God's judgment is real, and He told the truth about sin. But His mercy has the last word. The God who roars is the God who plants, and what He plants, He doesn't pull up.

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Points to remember

  1. God sees“Though they dig into hell, thence shall mine hand take them” 9:2You can't hide from God. For a man who comes home, that's the best news there is.
  2. Who God is“The LORD is his name” 9:6He's God of the heavens, the earth, and the sea, not a god of one shrine.
  3. God's mercy“Yet shall not the least grain fall upon the earth” 9:9God shakes, but He doesn't lose what is His.
  4. Christ“I will raise up the tabernacle of David” 9:11The apostles saw it fulfilled in Jesus, the risen Son of David.
  5. The nations“All the heathen, which are called by my name” 9:12Gentiles come in by faith. There's room for us.
  6. Restoration“They shall no more be pulled up” 9:15God keeps His word, and He rebuilds what sin tore down.

Question 1 of 6

God sees

Can anyone hide from God?

What verses tell us about this?

“Whither shall I go from thy spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence? If I ascend up into heaven, thou art there: if I make my bed in hell, behold, thou art there.”

Psalm 139:7–8

Answer

No. Not in the grave, not in the sky, not on a mountain or at the bottom of the sea, not in a far country. For a man running from God, that's a warning. For a man who comes home to Him, it's a comfort: there's nowhere you can go that He isn't with you.

Question 2 of 6

Who God is

Who is the God Amos served?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The LORD of hosts, who touches the land and it melts, builds His rooms in the heavens, and pours out the sea. Three times Amos stops to sing it: “The LORD is his name.” He's God over everything, and no idol comes close.

Question 3 of 6

God's mercy

Did God give up on Israel completely?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the Lord said, Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat: But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Luke 22:31–32

Answer

No. The sinful kingdom fell, “saving that I will not utterly destroy the house of Jacob.” God shook them among the nations like grain in a sieve, and not the least grain was lost. Jesus kept Peter the same way through his worst night.

Question 4 of 6

Christ

How did the apostles read Amos 9:11–12?

What verses tell us about this?

“And to this agree the words of the prophets; as it is written, After this I will return, and will build again the tabernacle of David, which is fallen down; and I will build again the ruins thereof, and I will set it up:”

Acts 15:15–16

Answer

As fulfilled in Jesus. David's house had fallen to a hut, with no king on the throne. God raised up Jesus, the Son of David, from the dead, and through Him He is building His house. James quoted Amos to show that what God was doing in the church was what the prophets had said.

Question 5 of 6

The nations

Is there room for Gentiles among God's people?

What verses tell us about this?

“Simeon hath declared how God at the first did visit the Gentiles, to take out of them a people for his name.”

Acts 15:14

“That the residue of men might seek after the Lord, and all the Gentiles, upon whom my name is called, saith the Lord, who doeth all these things.”

Acts 15:17

Answer

Yes. God always meant for the nations to be “called by my name.” The council in Jerusalem saw it happening: Gentiles saved by faith, without becoming Jews first. Whatever your background, there's room for you in God's house through Jesus.

Question 6 of 6

Restoration

What does God do with ruins?

What verses tell us about this?

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Answer

He rebuilds them. He promised Israel that the cities would be built and lived in, the vineyards planted and enjoyed, and the people planted for good, and He will keep that promise to Israel. It shows us who He is. The God who began a good work in you will finish it.
