Genesis 50

God meant it unto good

Jacob is mourned and buried where he asked. The brothers still fear Joseph, and he answers, “ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good.” Then Genesis ends with a coffin, and a promise.

Part one

“Joseph fell upon his father's face”

Verses 1–14

A son weeps, a great procession goes up, and Jacob is buried where he asked.

Joseph wept

Joseph was the second man in Egypt, and he fell on his father's face, wept, and kissed him. No one in the Bible tells a believing man not to grieve. He is allowed to weep.

“Jesus wept.”

John 11:35

That is the shortest verse in the English Bible, and it was written about the Son of God standing at the grave of a friend. If he cried, you are allowed to.

The men in this room carry griefs of their own: fathers, friends, children, years. Say who you miss. A believer does not grieve like a man with no hope, but he does grieve, and he does not have to do it alone. Tell your brothers in Christ.

Embalmed, and mourned for seventy days

  • embalmto treat a dead body with spices and oils so that it keeps
  • physiciansdoctors, here the household's own
  • fulfilledcompleted: forty days were filled up
  • threescore and tensixty plus ten: seventy

Egypt was famous for keeping its dead. Forty days for the embalming, and seventy for the mourning: a long public mourning, an honour for a man who had once saved the country.

Joseph used Egyptian ways to honour Jacob, but not Egyptian religion. He did not bury his father in Egypt. He carried him home.

A promise kept

Joseph spoke to “the house of Pharaoh”, not to Pharaoh himself. Some teachers think mourners could not go before the king, and that may be why.

But look at the words: if now I have found grace in your eyes. The second man in Egypt asks. And he says he is going to come again: he is not planning to leave Egypt. Pharaoh said yes, “according as he made thee swear.”

Seventeen years before, Joseph had put his hand under his father's thigh and promised (47:29–31). He kept the promise, even though it cost him weeks on the road. A man's word is worth the time it costs to keep.

A very great company

From Goshen up to Hebron: a long road with Pharaoh's servants, the elders of Egypt, chariots, horsemen, Joseph's brothers and their households.

The children, the flocks and the herds stayed in Goshen. That showed they meant to come back.

It was the funeral of a shepherd and a foreigner, and Egypt turned out for him, because of the son who had saved the country.

Atad, and “the mourning of the Egyptians”

Atad → Abel-mizraim “The mourning of Egypt”: abel means mourning. The Canaanites gave the place its name.

A threshingfloor is a flat, hard open ground where grain was beaten out. The Egyptians stopped there for a seven-day lament, so loud that the people of the land named the place. We do not know where Atad was, and the Bible says only that it was “beyond Jordan.” Teachers differ about which side of the river that means.

Lamentation is a loud, open cry of grief. Nobody there was ashamed of it. These were hard men, and they wept.

“His sons carried him”

The great company waited at Atad. Only his sons carried him into the cave at Machpelah, the same cave Jacob had asked for (49:29–30), beside Abraham and Sarah, Isaac and Rebekah, and Leah.

There were twelve sons, and the ones who once sold their brother stood beside him at their father's grave. A family that had been broken for years carried the same body.

Then Joseph went back to Egypt, as he had said he would. Funerals do not fix a family. But sometimes a grave is where a family finally stands together.

Part two

“Fear not”

Verses 15–21

Seventeen years of kindness, and the brothers still think the other shoe is about to drop.

Forgiven, and still afraid

the brothersJoseph will peradventure hate us, and will certainly requite us all the evil which we did unto him.

Joseph, in chapter 45Be not grieved, nor angry with yourselves, that ye sold me hither: for God did send me before you to preserve life. 45:5

Peradventure means perhaps. Requite means pay back. Joseph had already forgiven them seventeen years earlier, in this very country, and had fed their children ever since. Yet the moment their father died, they thought: now he will get us.

They never really believed they were forgiven. The guilt did not match the facts. You may know that feeling: God has said he will remember our sins no more (Hebrews 8:12), and you wait for the hammer to fall anyway.

“Forgive, I pray thee”

  • trespassstepping over a line, doing wrong
  • sinmissing the mark, rebellion
  • evil“they did unto thee evil”: they name it for what it is

They asked for forgiveness in the plainest words, and they called their act what it was: trespass, sin, evil. No excuses. And “Joseph wept when they spake unto him.” It broke his heart that they still thought he might not forgive.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

God is not like a man who might be saving up revenge. He is “faithful and just” to forgive. If you have confessed, you are forgiven. Do not keep waiting for the other shoe to drop.

“Am I in the place of God?”

Joseph has the power. He is the man who could settle the account. He says: am I in the place of God? Vengeance is not his to take.

“Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

Romans 12:19

When a man has been wronged, the hardest thing is to put the sword down and leave the judging with God. Joseph did it.

And “he comforted them, and spake kindly unto them”: the Hebrew is spoke to their hearts. He did not just let them off. He spoke to the fear.

“Ye thought evil… but God meant it unto good”

What they meant

Evil

“Ye thought evil against me.” They hated him, threw him in a pit and sold him. Joseph does not call it anything else.

What God meant

Good

“God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive.” The same event, with a second author behind it.

Joseph never calls what they did good. It was evil, and he says so. But God was working in it, and brought something saving out of it: Egypt fed, a family preserved, and a nation kept alive.

What this verse does and does not say

“And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.”

Romans 8:28

It does not say sin is good, or that we may do wrong so that good may come. It does not say every hurt was meant to happen. It says God is working in all of it, for the ones who love him.

“Him, being delivered by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God, ye have taken, and by wicked hands have crucified and slain:”

Acts 2:23

The greatest case is the cross. Wicked men did a wicked thing, and it was still God's plan to save us. Whatever you have done, or had done to you, is not too big for him to use. Bring it to him, and ask him to start with you.

Real world: how he forgave

  • He did not take God's place“Am I in the place of God?”
  • He told them the truth“Ye thought evil against me.”
  • He saw God's hand“God meant it unto good.”
  • He fed them“I will nourish you, and your little ones.”
  • He spoke kindly“He comforted them.”

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

Is there someone you have been waiting to hit back at? Or someone you hurt who is waiting to see if you will pay? Today, take one step: send the message, or make the call. Ask your pastor how, if you do not know where to start.

Part three

“God will surely visit you”

Verses 22–26

The last picture of Joseph: a long life, a short oath, and a box in Egypt.

One hundred and ten years

  1. age 17sold by his brothersGenesis 37
  2. age 30second to Pharaoh41:46
  3. age 39the family comes downabout 1875 BC
  4. about 56Jacob diesabout 1858 BC
  5. age 110Joseph diesabout 1800 BC

Joseph lived another fifty-four years after Jacob. He saw Ephraim's grandchildren, and Machir's children “brought up upon Joseph's knees.” That means he took them as his own, as Jacob had taken his sons.

Machir's family later settled east of the Jordan (Numbers 32:39). The boy sold at seventeen died a great-grandfather and the head of a family in Egypt.

“God will surely visit you”

Joseph's last words are not about himself. “I die: and God will surely visit you, and bring you out of this land unto the land which he sware to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.”

“By faith Joseph, when he died, made mention of the departing of the children of Israel; and gave commandment concerning his bones.”

Hebrews 11:22

Hebrews lists this among the great acts of faith. He had a palace, and a great name in Egypt. And he asked to be put in a box and carried out, because he believed God's word to Abraham that the land would be theirs. He wanted to rest in the promise.

Visit here means God coming to look after his people, to rescue them. A dying man could say it with all his heart, because he knew Whom he had trusted.

The bones that waited

  1. about 1800 BCJoseph dies“a coffin in Egypt”
  2. about 400 yearsin Egyptthe bones wait
  3. about 1446 BCthe ExodusMoses takes the bones
  4. about 1400 BCburied at Shechemin the land of promise

“And Moses took the bones of Joseph with him: for he had straitly sworn the children of Israel, saying, God will surely visit you; and ye shall carry up my bones away hence with you.”

Exodus 13:19

Straitly means strictly. A coffin is a chest or case for a body. Joseph's sat in Egypt for hundreds of years, a reminder to every Israelite that they were not at home. Then it went up with them, and was buried at Shechem (Joshua 24:32).

Where the book ends

Genesis opens with “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.” It ends with a dead man in a coffin, in Egypt.

That seems like a bad place to stop. But look at the last thing Joseph said: God will surely visit you. The book ends in a coffin and a promise. The coffin is what sin did. The promise is what God will do.

Every man in this room has a place where the story seems to have stopped: a box, a cell, a failure, a grave. The last word is not there. It is: God will surely visit you.

  2. Guilt“Joseph will peradventure hate us” 50:15A man can be forgiven and still be afraid. Trust the forgiveness that was given.
  3. Forgiveness“Fear not: for am I in the place of God?” 50:19Joseph left the judging to God, and spoke kindly to the men who wronged him.

Question 1 of 6

Grief

How did Joseph and Egypt mourn for Jacob?

What verses tell us about this?

“Jesus wept.”

John 11:35

Answer

Joseph wept on his father's face, and Egypt mourned for seventy days. A believer does not grieve as one with no hope, but he does grieve. If you carry a loss, say so, and tell your brothers in Christ and your pastor.

Question 2 of 6

Guilt

Why were the brothers still afraid, seventeen years later?

What verses tell us about this?

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

They had never believed they were forgiven. Joseph had been kind for seventeen years, yet they still waited to be paid back. If you have confessed to God, he is faithful and just to forgive. Believe it.

Question 3 of 6

Forgiveness

How did Joseph answer men who had wronged him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He did not take revenge. He left the judging to God, and fed them and spoke kindly. Forgiveness is not pretending nothing happened. It is laying down the sword and doing good. Ask the Lord for the strength to do it.

Question 4 of 6

God's purpose

What did Joseph mean by “ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good”?

What verses tell us about this?

“And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.”

Romans 8:28

Answer

That what they did was evil, and God was working in it for good: to save many people alive. It does not make sin good. The cross is the greatest example. Whatever was done to you, or by you, God is able to use it for good if you bring it to him.

Question 5 of 6

Hope

Why did Joseph ask them to carry his bones out of Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

“By faith Joseph, when he died, made mention of the departing of the children of Israel; and gave commandment concerning his bones.”

Hebrews 11:22

Answer

Because he believed God's promise that Israel would go home. Egypt was a refuge, not their land. He wanted to rest in the promise, and his bones left with Moses 400 years later. A man can die believing, because God keeps his word.

Question 6 of 6

The promise

How does Genesis end, and what comes next?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the LORD said, I have surely seen the affliction of my people which are in Egypt, and have heard their cry by reason of their taskmasters; for I know their sorrows;”

Exodus 3:7

Answer

With a coffin in Egypt and a promise. Exodus begins where Genesis leaves off, with Israel in slavery, and God coming down to keep his word: “I have surely seen the affliction of my people.”

Genesis, looking back

  1. chapters 1–2Creation“very good”
  2. chapter 3The falland a promise, 3:15
  3. chapters 6–11Flood and BabelNoah, then the nations scattered
  4. chapters 12–25Abrahamcalled, about 2090 BC
  5. chapters 25–36Isaac and Jacobthe promise handed down
  6. chapters 37–50Joseph, and Egyptabout 1875 BC

Fifty chapters, and one pattern: man sins, God does not walk away. He kills a lamb, closes the ark, calls Abram, answers Hagar, finds Jacob at Bethel, preserves Judah, and saves Joseph's family.

Each time a man failed, God came to him. That is what we have been reading. And the promise made in the garden has been narrowing down: a woman's seed, a man of faith, a nation, a tribe. Judah.

Genesis 3:15

The seed of the woman

Genesis 3 to Revelation 22. Everything in between, Abraham, Judah, Joseph's bones, Moses, David, the cross, is one God keeping one promise. “God will surely visit you.” He did. He came in Jesus, and he will come again.
