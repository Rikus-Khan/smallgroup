In chapters 19 and 20 Christ rode out as King of kings, the devil was thrown into the lake of fire, and the dead stood before a great white throne. Every enemy of God's people is gone.

Now the last two chapters of the Bible. After all the judgment, here is what God was building the whole time: home.

Part one

All things new

Verses 1–8

A new heaven and a new earth, a city coming down, and the voice of God: “Behold, I make all things new.”

No more sea

God promised it through Isaiah: “I create new heavens and a new earth” (Isaiah 65:17).

For John, the sea was what stood between him and everyone he loved. In this book it's also where the beast came from (13:1). No more sea: nothing will ever cut God's people off again.

A sentence, a court order, years of damage: whatever stands between you and someone you love, it won't be there for ever.

A city coming down

At Babel men tried to build their way up to heaven. Here God brings His city down. Nobody climbs up; God comes.

Jesus said, “I go to prepare a place for you” (John 14:2). Here it is, prepared.

And it comes as a bride: a real place, and the people in it, the church dressed for her wedding day. Verse 9 calls her “the Lamb’s wife.”

God moving in

  1. Genesis 3:8“the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day”Eden: lost by sin
  2. Exodus 25:8“that I may dwell among them”a tent in the camp
  3. John 1:14“the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us”Jesus
  4. 1 Corinthians 6:19“the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you”every believer
  5. Revelation 21:3“he will dwell with them”for ever

A tabernacle was a tent. In the wilderness God's tent stood in the middle of Israel's camp. The word John used for Jesus, “dwelt among us,” means pitched His tent. The whole Bible is God coming closer, until at last He moves in with His people and never leaves.

Gone, one by one

“And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes”

  1. tearsthe ones you cried where nobody could see. In a cell, in a truck, in the shower.wiped
  2. deaththe friend who overdosed. The funeral you couldn't go to. The child you buried.no more
  3. sorrowthe years you can't get back. The grief that sits on your chest in the morning.no more
  4. cryinga mother on the phone. A kid asking when Daddy's coming home.no more
  5. painthe body still paying for the old life. The ache that never quite leaves.no more

“for the former things are passed away.”

Who wipes them

Not time. Not a bottle, not a pill. God wipes them, with His own hand, the way a father wipes his boy's face. Isaiah saw it coming:

“He will swallow up death in victory; and the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from off all faces; and the rebuke of his people shall he take away from off all the earth: for the LORD hath spoken it.”

Isaiah 25:8

Until then, it's not weak to grieve. “Jesus wept” at His friend's grave (John 11:35), and He knew He was about to raise him. Believers grieve too, just not like men with nothing to hold on to:

“But I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope.”

1 Thessalonians 4:13

All things new, starting with you

2 Corinthians 5:17

Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.

Revelation 21:4–5

…for the former things are passed away. And he that sat upon the throne said, Behold, I make all things new.

Same words, same God. What He'll do to the whole world one day, He has already started in the man who trusts Christ. You are the first part of the new creation. The rest is coming.

On the cross Jesus said, “It is finished” (John 19:30): the price was paid. Here He says, “It is done”: the work is complete. He is Alpha and Omega, the A and the Z. He doesn't start what He won't finish (Philippians 1:6).

Thirsty men drink free

Every addiction is a thirst aimed at the wrong well (Jeremiah 2:13). God asks only one thing of the man who comes: that he's athirst. And the price is freely: “without money and without price” (Isaiah 55:1).

Who is the overcomer?

Not a super-Christian who never falls. John tells us exactly who:

“Who is he that overcometh the world, but he that believeth that Jesus is the Son of God?”

1 John 5:5

And look what he gets. “All things”, like an heir. And a Father: “he shall be my son.” Some of you never had a father who stayed. In the new creation God Himself calls you His son, and He stays.

The other list

Verse 8 is the hardest verse in the chapter, and God says it plainly, so we will too:

the fearfulunbelievingthe abominablemurdererswhoremongerssorcerersidolatersall liars

The fearful are cowards who wouldn't trust Christ, not men who feel afraid. All liars takes in every man here. It's what men are when they turn down the water and stay as they are, and it ends in the second death.

But verse 6, the offer, comes before verse 8. The door is open until the end. Paul wrote a list like this, then said:

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Part two

The bride, the Lamb's wife

Verses 9–21

An angel takes John up a high mountain to see the city: its gates, its walls, its foundations, its gold.

A tale of two cities

Revelation 17

Babylon, the harlot

One of the seven angels with the vials says, “Come hither; I will shew unto thee”… and carries John “into the wilderness.” She is “decked with gold and precious stones and pearls,” holding a golden cup “full of abominations.” And she falls.

Revelation 21

Jerusalem, the bride

One of the same seven angels says, “Come hither, I will shew thee”… and carries John to “a great and high mountain.” She has gold, precious stones and pearls too, and they're real. She has “the glory of God,” and she lasts for ever.

The world sells the same things God gives: beauty, riches, a good time. But its cup is poison, and its city burns. Every high you ever chased was a cheap copy of what God is keeping for you.

The city plan

Three gates on every side. Whatever direction a man comes from, there's a gate facing him.

On the gates, Israel's twelve tribes. Ezekiel saw a city like this and named its gates the same way (Ezekiel 48:31–34). Its name: “The LORD is there.”

In the foundations, the twelve apostles. Old Testament saints and New Testament believers: one people, one city.

A perfect cube

The other perfect cube in the Bible is the Holy of Holies, where the high priest went “alone once every year, not without blood” (Hebrews 9:7).

Now the whole city is the Holy of Holies. Everyone lives where only the high priest could go, because the Lamb's blood got them in.

Some teachers take the size literally; others see a picture of perfection (12 × 1,000; a wall of 144, 12 × 12). Either way, there's room for you.

Twelve stones

Garnished means decorated. Nobody is sure of every stone's color today; picture every color of light.

Israel had seen stones like these. The high priest carried twelve over his heart, in four rows, when he went in to God:

“And the stones shall be with the names of the children of Israel, twelve, according to their names, like the engravings of a signet; every one with his name shall they be according to the twelve tribes.”

Exodus 28:21

Pearl gates, gold street

21:21

Every gate one pearl

A pearl is how an oyster deals with a wound. Something gets in and hurts it, and it covers the hurt, layer on layer, until it's beautiful. The way into God's city is through a gate made from a wound, and we know whose wounds they were.

21:21

A street of gold

Gold is what men lie for, steal for, kill for. In God's city they pave the road with it and walk on it. It's so pure it's “as it were transparent glass”: nothing hidden, nothing fake.

What the world told you was worth everything is the pavement there. What's worth everything there is the Lamb.

Part three

The Lamb is the light

Verses 22–27

No temple, no sun, no night, no locked gates. And only one way in.

No temple, no sun

In the old temple a thick curtain kept men out of the holiest place. Here there's no building at all, because there's no distance left. God Himself is the temple.

And no sun. “The LORD shall be unto thee an everlasting light” (Isaiah 60:19). Every bit of light in that city comes from the Lamb who was slain.

Gates that never shut

Old cities barred their gates at sundown, because the dark was when enemies came. Here there's no night, so no lockdown: nothing to keep out, nobody kept in.

If you've done time, you know the sound of a door slamming at count. You'll never hear it again.

“Therefore thy gates shall be open continually; they shall not be shut day nor night…”

Isaiah 60:11

Whose names are in the book

Nothing that defiles gets in, and every one of us has defiled something. The way in isn't being clean. It's being written.

And it's the Lamb's book. He bought every name in it with His blood, and He doesn't write in pencil:

“Notwithstanding in this rejoice not, that the spirits are subject unto you; but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven.”

Luke 10:20

Take it to work today

21:4Grieve with hope

Name the loss you carry. Tell God about it, and tell one brother. It won't have the last word.

21:6Go to the fountain

When the thirst hits today, and it will, don't go back to the leaking well. Pray before you do anything else. The water is free.

21:27You're written in

Walk like a citizen of that city. Tell the truth today, even when a lie would be easier.

  2. Tears“God shall wipe away all tears” 21:4No more death, sorrow, crying or pain: the former things are passed away.
  3. New“Behold, I make all things new” 21:5What He started in every believer, He will finish in the whole world.
  4. Water“Of the fountain of the water of life freely” 21:6Anyone who is thirsty may drink, and it costs nothing.

Question 1 of 6

With us

Where will God live when all things are made new?

What verses tell us about this?

“And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also.”

John 14:3

Answer

With His people. God will dwell with men, they will be His people, and He will be their God. No temple is needed: God and the Lamb are the temple.

Question 2 of 6

Tears

What will God wipe away, and what will be no more?

What verses tell us about this?

“He will swallow up death in victory; and the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from off all faces; and the rebuke of his people shall he take away from off all the earth: for the LORD hath spoken it.”

Isaiah 25:8

Answer

God Himself will wipe away all tears. There will be no more death, sorrow, crying or pain: the former things are passed away. Until then we grieve, but with hope.

Question 3 of 6

New

What does He mean when He says, “Behold, I make all things new”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Answer

God will make a new heaven and a new earth, with everything sin broke put right. He has already begun it in every man who is in Christ, and He will finish it. His words are true and faithful.

Question 4 of 6

Water

Who may drink of the water of life, and what does it cost?

What verses tell us about this?

“Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath no money; come ye, buy, and eat; yea, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price.”

Isaiah 55:1

Answer

Anyone who is thirsty. It costs nothing: God gives it freely. Jesus paid for it, so all a man has to do is come and drink.

Question 5 of 6

The city

What is the holy city like?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It shines with the glory of God: twelve gates of pearl, twelve foundations of precious stones, and a street of pure gold like clear glass. God and the Lamb are its temple, the Lamb is its light, and its gates never shut.

Question 6 of 6

Written

Who will enter the city, and who will not?

What verses tell us about this?

“And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.”

1 Corinthians 6:11

Answer

Those written in the Lamb's book of life. Nothing that defiles will enter, and verse 8 names those who refuse the water. But the offer comes first: any sinner who comes to the Lamb is washed and written in.
