Numbers 8

The lamps, and the Levites taken instead of the firstborn

Aaron lights the seven lamps over against the candlestick, and then the LORD has the Levites washed, shaved, and offered before him: taken for his own, instead of the firstborn of all Israel. They serve from twenty and five years old, and after fifty they still keep the charge.

Part one

“Over against the candlestick”

Verses 1–4

Seven lamps, lit by Aaron, in a lampstand of beaten gold.

The seven lamps

  • the candlestickthe lampstand: one shaft with branches, and a lamp on each of the seven
  • “beaten work”the whole of it hammered out of one piece of gold: shaft and flowers (verse 4)

“And thou shalt make the seven lamps thereof: and they shall light the lamps thereof, that they may give light over against it.”

Exodus 25:37

Over against means facing, in front of: the lamps were set to throw their light forward.

The pattern, and the light

“Who serve unto the example and shadow of heavenly things, as Moses was admonished of God when he was about to make the tabernacle: for, See, saith he, that thou make all things according to the pattern shewed to thee in the mount.”

Hebrews 8:5

Moses made the candlestick “according unto the pattern which the LORD had shewed Moses” (verse 4). He did not design it, and he did not improve it. That is why Hebrews can call the tabernacle “the example and shadow of heavenly things”.

The light of the world

Traditional teaching reads the candlestick as a picture of Christ, the light in the holy place where no sun could reach, and the New Testament gives us the words:

“Then spake Jesus again unto them, saying, I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”

John 8:12

“Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid. Neither do men light a candle, and put it under a bushel, but on a candlestick; and it giveth light unto all that are in the house. Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.”

Matthew 5:14–16

He is the light of the world, and he says to his people, “Ye are the light of the world”. A man who has been in the dark knows what a lit lamp is worth to the man coming in behind him.

Part two

“The Levites shall be mine”

Verses 5–22

How the LORD took a tribe for himself, and how it was done.

Cleansed before they served

  • water of purifyingwater for cleansing, sprinkled on them. Purifying means making clean
  • “shave all their flesh”a razor over the whole body: a new start, with nothing left of the old growth
  • wash their clothesthe clothes they came in were not the clothes they served in
  • “so make themselves clean”they took part in it themselves
  • two bullocksa bullock is a young bull: one with its meat offering of fine flour and oil, and one for a sin offering (verse 8)

Moses does not say why the razor. He says what: water, razor, clean clothes, and then blood for sin. The order matters. Nobody was handed a job at the tabernacle and told to clean himself up later. The LORD cleansed them first.

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh; And having an high priest over the house of God; Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.”

Hebrews 10:19–22

Our hearts sprinkled, our bodies washed with pure water: the same order. Clean first, then serve.

Hands on, from one to the next

the children of Israelput their hands uponthe Leviteslay their hands uponthe two bullocksone for a sin offering, one for a burnt offering: an atonement

The whole assembly was gathered, and the Levites brought before the tabernacle (verse 9), and the people put their hands on the men who would serve for them. Then the Levites put their hands on the bullocks. A hand laid on means: this one stands for me. The people's hands on the Levites, the Levites' hands on the bullocks, and the blood for “an atonement for the Levites” (verse 12).

Aaron offered the Levites “before the LORD for an offering of the children of Israel” (verse 11). The offering here is the men themselves, given over to God to serve.

“I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.”

Romans 12:1

Taken instead of the firstborn

Under the LORD’s claimsince the night in Egypt

Free to go homewith their families

the firstborn of Israel“mine, both man and beast” (verse 17)
the Levites“for all the firstborn” (verse 18)

The LORD takes the Levites, and the firstborn go home. A tribe stands in the place of the first son of every house.

“Sanctify unto me all the firstborn, whatsoever openeth the womb among the children of Israel, both of man and of beast: it is mine.”

Exodus 13:2

Verse 17 tells the story: “on the day that I smote every firstborn in the land of Egypt I sanctified them for myself”, and they were his from that night (Numbers 3:13 says it too). Now he says he will take the Levites in their place. A firstborn is the first son; to sanctify is to set apart for him. And the law was still standing when Jesus was a baby: “Every male that openeth the womb shall be called holy to the Lord” (Luke 2:23).

“That there be no plague”

Verse 19 gives one more reason. The Levites were a gift to Aaron and his sons, to do the service of Israel and “to make an atonement for the children of Israel: that there be no plague among the children of Israel, when the children of Israel come nigh unto the sanctuary”. Nigh means near.

A holy place is dangerous to come near unprepared; Nadab and Abihu had learned that (Leviticus 10:1–2). The Levites stood between the people and the sanctuary and kept the people from drawing near in the wrong way.

We do not need a go-between of that kind any more. The one Mediator has made the way, and Paul says in Hebrews that we may draw near with a true heart (Hebrews 10:22).

Part three

“From twenty and five years old”

Verses 23–26

How long a Levite served, and what he did after.

Twenty-five to fifty, and after

Waiting upon the servicefrom twenty and five years old
Keeping the chargewith his brethren, and no heavy service
2550 and beyond
  • “wait upon”serve, be on hand for the work
  • “the charge”the duty given to a man to look after
  • “shall do no service”the heavy work of the tabernacle was for the younger men

A man did not stop being a Levite at fifty. The work changed. He still ministered with his brethren and kept the charge: the older man stood beside the younger and watched over what mattered.

“Those that be planted in the house of the LORD shall flourish in the courts of our God. They shall still bring forth fruit in old age; they shall be fat and flourishing;”

Psalm 92:13–14

Real world: washed first, then given work

  • SinThe man who thinks his past rules him out of any service for God. The Levites had to be made clean too: water, razor, clean clothes and blood.
  • RegretYears spent in the wrong life, and a feeling that it is too late to be of use. The Levite began at twenty-five and finished at fifty, and kept the charge after that.
  • GraceThe LORD did the cleansing, and then said: “the Levites shall be mine”. He takes a man for himself, in the place of another, and does not ask where he has been.
  • HopeWe have “boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus” (Hebrews 10:19). The Levites needed water, a razor, blood and a priest’s hands before they could go in; he has done it all, once, for good.
  • TodayBe clean before you serve: confess it, and turn from it. Then do one plain job for your brothers today, something that needs doing.

Points to remember

  1. The lamps“according unto the pattern which the LORD had shewed Moses, so he made the candlestick” 8:4Beaten gold, and made as the LORD showed him: Moses did not improve on the pattern.
  2. The cleansing“Sprinkle water of purifying upon them, and let them shave all their flesh, and let them wash their clothes, and so make themselves clean” 8:7Cleansed first, then served: the order is the LORD's.
  3. The hands“the children of Israel shall put their hands upon the Levites” 8:10The people's hands on the Levites, the Levites' hands on the bullocks: someone stands for us.
  4. The firstborn“I have taken the Levites for all the firstborn of the children of Israel” 8:18The LORD took a tribe for himself in the place of the first son of every house.
  5. The obedience“as the LORD had commanded Moses concerning the Levites, so did they unto them” 8:22Every step was done as it was commanded.
  6. The years“from twenty and five years old and upward they shall go in to wait upon the service” 8:24A man serves in his strength, and still keeps the charge after fifty.

Question 1 of 6

The lamps

How was the candlestick made, and after what pattern?

What verses tell us about this?

“Who serve unto the example and shadow of heavenly things, as Moses was admonished of God when he was about to make the tabernacle: for, See, saith he, that thou make all things according to the pattern shewed to thee in the mount.”

Hebrews 8:5

Answer

Of beaten gold, all one beaten work, shaft and flowers, after the pattern the LORD showed Moses. He made it as he was shown.

Question 2 of 6

The cleansing

How were the Levites cleansed?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Water of purifying sprinkled on them, the whole body shaved, their clothes washed, and a bullock for a sin offering with another for a burnt offering.

Question 3 of 6

The hands

Whose hands were laid on the Levites, and whose on the bullocks?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The children of Israel put their hands on the Levites, and the Levites laid their hands on the heads of the bullocks, offered for a sin offering and a burnt offering to make an atonement for them.

Question 4 of 6

The firstborn

Whose place did the Levites take?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The firstborn of all the children of Israel, who were the LORD's since the night he smote the firstborn of Egypt.

Question 5 of 6

The obedience

Was it all done as the LORD commanded?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Yes. Aaron lit the lamps as he was told, and Moses, Aaron and the whole congregation did to the Levites all that the LORD had commanded.

Question 6 of 6

The years

At what age did a Levite begin to serve, and what was he to do after fifty?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He began at twenty-five. At fifty he ceased the heavy service, but still ministered with his brethren to keep the charge.
