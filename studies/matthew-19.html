Matthew 19

With God all things are possible

Jesus leaves Galilee for the last time. He answers a trap about divorce, welcomes little children, and watches a rich young man walk away sad.

Part one

From the beginning

Verses 1–12

A new place, an old argument, and Jesus takes it back to the Garden.

Leaving Galilee

Galilee had been His home base. Now He leaves it, and Matthew doesn't show Him teaching there again before the cross.

“Coasts” means the region. “Judaea beyond Jordan” is the land east of the river, called Perea. Galilean pilgrims often came down this side, then crossed near Jericho and climbed to Jerusalem.

Herod Antipas ruled here, the man who had John the Baptist killed (14:1–12).

“Behold, we go up to Jerusalem; and the Son of man shall be betrayed unto the chief priests and unto the scribes, and they shall condemn him to death,”

Matthew 20:18

The last road to Jerusalem

  1. Galileeteaching and healingchapters 4–18
  2. beyond Jordantoday's chapterchapter 19
  3. Jerichotwo blind men see20:29–34
  4. Jerusalemthe King rides in21:1–11
  5. the crossand the empty tombchapters 26–28, about AD 30

Every step from here on is toward the cross, and Jesus knows it. Even so, “great multitudes followed him; and he healed them there.” He kept giving all the way.

A trap with two sides

To “put away” a wife meant to divorce her. The law of Moses let a man give a bill of divorce if he found “some uncleanness” in her (Deuteronomy 24:1). The rabbis argued over what that meant.

One school (Hillel)

“for every cause”

Almost any reason would do. A later Jewish law book even names spoiling his dinner.

The other school (Shammai)

only for unfaithfulness

“Uncleanness” meant sexual sin, and nothing less.

Whichever side Jesus took, half the crowd would turn on Him. And they were standing in Herod's land, where John had lost his head for speaking about Herod's marriage. They were “tempting” Him: testing, hoping He'd trip.

Back to the beginning

They asked about Moses. Jesus went further back, to the Garden before sin, and quoted Genesis 1:27 and 2:24:

“Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh.”

Genesis 2:24

“Twain” means two. “Cleave” means hold fast, be glued to. Marriage isn't just a contract two people sign. God does the joining.

Commanded, or allowed?

The Pharisees said

“Moses… command”

As if divorce were something God ordered.

Jesus said

“Moses… suffered you”

Suffered means allowed, put up with. Moses allowed it because hearts were hard.

The “writing of divorcement” was a written paper the man had to put in his wife's hand. It protected her: it proved she was free and could marry again, instead of being thrown out with nothing.

God's law set limits on what hard hearts would do anyway. But “from the beginning it was not so.” God's plan was one man and one woman, for life.

A hard word, read with care

Jesus didn't take either school's side. He held up God's design and named one exception: “fornication,” sexual unfaithfulness. He said the same in the Sermon on the Mount (5:32).

Many men in rooms like this one have a divorce behind them. Some were wronged, some did the wronging, and some of it happened before they knew the Lord. This slide is not here to rule on anyone's story. Take your own situation to the Lord, and to your pastor.

Here's what we can say plainly: divorce is not the unforgivable sin. The blood of Jesus covers this too.

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Single, for the kingdom

The disciples were stunned: if marriage is that binding, maybe a man is better off never marrying. Jesus answers that staying single is a calling, given to some.

“so born”from birthMen who could not marry or father children because of how they were born.
“made… of men”by othersA eunuch was a man who had been castrated. Kings did this to servants who worked around the royal women.
“for the kingdom”by choiceMen who choose to stay unmarried to give themselves fully to God's work.

A single man is not half a man. Paul was single, and he called it a gift: “every man hath his proper gift of God” (1 Corinthians 7:7).

Part two

What lack I yet?

Verses 13–22

Little children who had nothing get the kingdom. A young man who had everything walks away.

Let them come

“Suffer” → let them In the King James, suffer often means allow. Jesus is saying: let them come, don't stop them.

Parents brought their children for a blessing, the way Jewish fathers laid hands on their sons (Genesis 48:14). The disciples thought Jesus was too busy for kids. Mark says He was “much displeased” with them (Mark 10:14).

Just one chapter ago He set a child in the middle of them (18:2). Children bring nothing and earn nothing. “Of such is the kingdom.”

What shall I do?

Luke tells us he was “a certain ruler” (Luke 18:18). Young, rich, important, and still not sure he had eternal life.

“Why callest thou me good?” Jesus isn't denying that He is good, or that He is God. He's making the man think about what he just said. Only God is truly good. If Jesus is good, then the man should listen to whatever He says next.

The man asks what to do, so Jesus answers on those terms: keep the commandments. Not because the law can save, but because the law shows a man where he stands.

“Therefore by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight: for by the law is the knowledge of sin.”

Romans 3:20

The ones Jesus didn't list

Jesus listed

how you treat your neighbour

No murder, no adultery, no stealing, no false witness, honour your parents, love your neighbour. The young man said, “Kept them all.”

Jesus left out

God first, and “Thou shalt not covet”

The first commandment and the last. Those were the ones about to be tested.

“Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour’s house, thou shalt not covet thy neighbour’s wife, nor his manservant, nor his maidservant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any thing that is thy neighbour’s.”

Exodus 20:17

“What lack I yet?” Something in him knew that keeping rules hadn't settled it.

He went away sad

“Then Jesus beholding him loved him, and said unto him, One thing thou lackest…”

Mark 10:21

Jesus loved him, and put His finger on the one thing his heart held tighter than God. He doesn't ask every man to sell everything. He asked this man, because his money was his god.

For some of us it was a bottle, a pill, a woman, or our pride. Whatever it is, He loves us too much to let us keep it in His place.

Part three

Who then can be saved?

Verses 23–30

A camel, a needle, and the only One who can do the impossible.

A camel through the eye of a needle.

“Hardly” means with great difficulty. The camel was the biggest animal in the land, and a needle's eye the smallest opening anyone knew. Jesus means: impossible. That's how the disciples heard it (v. 25).

Riches aren't sin. But money makes a man feel he doesn't need anyone, not even God. And a man who doesn't think he needs saving won't come to be saved.

Rich men can come. Matthew later tells of “a rich man of Arimathaea, named Joseph,” a disciple who buried Jesus (27:57). With God, the camel goes through.

Impossible, and possible

People thought wealth was a sign of God's favor. If the blessed rich man can't get in, “who then can be saved?”

With men

impossible

Not by money, not by rule-keeping, not by willpower. Most of us tried that last one for years.

With God

all things are possible

He saves the rich man, the poor man, the addict, and the Pharisee, the same way: by grace, through faith.

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

What shall we have?

Peter had watched the rich man walk away from everything, and he thought: we did what he wouldn't. Boats, nets, a tax booth. What's in it for us? Jesus doesn't scold him. He answers.

“The regeneration” means the making new of all things, the day the Son of man sits on “the throne of his glory” and sets the world right. Jesus doesn't give us a calendar or the details here, and we shouldn't invent them.

What He does say is plain: the men who left their nets to follow Him will share in His rule. Fishermen and a tax collector, on thrones. Nothing given up for Jesus is lost.

An hundredfold

A hundred times back. Mark adds two words Matthew leaves out:

“But he shall receive an hundredfold now in this time, houses, and brethren, and sisters, and mothers, and children, and lands, with persecutions; and in the world to come eternal life.”

Mark 10:30

Not a get-rich promise: “with persecutions.” Some of us lost family and friends along the way. God gives back brothers, fathers, homes, and tables in His family. Look around this group.

First and last

first→lastThe rich young man, first in everyone's eyes, walked away.
last→firstChildren, fishermen, a tax collector: last in line, first in the kingdom.

God doesn't rank men the way the world does. Jesus says it again at the end of His next story, about workers hired at the last hour (20:1–16). Nobody is too late, and nobody gets in by being first.

Points to remember

  1. Marriage“What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder” 19:6God made marriage one man and one woman, joined by Him for life.
  2. Hard hearts“Because of the hardness of your hearts” 19:8Moses allowed divorce because of hard hearts. From the beginning it was not so.
  3. Children“Suffer little children… to come unto me” 19:14Jesus is never too busy for the small. The kingdom belongs to such as these.
  4. Riches“He went away sorrowful: for he had great possessions” 19:22Whatever we hold tighter than Jesus will cost us Him.
  5. Salvation“With God all things are possible” 19:26No man can save himself. God can save anyone.
  6. Reward“Shall receive an hundredfold, and shall inherit everlasting life” 19:29Nothing given up for Jesus is lost.

Question 1 of 6

Marriage

What did God mean marriage to be from the beginning?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

One man and one woman, made male and female by God, leaving their parents and holding fast to each other as one flesh. God Himself does the joining, so it's meant to last for life.

Question 2 of 6

Hard hearts

Why did Moses allow a writing of divorcement?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because of the hardness of men's hearts. Moses didn't command divorce; he allowed it and set limits on it, and the written bill protected the wife. But it was never God's plan. From the beginning it was not so.

Question 3 of 6

Children

How did Jesus treat the little children His disciples turned away?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He welcomed them. He told His disciples to let them come, laid His hands on them, and said the kingdom of heaven belongs to such as these. Nobody is too small or too unimportant for Jesus.

Question 4 of 6

Riches

What kept the rich young man from following Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

His great possessions. He thought he had kept the commandments, but his money held first place in his heart. When Jesus asked him to let it go and follow, he walked away sad. Whatever we won't let go of becomes our god.

Question 5 of 6

Salvation

If it is impossible with men, how can anyone be saved?

What verses tell us about this?

“For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.”

Ephesians 2:8–9

Answer

Only by God. No man can save himself by money, rules, or willpower. But with God all things are possible. He saves anyone who comes to Jesus by faith, as a free gift.

Question 6 of 6

Reward

What does Jesus promise those who leave things behind to follow Him?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A hundred times back, and everlasting life. Not easy riches, but a new family of brothers and a place in His kingdom when He sets all things right. Nothing given up for Jesus is lost.
