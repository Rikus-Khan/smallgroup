Numbers 33

The journeys of Israel, written down

“And Moses wrote their goings out according to their journeys by the commandment of the LORD.” Forty-two names, from Rameses to the plains of Moab, every camp in order: the oasis, the dry place, the grave, and the last night before the land.

Part one

“These are the journeys”

Verses 1–15

Out of Rameses, through the sea, and on to Sinai.

Moses kept the record

  • who wrote it“Moses wrote their goings out”: the man who led them also kept the diary
  • by whose word“by the commandment of the LORD”: this list was not Moses' idea; God told him to write it
  • their goings outthe places they set out from, one after another

The whole road, written down, in the order they walked it. Nothing is tidied up. And the same words stand at Rephidim: “journeyed… after their journeys, according to the commandment of the LORD” (Exodus 17:1). They were led, and they were told to remember it.

Count the names from Rameses to the plains of Moab and there are forty-two: Rameses, and forty-one camps after it. Only a few can be put on a map by what the text itself says. Those are lit: Rameses; the sea they passed through; Sinai; Ezion-gaber; mount Hor; and the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho. The rest are names, and we will not guess where they stood.

The morning after

  1. The nightThe firstborn smittenExodus 12:29
  2. 15th day, 1st month“went out with an high hand”Numbers 33:3
  3. 3rd monthIsrael comes to SinaiExodus 19:1
  4. 40th year, 1st day, 5th monthAaron dies on mount HorNumbers 33:38

“An high hand” is a lifted hand: openly, boldly, in front of everyone. They did not slip out of Egypt in the dark. “In the sight of all the Egyptians” the LORD brought them out, on the day after the passover.

“For I will pass through the land of Egypt this night, and will smite all the firstborn in the land of Egypt, both man and beast; and against all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgment: I am the LORD.”

Exodus 12:12

Verse 4 looks back at that night. Egypt buried its firstborn, and its gods were judged too. The men who had served those gods now stood outside the door, free.

Three places the text fixes

  1. Rameses“they departed from Rameses” (verse 3)
  2. The sea“passed through the midst of the sea” (verse 8), by Nuweiba
  3. Sinai“the wilderness of Sinai” (verse 15), the mount in Arabia

This study follows Ron Wyatt's research for these two: the crossing at Nuweiba and Mount Sinai at Jabal al-Lawz in Arabia (“mount Sinai in Arabia”, Galatians 4:25). His research only puts a place to what the Bible already names; the teaching stays with the text. Etham is “in the edge of the wilderness”, and the stops between are not drawn: Moses gives their names, not their positions.

The first twelve names are ones you already know from Exodus: the places and chapters are beside each name. Moses writes “pitched” twenty-nine times and “encamped” thirteen times in verses 5–49, and every one of them is a night the LORD brought them through.

Bitter, sweet and dry

Marah

verse 8

Three days' journey in the wilderness, and bitter water at the end of it: “they could not drink of the waters of Marah” (Exodus 15:23).

Elim

verse 9

“Twelve fountains of water, and threescore and ten palm trees”: twelve springs and seventy palms. A fountain is a spring.

Rephidim

verse 14

“Where was no water for the people to drink.” Dry again, after the oasis.

Three camps, three kinds of night, in the order Moses wrote them: bitter, plenty, dry. The LORD led them to all three. The road to the promised land did not go from oasis to oasis. If you are in a dry place today, that does not mean you took a wrong turn.

Only Kibroth-hattaavah, Hazeroth and Ezion-gaber have another chapter beside them here. For the rest, Moses gives the name and nothing more in this chapter.

Most of the road was ordinary

From Rithmah to Ebronah, seventeen names in a row, and Moses tells no story about one of them. He doesn't say how long they stayed at any. The only length he gives anywhere is the whole:

“And the space in which we came from Kadesh-barnea, until we were come over the brook Zered, was thirty and eight years; until all the generation of the men of war were wasted out from among the host, as the LORD sware unto them.”

Deuteronomy 2:14

Moses doesn't say which camps fell in those years. He wrote the names, and the LORD kept the people through every one. Most of a man's road is like this: not a miracle, not a disaster, just another camp and the next morning. The LORD is in those too.

Kadesh, and the graves of lust

  • Kibroth-hattaavahverse 16. In chapter 11 “they buried the people that lusted” (11:34). Moses writes the name like any other.
  • The next line“And they departed from Kibroth-hattaavah, and encamped at Hazeroth” (verse 17). The graves were not the last camp.
  • “The wilderness of Zin, which is Kadesh”verse 36. Moses gives the place no more than its name here; it is where Miriam died (20:1).

The bad nights are in the record. God doesn't edit his people's road to make it look better, and he doesn't leave them there either. Each failure ends with the same short sentence: they departed, and encamped at the next place.

Real world: write down your own road

God had Moses keep a list. You can keep one too.

  • SinSome of your camps you would sooner forget: the nights, the places, the people. They were real, and they are on the list.
  • RegretMoses did not leave Kibroth-hattaavah out to spare Israel's feelings. He wrote it, and then he wrote the next camp.
  • Grace“All the way which the LORD thy God led thee” (Deuteronomy 8:2). Even the bitter and the dry places were on a road he was leading.
  • HopeYou are not at the end of the list. After the worst camp on it there is still a line that begins “and they departed.”
  • TodayTake ten minutes and write three places God has brought you through, with the year. Read them to a brother. “Hitherto hath the LORD helped us” (1 Samuel 7:12).

Part three

“Pitched in the plains of Moab”

Verses 37–56

Aaron on mount Hor, the last camp by Jordan, and the LORD's word about the land.

Aaron, a hundred and twenty and three

83Aaron's age before Pharaoh (Exodus 7:7)
+ 40“in the fortieth year after the children of Israel were come out”
123his age when he died on mount Hor

The Bible's own ages fit: Aaron was fourscore and three when he and Moses spoke to Pharaoh, and an hundred and twenty and three when he died, forty years later. He died “at the commandment of the LORD”, “in the first day of the fifth month”, on the mount at the edge of Edom. The story of his death is in chapter 20 (20:23–29).

This is the only date in the whole list besides the first. Moses marks the leaving of Egypt and the death of his brother, the beginning and the near end of the forty years.

Verse 40 puts king Arad in a single line: he “heard of the coming of the children of Israel”. His fight is in chapter 21. “Iim” in verse 45 is the short form of Ije-abarim (verse 44). Verse 49 says how far the last camp stretched along the river: “from Beth-jesimoth even unto Abel-shittim”.

Three more places the text fixes

  1. Ezion-gaber“beside Eloth, on the shore of the Red sea, in the land of Edom” (1 Kings 9:26)
  2. Mount Hor“in the edge of the land of Edom” (verse 37)
  3. The plains of Moab“by Jordan near Jericho” (verse 48)

The line on the map joins the places the text fixes. It is not the road: Moses names the camps between and does not say where they were. From mount Hor Israel went round the land of Edom (21:4) to the plains of Moab.

A road of camps, not a home

Forty-two tents pitched and taken down. Hebrews says the believers who went before us lived this way, and Peter says it of us.

“These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off, and were persuaded of them, and embraced them, and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth.”

Hebrews 11:13

“Dearly beloved, I beseech you as strangers and pilgrims, abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul;”

1 Peter 2:11

A pilgrim does not unpack as if the camp were home. He keeps his eyes on where the road goes, and he does not carry what weighs him down.

“I have given you the land”

  • “When ye are passed over Jordan”the LORD speaks in the plains of Moab, with the river still in front of them
  • “drive out… dispossess”take the land from those living in it
  • picturescarved stones and figures the nations bowed to
  • molten imagesidols melted and cast in a mould
  • high placesthe hilltop shrines where they worshipped
  • “quite pluck down”pull down completely

The first word is a gift: “I have given you the land to possess it.” The second is what to do with the idols: God's people were not to keep the places where other gods were served.

“Thou shalt not bow down to their gods, nor serve them, nor do after their works: but thou shalt utterly overthrow them, and quite break down their images.”

Exodus 23:24

By lot

The land is to be divided “by lot”, and the lot is no chance: “every man's inheritance shall be in the place where his lot falleth.” The bigger families got the bigger share, the smaller the smaller, and each man got the place the LORD gave him.

“The lot is cast into the lap; but the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD.”

Proverbs 16:33

“By lot was their inheritance, as the LORD commanded by the hand of Moses, for the nine tribes, and for the half tribe.”

Joshua 14:2

Next, in chapter 34, the LORD draws the borders and names the men who will do the dividing.

What you let remain

thenpricks in your eyes, thorns in your sides

Those they let remain “shall be pricks in your eyes, and thorns in your sides, and shall vex you” (verse 55). A prick is a sharp point, a goad; to vex is to torment. What they let stay would not stay quiet. And the last sentence is the hard one: “I shall do unto you, as I thought to do unto them.” God was not playing favourites between nations. A people that took on their ways would share their end.

“Wherefore I also said, I will not drive them out from before you; but they shall be as thorns in your sides, and their gods shall be a snare unto you.”

Judges 2:3

Real world: the thing you left for later

Every man in recovery knows a “remain”: the one old habit, contact or route home you meant to deal with later.

  • SinIt is rarely the big thing that undoes a man. It is the small one he let stay, the one he called harmless, until it became a thorn in his side.
  • RegretIf one has already pricked you, that is not the end. The people did let some remain, and the LORD did not stop being their God.
  • GraceThe same verse that gives the warning begins with the gift: “I have given you the land.” He goes first. You are not asked to clear the ground alone.
  • Hope“Let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us” (Hebrews 12:1). You can lay it down, today.
  • TodayName one thing you have left in place. Tell a brother in Christ, or your pastor, and take one step to remove it today: delete the number, change the route, close the account.

Points to remember

  1. The record“Moses wrote their goings out according to their journeys by the commandment of the LORD” 33:2The whole road was written down, by Moses, at God's command.
  2. The start“on the morrow after the passover the children of Israel went out with an high hand” 33:3Out in the open, in front of Egypt, the morning after the passover; and the gods of Egypt were judged.
  3. The sea“passed through the midst of the sea into the wilderness” 33:8The sea, then three days in the wilderness, then bitter water: he leads through hard places too.
  4. The death“Aaron the priest went up into mount Hor at the commandment of the LORD, and died there” 33:38An hundred and twenty and three years old, in the fortieth year: the LORD's timing, to the day.
  5. The last camp“the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho” 33:48The last camp stood at the river, with the land in sight, and the LORD spoke there.

Question 1 of 6

The record

Who wrote down the journeys of Israel, and by whose commandment?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Moses wrote them, by the commandment of the LORD. The man who led Israel kept the record of the road, in the order they walked it.

Question 2 of 6

The start

On what day did Israel leave Rameses, and what had the LORD done to the gods of Egypt?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

On the fifteenth day of the first month, the morrow after the passover, with an high hand in the sight of all the Egyptians. The LORD had executed judgments upon the gods of Egypt, as well as smiting their firstborn.

Question 3 of 6

The sea

What happened between Pi-hahiroth and Marah?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They departed from before Pi-hahiroth and passed through the midst of the sea into the wilderness, and went three days' journey in the wilderness of Etham, and pitched in Marah.

Question 4 of 6

The death

Who died on mount Hor, when, and how old was he?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Moses was fourscore years old, and Aaron fourscore and three years old, when they spake unto Pharaoh.”

Exodus 7:7

Answer

Aaron the priest, at the commandment of the LORD, in the fortieth year after they came out of Egypt, on the first day of the fifth month. He was an hundred and twenty and three years old.

Question 5 of 6

The last camp

Where did the last camp stand, and what did the LORD say to Moses there?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

In the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho, along the river from Beth-jesimoth to Abel-shittim. The LORD told Moses to tell Israel what to do when they were passed over Jordan into Canaan: drive out the inhabitants and divide the land by lot.

Question 6 of 6

The warning

What would the people left in the land become if Israel let them remain?

What verses tell us about this?

Judges 2:3

Answer

Pricks in their eyes and thorns in their sides, to vex them in the land. And the LORD said he would do to Israel as he thought to do to them.
