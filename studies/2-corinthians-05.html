A new creature

A tent that will come down, a house that will not, and the verse every man in recovery needs to hear: old things are passed away.

Chapter 4 ended by looking at the things that are not seen. Chapter 5 shows what is waiting there, and what to do with the days while we wait.

Paul wrote this letter from Macedonia in about AD 56, a year after 1 Corinthians. In between came a sharp letter, a painful visit, and a long wait for news. Titus brought good news at last (7:6–7), and this letter is Paul pouring out his heart.

Part one

A tent and a house

Verses 1–8

What waits on the other side of a worn-out body.

Camping out

Paul calls the body a tabernacle: a tent. He knew tents, because he made them for a living (Acts 18:3). A tent is good for the road and not for forever.

When the tent is dissolved, taken down, there's a building of God waiting. Not rented, not temporary, eternal in the heavens.

Paul is honest: we groan. Bad backs, bad lungs, a body some of us wore out young. He doesn't say it doesn't hurt. He says it isn't the end, and he's not looking to be a ghost, but clothed upon with something better. “Mortality swallowed up of life.”

A down payment

Today“The earnest of the Spirit”: God's Spirit living in you.
→
On that dayAll that He promised, paid in full.

Earnest is an old word for a deposit, the money you put down to say this one is mine. God has put down His Spirit in every believer, as a guarantee that the rest is coming.

“Which is the earnest of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, unto the praise of his glory.”

Ephesians 1:14

If you were afraid you might lose it all the next time you fall: God doesn't put down a deposit and then walk away from the deal. He finishes what He starts.

From this body to the Lord

Paul says to be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord. One step. No in-between he tells us about.

“And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.”

Luke 23:43

We walk by faith, not by sight is how every day goes until then: you can't see Him, but you can trust Him. When you bury a brother who died in Christ, this is what you can say.

Part two

Before the Lord

Verses 9–15

Every one of us will stand before Christ. Here's what that is, and what it is not.

The judgment seat of Christ

Not the question

“Am I in?”

That was settled at the cross. The man who believes “shall not come into condemnation” (John 5:24).

The question

“What did I do with it?”

The life He gave back, the years, the gifts. “The things done in his body.”

The Greek word is bēma: a raised platform where a judge sat. The Corinthians knew it well. There was a bēma in their own marketplace, where Paul himself had once been dragged (Acts 18:12). It was also where prizes were handed out.

“If any man's work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss: but he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire.”

1 Corinthians 3:15

Notice the words: he himself shall be saved. A believer can lose reward but not his place. And “now no condemnation” still stands (Romans 8:1). So don't dread it. Do live today knowing the One who will look at it is the One who died for you.

What drives a man

One died for allso all were counted dead in Him.
→
Now those who livelive for Him, not for themselves.

The terror of the Lord (verse 11) means a deep, reverent respect for Someone you will answer to. But the engine that drives Paul is verse 14: “the love of Christ constraineth us.” Constrain is to press in from both sides, like a river between banks.

Some of us lived unto ourselves for years, and it cost us everything. Now the One who died for you asks for the rest of your days. Don't do it to pay Him back. Do it because He loved you first.

Notice verse 12 too. Critics in Corinth bragged about appearance, what shows on the outside. God looks at the heart. A man doesn't have to look impressive to be right with God.

Part three

New, and reconciled

Verses 16–21

The heart of the chapter, maybe the heart of the letter.

A new creature

It looks dead in winter. But the same tree comes back in spring, and it was not planted again. The life was in it all along.

“If any man be in Christ”: anyone, whoever, as soon as he trusts Jesus. He is a new creature. Not a patched-up old one. Something new that was not there yesterday.

Passed away and become new are Paul's words for the moment of trusting Christ: your standing with God changes at once, and your life starts to follow.

Old labels, new name

  1. “Addict. Felon. Failure.”→In Christwho you are before God
  2. “Guilty of everything.”→Forgivenevery charge carried by Him
  3. “Belongs to the old crowd.”→God's own sonadopted, not on trial
  4. “Headed for the same hole.”→A new roadwalked one day at a time

That's verse 16: “henceforth know we no man after the flesh.” Paul has stopped sizing people up by their past or their looks, including himself. God does the same with you: He sees a man in Christ.

Be honest, though. New creature doesn't mean no more temptation or no more bad days. It means you start from a new place, with new power, and the old master has no claim. Today, when your head calls you by the old name, answer with the new one.

The way across

Reconciled means enemies made friends again. We didn't reach across. We couldn't. The gap was our own sin.

“God was in Christ.” God did the reaching, in the person of His Son. He came all the way over to us.

“For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.”

Romans 5:10

Not imputing

Impute means to count to someone's account. The man who is reconciled to God has a ledger with nothing left on it.

God was “not imputing their trespasses unto them.” Not because He looked away, but because they were counted somewhere else: on Christ.

If you're carrying regret about a fall, take this to heart. God isn't keeping the list you are. Confess it, thank Him it's covered, and go make right with people what you can.

An ambassador

  1. Sent by a kinghe has no authority of his own
  2. Living abroadhis home is somewhere else
  3. Speaks the king's words“as though God did beseech you by us”
  4. Youat work, at home, in the meeting: Christ's man there

The message is an appeal, not a threat: “we pray you in Christ's stead, be ye reconciled to God.” You don't have to be a preacher. You tell the simple truth about what He did for you, and say: He'll do it for you too.

Today, think of one man who is still at war with God. Tell him, in a sentence, what changed for you.

Then they trade. Jesus, who never sinned, took your sin on Himself. And you, who sinned, are given the righteousness of God.

Not made to look righteous. Made righteous, “in him”: the perfect record of Jesus, counted as yours. That's why a believer can stand before the judgment seat without fear.

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Points to remember

  1. Our house“Absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord” 5:8The tent comes down, and the believer goes straight to the Lord.
  2. A down payment“The earnest of the Spirit” 5:5The Spirit in you is God's deposit: the rest is guaranteed.
  3. The judgment seat“We must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ” 5:10It's about what we did with our life, not whether we are saved.
  4. New creature“If any man be in Christ, he is a new creature” 5:17Old things passed away. You start from a new place, in Him.
  5. Reconciled“God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself” 5:19God came all the way across to us. And He isn't counting our sins.
  6. The exchange“Made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin” 5:21He took our sin. We get His righteousness.

Question 1 of 6

Our house

What happens to a believer when his body dies?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.”

Luke 23:43

Answer

The tent comes down, and he goes to be with the Lord. A house not made with hands is waiting. He is absent from the body and present with the Lord, which is far better than anything here.

Question 2 of 6

A down payment

What has God given a believer as a down payment?

What verses tell us about this?

“In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,”

Ephesians 1:13

Answer

His Holy Spirit. The Spirit lives in every believer from the day he trusts Christ, and He is the deposit and guarantee that God will finish the job. A man who has the Spirit is sealed.

Question 3 of 6

The judgment seat

What is the judgment seat of Christ for?

What verses tell us about this?

“If any man's work shall be burned, he shall suffer loss: but he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire.”

1 Corinthians 3:15

“There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.”

Romans 8:1

Answer

It's where a believer's life and works are weighed and rewarded. It isn't where his salvation is decided: that was settled at the cross. He may lose reward, but he himself is saved.

Question 4 of 6

New creature

What changes when a man is in Christ?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life.”

Romans 6:4

Answer

Everything starts new. The old standing is gone, and he is a new creature in Christ, with new power to walk in a new life. The old labels no longer fit.

Question 5 of 6

Reconciled

How did God make peace with us?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

He did the reaching. God was in Christ, reconciling the world to Himself and not counting our sins against us. Now He asks us to take the offer: be reconciled to God.

Question 6 of 6

The exchange

What did Jesus take, and what do we get?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

He took our sin, though He never sinned. And in return we are given the righteousness of God, in Him. It is a trade we could never earn, offered to whosoever believes.
