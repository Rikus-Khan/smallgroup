Part one

Be strong

Verses 1–9

About a month into the work, the builders are losing heart. God speaks again.

Nothing like the old days

Chapter 1 recap: in about 520 BC, after the exile, Haggai got the people building God's house again. Now it is the seventh month, near the end of the Feast of Tabernacles, a feast that used to be the high point of the year in Solomon's temple.

Solomon's temple

7 years

Cedar, carved stone, and gold everywhere. Burned by Babylon in 586 BC.

This house

“as nothing”

A poor remnant with what they could find. The old men who remembered the first one wept.

“But many of the priests and Levites and chief of the fathers, who were ancient men, that had seen the first house, when the foundation of this house was laid before their eyes, wept with a loud voice; and many shouted aloud for joy:”

Ezra 3:12

Three times: be strong

  1. “Be strong, O Zerubbabel”the governor
  2. “Be strong, O Joshua”the high priest
  3. “Be strong, all ye people”everyone with a shovel
  4. “For I am with you”the reason

The strength isn't in the men. It comes from the One standing with them. These are nearly the words God gave the first Joshua, as he stood at the edge of the promised land:

“Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.”

Joshua 1:9

And verse 5 reaches back to Egypt. The God who brought their fathers out still keeps His word, and His Spirit was still among them. Fear ye not.

Don't despise a small start

  • compare it to the old daysthat's how the old men lost heart
  • put the shovel downthey did that once already, for sixteen years
  • be strong, and work“for I am with you”

Men in recovery know this. A man looks at his life now (a room, a bus pass, a few weeks clean) and compares it to what he had, or what he thinks he should have by now. It looks like nothing. God says: keep building.

“For who hath despised the day of small things? for they shall rejoice, and shall see the plummet in the hand of Zerubbabel with those seven; they are the eyes of the LORD, which run to and fro through the whole earth.”

Zechariah 4:10

Everything shaken

The New Testament quotes this very verse. God shook the earth once at Mount Sinai. One day He will shake everything, and only what cannot be shaken will be left.

Haggai 2:6

Yet once, it is a little while, and I will shake the heavens, and the earth, and the sea, and the dry land;

Hebrews 12:26–27

Whose voice then shook the earth: but now he hath promised, saying, Yet once more I shake not the earth only, but also heaven. And this word, Yet once more, signifieth the removing of those things that are shaken, as of things that are made, that those things which cannot be shaken may remain.

Empires, money, the things a man builds his life on: all of it can be shaken. A believer has something that can't.

“Wherefore we receiving a kingdom which cannot be moved, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear:”

Hebrews 12:28

Greater glory

What they saw

No gold

A plain building, put up by poor men. Nothing to look at.

What God says

“The silver is mine”

He owns every ounce of gold on earth. He isn't short of anything, and He will fill this house with glory.

How could this plain house have more glory than Solomon's? Centuries later it was enlarged by Herod, and it was into this temple that Jesus was carried as a baby, and where He later taught. Old Simeon held Him there and said:

“For mine eyes have seen thy salvation, Which thou hast prepared before the face of all people; A light to lighten the Gentiles, and the glory of thy people Israel.”

Luke 2:30–32

Part two

Clean and unclean

Verses 10–19

Two months later, Haggai asks the priests two questions. The answers tell the people something about themselves.

Two questions for the priests

Holy meat touches bread. Is the bread now holy?NoMeat from a sacrifice, carried in the fold of a robe. Holiness doesn't rub off on what it touches.
A man unclean from a dead body touches bread. Is it unclean?YesUnder the law, touching a corpse made a man unclean, and he passed it on to whatever he touched.

Clean doesn't spread. Unclean does. You can't catch holiness by standing near it, but sin gets into everything.

So is this people

The people were offering sacrifices on the altar among the ruins. They may have thought being near the holy place made them right with God. Haggai says no: their hearts had been away from God, and it had spread into everything they touched.

Being aroundchurch, the altar, good people
→
can't makea man clean on the inside

But there is One whose touch turns it around. Under the law, touching a leper made a man unclean. When Jesus touched one, the leper was made clean.

“And Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, and touched him, and saith unto him, I will; be thou clean.”

Mark 1:41

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Half, or less

The grain heap

looked for 20got 10

The vat at the press

looked for 50got 20

A man would walk out to a pile of grain expecting twenty measures and find ten. The “pressfat” was the vat at the press where the juice of the grapes or olives ran down. He'd go to draw fifty jars and get twenty.

Blight, mould and hail hit everything they planted, and still, God says, “ye turned not to me.” Now He asks them to look back and remember those years.

From this day will I bless you.

It was about mid-December. The seed was still in the barn or just sown, and the trees were bare. Nothing in the fields had changed yet. But God dates the blessing to the day they got back to work, before a single sprout came up.

Haggai keeps coming back to one word, “consider.” It runs through the whole book:

1:5×1 1:7×1 2:15×1 2:18×2

In chapter 1: consider how bad it got. Here: consider the turning point. Watch what God does from today. A man doesn't have to see the harvest yet to know the day things turned.

Part three

As a signet

Verses 20–23

The same day, a last word, just for Zerubbabel.

Thrones come down

Zerubbabel governed a tiny province for the mighty Persian Empire. God reminds him who is really on the throne.

  1. thronesoverthrown
  2. kingdomstheir strength destroyed
  3. chariotsoverthrown, with their riders
  4. armiesturning their swords on each other

God has done this before: at the Red Sea, and when Gideon's enemies turned on one another (Judges 7:22). Every empire that looks permanent is on borrowed time. God's kingdom is not.

The ring back on the hand

A signet was a king's seal ring, pressed into wax to sign with his authority. About seventy years before, God had said this about Zerubbabel's grandfather, King Coniah (Jehoiachin):

“As I live, saith the LORD, though Coniah the son of Jehoiakim king of Judah were the signet upon my right hand, yet would I pluck thee thence;”

Jeremiah 22:24
Coniah: plucked off → Zerubbabel: as a signet

God took the ring off for sin, and now He puts it back on. David's royal line was not finished. And the New Testament names Zerubbabel in the family line that leads to Jesus:

JechoniasbegatSalathielbegatZorobabel…Jesus

“And after they were brought to Babylon, Jechonias begat Salathiel; and Salathiel begat Zorobabel;”

Matthew 1:12

God keeps His word

That promise was made to Zerubbabel and David's line, not to us. But it shows us what kind of God we have. Sin had cost Judah its throne, its temple, and seventy years. God didn't throw His promise away. He picked it back up, and in the end He kept it in Jesus.

“For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen, unto the glory of God by us.”

2 Corinthians 1:20

A man may feel his failures have cancelled him out. Haggai ends with God saying to a broken family line: “I have chosen thee.”

Points to remember

  1. Small beginnings“Is it not in your eyes … as nothing?” 2:3Don't measure a new start against the old days. Keep building.
  2. Courage“Be strong … and work: for I am with you” 2:4The strength to keep going comes from the God who stands with us.
  3. God's provision“The silver is mine, and the gold is mine” 2:8Everything belongs to God. He isn't short of what we need.
  4. Clean and unclean“That which they offer there is unclean” 2:14Being near holy things can't make a man clean. Only Jesus can.
  5. Blessing“From this day will I bless you” 2:19God's blessing starts the day we turn back, before we see the harvest.
  6. God's promises“I will … make thee as a signet” 2:23Sin couldn't cancel God's promise. He keeps His word, and He kept it in Jesus.

Question 1 of 6

Small beginnings

What do we do when a new start looks small next to the old days?

What verses tell us about this?

“For who hath despised the day of small things? for they shall rejoice, and shall see the plummet in the hand of Zerubbabel with those seven; they are the eyes of the LORD, which run to and fro through the whole earth.”

Zechariah 4:10

Answer

Keep building. The new temple looked like nothing to the old men, but God said its glory would be greater. A small start with God in it is worth more than the best of the old days without Him. Don't despise the day of small things.

Question 2 of 6

Courage

Where does a man get the strength to keep going?

What verses tell us about this?

“Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.”

Joshua 1:9

Answer

From God, who is with him. God said “be strong” three times, and each time the reason was the same: “for I am with you.” A believer has the Holy Spirit living in him from the day he is saved. The strength isn't in us. It's in the One with us.

Question 3 of 6

God's provision

Who really owns what we need?

What verses tell us about this?

“The earth is the LORD’S, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein.”

Psalm 24:1

“But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus.”

Philippians 4:19

Answer

God does. The builders were poor, but the silver and the gold were His. That isn't a promise to make us rich. It's a promise that the God who owns everything can supply what His work and His people truly need.

Question 4 of 6

Clean and unclean

Can being around holy things make a man clean?

What verses tell us about this?

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin.”

1 John 1:7

Answer

No. Holiness doesn't rub off, but uncleanness spreads. Sitting near the altar didn't make the people clean, and sitting in church doesn't save a man. Only Jesus can make a man clean, and His blood cleanses from all sin.

Question 5 of 6

Blessing

When does God's blessing begin?

What verses tell us about this?

“Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.”

Isaiah 41:10

Answer

The day we turn back to Him. The fields were still bare and the seed was still in the barn, but God said, “from this day will I bless you.” A man may not see the fruit for a while. The turning point is still real.

Question 6 of 6

God's promises

What does the signet ring tell us about God's promises?

What verses tell us about this?

“As I live, saith the LORD, though Coniah the son of Jehoiakim king of Judah were the signet upon my right hand, yet would I pluck thee thence;”

Jeremiah 22:24

“For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen, unto the glory of God by us.”

2 Corinthians 1:20

Answer

That God keeps them. He pulled the ring off Coniah for sin, and put it back on his grandson. David's line went on, all the way to Jesus. Our sin is real, but it can't make God break His word.
