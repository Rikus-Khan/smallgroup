Part one

Heads of Jacob

Verses 1–4

Micah, the countryman, stands up in front of the men who run the country.

Is it not for you to know judgment?

“Judgment” here means justice: knowing right from wrong, and doing right by people. The “heads” and “princes” were the judges and rulers of Judah, about 700 years before Christ, in the days of King Hezekiah (Jeremiah 26:18).

If anyone should know what's fair, it's the men in charge. That was their whole job. God holds leaders to a higher standard, not a lower one.

Every man here leads someone: a family, a crew, a younger man watching how you live. The question lands on us too.

Butchers, not shepherds

Nobody was literally eaten. Micah uses a butcher's words on purpose: skin, bones, the cooking pot. The leaders were treating people like meat, using them up for whatever they could get out of them.

Some of us have been used up by someone with power. Some of us were the ones doing the using. God sees both, and He takes the side of the people being eaten.

When God doesn't answer

This is hard. Why would God hide His face? “As they have behaved themselves ill.” They wanted rescue from the consequences, not from the sin. They planned to keep on eating the sheep.

The man who won't turn

“He that turneth away his ear from hearing the law, even his prayer shall be abomination.”

Proverbs 28:9

The man who does

“Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts: and let him return unto the LORD, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon.”

Isaiah 55:7

God has never turned away a man who comes honestly, ready to let go. The door is open today.

Part two

Prophets for hire

Verses 5–8

Preachers who say whatever the man feeding them wants to hear.

The message depends on the meal

“Bite with their teeth” means have something to chew on. Feed these prophets and they had good news for you. Forget to pay them and suddenly God was against you.

You put food in their mouths?Peace“All is well. God is pleased with you.”
You put nothing in their mouths?WarThey “prepare war against him,” a holy war, as if they spoke for God.

“They have healed also the hurt of the daughter of my people slightly, saying, Peace, peace; when there is no peace.”

Jeremiah 6:14

A voice whose message changes with what you give it isn't speaking for God. Watch out for anyone, preacher or friend, whose “truth” always lines up with what they get from you.

The lights go out

“Divine” means to tell the future by signs and tricks. “Seers” were another name for prophets. They had lied about what God said for so long that God stopped speaking to them at all. Night falls. They'll cover their mouths in shame, because “there is no answer of God.”

Part three

Zion plowed as a field

Verses 9–12

Everybody has a price, and everybody is sure God is on their side.

Everybody has a price

Judges “for reward,” priests “for hire,” prophets “for money” (v. 11). The three groups meant to keep the nation honest were all for sale, and Jerusalem was built on money squeezed from the poor: “with blood.”

Once money is on the scale, it always outweighs what's right.

“And thou shalt take no gift: for the gift blindeth the wise, and perverteth the words of the righteous.”

Exodus 23:8

“Is not the LORD among us?”

Here is the scariest part. They were sure they were fine. The temple was in town, they went through the motions, so nothing bad could happen to them. They leaned on God with one hand and robbed His people with the other.

“Trust ye not in lying words, saying, The temple of the LORD, The temple of the LORD, The temple of the LORD, are these.”

Jeremiah 7:4

A man can sit in church every day and still be running his own show. Being near holy things doesn't make us safe. Being honest with God does. The good news is that He welcomes the man who stops pretending.

The warning that bought a hundred years

  1. about 700 BCMicah: “Zion… plowed as a field”

Micah's sermon worked. The king listened, turned, and prayed, and God held the judgment back for over a hundred years. God's warnings are not God being mean. They are God giving us a chance to turn.

Question 1 of 5

Leadership

What does God expect from men in charge?

What verses tell us about this?

“Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind;”

1 Peter 5:2

Answer

To know what's right and do it, and to care for the people under them like a shepherd, not use them up like a butcher. Power is a trust from God, not a chance to help yourself. Whether you lead a family, a crew, or one younger man, the question is the same: am I feeding them, or feeding on them?

Question 2 of 5

Prayer

Why would God not answer a man's prayer?

What verses tell us about this?

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

1 John 1:9

Answer

These men wanted God to get them out of trouble while they kept on doing what caused it. God won't be used like that. But look at the other side: the man who confesses and forsakes his sin shall have mercy. God is faithful to forgive. If you come to Him honestly, He hears you.

Question 3 of 5

Truth

How can you tell a false voice from a true one?

What verses tell us about this?

“They have healed also the hurt of the daughter of my people slightly, saying, Peace, peace; when there is no peace.”

Jeremiah 6:14

Answer

Watch what the message is tied to. The false prophets' word changed with the money. They said “peace” to whoever fed them and never named sin. A true voice tells you the truth about your sin even when it costs him, and it lines up with what God has already said in His Word.

Question 4 of 5

The Holy Spirit

Where does real courage come from?

What verses tell us about this?

“But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.”

Acts 1:8

Answer

From God's Spirit. Micah, a farm-town nobody, stood up to the whole government because he was “full of power by the spirit of the LORD.” Every believer has the Holy Spirit living in him from the day he was saved. You don't have to work up courage on your own. Ask Him for it, and step out.

Question 5 of 5

Mercy

Can a warning from God turn into mercy?

What verses tell us about this?

“Did Hezekiah king of Judah and all Judah put him at all to death? did he not fear the LORD, and besought the LORD, and the LORD repented him of the evil which he had pronounced against them? Thus might we procure great evil against our souls.”

Jeremiah 26:19

“Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near:”

Isaiah 55:6

Answer

Yes. Hezekiah heard Micah's warning, feared the LORD, and prayed, and God held back the judgment. A warning is an open door. Whatever God has been putting His finger on in your life, it's not too late to turn while He is near.
