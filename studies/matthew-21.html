Matthew 21

Thy King cometh

Jesus rides into Jerusalem on an ass, turns over the tables in the temple, curses a fig tree with nothing but leaves, and tells the leaders two stories they can't miss.

Part one

Thy King cometh

Verses 1–11

A borrowed animal, a road covered in coats, and a whole city asking one question.

Over the Mount of Olives

The road from Jericho comes over the mount of Olives, a ridge just east of the city. Bethphage sat on its slope; Bethany, Lazarus' town, a little farther out. From the top, the temple shines right across the valley.

“The Lord hath need of them.” The King borrows a young donkey. He uses ordinary things, and ordinary men, for His work.

The last week

Matthew gives eight of his twenty-eight chapters to these few days. Passover fell in spring, about AD 30 or 33.

  1. SundayHe rides into Jerusalem21:1–11
  2. Mondaythe fig tree; the temple cleared21:12–19
  3. Tuesdayquestions and parables in the temple21:23 – 25:46
  4. Thursdaythe Last Supper; Gethsemanech. 26
  5. Fridaythe crossch. 27
  6. SundayHe is risench. 28

Mark lays out the days more exactly: Jesus looked around the temple on Sunday evening, went back to Bethany, and cleared it the next morning (Mark 11:11–15). Matthew tells the temple story right alongside the entry, because they belong together: the King comes to His city, and then to His Father's house.

Spoken by the prophet

Zechariah 9:9, about 500 years before

Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion; shout, O daughter of Jerusalem: behold, thy King cometh unto thee: he is just, and having salvation; lowly, and riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass.

Matthew 21:5

Tell ye the daughter of Sion, Behold, thy King cometh unto thee, meek, and sitting upon an ass, and a colt the foal of an ass.

Jesus didn't stumble into this. He sent for the animals so the prophecy would be fulfilled in front of everyone. Sion is Zion, the hill of Jerusalem; its daughter is its people.

Meek, and sitting upon an ass

a conquering king

a war horse

comes to take a city by force

this King

a donkey

comes in peace, the way ordinary folk travelled

Meek isn't weak. It's strength that comes gently. He comes to you the same way.

“Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.”

Matthew 11:29

Hosanna

הוֹשִׁיעָה נָּא said hoshia na save now, we pray

The crowd was singing Psalm 118, a psalm the pilgrims sang every Passover. They were shouting it at Jesus:

“Save now, I beseech thee, O LORD: O LORD, I beseech thee, send now prosperity. Blessed be he that cometh in the name of the LORD: we have blessed you out of the house of the LORD.”

Psalm 118:25–26

Spreading coats on the road was how you welcomed a king (2 Kings 9:13). John says the branches were palms (John 12:13). They were saying: This is the King. Save us now.

Who is this?

The whole city was moved, stirred up, shaken. The crowd's answer was true as far as it went: “the prophet of Nazareth of Galilee.” This one chapter gives a fuller answer:

  1. thy King21:5
  2. the Son of David21:9, 15
  3. he that cometh in the name of the Lord21:9
  4. the prophet21:11
  5. his son, the heir21:37–38
  6. the stone, the head of the corner21:42

Within the week a crowd in this same city would shout, “Let him be crucified” (27:22). Cheering Jesus is easy on a good day. The question is still the one the city asked: Who is this? Every man has to answer it for himself.

Part two

My house

Verses 12–22

Tables crash, the blind see, children sing, and a tree full of leaves has nothing to give.

A market in God's house

The outer court was the one place a Gentile could pray. It had become a market: animals for sacrifice, doves for the poor man's offering, and moneychangers trading pilgrims' coins for temple money, at a cut.

Jesus cleared it out, as He had once before (John 2:13–16). When He comes into a man's life, some tables get turned over. That's a house being made clean.

It is written

Jesus puts two prophets side by side: what God meant His house to be, and what they had made it.

Isaiah 56:7

…for mine house shall be called an house of prayer for all people.

Jeremiah 7:11

Is this house, which is called by my name, become a den of robbers in your eyes? Behold, even I have seen it, saith the LORD.

A den of thieves is a robbers' hideout, the cave they run back to after the job. They cheated people, then hid behind religion. And notice Isaiah's “for all people.” The market filled the only room the outsiders had.

Who was glad, and who wasn't

the blind, the lame, the children

healed and singing

The broken came, and He healed them. Children picked up the crowd's song: “Hosanna to the Son of David.”

the chief priests and scribes

“sore displeased”

They saw “the wonderful things that he did” and were angry. Hearest thou what these say?

Jesus answers from Psalm 8: God gets His praise out of the mouths of babes and sucklings, little children and nursing babies. The men in charge missed it. The kids got it.

You don't need a title, a long church history, or the right words to praise Him. The blind and the lame are welcome in His house, and so are you.

All leaves, no fruit

A fig tree in full leaf looked like it should have figs. It had nothing.

It's a picture of the temple He'd just seen: busy, impressive, and no fruit for God.

Leaves are easy: the right clothes, the right words. He's looking for fruit, and it grows only one way: “He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit” (John 15:5).

This mountain

Ifye have faith, and doubt not,
→
thenthis mountain… it shall be done.

They were likely on the Mount of Olives, on the road from Bethany, so “this mountain” was under their feet. The power isn't in how hard a man believes. It's in the God he trusts. And it isn't a blank check: faith asks in line with what God wants.

“And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us:”

1 John 5:14

Facing a mountain, a habit, a debt, a broken family? Bring it to Him, believing. Mountains have moved before.

Part three

By what authority?

Verses 23–46

The leaders come to trap Him, and walk away caught in two stories about vineyards.

Trapped by their own question

The chief priests and elders ran the temple. “By what authority?” meant: Who said you could clear our temple? Jesus asks about John the Baptist, and they huddle:

If we say“From heaven,”
→
then he'll say“Why did ye not then believe him?”
If we say“Of men,”
→
thenthe people will turn on us.

“We cannot tell.” They could. They just wouldn't. Their trouble wasn't lack of proof but pride and fear. A man who won't follow the light he has won't be given more.

Two sons

the first son

“I will not”

Then “afterward he repented, and went.” He changed his mind, and his feet followed.

the second son

“I go, sir”

Polite, respectful, all the right words. “And went not.”

Twain means two. The leaders answer right: the first son. What counts isn't what a man said at the start. It's what he did afterward.

Plenty of us started out saying “I will not” to God, loudly, for years. That isn't the end of the story. The door is open to the man who turns around and goes.

Before you

Publicans were tax collectors who worked for Rome and skimmed off their own people. Harlots were prostitutes.

Not how you started. Whether you went.

They believed John and turned around (Luke 3:12). The religious men watched their lives change and “repented not afterward.” They were the second son.

Jesus says “before you,” not “instead of you.” The door was still open, even to them. It's open to any man who turns, whatever list he's been on.

God's vineyard

Isaiah sang of this vineyard about 700 years before (Isaiah 5:1–2):

“For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant: and he looked for judgment, but behold oppression; for righteousness, but behold a cry.”

Isaiah 5:7

Husbandmen are tenant farmers who owed the owner his share. Here the tenants are the leaders; the servants are God's prophets.

Last of all, his son

  1. servantsbeat one, killed another, stoned anotherv. 35
  2. more servants“they did unto them likewise”v. 36
  3. his son“They will reverence my son”v. 37
  4. cast out, slain“out of the vineyard”v. 39

The owner's patience is the most surprising thing in the story. Who keeps sending after the first servant is killed? God does. And then He sends His Son. Jesus is telling them, a few days ahead, exactly what they are about to do to Him.

They knew who He was: “This is the heir.” And they took Him outside and killed Him. Jesus died outside the city walls:

“Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate.”

Hebrews 13:12

Their own verdict

JesusWhen the lord therefore of the vineyard cometh, what will he do unto those husbandmen?

The chief priests and eldersHe will miserably destroy those wicked men. their own words

They judged themselves without knowing it, like King David when Nathan told him the story of the stolen lamb, then said, “Thou art the man” (2 Samuel 12:7).

David broke and repented. These men saw it too (v. 45), and dug in. When God's Word puts a finger on us, the right answer is David's, not theirs.

The corner stone

Psalm 118 again, the crowd's song of two days before. Builders threw a stone aside as no good.

God made it the head of the corner, the stone that holds the walls together.

The leaders rejected Jesus and crucified Him. God raised Him and made Him the one foundation everything else rests on (1 Corinthians 3:11). “This is the Lord’s doing.”

Broken, or ground to powder

a man falls on the stone

“shall be broken”

He comes to Jesus, and his pride breaks. That hurts, and it's the beginning of being made whole.

the stone falls on a man

“grind him to powder”

He refuses Jesus to the end, and meets Him as Judge.

Every man meets this Stone one way or the other. Being broken on Him is mercy. Many of us came to Him broken: hit bottom, out of answers. God doesn't turn that man away:

“The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise.”

Psalm 51:17

“Taken from you” (v. 43): the kingdom would go to “a nation bringing forth the fruits thereof,” His people from every nation, Jew and Gentile, who believe.

Points to remember

  1. The King“Thy King cometh unto thee, meek” 21:5He's the King, and He comes gently, on a donkey, to men who need saving.
  2. House of prayer“My house shall be called the house of prayer” 21:13He clears out what doesn't belong. That's how a house gets clean.
  3. Fruit“Nothing thereon, but leaves only” 21:19Leaves are easy. He's looking for fruit, and it grows from staying close to Him.
  4. Prayer“Whatsoever ye shall ask in prayer, believing, ye shall receive” 21:22Bring your mountain to Him, asking in line with His will.
  5. Turning around“Afterward he repented, and went” 21:29It's not how you started. It's whether you turn around and go.
  6. The cornerstone“The same is become the head of the corner” 21:42Men threw Jesus away. God made Him the foundation. Fall on Him and be broken, and be saved.

Question 1 of 6

The King

What kind of King rode into Jerusalem?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

A meek King, the Son of David, riding a donkey just as Zechariah said. He came in peace, not on a war horse. He is strong, and He comes gently, to save.

Question 2 of 6

House of prayer

Why did Jesus turn over the tables in the temple?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

God's house was meant to be a house of prayer for all people, and they had made it a den of thieves. He cleared out the market, then healed the blind and the lame who came to Him there.

Question 3 of 6

Fruit

What did Jesus find on the fig tree?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Nothing but leaves. It looked fruitful and had nothing to give, like the busy temple. Jesus is looking for real fruit in our lives, not just the look of it, and that fruit grows as we abide in Him.

Question 4 of 6

Prayer

What did Jesus promise about faith and prayer?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That faith without doubting can move a mountain, and that what we ask in prayer, believing, we shall receive. The power is God's, and faith asks according to His will. No mountain is too big for Him.

Question 5 of 6

Turning around

Which of the two sons did the will of his father?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The first, who said “I will not” but afterward repented and went. Fine words don't count; turning around does. That's why publicans and harlots who believed went in ahead of the religious leaders.

Question 6 of 6

The cornerstone

What happened to the stone the builders rejected?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It became the head of the corner. Men rejected Jesus and crucified Him, and God raised Him up and made Him the foundation. The man who falls on Him is broken and saved; the man who refuses Him meets Him as Judge.
