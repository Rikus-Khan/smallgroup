Zephaniah 2

Seek ye the LORD

Part one

Before the day

Verses 1–3

Zephaniah is still preaching to Judah under King Josiah, about 630 BC. Chapter 1 ended in fire. Chapter 2 opens with a door still open.

Before, before, before

Four times in one verse: before. The decree has been written. The day is coming. But it isn't here yet, and that gap is mercy.

Chaff is the light husk a farmer tossed into the wind at threshing. It blew away in a second. That's how fast the time can go.

“(For he saith, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.)”

2 Corinthians 6:2

Seek, seek, seek

  1. Seek the LORDHimself, first
  2. Seek righteousnessdo what's right
  3. Seek meeknessbow low before God

Notice who He's talking to: the meek, the ones who have already been doing His will. Even they need to keep seeking. Nobody graduates from it.

Meek doesn't mean weak. Moses stood up to Pharaoh and led Israel out of Egypt, and the Bible says this about him:

“(Now the man Moses was very meek, above all the men which were upon the face of the earth.)”

Numbers 12:3

Meekness is strength that has bowed to God. It's a man who's quit fighting God and started listening.

Hidden

צְפַנְיָה said Tsephan-yah the LORD hides, the LORD treasures

That's Zephaniah's own name. The man who preached the day of wrath carried a promise in his name: God hides His own. David used the same word:

“For in the time of trouble he shall hide me in his pavilion: in the secret of his tabernacle shall he hide me; he shall set me up upon a rock.”

Psalm 27:5

Why “it may be”? Judah was about to be invaded, and God didn't promise every humble man would escape the war. But there's no “maybe” about salvation: “whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved” (Romans 10:13).

Part two

West and east

Verses 4–10

The Philistines on the coast, Moab and Ammon across the Jordan. And a promise tucked in the middle.

The names tell the story

עַזָּה עֲזוּבָה said Azzah azubah “Gaza, forsaken”: the city's name and its fate sound alike

Ekron gets the same treatment: Ekron will be “rooted up” (in Hebrew, te'aqer). Their own names become their sentence.

These were the Philistines, Israel's old enemies on the coast, the people of Goliath. The Cherethites were one of their clans, likely from Crete, where the Philistines first came from (Amos 9:7). “At the noonday”: in broad daylight, when nobody expects an attack.

The way home, promised first

  1. about 630 BC“turn away their captivity”verse 7
  2. 586Judah carried to Babylon
  3. about 538the Jews start coming home

Look at the order. Judah hadn't even been carried off yet, and God was already promising to bring them back. The judgment was coming, but it was never the end of the story.

That promise belonged to Judah, and God kept it. But it shows us who He is. When He disciplines His people, He's already got the way home planned. For a man who has wrecked his life, that matters. The God who lets you hit bottom has a road back up.

“I have heard”

Moab and Ammon lived east of the Jordan and the Dead Sea. They were relatives: both came from Lot, Abraham's nephew, after he fled from Sodom (Genesis 19). Now God says they'll end up like Sodom: weeds and salt pits, like the barren shore of the Dead Sea.

What Moab and Ammon did

Mocked and bragged

They taunted God's people when they were down, and grabbed at their land.

What God did

He heard every word

He calls them “my people” and takes the insult as His own.

Some of us get mocked for getting clean, or for our faith. You don't have to settle that score. Jesus didn't:

“Who, when he was reviled, reviled not again; when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously:”

1 Peter 2:23

Part three

South and north

Verses 11–15

Every idol starved, and the proudest city on earth left to the birds.

God will starve the idols.

An idol lived on offerings: food, drink, incense, money, and sometimes worse. When the nations that fed it fell, the god went hungry and was forgotten.

That's true of the idols we know too. A bottle, a pill, a screen: an idol only lives as long as you keep feeding it. Stop feeding it, and it gets weaker. And God means to take every one of them down.

Then: “men shall worship him, every one from his place.” Not just at Jerusalem, but from every country, even the far coastlands (“isles”). Jesus said that time had come:

“But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him.”

John 4:23

The city that couldn't fall

Nineveh was the capital of Assyria, the empire that had swallowed Israel in the north. Massive walls, palaces carved with cedar, the greatest city in the world. When Zephaniah preached, nobody could picture it falling.

  1. ✓will make Nineveh a desolationZephaniah 2:13, about 630 BC612 BC

About twenty years later, Babylon and the Medes broke in and burned it. It was never rebuilt. Its ruins sank under grassy mounds, and for centuries people forgot where it stood. The prophet Nahum had said the same:

“And it shall come to pass, that all they that look upon thee shall flee from thee, and say, Nineveh is laid waste: who will bemoan her? whence shall I seek comforters for thee?”

Nahum 3:7

Only one can say it

Nineveh, in her heart

“I am, and there is none beside me”

Rich, careless, and sure nobody could touch her.

The LORD, in Isaiah 45:5

“There is no God beside me”

The words belong to Him. A city that says them is claiming to be God.

“I've got this. I don't need anybody.” That's Nineveh talking, and a lot of us said it right up until the wreck. Pride says it in our heart long before it comes out of our mouth.

“Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.”

Proverbs 16:18

Every direction

West: the Philistine cities on the coast (vv. 4–7).

East: Moab and Ammon, the mockers (vv. 8–10).

South: the Ethiopians, far up the Nile beyond Egypt (v. 12).

North: Assyria and its capital, Nineveh (vv. 13–15).

No nation is outside God's reach. And who's left in the middle? Jerusalem. Chapter 3 turns there next.

Points to remember

  1. Time“Before the day pass as the chaff” 2:2The time between the warning and the day is mercy. Seek Him now.
  2. Meekness“Seek righteousness, seek meekness” 2:3Meekness is strength that has bowed to God. He hides the humble.
  3. Being mocked“I have heard the reproach of Moab” 2:8God hears every insult thrown at His people. Leave the score with Him.
  4. Idols“He will famish all the gods of the earth” 2:11Idols live only as long as we feed them. God will starve them all.
  5. Pride“I am, and there is none beside me” 2:15Only God can say that. Pride that says it is headed for a fall.

Question 1 of 5

Time

What does God tell people to do before the day comes?

What verses tell us about this?

“Seek ye the LORD while he may be found, call ye upon him while he is near:”

Isaiah 55:6

Answer

Gather together and seek Him, now, before it's too late. God didn't send the warning to scare people off. He sent it so they'd come to Him while the door was open. The time between the warning and the day is mercy, and today is part of that time.

Question 2 of 5

Meekness

What is meekness, and why does God look for it?

What verses tell us about this?

“(Now the man Moses was very meek, above all the men which were upon the face of the earth.)”

Numbers 12:3

“Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.”

Matthew 5:5

Answer

Meekness isn't weakness. Moses was a strong leader and the meekest man on earth. It's strength that has bowed to God: a man who has quit fighting Him and does what He says. God looks for it because a proud man won't come to be hidden. The meek man knows he needs a hiding place, and God gives him one.

Question 3 of 5

Being mocked

What does God do about people who mock His people?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed.”

Genesis 12:3

“Who, when he was reviled, reviled not again; when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously:”

1 Peter 2:23

Answer

He hears it, and He takes it personally. Moab and Ammon mocked Judah, and God answered for His people. If you've been laughed at for your faith or for getting sober, God heard it. You don't have to fight back. Do what Jesus did: hand it over to the One who judges fairly.

Question 4 of 5

Idols

What happens to the gods men trust in?

What verses tell us about this?

“I am the LORD, and there is none else, there is no God beside me: I girded thee, though thou hast not known me:”

Isaiah 45:5

“But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him.”

John 4:23

Answer

They starve. An idol has no life of its own; it only lasts as long as people keep feeding it. God will take them all down, and men from every nation will worship Him instead. Whatever we've been feeding, it can't save us. The Father is looking for men who will worship Him for real.

Question 5 of 5

Pride

What did Nineveh say in her heart?

What verses tell us about this?

“Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall.”

Proverbs 16:18

“Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up.”

James 4:10

Answer

“I am, and there is none beside me.” Words only God can say. Nineveh thought she was untouchable, and within about twenty years she was a ruin. Pride tells a man he can handle it alone. The way up is the way down: humble yourself before God, and He lifts you up.
