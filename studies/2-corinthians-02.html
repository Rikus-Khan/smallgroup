2 Corinthians 2

Forgive him, and comfort him

Paul asks a church to take back a man who failed, because a man can be swallowed up by too much sorrow. Then he finds that Christ always leads us in victory.

Where we are: how we got here

  1. 1 Corinthians 5A man sins openlythe church did nothing
  2. thenPaul says: deal with it1 Corinthians 5:5, 13
  3. thenA painful visit2:1
  4. thenA letter with many tears2:4
  5. thenThe church acts“inflicted of many” (2:6)
  6. nowThe man is sorryforgive him (2:7–8)

Paul never names the man. Many think he is the man of 1 Corinthians 5, who was living in open sin. Some think he is a man who insulted Paul himself. Either way the lesson is the same, and it is for every church and every man who has ever been shown the door.

Last chapter ended with Paul sparing the church a visit. This one begins with why: “I would not come again to you in heaviness.”

Part one

A letter written with tears

Verses 1–4

Hard words from a man who loved them.

Two kinds of hard letter

“not that ye should be grieved”

A letter to wound

Written to score points, to put people in their place, or to feel big. Plenty of men have received that kind, and some have written it.

“that ye might know the love which I have”

A letter to heal

Written through tears, because the writer cannot stand to see someone he loves hurting himself. The hard truth comes with a wet face.

“Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.”

Proverbs 27:6

If someone has ever told you the truth about yourself and you could tell he was hurting too, that was love. And if you have to confront a brother, check the heart behind it first: am I writing to wound, or to heal?

Part two

Forgive him, and comfort him

Verses 5–11

The discipline worked. Now the danger is the opposite one.

Two ways to get it wrong

1 Corinthians 5:2

Too soft

“Ye are puffed up, and have not rather mourned.” The church shrugged at open sin and called it tolerance. That's not love. Sin left alone grows.

2 Corinthians 2:7

Too hard

“Such a one should be swallowed up with overmuch sorrow.” Now the church had gone the other way, and was still punishing a man who had already turned back.

Church discipline is meant to restore, never to destroy. Paul said the first goal was “that the spirit may be saved” (1 Corinthians 5:5). Once the man is sorry, “sufficient” means enough: the punishment has done its work.

Paul gives three things to do: forgive him, comfort him, and “confirm your love toward him”, which means say it out loud and make it official. Not a cold truce. A welcome.

“Swallowed up”

The word means gulped down, like a sea closing over a man. Sorrow over sin is good when it turns a man around (7:10). But when it goes on and on, it drowns him.

Here is the real danger for a man who has failed: not only the sin, but the shame after it. “I'm done. They'll never take me back.” That's how men quit.

The church's job is to throw him a line: forgive, comfort, love him out loud. Paul wants this man pulled up, not held under.

How Satan gets an advantage

  1. “I blew it again”the fall
  2. “They'll never take me back”shame starts talking
  3. “Why even show up?”he stays away
  4. Alone and ashamed“swallowed up with overmuch sorrow”
  5. Back to the old way“an advantage of us”

Devices means schemes, plans of attack. Paul says we aren't ignorant of this one. The enemy loves a church that won't forgive, and he loves a fallen man who thinks he can't come back. Both ways, he wins ground.

“Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour:”

1 Peter 5:8

So break the chain on the second step. If you fell yesterday, do the opposite of what shame says: show up, tell someone, and let the group forgive you. And if a brother comes back to you, don't make him earn it.

Forgiving the way Christ does

Paul says he forgives “in the person of Christ”, as though Christ Himself were standing in the room. This is how God has treated every one of us:

“And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.”

Ephesians 4:32

“And he arose, and came to his father. But when he was yet a great way off, his father saw him, and had compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

The father didn't wait for the son to finish his apology. He ran. That is the model for how a group of men should meet a brother who comes back.

Part three

No rest, and then a triumph

Verses 12–17

An open door and a worried heart, and then a victory parade.

An open door, and no peace

In Troas “a door was opened”: real work to do. But Paul had no peace, because Titus, whom he had sent to Corinth, hadn't come back.

So he left the open door to look for him.

Titus was coming the other way, up from Corinth. Chapter 7 tells how they met: “God, that comforteth those that are cast down, comforted us by the coming of Titus.”

Even an apostle was anxious. If you are today, it doesn't mean your faith has failed. Paul went and found his friend.

The victory parade

Paul has a Roman triumph in mind: the victory parade for a returning general, with the victor in front, his army behind, and incense burning along the road.

Teachers differ on whether Paul sees us as captives being led or soldiers sharing the win. The plain point: Christ has won, and He leads His people along. You aren't trying to win the war today. You're walking behind the One who did (Colossians 2:15).

The same fragrance, two reactions

“in them that perish”

Death unto death

To a man who has decided against Christ, the message smells like a threat. It reminds him of what he wants to forget.

“in them that are saved”

Life unto life

To a man who is hungry for God, the same message smells like rescue, like food after a long fast.

Savour is the old word for smell. Paul says we carry Christ's fragrance into every room we walk into. Some will lean in. Some will pull away. Same you, same message.

“And who is sufficient for these things?” The answer is next chapter: “our sufficiency is of God.” And Paul adds that he doesn't “corrupt the word of God”. That word was used of a tavern keeper who watered down his wine to sell more. Don't water down the gospel, and don't hide it.

Points to remember

  1. Tears“I wrote unto you with many tears” 2:4Real correction comes from love, to heal and never to wound.
  2. Forgiving“Ye ought rather to forgive him, and comfort him” 2:7When a man is sorry, enough is enough: forgive, comfort, and say you love him.
  3. The enemy“We are not ignorant of his devices” 2:11Satan wants a church that won't forgive and a fallen man who won't come back.
  4. No rest“I had no rest in my spirit” 2:13Paul left an open door to go find a friend. An anxious heart isn't a failed faith.
  5. Triumph“Always causeth us to triumph in Christ” 2:14Christ has won. We walk in His victory parade.
  6. The savour“A sweet savour of Christ” 2:15We carry His fragrance wherever we go: life to some, a warning to others.

Question 1 of 6

Tears

Why did Paul write his hard letter “with many tears”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Faithful are the wounds of a friend; but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful.”

Proverbs 27:6

Answer

Because he loved them. He did not want to hurt them. He wanted them to know the love he had for them. A hard word from a friend who is weeping is not an attack.

Question 2 of 6

Forgiving

What should a church do when a man who sinned is truly sorry?

What verses tell us about this?

“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.”

Galatians 6:1

Answer

Forgive him, comfort him, and confirm their love for him. The discipline is meant to restore, not to crush. Once he has turned, keep welcoming him back, so he isn't swallowed up by sorrow.

Question 3 of 6

The enemy

What is Satan after, and why are we “not ignorant of his devices”?

What verses tell us about this?

“Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour:”

1 Peter 5:8

“Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil.”

Ephesians 6:11

Answer

He wants an advantage, and he will use anything to get it: a church that won't forgive, or a fallen man drowning in shame. We know his schemes, so we can spot them and do the opposite.

Question 4 of 6

No rest

What did Paul do when a door was opened but he had no rest in his spirit?

What verses tell us about this?

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

Matthew 11:28

Answer

He left the open door and went looking for Titus, the friend he was worried about. He was honest that his heart had no peace. And the real rest comes from Jesus, who gives rest to the heavy laden.

Question 5 of 6

Triumph

What does it mean that God “always causeth us to triumph in Christ”?

What verses tell us about this?

“And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it.”

Colossians 2:15

“But thanks be to God, which giveth us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.”

1 Corinthians 15:57

Answer

Christ has already won, and we walk in His victory parade. We don't win it ourselves. That doesn't mean life is easy. It means the outcome is settled in Him.

Question 6 of 6

The savour

What is “the savour of Christ” that Paul says we carry?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The fragrance of knowing Christ, which clings to the people who belong to Him. To those who are being saved it smells like life, and to those who refuse Him it is a warning. Either way, we are His, and we carry it wherever we go.
