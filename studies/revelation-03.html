Revelation 3

A door, and a knock

Three churches: one with a big name and no life, one with little strength and an open door, one with plenty and no idea how poor it was. And Christ, knocking.

John, an old man exiled on the island of Patmos about AD 95, saw the risen Christ walking among seven lampstands: seven real churches in Asia. Chapter 2 gave four of them a letter each.

Now the last three. Before God shows John anything about the future, Christ talks to His churches about today.

The road of the seven letters

Many teachers point out that the seven churches sit on a loop of Roman road, in the very order Christ names them. A messenger landing from Patmos could carry the book from town to town.

Today we walk the last stretch: Sardis, Philadelphia, and Laodicea.

And every letter ends the same way: “He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches.” Plural. These letters are for every church, and every man in one.

Part one

Sardis: a name, and no life

Verses 1–6

Everybody thought this church was alive. Christ looked inside.

To Sardis 3:1–6

Who He is
“he that hath the seven Spirits of God, and the seven stars”
He knows
“thou hast a name that thou livest, and art dead”
Praise
“a few names… which have not defiled their garments”
Rebuke
“I have not found thy works perfect before God”
Command
“Be watchful… Remember… hold fast, and repent”
Promise
“clothed in white raiment… I will not blot out his name”

Hardly any praise: only “a few names.” A dead church, and still Christ writes to it.

A name, and no life

Sardis had a reputation. People said, “That church is alive.” Christ didn't argue with the reputation. He just told them what He saw.

A man can do this too. Good at work, smiling at church, saying the right words, and dead inside. A name is what people see. Life is what God sees.

But it isn't over: “strengthen the things which remain.” Christ doesn't say tear it down. He says fan what's left back into a flame.

The city that fell asleep

Sardis was built on a high, steep hill that seemed impossible to take. Ancient historians say it was taken anyway, more than once, because the men on the wall thought they were safe and nobody was watching. Many teachers point out that Sardis knew exactly what “as a thief” meant.

3:3Remember

How you first heard the gospel. What He saved you from.

3:3Hold fast

Grab what's left and don't let go of it.

3:3Repent

Turn around. Today, not when you feel ready.

“Watch therefore: for ye know not what hour your Lord doth come.”

Matthew 24:42

A few names, even in Sardis

In a dead church, Christ still knew a few people by name. It's possible to stay faithful in a place where almost nobody else is. Maybe you're the one clean man at your job, or in your family, or on your block. He sees you.

“Defiled their garments”: stained their clothes. It's a picture of a life soiled by sin. And what does Christ do with stained clothes? He gives white ones.

“Come now, and let us reason together, saith the LORD: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool.”

Isaiah 1:18

A promise, not a threat

Some read “I will not blot out his name” and hear a warning that God might. Christ said the opposite. It's a promise. In that day a city could strike a citizen's name off its roll in disgrace. Christ says: Mine stay.

And He goes further: “I will confess his name before my Father.” Jesus Christ will say your name in heaven and claim you.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Who is “he that overcometh”?

  • not a super-Christian who never fallsthen none of us would make it
  • not a man who earns his place by trying hardersalvation is a gift, received by faith
  • every man who believes in JesusJohn, who wrote Revelation, says so in his letter

“Who is he that overcometh the world, but he that believeth that Jesus is the Son of God?”

1 John 5:5

So every promise “to him that overcometh” in these letters is yours if you belong to Christ. You overcome because He overcame.

Part two

Philadelphia: an open door

Verses 7–13

A weak little church, and not one word of rebuke. A little strength was enough.

To Philadelphia 3:7–13

Who He is
“he that is holy, he that is true, he that hath the key of David”
He knows
“thou hast a little strength, and hast kept my word”
Praise
“and hast not denied my name”
Rebuke
none: not one word
Command
“hold that fast which thou hast”
Promise
“a pillar in the temple of my God… my new name”

Not one word of rebuke. Only Smyrna, in chapter 2, got a letter like this. And it was the weak one.

A little strength is enough

Christ holds “the key of David.” In Isaiah 22 God gave the key of the king's house to a faithful steward, Eliakim: what he opened stayed open. Jesus holds that key now.

“And the key of the house of David will I lay upon his shoulder; so he shall open, and none shall shut; and he shall shut, and none shall open.”

Isaiah 22:22

Philadelphia wasn't strong. It didn't need to be. The door is open because He opened it. You don't need to be strong today. You need to walk through.

“Thou hast a little strength.”

Some days that's all you've got: enough to not pick up, enough to make one call, enough to show up. Christ doesn't scold them for it. He sets a door in front of it.

“And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

What did they do with their little strength? “Kept my word, and… not denied my name.” That's it. That's faithfulness.

The people who looked down on you

Some in Philadelphia claimed to be God's people while they fought Christ's people. Smyrna had the same enemies (2:9). It was never a word against Jewish people: Jesus, John and the first church were Jews. It was about using God's name to fight God.

Christ's promise: one day those who mocked them would see, and “know that I have loved thee.” The old crowd may laugh at you for following Jesus. You don't have to win the argument. He'll settle it.

“Keep thee from the hour”

An hour of trial is coming on “all the world.” Good Baptist teachers read the promise two ways:

many teachers

Kept out of it

Christ will take His church to Himself before that hour falls on the world (1 Thessalonians 4:17).

others

Kept through it

He will keep His own safe in it, as He prayed: “not… take them out of the world, but… keep them from the evil” (John 17:15).

Either way, the promise is plain: “I also will keep thee.” Whatever that hour is, His people will be kept.

“For God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ,”

1 Thessalonians 5:9

A pillar that doesn't move

In Jesus' lifetime an earthquake flattened Philadelphia, and tremors went on for years. Many teachers point out that people camped outside the city, ready to run. Then: “he shall go no more out.”

A man in recovery knows shaky ground: always ready to bolt, never settled anywhere. Christ says: I'll make you a pillar. Settled. Home. With My name on you.

“That no man take thy crown”: not losing salvation, but don't let anyone rob you of a faithful life's reward.

Part three

Laodicea: the knock

Verses 14–22

The richest church, the worst report, and Christ standing outside its door, still knocking.

To Laodicea 3:14–22

Who He is
“the Amen, the faithful and true witness”
He knows
“thou art neither cold nor hot”
Praise
none: not one word
Rebuke
“thou art lukewarm… and knowest not”
Command
“buy of me… be zealous therefore, and repent”
Promise
“to sit with me in my throne”

Not one word of praise. And yet, as we'll see, the biggest promise of all seven letters.

Neither cold nor hot

Many teachers point out the three towns of the Lycus valley.

Hierapolis, six miles north, had hot springs. People went there to be healed.

Colosse had cold, clean water running down from the mountain. It refreshed you.

Laodicea had no good water of its own. It came in by aqueduct and arrived lukewarm and full of minerals. You took a mouthful and spat it out. Hot heals; cold refreshes. Lukewarm is just useless.

What lukewarm looks like

Not a church that hated Christ. A church that didn't need Him. Comfortable. A little religion, not too much. Nobody's life changed, and nobody else's life changed because of them.

Recovery has a name for this: going through the motions. You show up, say the words, and keep one foot in the old life. It makes Christ sick, because it's a lie told with His name on it.

But notice what He calls Himself to them: “the Amen, the faithful and true witness.” He won't flatter you. He'll tell you the truth, because the truth is the only way out.

“And knowest not”

What they said

“I am rich”

“increased with goods”

“need of nothing”

What Christ saw

wretched

miserable, poor

blind, naked

This is denial, and it's exactly what addiction does. “I'm fine. I can handle it. I don't need a meeting.” The worst part isn't being poor and blind. It's not knowing it.

The heart lies to its owner

“The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?”

Jeremiah 17:9

You can't see your own blind spot; that's what makes it blind. So you need someone outside you to tell you the truth: Christ in His word, and a brother who'll be straight with you.

Regret starts here, and it's a good start. Admitting “I am poor and blind” is not the end of a man. It's where Christ can begin.

“He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy.”

Proverbs 28:13

Buy of Me

Many teachers point out that Laodicea was proud of three things: its banks, its fine black wool, and an eye salve its doctors sold. Christ answers each one.

the banks: “I am rich”→gold tried in the firereal riches that last
the black wool: fine clothes→white raimentHis righteousness, covering your shame
the famous eye salve→eyesalve, that thou mayest seeeyes opened to the truth

“Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath no money; come ye, buy, and eat; yea, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price.”

Isaiah 55:1

The hard words were love

After all that, the next words are “As many as I love.” Christ didn't write Laodicea off. He rebuked them because He loved them. A man who doesn't care about you will let you keep lying to yourself.

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

“Be zealous therefore, and repent.” Zealous means boiling, on fire. The cure for lukewarm is to heat up: turn around with your whole heart.

Behold, I stand at the door

He could kick it in. He doesn't. He stands, and knocks, and keeps knocking.

You hear Him: in a verse, a sermon, a brother's word, a night you can't sleep.

The door opens from the inside. Many teachers point out there's no handle on His side. He won't force it. You open it.

“I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me.” Not a lecture. Supper. Sitting at your table, in your mess, as a friend.

“If any man…”

He said it to a church that made Him sick. If He'd knock on Laodicea's door, He'll knock on yours. No man is too far gone, too dirty, or too late.

to a man who's never opened

Let Him in

Christ is offering Himself to you. Open the door by faith: trust Him to save you, and He comes in to stay.

to a believer gone cold

Let Him back to the table

He hasn't left you; you've shut Him out of the room where you live. Open up, and eat with Him again.

“All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.”

John 6:37

Points to remember

  1. Sardis“Thou hast a name that thou livest, and art dead” 3:1A good reputation over an empty life. Wake up and strengthen what remains.
  2. Philadelphia“I have set before thee an open door” 3:8A door no man can shut. A little strength, kept faithful, is enough.
  3. Lukewarm“Thou art neither cold nor hot” 3:15Not healing, not refreshing: useless, and comfortable without Christ.
  4. Blind“And knowest not” 3:17They thought they were rich; they were poor, blind and naked, and didn't know it.
  5. Love“As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten” 3:19His rebuke is love, calling us to turn around with our whole heart.
  6. The door“If any man hear my voice, and open the door” 3:20He knocks for any man, and comes in to eat with him as a friend.

Question 1 of 6

Sardis

What was wrong with the church that had a name that it lived?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

It had a reputation for being alive, but Christ said it was dead. Its works weren't finished before God. He told it to wake up, strengthen what was left, remember the gospel, hold fast and repent. Even there, a few stayed faithful.

Question 2 of 6

Philadelphia

What did Christ promise a church with only a little strength?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

An open door no man can shut, that those who mocked them would know He loved them, that He would keep them from the hour of trial, and that they would be pillars in God's temple, never to go out, with His names written on them.

Question 3 of 6

Lukewarm

Why was being lukewarm so bad?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Like Laodicea's water, it was good for nothing: not hot to heal, not cold to refresh. A church comfortable without Christ, going through the motions in His name, makes Him sick.

Question 4 of 6

Blind

What did the Laodiceans not know about themselves?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That they were wretched, miserable, poor, blind and naked. They thought they were rich and needed nothing. Christ told them the truth and offered what only He can give: true riches, white raiment, and eyes that see.

Question 5 of 6

Love

Why does Christ rebuke and chasten?

What verses tell us about this?

“For whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth, and scourgeth every son whom he receiveth.”

Hebrews 12:6

Answer

Because He loves us. He won't let us keep lying to ourselves. His rebuke is meant to bring us back to Him, zealous and turned around.

Question 6 of 6

The door

Who does Christ knock for, and what does He promise?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Any man. Whoever hears His voice and opens the door, Christ comes in and eats with him, like a friend at his table. And the one who overcomes by faith will sit with Him on His throne.
