Exodus 36

More than enough

The people bring so much that the workmen come to Moses and say it is enough, and Moses has to tell the camp to stop giving. Then the curtains, the boards and the vail are made, just as the LORD had said.

Part one

“The people bring much more than enough”

Verses 1–7

The work begins, and the gifts keep coming until Moses has to say stop.

Old words in these verses

  • wroughtworked, made. “Then wrought Bezaleel” means Bezaleel got to work
  • sanctuarythe holy place: here, the tent being built
  • free offeringsgifts brought on top of the first gift, freely, with nothing owed
  • withalwith it, to do it with
  • restrainedheld back: the people were stopped from bringing any more

Verse 1 says it plainly: the LORD “put wisdom and understanding” in these men “to know how to work all manner of work.” The skill came from Him, and the work was done “according to all that the LORD had commanded.”

Enough, and too much

The work needed
enough
The people brought
too much

“The people bring much more than enough.” The men who ran the work came from their benches and told Moses, and “every morning” the gifts had still been arriving (verse 3).

Stop

Moses sends word through the camp: “Let neither man nor woman make any more work for the offering of the sanctuary. So the people were restrained from bringing.” They had to be held back from giving.

A leader who says “enough”

Most of what we hear about money is the opposite: a man asking for more. Here a leader counts what has come in, hears from the men doing the work, and says: that is sufficient. “Sufficient” means enough. “And too much” means more than the job needed.

  • He heard the workmenThe report came from the men on the job, not from a guess.
  • He didn't ask for moreIt stops at what the work needs.
  • He told everyoneIt was “proclaimed throughout the camp.” Nothing was hidden.

That is how God's work is meant to be run: in the open, and honest to the last shekel. Verses 5 to 7 show it, and the people loved it enough to keep giving until they were stopped.

After the calf: much more

A few chapters back, these same people were on their faces before a golden calf. Now the work of the LORD's house is being flooded with their gifts. There is nothing in the camp that says they deserved this turn.

“Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound. But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound:”

Romans 5:20

Where the sin was great, the LORD's mercy was greater, and it changed hearts so that they gave gladly. If you are a man with a lot to be sorry for, that is the same word for you: much more. The LORD does not bring you back barely. He brings you back with more than enough.

Real world: when you have something to give, a few dollars, a few hours, a ride, a word, give it plainly and gladly. And when someone tells you “that's enough,” take it as a kindness.

Part two

“With cherubims of cunning work”

Verses 8–19

The curtains are sewn and joined, and four coverings go over the top.

Old words in the curtains

  • twined linenfine linen thread, twisted together for strength
  • cherubimsangel-figures, “of cunning work”: woven into the cloth
  • cunningskilled, expert
  • cubitabout 18 inches: 28 cubits is about 42 feet, and 4 cubits is about 6 feet

Ten curtains, each about 42 feet long and 6 feet wide, all of one size. Sewn side by side that is a cloth about 60 feet across.

The words match nearly letter for letter. That is on purpose. The LORD gave a pattern, and the workmen kept it: not improving it, not changing it, not adding a favorite color.

Loops and taches: “that it might be one”

  • coupledjoined: five curtains sewn into one set, and the other five into another
  • loops of bluefifty loops on the edge of each set
  • tachesfifty clasps, of gold for the linen and brass for the goats' hair, that hook the loops together
  • selvedgethe finished edge of a cloth

Two sets of cloth, joined with fifty clasps, and “so it became one tabernacle.” For the goats' hair tent the clasps were brass, “that it might be one.” Each part is fastened to the rest. Nothing in the LORD's house is left loose.

There is a lesson for a man who has come in from outside: you are meant to be clasped to the others. Gold clasps hold the inner curtain to the inner curtain. Find your brothers and stay hooked to them.

Four coverings

Fine linen with cherubimsblue, purple and scarlet: the inside, seen by the priests (36:8)
Goats’ haireleven curtains, the tent over the tabernacle (36:14)
Rams’ skins dyed reda covering for the tent (36:19)
Badgers’ skinsthe outermost covering, above all (36:19)

From the outside a man would have seen a plain, weather-beaten tent. The blue and purple and the woven cherubim were inside, where only the priests went.

“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

John 1:14

“Dwelt among us” is the word for pitching a tent. The tabernacle was God's tent among His people. John says the Word “dwelt”, pitched His tent, among us. Many see a picture here: God in a tent of flesh, the glory inside.

What went up

The linen and the three coverings make the tent. The boards in the next verses are the frame under them. The gate, the altar, the washing basin and the tent: this is how the camp saw it, from the east.

A priest came in from the gate and met the altar first, then the basin, then the door of the tent. Every step has a purpose: you do not get to the holy place without passing the altar.

Part three

“Boards… standing up”

Verses 20–38

The frame stands on silver, and the vail hangs between the holy place and the most holy.

A board and its feet

  • boardsupright planks of shittim wood, about 15 feet tall and 27 inches wide, overlaid with gold
  • tenonstwo pegs cut at the foot of each board
  • socketssilver bases the tenons slot into
  • barsfive on each side, and “the middle bar” running right through (verse 33)

Twenty boards on the south, twenty on the north, and eight at the west: 48 boards on 96 silver sockets. Bars through gold rings hold the whole wall together.

What the silver was

Every board stood on silver. Where did all that silver come from? It was not a free gift. It was the ransom money every man paid when Israel was counted:

“The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less than half a shekel, when they give an offering unto the LORD, to make an atonement for your souls.”

Exodus 30:15

“And of the hundred talents of silver were cast the sockets of the sanctuary, and the sockets of the vail; an hundred sockets of the hundred talents, a talent for a socket.”

Exodus 38:27

So the house of God stood on atonement money: 96 sockets under the boards and four under the pillars of the vail, a hundred in all. Rich and poor paid the same, and every man's half-shekel went into the foundation.

“Forasmuch as ye know that ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, from your vain conversation received by tradition from your fathers; But with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot:”

1 Peter 1:18–19

Peter says our redemption was not paid in silver and gold, but in the blood of Christ. The tent stood on ransom money. We stand on a ransom already paid.

The vail, and the door

The vail (verses 35–36)

Closed the inner room

Blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen, with cherubims. Four gold pillars, on four silver sockets. Between God's throne and everyone else.

The door (verses 37–38)

Opened the tent

An “hanging” of needlework in the same colors, on five pillars, with gold hooks and brass sockets. This is the way in to the holy place.

Chapiters are the tops of pillars, and fillets are bands. Verse 38 says the tops and the bands were overlaid with gold. Vail is veil.

Silver under the vail, brass under the door: the nearer to the holy of holies, the costlier the metal. And there was only one way past the vail, shut to everyone but the high priest, once a year.

The vail torn

“And, behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom; and the earth did quake, and the rocks rent;”

Matthew 27:51

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;”

Hebrews 10:19–20

The vail Bezaleel made kept sinful men out. When Jesus died, the vail in the temple tore from the top to the bottom, from God's side. The way in is open: not because we have cleaned ourselves up, but “by the blood of Jesus.”

If you have fallen lately, you are not shut out. You do not stand at the door ashamed. Come in by the same way every believer comes in, and tell a brother and your pastor where you are.

Points to remember

  1. The workers“Then wrought Bezaleel and Aholiab, and every wise hearted man, in whom the LORD put wisdom and understanding” 36:1The skill came from the LORD, and the work was done as He commanded.
  2. The report“The people bring much more than enough for the service of the work” 36:5After the calf, a changed people gave gladly. His mercy is much more.
  3. The stop“Let neither man nor woman make any more work for the offering of the sanctuary” 36:6An honest leader says “enough.”
  4. The curtains“ten curtains of fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet: with cherubims of cunning work” 36:8Made to the plan, clasped together as one. Plain outside, glory inside.
  5. The boards“forty sockets of silver he made under the twenty boards” 36:24The house stood on ransom silver. We stand on a ransom already paid.
  6. The vail“And he made a vail of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen” 36:35The vail shut sinners out. Christ's blood opened the way in.

Question 1 of 6

The workers

Who began the work, and what had the LORD put in them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Bezaleel, Aholiab and every wise hearted man. The LORD put wisdom and understanding in them, and their hearts stirred them to come to the work.

Question 2 of 6

The report

What did the wise men tell Moses?

What verses tell us about this?

“Moreover the law entered, that the offence might abound. But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound:”

Romans 5:20

Answer

“The people bring much more than enough.” After the golden calf, a forgiven people gave until it was too much. Where sin was great, grace was much more.

Question 3 of 6

The stop

What did Moses command, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

That no one bring any more. What they had was enough for all the work, and too much. A good leader says “enough.”

Question 4 of 6

The curtains

What were the first curtains made of, and what was worked into them?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Ten curtains of fine twined linen in blue, purple and scarlet, with cherubims woven in, joined by fifty gold clasps until “it became one tabernacle.”

Question 5 of 6

The boards

What did the boards of the tabernacle stand on?

What verses tell us about this?

“And of the hundred talents of silver were cast the sockets of the sanctuary, and the sockets of the vail; an hundred sockets of the hundred talents, a talent for a socket.”

Exodus 38:27

Answer

Silver sockets, two to a board, cast from the ransom money every man paid. The house of God stood on a ransom. So do we, because of the blood of Christ.

Question 6 of 6

The vail

What was made to hang across the inner room, and what hung at the door?

What verses tell us about this?

“Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh;”

Hebrews 10:19–20

Answer

A vail of blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen with cherubims across the inner room, and a hanging of needlework at the door. The vail shut sinners out; it tore when Jesus died, and His blood opened the way.
