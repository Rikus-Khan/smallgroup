Hebrews 7

A priest for ever

A mysterious king-priest who met Abraham points to Jesus: the High Priest who never dies, never needs replacing, and is praying for you right now.

Where we are in Hebrews

The writer of Hebrews is writing to Jewish believers, about AD 64 to 68, who are tempted to slip back into the old temple religion. His whole case is one word: Jesus is better.

Part one

The king who met Abraham

Verses 1–10

A king and priest steps out of nowhere in Genesis 14, blesses Abraham, and steps back out again.

Genesis 14: the night raid

Four kings raided the cities by the Dead Sea and carried off Abram's nephew Lot. Abram took 318 of his own men, chased them the length of the land to Dan, struck them by night, and brought everyone home.

On the way back, near Salem, a king came out to meet him.

“And Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine: and he was the priest of the most high God. And he blessed him… And he gave him tithes of all.”

Genesis 14:18–20

His name is a sermon

Notice the order: “first… righteousness, and after that… peace.” God never gives a man peace by pretending his sin doesn't matter. Jesus dealt with the sin first, at the cross. That's why the peace He gives is real, and why it lasts.

Genesis is a book of family lines: who begat whom, how long he lived, “and he died.” Melchisedec gets none of it. He had parents like any man. Scripture simply leaves them out, on purpose.

So on the page he has no start and no finish. He is “made like unto the Son of God”, who really has no beginning and no end, and whose priesthood never runs out.

Greater than Abraham

To a Jew, nobody stood taller than Abraham: “the patriarch”, the father of the nation, the man God made promises to. Yet two things happened that day that put Melchisedec above him.

1 · The tithe

Abraham gave him a tenth

A tenth of everything taken in the battle. You give a tenth to God's priest, not to your equal. Abraham was saying thank you to God for the win.

2 · The blessing

Melchisedec blessed Abraham

“Without all contradiction the less is blessed of the better.” Everyone knew it: the greater man lays his hand on the lesser, never the other way round.

If Melchisedec, the picture, is greater than Abraham, then Jesus, the real thing, is greater than everything the old religion was built on.

Levi bowed too

The priests of Israel all came from Levi, Abraham's great-grandson. They collected the tithes. But “as I may so say”: in a manner of speaking, Levi paid a tithe to Melchisedec too.

He wasn't born yet. He was “in the loins of his father”: everything Levi would ever be was still inside Abraham. When Abraham bowed, the whole priestly line bowed with him.

And notice verse 8: the Levite priests were “men that die.” Of Melchisedec it is “witnessed that he liveth.” Hold on to that. It's where the chapter is going.

Part two

A different kind of priest

Verses 11–22

Why God promised a priest from the wrong tribe, and swore an oath to make it sure.

The wrong tribe, on purpose

ReubenSimeonLevipriestsJudahkingsDanNaphtali GadAsherIssacharZebulunJosephBenjamin

Under Moses, only Levi's tribe could serve as priests, and only Aaron's family as high priest. Judah was the tribe of kings: David's line. Jesus was born in Judah's line. By the old law, He could never have served at the altar.

Jesusfrom Judah · King, and priest for ever, after the order of Melchisedec

So if God promised a priest from outside Levi, the old system was never meant to be the last word. “If therefore perfection were by the Levitical priesthood… what further need?” It couldn't make anyone perfect. A new priest means a new arrangement.

What made a man a priest

The old way

“A carnal commandment”

Carnal here means bodily: who your father was. A Levite was priest because of his birth, and stopped being priest at his death. Then the next man took over.

Jesus

“The power of an endless life”

He is priest because of who He is. He died, rose, and can't die again. His work for you will never be interrupted by a funeral.

“The law made nothing perfect.” It showed the problem but couldn't fix it. Jesus brings “a better hope… by the which we draw nigh unto God.” If you've spent years far from God, that's the word for you: nigh. Near. Not at a distance, through a go-between who might die. Close.

“The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.”

Psalm 110:4

No Levite priest was ever sworn in by God's own oath. Jesus was. “Will not repent” means God will never change His mind about it. And Jesus is “a surety of a better testament”: a co-signer who guarantees the deal. Your side of it rests on Him.

Part three

He ever liveth

Verses 23–28

The priest who never dies, who saves all the way, and who is praying for you right now.

Priest after priest after priest

Every high priest of Israel was a mortal man with an expiry date. He served, he grew old, he died, and someone new had to learn your name. Jesus “continueth ever.” His priesthood is unchangeable: it never passes to anyone else.

To the uttermost

“Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost.” Uttermost means all the way: completely, to the very end, with nothing left undone.

There is no depth a man can reach that is lower than Christ can reach. Not your worst year, not your record, not the thing you've never said out loud. The rope goes all the way down.

Who is it for? “Them that come unto God by him.” Not the good ones. Not the ones who've cleaned up first. Whoever comes.

Jesus is praying for you. Today.

Intercession means standing up for someone before God and pleading his case. Jesus is doing that now, for every man who has come to God by Him. Ask Peter:

“Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat: But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Luke 22:31–32

Jesus prayed for Peter before he denied Him three times, and planned his way back before he fell. If you fell yesterday, He was already praying for you, and He's still at it.

“My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous:”

1 John 2:1

Why a believer is safe

Your safety doesn't hang on how tightly you hold on today. It hangs on His life, and His life can't end. A saved man isn't one bad night away from being lost. He has a Priest who never stops praying and never dies.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Daily, or once

The old priests: morning and evening, every day, for centuries

Once.“when he offered up himself”

The old high priest had to offer first for his own sins, then for the people's, and do it again the next day. Jesus had no sin of His own (“holy, harmless, undefiled”), and He offered Himself one time. On the cross He said “It is finished” (John 19:30), and He meant it.

What to do with this today

  1. SinThe old priests needed a sacrifice for their own sins. So do we. Call yours what it is.
  2. RegretJesus prayed for Peter before he fell. Your failure didn't catch Him off guard.
  3. GraceOne offering, once. Nothing left to pay, and no daily sacrifice to keep up.
  4. HopeHe saves to the uttermost, and He ever liveth. Your story isn't over.

For the real world: some time today, when the old shame or the old craving shows up, stop for ten seconds and say it plainly: “Jesus is praying for me right now.” Then do the next right thing, as a man who has a Priest on his side.

Question 1 of 6

Melchisedec

Who was Melchisedec, and why does he matter to us?

What verses tell us about this?

“And Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine: and he was the priest of the most high God.”

Genesis 14:18

Answer

The king of Salem and priest of the most high God, who blessed Abraham. Scripture leaves out his family, birth and death, so on the page he is a priest with no start and no end: a picture of Jesus, our King and Priest for ever.

Question 2 of 6

A new priest

Why did God need a priest who didn't come from Levi?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The old priesthood couldn't make anyone perfect. Its priests got the job by birth and lost it by death. God promised a different Priest, from Judah, who serves by His endless life, and through Him we draw near to God.

Question 3 of 6

The oath

What did God swear about His Son?

What verses tell us about this?

“The LORD hath sworn, and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek.”

Psalm 110:4

Answer

That He is a priest for ever, after the order of Melchisedec, and that God will never take it back. No Levite was ever sworn in like that. Jesus is the guarantee, the surety, of a better covenant.

Question 4 of 6

Uttermost

How far can Jesus save a man who comes to God by Him?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Answer

To the uttermost: all the way down to where a man is, and all the way through to the end. No sin is too deep, and no saved man is let go. It's for whoever comes to God by Him.

Question 5 of 6

Praying

What is Jesus doing for His people right now?

What verses tell us about this?

“But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.”

Luke 22:32

“Who is he that condemneth? It is Christ that died, yea rather, that is risen again, who is even at the right hand of God, who also maketh intercession for us.”

Romans 8:34

Answer

He is praying for them, pleading their case before the Father, and He never stops, because He never dies. He prayed for Peter before he fell and brought him back. He does the same for every believer.

Question 6 of 6

Once

Why doesn't anyone need to offer a sacrifice for sin any more?

What verses tell us about this?

“But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God;”

Hebrews 10:12

Answer

Because Jesus, who had no sin of His own, offered Himself once, and it was enough for ever. The old priests had to keep offering every day. He finished it and sat down. There is nothing left to pay.
