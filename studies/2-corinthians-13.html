2 Corinthians 13

Examine yourselves

Paul's last words to a church he nearly lost: a warning, a check-up, and one of the most loved blessings in the Bible.

Some men in Corinth had been saying Paul was weak and not a real apostle. In chapter 12 he answered: the Lord told him my strength is made perfect in weakness, and he will not be ashamed of it.

Chapter 13 is the end of the letter. Paul gets ready to come, tells them what he hopes to find, turns the question back on them, and says goodbye. At the end of the study we'll look back over all thirteen chapters.

Part one

The third visit

Verses 1–4

A fair warning, and the strangest kind of power.

On his way

Paul is writing from Macedonia (northern Greece, around Philippi). This is the third time I am coming to you. The way to Corinth is long, and mostly by land.

It is about AD 56. When he gets there, he stays three months in Greece (Acts 20:2–3), and during that time he writes the letter to the Romans.

The letter was sent ahead so the church could put things right before he arrived. He would rather fix it by letter than with a fight.

Two or three witnesses

Paul is quoting an old rule of fairness from the law of Moses. A charge could not stand on one man's word.

“One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity, or for any sin, in any sin that he sinneth: at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established.”

Deuteronomy 19:15

Notice the tone. Paul has told them twice. I told you before, and foretell you: he is not springing anything on them. He warns them, and then I will not spare means he will deal with it if it has not changed.

That is how to confront anyone. Say it plainly, say it ahead of time, and have it settled by facts, not by gossip. And if you are the one being warned, take it as kindness: the man who warns you still wants you.

Weak on the cross, alive in power

The Corinthians wanted proof of power. Paul says: look at Christ. He was weak enough to be nailed to a cross, and He was raised by the power of God. That is the order, every time.

It is the same pattern as chapter 12. If you are weak today, tired, tempted, behind, you are standing exactly where God's power starts. We shall live with him.

Part two

Examine yourselves

Verses 5–10

An honest check, not a nervous breakdown.

Two ways to look at yourself

Verse 5 has been used to make believers feel sick, so look at it carefully. Examine and prove are tests, like testing metal to see if it is real. And the test question is not was I good enough lately?

The anxious way

“Am I good enough?”

You count your failures, check your feelings, and wonder if you have lost it. There is no end to that, and nothing in verse 5 asks for it.

The honest way

“Is Christ in me?”

You ask the one question Paul asks: know ye not your own selves, how that Jesus Christ is in you? It has an answer, and you can know it.

Reprobate means a man who failed the test, like a coin that turns out to be fake. Paul's point to Corinth is gentle: you wanted proof that Christ speaks through me. You are the proof, if Christ is in you.

An honest check-up

  1. ✓Have I trusted Jesus Himself?not just admired Him, or joined something. Trusting Him to save me.the faith
  2. ✓Is the Spirit bearing witness?the quiet sense, however faint, that I belong to Himthe witness
  3. ✓Is anything changing?slowly, with falls, but turning more toward Him than awaythe fruit

“The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God:”

Romans 8:16

“Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts: And see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.”

Psalm 139:23–24

David asks God to do the searching, and he ends with the way everlasting. That is the right spirit for a self-check: open, not scared. If you find a wicked way, bring it to God today. That is what a check-up is for.

A check-up is not a trial

If Christ is in you, you are held. The check does not put you at risk; it shows you where you stand.

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

A bad night does not undo it. Be honest, be sorry, get up and go on.

“Know that ye have eternal life”

“These things have I written unto you that believe on the name of the Son of God; that ye may know that ye have eternal life, and that ye may believe on the name of the Son of God.”

1 John 5:13

God wants you to know. Not hope, not guess. If the check-up leaves you unsure that you ever really trusted Jesus, settle it now. It takes one honest prayer:

“For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Romans 10:13

Whosoever means you. Tell Him you trust Him, and tell a believer you did. If you are already His, say thank you, and read verse 5 again as a man who is held.

What Paul wants for them

  1. Do no evilhis prayer for them, before anything else (v. 7)
  2. Be honestdo what is right, even if it makes him look bad
  3. Be perfecthis wish: your perfection, being made whole (v. 9)
  4. Build uppower “to edification, and not to destruction” (v. 10)

Verse 7 is Paul at his most unselfish. Not that we should appear approved. He would rather they do right and he looks like a failure than that he looks good and they do wrong. Verse 8: we can do nothing against the truth, but for the truth.

And verse 10 is for anyone with any authority over other men, a boss, a father, a leader. Power is given to build people up. Paul wrote this chapter so that when he arrived, he would not have to use the other kind.

Part three

Farewell

Verses 11–14

Four short commands, and the Father, Son and Spirit in a single sentence.

Four things to do today

  1. Be perfectbe mended, put in order, like a torn net fixed so it can work again
  2. Be of good comforttake heart, and take comfort from God and from each other
  3. Be of one mindaim the same way, with the same Lord
  4. Live in peacewith one another, and with God

The word perfect here is not “never makes a mistake.” It means mended, in working order: the word used for fixing a net. Paul is not asking for a perfect record. He is asking them to let God put them back together.

Today: pick one. Make peace with one person, or ask one man how he is really doing and listen. Then the God of love and peace shall be with you: He promises to go along.

An holy kiss was the warm greeting of the time: a plain way of saying you are welcome and we are family. We would shake a hand and mean it.

One blessing, three Persons

The whole letter ends with this. Read it slowly.

The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ: His undeserved favor, shown on the cross. Every good thing we have starts here.

The love of God: the Father, who loved us first and sent His Son.

The communion of the Holy Ghost: communion means sharing, fellowship. The Spirit lives in every believer, and He joins us to God and to one another.

Be with you all. All. Not just the strong ones, not just the ones who have it together. Even Corinth, with all its trouble.

Father, Son and Spirit together

Paul does not stop to explain it. The three are named together as a matter of course, because the early believers knew God that way. They did not think of three gods, but of one God in three Persons.

“Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost:”

Matthew 28:19

“And Jesus, when he was baptized, went up straightway out of the water: and, lo, the heavens were opened unto him, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove, and lighting upon him: And lo a voice from heaven, saying, This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

Matthew 3:16–17

At Jesus' baptism all three are there at once: the Son in the water, the Spirit like a dove, the Father speaking from heaven. A man who has been alone a long time can lean on that: you are not facing today alone. The Father loves you, the Son has made peace for you, the Spirit is in you.

Part four

Looking back

The whole letter

That was the last chapter. Here is where the letter has taken us.

Thirteen chapters in seven steps

  1. 1The God of all comfortand comfort to hand on
  2. 2–3Forgive, and the Spirit gives lifethe new covenant
  3. 4–5Treasure in earthen vesselsa new creature; be reconciled
  4. 6–7Now is the day of salvationgodly sorrow
  5. 8–9GivingGod loveth a cheerful giver
  6. 10–11Paul answers his criticsand his long list of sufferings
  7. 12–13The thorn, and the check-upgrace enough; examine yourselves

Read it as a man who nearly lost a church, and who is writing it back to health. Everything in the letter points the same way: God works through broken, weak people, and He does not leave them there.

What this letter gave us

  1. Sinnamed, never hidden: the quarrels, the gossip, the uncleanness (12:20–21)
  2. Regret“godly sorrow worketh repentance… not to be repented of” (7:10)
  3. Grace“My grace is sufficient for thee” (12:9)
  4. Hope“the inward man is renewed day by day” (4:16)
  5. The real world“be ye reconciled to God” (5:20): start there, then be peace to the next man

If you remember one thing, make it Paul's own summary: for when I am weak, then am I strong. Your weak places are not the end of the story. They are where Christ's power shows up.

And the last word of the letter is a blessing, for all of us: the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Ghost, be with you all.

Points to remember

  1. Weakness“Crucified through weakness, yet he liveth by the power of God” 13:4God's power starts where ours stops. Christ was weak on the cross and alive in power.
  2. Self-check“Examine yourselves, whether ye be in the faith” 13:5An honest check-up, in the open, not an anxious count of failures.
  3. Assurance“Jesus Christ is in you” 13:5The question is whether Christ is in you, and God wants you to know it.
  4. Security“Neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand” John 10:28A believer is held by Christ, not by his own grip.
  5. Farewell“Be perfect, be of good comfort, be of one mind, live in peace” 13:11Let God mend you, take comfort, aim together, and make peace.
  6. The blessing“The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Ghost” 13:14Son, Father and Spirit: all three are with you.

Question 1 of 6

Weakness

How did Christ show power through weakness?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.”

2 Corinthians 12:9

Answer

He was crucified through weakness, and He lives by the power of God. We are weak in Him too, and we shall live with Him by that same power. God's strength shows up where ours has run out.

Question 2 of 6

Self-check

How should a believer examine himself?

What verses tell us about this?

“Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts: And see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.”

Psalm 139:23–24

Answer

Honestly and openly, asking God to search him. The question is whether he is in the faith and whether Christ is in him, not whether he was perfect lately. If he finds something, he brings it to God today.

Question 3 of 6

Assurance

How can a man know that Christ is in him?

What verses tell us about this?

“The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God:”

Romans 8:16

“These things have I written unto you that believe on the name of the Son of God; that ye may know that ye have eternal life, and that ye may believe on the name of the Son of God.”

1 John 5:13

Answer

He has trusted Jesus, and the Spirit bears witness with his spirit that he is a child of God. God wrote it down so that believers may know they have eternal life. And if he is not sure, he can call on the Lord today.

Question 4 of 6

Security

Can a believer be plucked out of Christ's hand?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

“Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:”

Philippians 1:6

Answer

No. Christ gives eternal life, and they shall never perish. He who began the good work will finish it. A bad day isn't the end: be honest, get up, and go on.

Question 5 of 6

Farewell

What does Paul ask of the Corinthians as he says goodbye?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Be perfect, be of good comfort, be of one mind, live in peace. Let God mend you, take comfort, aim together, and make peace, and the God of love and peace shall be with you.

Question 6 of 6

The blessing

Who is named in Paul's closing blessing?

What verses tell us about this?

“Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost:”

Matthew 28:19

Answer

The Lord Jesus Christ, with His grace; God the Father, with His love; and the Holy Ghost, with His communion. The Father, Son and Spirit are all named in one sentence, and the blessing is for you all.
