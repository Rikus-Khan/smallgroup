John 15

The vine and the branches

On the way to the garden, Jesus tells His men how to stay connected to Him, how much He loves them, and what the world will think of them.

Part one

The true vine

Verses 1–8

A branch has one job: stay on the vine.

The last of seven “I am”s

Seven times in John, Jesus takes God's own name, “I AM” (Exodus 3:14), and finishes the sentence with a picture of what He is to us. This is the seventh:

  1. The bread of lifeJohn 6:35
  2. The light of the worldJohn 8:12
  3. The doorJohn 10:9
  4. The good shepherdJohn 10:11
  5. The resurrection and the lifeJohn 11:25
  6. The way, the truth, and the lifeJohn 14:6
  7. The true vineJohn 15:1

Why “true”? In the Old Testament, Israel was God's vine, and it kept letting Him down:

“For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant: and he looked for judgment, but behold oppression; for righteousness, but behold a cry.”

Isaiah 5:7

Jesus is the vine that never fails. The husbandman is the farmer, the vinedresser. That's the Father, and He tends every branch Himself.

What the farmer does with a branch

airoEYE-rohto lift up, or to take away

The word behind “taketh away” usually means to lift up. John uses it twice in one verse at Lazarus' tomb: “they took away the stone,” and Jesus “lifted up his eyes” (11:41).

That fits a vineyard. A vine branch that trails in the dirt gets moldy and won't bear grapes, so many Bible teachers point out that a vinedresser lifts it up off the ground and props it in the sun where it can bear fruit. Either way, the Father doesn't ignore a fruitless branch. He deals with it.

Notice who is talking: Jesus is speaking to eleven men who already belong to Him. Judas has already walked out into the night (13:30). This is a lesson about fruit, not about how a man gets saved.

Pruned to bear more

A healthy vine throws out shoots everywhere, and they soak up the strength the grapes need.

So the vinedresser cuts them back. It looks like loss, but it's how a good branch becomes a great one.

“Purgeth” and “clean” (v. 3) come from the same Greek word. Hard seasons aren't the Father throwing a believer away. They're His hands on the branch.

The life is in the vine

A branch doesn't strain to make grapes. It can't. The sap, the life, comes up from the vine, and the fruit just shows up on the branch.

Cut a branch off and set it on the ground. Same wood, same shape, and it will never grow a single grape. “Without me ye can do nothing.”

A lot of us tried to fix our lives by willpower. It didn't work. That's not the plan. The plan is to stay connected to Jesus and let His life do in you what you never could do of yourself.

Abide: the word of the chapter

Watch for it. “Abide” means to stay, to remain, to live somewhere. Here's where it shows up in verses 4 to 16:

4×3 5×1 6×1 7×2 8  9×1 10×2 11×1 12  13  14  15  16×1

“Abide” nine times, plus “continue” (v. 9) and “remain” (v. 11, 16), which are the same Greek word. Twelve times in thirteen verses.

Abiding isn't a feeling or a religious high. It's staying close day after day: in His word (v. 7), in prayer (v. 7), and in obedience (v. 10). The Spirit already lives in you. Abiding is living like it.

Can a believer be cut off and lost?

No. Jesus has already settled that:

“And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand.”

John 10:28

Bible teachers read verse 6 two ways. Neither says a saved man gets unsaved:

Only looked attached

Judas walked with Jesus three years and never believed (6:64). No life ever flowed into him. That night he “went immediately out” (13:30).

A wasted life

It's men who gather and burn, not the Father. A believer who drifts loses his fruit and his testimony. His works can burn, “but he himself shall be saved; yet so as by fire” (1 Corinthians 3:15).

Either way: stay close to Jesus. You're held by His hand, not by your grip.

Ask what ye will?

This isn't a blank check for a new truck. Look at the “if”: if you abide in Him and His words abide in you. A man soaked in Jesus' words starts wanting what Jesus wants, and those are the prayers God loves to answer.

“And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us:”

1 John 5:14

And what's the fruit (v. 8)? It's Christ's character growing in you, and other people coming to know Him through you:

“But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law.”

Galatians 5:22–23

Part two

Friends

Verses 9–17

The love that came down from the Father, and where it goes next.

Love that flows downhill

The FatherlovedJesus
Jesus“so have I loved you”His disciples
His disciples“love one another” (v. 12)each other

Jesus loves you the same way the Father loves Him. Not a smaller, leftover love. You can't earn it, but you can “continue” in it: live inside it, by keeping His commandments the way He kept His Father's (v. 10).

And the point of it all (v. 11)? “That your joy might be full.” Jesus isn't trying to take your joy. He's trying to fill it.

Greater love hath no man than this.

Jesus said this a few hours before He did it. By the next afternoon, He would lay His life down on a cross.

Most men would die for a friend, if it came to that. Jesus went further. He died for us while we were still against Him:

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Whatever you've done, whatever you've been, that's the measure of how much He loves you.

Servant or friend?

A servant

Does what he's told

He takes orders, but he “knoweth not what his lord doeth.” Nobody explains the plan to him.

A friend

Is let in on the plan

He still obeys (v. 14), but he knows why. Jesus told these men everything the Father told Him.

Only one man in the Old Testament got that title:

“And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God.”

James 2:23

In verse 16, “I have chosen you, and ordained you” is Jesus reminding the Eleven that He picked them for a job: to go and bear fruit that lasts (6:70). He calls; whosoever believes may come (3:16). And men who once were nobody's friend are now His.

If you've lost old friends since you started following Jesus, or the old crowd mocks you now, that isn't a sign you're doing it wrong. It's a sign you're “not of the world” anymore (v. 19).

But look at the last pair too. Some will keep your word, the same way some kept His. Keep telling it.

No cloke

A cloke is a cloak, a cover to hide under. Jesus means an excuse. These men had run out of them:

  • “Nobody ever told us.”He came and spoke to them (v. 22).
  • “We never saw any proof.”He did works no other man ever did (v. 24).
  • “He gave us a reason.”They hated Him without a cause (v. 25).
  • No excuse left.Only the choice to hate Him, and the Father with Him (v. 23).

“They that hate me without a cause are more than the hairs of mine head: they that would destroy me, being mine enemies wrongfully, are mighty: then I restored that which I took not away.”

Psalm 69:4

“Their law” here means their own Scriptures. David's psalm said the Messiah would be hated for nothing, and He was.

When the world pushes back, you're not on your own. The Holy Spirit lives in every believer, and He's telling the same story you are. You just tell what you've seen Jesus do in your life. He does the convincing.

Points to remember

  1. Who Jesus is“I am the true vine” 15:1He's the vine that never fails. Every bit of real life in us comes from Him.
  2. Abiding“Without me ye can do nothing” 15:5Fruit comes from staying connected, not from trying harder.
  3. Pruning“He purgeth it, that it may bring forth more fruit” 15:2Hard seasons are the Father's hands on the branch, not His rejection.
  4. His love“Greater love hath no man than this” 15:13He laid down His life for us, and He loves us as the Father loves Him.
  5. Friendship“I have called you friends” 15:15He lets His friends in on what He's doing.
  6. The world“It hated me before it hated you” 15:18The world's hatred means you belong to Him now. Keep bearing witness.

Question 1 of 6

Who Jesus is

What does it mean that Jesus is the true vine?

What verses tell us about this?

“For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant: and he looked for judgment, but behold oppression; for righteousness, but behold a cry.”

Isaiah 5:7

Answer

Israel was God's vine, and it failed to bear the fruit He looked for. Jesus is the real thing, the vine that never fails. All the life a believer has flows from Him, the way sap flows from the vine into every branch. And the Father Himself is the farmer who tends us.

Question 2 of 6

Abiding

Where does a branch get the power to bear fruit?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Not from itself. A branch can't make one grape on its own; the life comes from the vine. We bear fruit by abiding: staying close to Jesus in His word, in prayer, and in obedience. Willpower alone never changed a heart. His life in us does.

Question 3 of 6

Pruning

Why does the Father prune a branch that already bears fruit?

What verses tell us about this?

“Now no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous: nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby.”

Hebrews 12:11

Answer

So it can bear more. Pruning cuts away the growth that soaks up strength without making fruit. It hurts at the time, but it's a sign the Father is working on a branch He cares about. Discipline isn't Him throwing us away. It's Him making us fruitful.

Question 4 of 6

His love

How great is Jesus' love for us?

What verses tell us about this?

“But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8

Answer

He loves us the way the Father loves Him, and He proved it by laying down His life. He didn't wait for us to clean up first. He died for us while we were still sinners. There is no greater love than that.

Question 5 of 6

Friendship

What's the difference between a servant and a friend of Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God.”

James 2:23

Answer

A servant just takes orders and isn't told the plan. A friend still obeys, but he's let in on what his master is doing and why. Jesus calls believers His friends and tells us what the Father told Him. That's an honor no man earns.

Question 6 of 6

The world

Why does the world hate those who follow Jesus?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because it hated Jesus first, and believers now belong to Him instead of the world. The world doesn't know the Father, so it can't stand the people who carry His Son's name. But we're not on our own: the Comforter testifies with us, and some will still believe.
