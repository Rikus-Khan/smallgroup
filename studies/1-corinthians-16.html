Raised, and goodbye

Paul wrote this letter from Ephesus, about AD 55, to a church he started in Corinth. Chapter 15 ended with “therefore… your labour is not in vain.” Now the “therefore” gets practical: money, plans, people.

It is the last chapter, so it's also the goodbye. At the end we'll look back over the whole letter.

Part one

The collection

Verses 1–4

Straight after the resurrection, Paul talks about money. It is the same “therefore.”

Who the money was for

“The saints” are fellow believers, here the poor ones in Jerusalem. Hard times had hit them (Acts 11 tells of a famine). Paul was gathering a gift from the churches of Galatia, Macedonia and Achaia, to carry to them himself.

“But now I go unto Jerusalem to minister unto the saints. For it hath pleased them of Macedonia and Achaia to make a certain contribution for the poor saints which are at Jerusalem.”

Romans 15:25–26

The first day of the week

Believers met on the first day, Sunday, the day Jesus rose (Acts 20:7). Paul's plan: each one sets something aside at home, “as God hath prospered him.”

It's every man, in proportion to what he has, and ahead of time, so “there be no gatherings when I come”: no panic, no pressure.

Do one thing today. Before you spend on anything else, put a small amount aside, on the same day each week. A man who sets aside first has money that no longer runs him.

Handled in the open

  1. ✓whomsoever ye shall approveThe church chooses the men who carry the gift. Paul doesn't pick them himself.v. 3
  2. ✓by your lettersTheir own letters back the men, so nobody can say the money went missing.v. 3
  3. ✓if it be meet that I go alsoPaul goes along only if it's fitting. He doesn't take over.v. 4

Paul had been accused of everything. He keeps this money where everyone can see it. If you've had trouble with money, take that same road: let a trusted brother see your books. That isn't a punishment. It keeps you safe.

Part two

Plans and helpers

Verses 5–12

Paul tells them where he's going and who is coming ahead of him.

Paul's plan

He is in Ephesus now. He'll stay until Pentecost, the spring feast about fifty days after Passover. Then he'll go on through Macedonia, which lies north of Corinth, and come down to them, maybe staying the winter.

“If the Lord permit” (verse 7). Paul makes plans and holds them loosely. A man who has fallen knows that tomorrow isn't promised. Make the plan, and hold it open-handed.

From Ephesus, one step at a time

  1. NowEphesus, about AD 55writing this letter
  2. Pentecostleaves Ephesusspring
  3. MacedoniaPhilippi, Thessalonica
  4. Corinthmaybe the winter
  5. Jerusalemwith the giftand then Rome (Romans 15)

Paul wrote this about AD 55, and by about AD 57 he was in Corinth writing Romans (see Romans 15:25). His plans changed along the way, as plans do, but the direction held: toward Jerusalem with the gift.

A door open, and men against it

“A great door and effectual”: a real chance to do good work. “And there are many adversaries.” Paul doesn't say although. He says and. The same place that has the open door has the enemies.

That is why he stays. Many of us have thought: it's hard, so I must be in the wrong place. Hard isn't always wrong. A new life often opens a door, and the old crowd pushes back.

Don't walk through it alone. Take a brother with you.

Two helpers, treated two ways

Timothy: sent ahead

“Let no man despise him”

Timothy was young, and Paul's helper (Acts 19:22). He may have been nervous, and a rough church could eat him alive. Paul says: make him feel safe, “without fear,” and send him home in peace. He works “the work of the Lord, as I also do.”

Apollos: “not at all to come at this time”

Paul doesn't push

In chapters 1 and 3 some Corinthians made Apollos their hero against Paul. Yet Paul “greatly desired” him to come. Apollos said not now, and Paul leaves it with him: “when he shall have convenient time.” A friend, not a rival.

“Let no man despise thy youth; but be thou an example of the believers, in word, in conversation, in charity, in spirit, in faith, in purity.”

1 Timothy 4:12

Some of you are the new man in the group, and some have a record. Nobody gets despised here for what he used to be. And when your turn comes, you'll be the one who makes the next new man feel safe.

Part three

Stand fast

Verses 13–18

Four short orders, the rule that goes with them, and a family worth thanking.

Four orders, one rule

Watch yeStay awake. Know where you're weak, and what's coming.
Stand fast in the faithPlant your feet on what's true: Christ died, rose, and is yours.
Quit you like menAct like grown men: brave, steady, doing what's right when it's hard.
Be strongNot tough for show. Strong enough to keep going, one day at a time.

“Let all your things be done with charity.” “Charity” is the old word for love: the love of chapter 13. Strength without love is how a man hurts people. Love is what the strength is for.

This verse is aimed straight at men like us, in a church that was proud and quarrelsome. Be brave, and be kind. The two go together.

“All your things” means all of them

  1. ✓At workDo the job right, even when the boss isn't looking. Treat the new guy as well as the foreman.charity
  2. ✓On the phoneAnswer your mother. Don't send the text that you'd want back.charity
  3. ✓At homeDo the dishes. Tell your kids the truth. Say sorry first.charity
  4. ✓When you've fallenTell a brother the same day. Don't hide it, and don't beat yourself up. Love yourself enough to get up.charity

The household of Stephanas

StephanasOne of the first in Achaia to believe: its “firstfruits,” the same word as in chapter 15. Paul baptized his household himself (1:16).
FortunatusWith Stephanas, he came to Paul at Ephesus. They probably carried the church's letter (7:1).
AchaicusHe, too, travelled to Paul. “That which was lacking on your part they have supplied.”

Here's an old word for you: “addicted.” In 1611 it meant given over to. This whole house gave itself over to one thing, helping other believers. It had nothing to do with preaching or money. Quiet, steady service.

Paul's word for them is “refreshed.” And his order to the church: therefore acknowledge such. Say thank you to the men who show up.

Do one thing today. Think of one man who has refreshed your spirit, a sponsor, a brother, a friend. Tell him so today. And ask yourself if you could be that for someone this month.

Part four

Goodbye

Verses 19–24

Greetings, a signature, a warning, a prayer, and the last two lines of the letter.

Greetings, and a signature

“Salute” means greet. The churches of Asia, the region around Ephesus, send their love. So do Aquila and Priscilla, the couple who knew Corinth well: they had lived there with Paul (Acts 18). The church met “in their house,” with no church building.

“A holy kiss” was an ordinary greeting between friends, today a handshake or a hug. Then Paul takes the pen from his scribe and signs with his own hand: this one is really from me.

the scribe's hand The salutation of me Paul with mine own hand.

A hard word, and a hopeful one

Ἀνάθεμα
Anathema
Greek: set apart for God's judgment
Maran atha
“Our Lord, come!”
Aramaic, Jesus' own language

Who is Paul talking about? “If any man love not the Lord Jesus Christ”: a man who has turned his back on Him for good. He isn't talking about a believer who stumbled and hates it. The man who fell yesterday and is sick about it is, in fact, the opposite of “loves not.”

“Maranatha” was a cry of the first Christians, left in Aramaic and not translated. Teachers read it as “Our Lord, come!”, or as “Our Lord has come.” Either way it looks ahead: Jesus is coming back.

“He which testifieth these things saith, Surely I come quickly. Amen. Even so, come, Lord Jesus.”

A church with every one of these problems. Divided, sleeping around, suing each other, proud of their gifts, some of them doubting the resurrection. Yet Paul called them “saints” on the first page, and he loves them on the last.

How the letter begins, and how it ends

The first thing Paul says to them (1:4)

grace

“I thank my God always on your behalf, for the grace of God which is given you by Jesus Christ.” Before one correction, he names what God had already done.

The last thing (16:23–24)

grace, and love

“The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you. My love be with you all in Christ Jesus. Amen.” All. Even the ones he had just corrected.

That's how a pastor tells hard things: grace before and love after. You can take correction from a man like that. And the same Lord says the same to you today, whatever you did yesterday.

Points to remember

  1. Giving“Upon the first day of the week let every one of you lay by him in store, as God hath prospered him” 16:2Set something aside first, a little at a time, for believers in need. Do it in the open.
  2. Open doors“A great door and effectual is opened unto me, and there are many adversaries” 16:9An open door and opposition come together. Hard doesn't mean wrong.
  3. New believers“Let no man therefore despise him” 16:11Make the young and the new feel safe. Nobody is despised for what he used to be.
  4. Stand fast“Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong. Let all your things be done with charity.” 16:13–14Be awake, steady, brave and strong, and do it all in love.
  5. Faithful helpers“They have addicted themselves to the ministry of the saints… acknowledge ye them that are such” 16:15, 18Quiet servants refresh the whole church. Thank them, and be one.
  6. Looking ahead“Maranatha. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you.” 16:22–23“Our Lord, come.” Jesus is coming back, and until then grace goes with you.

Question 1 of 6

Giving

How did Paul say to set money aside, and who was it for?

What verses tell us about this?

“For it hath pleased them of Macedonia and Achaia to make a certain contribution for the poor saints which are at Jerusalem.”

Romans 15:26

Answer

Set something aside on the first day of each week, according to what he had, so there was no scramble when Paul came. It was for the poor believers in Jerusalem.

Question 2 of 6

Open doors

What did Paul say about the “great door” at Ephesus?

What verses tell us about this?

“So he sent into Macedonia two of them that ministered unto him, Timotheus and Erastus; but he himself stayed in Asia for a season.”

Acts 19:22

Answer

A great door and effectual was open to him, and there were many adversaries. He stayed in Ephesus because of both: opposition didn't mean he was in the wrong place.

Question 3 of 6

New believers

How were the Corinthians to treat young Timothy?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Make him feel safe, with no fear, don't despise him, and send him on his way in peace. He was doing the Lord's work, just as Paul was.

Question 4 of 6

Stand fast

What are Paul's four commands in verse 13, and what must go with them?

What verses tell us about this?

“Therefore, my beloved brethren, be ye stedfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labour is not in vain in the Lord.”

1 Corinthians 15:58

Answer

Watch, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong. And let all your things be done with charity: love goes with every one of them.

Question 5 of 6

Faithful helpers

Who was Stephanas, and what should we do for men like him?

What verses tell us about this?

“And I baptized also the household of Stephanas: besides, I know not whether I baptized any other.”

1 Corinthians 1:16

Answer

One of the first believers in Achaia, whose household gave themselves over to serving other believers. Paul says: acknowledge such men. Thank them, follow their lead, and be one.
