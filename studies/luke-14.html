Luke 14

Come; for all things are now ready

A sabbath dinner at a Pharisee's house: a man healed, guests jostling for seats, a great supper nobody wanted to come to, and the cost of following Jesus.

Part one

They watched him

Verses 1–6

A dinner invitation from a leading Pharisee, every eye in the room on Jesus, and a sick man standing right in front of Him.

On the road to Jerusalem

Luke doesn't name the town. Since 9:51 Jesus has been on His last journey to Jerusalem, teaching in town after town. Herod's threats had just reached Him (13:31), so He was probably in Herod's lands: Galilee, or Perea across the Jordan.

It was the sabbath, the day of rest. After synagogue, a leading Pharisee had guests in for a meal cooked the day before. Jesus was invited, and “they watched him”: watched closely, waiting for Him to slip.

Where we are in Luke

From chapter 9 to chapter 19, Luke follows Jesus on one long walk toward Jerusalem and the cross. He knows where the road ends, and He keeps teaching all the way.

  1. He set His faceto go to Jerusalem9:51
  2. Martha's house“a certain village”10:38
  3. Town to townteaching, journeying13:22
  4. A Pharisee's tablea sabbath dinner14:1
  5. Jerichoa blind man, Zacchaeus18:35
  6. JerusalemHe wept over the city19:41

“And he went through the cities and villages, teaching, and journeying toward Jerusalem.”

Luke 13:22

A man with the dropsy

Dropsy is swelling, when fluid builds up in the body. It was usually a sign that something deeper was failing: the heart, the liver, or the kidneys. This man was seriously ill.

He was standing “before him”, right in front of Jesus, at a private dinner. Some think he was put there on purpose, as bait. Either way, the trap was set: heal him, and they could charge Jesus with working on the sabbath.

So Jesus asked first: “Is it lawful to heal on the sabbath day?” The experts in the law had nothing to say.

An ox in a pit

They'd break their rules for their own livestock, their own money. Jesus asked them to show the same care for a sick man. “They could not answer him again.” God made the sabbath for rest and mercy, not for leaving a man in his misery.

Part two

Friend, go up higher

Verses 7–14

While they watched Him, He watched them, and saw every guest grab for the best seat.

The highest room, the lowest room

A room is a place at the table. The places nearest the host were the honoured ones. Worship (verse 10) means honour in front of everyone.

Grab the top seat, and you may be sent to the bottom with everyone watching.

Take the bottom seat, and the host may lift you up. Same table. The difference is who did the lifting.

Let God do the lifting

This is more than table manners. It's how God's kingdom works. A man who pushes himself up will be brought down. A man who takes the low place will be lifted, by God, in His time.

“Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up.”

James 4:10

Pride says, I deserve the seat. Humility says, I'm glad to be at the table at all. Most of us know which one wrecked our lives. And Jesus lived it: the Son of God took the lowest place of all, at the cross, and God raised Him highest.

Who's on your guest list?

The usual list

They'll pay you back

Friends, brothers, relatives, rich neighbours. They'll invite you next time. A recompence, a payback, and you're even.

Jesus' list

They can't

The poor, the maimed (crippled), the lame, the blind. They have nothing to give back. God Himself will repay you, “at the resurrection of the just.”

That second list is how God treated us. We came to His table with nothing to pay Him back. Now Jesus asks us to treat others the same way: the man fresh out, the guy nobody wants to sit with, the brother who can't do a thing for you.

Part three

Yet there is room

Verses 15–24

A guest says something pious about the kingdom of God. Jesus answers with a story about a supper the invited guests skipped.

Two invitations

The guest was sure he'd be at God's great feast. Jesus' story asks: would you come if you were called? In that day a big supper took two invitations.

  1. “Bade many”the first invitation, days ahead
  2. They said yesso the host could plan
  3. “Come”at supper time: “all things are now ready”
  4. Excusesthe same guests back out

Israel had the first invitation for centuries, through Moses and the prophets. Now Jesus stood at the table saying, Come, it's ready. The supper is free. The host has paid for everything. All a guest has to do is come.

Excuses

  • “I have bought a piece of ground”Who buys land without seeing it? And it would still be there tomorrow.
  • “I have bought five yoke of oxen”Ten oxen, paid for, then tested? At supper time?
  • “I have married a wife”He doesn't even ask to be excused. Just “I cannot come.”
  • They didn't want to comeAn excuse is a polite way of saying no.

None of these things are sins. Land, work, a wife: all good gifts. But a good thing in the wrong place keeps a man from God. We've all had our reasons not to come yet. The supper doesn't wait forever.

That my house may be filled

First the servant goes into town for the people nobody invites: the poor, the crippled, the halt (lame), the blind. Think of the publicans and sinners who crowded around Jesus.

Then out past the town, to the roads and the hedges along them: drifters, strangers, outsiders. That reaches all the way to Gentiles like most of us. Compel here means urge, press hard. Don't take no for an answer. The host wants a full house.

There's still room

“And yet there is room.”

The men who thought they had a seat for sure never tasted the supper (14:24). The men who never expected an invitation filled the house. Nobody in that house earned his place. Every one of them was brought in.

“All that the Father giveth me shall come to me; and him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out.”

John 6:37

If you've ever thought you were too far gone for God's table, you're exactly who the servant was sent to find.

Part four

Counteth the cost

Verses 25–35

Big crowds are following now. Jesus turns around and tells them what it really means to come after Him.

Hate?

Jesus isn't telling men to despise their families. The same Bible says honour your father and mother and love your wife. Hate here is a strong Hebrew way of saying love less by comparison. Matthew records the same teaching in plain words:

“He that loveth father or mother more than me is not worthy of me: and he that loveth son or daughter more than me is not worthy of me.”

Matthew 10:37

Next to your love for Christ, every other love should look small. Strange as it sounds, a man who puts Jesus first loves his family better, not worse.

Bear his cross

When these men saw a man carrying a cross down the road, they knew he wasn't coming back. Rome made the condemned carry their own cross to the place of death.

Following Jesus means dying to the old life: the old self, the old ways, the old crowd. And it isn't once. Luke says it's daily:

“And he said to them all, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me.”

Luke 9:23

Men in recovery know about one day at a time. This is the same idea with a cross on it: today, say no to self and yes to Jesus. Tomorrow, do it again.

The half-built tower

A farmer built a stone tower to watch his vineyard or store his crop. A wise man sits down and does the sums first. A foolish one lays a foundation, runs out, and leaves a stump the whole valley laughs at.

Jesus wants men who know what they're signing up for. A lot of men start strong, in church and in recovery, and quit at the foundation when it gets hard.

Don't hear the wrong thing. Salvation costs you nothing. The supper was free. But following Jesus costs everything you have. Count it, and then keep building. He doesn't quit on His work in you.

Ten thousand against twenty

Each dot is a thousand men.

his army10,000

the king coming against him20,000

A king who can't win doesn't wait to be crushed. While the other army is still a great way off, he sends an ambassage, a delegation, to ask for terms of peace. Jesus says: count the cost of following Me, and count the cost of standing against God. Any man who adds it up honestly will come to terms.

“Forsaketh not all that he hath”: a disciple holds everything with an open hand. Jesus comes first, and He gets the last word over all of it.

Salt that lost its savour

Salt kept food from spoiling and gave it taste. Much of the salt in that land came from around the Dead Sea, mixed with other minerals. If damp washed the real salt out, what was left still looked like salt, but its savour, its saltiness, was gone. It was good for nothing, not even the manure pile.

A disciple who quits following is like that: looks the part, does no good. Jesus isn't saying a believer can lose his salvation. He's saying a man who won't follow loses his usefulness and his flavour.

“He that hath ears to hear, let him hear.” In other words: this means you. Don't just admire it. Do something with it.

Points to remember

  1. Sabbath“Is it lawful to heal on the sabbath day?” 14:3Mercy fits the sabbath. A man is worth more than an ox.
  2. Humility“Friend, go up higher” 14:10Take the low seat, and let God do the lifting.
  3. Grace“Call the poor, the maimed, the lame, the blind” 14:13Give to people who can't pay you back. That's what God did for us.
  4. Excuses“They all with one consent began to make excuse” 14:18An excuse is a polite no. Good things can keep a man from God.
  5. Room“And yet there is room” 14:22The invitation goes out to the hedges. There's room for you.
  6. Cost“Bear his cross, and come after me” 14:27Salvation is free. Following Jesus costs everything. Count it, and keep going.

Question 1 of 6

Sabbath

Why did Jesus heal the man on the sabbath?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Because mercy is right on the sabbath. They would pull their own ox out of a pit on the sabbath. A sick man matters far more than an ox. God gave the sabbath for rest and good, not to leave a man suffering.

Question 2 of 6

Humility

Where should a guest sit, and why?

What verses tell us about this?

“Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up.”

James 4:10

Answer

In the lowest place. A man who pushes himself up gets brought down, but a man who humbles himself gets lifted up by the host. In God's kingdom, God does the lifting.

Question 3 of 6

Grace

Who did Jesus say to invite to a feast?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The poor, the crippled, the lame and the blind: people who can't pay you back. God will repay at the resurrection. That's how God treated us, and how He wants us to treat others.

Question 4 of 6

Excuses

Why did the invited guests miss the supper?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They made excuses: land, oxen, a new wife. The real reason was they didn't want to come. Everything was ready and free, and they said no. And they never tasted the supper.

Question 5 of 6

Room

Who ended up at the great supper?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

The people nobody invites: the poor, the crippled, the lame, the blind, and then strangers from the highways and hedges. The house was filled with people who were brought in, not people who earned a seat.

Question 6 of 6

Cost

What does it cost to be Jesus' disciple?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said to them all, If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me.”

Luke 9:23

Answer

Everything. Christ comes before family, possessions and our own life, and we carry our cross every day. Salvation is a free gift. Following Jesus costs all we have, so we count the cost and keep going.
