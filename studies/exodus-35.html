Exodus 35

A willing heart

After the golden calf, the people are asked for gold again, and this time they bring it gladly. Moses names the two men the LORD has filled with His Spirit to build the tent: Bezaleel and Aholiab.

Things to watch for

Where we are in the story

  1. 25–31Commandedon the mount, the pattern for the tent
  2. 32–34Brokenthe golden calf, then mercy and new tables
  3. 35–40Donebuilt, “as the LORD commanded”

Chapters 25 to 31 were the plan, given to Moses on the mountain. Then the people fell, and Moses prayed, and the LORD forgave and wrote the tables again. Now the plan is picked up and carried out. Nothing is new in these chapters except one thing: the people.

The same men who made a calf of gold now bring gold for the LORD's house. That is the story of this chapter, and it is a story about a heart that was changed.

Part one

“This is the thing which the LORD commanded”

Verses 1–19

Moses starts with the rest, then asks for the gifts, and lists what is to be built.

Rest first, then the work

Moses is about to ask for the most important building project in Israel's history, and the first thing he says is: stop on the seventh day. Even holy work is not allowed to push out the rest.

  • a sabbath of restthe seventh day, set aside to stop
  • kindle no fireno fire to start for the day's work: the work really stops
  • put to deaththe penalty in Israel's own courts, for a nation the LORD ruled directly. It shows how seriously God meant the day

“And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:”

Mark 2:27

“There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his.”

Hebrews 4:9–10

Jesus says the day was made for man: a gift to tired men, not a trap. And Hebrews says the real rest is found in Christ, who finished the work. A man in recovery can hear that twice over: he is not meant to run on empty, and he does not have to earn his place.

The list is long, and every item has a use. Nobody is asked for anything that will sit in a pile.

Old words in the list

  • shittim woodacacia, a hard desert wood that does not rot easily
  • badgers' skinsa tough, weatherproof hide for the outer roof. The Hebrew word is uncertain: it may be a sea animal's skin
  • ephodthe priest's vest, worn over his robe
  • breastplatethe square of stones the high priest wore on his chest, one for each tribe
  • tachesclasps, here in gold and brass, that join the curtains into one
  • cunningskilled: “cunning work” is craftsman's work, not trickery

The key phrase is in verse 5: “whosoever is of a willing heart.” Nobody is taxed. Nobody is shamed into it. The offering belongs to the one whose heart is willing, and to no one else.

Everything on the list

The tent, its covering and boards, the ark with the mercy seat, the table, the lampstand, the incense altar, the altar of burnt offering, the washing basin, the court's hangings, and the priests' clothes.

  • stavespoles, slid through rings to carry a piece
  • brasen gratea bronze grill inside the altar for the fire
  • lavera washing basin for the priests
  • cloths of servicecloths for the service of the holy place, probably the coverings used when the furniture was carried

The whole of it is verse 10 spelled out: “make all that the LORD hath commanded.” Not a new design, but the design He had already given.

Part two

“Every one whose heart stirred him up”

Verses 20–29

The whole camp goes home, and comes back with its gold.

The same gold, two hearts

Exodus 32:3–4

Earrings for a calf

“And all the people brake off the golden earrings which were in their ears, and brought them unto Aaron.” The calf came out of that gold.

Exodus 35:22

Earrings for the LORD's house

“bracelets, and earrings, and rings, and tablets, all jewels of gold.” The same kind of jewels, the same people, brought to a different place.

A man's gold was never the problem. It is what he gives his heart to. They gave earrings twice. The first time was for a god of their own making. The second time was because the LORD had forgiven them and was still going to live among them.

If you have ever poured your money, time and strength into the wrong thing, this is hope. The same hands can build the right thing.

Where did the gold come from?

“And the children of Israel did according to the word of Moses; and they borrowed of the Egyptians jewels of silver, and jewels of gold, and raiment: And the LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians, so that they lent unto them such things as they required. And they spoiled the Egyptians.”

Exodus 12:35–36

Out of Egypt they walked with silver and gold the LORD Himself had given them. Now it comes back to Him. Nobody in the camp can say, “I earned all this.” It was handed to them on their way out of slavery.

That is true of whatever you have today. Your hands, your job, your wage, your clear head this morning: they were given. Giving some of it back is not losing. It is putting it where it belongs.

The women

The rulers

The onyx and the stones to be set, the spice and the oil.

Notice the phrase “with whom was found.” It is not what a man ought to have, or wishes he had. It is what was in his tent. Every one gave out of what he had, and every gift went into the work.

“Wise hearted” hands

In verse 25 the women “were wise hearted,” and what they did was spin. They spun thread by hand, in the blue, purple, scarlet and white of the curtains. In verse 26 their heart “stirred them up in wisdom” and they spun goats' hair, which is rough work.

Here wisdom is skill. It means knowing how to do something well with your hands. It was counted as a gift from God, as truly as gold.

  • spintwist fibre into thread by hand, a day's work for a pile of thread
  • blue, purple, scarletdyed thread, costly, the colors of the curtains and the veil
  • goats' haircoarse, dark thread for the tent's outer cover

Nobody records their names. What they did is in the Bible, and it is in the building. You may not be able to preach or sing. You may be able to cook, drive, fix, listen, or show up at six in the morning. That is a gift of the same kind.

Willing, not squeezed

“Whose heart stirred him up.”

The chapter keeps saying the same thing: willing, five times in all (verses 5, 21, 22 and 29, which has it twice), and stirred twice (21, 26). The gift was never forced. It came from a heart that had been moved.

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7

“For if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath, and not according to that he hath not.”

2 Corinthians 8:12

Paul says it almost word for word, fifteen hundred years later. He does not say that giving buys a blessing. He says God looks at the heart, and accepts what a man has.

Real world: what is in your tent?

A man in recovery can think he has nothing to bring: no money, a wrecked name, a few weeks clean. Read verse 23 again: “with whom was found.” Bring what is found.

  • TimeShow up. Sit with the new man. Give a brother a ride to the meeting.
  • SkillWhatever you can do with your hands, you can do it for someone in need.
  • A little moneyNot out of guilt, and not to buy anything. Out of what you have, gladly.
  • Your storyThe truth about what God has done for you is a gift to a man who thinks he is the only one.

You are not giving to earn a place. You already have one, by the blood of Christ. You give because God has been good, and because there is something of His that needs building in your church and your home.

Part three

“Filled with the spirit of God”

Verses 30–35

The LORD calls two craftsmen by name.

Two men, two tribes

  • The twelve tribes
    • Judah
      • Hurprobably the Hur of 17:12
        • Uri
          • Bezaleel“in the shadow of God”
    • Dan
      • Ahisamach
        • Aholiab“tent of the father”

The LORD calls Bezaleel “by name,” with his father and grandfather. Hur is probably the Hur who held up Moses' arm at Rephidim (17:12), though the text doesn't say.

Aholiab is from the tribe of Dan, the son of Jacob's servant woman Bilhah. A man from the royal tribe and a man from a handmaid's line build side by side. The tribe is not the point. The LORD is.

What the Spirit gave

  • curious worksskilfully designed, finely made: “curious” meant made with care
  • gold, silver, brassmetalwork: casting, beating, overlaying
  • cutting of stonessetting jewels, as in the breastplate
  • carving of woodjoinery and carving
  • the embroiderer, the weaverthe needlework of the veil and the hanging
  • that he may teachthe gift to pass on what you know

Back in 31:3 the Bible said it of this same man: he was filled with the spirit of God. And what was it for? Woodwork. Metalwork. Weaving. Not preaching, not miracles: skill, and the heart to teach it.

The Spirit's gifts are not limited to the pulpit. God put His own Spirit on a carpenter and a goldsmith, because the work was His.

Filled, for the work

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

A man who has been drunk knows what it is to be filled by something that is not good for him. Paul puts the two side by side: not filled with wine, but filled with the Spirit. A believer has the Spirit living in him from the day he trusts Christ, and being filled is giving Him the whole house.

Bezaleel's filling was to build a tent. Yours is for a job, a marriage, a house, a few men who need you. God does not ask for a man with no past. He asks for a man who will let Him fill the place the old thing held.

If you feel empty and tempted today, do not carry it alone. Call or text your brothers in Christ, and your pastor.

Points to remember

  1. The rest“Six days shall work be done, but on the seventh day there shall be to you an holy day, a sabbath of rest to the LORD” 35:2Rest comes before the work, even holy work. It is a gift to tired men.
  2. The offering“whosoever is of a willing heart, let him bring it, an offering of the LORD” 35:5God asks for what is willingly given. Nobody is squeezed.
  3. The giving“they came, both men and women, as many as were willing hearted” 35:22Everyone gave something, men and women, out of what they had.
  4. The women“all the women that were wise hearted did spin with their hands” 35:25Everyday skill is a gift from God, and it built His house.
  5. The called“the LORD hath called by name Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah” 35:30The LORD knows each worker by name, from the tribe of kings and from a handmaid's son.
  6. The filling“he hath filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, in understanding, and in knowledge” 35:31God fills a man for ordinary work, and puts it in his heart to teach it.

Question 1 of 6

The rest

What did Moses say about the seventh day, before he said a word about the building?

What verses tell us about this?

“And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:”

Mark 2:27

Answer

That the seventh day was a sabbath of rest, and no work was to be done. The rest comes before the building. Jesus says it was made for man: a gift, not a trap.

Question 2 of 6

The offering

Who was to bring the offering, and how was it to be given?

What verses tell us about this?

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7

Answer

Whoever had a willing heart, and only gladly. Nobody was taxed or pushed into it. God loves a cheerful giver.

Question 3 of 6

The giving

Who came, and what did they bring?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Men and women, rulers and ordinary people, all brought what they had: gold, silver, brass, cloth, skins, wood, stones, oil and spice. The same earrings that made a calf now build a tent.

Question 4 of 6

The women

What did the wise hearted women do with their hands?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

They spun: blue, purple, scarlet and fine linen thread, and goats' hair. Ordinary skill, given to God, and it ended up in the curtains of His house.

Question 5 of 6

The called

Who did the LORD call by name, and from which tribes?

What verses tell us about this?

Answer

Bezaleel of the tribe of Judah and Aholiab of the tribe of Dan. The LORD calls His workers by name. He knows who you are too.

Question 6 of 6

The filling

What did the LORD fill them with, and what did He put in their hearts?

What verses tell us about this?

“And be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit;”

Ephesians 5:18

Answer

With the Spirit of God, in wisdom, understanding, knowledge and every kind of workmanship, and He put it in their hearts to teach. God fills a man for ordinary work, so that others can learn it too.
